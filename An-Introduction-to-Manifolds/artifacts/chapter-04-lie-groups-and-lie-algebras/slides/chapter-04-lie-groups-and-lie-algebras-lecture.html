Lie Groups Turn Symmetry Into Calculus

Smooth multiplication, tangent vectors at the identity, and brackets give a local language for continuous symmetry.

Smooth Group

Multiplication and inversion are smooth maps on a manifold.

Matrix Model

Classical examples are open sets, level sets, or closed subgroups inside matrices.

Local Algebra

The tangent space at the identity carries the infinitesimal bracket.

Source grounding: printed pages 164-189; PDF pages 183-208. Notebook: An-Introduction-to-Manifolds/chapter-04-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras/04-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras.ipynb

Open the lecture by naming the central compression of the chapter. A Lie group is a manifold with algebraic operations, but the reason the chapter matters is that global symmetry becomes local calculus at the identity. Tell students that the deck will keep moving information toward the identity: translations move tangent vectors, the exponential creates curves, determinant and trace detect tangent constraints, and left-invariant vector fields turn the tangent space into a Lie algebra.

A Lie Group Has Smooth Algebra

Definition
$$\mu:G\times G\to G,\qquad \mu(a,b)=ab$$
$$\iota:G\to G,\qquad \iota(a)=a^{-1}$$
Manifold plus algebra

The word smooth is doing real work: multiplication and inversion are maps between manifolds, not just set-theoretic operations.

Source grounding: Chapter 4 definition of Lie groups and smooth group operations.

Give the definition slowly, because it is the doorway to everything else in the lecture. The group axioms are familiar, but the new condition is that the two algebraic operations are smooth as maps of manifolds. Multiplication is a map from the product manifold to the group, and inverse is a self map. This means that all later constructions can be differentiated, so the algebraic structure can produce tangent maps, vector fields, and eventually a bracket.

Translations Move Calculus To The Identity

Functoriality
$$\ell_g(h)=gh,\qquad r_g(h)=hg$$
$$F\circ \ell_h=\ell_{F(h)}\circ F$$
Use

Translations identify tangent spaces around the group with the single model space \(T_eG\).

Source grounding: homomorphism identity for left translations in Chapter 4.

This slide should make left translation feel like the basic transport mechanism. Multiplying by a fixed group element is a diffeomorphism, so its differential carries tangent vectors from one point of the group to another. The displayed homomorphism identity is the first sign that these transports behave naturally under maps of Lie groups. Later, differentiating this kind of square is exactly how a group homomorphism produces a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Use this slide to separate examples by how their manifold structure is found. General linear matrices form an open subset because nonzero determinant is an open condition. Special linear and orthogonal groups are constraint sets, so tangent spaces come from differentiating equations. The important subtlety is the target space of the constraint map. For orthogonal matrices, A transpose A is symmetric, and using that smaller target gives the correct dimension count.

Lie Subgroups Have Several Geometric Personalities

Subgroup geometry
Embedded

A subgroup sits as a submanifold with the subspace topology. Closed matrix subgroups behave this way.

Immersed

A smooth injective parameterization can be a subgroup even when the image topology is not the inherited topology.

Dense

Some subgroups wind everywhere in a compact group, so visual closure and group membership diverge.

Source grounding: Lie subgroups, embedded versus immersed examples, and dense subgroup behavior.

Students often expect every subgroup of a Lie group to inherit a neat submanifold structure. This chapter asks them to be more precise. Some subgroups are embedded, some are only immersed images, and compact groups can contain dense one-parameter subgroups. The distinction matters because the same algebraic subset can have different topological behavior depending on the inclusion map and the topology we put on the subgroup.

Closed Subgroups Recover Embedded Geometry

Structure theorem
Closed Subgroup Principle

A closed subgroup of a Lie group has a unique smooth structure making it an embedded Lie subgroup.

Matrix consequence

Closed matrix groups can be studied by differentiating their defining matrix equations at the identity.

Boundary of use

Dense subgroups fail the closed hypothesis; they require immersed subgroup language.

Source grounding: closed subgroup theorem and matrix subgroup applications.

State the theorem as an organizing rule rather than a technical detour. Closedness converts an algebraic subgroup into an embedded smooth object, so the surrounding Lie group supplies the right local charts and tangent spaces. This is why matrix groups defined by equations are so useful: once closedness or a regular level set argument is available, the infinitesimal object can be found by differentiating the equations at the identity.

The Matrix Exponential Builds Curves Through The Identity

One-parameter curves
$$e^X=I+X+\frac{X^2}{2!}+\frac{X^3}{3!}+\cdots$$
$$\frac{d}{dt}e^{tX}=Xe^{tX}=e^{tX}X$$
Initial velocity

The curve \(t\mapsto e^{tX}\) starts at \(I\) with velocity \(X\).

Source grounding: matrix exponential and its derivative identity.

The exponential is the bridge from a tangent matrix to a curve in the group. Read the power series as the definition that makes sense for matrices, and then emphasize the derivative identity. Since X commutes with its own exponential series, the derivative can be written on either side. At t equals zero the curve is at the identity and the velocity is X, which is why exponential curves are the main way to test candidate Lie algebra elements.

Treat trace as the infinitesimal volume counter. The determinant of the exponential is exponential of the trace, so trace zero should be read as infinitesimal volume preservation. The cyclic trace identity is one of the algebraic reasons these formulas behave well under conjugation and commutators. The numeric residual is not the proof, but it verifies that the chapter artifact is measuring the same determinant-trace identity used in the lecture.

The Determinant Differential At \(I\) Is Trace

Linearization
$$\det_{*,I}(X)=\operatorname{tr}X$$
$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\det(I+tX)\right|_{t=0}=x_{11}+\cdots+x_{nn}$$
Consequence

The tangent vectors to \(\operatorname{SL}(n)\) at \(I\) are exactly the trace-zero matrices.

Artifact grounding: det-trace-exponential-check.json.

This slide is the tangent-space computation for special linear groups. Linearize the determinant level set at the identity: the derivative of determinant in the direction X is the trace of X. Therefore the tangent condition for det equals one is trace zero. The picture separates directions tangent to the level set from directions that immediately leave it. This is the first explicit example of a Lie algebra appearing as a linearized group constraint.

Now put the determinant and orthogonality computations side by side. For special linear matrices, differentiating det equals one gives trace zero. For orthogonal matrices, differentiating A transpose A equals I along a curve through I gives X transpose plus X equals zero. These are not arbitrary definitions. They are the linear constraints forced by the defining group equations, and they define the classical Lie algebras at the identity.

Left-Invariant Fields Are Global Copies Of One Vector

Transport
$$X_{gh}=\ell_{g*}(X_h)$$
$$(\widetilde A)_g=\ell_{g*}A$$
Positive diagonal check

formula: X_g = gB
left transport residual: 0.0

Artifact grounding: left-invariant-field-transport.json.

This is where the tangent space at the identity becomes global. Choose one vector A in the identity tangent space, then push it forward by every left translation. The resulting vector field is left-invariant because translating the group translates the field values in the same way. Stress that this construction is reversible: a left-invariant field is completely determined by its value at the identity, so the space of such fields is naturally identified with T_eG.

The Bracket Lives On The Identity Tangent Space

Transfer
$$[A,B]=[\widetilde A,\widetilde B]_e$$
Lie algebra

The bracket of left-invariant fields is left-invariant, so the operation closes on \(T_eG\).

Meaning

The bracket measures infinitesimal noncommutativity of flows.

Source grounding: left-invariant vector fields and the induced Lie bracket on \(T_eG\).

The ordinary vector-field bracket lives on vector fields, not directly on tangent vectors. The left-invariant construction lets us transfer it. Given A and B at the identity, extend them uniquely to left-invariant fields, take the vector-field bracket, and evaluate back at the identity. The theorem behind the definition is closure: the bracket of left-invariant fields is again left-invariant, so the result is again controlled by one vector at the identity.

Homomorphisms Differentiate To Lie Algebra Homomorphisms

Pushforward
$$F_*[A,B]=[F_*A,F_*B]$$
$$F\circ\ell_h=\ell_{F(h)}\circ F$$
Ad example

Inner automorphisms differentiate to the adjoint action, and the bracket is preserved.

Check

inner automorphism bracket residual: 5.83e-16

Artifact grounding: homomorphism-differential-checks.json.

Return to the translation identity from the beginning of the lecture. A Lie group homomorphism respects multiplication, so it respects left translations. Differentiating that compatibility gives a pushforward map from T_eG to T_eH, and the left-invariant-field bracket is natural under this pushforward. The result is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Mention the adjoint representation as the important self-map example: conjugation by a group element differentiates to a bracket-preserving linear map.

Closing synthesis for Chapter 4: Lie Groups and Lie Algebras.

Close by giving students the full circuit. Smooth group operations allow translations and homomorphisms to be differentiated. Matrix constraints turn group equations into tangent equations at the identity. Exponential curves test whether tangent matrices integrate back into the group. Left-invariant fields move from individual tangent vectors to vector fields and back, giving a bracket on T_eG. The main takeaway is that Lie theory lets us study continuous symmetry by doing linear algebra plus one noncommutative bracket at the identity.
