Tangent vectors at one point

A Tangent Vector Is a Derivation on Germs

Definition

A tangent vector at \(p\) is a linear map \(X_p:C^\infty_p(M)\to \mathbb{R}\) satisfying the product rule

\(X_p(fg)=X_p(f)g(p)+f(p)X_p(g)\).

Why This Is First-Order

  • Constants vanish under every derivation.
  • Only the germ of \(f\) near \(p\) matters.
  • In coordinates, derivations become directional derivatives.
concept: tangent vector as derivationprinted pages 86-163
Start with the algebraic definition because it is the coordinate-free anchor for the chapter. A tangent vector at p is not drawn as a little arrow first; it is a rule that differentiates germs and obeys the Leibniz identity. The arrow picture will reappear after this definition has earned its invariance.
Smooth maps act on tangent vectors

Differentials Push Derivations Forward

Definition

For \(F:M\to N\), \(p\in M\), and \(g\in C^\infty_{F(p)}(N)\), define

\(F_{*,p}X_p(g)=X_p(g\circ F)\).

Coordinate Translation

In charts, \(F_{*,p}\) is represented by the Jacobian matrix \(J_F(p)\). The derivation formula explains why that matrix is intrinsic.

formula: pushforward by precompositiontarget functions pull back to source functions
The pushforward is defined by asking the old derivation to act on a pulled-back target function. This is the key teaching move: the formula is not a matrix formula yet, it is a way to transport first-order tests through a smooth map. Coordinates later reveal the same operation as the Jacobian.
Functoriality of tangent spaces

The Chain Rule Says Pushforwards Compose

Chain Rule

\((G\circ F)_{*,p}=G_{*,F(p)}\circ F_{*,p}\).

Smooth maps induce linear maps on tangent spaces, and composition is preserved.

Read It Two Ways

  • Push vectors forward: \(T_pM\to T_{F(p)}N\to T_{G(F(p))}P\).
  • Pull functions back: \(h\mapsto h\circ G\mapsto h\circ G\circ F\).
category bridge: maps induce tangent mapschain rule is functoriality
Use the commutative diagram to make the chain rule look inevitable. Pull functions back through G and then F, or push a tangent vector forward through F and then G; both descriptions encode the same first-order information. This is the first glimpse of tangent spaces behaving functorially.
Coordinates make derivations computable

Coordinate Bases and Transition Matrices

Local Basis

In a chart \((U,x^1,\ldots,x^n)\), every tangent vector has the form

\(X_p=\sum_i X_p(x^i)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p\).

Transition Rule

If \(y=y(x)\), then

\(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p=\sum_j \frac{\partial y^j}{\partial x^i}(p)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial y^j}\right|_p\).
coordinate bases are computational, not intrinsictransition matrix: Jacobian of chart change
Once coordinates are chosen, derivations acquire a basis and look like column vectors of partial derivatives. The transition rule is the important invariant content: a different chart changes the coefficients by the Jacobian of the coordinate change, so the tangent vector itself is not tied to one coordinate grid.
Velocity realizes tangent vectors

Curves Recover the Same Differential

Velocity as a Pushforward

\(c'(t)=c_{*,t}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)\).

The curve \(c:\mathbb{R}\to M\) converts the standard vector \(d/dt\) into a vector at \(c(t)\).

Image velocity and Jacobian push: \(\left(-\frac13,-\frac23+\cos\frac12\right)\).

Residual: \((0,0)\).

Rank as local diagnostic

The Differential Detects Critical Behavior

Model Map

\(F(x,y)=(x^2-y^2,2xy)\), \(\det(dF)=4(x^2+y^2)\).

The determinant vanishes only at the origin; the rank is full at \((1,0)\) and drops at \((0,0)\).

Diagnostic Question

Where does the linear approximation lose rank? Those points are the first suspects for folds, crossings, and singular level behavior.

artifact check: rank-detector-check.jsonlocal figure: differential-rank-detector.png
Rank is the chapter's local diagnostic. For the square map shown here, the determinant of the Jacobian is positive away from the origin and zero at the origin, so the differential sees exactly where the map stops being locally full rank. This visual prepares the regular and critical level-set contrast.
Submanifolds from equations

Regular Level Sets Have Tangent Spaces Given by Kernels

Regular Level Formula

\(T_p(F^{-1}(c))=\ker F_{*,p}\).

For a regular value, the kernel has the expected dimension.

Notebook Check

Regular circle: minimum gradient norm \(2.0\), expected dimension \(1\).

Critical zero crossing: gradient at the origin is \(0\), so regularity fails.

artifact check: regular-level-set-check.jsonlocal figure: regular-critical-level-sets.png
Regular level sets turn equations into manifolds because the differential has enough independent rows to solve for the missing coordinates. The tangent space is the kernel of the differential. On the critical crossing, the kernel test loses its expected dimension, and the picture stops being a clean embedded curve at the origin.
Local normal form

Constant Rank Straightens the Map in Suitable Charts

Normal Form

If \(f\) has constant rank \(k\), then in suitable coordinates

\(f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(x_1,\ldots,x_k,0,\ldots,0)\).

Regular Submanifold Model

A subset \(S\subset N\) is regular of dimension \(k\) when charts make it a coordinate slice.

\(S\cap U=\{x^{k+1}=\cdots=x^n=0\}\).
  • Regular level sets are kernel slices.
  • Embedding images inherit this local model.
constant rank theorem: stable rank gives a normal formlocal, not global
The constant-rank theorem is the stabilizing result behind many examples. If the rank is locally fixed, charts on source and target straighten the map into projection onto the first k coordinates followed by zeros. Use this to name the regular-submanifold model: locally a subset is a coordinate slice, with the last coordinates set to zero. Regular level sets and embedding images earn their manifold structure by reducing to that model.
Rank hypotheses and global failures

Immersions, Submersions, Embeddings: Same Rank Test, Different Guarantees

Map Type
What the differential says
Immersion
Injective \(F_{*,p}\) at every \(p\)
Submersion
Surjective \(F_{*,p}\) at every \(p\)
Embedding
Immersion plus homeomorphism onto its image

Failure Mode

A parametrized curve can be an immersion while its image crosses itself. Rank is local; embeddedness also has a global topological demand.

rank is localembeddedness is local plus topology
Separate three issues that students often blend together: injectivity of the differential, surjectivity of the differential, and global topological behavior. An immersion can pass the tangent-space test and still self-intersect. An embedding adds the topological condition needed for the image to inherit the correct manifold structure.
A concrete tangent space

The Tangent Plane Is the First-Order Model of a Surface

Parametrized Surface

If \(r(u,v)\) parametrizes \(M\) near \(p=r(u_0,v_0)\), then

\(T_pM=\operatorname{span}\{r_u(u_0,v_0),r_v(u_0,v_0)\}\).

Implicit Surface

For \(M=F^{-1}(c)\) with \(c\) regular, the same plane is \(\ker F_{*,p}\).

two descriptions: parametrized velocities and kernel of differentialsame tangent space
The surface example returns to the arrow intuition after the invariant machinery is in place. A tangent plane is spanned by coordinate velocity vectors, and for a level surface it is also a kernel. The point is that both the parametrized and implicit descriptions agree through the same differential language.
All tangent spaces at once

The Tangent Bundle Packages Fibers Over the Manifold

Bundle Definition

\(TM=\coprod_{p\in M}T_pM=\{(p,v):v\in T_pM\},\qquad \pi(p,v)=p\).

The projection sends a vector to the point where it is based.

Local Trivialization

A chart \(x:U\to\mathbb{R}^n\) identifies \(\pi^{-1}(U)\) with \(U\times\mathbb{R}^n\): base coordinates plus vector coefficients.

bundle viewpoint: base point plus vector fiberlocal product, global geometry
Move from one tangent space to all tangent spaces at once. The tangent bundle is a disjoint union, or equivalently the set of pairs consisting of a base point and a tangent vector at that point. The projection remembers the base point, while local coordinates turn the bundle into U times R to the n. This is the local triviality pattern that will guide vector bundles and sections.
Vector fields are sections

Fields, Frames, and Coefficient Functions

Local Form

\(X=\sum_i a_i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\).

The coefficients \(a_i\) are smooth functions on the chart domain.

Frames

A local frame is a smooth basis of sections. It lets every nearby vector field be written by coefficient functions.

vector field: smooth section of \(TM\)local coefficients: smooth functions
A vector field is a smooth choice of tangent vector at every point, so it is a section of the tangent bundle. In a coordinate chart, a field is a sum of coefficient functions times coordinate basis fields. Frames package local bases of sections and make computations look like moving linear algebra.
Gluing local data smoothly

Partitions of Unity Turn Local Constructions Global

Definition Data

  • \(\rho_\alpha\ge 0\).
  • The family is locally finite.
  • \(\sum_\alpha \rho_\alpha=1\).
  • \(\operatorname{supp}\rho_\alpha\subset U_\alpha\).

Use Pattern

Build locally on \(U_\alpha\), multiply by \(\rho_\alpha\), and sum. Local finiteness keeps the result smooth.

artifact check: bundle-partition-check.jsonlocal figure: tangent-bundle-partition-unity.png
Partitions of unity are the chapter's glue. The conditions of nonnegativity, local finiteness, unit sum, and support control let local constructions be blended without losing smoothness. This is why bump functions appear next to tangent bundles: they make local frames and local data usable on the whole manifold.
Vector fields generate motion

Integral Curves Assemble Into Local Flows

Integral Curve

\(c'(t)=X_{c(t)}\).

Local Flow Laws

\(F_0=\operatorname{id},\qquad F_t\circ F_s=F_{t+s}\)

The composition law is asserted only where both sides are defined.

ODE viewpoint: vector field as motion generatorlocal flow is a partial one-parameter group
Vector fields are not only derivations; they generate motion. Integral curves solve an ordinary differential equation on the manifold, and the local flow records all such solutions at once. The identity and composition laws hold where both sides are defined, which is why local flows behave like partial one-parameter groups.
Noncommuting first-order directions

Lie Brackets and Related Vector Fields

Bracket Definition

\([X,Y]f=X(Yf)-Y(Xf)\).

Relatedness Through \(F\)

\(F_{*,p}(X_p)=\bar X_{F(p)},\qquad X(g\circ F)=(\bar X g)\circ F\).

Pushforward Warning

If \(F(p)=F(q)\), a pushed field on the target is well defined only when \(F_{*,p}X_p=F_{*,q}X_q\). Diffeomorphisms avoid this; \(F\)-relatedness is the replacement condition.

Notebook Check

Symbolic bracket: \((-y/2,-x/2)\). At the base point, the flow commutator estimate matches \((-0.2,-0.4)\) to numerical precision.

artifact check: flow-lie-bracket-check.jsonlocal figure: flow-commutator-lie-bracket.png
The Lie bracket measures the first-order defect in commuting two vector-field directions. It is defined as a commutator of differential operators and can also be detected by a small flow commutator. Pause on the pushforward warning: if F sends two source points p and q to the same target point, then F star of X at p and at q must agree before they define one target vector field. Diffeomorphisms avoid the ambiguity, and F-relatedness is the replacement condition for general smooth maps.
