Chapter 7: De Rham Theory

Differential forms become topological instruments: closed forms record local conservation, exact forms have global potentials, and cohomology measures the obstruction between them.

Lecture Arc

  • One closed form can expose a missing global potential.
  • Exact sequences and Mayer-Vietoris turn obstructions into computations.
  • Homotopy proves the invariant is topological, not coordinate-bound.
  • Periods, Betti numbers, products, genus, and degree give concrete tests.

1. The first obstruction

Closed Need Not Mean Exact

Local Test

\(d\omega = 0\)

A closed form has no local exterior derivative. On a small contractible patch, it often behaves like a differential of something.

Global Test

\(\omega = d\eta\ ?\)

An exact form has a single global potential. A loop can detect when local potentials fail to fit together around a hole.

Exact forms are automatically closed because \(d^2=0\)Core question: what closed forms remain after quotienting by exact ones?
Pause on the distinction because it drives every later construction. Closed is a local differential condition, while exact is the existence of a global primitive. Students should hear that exact implies closed for a formal reason, namely d squared is zero, but the reverse can fail because a global loop detects incompatible local primitives.
Punctured plane model

The Angular Form Carries A Period

Closed, But Not Exact

\(\omega=\dfrac{-y\,dx+x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}\)
\(d\omega=0,\qquad \int_{S^1}\omega=2\pi\)

If \(\omega=d f\), then every closed-loop integral would vanish. The unit circle gives a nonzero period, so the class survives in cohomology.

Artifact reference: angular-period figureCheck: closed-not-exact-angular-check.json
Use the punctured plane as the chapter's first concrete witness. The displayed form is closed away from the origin, so locally it passes the exterior derivative test. The period around the unit circle is two pi, and that number rules out exactness because integrals of exact one-forms over closed loops are zero.
2. Quotienting out global potentials

Definition Of De Rham Cohomology

Definition
$$Z^k(M)=\ker\!\left(d:\Omega^k(M)\to \Omega^{k+1}(M)\right),\qquad B^k(M)=\operatorname{im}\!\left(d:\Omega^{k-1}(M)\to \Omega^k(M)\right)$$ $$H^k(M)=Z^k(M)/B^k(M).$$
Because \(d^2=0\), exact \(k\)-forms lie inside closed \(k\)-formsThe quotient is the obstruction space
Present the quotient slowly and tie every symbol to the previous slide. Z k is the closed part, B k is the exact part, and d squared equal to zero ensures B k sits inside Z k. The quotient forgets changes by global potentials and keeps precisely the period data that exact forms cannot carry.
Component count and dimension bounds

First Calculations: Points, Lines, Circles

Degree Zero

\(H^0(M)\cong \mathbb{R}^r\)

Closed functions are locally constant, so \(r\) connected components give \(r\) independent constants.

Above Dimension

\(H^k(M)=0\quad(k>\dim M)\)

There are no nonzero \(k\)-forms once the degree exceeds the manifold dimension.

First Examples

\(H^0(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R},\ H^1(\mathbb{R})=0\)
\(H^0(S^1)=\mathbb{R},\ H^1(S^1)=\mathbb{R}\)
Dimension statements are vector-space statements over \(\mathbb{R}\)The circle generator will be recomputed by Mayer-Vietoris
Use these examples to calibrate the invariant before introducing heavier machinery. Degree zero counts connected components because a closed function is locally constant. On the real line every closed one-form has a global primitive, while the circle retains one independent period. The dimension bound reminds students that the complex stops at the manifold dimension.
Maps reverse direction

Pullback Makes Cohomology Contravariant

Functoriality

  • If \(d\omega=0\), then \(d(f^*\omega)=f^*(d\omega)=0\).
  • If \(\omega=d\eta\), then \(f^*\omega=d(f^*\eta)\).
  • So \(f:N\to M\) induces \(f^*:H^k(M)\to H^k(N)\).
Ring Structure
The wedge product descends to cohomology and gives a graded anticommutative ring: $$[\alpha]\wedge[\beta]=[\alpha\wedge\beta],\qquad [\alpha]\wedge[\beta]=(-1)^{kl}[\beta]\wedge[\alpha].$$
Contravariance: maps of manifolds reverse arrows on cohomologyProducts remember more than Betti numbers alone
Emphasize that pullback is the natural operation on forms, so cohomology runs opposite to smooth maps. The key calculation is d commuting with pullback, which preserves both closed and exact forms. Then add the second payoff: wedge product survives the quotient and turns de Rham cohomology into a graded ring with signs.
3. Algebraic machines for forms

Exactness Is Image Equals Kernel

At Every Node

\(\operatorname{im} f=\ker g\)

The output from the previous arrow is exactly the input killed by the next arrow. This is the bookkeeping rule behind long exact sequences.

Cochain Cohomology

\(H^k(C)=\ker d^k/\operatorname{im} d^{k-1}\)

De Rham cohomology is one instance of a general cochain-complex calculation.

Artifact reference: exact-sequence ledgerCheck: exact-sequence-check.json
Before using exact sequences, make the phrase exact at a node completely concrete. The image of one map must be the entire kernel of the next map. The cohomology formula is exactly the measure of failure of exactness: closed things in the kernel modulo exact things coming from the previous image.
Connecting homomorphisms

The Zig-Zag Creates The Next Degree

Short Exact Complexes
A degreewise short exact sequence \(0\to A^*\to B^*\to C^*\to 0\) gives a long exact sequence in cohomology, with connecting maps \(\delta:H^k(C)\to H^{k+1}(A)\).
The connecting map is not magic; it is a controlled lift-and-differentiate moveLong exact sequences compare cohomology degree by degree
Explain the connecting map as a construction rather than as a symbol. Start with a closed class in C, lift it to B, apply the differential, and exactness forces the result to come from A in the next degree. The zig-zag proof is the algebraic engine later used for Mayer-Vietoris.
4. Cut the manifold into computable pieces

Mayer-Vietoris Starts From A Short Exact Complex

Mayer-Vietoris Forms Complex
$$0\to\Omega^*(M)\to\Omega^*(U)\oplus\Omega^*(V)\to\Omega^*(U\cap V)\to0.$$

Partition functions split overlap data so local pieces on \(U\) and \(V\) glue back to a global form.

How To Read It

  1. Restrict a form on \(M\) to \(U\) and \(V\).
  2. Subtract the two restrictions on \(U\cap V\).
  3. Exactness says compatible local forms glue globally.
  4. The long exact sequence computes \(H^*(M)\).
The short exact complex is the local-to-global bridgeNext: use it on \(S^1\)
Frame Mayer-Vietoris as the chapter's main computation machine. Forms restrict to U and V, and the difference of the two restrictions on the overlap measures whether local data agree. The partition-of-unity move splits overlap data into pieces that extend over U and V, which is the practical reason the short complex is exact. The resulting long exact sequence converts cover information into cohomology.

5. Moving without changing cohomology

Deformation Retraction Keeps The Class

Homotopy Message

  • A deformation retraction supplies maps \(M\to A\to M\) whose composite is homotopic to the identity.
  • Homotopy equivalent manifolds have isomorphic de Rham cohomology.
  • The punctured plane therefore has the same cohomology as \(S^1\).
The angular period survives the radial motion.
Artifact reference: deformation-retraction figureCheck: deformation-retraction-check.json
This is the geometric reason cohomology deserves to be called topological. The punctured plane looks two-dimensional and noncompact, but radial deformation keeps its circle of directions. The angular class is unchanged under the retraction, so the computation reduces to the circle without losing the period obstruction.
Local exactness, global topology

Homotopy Axiom Meets The Poincare Lemma

Homotopy Axiom
If \(f_0,f_1:N\to M\) are smoothly homotopic, then \(f_0^*=f_1^*\) on \(H^*(M)\).
Poincare Lemma
On a star-shaped or contractible patch, every closed \(k\)-form with \(k>0\) is exact.
Local exactness does not erase global periodsHomotopy invariance explains why deformation retractions compute cohomology
Connect two ideas that can otherwise feel separate. The Poincare lemma says de Rham cohomology vanishes locally in positive degree, so nonzero classes must be global obstructions. The homotopy axiom says smooth deformation does not alter those classes. Together, they justify computing with simpler homotopy models while still respecting local calculus.

Concrete invariants

Surface Genus And Degree-Style Scaling

Closed Oriented Surface

\(b_0,b_1,b_2=(1,2g,1)\)
\(\chi=2-2g\)

Each handle contributes two independent one-dimensional periods. The top class records orientation.

Pullback Scaling

\(F^*[\nu_N]=\deg(F)\,[\nu_M]\)

For normalized top classes on closed oriented manifolds of the same dimension, pullback scales the top cohomology generator by the map degree.

Genus is visible in \(b_1\), while degree is visible in top cohomologyRing products refine the vector-space summary
Pull the computations into recognizable invariants. For a closed oriented surface of genus g, the first Betti number is two g, one pair for each handle, and the Euler characteristic is two minus two g. Then explain the degree-style statement: on top cohomology, pullback by a degree d map multiplies the orientation generator by d.
6. Why homotopy invariance is true

Cochain Homotopies Turn Motion Into A Boundary

Operator Identity

\(\phi-\psi=dK+Kd\)

If two cochain maps differ by \(dK+Kd\), they induce the same map in cohomology because boundaries vanish after passing to classes.

\(i_1^*-i_0^*=dK+Kd\)
\(K\omega=\int_0^1 i_{\partial_t}\omega\,dt\)
Proof Mechanism
Forms on \(M\times[0,1]\) split into spatial parts and \(dt\)-parts. Integrating the \(dt\)-part defines \(K\), and the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the endpoint difference.
Artifact reference: cochain-homotopy scaffoldCheck: cochain-homotopy-proof-check.json
Close with the proof idea rather than a long formal proof. The endpoint pullbacks from M times the interval differ by an exact cochain-level expression, d K plus K d, where K integrates the contraction of the form with the interval vector field partial t from zero to one. Once a closed form is passed to cohomology, that difference disappears. The whole homotopy axiom is therefore the fundamental theorem of calculus written in the language of forms.
