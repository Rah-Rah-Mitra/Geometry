Motivation

Why \(\mathbb{R}^n\) Is The Laboratory

Chapter Goal

Keep the computational power of multivariable calculus while replacing global arrows and coordinates by local algebraic tests.

Coordinates

Partial derivatives, curves, and Taylor formulas are available for inspection.

Locality

Germs ignore what a function does away from the point being tested.

Algebra

Derivations and wedges record behavior without needing a surrounding space.

Portability

Every formula is kept only if it has a chart-level interpretation.

Euclidean space is the rehearsal room, not the endpointtheme: local formulas with coordinate-independent meaning
Frame Euclidean space as a controlled test site. Coordinates, lines, and partial derivatives are available, so every abstract idea can be checked against ordinary calculus. The point is to identify which formulas depend on the grid and which ones can be transported through a chart.
Smooth functions

Smooth Does Not Mean Analytic

Flat Function
\(f(x)=e^{-1/x^2}\) for \(x>0\), and \(f(x)=0\) for \(x\le 0\).
Inspection Target

The Taylor polynomial at the origin is the zero polynomial, but the function is not zero on every neighborhood to the right of zero.

Recorded Ratios

At the smallest sampled point, every checked ratio \(f(x)/x^k\) is tiny, matching flatness through degree five.

evidence: flat-function-ratios.csvcheck: flat smooth nonanalytic
Use the flat function to separate smoothness from analyticity. It is nonzero on the positive side, but every sampled quotient f(x) over x to the k collapses rapidly near zero. The Taylor series at zero sees only zeros, so smooth local behavior is weaker than analytic determination.
Local algebra

Taylor Remainder As A Local Engine

Remainder Form
\(f(x)=f(p)+\sum_i (x^i-p^i)g_i(x)\)

with \(g_i\) smooth near \(p\) and \(g_i(p)=\partial_i f(p)\).

Why it matters

Every local change in \(f\) is factored through coordinate differences. A derivation only has to know what it does to those coordinates.

local statement: only a neighborhood of p mattersnext: apply a derivation to this identity
Treat the Taylor remainder formula as the algebraic hinge for derivations. It rewrites the variation of f near p using coordinate differences times smooth coefficient functions. At p, those coefficients become the ordinary partial derivatives, which lets an abstract product-rule operator recover a vector.
Tangent vectors

From Arrow To Operator

Directional Derivative
\(D_v f(p)=\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_0 f(p+tv)=\sum_i v^i\,\partial_i f(p)\)
Recorded Agreement

For the notebook test field, the curve derivative and gradient pairing both give \(687/1000-\cos(3/5)/4\).

Operator View

The vector is identified by how it differentiates every smooth test function at \(p\).

check: derivation-leibniz-check.jsonsame directional value: true
Connect the geometric arrow to its action on functions. A vector v at p can be seen by running along the line p plus tv and differentiating f at t equals zero. The coordinate formula and the gradient pairing are not extra structures; they are the operator D_v in coordinates.
Definition

Point Derivations

Derivation At p

A point derivation is a linear map \(D:C_p^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)\to\mathbb{R}\) satisfying

\(D(fg)=D(f)g(p)+f(p)D(g)\).
Germ Principle

If two functions agree on some neighborhood of \(p\), a derivation at \(p\) cannot distinguish them.

algebraic rule: linearity plus Leibnizlocal object: germs of smooth functions at p
Slow down on the definition because it is the first genuinely manifold-ready move. A point derivation is a linear rule on germs of smooth functions at p, and the Leibniz identity only samples f and g at p after differentiating one factor. The germ language removes irrelevant far-away behavior.
Theorem 2.2 roadmap

\(T_p(\mathbb{R}^n)\cong D_p(\mathbb{R}^n)\)

Isomorphism
\(T_p(\mathbb{R}^n)\to D_p(\mathbb{R}^n),\quad v\mapsto D_v\)
Proof Moves
  • Coordinate functions detect the components of \(v\).
  • The Taylor formula writes every germ using those coordinates.
  • The Leibniz rule reduces \(D(f)\) to first-order data.
coordinate data recovered: \(D(x^i-p^i)\)the arrow picture and derivation picture agree
Present the isomorphism as a proof strategy rather than a finished black box. Injectivity is tested on coordinate functions. Surjectivity uses the Taylor remainder to express f minus f(p), then the derivation rule kills constants and forces Df to be a coordinate-weighted directional derivative.
Global derivations

Vector Fields As Global Derivations

Coordinate Form
\(X=\sum_i a^i(x)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\), so \(Xf=\sum_i a^i\,\partial_i f\).
Algebra Rule
\(X(fg)=(Xf)g+f(Xg)\)

Pointwise To Global

At each \(p\), \(X_p\) is a point derivation; as \(p\) varies smoothly, \(X\) becomes a derivation of \(C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)\).

local rule repeated smoothly over the domainpreview: vector fields on manifolds use the same algebra
Move from one point to all points. A smooth vector field differentiates every smooth function and returns another smooth function, so it acts as a derivation of the function algebra. This is the global version of the point story and previews vector fields on manifolds.
Covectors

Dual Space And Cobasis

Dual Basis
\(\alpha^i(e_j)=\delta^i_j\)

Interpretation

A covector reads one component of a vector. The full cobasis reads all components.

Later Name

On \(\mathbb{R}^n\), the coordinate cobasis is written \(dx^1,\ldots,dx^n\).

dual object: linear functional on vectorscoordinate notation becomes \(dx^i\)
Make covectors feel like coordinate readers rather than mysterious dual objects. The covector alpha superscript i evaluates basis vector e_j by the Kronecker delta. In coordinates this is the identity matrix, and later the same notation becomes dx^i on a chart.
Exterior algebra

Alternating Oriented Measurement

Alternating k-Covector

A multilinear function of \(k\) vectors that changes sign when two inputs are swapped.

Collapse Rule
repeated direction \(\Longrightarrow\) measured \(k\)-volume \(=0\)

Geometry

Alternation is the algebraic form of oriented length, area, volume, and higher-dimensional measurement.

orientation is encoded by signsthe wedge product is the multiplication that preserves that bookkeeping
Introduce alternating covectors as signed measuring devices. Swapping two input vectors reverses orientation, while repeating a direction collapses the measured k-dimensional volume to zero. The parallelogram picture should make the sign convention feel geometric before the wedge notation arrives.

Forms on Euclidean space

Differential Forms On \(\mathbb{R}^n\)

Coordinate Expansion
\(\omega=\sum_I a_I(x)\,dx^I\)

The coefficient functions vary smoothly with \(x\).

Degree 0

Functions measure points: \(f(x)\).

Degree 1

One-forms read directed motion.

Degree 2

Two-forms read oriented area elements.

forms are smooth fields of alternating covectorsnotation: \(dx^I=dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}\)
Now attach the exterior algebra smoothly to each point. A differential k-form has coefficient functions a_I and local basis wedges dx^I. Zero-forms are functions, one-forms read directed motion, and two-forms read oriented area, all with coefficients varying over the Euclidean domain.
The operator d

Exterior Derivative

Functions
\(df=\sum_i \partial_i f\,dx^i\)
General Form
if \(\omega=\sum_I a_I\,dx^I\), then \(d\omega=\sum_I da_I\wedge dx^I\).
Product Rule
\(d(\omega\wedge\eta)=d\omega\wedge\eta+(-1)^k\omega\wedge d\eta\)
d raises degree by onesame rule controls gradients, curls, and divergences
Present d as the single operation that raises degree and packages many familiar derivatives. For functions, df is the coordinate gradient written as a one-form. For a general form, d differentiates the coefficient functions and wedges the result onto the existing basis wedge.
Structural identity

Why \(d^2=0\)

Identity
\(d(d\omega)=0\)
Applied Lab Check

The symbolic test for a general-looking \(1\)-form in three variables records \(d^2\alpha=0\).

Immediate Consequence
exact \(\Longrightarrow\) closed
check: applied-lab-d-squared-check.jsonreason: symmetry of partials meets antisymmetry of wedges
Explain d squared equals zero by pairing mixed partial derivatives with antisymmetric basis wedges. The analytic symmetry of second partials meets the algebraic sign flip of the wedge, so every term cancels its reversed partner. This immediately proves that every exact form is closed.
Closed versus exact

Closed Forms Can Remember A Puncture

Angular Form
\(\omega=\dfrac{-y\,dx+x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}\)
Local Derivative Test

\(d\omega=0\) away from the origin, so the form is closed on the punctured plane.

Global Obstruction

The recorded unit-circle circulation is \(6.2727\), within \(0.0105\) of \(2\pi\). A global potential would give zero.

check: exterior-derivative-circulation-check.jsonclosed need not mean exact on a punctured domain
Use the punctured plane as the first warning that local derivative tests are not the whole story. The angular form is closed away from the origin, yet its circulation around the missing point is approximately 2 pi. A global potential would force that integral to vanish.
