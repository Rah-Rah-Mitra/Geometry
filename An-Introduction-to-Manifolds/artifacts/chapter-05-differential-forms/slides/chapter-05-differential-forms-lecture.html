Differential Forms As Measurements

Forms measure tangent directions, pull back naturally, multiply with signs, differentiate intrinsically, and interact with flows.

Local

Every computation begins in \(T_p^*M\) and a coordinate basis \(dx^i\).

Functorial

Forms pull back through maps even when vector fields do not push forward globally.

Intrinsic

The operator \(d\) is local in charts, but its laws force a global construction.

Differentials Of Functions

1-form from a scalar
Differential

For a smooth function \(f\), the covector \(df_p\) is defined by

$$df_p(X_p)=X_p f.$$

Coordinate Expression

$$df=\sum_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}\,dx^i.$$

The symbols \(dx^i\) are the dual covectors to coordinate vector fields.

A gradient requires a metric to identify vectors and covectors. The differential itself does not.

Key identity: \(df_p(X_p)=X_p f\) Source span: printed pages 190-235
Use the contour picture to make df tangible. A vector tangent to a level curve gives zero measurement, while a vector crossing contours gives the directional derivative. Stress that df is defined without a metric. Calling it a gradient already assumes extra structure that the chapter has not chosen.

Smooth One-Forms

Sections of the cotangent bundle
One-Form Field

A smooth 1-form assigns \(\omega_p\in T_p^*M\) to each \(p\in M\) smoothly.

Local Test

$$\omega=\sum_i a_i\,dx^i$$

Smoothness is equivalent to smooth coefficient functions \(a_i\) in every chart.

\(T^*M=\bigsqcup_{p\in M}T_p^*M\) packages all cotangent spaces into one bundle.

Local form: \(\omega=\sum_i a_i dx^i\) Cotangent bundle viewpoint
Move from a single cotangent space to a field of covectors. In a chart, smoothness is inspected through the coefficient functions a i, but the object is global because coordinate changes transform the basis and the coefficients together. This is the first place where local notation and invariant meaning have to be held at once.

Restriction Is Inclusion Pullback

Submanifold test
Restriction
$$\omega|_S=i^*\omega,\qquad i:S\hookrightarrow M.$$

Operational Meaning

  • Keep only tangent directions that lie in \(T_pS\).
  • Evaluate the ambient form after applying \(i_*\).
  • Normal components never enter the restricted form.

The same definition works for immersed submanifolds and for parametrized curves.

Formula: \(\omega|_S=i^*\omega\) Use this again for \(S^1\subset\mathbb R^2\)
Restriction should not be presented as a separate ad hoc operation. If a submanifold sits inside M by an inclusion i, then a form restricts by pulling back along i. This explains why only tangent directions to the submanifold are tested and why ambient normal information disappears automatically.

Differential K-Forms

Alternating slots
Differential k-form

\(\Omega^k(M)=\Gamma(\Lambda^kT^*M)\): smooth fields of alternating \(k\)-linear covectors.

Alternation

$$\omega_p(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(k)})=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\omega_p(v_1,\ldots,v_k).$$

Local Basis

$$\omega=\sum_I a_I\,dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}.$$
From 1-form measurements to oriented k-dimensional measurements \(dx^I=dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}\)
Generalize the measurement picture from one input to k inputs. Alternation means the form is sensitive to oriented k-dimensional content: swapping two directions reverses sign and repeating a direction kills the measurement. The local basis dx I is compact notation for ordered wedges of coordinate covectors.

Antisymmetry by accounting
Graded Commutativity
$$\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{k\ell}\beta\wedge\alpha,\qquad \alpha\in\Omega^k,\ \beta\in\Omega^\ell.$$

Repeated basis factors vanish because an alternating form changes sign when two equal slots are swapped.

Invariant Forms On Lie Groups

Transport from the identity
Left-Invariant One-Form

A covector \(\lambda\in T_e^*G\) defines

$$\omega_g(v_g)=\lambda\big((L_{g^{-1}})_*v_g\big).$$

Circle Group

For \(S^1\), rotations preserve the angular form. The value at any point is determined by the value at the identity.

The group law gives a built-in way to compare tangent and cotangent spaces across the manifold.

Invariant form principle on Lie groups Circle example: angular form is rotation invariant
Use the circle as the concrete Lie group before naming the general construction. A covector chosen at the identity can be transported by left translations to every group element, producing a left-invariant form. The point is that group multiplication supplies a canonical way to compare tangent spaces.

Exterior Derivative In A Chart

Differentiate coefficients
Local Formula
$$d\!\left(\sum_I a_I\,dx^I\right)=\sum_I da_I\wedge dx^I.$$

One-Form In The Plane

$$d(P\,dx+Q\,dy)=\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dx\wedge dy.$$

Think of \(d\) as differentiating the coefficient data and then enforcing alternation.

Locality Gives A Global Operator

Chart formulas glue
Characterization

The exterior derivative is the unique operator \(d:\Omega^k(M)\to\Omega^{k+1}(M)\) satisfying linearity, the function rule, the graded product rule, and \(d^2=0\).

Graded Product Rule

$$d(\omega\wedge\tau)=d\omega\wedge\tau+(-1)^k\omega\wedge d\tau.$$

Locality says the value of \(d\omega\) on an open set depends only on \(\omega\) on that open set.

Existence from chart formulas; uniqueness from the operator laws Source span: exterior derivative locality and global existence
This slide is about trust in the construction. The formula for d is written in coordinates, but it is local and compatible on overlaps, so the pieces glue. The uniqueness statement matters pedagogically: any operator with the listed behavior on functions and products must be the exterior derivative.

Pullback Commutes With \(d\)

Move computations to easier coordinates
Naturality
$$F^*(d\omega)=d(F^*\omega).$$

Two Companion Laws

$$F^*(dh)=d(h\circ F),\qquad F^*(\omega\wedge\tau)=F^*\omega\wedge F^*\tau.$$

Parametrizations become a computational privilege: pull the form to the parameter space, compute there, and interpret the result back on the manifold.

pullback check Circle period used \(h^*\omega=dt\)
Now connect the two main operations. Pullback respects functions, wedge products, and exterior derivative, so computations can move to whichever manifold has the easier coordinates. This is why parametrizations are so powerful: the circle period was computed by pulling the form back to the line.

Lie Derivative And Flow

Differentiate pullback along time
Lie derivative
$$\mathcal L_X T=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\phi_t^*T.$$

Basic Cases

$$\mathcal L_X f=Xf,\qquad \mathcal L_XY=[X,Y].$$

The bracket is the infinitesimal failure of the flows of \(X\) and \(Y\) to commute.

Flow definition: pull back first, differentiate at time zero Vector fields: \(\mathcal L_XY=[X,Y]\)
The Lie derivative measures infinitesimal change along a vector field by pulling tensors back along the flow and differentiating at time zero. For functions it recovers directional derivative. For vector fields it recovers the bracket, which measures the failure of the two infinitesimal motions to commute.
