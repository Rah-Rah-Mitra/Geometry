IX.1 Gene expression

Gene Expression As A Signal

Signal Model

\( f_g(t)=\) expression level of gene \(g\) at time \(t\)

A periodic candidate should have a dominant oscillation whose lifetime survives sampling noise and small perturbations.

Inspection Questions

  • Which extrema are robust?
  • Is the dominant peak paired with a deep trough?
  • Does a small perturbation change the ranking?
The gene-expression section is a friendly entry point because the data are already function-like. Make the conversion explicit anyway: a gene gives a sampled time course, interpolation gives a scalar function, and sublevel or superlevel sweeps turn extrema into birth-death information. The scientific question is periodicity, but the topological question is whether one oscillatory feature remains large after the minor wiggles have been cancelled.

A periodicity score should not flip under a perturbation smaller than the margin separating the dominant feature from the threshold.

IX.2 Protein docking

Protein Docking As Surface Matching

Surface-Matching View

Protein docking asks whether complementary surface patches can align without clashes. Computational topology enters by replacing the raw surface with event summaries from elevation functions.

Pipeline Translation

  • data: two triangulated molecular surfaces
  • function: elevation along candidate normal directions
  • filtration: sweep by height across each surface
  • decision: compare stable event patterns before scoring contact

A docking configuration is plausible when geometric complementarity and topological event compatibility point to the same interface.

Introduce docking as surface matching rather than as a chemistry lesson. The topological part is the construction of summaries that are less brittle than raw mesh coordinates. A candidate interface can be tested by looking at how elevation events line up across surfaces. The main warning is that the chosen direction matters, so the pipeline must record ambiguity instead of hiding it inside one score.

IX.2 Protein docking

Elevation From Normal Directions

Elevation Function

\(h_v(p)=\langle p,v\rangle\)

For a chosen direction \(v\), the surface is swept by height and critical events are recorded.

Ambiguity Check

When two candidate directions produce near-tied event orders, the docking score should not pretend the direction was unique.

The key teaching move is to make direction choice visible. Students know how to project points onto an axis, but a molecular surface has many plausible local normals. If two directions nearly agree, small geometric noise may swap the order of events. The artifact records that ambiguity, which is exactly the kind of modeling detail an application needs before a topological summary becomes trustworthy.

IX.2 Protein docking

Curve And Surface Elevation Events

Event Types

  • local minimum: component appears
  • saddle: components join or a tunnel changes
  • local maximum: component or cavity closes
Modeling Warning

Event compatibility is evidence, not a docking proof. It must be combined with collision checks and domain-specific constraints.

Use this as the docking analogue of the gene-expression persistence ledger. The events now come from a surface sweep, so they carry more geometry than a one-dimensional time course. Still, the habit is the same: do not summarize a surface by a vague shape word when a sequence of critical events can be recorded and compared across possible alignments.

IX.3 Image segmentation

Image Segmentation As A Landscape

Landscape Model

An image becomes a height function: dark or bright regions act like basins, ridges, and saddles. Watershed segmentation traces which basin each pixel would flow into.

Why Persistence Helps

  • raw watershed often creates too many basins
  • tiny basins usually correspond to shallow minima
  • persistence pairs minima with merge saddles
  • low-lifetime basins can be merged in a recorded order

Segmentation is not only boundary detection; it is a controlled simplification of a topographic graph.

Students may already know watershed segmentation from image processing, so frame the topological contribution carefully. The raw watershed transform is sensitive: every small depression can become a region. Persistence supplies an ordered cleanup rule. The useful distinction is between a basin that exists geometrically and a basin whose lifetime is large enough to survive the intended scale of analysis.

IX.3 Image segmentation

Watershed Graph Before Cleanup

Before Cleanup

  • four local minima create four candidate regions
  • two basins have shallow merge saddles
  • the graph is a bookkeeping device for cleanup
Merge Order

Merge low-persistence regions first, then stop at the threshold. This prevents a later strong feature from being swallowed by an earlier local choice.

Pause on the phrase before cleanup. A raw watershed graph is not a failure; it is an intentionally detailed starting point. The problem is that not every basin should become a final segment. By turning minima and saddle merges into a ledger, we can explain exactly why one basin disappears before another and where the threshold stops the process.

IX.3 Image segmentation

3D Critical Cells And Simplification

0-Cells

Minima create components or candidate basins in a voxel landscape.

1- and 2-Cells

Saddles connect components, create loops, or fill weak tunnels depending on dimension.

3-Cells

Maximal cells cap small cavities or record large volumetric events.

Discrete-Morse Simplification

Persistence-guided cancellation pairs a \(k\)-cell with a \((k+1)\)-cell when the associated feature is below tolerance. The simplified complex should preserve the high-persistence segmentation structure.

Extend the image discussion from two-dimensional basins to volumetric data. The vocabulary of critical cells helps students avoid thinking only in contours. In three dimensions, simplification can remove weak tunnels and small cavities as well as shallow components. The exact same audit principle applies: pair the cells, record the dimension, and preserve features above the tolerance.

IX.4 Root architectures

Space Carving And Distance Filtrations

Distance Filtration

\(X_r=\{x: d(x,S)\le r\}\)

The skeleton \(S\) is thickened by radius \(r\). Components merge as branches connect; loops or cavities may appear when imaging artifacts create cycles.

Betti Summary

In the toy ledger, beta0 drops from 7 to 1 as radius grows, while beta1 appears late and remains sparse.

This slide connects root analysis to the familiar offset-filtration idea. The skeleton is not only a drawing; it is a set whose distance function can be filtered. As the radius grows, nearby samples join into a coherent architecture. Betti summaries then become sanity checks for the reconstruction, especially when a loop appears that may indicate either biology or an imaging artifact.

Chapter synthesis

Application Design Checklist

1. Name the data model and the units of measurement.

2. Choose the function and state its perturbation tolerance.

3. Record the filtration order and all persistence pairings.

4. Separate simplification thresholds from scientific decisions.

5. Keep a failure case for each modeling hypothesis.

Reusable Across The Chapter

Genes rank oscillations, docking compares elevation events, images merge basins, and roots classify local architecture. The visible object changes; the audit trail stays the same.

Closing Principle

Applied computational topology is strongest when invariants are treated as measurements with provenance, stability checks, and cancellation ledgers.

Close by asking students to reuse the checklist rather than memorize the four case studies separately. Each application succeeds when the topological object is tied to a measured function, a filtration, and a decision threshold. The final message is practical: persistent homology is not only an invariant machine, but also a disciplined way to keep scientific simplification auditable.
