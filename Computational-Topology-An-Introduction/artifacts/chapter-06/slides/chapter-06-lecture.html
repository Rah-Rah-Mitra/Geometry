Let \(M_a=f^{-1}(-\infty,a]\). As \(a\) rises, the topology is unchanged except at the heights of \(u,v,w,z\).

The torus example should feel like the whole chapter in miniature. Below the minimum there is nothing, then a disk appears, then two saddles add one-cells, and the maximum caps the surface. Read the four critical points as the vector c equals (1,2,1), matching the Betti vector beta equals (1,2,1).

Critical Point Classifier

Critical Point

A point is critical when \(Df_x=0\), equivalently every first partial derivative vanishes in local coordinates.

Nondegenerate

The Hessian matrix \(H(x)\) is nonsingular. Its negative eigenvalues define the index.

2D typedet \(H\)indexcell
minimumpositive0birth component
saddlenegative1add tunnel or merge
maximumpositive2cap a void
Slow down at the Hessian. The first derivative finds candidates, but the second derivative decides whether the candidate is Morse and what index it has. In dimension two, determinant positive with positive trace gives a minimum, determinant negative gives a saddle, and determinant positive with negative trace gives a maximum.

Morse Lemma Normal Forms

Morse Lemma

Near a nondegenerate critical point \(u\), there are local coordinates with \(u=0\) and

\(f(x)=f(u)-x_1^2-\cdots-x_q^2+x_{q+1}^2+\cdots+x_d^2.\)
\(q=0\)

local minimum; a new component can appear.

\(q=1\)

a one-dimensional handle is attached.

\(q=d-1\)

a complementary saddle splits or merges level contours.

\(q=d\)

local maximum; the final cap is attached.

The Morse lemma is the local straightening theorem for this chapter. After a coordinate change, a nondegenerate critical point has no hidden higher-order behavior nearby. The index is simply the number of negative square terms, so crossing the critical value adds exactly the cell dimension predicted by the local model.

Failure Modes

Morse theory is powerful because it rules out just enough nongeneric behavior.

Flat Critical Point

\(f(x)=x^3\) has \(Df_0=0\), but the Hessian vanishes. There is no quadratic classifier.

Multi-Saddle

\(x^3-3xy^2\) has more than two alternating sectors. One index cannot encode the local event.

Critical Ties

If two critical points share a value, a sweep sees simultaneous topology changes.

Critical Families

A height direction can create a circle of minima or maxima instead of isolated points.

This warning slide separates genericity from wishful drawing. A cubic flat point has a vanishing Hessian, the multi-saddle has too many sectors meeting at one point, equal critical values make two events happen at once, and a sideways torus can have critical circles instead of isolated points. These are precisely the cases the clean theory refuses.

Cell Attachment

Regular Interval

If \(f^{-1}[a,b]\) contains no critical point and is compact, then \(M_a\) and \(M_b\) are diffeomorphic.

One Critical Point

If \(f^{-1}[a,b]\) contains exactly one critical point of index \(q\), then \(M_b\) has the homotopy type of \(M_a\) with a \(q\)-cell attached.

\(M_b \simeq M_a \cup_g B^q,\quad g:S^{q-1}\to \partial M_a\)
The attachment theorem is the topological payload of the Morse lemma. Between regular values nothing changes up to diffeomorphism; across one index q critical point, the sublevel set gains a q-cell up to homotopy. The point is not the literal shape of the neighborhood but the dimension of the handle it contributes.

Gradient Flow

Integral Lines

An integral line solves \(\dot\gamma(t)=\nabla f(\gamma(t))\). Along it, the function increases.

Endpoints

On a compact manifold, a regular integral line has an origin and destination critical point.

\(org(\gamma)=\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma(t),\quad dest(\gamma)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)\)

Stable / Unstable

\(S(u)\) collects points flowing to \(u\). \(U(u)\) collects points flowing out of \(u\).

Gradient flow gives a dynamic way to organize the same sweep. Integral lines climb the function, so on a compact manifold they limit from one critical point to another. Stable and unstable manifolds group together all points with the same future or past endpoint, setting up the cell decomposition used by Morse-Smale theory.

Morse-Smale Condition

Definition

A Morse function is Morse-Smale when stable and unstable manifolds intersect transversally.

\(D\sigma_x(T_x\mathbb{R}^q)+D\nu_y(T_y\mathbb{R}^p)=T_zM\)

Dimension Count

If \(S\) has dimension \(q\) and \(U\) has dimension \(p\) inside a \(d\)-manifold, a transverse intersection has dimension \(q+p-d\).

Why It Matters

Cell boundaries become unions of lower-dimensional stable cells, so the flow decomposition behaves like a complex.

The extra Morse-Smale hypothesis is about intersections behaving like transverse crossings rather than accidental shared paths. If stable and unstable manifolds meet transversally, cell boundaries are made from lower-dimensional cells. Without it, a saddle-to-saddle trajectory can make a stable manifold boundary miss the complex structure we want.

Morse Inequalities

Weak Inequalities

\(c_q \ge \beta_q(M)\quad 0\le q\le d\)

Critical points of each index must be numerous enough to support homology in that dimension.

Strong Inequalities

\( \sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-q}c_q \ge \sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-q}\beta_q(M)\)

The final case \(j=d\) is equality, giving the Euler characteristic.

Index \(q\)Torus \(c_q\)\(\beta_q\)Check
011component birth
122two independent one-cycles
211final cap

Euler Ledger

\(\sum_q (-1)^q c_q = 1-2+1=0\)
\(\sum_q (-1)^q \beta_q = 1-2+1=0\)
Present the inequalities as an audit of any proposed critical-point inventory. The weak inequalities say each index count must cover the corresponding Betti number, while the strong inequalities include the alternating partial sums. For the torus height function, the counts and Betti numbers match exactly, and both Euler computations give zero.

Morse-Smale-Witten Complex

Chain Groups

\(C_q\) is generated by index \(q\) critical points, commonly over \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) in this chapter.

torus: \(C_0\simeq G,\ C_1\simeq G^2,\ C_2\simeq G\)

Boundary Rule

The boundary of an index \(q\) critical point counts flowline edges to index \(q-1\) critical points.

Algebra Check

Counting modulo two makes paired connections cancel in the tilted torus example.

\(\partial^2=0,\quad H_q=\ker\partial_q/\operatorname{im}\partial_{q+1}\)
The chain complex replaces cells by critical points and replaces geometric boundary by counting connecting flowlines, here over Z2. The tilted torus has one generator in dimension zero, two in dimension one, and one in dimension two. In the symmetric count shown here, each relevant connection appears twice, so the boundary maps vanish mod two.

PL Lower-Star Filtration

Generic PL Function

Vertex values are distinct, so \(f(u_1)<f(u_2)<\cdots<f(u_n)\).

Lower Star

\(\operatorname{St}^{-}u_i\) consists of cofaces for which \(u_i\) is the vertex of maximum function value.

Filtration

The nested complexes are built by adding closed lower stars.

\(K_0\subset K_1\subset\cdots\subset K_n=K\)
This is the algorithmic bridge. A generic PL function orders vertices, and every simplex enters when its highest vertex enters. The closed lower star is the batch of simplices added at that vertex, glued along the lower link. The nested complexes K0 through Kn model the homotopy type of the continuous sublevel sets.

PL Critical Vertices

Lower Link

\(\operatorname{Lk}^{-}u_i\) is the part of the link already below \(u_i\). The closed lower star is glued to \(K_{i-1}\) along this lower link.

Vertex typeLower linkReduced Betti signal
regularnonempty, trivialall zero
minimumempty\(\tilde\beta_{-1}=1\)
saddletwo components\(\tilde\beta_0=1\)
maximumcircle\(\tilde\beta_1=1\)
The lower link classifier is the PL replacement for the Hessian. Empty lower link means a new component, two lower-link components mean a one-cell saddle, and a full lower-link circle means a two-cell cap on a surface. Regular vertices have homologically trivial lower links, so they do not change homology.

Unfolding + Mayer-Vietoris

PL Multi-Saddle

A \(k\)-fold saddle has \(\tilde\beta_0=k\) in the lower link. On a 2-manifold it can be unfolded into \(k\) simple saddles by adding \(k-1\) nearby vertices.

Mayer-Vietoris Step

For \(K_i=K_{i-1}\cup \overline{\operatorname{St}^{-}u_i}\), the intersection is \(A=\operatorname{Lk}^{-}u_i\).

\( \widetilde H_{p+1}(K_i)\to \widetilde H_p(A)\to \widetilde H_p(K_{i-1})\oplus \widetilde H_p(\overline{\operatorname{St}^{-}u_i})\to \widetilde H_p(K_i)\)

Inequality Mechanism

A lower-link class either creates a \(p\)-cycle in \(K_i\) or kills a \((p-1)\)-cycle already present. The strong Morse inequalities survive both cases.

A PL multi-saddle shows why PL Morse functions are not simply guaranteed by tiny perturbation. In two dimensions it can be unfolded locally into simple saddles by adding nearby vertices. The Mayer-Vietoris proof then tracks whether the new lower-link cycle creates a class in the current dimension or kills one in the previous dimension.

Reeb Graph Definition

Contour Equivalence

For \(f:X\to\mathbb{R}\), points \(x,y\) are equivalent when they lie in the same connected component of a level set.

Quotient

The Reeb graph \(R(f)\) is the space of contours with quotient topology, and \(\pi:R(f)\to\mathbb{R}\) satisfies \(f=\pi\circ\psi\).

Topology Retained

\(\beta_0(R(f))=\beta_0(X)\), and \(\beta_1(R(f))\le \beta_1(X)\).

The Reeb graph changes the viewpoint from sublevel sets to level-set components. Collapse each contour to a point, keep the induced height map, and the result is a one-dimensional summary of how contours are born, merge, split, and die. It preserves connected components and cannot have more independent graph loops than the original space has one-dimensional homology.

Reeb Graph Algorithm + Loop Lemma

Sweep Algorithm For PL 2-Manifolds

Sort vertices by value. Between vertex levels, represent each contour by a cyclic triangle list. At a vertex, cut, drop lower-star triangles, append upper-star triangles, and glue list ends.

Loop Lemma

For a connected orientable 2-manifold of genus \(g\), every Morse Reeb graph has exactly \(g\) independent loops.

torus: \(g=1\Rightarrow \#\operatorname{loops}=1\)
Close by tying the smooth and PL stories together. The Reeb graph algorithm sweeps vertices, updates cyclic contour lists, and records nodes for minima, saddles, regular reconnects, and maxima. For connected orientable surfaces, the loop lemma says the cycle rank of a Morse Reeb graph is the genus, so the torus case correctly reports one loop.
