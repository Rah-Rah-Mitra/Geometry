Simplex anatomy

A Simplex Is A Finite Vertex Set Plus All Its Faces

Definition
A \(k\)-simplex \(\sigma=[v_0,\ldots,v_k]\) is determined by \(k+1\) affinely independent vertices. Every nonempty subset of vertices is a face.
number of nonempty faces: \(2^{k+1}-1\)
Boundary
\(\partial[v_0v_1v_2]=[v_1v_2]-[v_0v_2]+[v_0v_1]\)
triangle faces = 7 boundary uses 3 edges
computational object: finite subsetsorientation appears when chains enter the story
Slow down on the combinatorics of one simplex. A k-simplex has k plus one vertices, every nonempty subset is a face, and therefore the full simplex contains two to the k plus one minus one nonempty faces. The triangle example lets students see vertices, edges, the two-dimensional face, and the boundary chain before the notation gets heavier.
What makes a complex

Closure And Clean Intersections Are The Two Gatekeepers

Valid Complex

  • every face of every simplex is present
  • intersections are empty or shared faces

Invalid Data Or Geometry

  • not face-closed if a boundary edge is absent
  • not geometric if interiors cross at a non-face
face-closure: all tau subset sigma intersection test: sigma cap tau is a face
abstract condition plus geometric conditionfirst data-quality test for complexes
This slide is the first structural test. A simplicial complex is not just a set of triangles; it must be closed under faces, and two geometric simplices may intersect only in a shared face. The invalid panel is useful because it shows the two common failures separately: a missing edge in the abstract data and a geometric crossing that is not an allowed face.

Present local views as query operators on a complex. The star asks which simplices contain a vertex, the closed star adds every face of those simplices, the link removes the vertex while keeping the surrounding directions, and the skeleton truncates by dimension. These definitions become practical when algorithms need local neighborhoods or dimension-limited graphs.
Abstract to geometric

Vertex Sets Become Convex Hulls In A Realization

Abstract Complex
A family \(K\) of finite vertex sets is a complex when \(\tau \subseteq \sigma \in K\) implies \(\tau \in K\).
Realization Idea
If \(\dim K=d\), place vertices in general position in \(\mathbb{R}^{2d+1}\). Convex hulls of disjoint abstract simplices avoid accidental intersection.
barycentric weights >= 0 sum lambda_i = 1
abstract inclusion controls geometric incidencegeneral position prevents unwanted intersections
Connect abstract data to coordinates without pretending coordinates are always given. The abstract complex only knows vertex sets and inclusion; a geometric realization assigns vertices to points and fills each simplex by convex hulls. The proof idea for realization in R to the two d plus one is a general position argument: choose vertex positions so disjoint abstract simplices do not accidentally intersect.
Simplicial maps

A Vertex Map Extends Linearly By Barycentric Hats

Simplicial Map
A vertex map \(f:K^0\to L^0\) is simplicial if images of vertices of every \(\sigma\in K\) span a simplex in \(L\).
Hat Functions
Barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_i(x)\) are local basis functions: one at vertex \(v_i\), zero at the other vertices, linear on each simplex.
lambda_i >= 0 inside sum lambda_i = 1 faces map to faces
vertex rule plus linear extensionbarycentric coordinates are computable weights
Simplicial maps are computable because a vertex map determines everything after linear extension. Barycentric hat functions are the coordinate system: they are nonnegative, sum to one inside the simplex, and tell us how to blend the images of vertices. The check to surface is that every point maps by the same convex-weight recipe, so face relations are respected.
Subdivision and mesh

Barycentric Subdivision Keeps Shape While Shrinking Mesh

Mesh
\(\mu(K)\) is the maximum diameter of a simplex in the geometric complex.
\(\mu(\operatorname{sd} K)\le \frac{d}{d+1}\mu(K)\)
Proof Move
Each new vertex is a barycenter of an old face. The farthest pair in a new simplex lies between barycenters of nested faces, which bounds the diameter by the stated factor.
d=2 shrink <= 2/3 same polyhedron |K|
subdivision changes combinatorics, not the realized spacemesh control prepares approximation
Subdivision should feel both topological and numerical. Barycentric subdivision replaces each simplex by smaller simplices indexed by chains of faces, so the shape is unchanged while the mesh gets finer. In dimension d, the standard mesh estimate contracts by d over d plus one, and repeated subdivision gives the small simplices needed for approximation arguments.
Simplicial approximation

Continuous Maps Become Vertex Rules After Enough Subdivision

Simplicial Approximation
After sufficiently fine subdivision, a continuous map can be approximated by a simplicial map satisfying a star condition.

Star Condition

If \(f(\operatorname{st} v)\) lands inside \(\operatorname{st} w\), send \(v\) to \(w\). Linear extension then gives the approximation.

mesh small enough nearby images share target star
continuous control becomes combinatorial datasubdivision is the resolution dial
Frame simplicial approximation as replacing a continuous map with a combinatorial one after enough subdivision. The curve in the left panel is continuous and not yet a vertex rule; the right panel follows vertices and edges of a subdivided complex. The star condition is the bridge: nearby points in the domain must land inside a compatible star in the target.
Convex set systems

Helly Turns Many Convex Intersections Into Small Tests

Helly Theorem
For a finite family of convex sets in \(\mathbb{R}^d\), if every \(d+1\) of them intersect, then the whole family intersects.

Why It Matters

Cech membership asks whether many balls have a common point. Helly reduces some convex intersection checks to small certificates.

R^2 needs triples convexity is essential
finite certificate for convex intersectionused implicitly by proximity complexes
Helly theorem is included because convexity turns many intersection questions into finite tests. In R squared, it is enough to check triples of convex sets. The proof skeleton is a dependency diagram: pick a minimal witness, use a small simplex or separating line argument, and conclude that a missing common point must already be visible in a triple.
Nerves preserve shape

A Good Cover Lets The Nerve Carry The Union's Homotopy Type

Nerve Principle
If a cover has contractible nonempty finite intersections, the nerve of the cover has the same homotopy type as the union.

Construction

One vertex for each set. A simplex appears whenever the corresponding sets have common intersection.

convex intersections contractible nerve simplex iff intersection nonempty
cover geometry converted to a simplicial complexgood-cover hypothesis is not optional
This is the nerve theorem intuition. For a good convex cover, the union and the nerve have the same homotopy type, so a shape made of overlapping sets can be represented by a combinatorial complex. The warning matters: if intersections are not contractible or the cover is not good, the nerve can record the wrong loop or fill a hole incorrectly.
Cech versus Rips

Common Intersection And Pairwise Proximity Are Different Tests

Cech Complex

\([p_0,\ldots,p_k]\in C_r\) when \(\bigcap_i B(p_i,r)\ne\emptyset\).

Rips Complex

\([p_0,\ldots,p_k]\in R_r\) when \(d(p_i,p_j)\le r\) for all pairs.

Cech_r subset Rips_2r Rips_r subset Cech_alpha(r) in Euclidean space pairwise close does not force common center
Cech is geometric but harderRips is cheap but can add simplices early
Compare Cech and Rips as two radius-based complexes built from the same point cloud. Cech membership requires a common ball intersection, while Rips only checks pairwise distances. Inclusions are the practical memory hook: Cech at radius r sits inside Rips at radius two r, and Rips at radius r sits inside Cech at a scaled larger radius in Euclidean space.
Miniball test

Cech Membership Reduces To A Smallest-Enclosing-Ball Decision

Decision
A finite set of points spans a Cech simplex at radius \(r\) exactly when its smallest enclosing ball has radius at most \(r\).

Boundary Size

In \(\mathbb{R}^d\), at most \(d+1\) support points determine the smallest enclosing ball in the generic case.

miniball radius <= r support points <= d+1
algorithmic route for Cech membershiplightweight predicate; no external topology library required
The miniball test turns Cech membership into a finite decision. For a candidate simplex, compute the smallest enclosing ball of its vertices and compare the radius to the scale parameter. The recursive diagram is deliberately algorithmic: remove a point, update the boundary set, and stop when the boundary determines the ball or the radius test fails.
Voronoi to Delaunay

Delaunay Simplices Are Dual To Shared Voronoi Faces

Voronoi Cell
\(V_p=\{x: d(x,p)\le d(x,q)\ \text{for every site }q\}\).
Delaunay Dual
A simplex appears when the corresponding Voronoi cells have nonempty common intersection.
empty circumcircle: no interior sites dual edge iff cells share a face
proximity diagram becomes a triangulationDelaunay sparsifies later radius complexes
Voronoi and Delaunay are dual descriptions of proximity. Voronoi cells are regions of nearest sites; a Delaunay edge or triangle appears when the corresponding cells meet. The empty circumcircle check is the computational certificate for triangles: the circumcircle through the vertices must contain no other sample point in its interior.
Alpha complexes

Alpha Keeps The Cech Shape Inside The Delaunay Skeleton

Cech

all balls; nerve of full disks can be large.

Delaunay

static triangulation; ignores radius unless filtered.

Alpha

balls clipped by Voronoi cells; radius-aware and sparse.

Alpha_r subset Delaunay Alpha_r subset Cech_r monotone as r increases
nerve of clipped ballssame geometric scale with fewer candidate simplices
Alpha complexes combine the radius parameter with Delaunay sparsity. Instead of taking all Cech simplices, intersect each ball with its Voronoi cell and keep the nerve of those clipped cells. This makes the alpha complex a subcomplex of the Delaunay triangulation while preserving the Cech-like shape information at the chosen radius.
Filtration events and collapse

Radius Time Separates Regular Collapses From Critical Events

Filtration
\(K_{r_0}\subseteq K_{r_1}\subseteq K_{r_2}\subseteq\cdots\). Simplices enter as the scale parameter grows.

Regular Collapse

Removing a simplex with a free face preserves homotopy type, so Betti numbers stay fixed.

Critical Event

A bridge or filling simplex changes connected components or loops.

monotone: K_i subset K_j before: beta0=2, beta1=1 after critical: feature changes
filtration is radius as timecollapse distinguishes redundant from topologically active cells
Close by making filtration time explicit. As radius grows, simplices enter monotonically; regular collapse events remove parts without changing homotopy type, while critical events create or destroy features. The small Betti summary is a classroom diagnostic: before the critical bridge there are two components and one loop, after the bridge the component count changes and the loop can be filled.
