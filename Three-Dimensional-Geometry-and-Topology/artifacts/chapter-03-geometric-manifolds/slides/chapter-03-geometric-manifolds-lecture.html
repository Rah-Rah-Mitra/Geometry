Section 1
model + pseudogroup

(G,X)-Structures and the Pseudogroup Viewpoint

Data

Model space \(X\), group \(G \subset \mathrm{Homeo}(X)\) (or smooth, analytic). An atlas of \(X\)-charts whose transitions lie in \(G\).

Pseudogroup

The collection \(\mathcal{G}\) of all local restrictions of elements of \(G\), closed under restriction, inverse, and composition.

Examples

Euclidean (\(G=\mathrm{Isom}(E^3)\)), hyperbolic (\(G=\mathrm{Isom}(H^3)\)), spherical (\(G=\mathrm{O}(4)\)), affine, projective, conformal.

What changes vs. plain manifold?

Charts now take values in a fixed model and transitions are constrained — you carry geometry, not just topology, between charts.

What it buys

Holonomy, developing maps, and rigid invariants — the whole machinery in this chapter.

A geometric manifold = manifold + constrained transitionsPseudogroup is the bookkeeping of those constraints
Define a (G,X)-structure. Choose a model space X and a group G acting on it. A (G,X)-manifold is a manifold with an atlas of X-charts whose transitions lie in G. The pseudogroup viewpoint axiomatizes this: charts plus restrictions plus composition produce transition maps with values in G.
Section 2
vertex link test

Triangulations and Vertex Links

Manifold criterion

For a 3-dimensional simplicial complex, the link of every vertex must be a 2-sphere (or disk on the boundary).

Failure modes

If a vertex link is a higher-genus surface or a non-spherical 2-complex, the gluing is not a manifold; it may be an orbifold or worse.

Notebook test

Single tetrahedron: the link of any vertex is a triangle (a 2-disk for the boundary case).

Local link test certifies manifoldnessWithout it, the gluing can hide singularities
Triangulations are useful only when local link tests certify that the glued space is actually a manifold. For a three-dimensional triangulation, every vertex link must be a two-sphere; otherwise the gluing has an orbifold point or a non-manifold singularity. The notebook test checks the smallest case: a vertex in a single tetrahedron has triangular link.
Section 3
unrolling along paths

Developing Maps and Holonomy

Developing map

\(\mathrm{dev}: \widetilde M \to X\)

extends local charts along paths in the universal cover.

Holonomy

\(\mathrm{hol}: \pi_1(M) \to G\)

records the mismatch after closing a loop.

Completeness

\(M\) is complete as a (G,X)-manifold when \(\mathrm{dev}\) is a covering of \(X\); then \(M = X / \mathrm{hol}(\pi_1)\).

Holonomy = monodromy of (G,X)-chartsCompleteness is a property of dev, not of M
A developing map unrolls local charts along paths in the universal cover and records the mismatch after closing a loop as the holonomy. Completeness becomes geometric: the developing map should cover the whole model space, and the holonomy should act properly. The visual unrolls four chart patches and records the developed image.
Section 4
proper discontinuity

Discrete Actions and Fundamental Domains

Proper discontinuity

A group \(\Gamma\) acts properly discontinuously on \(X\) if, for every compact \(K \subset X\), only finitely many \(\gamma \in \Gamma\) satisfy \(\gamma K \cap K \ne \emptyset\).

Free action

No \(\gamma \ne e\) has a fixed point. With proper discontinuity, the quotient \(X/\Gamma\) is a manifold.

Notebook sampler

Square fundamental domain in the plane translated by a lattice; overlap count is the finite proxy.

Quotient = X / Gamma when action is properly discontinuous and freeSample finite overlap to detect failure
Quotient manifolds behave well only when the group action separates nearby translates correctly. The sampler places a square fundamental domain in the plane and translates by a small lattice. The overlap count is a finite proxy for proper discontinuity: only finitely many translates can meet a compact test region.

Bundles, Connections, and Contact Structures

Bundle

Fiber-by-fiber model added to the base manifold. Local trivializations + transition cocycle in \(G_{\mathrm{fiber}}\).

Connection

Horizontal distribution complementary to the fibers; transports vectors along base paths.

Standard contact form

\(\alpha = dz - y\, dx\)

\(\alpha \wedge d\alpha\) is nonzero — maximally nonintegrable.

Bundles + connections: extra (G,X)-data on top of the baseContact is the canonical odd-dimensional twin of symplectic
Bundles add a fiber to every base point; connections specify horizontal motion. A standard contact one-form alpha equals dz minus y dx illustrates the contact structure: the plane field defined by the kernel of alpha is maximally nonintegrable. The chapter introduces these as refinements of the basic (G,X) story.

Section 7
PL = smooth in dim 3

PL, Smooth, and Topological Categories

Dimension ≤ 3

Topological = PL = smooth: every topological manifold has a unique (up to isotopy) PL/smooth structure.

Dimension ≥ 4

The categories diverge: exotic smooth structures exist on \(\mathbb{R}^4\), Donaldson-style invariants distinguish them.

Practical reading

For three-manifolds, freely move between triangulations and smooth presentations without worrying about the structure type.

Consequence for this course

Geometric structures, triangulations, and smooth structures can be used interchangeably in the three-manifold setting.

The chapter takes advantage of low-dimensional coincidenceAbove dim 3, life is harder
Comment on PL and smooth structures. In dimensions one through three, PL and smooth categories agree, so triangulations and smooth structures are interchangeable. In higher dimensions this fails. The chapter records this fact so that three-manifold topology can move freely between triangulated and smooth pictures.
Applied lab
three filters

Lab: Holonomy versus Completeness

Translation

Acts freely on \(\mathbb{R}^2\); discrete cyclic subgroup gives a cylinder.

Rotation

Has a fixed point. Quotient is an orbifold, not a manifold.

Hyperbolic scaling

Acts freely but contracts toward the origin; need to remove a fixed locus or restrict the domain to recover a quotient manifold.

Filters

Discreteness, proper discontinuity, freeness are three independent checks. Pass all three and the quotient is a manifold; fail one and something specific is wrong.

Same picture, three failuresEach filter has a separate diagnostic
Walk through the applied lab. Compare three toy holonomy generators on the Euclidean plane: a translation acts freely, a rotation has a fixed point, and a hyperbolic scaling expands and contracts. The lab illustrates why completeness, proper discontinuity, and discreteness are independent filters on the quotient construction.
