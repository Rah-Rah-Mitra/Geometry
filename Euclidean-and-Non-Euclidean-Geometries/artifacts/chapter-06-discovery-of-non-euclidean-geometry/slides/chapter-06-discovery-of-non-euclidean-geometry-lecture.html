Model dictionary

The Poincare disk translates the new geometry into inspectable objects

Dictionary entries

  • Points are interior disk points.
  • Lines are diameters or arcs orthogonal to the boundary.
  • The boundary records ideal endpoints, not ordinary points.
  • Angles are read as Euclidean angles between curves.
Notebook translation: geodesics become orthogonal arcsSTIX and KaTeX support declared in HTML
Explain the disk as a translation dictionary, not as the geometry itself. The disk lives in a Euclidean page, but its interior points, geodesics, and angle readings are interpreting hyperbolic statements. The boundary is especially important: it helps name limiting behavior even though it is not part of the hyperbolic plane.
Constructing geodesics

Orthogonality to the boundary replaces ordinary straightness

Working rule

A disk geodesic is a diameter or a boundary-orthogonal circle arc.

The Euclidean curve can bend on the page while still representing the shortest hyperbolic route.

Pause on the word straight. In the disk model, a geodesic may appear curved because the Euclidean page is only the model surface. What matters is orthogonality to the boundary circle or being a diameter. Students should learn to inspect the boundary contact rather than judging straightness by ordinary page geometry.
Limiting parallels

Sharing an ideal endpoint is the borderline case

Classification through P

  • Intersecting geodesics meet the given line inside the disk.
  • Limiting parallels share one ideal endpoint with the line.
  • Ultraparallels avoid the line and share no ideal endpoint.
Notebook translation: limiting parallels become boundary endpointsParallelism becomes boundary behavior
This slide gives the most important new visual habit. A hyperbolic line has two ideal endpoints, and geodesics through the point can be sorted by how their endpoints relate to those of the given line. Limiting parallels are not merely any disjoint lines; they are the boundary cases that share one ideal endpoint.
Angle of parallelism

Distance to the line controls the limiting angle

Qualitative law

The farther P is from ell, the narrower the limiting cone becomes.

tan(Pi(d) / 2) = e-d

The formula is a useful curvature -1 model witness; the synthetic point is monotonic dependence.

Limiting parallels are measurable, not just pictorialCurvature normalized to -1
Treat the displayed formula as a model witness, not as a replacement for the synthetic development. The main teaching point is monotonic: as a point moves farther from the line, the cone of intersecting geodesics narrows and the limiting angle decreases. This makes the parallel phenomenon measurable rather than merely strange.
Triangle defect

Angle sum below pi becomes a positive area signal

Defect-area rule

area = pi - (A + B + C)

For curvature -1, defect is not a measurement error. It is the hyperbolic area of the triangle.

defectarea witness
Notebook lab: hyperbolic angle defect areaAngle sum is a geometric diagnostic
Connect this directly to the neutral geometry chapters: earlier results allowed angle sums at most pi, while hyperbolic geometry makes the strict case systematic. In curvature -1 normalization, the defect is literally area. That identity lets students treat angle sum as a robust diagnostic of the underlying geometry.
Similarity rigidity

Same angles force the same size

Euclidean habit

A triangle can be scaled while keeping all three angles fixed.

AAA means similarity, not congruence.

Hyperbolic correction

In the hyperbolic plane, fixed angles fix the defect and therefore constrain the side lengths.

AAA becomes congruence.

Notebook route: see why similar triangles become congruentNo Euclidean scaling symmetry remains
This is often the moment where students feel the geometry change. In Euclidean geometry, angle data leaves a scale parameter free. Hyperbolic defect removes that freedom because changing size changes area and hence angle sum. Present AAA congruence as a global consequence of the new metric, not as an isolated curiosity.
Radial distance

The disk is finite on the page and infinite in its metric

Model witness

d(0,r) = 2 atanh(r)

As r approaches 1, the displayed disk edge recedes to infinite hyperbolic distance.

Notebook sanity: origin-to-half is farther than origin-to-quarterFinite picture, infinite geometry
Use the radial formula to prevent a common mistake: the disk boundary is close in Euclidean drawing distance but infinitely far in the hyperbolic metric. The same page distance does not mean the same hyperbolic distance. This also explains why ideal endpoints can organize lines without being ordinary points of the plane.

Course habit: separate syntax from semanticsModels support proof discipline
Make this a metacognitive slide. In non-Euclidean geometry, the risk is not that diagrams are useless; the risk is confusing what a diagram suggests with what the axioms prove. The model supplies a disciplined interpretation, and proof identifies the assumptions actually used. That separation is the chapter's lasting habit.
Reading the disk safely

Common misreadings and their corrections

Misreading 1

The boundary circle is a wall of the plane.

Correction: it records ideal endpoints outside the point set.

Misreading 2

Curved arcs are less straight than diameters.

Correction: both are geodesics in the hyperbolic metric.

Misreading 3

All disjoint geodesics are the same kind of parallel.

Correction: limiting and ultraparallel cases differ by ideal endpoints.

Ask of every picture: point set, line set, metric, and axiom claim.

Use this slide as a quick diagnostic checkpoint before exercises. Students often import Euclidean habits into the disk: treating the boundary as reachable, treating bent arcs as less straight, or treating every disjoint pair as equivalent. The correction is to ask which objects belong to the model and which relations are metric or ideal-boundary facts.

Board exercise

Turn a visual claim into a proof question

Exercise

In the disk model, choose a geodesic ell and a point P not on ell. Identify one geodesic through P that intersects ell, one limiting parallel, and one ultraparallel.

For each choice, state the model feature that justifies the classification.

Expected reasoning

  • Intersection is checked inside the disk.
  • Limiting behavior is checked by a shared ideal endpoint.
  • Ultraparallel behavior has neither an interior intersection nor a shared endpoint.

The answer is not a drawing style; it is a relation in the model dictionary.

Assessment target: separate visual evidence from licensed relationNo textbook exercise text copied
The exercise is short but revealing. Students must classify three geodesics using the model dictionary rather than visual resemblance. Listen for whether they say inside intersection, shared endpoint, or no shared endpoint. Those phrases show that they are reasoning with the hyperbolic model instead of with an ordinary Euclidean sketch.
Takeaways

What the discovery changes

Parallelism

The Euclidean uniqueness claim is replaced by a richer boundary classification.

Triangles

Angle defect is positive and, in curvature -1, measures hyperbolic area.

Similarity

There is no free scaling family; fixed angles determine size.

The chapter's deeper lesson is methodological: axioms, models, diagrams, and computations must be kept distinct, then made to illuminate one another.
