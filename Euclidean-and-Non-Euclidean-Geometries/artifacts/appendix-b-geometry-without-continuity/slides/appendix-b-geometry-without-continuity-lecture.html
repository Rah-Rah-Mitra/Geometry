The lecture problem

What survives after continuity is removed?

The danger

A Euclidean drawing quietly supplies limiting points, real-number distances, and order completeness unless the lecturer blocks those assumptions.

The appendix asks for geometry that records exactly which assumptions are active.

Keep

Incidence, perpendicularity, reflections, and enough congruence to move figures.

Question

Existence of limiting rays, square roots, coordinate fields, and order completeness.

Recover

A projective plane, algebraic coordinates, and a classification of unusual H-planes.

axioms + models + algebra → controlled geometry

This slide should slow the room before the technical material begins. The point is not that continuity is wrong or dispensable everywhere. The point is that continuity is a separate permission. When it is absent, a proof must make do with incidence, perpendicularity, and transformations, or rebuild the missing analytic objects from algebra.
Vocabulary checkpoint

H-Planes are the continuity-free starting point

H-plane

A plane satisfying incidence, betweenness, and congruence style axioms modeled after Hilbert, Hessenberg, and Hjelmslev, before imposing strong continuity.

Absolute plane

A geometry organized by incidence, perpendicularity, and reflections; Euclidean, hyperbolic, elliptic, and stranger planes can appear as special cases.

H-plane and absolute-plane languageNo hidden completeness axiom
Use this slide to separate vocabulary before the construction begins. H-plane language keeps the Hilbert style base visible, while absolute plane language highlights perpendicularity and reflection. Students should hear that the appendix is not abandoning rigor. It is changing the inventory of permitted objects and asking what can still be reconstructed.
Bachmann route

A four-step program replaces limits with structure

1. Complete

Embed the absolute plane into a projective plane by adding points made from pencils of lines.

2. Coordinate

Recover homogeneous coordinates over a field K once enough projective incidence is present.

3. Perpendicular

Express orthogonality by a quadratic relation rather than by Euclidean measurement.

4. Classify

Use algebra over K to identify Euclidean, hyperbolic, and exotic H-planes.

The proof strategy is structuralContinuity is not the first tool
Present the program as the backbone of the appendix. Instead of assuming real coordinates at the beginning, the construction earns coordinates by projective completion. Instead of measuring perpendicularity with a metric already known to be real valued, it encodes perpendicularity algebraically. The payoff is a classification problem that can be stated in field language.
Projective completion

New points are pencils of old lines

First kind

The old point A becomes the pencil of all old lines through A.

Second kind

A line t has a pole, represented by all lines perpendicular to t.

Third kind

A parallel direction is defined without first assuming limiting parallel rays exist.

Points are rebuilt from line familiesPencils replace missing limits
This is the first genuinely projective move. Old points are easy because the lines through the point already form a pencil. The second kind is controlled by perpendicularity to a fixed line. The third kind is the subtle one, because calling it an ideal point cannot depend on limiting parallel rays whose existence is under investigation.
Avoiding circular reasoning

Parallel pencils are certified by reflections

n ∈ p(lm) when Rl Rm Rn is a reflection

Why this matters

If the definition used limiting rays, it would assume the continuity-dependent object that the construction is trying to prove or replace.

Invariant intuition

The product test says that a direction is recognized by transformation behavior, not by a point already sitting at infinity.

Reflection products are algebraic witnessesNo prior ideal point needed
Explain the formula as a definition by behavior. A line belongs to the third-kind pencil when the three reflected operations collapse back to a reflection. The audience does not need the full proof here. They need the logical discipline: the direction is detected by transformations available in the absolute plane, not by a completed boundary already assumed.
Hjelmslev move

One common line is pinned down by a reflection equation

The incidence target

A first-kind pencil and a third-kind pencil should meet in exactly one line of the completed projective plane.

The construction finds an old line b that serves as that common member.

Ra Rb Rc = Rd
Original diagram after the appendix constructionThe equation certifies uniqueness
Do not treat this as a copied figure; treat it as a schematic of the proof idea. The old point D supplies one pencil, and the parallel-direction pencil supplies the other. The constructed line b is not guessed from a drawing. It is forced by a reflection equation, which is exactly the kind of replacement for continuity the appendix is teaching.
Filling the projective plane

Old lines extend, but new lines are also needed

Old line extension

An old line a becomes a projective line l(a) by including every pencil that contains a.

Polar lines

For an old point A, the polar line collects poles of lines through A; in the Klein picture it lies outside the absolute.

This slide distinguishes a model drawing from the incidence definition. The line is recognized by its pencil membership.

Projective completion requires more than extending old linesPolarity records perpendicularity
Point out that simply extending every old line is not enough for a full projective plane. The construction also needs lines made from poles and other completed incidence classes. The disk picture is only a model cue. The real definition is combinatorial and transformation based: which pencils are incident with which completed lines.
Snail maps

Half-rotations manufacture projective lines

Construction idea

Fix a point O and two nonperpendicular lines through it. Their reflection product acts like a rotation through twice an angle.

Half-rotation

For A ≠ O, send A to the midpoint of the segment joining A to its rotated image.

Hjelmslev's insight is that these maps extend to collineations and move completed lines into place.

Half-rotation maps are collineation toolsMidpoint behavior replaces analytic continuity
Use the diagram as an operational picture, not as a Euclidean proof. The reflection product around O behaves like a rotation, and the half-rotation sends A to a midpoint A*. The important feature is projective: these maps can carry completed lines to other completed lines, which helps verify the incidence axioms of the enlarged plane.
Coordinates without real continuity

Pappus supplies the field machinery

Affine reduction

Remove the polar of the base point O. The remaining incidence structure behaves like an affine plane when Pappus' theorem holds.

From this affine plane, coordinates can be constructed over a field K.

Pappus turns incidence into algebraThe field is constructed, not assumed
The Pappus slide is where the audience should see the algebra enter cleanly. Once the completed geometry has a projective and affine interpretation, a field of coordinates is no longer an imported real-number background. It is recovered from incidence. Stress that this is why continuity-free geometry can still become computationally precise.
Ordering the coordinate field

Betweenness defines positive scalars

Positive set P

Choose two old points A, B. Ratios from third collinear points outside the segment generate the positive scalars.

Independence

Parallel projection invariance shows the definition does not depend on the initial pair A, B.

Menelaus-style relations then supply the order-field behavior needed for later classification.

Order is recovered from incidence and betweennessContinuity is still not assumed
This slide connects synthetic betweenness to algebraic order. The definition of positive scalar is not a measurement with a real ruler. It comes from ratios along a completed affine line and from invariance under parallel projection. Mention that a Menelaus-type theorem supplies the algebraic consistency needed for an ordered field.
What continuity would have forced

The field may be Pythagorean without being Euclidean

Free mobility gives a Pythagorean field

The sum of two squares is a square in K, so many familiar right-triangle computations have algebraic support.

Elementary continuity is stronger

Only with the appropriate continuity principle does every positive element acquire a square root, making K Euclidean.

a2 + b2 square does not imply every positive c has sqrt(c)
Algebra records exactly what continuity contributesSquare roots are a geometric issue
This is a good place to prevent a common misunderstanding. The coordinate field is not automatically the real numbers. Free mobility gives strong algebraic information, but elementary continuity adds a different strength: square roots for positive elements. That is why unusual non-Archimedean or non-Euclidean fields can support geometries that look familiar locally and strange globally.
Perpendicularity becomes algebra

A single parameter controls orthogonality

a1b1 + a2b2 + k a3b3 = 0

Lines with homogeneous coordinates [a1, a2, a3] and [b1, b2, b3] are perpendicular when this bilinear relation vanishes.

Some lines can be isotropic: they pass through their own poles and are perpendicular to themselves.

k(a,b) = 0
The metric is encoded by a quadratic relationThe sign and square classes of k matter
The formula is the key algebraic compression of perpendicularity. It extends the relation to projective lines, assigns poles, and makes isotropic behavior visible. The parameter k is not decoration. Its sign and square-class behavior sort the geometry into different regimes, including cases with an empty absolute or self-perpendicular lines.
The absolute as a quadratic locus

The boundary appears from poles of isotropic lines

x2 + y2 = -k-1
|k|(x2 + y2) < 1 describes the H-plane region

For k = -1, the familiar unit circle appears as the absolute in a Klein-style model.

The absolute is not a copied picture; it is a locus from q_kRegion depends on K and k
Use the equation to connect the previous slide to a geometric model. The absolute is the locus associated with isotropic behavior and poles. When k is negative the circle model is natural; when k is zero the Euclidean case emerges differently; when k is positive the real ordered picture has no ordinary circular absolute. The field still matters.

Synthesis

Continuity-free geometry is still highly structured

Takeaways

  • Removing continuity does not remove incidence, perpendicularity, or reflection structure.
  • Projective completion turns pencils of lines into new points and lines.
  • Pappus and betweenness rebuild a coordinate field and an order.
  • The parameter k and the field K classify familiar and exotic H-planes.
  • Every proof step should name its license: incidence, transformation, algebra, or continuity.
