Logical separation

Independence Starts by Separating Sentences from Meanings

Syntax

Formal words: point, line, between, congruent, parallel, prove.

Semantics

A model assigns those words to objects and relations inside a host theory.

Independence target. Find a model where the neutral statements hold and Euclid's parallel postulate fails.

The same sentence can be evaluated after its terms receive model meanings.
syntax: formal proofsemantics: interpreted truth
This slide separates syntax from semantics. Axioms and theorems are sentences in a formal system, while a model assigns meanings to point, line, between, distance, and angle. The central move is to check that the neutral sentences become true after interpretation even though Euclid's parallel postulate becomes false inside the same interpreted world.
Relative consistency

A Model Turns Contradiction into Transfer

Contrapositive Form

If the model is built inside a trusted host, a contradiction in the interpreted neutral theory would already produce a contradiction in the host.

Independence Form

Since the model satisfies the neutral axioms but has many parallels, a neutral proof of unique parallels cannot exist.

model proof, not visual persuasionrelative to the Euclidean host
Present relative consistency as a contrapositive engine. If neutral geometry plus the negation of Euclid's parallel postulate produced a contradiction, then the Euclidean host containing the disk model would inherit that contradiction. The model does not prove absolute consistency; it shows that any failure would already be a failure of the host theory.
Beltrami-Klein model

Chords Make Incidence Visible

Interpretation

  • Points are interior points of the unit disk.
  • Lines are open chords with boundary endpoints removed.
  • Betweenness follows Euclidean order along the chord.

Warning

Straight chords do not mean Euclidean angle measurement is the hyperbolic angle measurement.

best for incidencenot conformal
The Klein disk is useful because geodesics look visually straight: each line is a chord of the boundary circle. This makes incidence and betweenness easy to inspect, but it also creates a warning. Euclidean angle measurement on the screen is not the interpreted hyperbolic angle measurement, so straightness and angle must be kept in separate columns.
Poincare disk model

Orthogonal Arcs Make Angles Honest

Interpretation

Lines are diameters or circular arcs meeting the boundary circle at right angles.

Conformal Feature

Euclidean angles between arcs are the model's hyperbolic angles.

orthogonal support: ||c||^2 - R^2 = 1
best for angle inspectiondistances are not Euclidean chord lengths
The Poincare disk trades straight-looking geodesics for conformal angle measurement. Diameters and boundary-orthogonal circular arcs are interpreted as lines, and Euclidean angles at intersections agree with hyperbolic angles. Ask learners to inspect the boundary condition: geodesics meet the ideal circle orthogonally, but the circle itself is not part of the plane.
Model dictionary

Klein and Poincare Are Two Views of the Same Points

Radial Translation

For a Klein point k inside the unit disk, the corresponding Poincare point lies on the same radius.

p = k / (1 + sqrt(1 - ||k||^2))

The formula is part of the notebook's translation guide: one model displays straightness, the other displays angles.

Klein to Poincaresame radius, different visual geometry
The radial map from Klein to Poincare is a model-to-model dictionary. It sends each interior point to a point on the same radius and converts Klein chords into Poincare arcs. The formula is not presented as a magic trick; it is a checkable coordinate translation that preserves the interpreted geometry while changing the visible shape of lines.
Inversion machinery

Euclidean Circles Certify Hyperbolic Lines

Inversion Check

For a circle centered at O with radius 1, a point X is sent to X* on ray OX with |OX| |OX*| = 1.

Geodesic Check

A circle orthogonal to the unit boundary is stable under boundary inversion and becomes a Poincare line.

inversion(inversion(X)) = X
host operation: circle inversionmodel role: geodesic certification
Inversion supplies the circle geometry that makes Poincare geodesics manageable inside the Euclidean host. A circle orthogonal to the unit circle inverts to itself, so it behaves like a stable candidate for a hyperbolic line. The important classroom distinction is that inversion is Euclidean machinery used to certify a non-Euclidean interpretation.

Disk Model Says

Cross-ratio distance

Boundary Endpoints Calibrate Interior Distance

Klein Metric Witness

For A, P, Q, B ordered on a chord with A and B ideal endpoints, distance is a logarithm of a cross-ratio.

d(P,Q) = (1/2) log( |AQ| |BP| / (|AP| |BQ|) )

Only P and Q are hyperbolic points. A and B are boundary data used by the host model to measure the interval.

distance is projective data plus logarithmboundary is a measuring scaffold
Cross-ratio distance is the metric witness for the Klein model. The endpoints on the ideal boundary are not ordinary points of the hyperbolic plane, but they calibrate the distance between interior points along a chord. Have students read the order of A, P, Q, B carefully, because the formula uses boundary data to measure interior separation.
Ideal boundary

The Boundary Names Directions Without Adding Points to the Plane

Inside

Interior points are actual points of the interpreted hyperbolic plane.

On the Circle

Boundary points are ideal endpoints: they organize directions and limiting parallels, but they are not reached.

d(0,r) = log((1 + r) / (1 - r))
r < 1 for model pointsthe boundary is a limiting instrument
The ideal boundary slide prevents a common confusion. Boundary points can name directions, endpoints, and limiting behavior, but they are not points of the modeled plane. That distinction explains why a chord can approach the boundary forever without reaching a final hyperbolic point, and why distance blows up near the edge.

Theorem filter

Neutral Results Survive; Unique Parallels Do Not

Independence Pattern

If a statement were provable from the neutral axioms, it would hold in every model of those axioms.

Observed Failure

The disk model is a model of the neutral part but violates the unique-parallel statement.

model failure blocks neutral proofthe postulate is genuinely extra

Why This Proves Independence

A theorem of the neutral axioms must be true in every model of those axioms. The disk interpretation supplies a countermodel to unique parallels.

What It Does Not Claim

The argument is relative: it does not prove geometry from nothing. It shows that a neutral proof of the postulate would contradict the model evidence.
