Symplectic Capacities

Numerical obstructions to symplectic embeddings

Invariant

A single number $c(M,\omega)$ measuring symplectic size.

Obstruction

If $c(A)>c(B)$ then $A$ cannot embed into $B$.

Founding result

Gromov nonsqueezing: $r\le R$ or no embedding.

What a Capacity Measures

One number

$c$ sends each $(M,\omega)$ to a value in $[0,\infty]$.

Acts like area

Scales with the square of a radius, not the full volume.

Capacity

$c:(M^{2n},\omega)\mapsto c(M,\omega)\in[0,\infty]$

Not volume

Two regions of equal volume can have different capacity.

Printed pp. 457-502What a Capacity Measures
A capacity assigns one positive number to every symplectic manifold in a fixed dimension. The number behaves like an area: it is monotone under embeddings, scales by lambda squared under conformal rescaling of the form, and is pinned by a normalization on the ball and the cylinder.

The Capacity Axioms

Monotonicity

If $(M,\omega)\hookrightarrow(N,\tau)$ then $c(M)\le c(N)$.

Conformality

$c(M,\lambda\omega)=\lambda\,c(M,\omega)$ for $\lambda>0$.

Normalization

$c(B^{2n}(r))=c(Z^{2n}(r))=\pi r^2$.

Defining identity

$c\big(B^{2n}(r)\big)=c\big(Z^{2n}(r)\big)=\pi r^2$

Same value

The thin cylinder and the round ball share one capacity.

Printed pp. 457-502The Capacity Axioms
Three axioms govern every capacity. Monotonicity forbids large objects from embedding into small ones, conformality fixes how capacity reacts to scaling the form, and normalization gives the shared value pi r squared on the unit ball and the standard cylinder. Together they make capacity an honest symplectic invariant.

Gromov Nonsqueezing

Theorem

$B^{2n}(r)\hookrightarrow Z^{2n}(R)$ symplectically only if $r\le R$.

Cylinder

$Z^{2n}(R)=B^2(R)\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}$.

Nonsqueezing

$B^{2n}(r)\xhookrightarrow{\ \text{symp}\ } Z^{2n}(R)\ \Longrightarrow\ r\le R$

Surprise

Volume allows the embedding; symplectic structure forbids it.

Printed pp. 457-502Gromov Nonsqueezing
Gromov nonsqueezing is the founding theorem of the subject. A symplectic embedding of the radius r ball into the cylinder of radius R can exist only when r is at most R. Volume places no such limit, so the obstruction is genuinely symplectic and not measure theoretic at all.

The Gromov Width

First capacity

The smallest of all capacities, denoted $c_B$.

Largest ball

Area of the biggest ball embedding into $(M,\omega)$.

Definition

$c_B(M,\omega)=\sup\{\pi r^2: B^{2n}(r)\hookrightarrow M\}$

Consequence

Nonsqueezing $\Rightarrow$ $c_B$ is finite and bounded by $R$.

Printed pp. 457-502The Gromov Width
The Gromov width is the first and smallest capacity. It equals the area pi r squared of the largest ball that embeds symplectically into the manifold. Nonsqueezing is precisely the statement that this width is finite and bounded by the cylinder, giving the capacity axioms a concrete model.

Symplectic Rigidity

Symplectic maps

Preserve every capacity, not only volume.

Volume maps

Can reshape a ball into any equal-volume blob.

Closure

$\overline{\mathrm{Symp}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{vol}}$ in the $C^0$ topology

Eliashberg-Gromov

$C^0$ limits of symplectomorphisms stay symplectic.

Printed pp. 457-502Symplectic Rigidity
Capacities expose symplectic rigidity. A volume preserving map can deform a ball into any equal volume shape, but a symplectomorphism must respect every capacity. The group of symplectic maps is therefore closed inside the volume preserving group, a closure phenomenon that no purely measure theoretic argument could ever produce.

The Hofer-Zehnder Capacity

Dynamical

Built from Hamiltonian flows, not embeddings.

Admissible $H$

Flow has no nonconstant fast periodic orbits.

Definition

$c_{HZ}(M,\omega)=\sup_{H\ \text{admissible}}\big(\max H-\min H\big)$

Payoff

Finiteness forces a short closed orbit to exist.

Printed pp. 457-502The Hofer-Zehnder Capacity
The Hofer-Zehnder capacity is built from Hamiltonian dynamics. One takes admissible Hamiltonians supported in the manifold whose flows carry no fast periodic orbits, and the capacity is the supremum of their oscillation. Finiteness of this number forces the existence of a short closed orbit, linking embeddings to dynamics.

The Hofer Metric

Oscillation norm

Length of a path is the integrated oscillation of $H_t$.

Bi-invariant

Invariant under left and right translation on $\mathrm{Ham}$.

Hofer norm

$\|\phi\|=\inf_{H}\int_0^1\big(\max_M H_t-\min_M H_t\big)\,dt$

Nondegenerate

$\|\phi\|=0$ forces $\phi=\mathrm{id}$ — the deep part.

Printed pp. 457-502The Hofer Metric
The Hofer norm measures a Hamiltonian by the time integral of its oscillation, the maximum minus the minimum over the manifold. This yields a bi-invariant distance on the group of compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms. Its nondegeneracy, that the identity sits at positive distance from nearby maps, is deep and rigidity driven.

Variational Construction

Action

$\mathcal{A}_H(x)=\int_0^1\!\big(\lambda(\dot x)-H_t(x)\big)\,dt$

Critical points

Periodic orbits; a minimax picks the capacity.

Printed pp. 457-502Variational Construction
Capacities arise variationally from the symplectic action functional on loops. Critical points are periodic orbits, and a minimax over a suitable family of loops selects a distinguished action value. That value satisfies the capacity axioms, so the embedding obstruction and the dynamical orbit emerge from one variational principle.

Capacity Toolbox

Monotone

Embedding shrinks or preserves $c$.

Conformal

$c$ scales linearly with $\omega$, quadratically with radius.

Scaling laws

$c(\lambda r)=\lambda^2 c(r),\qquad \mathrm{vol}(\lambda r)=\lambda^{2n}\mathrm{vol}(r)$

Certificate

$c(A)>c(B)$ is a rigorous no-embedding proof.

Printed pp. 457-502Capacity Toolbox
We collect the working facts. Capacity is monotone and conformal, equals pi r squared on balls and cylinders, scales quadratically with radius while volume scales with the full dimension, and detects rigidity that volume misses. These properties make capacity the standard numerical certificate against symplectic embeddings.
