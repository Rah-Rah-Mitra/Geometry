The Cotangent Bundle and Its Canonical Form

Tautological form

The one-form $\lambda$ is defined without coordinates.

Zero section

The base sits inside $T^*Q$ as a Lagrangian.

Canonical forms

$\lambda = p\,dq, \qquad \omega_{\mathrm{can}} = d\lambda = dp \wedge dq$

Invariance

Every later graph inherits this closed nondegenerate form.

Printed pp. 356-384The Cotangent Bundle
The cotangent bundle is the universal phase space of the chapter. Its tautological one-form and canonical two-form are coordinate free, so every later construction inherits their invariance. Fix the sign conventions now: omega equals d-p wedge d-q, and the zero section is Lagrangian, which anchors the graphs we build.

Lagrangian Submanifolds as Graphs

Half dimensional

$\dim L = \tfrac12 \dim T^*Q$ and $\omega|_L = 0$.

Closed equals Lagrangian

A section $\sigma_\alpha$ is Lagrangian iff $d\alpha = 0$.

Graph criterion

$\sigma_\alpha^*\omega = -d\alpha, \quad d\alpha = 0 \Rightarrow \alpha = dS$

The scalar appears

Locally a closed form is exact: $L = \mathrm{graph}(dS)$.

Printed pp. 356-384Lagrangians as Graphs
A Lagrangian submanifold is half dimensional and annihilates the symplectic form. The decisive fact is that a one-form on the base is Lagrangian as a section exactly when it is closed. Locally closed means exact, so the section is the differential of a scalar, and that scalar is the generating function we want.

Generating Function of a Symplectomorphism

Twisted product

On $T^*Q \times T^*Q$ use $\Omega = \omega_1 \ominus \omega_2$.

Graph is Lagrangian

$\mathrm{graph}(\phi)$ is Lagrangian iff $\phi$ is symplectic.

Generated map

$p = \partial_q S(q,Q), \qquad P = -\partial_Q S(q,Q)$

Mixed partials

One scalar $S$ recovers both momenta of $\phi$.

Printed pp. 356-384Generating a Symplectomorphism
To encode a map rather than a single Lagrangian we move to the product with a twisted symplectic form. The graph of a symplectomorphism is Lagrangian there, and when it projects nicely onto a base of mixed coordinates a generating function S appears. Its mixed partials reproduce the old and new momenta exactly.

Twist Maps and the Type-One Function

Twist condition

$\partial Q / \partial p$ invertible: $(q,Q)$ are good coordinates.

First kind

$S=S(q,Q)$ is the classical generating function.

Printed pp. 356-384Twist Maps
A twist condition guarantees that old and new positions form good coordinates on the graph. Then S depends on q and Q, and differentiating recovers p and P with opposite signs. This is the classical generating function of the first kind, and it is the cleanest bridge between the abstract Lagrangian and explicit mechanics.

The Symplectic Action Functional

Action of a path

Integrate $p\,dq$ minus the Hamiltonian.

Stationarity

$\delta \mathcal{A} = 0$ on Hamilton's equations.

Action functional

$\mathcal{A}(\gamma) = \int_0^T \big( p\,\dot q - H(q,p)\big)\,dt$

Critical = orbit

Trajectories are exactly the stationary paths.

Printed pp. 356-384Symplectic Action
The symplectic action assigns to a path the integral of p dq minus the Hamiltonian over time. Its first variation vanishes precisely on solutions of Hamilton's equations, so critical points of the action are exactly the trajectories. This variational characterization is the engine that converts dynamics into a search for stationary points.

Discrete Hamiltonian Mechanics

Slice time

One generating function $S_k$ per step.

Finite model

Action becomes a function of $q_0,\dots,q_N$.

Printed pp. 356-384Discrete Mechanics
Discretizing time replaces the action integral by a finite sum of generating functions, one per step, evaluated on a chain of positions. The sum is a function of finitely many variables, so stationarity becomes an ordinary gradient condition. Broken trajectories that make this discrete action critical are the discrete analogues of orbits.

The Discrete Action Sum and Its Critical Points

Discrete Euler equation

Interior gradient vanishes at each junction.

Momentum matching

$\partial_{q_k} S_k + \partial_{q_k} S_{k+1} = 0$.

Critical-point condition

$\nabla \mathbb{A}(q_0,\dots,q_N) = 0, \quad \mathbb{A} = \sum_{k} S_k(q_{k-1},q_k)$

Verified orbit

Notebook checks $\|\nabla\mathbb{A}\| < 10^{-10}$ at corners.

Printed pp. 356-384Discrete Action Sum
Setting the gradient of the discrete action to zero gives a three-term recurrence, the discrete Euler equation, matching interior momenta across each junction. A solution is a broken path whose corners are consistent, that is, a genuine discrete orbit. The notebook checks that the gradient norm at marked corners falls below tolerance.

Hamilton-Jacobi Viewpoint

Flatten the flow

Seek $S$ making new coordinates constant.

Continuum limit

The discrete sum becomes the action integral.

Hamilton-Jacobi equation

$\partial_t S + H\!\big(q,\partial_q S\big) = 0$

Integrability link

A complete solution trivializes the dynamics.

Printed pp. 356-384Hamilton-Jacobi
The Hamilton-Jacobi equation asks for a generating function whose level structure flattens the dynamics into constant new coordinates. Solving it produces a symplectomorphism that trivializes the flow, and the action evaluated along orbits supplies the solution. This is the continuum limit of the discrete picture and links generating functions to integrability.

Generating Functions Quadratic at Infinity

Tame at infinity

$S$ equals a fixed nondegenerate $Q_\infty$ far out.

Finite criticals

Sublevel topology is computable.

GFQI condition

$S(\xi) = Q_\infty(\xi) \;\; \text{for}\;\; \|\xi\| \gg 0$

Why it matters

Existence without an explicit formula for $\phi$.

Printed pp. 356-384Quadratic at Infinity
For global results we restrict to functions that agree with a fixed nondegenerate quadratic form outside a compact set. This tameness makes the critical points finite to count and the topology of sublevel sets computable. Such functions are the workhorses behind existence statements when no explicit formula for the map is available.

Fixed Points and Lagrangian Intersections

Fixed point = intersection

$\mathrm{graph}(\phi) \cap \Delta$ inside $T^*Q \times T^*Q$.

Morse lower bound

Topology forces a minimum critical count.

Printed pp. 356-384Fixed Points
Fixed points of a symplectomorphism are intersections of its graph with the diagonal, equivalently critical points of a generating function quadratic at infinity. Morse theory then forces a minimum number of such points, turning a dynamical existence question into a count of critical points the topology cannot avoid.

Lagrangian Graphs over the Zero Section

Graph of a closed form

A projecting Lagrangian is $\mathrm{graph}(\alpha)$, $d\alpha=0$.

Zeros of $\alpha$

Intersections with the zero section are critical points of $S$.

Recap

$L \cap 0_Q = \{\,dS = 0\,\} = \mathrm{Crit}(S)$

Threads tied

Graphs, generating functions, and fixed points are one story.

Printed pp. 356-384Graphs over the Zero Section
A Lagrangian that projects diffeomorphically onto the base is the graph of a closed one-form, hence locally a differential. Intersections with the zero section are the zeros of that one-form, so its critical points are forced by the topology of the base. This recap ties graphs, generating functions, and fixed points together.
