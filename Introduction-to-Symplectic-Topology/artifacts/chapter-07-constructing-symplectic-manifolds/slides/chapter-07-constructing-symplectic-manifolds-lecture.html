The Symplectic Blow-Up

Exceptional divisor

$E \cong \mathbb{CP}^{n-1}, \quad \text{area}(E) = \pi\lambda^2$

Size parameter

$\lambda$ is the radius of the excised ball; it fixes the area of $E$.

Printed pp. 289-340The Symplectic Blow-Up
Blowing up a point removes a small open ball and replaces its boundary sphere by an exceptional divisor, a copy of complex projective space whose lines are collapsed to points. The construction depends on a size parameter equal to the radius of the excised ball, which fixes the symplectic area of the exceptional class.

Local Model of the Blow-Up

Tautological bundle

$\mathcal{O}(-1) \to \mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ carries a fibrewise symplectic form.

Matching collar

Away from the zero section it agrees with the punctured ball.

Glue the model

$\big(\mathbb{C}^n \setminus 0\big) / {\sim}\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\; \mathcal{O}(-1)\setminus E$

Outside is untouched

The form changes only inside the ball of radius $\lambda$.

Printed pp. 289-340Local Model of the Blow-Up
The tautological line bundle over projective space carries a fibrewise form that, away from the zero section, matches the standard form on the punctured ball. Gluing this model into the manifold realizes the blow-up symplectically. The exceptional divisor receives area proportional to the chosen size, and away from it nothing changes.

Blow-Down and the Size Parameter

Reverse the surgery

Collapse an exceptional sphere of the right area back to a point.

Self-intersection

In dimension four, $E\cdot E = -1$.

Inverse operation

$\widetilde{M} = M \,\#\, \overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}, \quad \text{area}(E) = \pi\lambda^2$

Bookkeeping

The same $\lambda$ controls both directions; areas must agree.

Printed pp. 289-340Blow-Down and the Size Parameter
Blow-down reverses the surgery: an exceptional sphere of the right area is collapsed back to a point, restoring a ball. The size parameter is the bookkeeping device. In dimension four the exceptional class has self-intersection negative one, and its area must equal the parameter for the inverse construction to be symplectic.

Moment Polytope Picture

Area loss

Printed pp. 289-340Moment Polytope Picture
For toric manifolds the blow-up is visible as a corner chop of the moment polytope. Slicing a triangle of side epsilon off a vertex introduces a new edge, the image of the exceptional divisor, whose length records the blow-up size. The lost Liouville area equals one half the square of the clipping size.

Connected Sums of Manifolds

Naive sum fails

Removing balls and gluing spheres usually destroys $\omega$.

Why

A separating sphere bounds, so $\omega$ cannot stay nondegenerate across it.

Smooth vs symplectic

$M_1 \,\#\, M_2 \;\neq\; \text{symplectic in general}$

Fix

Operate along codimension-two symplectic submanifolds instead of points.

Printed pp. 289-340Connected Sums of Manifolds
An ordinary smooth connected sum removes a ball from each manifold and glues along the resulting spheres, but this generically destroys a symplectic form because spheres bound. Symplectic topology therefore replaces point surgery with surgery along codimension-two symplectic submanifolds, where normal data can be matched compatibly.

The Symplectic Fiber Sum (Gompf)

Fiber sum

$M_1 \#_Q M_2 = (M_1\setminus \nu Q) \cup_\phi (M_2\setminus \nu Q)$

Matching map

$\phi$ identifies boundary fibrations reversing orientation.

Printed pp. 289-340The Symplectic Fiber Sum
Gompf's fiber sum glues two symplectic manifolds along symplectomorphic codimension-two submanifolds whose normal bundles are opposite in Euler number. Cutting out tubular neighborhoods and identifying the boundary fibrations by an orientation-reversing bundle map produces a closed symplectic manifold whose form restricts correctly on each piece.

Normal Bundles Must Match

Opposite Euler numbers

The two normal bundles must be inverse to each other.

Standard collars

The symplectic neighborhood theorem supplies matching tubes.

Balance condition

$e(\nu_1 Q) + e(\nu_2 Q) = 0$

Result

The patched form stays closed and nondegenerate across the seam.

Printed pp. 289-340Normal Bundles Must Match
The fiber sum succeeds only when the normal bundles of the two copies of the gluing submanifold are inverse, so their Euler numbers sum to zero. This balance lets the symplectic neighborhood theorem provide standard collars that the gluing diffeomorphism can identify while keeping the patched form closed and nondegenerate throughout.

The Telescope and Symplectic Cut

Lerman's cut

Slice along a level set of a Hamiltonian circle action.

Cap each side

Collapse the boundary by the circle to close up both pieces.

Cut at a level

$M^{\,\geq c}_{\mathrm{cut}}, \;\; M^{\,\leq c}_{\mathrm{cut}} \;\;\text{from}\;\; \mu^{-1}(c)/S^1$

Dual to summing

Reversing the cut recovers the symplectic sum along the reduced space.

Printed pp. 289-340The Telescope and Symplectic Cut
The telescope construction, dual to gluing, cuts a manifold along a level set of a circle action and caps each side with a collapsed boundary. Lerman's symplectic cut produces two closed symplectic manifolds from one with a Hamiltonian circle action, and reversing it recovers the symplectic sum along the shared reduced space.

Donaldson Submanifolds

Integral class

Assume $[\omega]/2\pi$ is integral, with line bundle $L$.

Approx. holomorphic

Sections of $L^{\otimes k}$ become nearly holomorphic for large $k$.

Zero set theorem

$W = s_k^{-1}(0)\;\text{ is symplectic, PD to } k\,[\omega]$

Raw material

These hypersurfaces feed fiber sums and blow-ups.

Printed pp. 289-340Donaldson Submanifolds
Donaldson's theorem produces symplectic hypersurfaces Poincare dual to large multiples of an integral symplectic class. Approximately holomorphic sections of high tensor powers of a line bundle have zero sets that are symplectic submanifolds. This supplies the codimension-two pieces that fiber sums and blow-ups consume as raw material.

Lefschetz Pencils and Fibrations

Pair of sections

A pencil is the family of zero sets of $s_0 + t\,s_1$.

Base locus

Blowing up $\{s_0 = s_1 = 0\}$ yields a fibration over $\mathbb{CP}^1$.

Lefschetz fibration

$f: \widetilde{M} \to \mathbb{CP}^1,\quad \text{nondegenerate critical points}$

Payoff

A topological description of symplectic four-manifolds.

Printed pp. 289-340Lefschetz Pencils
Pushing Donaldson's method to pairs of sections yields Lefschetz pencils: a base locus together with a family of symplectic hypersurfaces having only nondegenerate critical points. Blowing up the base locus turns the pencil into a Lefschetz fibration over the sphere, giving a topological description of symplectic four-manifolds up to the construction.
