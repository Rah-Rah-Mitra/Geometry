What Is a Symplectic Fibration?

Locally trivial

Bundle $\pi:M\to B$ with model fiber $(F,\omega_F)$.

Symplectic structure group

Transitions are symplectomorphisms of $(F,\omega_F)$.

Fiberwise form

$\omega_b = \omega_F$ on each fiber $M_b=\pi^{-1}(b)$

Central question

Does $\omega_b$ extend to a closed form on all of $M$?

Printed pp. 252-288Definition
A symplectic fibration is a locally trivial bundle whose fibers carry symplectic forms varying smoothly and whose transition maps act by symplectomorphisms. The fiberwise form is intrinsic to each fiber but is not yet a form on the total space, so the central problem is extension across horizontal directions.

Model Example: Two-Sphere Bundles

Every area form is symplectic

On $S^2$, any area form is a symplectic form.

Topology controls gluing

Clutching map / Euler class governs the bundle.

Printed pp. 252-288Two-Sphere Bundles
Oriented two-sphere bundles are the smallest interesting symplectic fibrations because every area form on the two-sphere is symplectic. They illustrate how the topology of the base, captured by an Euler class or a clutching map, controls whether the fiberwise area forms can be glued into a coherent global symplectic structure.

The Symplectic Connection

Vertical space

$\mathrm{Vert}=\ker d\pi$, tangent to the fibers.

Horizontal = $\omega$-orthogonal

$\mathrm{Hor}=\mathrm{Vert}^{\,\omega}$, the symplectic complement.

Horizontal distribution

$H_x=\{v: \omega(v,w)=0\ \forall\, w\in \mathrm{Vert}_x\}$

Why it splits

$\omega_F$ nondegenerate on fibers $\Rightarrow TM=\mathrm{Hor}\oplus\mathrm{Vert}$.

Printed pp. 252-288Connections
Given a fiberwise symplectic form, the horizontal distribution is the symplectic orthogonal complement of the vertical tangent space. This complement is a genuine connection because the fiberwise form is nondegenerate on the fibers, so horizontal and vertical subspaces meet transversally and span the whole tangent space of the total space.

Horizontal Lifts and Parallel Transport

Unique lift

A base path $\gamma$ lifts horizontally from any start.

Transport is symplectic

$P_\gamma:M_{b_0}\to M_{b_1}$ is a symplectomorphism.

Printed pp. 252-288Parallel Transport
A base path lifts uniquely to horizontal curves once a starting fiber point is fixed. Parallel transport along that path carries one fiber to another, and because the horizontal distribution is the symplectic orthogonal, this transport is a symplectomorphism between the fibers, preserving the fiberwise areas it moves.

Curvature of the Connection

Failure to close

$[\tilde X,\tilde Y]$ has a vertical component.

Hamiltonian valued

That component is a Hamiltonian vector field on $F$.

Curvature

$R(X,Y)=\mathrm{vert}\,[\tilde X,\tilde Y]=X_{H(X,Y)}$

Reading

Curvature assigns a fiber Hamiltonian to each base 2-plane.

Printed pp. 252-288Curvature
Curvature measures the failure of horizontal lifts to close: the bracket of two horizontal lifts has a vertical component. For the symplectic connection this vertical component is a Hamiltonian vector field on the fiber, so curvature is valued in Hamiltonian functions rather than arbitrary fiber vector fields.

Holonomy by Symplectomorphisms

Loop transport

Around a base loop, $P_\gamma$ returns rotated.

Holonomy

The return map is a symplectomorphism of $F$.

Printed pp. 252-288Holonomy
Transport around a closed base loop returns to the starting fiber but generally rotates it by a nontrivial symplectomorphism, the holonomy. The notebook model computes a holonomy angle equal to integrated curvature times enclosed base area, the local prototype of how curvature accumulates into global fiber automorphisms.

The Coupling Form

Vertical part

Restricts to $\omega_F$ on each fiber.

Horizontal part

Built from the curvature Hamiltonians.

Coupling / extension form

$\Omega = \omega^{\mathrm{vert}} + \pi^{*}\alpha + (\text{curvature term}), \quad d\Omega=0$

Normalization

Fixed fiber integral $\int_F \Omega = \text{const}$.

Printed pp. 252-288Coupling Form
The coupling form is the distinguished closed extension of the fiberwise symplectic form, normalized so its integral over each fiber is fixed and its horizontal part is tied to the connection. It combines a vertical fiber contribution with a base contribution built from the curvature Hamiltonians of the connection.

When Does a Closed Extension Exist?

Obstruction

Curvature Hamiltonians must be chosen consistently.

Lives in cohomology

Obstruction sits in fiberwise $H^1$.

Sufficient condition

$H^1(F;\mathbb{R})=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \exists\ \text{canonical closed }\Omega$

Meaning

No fiber 1-cohomology means no obstruction to closing.

Printed pp. 252-288Extension
A fiberwise symplectic form extends to a closed two-form on the total space exactly when the curvature Hamiltonians can be chosen consistently, an obstruction living in fiberwise cohomology. When the fibers have vanishing first cohomology the obstruction disappears and a canonical closed coupling form always exists.

Hamiltonian Fibrations

Reduced structure group

Transitions lie in $\mathrm{Ham}(F,\omega_F)$.

Equivalent

$\omega_F$ extends to a closed form of fixed fiber class.

Hamiltonian fibration

$\mathrm{Ham}(F,\omega_F)\ \curvearrowright\ M, \quad [\,\Omega|_F\,]\ \text{fixed}$

Consequence

Holonomy and curvature take Hamiltonian values.

Printed pp. 252-288Hamiltonian Fibrations
A Hamiltonian fibration is a symplectic fibration whose structure group reduces to the group of Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms. Equivalently the fiberwise form extends to a closed form whose fiber integral is fixed, so the holonomy and curvature both take values in Hamiltonian data rather than merely symplectic data.

Canonical Coupling and Characteristic Numbers

Canonical $\Omega$

Unique closed extension with fixed fiber integral.

Top power

$\Omega^{\,k+1}$ computes characteristic numbers.

Coupling class

$\int_M \Omega^{\,n+1}\ \in\ \text{invariants of the bundle}$

Link

Bundle geometry $\leftrightarrow$ topology of $\mathrm{Ham}$.

Printed pp. 252-288Coupling Class
On a Hamiltonian fibration the coupling form is canonical and closed, and its top fiber power computes characteristic numbers of the bundle. Choosing it amounts to choosing a connection whose holonomy lies in the Hamiltonian group, linking the geometry of the bundle to the topology of that group.

Fibrations and the Topology of Ham

Loops build bundles

A loop in $\mathrm{Ham}$ clutches a fibration over $S^2$.

Coupling detects classes

$\Omega$ distinguishes nontrivial loops.

Clutching construction

$\pi_1(\mathrm{Ham})\ \longrightarrow\ \{\text{Ham. fibrations over }S^2\}$

Payoff

Fibrations probe homotopy and cohomology of $\mathrm{Ham}$.

Printed pp. 252-288Topology of Ham
These constructions feed back into the study of the Hamiltonian group itself. Loops in Ham build Hamiltonian fibrations over the two-sphere, and the coupling form detects nontrivial classes, so symplectic fibrations become a tool for proving that Ham has rich homotopy and cohomology.

Printed pp. 252-288Summary
We saw fibers plus a connection whose horizontal distribution is the symplectic orthogonal, holonomy acting by symplectomorphisms, and a coupling form packaging fiber and base contributions. Hamiltonian fibrations sharpen this picture and connect it to the topology of Ham, setting up the global rigidity results that follow.
