Symplectic Vector Spaces

Skew

$\omega(v,v)=0$ for all $v$; swapping flips sign.

Nondegenerate

If $\omega(v,\cdot)=0$ then $v=0$ — no blind directions.

Definition

$\omega:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}$ bilinear, $\omega(v,w)=-\omega(w,v)$

Consequence

Nondegeneracy forces $\dim V = 2n$ to be even.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Symplectic Vector Spaces
A symplectic form is a bilinear map that is skew and nondegenerate. Skewness forces every vector to pair to zero with itself, so the form measures oriented spreading rather than length. Nondegeneracy means no nonzero vector is invisible, which forces the dimension to be even.

The Standard Form on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$

Matrix model

Coordinates $(q_1,\dots,q_n,p_1,\dots,p_n)$ pair through $J_0$.

Canonical pairs

$\omega_0(e_{q_i},e_{p_j})=\delta_{ij}$, all else zero.

Standard form

$\omega_0(v,w)=v^{\mathsf T}J_0\,w,\quad J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I\\ I&0\end{pmatrix}$

Note

$J_0^2=-I$ and $J_0^{\mathsf T}=-J_0$.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106The Standard Form on R^{2n}
On the model space the form is encoded by the block matrix J zero, built from an identity and a minus identity. Pairing two vectors becomes a single bilinear product with this matrix, and evaluating it on the basis pairs recovers the canonical symplectic relations between position and momentum directions.

Signed Area in the Plane

Plane case $n=1$

$\omega_0(v,w)=v_1w_2-v_2w_1=\det[\,v\ \ w\,]$

Reading

The form is the oriented area of the parallelogram on $v,w$.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Signed Area in the Plane
In the symplectic plane the form is exactly the signed area of the parallelogram spanned by two vectors. The determinant of their coordinate matrix gives that area, including its sign, so orientation is built into the form. Swapping the two vectors flips the sign, witnessing skewness directly.

Symplectic Bases

Paired vectors

A basis $u_1,\dots,u_n,w_1,\dots,w_n$ with canonical pairings.

Linear Darboux

Every $(V,\omega)$ looks like $(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\omega_0)$.

Canonical relations

$\omega(u_i,w_j)=\delta_{ij},\quad \omega(u_i,u_j)=\omega(w_i,w_j)=0$

Upshot

One symplectic space per even dimension, up to isomorphism.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Symplectic Bases
Every symplectic space admits a basis of paired vectors on which the form looks standard. The pairs satisfy the canonical relations and all other pairings vanish. This linear Darboux theorem says there is essentially one symplectic vector space of each even dimension, so coordinates can always be chosen to make the form trivial.

The Symplectic Group $\mathrm{Sp}(2n)$

Form-preserving

$A$ is symplectic if $\omega_0(Av,Aw)=\omega_0(v,w)$.

Volume

$\det A=1$, so symplectic maps preserve volume.

Defining identity

$A^{\mathsf T}J_0\,A=J_0,\qquad \mathrm{Sp}(2n)=\{A:A^{\mathsf T}J_0A=J_0\}$

Check

A shear with residual $A^{\mathsf T}J_0A-J_0=0$ is symplectic.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106The Symplectic Group
A linear map is symplectic when it preserves the form, which in coordinates is the matrix identity A transpose J A equals J. These maps form the group of symplectic matrices. Taking determinants shows every symplectic matrix has determinant one, so symplectic maps preserve the volume form built from the symplectic form.

Symplectic Complements

The complement

$W^\omega$ is everything that pairs to zero with $W$.

Not a splitting

$W$ and $W^\omega$ may overlap, unlike metric orthogonals.

Definition & count

$W^\omega=\{v:\omega(v,w)=0\ \forall w\in W\},\ \ \dim W+\dim W^\omega=2n$

Source

The dimension count comes straight from nondegeneracy.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Symplectic Complements
The symplectic orthogonal of a subspace collects every vector that pairs to zero with all of it. Unlike a metric orthogonal it can intersect the original subspace. Nondegeneracy gives the dimension count: the subspace and its complement add to the full dimension, even when they overlap rather than splitting the space.

Isotropic, Coisotropic, Lagrangian

Isotropic

$W\subseteq W^\omega$: the form vanishes on $W$.

Coisotropic

$W\supseteq W^\omega$: the dual condition.

Lagrangian

$W=W^\omega\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \omega|_W=0\ \text{and}\ \dim W=n$

Maximal

A Lagrangian is a maximal isotropic subspace, dimension $n$.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Isotropic to Lagrangian
Subspaces are classified by how they sit inside their symplectic complement. Isotropic means contained in the complement, coisotropic means containing it, and Lagrangian means equal to it. A Lagrangian subspace is maximal isotropic and has exactly half the dimension, the central objects on which the form vanishes completely.

A Lagrangian Plane in $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$

Example

$L=\{p=0\},\quad \omega_0|_L=0,\quad L=L^\omega$

Split

$\mathbb{R}^{2n}=L\oplus L'$ into transverse Lagrangians.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106A Lagrangian Plane
In the model space the position plane is Lagrangian: the form vanishes on it because position directions never pair with each other. Its symplectic complement is itself, and the complementary momentum plane is a transverse Lagrangian. Splitting the space into two transverse Lagrangians recovers the position momentum decomposition.

The Lagrangian Grassmannian

Space of Lagrangians

$\Lambda(n)$ is a compact manifold of dimension $\tfrac{n(n+1)}2$.

Maslov

$\pi_1\Lambda(n)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ — winding is the Maslov index.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106The Lagrangian Grassmannian
The set of all Lagrangian subspaces forms a compact manifold, the Lagrangian Grassmannian. In dimension two it is a circle of lines through the origin, each line being its own complement. Its fundamental group is infinite cyclic, and the integer winding it detects is the Maslov index used throughout the subject.

Compatible Complex Structures

Complex structure

$J:V\to V$ with $J^2=-I$ makes $V$ a complex space.

Compatibility

$\omega(Jv,Jw)=\omega(v,w)$ and $\omega(v,Jv)>0$.

Induced metric

$g(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)$ is symmetric and positive definite

Contractible

The space $\mathcal{J}(\omega)$ of compatible $J$ is contractible.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Compatible Complex Structures
A complex structure squares to minus the identity, turning the space into a complex vector space. It is compatible with the form when the form is preserved and the pairing of a vector with its rotated image is a positive metric. This triangle of form, complex structure, and metric is the linear shadow of an almost Kahler structure.

$U(n)$ as a Triple Intersection

Three groups

Symplectic, orthogonal, and complex-linear maps.

Two of three

Any two of the conditions force the third.

Reduction

$U(n)=\mathrm{Sp}(2n)\cap O(2n)\cap GL(n,\mathbb{C})$

Canonical

Because $\mathcal{J}(\omega)$ is contractible, the choice is homotopically unique.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106U(n) as an Intersection
Fixing a compatible complex structure makes the symplectic, orthogonal, and complex linear groups overlap precisely in the unitary group. The unitary group is the intersection of any two of the three, and the space of compatible complex structures is contractible, so this reduction is canonical up to homotopy and carries no choices.

Symplectic Bundles & the First Chern Class

Symplectic bundle

Each fiber carries a varying form $\omega_x$.

Complexify

A compatible $J$ makes $E$ a complex bundle, canonically.

Invariant

$c_1(E,\omega):=c_1(E,J)\in H^2(B;\mathbb{Z})$, independent of $J$

Bridge

Linear data $\to$ a global topological invariant.

Printed pp. 37-93 · PDF pp. 50-106Bundles and the Chern Class
A symplectic vector bundle carries a smoothly varying symplectic form on each fiber. Choosing a compatible complex structure makes it a complex bundle, well defined up to homotopy because the choices are contractible. Its first Chern class is therefore a symplectic invariant, the bridge from this linear theory to global topology.
