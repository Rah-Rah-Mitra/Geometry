Definition of a Symplectic Manifold

Even-dimensional

Nondegeneracy forces $\dim M = 2n$.

Volume

$\omega^n$ is a canonical volume form.

Definition

$(M,\omega):\quad d\omega = 0,\ \ \omega^{\,n} \neq 0$

Orientable

The volume $\omega^n$ orients $M$ automatically.

Printed pp. 94-151Definition of a Symplectic Manifold
A symplectic manifold pairs an even-dimensional smooth manifold with a two-form that is simultaneously closed and nondegenerate. Closedness is a differential equation on the form, while nondegeneracy is a pointwise determinant condition. Together they force orientability and supply a canonical volume form built from omega.

Two Conditions, Two Roles

Nondegeneracy

$\omega$ identifies vectors with covectors.

Closedness

The integrability that preserves $\omega$ under flows.

Flat isomorphism

$\flat:\ TM \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } T^{*}M,\quad v \mapsto \iota_v\omega$

Why both

Drop either and the rigidity theory collapses.

Printed pp. 94-151Closed and Nondegenerate
Nondegeneracy means the form induces an isomorphism between tangent vectors and covectors at every point. Closedness, the vanishing exterior derivative, is the integrability condition that makes Hamiltonian flows preserve omega. Drop either property and the rich rigidity theory of later chapters simply collapses.

The Standard Model $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$

Constant form

Conjugate pairs $(q^i,p_i)$ wedge together.

Matrix $J$

Nondegeneracy is $\det J \neq 0$.

Darboux form

$\omega_0 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dq^i \wedge dp_i$

Reference

Every example is compared to this one.

Printed pp. 94-151The Standard Model
Euclidean space carries the constant Darboux form, a sum of wedge products of conjugate coordinate pairs. This standard model is the reference every other example is measured against. Its matrix is the block J, and nondegeneracy reduces to J being invertible everywhere on the space.

Cotangent Bundles $T^{*}Q$

Canonical forms

$\lambda = p\,dq,\qquad \omega = -d\lambda$

Phase space

The model for classical mechanics.

Zero section

$Q$ embeds as a Lagrangian.

Printed pp. 94-151Cotangent Bundles
Every cotangent bundle is symplectic through the tautological one-form, whose negative exterior derivative gives the canonical two-form. This construction is coordinate-free and governs classical phase space. The base manifold sits inside as a Lagrangian zero section, while fibers are themselves Lagrangian subspaces of the bundle.

A Wider Gallery of Examples

Surfaces

Printed pp. 94-151More Examples
Oriented surfaces are symplectic by their area form, since any nowhere-zero two-form on a surface is automatically closed. Complex projective space carries the Fubini-Study form, and Kahler manifolds supply a vast family where the symplectic and complex structures are compatible through a Riemannian metric.

Darboux's Theorem

Theorem

$\exists\,\varphi:\ \varphi^{*}\omega = \omega_0$ near each point

Consequence

No local invariants beyond $\dim M$.

Printed pp. 94-151Darboux's Theorem
Darboux's theorem is the structural heart of the chapter: near any point a symplectic form looks exactly like the standard model in suitable coordinates. Consequently symplectic geometry has no local invariants beyond the dimension. All interesting questions are therefore global, motivating the rigidity results that follow.

The Moser Argument

Interpolate

$\omega_t = \omega_0 + t(\omega_1-\omega_0)$.

Solve for $X_t$

Invert nondegeneracy on a primitive.

Homotopy equation

$\iota_{X_t}\omega_t = -\sigma,\qquad \tfrac{d}{dt}\psi_t^{*}\omega_t = 0$

Outcome

The flow $\psi_t$ pulls $\omega_1$ back to $\omega_0$.

Printed pp. 94-151The Moser Argument
Moser's trick proves Darboux and many isotopy results uniformly. One interpolates between two cohomologous forms and solves for a time-dependent vector field whose flow pulls one form back onto the other. The key algebraic step inverts nondegeneracy to define that vector field from a primitive.

Moser Stability

Theorem

$[\omega_t]\ \text{constant} \Rightarrow \psi_t^{*}\omega_t = \omega_0$

Obstruction

Only the cohomology class can obstruct.

Printed pp. 94-151Moser Stability
On a closed manifold, a smooth family of cohomologous symplectic forms is connected by ambient diffeomorphisms. The cohomology class is the only obstruction to deforming one form into another. This stability statement is what upgrades the pointwise Darboux normal form into a genuine global flexibility principle.

Submanifolds by Restriction

Symplectic

$\omega|_S$ stays nondegenerate.

Isotropic

$\omega|_S = 0$, so $TS \subseteq TS^{\omega}$.

Coisotropic

$TS^{\omega} \subseteq TS$.

Lagrangian

$\omega|_L = 0,\quad \dim L = \tfrac12 \dim M$

Printed pp. 94-151Submanifolds by Restriction
Submanifolds are classified by how omega restricts to their tangent spaces. Symplectic submanifolds keep nondegeneracy, isotropic ones kill the form, and coisotropic ones contain their own symplectic orthogonal. Lagrangians are the maximal isotropic case, sitting at exactly half the ambient dimension and central to everything later.

Weinstein's Lagrangian Neighborhood

Theorem

$\mathcal{N}(L) \;\cong\; \mathcal{N}\big(Q_0 \subset T^{*}L\big)$

Meaning

A Lagrangian's neighborhood is its own $T^{*}L$.

Printed pp. 94-151Weinstein's Neighborhood Theorem
Weinstein's theorem says a Lagrangian submanifold has a neighborhood symplectomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section in its own cotangent bundle. Thus the embedding carries no local invariants either; only the diffeomorphism type of the Lagrangian and its normal data matter for the local model.

Contact Structures: the Odd Cousin

Contact condition

$\alpha \wedge (d\alpha)^{n} \neq 0$ on $M^{2n+1}$

Where it appears

Hypersurfaces of contact type, boundaries.

Printed pp. 94-151Contact Structures
Contact structures are the odd-dimensional companions of symplectic geometry. A contact form satisfies a maximal nondegeneracy condition: alpha wedged with the top power of its exterior derivative never vanishes. Such forms arise naturally as boundary data and on hypersurfaces of contact type inside symplectic manifolds.
