The Arnold Conjecture

Fixed points of Hamiltonian maps obey a topological floor

The claim

A Hamiltonian symplectomorphism has at least as many fixed points as a function has critical points.

Why it matters

The count is forced by topology, not by the particular dynamics.

Printed pp. 417-456The Arnold Conjecture
This chapter spans printed pages 417 to 456 and centers on a single bold claim of Arnold: a Hamiltonian symplectomorphism of a closed manifold must have at least as many fixed points as a smooth function has critical points. We open by framing that count as a topological lower bound rather than an accident of dynamics.

From Functions to Fixed Points

Classical model

Gradient flow of $f$ has rest points exactly at the critical points of $f$.

Arnold's transport

Replace the gradient by a Hamiltonian flow; rest points become fixed points.

Lower bound

$\#\,\mathrm{Fix}(\phi_H) \;\ge\; \sum_{k} b_k(M)$

Reading

The sum of Betti numbers is the same floor Morse theory gives for functions.

Printed pp. 417-456From Functions to Fixed Points
Morse theory says any function on a closed manifold has critical points bounded below by the sum of Betti numbers. Arnold's insight transports this counting principle from gradients of functions to fixed points of Hamiltonian maps, asserting the same homological floor governs symplectic dynamics.

Statement of the Conjecture

Theorem (nondegenerate form)

$\#\,\mathrm{Fix}(\phi_H) \ge \sum_{k=0}^{\dim M} b_k(M;\mathbb{F})$

Degenerate form

The count is at least the cup-length plus one.

Nondegenerate

Every fixed point is transverse: $\det(\mathrm{id}-d\phi_H)\neq 0$.

Parallel

Mirrors the strong and weak Morse bounds for functions.

Printed pp. 417-456Statement of the Conjecture
We state the conjecture precisely: the number of fixed points of a Hamiltonian symplectomorphism is at least the sum of Betti numbers in the nondegenerate case. The degenerate cup-length bound is weaker but holds more generally, paralleling the two classical Morse lower bounds for functions.

The Torus Model

Betti numbers

For $T^2$: $b_0=1,\; b_1=2,\; b_2=1$, summing to four.

Critical points

One minimum, two saddles, one maximum: exactly four.

Check

$\#\mathrm{crit} = 4 \ge b_0+b_1+b_2 = 4$

Printed pp. 417-456The Torus Model
The torus gives the cleanest finite shadow of the conjecture. A height-style Morse function on the two-torus has one minimum, two saddles, and one maximum, four critical points matching the Betti sum one, two, one. This is the executable check carried by the notebook for this chapter.

Morse Inequalities

Weak form

$c_k \ge b_k$ for every index $k$.

Strong form

Alternating partial sums are bounded by Betti partial sums.

Strong inequality

$\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j} c_j \;\ge\; \sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j} b_j$

Outlook

Floer homology reproduces these inequalities in infinite dimensions.

Printed pp. 417-456Morse Inequalities
The Morse inequalities relate the number of critical points of index k to the Betti numbers, with the strong form bounding alternating sums. These inequalities are the precise mechanism that converts homology into a count, and Floer homology will reproduce them in infinite dimensions.

The Symplectic Action Functional

Domain

The free loop space $\mathcal{L}M$ of contractible loops $x:S^1\to M$.

Critical points

Exactly the one-periodic orbits of the Hamiltonian flow.

Action functional

$\mathcal{A}_H(x) = -\!\int_{D} \bar{x}^{*}\omega \;-\; \int_0^1 H_t(x(t))\,dt$

Equivalence

Periodic orbits $\leftrightarrow$ fixed points of the time-one map $\phi_H$.

Printed pp. 417-456The Symplectic Action Functional
On the free loop space we define the symplectic action, a functional whose critical points are exactly the one-periodic orbits of the Hamiltonian flow. Those orbits correspond to fixed points of the time-one map, recasting a dynamical counting problem as Morse theory on an infinite-dimensional space.

Action on the Loop Space

Landscape

The action is a Morse-style height over the loop space.

Gradient

Its flow lines solve Floer's perturbed Cauchy-Riemann equation.

Printed pp. 417-456Action on the Loop Space
We picture the action as a landscape over the loop space. Its critical points are the constant or periodic loops where the gradient vanishes, and the Morse picture suggests that homology of the loop space should bound their number, even though the functional is unbounded above and below.

The Conley-Zehnder Index

Problem

The action has infinite Morse index and coindex everywhere.

Fix

Grade orbits by a relative index built from the linearized flow.

Index

$\mu_{CZ}(x) \in \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \Psi:[0,1]\to \mathrm{Sp}(2n)$

Role

Replaces the Morse index as the grading of Floer generators.

Printed pp. 417-456The Conley-Zehnder Index
Because the action is neither bounded nor of finite index, ordinary Morse index fails. The Conley-Zehnder index assigns each nondegenerate periodic orbit an integer from the path of symplectic matrices its linearization traces, providing the grading that replaces the Morse index in Floer's construction.

Lagrangian Intersections

Setup

$L\subset M$ a closed Lagrangian; $\phi_H(L)$ its Hamiltonian image.

Claim

They must meet in at least the critical-point count for $L$.

Lagrangian form

$\#\big(L \cap \phi_H(L)\big) \;\ge\; \sum_k b_k(L)$

Analogy

Intersection points play the role of fixed points.

Printed pp. 417-456Lagrangian Intersections
The conjecture has a Lagrangian form: a closed Lagrangian submanifold and its image under a Hamiltonian isotopy must intersect in at least the number of critical points a function on the Lagrangian requires. Intersection points play the role fixed points did in the fixed-point version.

Forcing Intersections

Why forced

An exact Hamiltonian image cannot be displaced entirely off $L$.

Mechanism

Counting transverse meeting points is the geometric core.

Printed pp. 417-456Forcing Intersections
We visualize a Lagrangian and its Hamiltonian image. Energy considerations and the exactness of the displacement prevent the image from being pushed entirely off the original, forcing intersections that cannot be removed by isotopy. Counting these points is the geometric heart of the Lagrangian conjecture.

Floer Homology Overview

Generators

Periodic orbits graded by $\mu_{CZ}$.

Differential

Counts $J$-holomorphic cylinders between orbits.

Key theorem

$HF_*(M,H) \;\cong\; H_*(M)\;\Rightarrow\; \#\mathrm{Fix}(\phi_H)\ge \sum_k b_k$

Payoff

Infinite-dimensional Morse theory resolves the conjecture.

Printed pp. 417-456Floer Homology Overview
Floer homology realizes the loop-space Morse theory rigorously. Generators are periodic orbits graded by Conley-Zehnder index, the differential counts J-holomorphic cylinders between orbits, and the resulting homology equals the singular homology of the manifold, which yields the Betti-number lower bound Arnold predicted.
