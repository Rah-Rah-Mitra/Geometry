Existence and Classification of Structures

Existence

Which closed $M^{2n}$ admit a symplectic form $\omega$?

Deformation class

Classify forms up to deformation through symplectic forms.

Dimension four

Seiberg-Witten invariants give sharp obstructions.

Necessary conditions

$d\omega = 0,\quad \omega^n \neq 0,\quad [\omega]\in H^2(M;\mathbb{R})$

Open

In high dimension, deformation classification and uniqueness are largely unresolved; non-equivalent forms exist on the same manifold.

Printed pp. 549-573Existence and Classification of Structures
The first frontier asks which closed manifolds admit a symplectic form and how to classify them up to deformation. In dimension four gauge-theoretic invariants constrain existence sharply, but in higher dimensions the deformation classification is wide open, and uniqueness fails in subtle ways already known from exotic examples.

Topology of Symplectomorphism Groups

The object

$\mathrm{Symp}(M,\omega)$ and its Hamiltonian subgroup $\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)$.

Gromov's results

Homotopy type computed for $B^4$ and some rational surfaces.

Fibration tool

$\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)\hookrightarrow \mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)\twoheadrightarrow H^1(M;\mathbb{R})/\Gamma$

Open

For most $M$, even $\pi_0$ of $\mathrm{Symp}(M,\omega)$ — its connectedness — is unknown.

Printed pp. 549-573Topology of Symplectomorphism Groups
Here the object is an infinite-dimensional group rather than a manifold. We want the homotopy type of the symplectomorphism group and its Hamiltonian subgroup. Gromov computed these for the four-ball and some rational surfaces, yet for most manifolds even the connectedness of the symplectomorphism group remains genuinely unknown.

Lagrangians and the Nearby Conjecture

Nearby Lagrangian conjecture

$L\ \text{exact}\subset T^*B \;\Longrightarrow\; L \simeq_{\mathrm{Ham}} 0_B$

Known

Floer theory forces $L\to B$ to be a homotopy equivalence.

Open

Full Hamiltonian isotopy — and Lagrangian existence in general targets.

Printed pp. 549-573Lagrangians and the Nearby Conjecture
Lagrangian submanifolds carry the deepest existence and uniqueness questions. The nearby Lagrangian conjecture predicts that every closed exact Lagrangian in a cotangent bundle is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section. Floer theory forces it to be a homotopy equivalence onto the base, but full isotopy is still open.

Fano Manifolds and Donaldson Hypersurfaces

Fano

$c_1>0$: algebraic geometry meets symplectic uniqueness questions.

Donaldson divisor

Approximately holomorphic hypersurface dual to $k[\omega]$.

Donaldson hypersurface

$[\Sigma_k]=\mathrm{PD}\big(k[\omega]\big),\quad k\gg 0$

Payoff

Yields a symplectic Lefschetz pencil with controlled topology.

Open

Symplectic classification of Fano targets; uniqueness of pencils.

Printed pp. 549-573Fano Manifolds and Donaldson Hypersurfaces
Fano manifolds supply a rich testing ground where algebraic geometry meets symplectic topology, and their symplectic uniqueness is largely conjectural. Donaldson hypersurfaces provide approximately holomorphic divisors Poincare dual to a large multiple of the symplectic class, turning a symplectic manifold into a Lefschetz pencil with controlled topology.

Contact Geometry and Fillings

Contact boundary

$(Y^{2n-1},\xi)$ as the boundary of a symplectic $(W,\omega)$.

Tight vs overtwisted

The basic rigidity-flexibility dichotomy of contact structures.

Filling condition

$\partial(W,\omega)=(Y,\xi),\quad \xi=\ker\alpha,\;\; \omega|_Y=d\alpha$

Open

Which $\xi$ are fillable? How many fillings up to deformation? Classification in dimensions three and five.

Printed pp. 549-573Contact Geometry and Fillings
Contact manifolds are the odd-dimensional boundaries of symplectic ones. The open questions concern which contact structures arise as boundaries and how many symplectic fillings each admits. Tightness, overtwistedness, and the classification of fillings up to deformation drive much current research, especially in dimension three and five.

Continuous Symplectic Topology

The question

How much rigidity survives a uniform $C^0$ limit?

Symplectic homeomorphism

$C^0$-limit of symplectomorphisms; need not be smooth.

Gromov-Eliashberg rigidity

$\mathrm{Symp}(M,\omega)=\overline{\mathrm{Symp}(M,\omega)}^{\,C^0}\cap \mathrm{Diff}(M)$

Live questions

$C^0$-invariance of capacities; uniqueness of generating functions; Humilière-Leclercq-Seyfaddine results.

Printed pp. 549-573Continuous Symplectic Topology
This frontier asks how much symplectic rigidity survives under uniform limits. The Gromov-Eliashberg theorem says the symplectomorphism group is closed in the homeomorphism group, so symplecticity passes to continuous limits. Symplectic homeomorphisms, the Humiliere-Leclercq-Seyfaddine results, and C-zero capacity invariance are the live questions.

Symplectic Embedding Problems

The problem

When does domain $U$ symplectically embed into $V$?

Volume is not enough

Gromov nonsqueezing: $B^{2n}(r)\hookrightarrow Z^{2n}(R)$ needs $r\le R$.

Capacity obstruction

$U\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}V \;\Longrightarrow\; c(U)\le c(V)$

Ellipsoids

$E(a,b)\hookrightarrow B^4(R)$ is governed by McDuff-Schlenk's staircase function $c(a)$.

Printed pp. 549-573Symplectic Embedding Problems
Embedding questions ask when one domain symplectically embeds into another, a sharper test than volume alone. Gromov nonsqueezing already shows volume is not enough. Capacities give obstructions, and for ellipsoids into balls the answer is an intricate staircase function discovered by McDuff and Schlenk, still not fully understood.
Introduction to Symplectic TopologyChapter 14 · 14.8

Ball Packing and the Embedding Staircase

McDuff-Schlenk

$c(a)\ge \sqrt{a}\ \text{(volume)},\quad c(a)=\sqrt{a}\ \text{for}\ a\ge \tau^4$

Arithmetic

Steps indexed by Fibonacci numbers and the golden ratio $\tau$.

Open

Packing stability and packing numbers for higher targets.

Printed pp. 549-573Ball Packing and the Symplectic Camel
The ellipsoid embedding capacity exposes a Fibonacci staircase of obstructions before settling to the volume bound. Ball-packing stability and packing numbers for projective space encode the same arithmetic. We illustrate the staircase so students see that the obstruction is combinatorial, not merely a smooth volume inequality.

Embeddings into Euclidean Space

The question

Which $(M,\omega)$ embed in $(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\omega_0)$ with small codimension?

Flexibility

Gromov's $h$-principle grants embeddings above a threshold.

Codimension question

$(M^{2n},\omega)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},\omega_0),\quad N=?\,$

Tension

Whitney-type upper bounds vs. rigidity obstructions; the exact codimension where flexibility wins is open.

Printed pp. 549-573Embeddings into Euclidean Space
The final frontier asks which symplectic or contact manifolds embed into standard Euclidean or projective space with controlled codimension, and what flexibility Gromov's h-principle grants. Whitney-type bounds compete with rigidity phenomena, and the precise codimension at which flexibility takes over from rigidity is a recurring open theme.
