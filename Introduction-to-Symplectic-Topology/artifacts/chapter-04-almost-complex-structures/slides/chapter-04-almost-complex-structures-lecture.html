What Is an Almost Complex Structure?

Definition

An endomorphism $J:TM\to TM$ with $J^2=-\mathrm{id}$.

Each fibre

$J$ makes $T_pM$ a complex vector space; $i\cdot v := Jv$.

Constraint

Forces $\dim M$ to be even.

Defining equation

$$J \in \mathrm{End}(TM), \qquad J^2 = -\,\mathrm{id}_{TM}$$

Model

On $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$, $J_0(x,y)=(-y,x)$ is multiplication by $i$.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203What Is an Almost Complex Structure
A bundle endomorphism J of the tangent bundle squaring to minus the identity is the defining datum. On each fibre it acts like multiplication by i, so every tangent space becomes a complex vector space and the manifold must be even dimensional to admit one.

$J$ Rotates the Tangent Plane

Why the sign

$$J(Jv) = J^2 v = -v$$

Reading

$J$ is a complex multiplication, not a real reflection: it has no real eigenvalues.

Orientation

The pair $(v,Jv)$ orients each tangent plane.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203J Rotates the Tangent Plane
Geometrically J is a quarter turn of every tangent plane: applying it twice reverses a vector, which is exactly the equation J squared equals minus the identity. The picture of a vector rotated ninety degrees and then to its antipode is the whole intuition behind the algebra.

Taming and Compatibility

Tame

$\omega(v,Jv)>0$ for all $v\neq 0$.

Compatible

Tame and $\omega(Jv,Jw)=\omega(v,w)$.

Nesting

Compatible $\Rightarrow$ tame; compatibility is the stronger condition.

Taming positivity

$$\omega(v,Jv) > 0 \quad (v\neq 0)$$

Compatibility symmetry

$$\omega(Jv,Jw) = \omega(v,w)$$

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203Taming and Compatibility
A form tames J when omega of v with Jv is strictly positive on nonzero vectors, fixing an orientation of the planes. Compatibility adds symmetry, omega of Jv and Jw equals omega of v and w, so the two conditions sit nested with compatibility the stronger demand.

The Induced Riemannian Metric

Construction

Define $g(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)$ from a compatible $J$.

Symmetry

Compatibility makes $g$ symmetric.

Positivity

Taming makes $g$ positive definite.

The compatible triple

$$g(v,w) = \omega(v,Jw), \qquad \omega(v,w)=g(Jv,w)$$

Trinity

$(\omega, J, g)$ locks the symplectic, complex, and metric worlds to one another.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203The Induced Metric
Compatibility lets us define g of v and w as omega of v with Jw. Symmetry and positivity make g a genuine Riemannian metric, so a single triple omega, J, g locks together the symplectic, complex, and metric worlds. This is the central construction of the chapter.

The Space $\mathcal{J}(M,\omega)$

Nonempty

Every symplectic manifold admits a compatible $J$.

Contractible

The space deformation retracts to a point.

Consequence

Invariants built from $J$ ignore the choice.

Write J of M and omega for all compatible almost complex structures. For a fixed symplectic form this space is always nonempty and, crucially, contractible. Contractibility means any J can be deformed to any other, so invariants built from J never depend on the arbitrary choice.

Integrability & the Nijenhuis Tensor

Integrable

$J$ arises from holomorphic charts on $M$.

Obstruction

The Nijenhuis tensor $N_J$ measures the failure.

Not automatic

$J^2=-\mathrm{id}$ alone does not give integrability.

Nijenhuis tensor

$$N_J(X,Y) = [X,Y] + J[JX,Y] + J[X,JY] - [JX,JY]$$

Tensorial

$N_J$ is antisymmetric and depends only on values at a point.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203Integrability and the Nijenhuis Tensor
An almost complex structure is integrable when it comes from honest holomorphic charts. The obstruction is the Nijenhuis tensor N, an antisymmetric expression built from Lie brackets of J. When N vanishes identically the rotation field is rigid enough to assemble local complex coordinates.

The Newlander-Nirenberg Theorem

Theorem

For an almost complex structure $J$ the following are equivalent:

(1)

$J$ is integrable.

(2)

$N_J \equiv 0$.

(3)

$(M,J)$ is a complex manifold.

Equivalence

$$J\ \text{integrable} \iff N_J=0 \iff M\ \text{complex}$$

Depth

A pointwise tensor identity yields global holomorphic coordinate charts — a hard analytic theorem.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203Newlander-Nirenberg
The Newlander-Nirenberg theorem closes the loop: J is integrable, the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes, and the manifold is a genuine complex manifold are all equivalent statements. A hard analytic theorem turns a pointwise tensor identity into the global existence of holomorphic coordinate charts.

Kahler Manifolds

Definition

$\omega$ closed, $J$ integrable, and $(\omega,J)$ compatible.

Equivalent

$J$ is parallel: $\nabla J = 0$ for the Levi-Civita connection.

The triple

$g(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)$ is a Kahler metric.

Kahler conditions

$$d\omega = 0, \qquad N_J = 0, \qquad \nabla J = 0$$

Examples

$\mathbb{C}^n$, complex projective space $\mathbb{CP}^n$, smooth projective varieties.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203Kahler Manifolds
A Kahler manifold is the integrable compatible case: omega closed, J integrable, and the two compatible through g. Equivalently the complex structure is parallel for the Levi-Civita connection. Kahler geometry is the rich overlap where symplectic, complex, and Riemannian structures all coexist harmoniously.

Inclusion lattice

$$\text{compatible} \subset \text{tame } J,\quad \text{Kähler}=\text{closed}\,\cap\,\text{integrable}$$

Strictness

There exist symplectic manifolds with no Kahler structure and almost complex manifolds that are not integrable.

Picture three overlapping conditions. Symplectic plus complex compatible gives Kahler at their intersection. The inclusions of tame inside taming and compatible inside tame organize the structures into a lattice, with Kahler sitting at the most constrained and most fertile bottom corner.

Dimension Two Is Special

Claim

Every almost complex surface (real dimension $2$) is Kahler.

Reason

$N_J$ is forced to vanish — no room for an obstruction.

Effect

The almost-complex / complex distinction collapses.

Surface case

$$\dim_{\mathbb{R}} M = 2 \;\Longrightarrow\; N_J \equiv 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{Kähler}$$

Caution

This collapse is special to two real dimensions; in higher dimension integrability is a real constraint.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203Dimension Two Is Special
On a symplectic surface every compatible almost complex structure is automatically integrable, so every almost complex surface is Kahler. The Nijenhuis obstruction has no room to be nonzero in two real dimensions, collapsing the distinctions that genuinely matter in higher dimensions.

First View of $J$-Holomorphic Curves

Map

$u:(\Sigma,j)\to(M,J)$ from a Riemann surface.

Equation

The differential intertwines the two structures.

Why it matters

Counting these curves yields Gromov-Witten invariants.

Cauchy-Riemann condition

$$du \circ j = J \circ du, \qquad \bar\partial_J u = 0$$

Computation

The notebook tests holomorphicity through a vanishing residual on a disk map — the finite shadow of $\bar\partial_J u=0$.

Printed pp. 152-190 · PDF 165-203J-Holomorphic Curves
A J-holomorphic curve is a map u from a Riemann surface whose differential commutes with the structures, du composed with j equals J composed with du. This nonlinear Cauchy-Riemann equation launches the moduli theory that powers Gromov-Witten invariants and modern symplectic topology.
