The Variational Principle

Hamilton's principle

The true path makes the action $S$ stationary among nearby paths with fixed endpoints.

First variation

Set $\delta S = 0$, integrate by parts, and drop boundary terms.

Action functional

$S[q] = \displaystyle\int_{t_0}^{t_1} L\big(q,\dot q,t\big)\, dt$

Reading

Dynamics from one scalar, not a list of forces.

Printed pp. 11-36The Variational Principle
Mechanics begins not with forces but with a single number, the action integral of the Lagrangian. Hamilton's principle says the realized path makes the action stationary. Setting the first variation to zero, and integrating by parts with fixed endpoints, produces the Euler-Lagrange equations governing every classical motion.

Euler-Lagrange Equations

Stationarity

Vanishing first variation gives one equation per coordinate.

Newton recovered

For $L = \tfrac12 m\dot q^2 - V(q)$ this is $m\ddot q = -V'(q)$.

Equation of motion

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i} - \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q^i} = 0$

Order

A second-order system in the configuration $q$.

Printed pp. 11-36Euler-Lagrange Equations
The Euler-Lagrange equation is the stationarity condition written coordinate by coordinate. It is a second-order system in the configuration variable q. For a particle in a potential it reproduces Newton's law exactly, confirming that the variational viewpoint loses nothing while gaining a coordinate-free, geometric formulation we will exploit.

The Legendre Transform

Momentum

Define $p_i = \partial L / \partial \dot q^i$, the conjugate momentum.

Convex dual

When $L$ is convex in $\dot q$, the map $\dot q \mapsto p$ is invertible.

Printed pp. 11-36The Legendre Transform
To pass from velocities to momenta we apply the Legendre transform. The momentum p is the velocity-derivative of the Lagrangian, and the Hamiltonian is the corresponding convex dual. When this transform is invertible the velocity becomes a function of position and momentum, and the energy lands on phase space.

The Hamiltonian on Phase Space

Definition

$H = p_i\dot q^i - L$, re-expressed in $(q,p)$.

Phase space

The cotangent bundle $T^\ast Q$, coordinates $(q,p)$.

Energy landscape

$H(q,p) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,|p|^2 + V(q)$

Level sets

Constant-energy surfaces $\{H = E\}$ will carry the motion.

Printed pp. 11-36The Hamiltonian on Phase Space
The Hamiltonian H of position and momentum is an energy landscape on the cotangent bundle, our phase space. Its level sets are the constant-energy hypersurfaces. The whole dynamical story now lives on this even-dimensional space, where a single function will generate the flow that classical forces once dictated.

Hamilton's Equations

First order

One second-order system becomes a coupled first-order pair.

The minus sign

Antisymmetry between $\dot q$ and $\dot p$ drives every conservation law.

Canonical equations

$\dot q = \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p}, \qquad \dot p = -\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q}$

Compact form

$\dot z = J\,\nabla H$ with $z=(q,p)$.

Printed pp. 11-36Hamilton's Equations
The second-order Euler-Lagrange system splits into a first-order pair: q-dot equals the p-derivative of H, p-dot equals minus the q-derivative of H. This symmetric coupling is Hamilton's equations. The minus sign is the source of every conservation law and the rotation we will see preserving phase area.

The Symplectic Form

Standard form

$\omega_0 = \sum_i dp_i \wedge dq^i$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$.

Two properties

Closed, $d\omega = 0$; nondegenerate, $\omega(v,\cdot)=0 \Rightarrow v=0$.

Matrix model

$\omega_0(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T} J\, v,\quad J=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-I\\ I&0\end{psmallmatrix}$

What it does

Identifies vectors with covectors; pairs $\dot q$ against $\dot p$.

Printed pp. 11-36The Symplectic Form
On Euclidean phase space we equip the manifold with the standard two-form omega, the sum of dp wedge dq over all degrees of freedom. It is closed and nondegenerate. Nondegeneracy means it identifies vectors with covectors; closedness will later guarantee that flows preserving it form a group.

Hamiltonian Vector Fields

Defining equation

$\iota_{X_H}\,\omega = dH$ has a unique solution since $\omega$ is nondegenerate.

Coordinates

$X_H = J\,\nabla H$, reproducing Hamilton's equations.

Generator of flow

$\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \dot z = J\,\nabla H(z)$

Slogan

One function $H$ generates one flow.

Printed pp. 11-36Hamiltonian Vector Fields
The symplectic form turns the differential of H into a vector field. The defining equation, omega contracted with X_H equals dH, has a unique solution because omega is nondegenerate. In standard coordinates X_H is exactly J times the gradient of H, recovering Hamilton's equations in compact geometric form.
Introduction to Symplectic TopologyChapter 1

Flow Tangent to Level Sets

Energy conserved

$dH(X_H) = \omega(X_H,X_H) = 0$ by antisymmetry.

Geometry

$X_H$ is everywhere tangent to $\{H=E\}$.

Printed pp. 11-36Flow Tangent to Level Sets
Because omega is antisymmetric, applying dH to X_H gives zero, so H is constant along its own flow. Geometrically the Hamiltonian vector field is everywhere tangent to the energy level sets. Energy conservation is not an accident here; it is forced by the antisymmetry of the symplectic form itself.

Symplectomorphisms Preserve Area

Definition

$\phi$ is symplectic when $\phi^\ast\omega = \omega$ (a canonical transformation).

Liouville

In 2D, signed area is preserved; in $2n$, the volume $\omega^n$ is fixed.

Printed pp. 11-36Symplectomorphisms Preserve Area
A diffeomorphism that pulls omega back to itself is a symplectomorphism, the modern name for a canonical transformation. The central computation of the chapter shows that every Hamiltonian flow is symplectic, so it preserves omega and, in two dimensions, signed area. Liouville's theorem is the immediate corollary.

The Poisson Bracket

Pairing observables

$\{f,g\}$ feeds $g$ through the Hamiltonian field of $f$.

Lie algebra

Bilinear, antisymmetric, satisfies the Jacobi identity.

Definition and motion

$\{f,g\} = \sum_i \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial q^i}\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial p_i} - \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial q^i}$

Conservation

$\dot f = \{f,H\}$; so $f$ is conserved iff $\{f,H\}=0$.

Printed pp. 11-36The Poisson Bracket
The Poisson bracket pairs two observables into a third by feeding one through the Hamiltonian vector field of the other. It makes smooth functions a Lie algebra. A quantity is conserved exactly when its bracket with H vanishes, turning the search for constants of motion into pure bracket algebra.

Printed pp. 11-36First Global Questions
Local theory is now complete, so we ask global questions. Must an area-preserving map of an annulus have fixed points? Must energy surfaces carry periodic orbits? These innocent-looking questions, unanswerable by local algebra, are exactly where symplectic topology begins and where the rest of the book lives.
