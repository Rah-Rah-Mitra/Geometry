Local Models with Corners

The model space

Charts take values in $\mathbb{H}^n_k=[0,\infty)^k\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}$.

Depth $k$

The integer $k$ counts how many boundary half-lines meet at a point.

Corner model

$$\mathbb{H}^n_k=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:\ x_1\ge 0,\dots,x_k\ge 0\}$$

Smoothness test

A chart map is smooth iff it is the restriction of a $C^\infty$ map on an open neighborhood in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Local Models with Corners
Manifolds with corners are modeled on quadrant-type sets, the product of half-lines with a Euclidean factor. We test smoothness by demanding that, in every chart, a map extend smoothly across the boundary into the open ambient space, so that derivatives of all orders exist one-sidedly and agree across faces.

Why Corners Appear

Products

$M\times N$ with $\partial M,\partial N$ has a corner along $\partial M\times\partial N$.

Compactifications

Moduli of curves break: several gluing parameters hit zero together.

Cylindrical ends

Stretching necks produces stratified boundary structure.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Why Corners Appear
Corners are not pathology; they are forced. A product of two manifolds with boundary already has a corner along the product of the boundaries, and compactified moduli spaces of holomorphic curves carry strata where several breaking parameters vanish at once. The appendix exists to handle these spaces honestly rather than excise them.

Strata of a Corner Chart

Stratification

$$\mathbb{H}^2_2=\underbrace{\{0\}}_{\text{depth }2}\ \cup\ \underbrace{\text{two edges}}_{\text{depth }1}\ \cup\ \underbrace{\text{interior}}_{\text{depth }0}$$

Why it matters

Smoothness on the full quadrant exceeds smoothness on each stratum: cross-derivatives must match at the corner.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Strata of a Corner Chart
The quadrant decomposes into strata by counting vanishing coordinates: the open interior, two boundary edges where one coordinate is zero, and the single corner point where both vanish. Smoothness on the whole quadrant is strictly stronger than smoothness restricted to each stratum, since mixed cross-derivatives must match at the corner.

Smoothness via Charts

Definition

$F:M\to N$ is smooth if each chart representative extends to $C^\infty$ on an open set.

Chart independence

Transition maps are smooth in this sense, so the property does not depend on the atlas.

Local criterion

$$\psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}=\big.\widetilde{G}\big|_{\mathbb{H}^m_k},\qquad \widetilde{G}\in C^\infty(\text{open})$$

Equivalent form

All partial derivatives extend continuously to every boundary face.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Smoothness via Charts
A map between manifolds with corners is smooth when every local representative, read through charts, is the restriction of a genuinely smooth map on an open neighborhood. Equivalently, all partial derivatives extend continuously to the boundary faces. This definition is chart-independent because the transition maps are themselves smooth in exactly this sense.

The Extension Lemma

Theorem (Extension)

If $A\subset M$ is closed and $F$ is smooth on an open set $U\supset A$, then $F$ extends to a smooth map on a neighborhood of $A$ that agrees with the given data along $A$.

Submanifold version

A map smooth along a submanifold $S$ extends smoothly to a neighborhood of $S$.

Proof roadmap

  • Cover $A$ by charts where $F$ has local smooth extensions.
  • Choose a partition of unity subordinate to that cover.
  • Glue the local extensions with the partition weights.
  • The weighted sum is smooth and restricts to $F$ on $A$.
Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595The Extension Lemma
The central technical result: a map smooth on an open set containing a closed subset, or defined along a submanifold, extends to a smooth map on a neighborhood of that set. The proof patches local Taylor data with a partition of unity, so no single chart ever needs to see the whole subset at once.

Extending Sections of Bundles

Beyond functions

The same patching extends sections $s:A\to E$ of a vector bundle.

Cutoff trick

Multiply each local section by a bump supported near $A$ before summing.

Section extension

$$\tilde s=\sum_\alpha \rho_\alpha\, s_\alpha,\qquad \tilde s\big|_A=s$$

Where it is used

Propagating vector fields, metrics, and almost complex structures outward from a closed set.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Extending Sections
The extension principle applies to sections of vector bundles, not only to functions. A section defined over a closed subset or submanifold extends to a smooth global section after multiplying by a cutoff supported near the set. This is exactly how vector fields and almost complex structures are propagated outward in the main text.

The Standard Smooth Function

Definition

$$f(x)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/x},&x>0\\[2pt]0,&x\le 0\end{cases}$$

Flatness

Every derivative at $0$ vanishes: $f^{(n)}(0)=0$ for all $n$, yet $f\not\equiv 0$.

Why smooth

$f^{(n)}(x)=P_n(1/x)\,e^{-1/x}$; exponential decay beats polynomial growth.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595The Standard Smooth Function
Everything rests on one function: exponential of minus one over x for positive x and zero otherwise. It is smooth at the origin because every derivative is a rational expression times the exponential, which decays faster than any polynomial blows up, forcing all one-sided derivatives to vanish at zero.

Bump and Cutoff Functions

Cutoff from $f$

$$g(x)=\frac{f(x)}{f(x)+f(1-x)}\in[0,1]$$

On balls and collars

Products $\prod_i g(x_i)$ give plateaus over balls and over quadrant collars at corners.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Bump and Cutoff Functions
From the standard function we build a smooth cutoff: flat at one near the origin, descending, then flat at zero outside a chosen radius. Multiplying one-variable bumps gives plateaus on balls and on quadrant collars near corners, providing the localizers that every patching argument in this book silently uses.

Partitions of Unity

Theorem

For any open cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ of a manifold with corners there exist smooth $\rho_\alpha\ge 0$ with $\operatorname{supp}\rho_\alpha\subset U_\alpha$, locally finite, and $\sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha\equiv 1$.

Ingredients

Paracompactness gives a locally finite refinement; bumps supply each $\rho_\alpha$, even across faces and corners.

Normalization

$$\rho_\alpha=\frac{\chi_\alpha}{\sum_\beta\chi_\beta},\qquad \sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha=1$$

Local finiteness

Each point meets finitely many supports, so the sum is a finite, hence smooth, expression.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Partitions of Unity
Subordinate to any open cover of a manifold with corners there is a smooth partition of unity: nonnegative bumps, each supported in one cover element, summing to one. Local finiteness makes the sum well defined, and the cutoff construction supplies the individual functions even across boundary faces and corners.

Gluing Local Objects into Global Ones

The averaging move

Weight locally defined objects by $\rho_\alpha$ and sum to obtain one global smooth object.

Examples

Riemannian metrics, connections, and compatible almost complex structures are built this way.

Global from local

$$g=\sum_\alpha \rho_\alpha\, g_\alpha,\qquad \omega=\sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha\,\omega_\alpha$$

Caveat

Convex averaging preserves positivity (metrics) but not closedness; closed forms need a finer argument.

Printed pp. 574-582 · PDF 587-595Gluing Local to Global
Partitions of unity convert agreement on overlaps into a single global object. Locally defined smooth maps, metrics, or forms are averaged with the partition weights to produce a global smooth object that restricts correctly. This averaging is the recurring move behind existence theorems for the structures studied in the book.
