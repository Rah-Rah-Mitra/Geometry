Symplectic and Hamiltonian Actions

Symplectic action

Each $g$ preserves $\omega$: a homomorphism into $\mathrm{Symp}(M,\omega)$.

Hamiltonian action

Each generator $X_M$ is globally Hamiltonian.

Generator

$\iota_{X_M}\,\omega = d H_X \quad (X \in \mathfrak{g})$

Obstruction

The gap is measured in $H^1(M)$ and a Lie-algebra cohomology class.

Printed pp. 191-251Symplectic and Hamiltonian Actions
An action is symplectic when every group element preserves the form, and Hamiltonian when each infinitesimal generator is a globally Hamiltonian vector field. The gap between these notions is measured by cohomology classes, and only Hamiltonian actions admit the moment map machinery developed across the rest of the chapter.

The Moment Map

Definition

A map $\mu : M \to \mathfrak{g}^*$ pairing to Hamiltonians.

One bundle

All component Hamiltonians at once.

Defining identity

$d\langle \mu, X\rangle = \iota_{X_M}\,\omega$

Reading it

$\langle\mu,X\rangle$ is the Hamiltonian generating the flow of $X$.

Printed pp. 191-251The Moment Map
The moment map sends the manifold into the dual of the Lie algebra so that pairing it with any generator yields that generator's Hamiltonian function. The defining identity packages every component Hamiltonian at once, and its differential recovers the contraction of the fundamental vector field with omega.

Equivariance and Conservation

Equivariance

$\mu$ intertwines the action with the coadjoint action.

Noether

$\mu$ is conserved along the Hamiltonian flow.

Coadjoint equivariance

$\mu(g\cdot x) = \mathrm{Ad}^*_{g}\,\mu(x)$

Abelian case

Components Poisson-commute when $G$ is a torus.

Printed pp. 191-251Equivariance and Conservation
A moment map is equivariant when it intertwines the group action with the coadjoint action on the dual algebra. Equivariance makes the level sets group-invariant, so the moment map components Poisson-commute exactly when the group is abelian, giving the conserved quantities driving Noether's theorem.

Example: The Circle on the Sphere

Action

$S^1$ rotates $S^2$ about its vertical axis.

Moment map

$\mu = z$, the height function.

Image

The interval $[-1,1]$ between the poles.

Printed pp. 191-251The Circle on the Sphere
Rotation of the two-sphere about its vertical axis preserves the area form, and its moment map is simply the height function. The poles are the fixed points, the regular level sets are latitude circles, and the moment image is the closed height interval between the two poles.

Linear and Torus Actions

Phase rotation

$T^n$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^n$ by coordinatewise phases.

Squared modulus

Each component records half $|z_k|^2$.

Standard moment map

$\mu(z) = \tfrac12\big(|z_1|^2,\dots,|z_n|^2\big)$

Image

The positive orthant; on $\mathbb{CP}^n$ a bounded simplex.

Printed pp. 191-251Linear and Torus Actions
For the standard torus acting on complex space by phase rotations, the moment map records half the squared modulus in each coordinate. Its image is the positive orthant, and on projective space the constraint of fixed total radius cuts this orthant down to a bounded simplex.

Symplectic Reduction

Marsden-Weinstein

Cut $\mu^{-1}(0)$, then divide by $G$.

Hypotheses

$0$ a regular value, action free and proper.

Reduced space

$M /\!/ G = \mu^{-1}(0)\,/\,G$

Result

A smooth symplectic manifold $(M/\!/G,\,\omega_{\mathrm{red}})$.

Printed pp. 191-251Symplectic Reduction
Marsden and Weinstein showed that the zero level set of an equivariant moment map, divided by the group, is again a symplectic manifold. When zero is a regular value and the action is free, the reduced space inherits a unique form pulling back to the restriction of omega on the level set.

The Reduced Symplectic Form

Characterization

$\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ pulls back to $\omega$ on the level set.

Dimension

Drops by $2\dim G$.

Defining relation

$\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{red}} = \iota^*\omega$

Why twice

One direction killed by the constraint, one by the orbit.

Printed pp. 191-251The Reduced Symplectic Form
On the quotient the reduced form is characterized by the condition that its pullback along the quotient projection equals the pullback of omega to the level set. Dimension drops by twice the group dimension, since one direction is killed by the constraint and one by the group orbit.

Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg Convexity

Theorem

For a torus on compact connected $M$, $\mu(M)$ is convex.

Vertices

Images of the fixed points.

Fibres

Each fibre over an interior point is connected.

Printed pp. 191-251Convexity Theorem
For a torus acting on a compact connected symplectic manifold, the image of the moment map is a convex polytope. Its vertices are the images of the fixed points, and each fibre over an interior point is connected, a striking rigidity hidden inside the smooth dynamics.

The Moment Polytope

$\mathbb{CP}^2$

A triangle: three coordinate fixed points.

$S^2\times S^2$

A square: product of two intervals.

Printed pp. 191-251The Moment Polytope
The moment polytope is the convex hull of the fixed-point images. Complex projective plane gives a triangle whose three vertices are the coordinate fixed points, while the product of two spheres gives a square. The polytope encodes the manifold's toric combinatorics in a single picture.

Delzant's Theorem and Toric Manifolds

Toric manifold

$\dim T = \tfrac12\dim M$: a maximal torus action.

Classification

Manifolds $\leftrightarrow$ Delzant polytopes.

Delzant conditions

simple, rational, smooth at each vertex

Reconstruction

Each polytope rebuilds $(M,\omega,T,\mu)$ uniquely.

Printed pp. 191-251Delzant's Theorem
Delzant classified compact toric symplectic manifolds by their polytopes. A polytope is admissible when it is simple, rational, and smooth at every vertex, and each such Delzant polytope reconstructs a unique toric manifold together with its torus action and moment map.

Links to Geometric Invariant Theory

Kempf-Ness

Symplectic quotient $=$ GIT quotient of $G^{\mathbb{C}}$.

Selection

$\mu = 0$ picks minimal-norm points in each orbit.

Quotient identity

$\mu^{-1}(0)/G \;\cong\; M^{\mathrm{ss}}/\!/\,G^{\mathbb{C}}$

Stability

Zero level $\leftrightarrow$ polystable orbits.

Printed pp. 191-251Geometric Invariant Theory
The Kempf-Ness theorem identifies the symplectic quotient with the geometric invariant theory quotient of the complexified group. Vanishing of the moment map selects, within each closed complex orbit, the points of minimal norm, matching polystable orbits and tying reduction to stability in algebraic geometry.
