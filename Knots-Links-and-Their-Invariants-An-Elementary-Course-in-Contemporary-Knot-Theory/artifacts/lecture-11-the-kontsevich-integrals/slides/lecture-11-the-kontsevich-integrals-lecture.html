Printed pp. 95-102Knots in Slices
Place the knot in space so the height function is Morse, with finitely many critical points. At each height the knot meets a horizontal plane in finitely many points, giving a configuration that evolves continuously with height. Pairs of strands at each level are the data integrated to build the invariant.

The Iterated Integral

Sum over chords

One term for each number of paired strands.

Signs

Local orientation supplies the sign of each term.

Schematic

$Z(K)=\sum_m \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^m}\!\int \cdots\, D$

Values

A formal series of chord diagrams.

Printed pp. 95-102The Iterated Integral
Define the integral as a sum over the number of chords, integrating over increasing heights where chosen pairs of strands are joined. Each term carries a chord diagram recording which strands are paired and a sign from local orientation. The result is a formal series valued in the algebra of chord diagrams.

Invariance and Correction

Partial invariance

Stable under Morse-preserving deformations.

Defect

Critical points where strands turn around spoil it.

Correction

Divide by the integral of a standard hump.

Well defined

Convergence and the four-term relation secure it.

Printed pp. 95-102Invariance and Correction
Show the raw integral is invariant under deformations that keep the Morse structure but changes at critical points where strands turn around. Dividing by the integral of a standard unknotted hump corrects this, yielding a genuine isotopy invariant. Convergence and the four-term relation make every step well defined.

Universality

Theorem

Every finite type invariant factors through $Z$.

Composition

Apply a weight system to recover any invariant.

Factorization

$v = W\circ Z$

Consequence

Weight systems and Vassiliev invariants coincide.

Printed pp. 95-102Universality
State the central theorem: the Kontsevich integral is the universal Vassiliev invariant, meaning every finite type invariant is obtained by composing it with some weight system. Combined with the realization theorem, this proves that weight systems and Vassiliev invariants coincide, closing the loop opened in the previous lectures.
