Printed pp. 103-110Seifert Surfaces and Genus
Every oriented knot bounds an orientable surface, a Seifert surface, constructed by Seifert's algorithm from any diagram. The least genus among such surfaces is the knot genus, an additive invariant under connected sum. From the surface one reads off the Alexander polynomial via the Seifert matrix and homology of the complement.

Printed pp. 103-110Hyperbolic Knots
Thurston showed that the complement of most knots admits a complete hyperbolic metric of finite volume. By Mostow rigidity this metric is unique, so its volume and other geometric quantities are honest knot invariants. Hyperbolic geometry classifies knots far more finely than crossing number and connects to deep number theory.

Printed pp. 103-110Knot Homologies
Categorification replaces a polynomial invariant by a graded homology theory whose Euler characteristic recovers it. Khovanov homology categorifies the Jones polynomial and knot Floer homology categorifies the Alexander polynomial. These richer invariants detect the unknot, bound the genus, and carry information the polynomials alone cannot see.

Printed pp. 103-110Quantum Invariants and Physics
Witten interpreted the Jones polynomial through Chern-Simons quantum field theory, expressing it as a path integral over connections. This physical picture spawned a whole family of quantum invariants from quantum groups and conformal field theory, and explains the otherwise mysterious appearance of the Jones polynomial in statistical mechanics.
