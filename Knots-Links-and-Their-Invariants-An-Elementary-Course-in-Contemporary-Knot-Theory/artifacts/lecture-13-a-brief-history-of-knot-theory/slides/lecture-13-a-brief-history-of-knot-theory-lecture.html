Printed pp. 111-131Vortex Atoms
In the eighteen sixties Lord Kelvin proposed that atoms were knotted vortices in the ether, with different elements corresponding to different knots. Though the physics was wrong, it motivated Tait and others to begin classifying knots seriously, giving the subject its first sustained mathematical attention and a surprisingly fruitful false start.

Printed pp. 111-131Tabulation and Tait
Tait, Kirkman, and Little compiled the first knot tables by crossing number and formulated conjectures about alternating diagrams. These empirical observations, including claims about minimal crossings and writhe, stood unproven for a century until the Jones polynomial finally supplied the tools to settle them in the nineteen eighties.

Printed pp. 111-131Topological Foundations
In the twentieth century Reidemeister, Alexander, and others recast knot theory rigorously within topology. Reidemeister moves formalized diagram equivalence, the Alexander polynomial gave the first algebraic invariant, and the study of knot groups and covering spaces embedded knots firmly in the developing field of algebraic topology.

The Polynomial Revolution

Jones, 1984

A new polynomial from operator algebras.

Descendants

Printed pp. 111-131The Polynomial Revolution
Jones's discovery in nineteen eighty four of a new polynomial, born from operator algebras, ignited an explosion of activity. It resolved the Tait conjectures, linked knots to statistical mechanics and quantum field theory, and led through Vassiliev, Kontsevich, Khovanov, and others to the rich modern landscape of quantum and categorified invariants.
