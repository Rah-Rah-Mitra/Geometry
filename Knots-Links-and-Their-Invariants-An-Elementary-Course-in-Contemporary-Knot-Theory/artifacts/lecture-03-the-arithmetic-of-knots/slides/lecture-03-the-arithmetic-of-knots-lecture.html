Connected Sum

The operation

Cut each knot open and splice the loose ends into a single knot.

Well defined

With attention to orientation the result is unambiguous.

Notation

$K_1\,\#\,K_2$

Algebra

The operation is commutative and associative, like multiplication.

Printed pp. 21-28Connected Sum
Define the connected sum of two knots: cut each open and splice the loose ends together to form a single knot. With care about orientation this operation is well defined, commutative, and associative. It is the basic way to build complicated knots from simpler ones and the analogue of multiplying numbers.

The Unknot as Identity

Identity

Adding the unknot changes nothing, so it plays the role of one.

No inverses

You can never untie a nontrivial knot by adding another.

Identity law

$K\,\#\,\text{unknot}=K$

Why rigid

Irreversibility, proved via genus, makes the arithmetic interesting.

Printed pp. 21-28The Unknot as Identity
Observe that adding the unknot to any knot leaves it unchanged, so the unknot is the identity for connected sum. Crucially there are no inverses: you can never untie a nontrivial knot by adding another knot. This irreversibility, proved using genus, is what makes the arithmetic interesting and rigid.

Prime Knots

Definition

A nontrivial knot that is not a connected sum of two nontrivial knots.

Examples

The trefoil and figure-eight are prime.

Building blocks

Prime knots are the indivisible pieces of the theory.

Tables

Knot censuses list primes by minimal crossing number.

Printed pp. 21-28Prime Knots
Call a nontrivial knot prime when it cannot be written as a connected sum of two nontrivial knots. The trefoil and figure-eight are prime, and prime knots are the indivisible building blocks. Knot tables are organized by listing prime knots according to their minimal crossing number, the standard census of the subject.

Unique Factorization

Schubert's theorem

Every knot factors into primes, uniquely up to order.

The engine

Additivity of the genus forbids infinite descent.

Structure

$K=P_1\,\#\cdots\#\,P_n$

Monoid

Knots form a free commutative monoid on the primes, like the integers.

Printed pp. 21-28Unique Factorization
State the theorem of Schubert: every knot factors as a connected sum of prime knots, and this factorization is unique up to order. The proof rests on additivity of the genus, which forbids infinite descent. Knots thus form a free commutative monoid on the primes, exactly mirroring the integers under multiplication.
