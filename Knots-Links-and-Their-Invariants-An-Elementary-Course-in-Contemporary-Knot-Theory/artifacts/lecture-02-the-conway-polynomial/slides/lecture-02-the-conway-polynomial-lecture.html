One equation

Printed pp. 11-20The Skein Relation
Introduce the skein relation, a local rule connecting the three diagrams that differ only at one crossing: the over-crossing, the under-crossing, and the smoothed version. The Conway polynomial of these three is tied together by a single linear equation in a variable z. This local relation is the engine of the whole computation.

Normalization

Base case

The unknot is assigned the value one.

Uniqueness

Skein relation plus this base case pins the polynomial down completely.

Anchor

$\nabla(\text{unknot})=1$

Termination

Changing or smoothing crossings reduces any diagram to known unlinks.

Printed pp. 11-20Normalization
Fix the value on the unknot to be one. Together with the skein relation this normalization pins down the polynomial uniquely. Any crossing can be changed or smoothed using the relation, gradually simplifying any diagram down to unknots and unlinks whose values are already known, so the recursion always terminates.

Computing Examples

The trefoil

The skein recursion yields a nonconstant polynomial.

The Hopf link

A short computation gives a value proportional to $z$.

Trefoil

$\nabla(\text{trefoil})=z^2+1$

Payoff

The trefoil is provably not the unknot, our first real theorem.

Printed pp. 11-20Computing Examples
Work through the trefoil and the Hopf link. Applying the skein relation at a chosen crossing expresses each in terms of simpler diagrams, and the recursion yields explicit polynomials. The trefoil gives a nonconstant answer, immediately proving it is not the unknot, the first real payoff of an algebraic invariant.

Why It Is Well Defined

The worry

The recursion must not depend on which crossings we pick or their order.

Conway's insight

Consistency is verified against the Reidemeister moves.

Conclusion

The result is a genuine invariant, not a mere procedure.

Lesson

Well-definedness is exactly what makes a recipe a definition.

Printed pp. 11-20Why It Is Well Defined
Address the subtle point: the skein recursion must give the same answer regardless of which crossings are chosen and in which order. Conway's insight is that consistency can be checked against the Reidemeister moves, guaranteeing the result is a true invariant. This well-definedness is what separates a definition from a mere procedure.
