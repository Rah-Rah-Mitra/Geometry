The Braid Group

Strands

Descending strings join $n$ top points to $n$ bottom points.

Operation

Stacking braids gives an associative product.

Group

$B_n,\quad e=\text{trivial braid}$

Inverses

Flip a braid upside down to undo it.

Printed pp. 57-68The Braid Group
Define a braid on n strands as a collection of descending strings joining n top points to n bottom points, considered up to isotopy. Stacking braids defines a group operation, with the identity the trivial braid and inverses obtained by flipping upside down. This is Artin's braid group on n strands.

Generators and Relations

Generators

Each $\sigma_i$ swaps adjacent strands $i$ and $i+1$.

Commuting

Distant swaps commute: $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i$ for $|i-j|\ge 2$.

Braid relation

$\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$

Presentation

These relations define the whole group.

Printed pp. 57-68Generators and Relations
Present the standard generators, each swapping two adjacent strands with a fixed crossing sign. They satisfy two relations: distant swaps commute, and adjacent generators obey the braid relation. These finitely many relations present the entire braid group and encode all isotopies between braids in a purely algebraic form.

Closing a Braid

Closure

Join top endpoints to bottom endpoints by side arcs.

Result

The braid becomes an oriented link diagram.

Alexander's theorem

Every link is the closure of some braid.

Consequence

Braids capture all of knot and link theory.

Printed pp. 57-68Closing a Braid
Describe the closure: join each top endpoint to the corresponding bottom endpoint by arcs around the side, turning a braid into an oriented link diagram. Alexander's theorem asserts that every link arises as the closure of some braid, so braids capture all of knot and link theory.

Markov's Theorem

Two moves

Conjugation and stabilization relate braids.

Statement

Closures agree exactly when the braids are Markov equivalent.

Stabilization

$\beta \in B_n \ \leftrightarrow\ \beta\sigma_n^{\pm1}\in B_{n+1}$

Payoff

Topology of links becomes algebra of braid words.

Printed pp. 57-68Markov's Theorem
State Markov's theorem: two braids, possibly on different numbers of strands, have isotopic closures exactly when they are related by a finite sequence of conjugations and stabilizations. This converts the topological problem of equivalence of links into the algebraic problem of these two Markov moves on braid words.
