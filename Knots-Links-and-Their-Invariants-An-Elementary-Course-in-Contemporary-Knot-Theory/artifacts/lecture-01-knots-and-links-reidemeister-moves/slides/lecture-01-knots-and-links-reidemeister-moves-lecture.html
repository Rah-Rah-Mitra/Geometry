Knots and Links

Knot

A smooth embedding of a single circle into three-space.

Link

Several disjoint circles tangled together, such as the Hopf link.

The unknot

The trivial circle; deciding when a knot is secretly unknotted is the core question.

Examples

The trefoil and figure-eight are the workhorses of every later construction.

Printed pp. 1-10Knots and Links
Define a knot as a smooth embedding of a circle into three-space, and a link as several disjoint circles tangled together. The unknot is the trivial circle, and the central question is to decide when a complicated-looking knot is secretly trivial. Examples like the trefoil and the Hopf link anchor the whole subject.

Knot Diagrams

Projection

Flatten a knot onto a plane, recording transverse crossings.

Over and under

Each crossing is marked to show which strand passes above.

Combinatorial

Diagrams turn a spatial problem into bookkeeping with pictures on paper.

Working tool

Nearly all of knot theory manipulates diagrams, not embeddings.

Printed pp. 1-10Knot Diagrams
Project a knot onto a plane to get a diagram, a curve with finitely many transverse crossings, each marked to show which strand passes over. Diagrams turn a spatial problem into a combinatorial one on paper. Almost all of knot theory is carried out by manipulating these pictures rather than the embeddings themselves.

The Reidemeister Moves

Three local moves

A twist (I), a poke (II), and a slide (III).

Reidemeister's theorem

Two diagrams give the same knot exactly when related by these moves and planar isotopy.

Printed pp. 1-10The Reidemeister Moves
Introduce the three local moves: a twist, a poke, and a slide. Reidemeister's theorem says two diagrams represent the same knot exactly when one can be turned into the other by a finite sequence of these moves plus planar isotopy. This reduces equivalence of knots to a finite local calculus.

Invariants from Moves

The strategy

Attach a quantity to a diagram that survives all three moves.

Knot invariant

Such a quantity then depends only on the knot, not the picture.

Distinguishing knots

Different invariant values prove two knots are genuinely distinct.

The recipe

Check invariance move by move; this method recurs throughout the course.

Printed pp. 1-10Invariants from Moves
Explain the strategy that drives the course. To prove two knots are different, attach a quantity to a diagram that is unchanged by all three Reidemeister moves; such a knot invariant then depends only on the knot. Checking invariance move by move is the recurring method behind every invariant we build.
