Motivation
height interpolation

Terrain Samples To A Polyhedral Surface

A triangulation chooses which measured heights share a planar facet; the same samples can produce very different interpolated surfaces.

Model

A terrain is a height function over a planar domain. With finitely many samples, a triangulation supplies the domains for linear interpolation.

Why Triangles?

  • Each facet is determined by three lifted samples.
  • Shared edges keep the interpolated surface continuous.
  • Triangle shape controls which distant heights interact.

Context Only

The paraboloid/lower-hull viewpoint is useful background, but this lecture centers the planar duality and flip algorithm.

Use the terrain story only long enough to motivate why a triangulation matters. Heights are known at sample points, so a triangulation chooses which triples define planar facets between samples. The geometric quality of those triangles changes the interpolated surface even when all measured heights are identical at the vertices.
Local Intuition
one quadrilateral, two surfaces

A Bad Flip Can Manufacture A Bad Ridge

An edge flip changes only one quadrilateral, but it can force interpolation through long skinny triangles.

Failure Mode

A bad diagonal lets a query point be controlled by samples that are geometrically far away across the feature.

Angle Heuristic

Long skinny triangles expose small angles. The chapter turns this visual discomfort into a lexicographic angle objective.

Local Move

Flip one diagonal of a convex quadrilateral. If the local angle vector improves, the old edge was the wrong local choice.

Focus attention on one quadrilateral. Two diagonal choices interpolate the same four heights, but one diagonal may create long, skinny triangles whose planes connect distant samples across the feature. This is the informal reason the chapter ranks triangulations by angles before it ever names the Delaunay graph.

Quality Criterion
lexicographic angles

Angle Vector And Illegal Edge

Angle Vector

Sort all triangle angles increasingly:

\(A(T)=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_{3m})\)

Compare triangulations lexicographically.

Illegal Edge

An interior diagonal is illegal when the other diagonal of the same convex quadrilateral gives a larger local angle vector.

Introduce the angle vector as a lexicographic quality certificate. The illegal-edge definition is local: in a convex quadrilateral, the current diagonal is illegal when replacing it increases the sorted angle vector. Students should see that this is a local move designed to improve the global smallest-angle priority.
Predicate
determinant sign

In-Circle Predicate

A legal-edge test can be reduced to asking whether the opposite point lies inside the circumcircle of the adjacent triangle.

Determinant Sketch

\[ \operatorname{InCircle}(a,b,c,d)= \det\begin{pmatrix} a_x&a_y&a_x^2+a_y^2&1\\ b_x&b_y&b_x^2+b_y^2&1\\ c_x&c_y&c_x^2+c_y^2&1\\ d_x&d_y&d_x^2+d_y^2&1 \end{pmatrix} \]

Use

With a consistent orientation convention, the sign reports inside, on, or outside the circle through three sites.

Numerical Caution

Near cocircular inputs require robust predicates or symbolic tie handling; a wrong sign can flip the wrong edge.

The local angle test becomes practical through the in-circle predicate. For an oriented triangle, the sign of a lifted determinant tells whether the fourth point lies inside, on, or outside the circumcircle. The determinant visual is intentionally small; the main message is that legal-edge testing is a robust geometric predicate.
Flip Process
finite improvement

Legal Triangulation Terminates

Algorithmic Idea

  • Find an illegal interior edge.
  • Flip it inside its convex quadrilateral.
  • Repeat until every edge is legal.

Termination Certificate

The angle vector strictly increases, and only finitely many triangulations of \(P\) exist.

Role In The Chapter

This is too slow as a construction strategy, but it proves that local legality has global force.

Describe the all-flips procedure as a proof device rather than as the efficient algorithm. Each illegal flip strictly improves the angle vector in a finite set of triangulations, so infinite cycling is impossible. When the process stops, every edge is legal, which will later match the Delaunay condition.
Duality
from Vor(P) to DG(P)

Voronoi Dual Becomes DG(P)

A Delaunay edge connects two sites whose Voronoi cells share a boundary segment.

Definition

The Delaunay graph \(DG(P)\) is the straight-line dual of the Voronoi diagram: adjacent cells give adjacent sites.

Plane Graph

Voronoi adjacency prevents crossings in the straight-line dual, so \(DG(P)\) is embedded as a planar graph.

Terminology

With no four cocircular points, \(DG(P)\) is already a triangulation. Otherwise its faces may need triangulating.

Reconnect to Chapter 7. Join two sites when their Voronoi cells share an edge; the resulting straight-line graph is the Delaunay graph. The deck deliberately centers this duality instead of the lower convex hull, because dual Voronoi edges make the empty-circle and adjacency tests visually immediate.
Characterization
empty-circle witness

Empty Circle Characterization

Edge Certificate

Two sites \(p_i,p_j\) are adjacent in \(DG(P)\) iff some circle through both has no site of \(P\) in its interior.

Triangle Certificate

A triangle belongs to a Delaunay triangulation when its circumcircle is empty, subject to completing degenerate faces consistently.

Degenerate Case

Four cocircular sites can give a Delaunay face with more than three sides; multiple legal completions may exist.

Present the central certificate. Two sites form a Delaunay edge exactly when some empty circle has both sites on its boundary, and a Delaunay triangle has an empty circumcircle. This is the bridge between Voronoi vertices, legal edge tests, and the randomized construction.
Optimality
legal equals Delaunay

Delaunay Optimizes Angles

Implication Chain

legal edges \(\Rightarrow\) Delaunay triangulation \(\Rightarrow\) angle-vector optimum

Why It Matters

The rule is data-independent: it uses point positions only, yet it avoids the skinny triangles that create unstable interpolation.

Limits

Delaunay does not solve every meshing or terrain-quality objective, but it is a strong default with local certificates.

Tie the quality criterion back to Delaunay structure. Legal triangulations are Delaunay triangulations, and Delaunay triangulations maximize the angle vector among triangulations, up to degeneracies where several triangulations complete the same Delaunay graph. This explains why the data-independent rule improves terrain interpolation.
Construction
randomized incremental

Randomized Incremental Setup

Insert points in random order, maintain a Delaunay triangulation of the prefix, and repair only the local damage.

State At Stage r

A Delaunay triangulation of the inserted prefix plus symbolic outer points.

One Insertion

  • Locate the containing triangle or edge.
  • Connect the new point to local boundary vertices.
  • Legalize exposed edges recursively.

Why Random?

The random order makes backwards analysis possible and bounds expected structural change.

Move from characterization to construction. The randomized algorithm shuffles the points, surrounds them with symbolic sentinel points, and inserts one point at a time into the current Delaunay triangulation. Only the region near the inserted point can change, so the global update becomes a set of local legalizations.
Local Update
split one or two faces

Insert Point: Triangle Case Versus Edge Case

Inside A Triangle

Replace the containing triangle by the star from \(p_r\) to its three vertices.

On An Edge

Replace the two adjacent triangles by four triangles around \(p_r\).

After The Split

Only old edges on the boundary of the changed patch can have become illegal.

Separate the two geometric insertion cases. If the new point lies inside one triangle, replace that triangle by three. If it lies on an existing edge, remove the two adjacent triangles and replace them by four. The following legalization phase is identical in spirit: test the old boundary edges that may now be illegal.
Recursive Repair
paraphrased algorithm

LEGALIZE_EDGE Cascade

Procedure Logic

  • Test the edge opposite the inserted point.
  • If it is legal, stop along that branch.
  • If it is illegal, flip it and test the two newly exposed edges.

Cascade Shape

The changed triangles spread outward from \(p_r\) until every boundary edge passes the in-circle test.

Implementation View

A stack-based implementation can replace recursion; the mathematical invariant is the same.

Paraphrase the recursive procedure without copying pseudocode. When a boundary edge fails the in-circle test, flip it, then test the two newly exposed edges opposite the inserted point. The cascade is finite because each flip is a legalizing move, and it is local because only triangles incident to the changed region need attention.
Correctness
new edge certificates

Correctness: New Edges Are Delaunay

After A Split

Edges from \(p_r\) to the containing triangle's vertices have empty-circle witnesses, so they belong to the new Delaunay graph.

After A Flip

If an edge is flipped because \(p_r\) lies inside an adjacent circumcircle, the replacement diagonal has its own empty witness.

What Remains

Old edges untouched by the changed patch remain legal; boundary failures are exactly what legalization tests.

Give the correctness proof as a certificate story. Edges from the inserted point to vertices of the containing triangle are supported by circles that can be tightened around the new point. After a flip, the new diagonal is also supported by an empty-circle witness. Therefore the algorithm never creates a fundamentally non-Delaunay new edge.
Point Location
history DAG plus sentinels

Point-Location DAG With Sentinels

Query Route

Start at the initial sentinel triangle. At each replaced triangle, test which child contains the query point.

Why A DAG?

One old triangle may be replaced by several new triangles, and later replacements share descendants. The history is not a simple tree.

Sentinels

Artificial outer points keep all real points inside the maintained subdivision and simplify boundary cases.

The algorithm still needs to find the triangle containing the next point. The history DAG records replaced triangles and points queries through the replacement children. Sentinels give a stable outer frame, so every real input point is inside the maintained triangulation and the search has a well-defined starting triangle.
Analysis
expected structural change

Backwards Analysis Dashboard

Backward View

At stage \(r\), remove the last inserted point from \(DG(P_r)\). Only edges and triangles incident to that point disappear.

Planar Graph Bound

The Delaunay graph has linear size, so the average degree in the random prefix is constant.

Resulting Lens

Expected number of created triangles is \(O(n)\); point-location cost needs a conflict-size sum.

Summarize the expected-time lens. Look backward from a random stage: the last inserted point is equally likely among the r current points, and only features incident to that point disappear when it is removed. Since a planar Delaunay graph has linear complexity, the expected structural change per stage is constant.
Starred Framework

Configuration Space And Consequences

The final abstraction packages randomized incremental proofs into definitions, conflicts, and active configurations.

Four-Tuple

\((X,\Pi,D,K)\): inputs, possible configurations, defining objects, and killing or conflict objects.

Delaunay Fit

A corner is active when its defining sites appear on a Delaunay face boundary and its conflict set is absent.

Consequence

The framework bounds conflict traversal and yields expected \(O(n\log n)\) time for the randomized Delaunay construction.

Takeaway

Delaunay triangulations are where local predicates, planar duality, and randomized analysis meet cleanly.

Close with the starred framework. A configuration space packages the objects that can appear, the small defining set that creates one, and the conflict set that destroys it. Delaunay corners fit this pattern, giving the cost bound for point location history and the final expected O(n log n) construction result.
