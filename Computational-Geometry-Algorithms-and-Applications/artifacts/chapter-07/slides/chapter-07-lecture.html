Start with the operational problem: many places in the plane need to know which input site is closest. Chapter 7 first defines the Voronoi diagram and its basic geometric structure, then uses those properties to motivate an algorithm. The arc of the lecture is that a nearest-neighbor partition is not just a picture; its edges, vertices, and dual graph give algorithmic certificates.
Definition And Cells

Assignment Model: Nearest-Site Colored Cells

Cell Rule

For a site \(p_i\), its cell is all points \(x\) with \(d(x,p_i) \le d(x,p_j)\) for every other site \(p_j\).

Query Interpretation

  • Locate \(x\) in the subdivision.
  • Return the color, hence the nearest site.
  • Ties live on boundaries, not inside generic cells.
Give the definition as an assignment rule, not as a drawing rule. A point belongs to the cell of p_i when p_i is at least as close as every other site. The colored regions are the maximal areas where the answer to the nearest-site query is constant. Boundaries record ties, which is why bisectors are the real skeleton of the diagram.
Convexity

One Cell Is Half-Plane Intersection

Pairwise Constraint

The points at least as close to \(p_i\) as to \(p_j\) form one side of their perpendicular bisector.

\(d(x,p_i)^2 \le d(x,p_j)^2\)

Consequence

A point-site Voronoi cell is an intersection of half-planes, hence convex and possibly unbounded.

Derive convexity from one comparison. Squaring distances cancels the quadratic terms, so the inequality between p_i and p_j is linear in x. Intersect that half-plane over all j and the whole cell appears. This is a useful pause because it links Voronoi cells back to the half-plane language students already saw earlier in the course.
Planar Graph View

Structure And Complexity: Graph And Euler Bounds

Planar Subdivision

For nondegenerate point sites, cells are faces, bisector pieces are edges, and empty-circle centers are vertices.

Linear Size

  • Each bounded vertex has degree three in general position.
  • Euler's formula ties faces, edges, and vertices together.
  • The whole diagram has \(O(n)\) combinatorial complexity.
Reframe the diagram as a planar graph. The faces correspond to sites, the edges to two-site ties, and the vertices to three-site ties. Under the usual no-four-cocircular assumption, internal vertices have degree three. Euler's formula then gives the important algorithmic message: even though every pair of sites has a bisector, only a linear number of bisector pieces survive in the final diagram.
Local Certificates

Empty-Circle Characterization

Edge Feature

A point on a Voronoi edge has an empty circle touching two sites on its boundary.

Vertex Feature

A Voronoi vertex is the center of an empty circle through three sites in general position.

Use empty circles as the bridge from structure to algorithms. If a center is on an edge, two sites are equally close and no site is closer, so the corresponding circle is empty and touches two sites. At a vertex, three cells meet and the circle touches three sites. These certificates will reappear as circle events in Fortune's sweep and as the Delaunay dual later.
Fortune Sweep

Why Fortune Sweeps The Beach Line

Invariant

Above the beach line, the portion of the diagram is already determined; below it, unseen sites may still change the nearest answer.

Events

  • Site event: a new parabola appears on the beach line.
  • Circle event: a shrinking arc disappears and fixes a Voronoi vertex.
Explain the beach line as a moving frontier of uncertainty. The sweep line has processed sites above it. A point on the beach line is equally close to a processed site and to the sweep line, so parabolic arcs form the frontier. The important invariant is not the shape of a parabola by itself; it is that the completed diagram above the beach line will not be changed by sites still below the sweep line.
Fortune Sweep

Site Event: New Arc Splits Old Arc

What Happens

When the sweep reaches a new site, its zero-width arc appears directly under the site and splits the arc it lands on.

Bookkeeping

The two new breakpoints trace the same Voronoi edge in opposite directions until later events complete it.

At a site event, the sweep line reaches a site for the first time. The new site's parabola initially has no width, but immediately after the event it cuts into the beach line and splits one existing arc into two pieces. The breakpoints on either side of the new arc are the moving ends of a Voronoi edge between the new site and the site whose arc was split.
Fortune Sweep

Circle Event: Triple Disappears And Commits Vertex

Trigger

Three consecutive arcs define a circle. If its lowest point reaches the sweep line before being invalidated, the middle arc disappears.

Commitment

The converging breakpoints meet at a Voronoi vertex, and two unfinished edge records are joined there.

Circle events are the moment where empty-circle geometry becomes an algorithmic event. Three neighboring arcs may define a circle through their sites. When the sweep reaches the bottom of that circle, the middle arc has no future interval on the beach line and disappears. The two breakpoints collide at the center of the circle, which is exactly the Voronoi vertex to record.
Implementation Skeleton

Data Structures: Queue, Tree, DCEL Pointers

Event Queue

Priority by sweep position. Site events are known at the start; circle events are inserted and deleted as the beach line changes.

Beach-Line Tree

Leaves store arcs. Internal nodes represent breakpoints and guide search for the arc above a new site.

DCEL Records

Half-edges, twins, faces, and vertices are filled incrementally while breakpoints trace the diagram.

This is the engineering slide. The event queue determines the next sweep action. The beach-line tree lets us find, split, and remove arcs in logarithmic time. The DCEL is where geometric events become a persistent subdivision. Emphasize that invalid circle events are normal: when an arc is split before its predicted disappearance, the old event must be marked inactive or removed.
Robustness

Degeneracy Clinic

Cases To Name

  • Two sites share the same sweep height.
  • Four or more sites lie on one empty circle.
  • Numerical predicates create nearly zero-length artifacts.

Practical Posture

Use explicit tie-breaking, robust orientation/incircle predicates, and cleanup rules that preserve the intended planar subdivision.

Make degeneracy feel like part of the specification, not an embarrassment. Equal sweep heights require a stable event order. Cocircular sites mean several Voronoi edges meet at one geometric point rather than at a chain of distinct degree-three vertices. Small numerical errors can leave zero-length edge records. The goal is to preserve the combinatorial meaning even when the generic-position story is not literally true.
Beyond Points

Segment-Site Voronoi: Endpoint And Interior Distances

Distance To A Segment

The closest point on a segment may be an endpoint or an interior projection.

Mixed Bisectors

Segment-site diagrams combine line-like and parabolic bisector pieces, with endpoint features acting like point sites.

Move from point sites to segment sites by asking where the nearest point on a segment lies. If the perpendicular projection lands inside the segment, distance behaves like distance to a line. If the closest point is an endpoint, the endpoint behaves like a point site. That mixture explains why segment-site Voronoi diagrams contain both straight and curved pieces.
Motion Planning

Retraction For Disc Motion: Clearance Graph

Retraction Idea

For a moving disc, obstacle boundaries can be inflated; the safe center path retracts toward Voronoi features of obstacle sites.

Why It Helps

  • Edges maximize clearance between neighboring obstacles.
  • Planning reduces to reachability in a graph plus short connectors.
  • Clearance values label feasible radii.
The segment-site diagram becomes useful in disc motion planning. Inflate obstacles by the disc radius, or equivalently track the disc center among obstacle features. A path can be retracted toward the Voronoi skeleton because that skeleton keeps locally maximal clearance from obstacles. The continuous planning problem is then represented by connectors to a clearance graph and graph search on that skeleton.
Dual Variant

Farthest-Point Cells: Hull-Only, Unbounded Tree

Rule Reversed

A farthest cell contains points for which one site is at least as far as every other site.

Structural Surprise

Only convex-hull sites get nonempty farthest cells, and the diagram forms an unbounded tree-like subdivision.

Reverse the nearest-site inequality and students should immediately notice a different geometry. Interior points of the input set cannot win far away, and in fact only convex-hull sites have nonempty farthest cells. The farthest diagram is unbounded and tree-like because it records which hull point is most extreme in each direction.
Construction Strategy

Randomized Farthest Construction

Incremental View

Insert sites in random order, maintain the convex hull, and update only the farthest structure affected by newly exposed hull arcs.

Backwards Intuition

A random insertion is expensive mainly when it becomes a hull site that changes the farthest tree.

For farthest diagrams, the convex hull is the gatekeeper. A randomized incremental construction can ignore insertions that stay interior, because they have no farthest cell. When a point becomes part of the hull, the affected portion of the farthest tree is local to the newly exposed hull chain. This is the same randomized-incremental mindset as earlier, now applied to a different diagram.
Optimization Application

Smallest-Width Annulus: Nearest And Farthest Candidates

Objective

Choose a center \(c\) minimizing the gap between the farthest and nearest input distances.

\(\min_c\; \max_i d(c,p_i)-\min_i d(c,p_i)\)

Where To Look

Candidate centers arise from interactions between nearest-feature and farthest-feature diagrams.

The annulus problem combines the two diagrams. For a chosen center, the outer radius is determined by a farthest site and the inner radius by a nearest site. We want the smallest difference between those two radii. The candidate centers occur where the responsible nearest and farthest features can change, so the nearest and farthest Voronoi structures give a finite search framework.
Closing Map

Generalizations And Bridge To Delaunay

Generalizations

  • Sites can be points, segments, regions, or weighted objects.
  • Distances can be Euclidean or problem-specific metrics.
  • Robust predicates protect the subdivision.

Next Bridge

Connect two sites when their Voronoi cells share an edge: the Delaunay graph records empty-circle adjacency.

Close by naming the two durable ideas. First, Voronoi diagrams generalize whenever a distance or site model gives meaningful nearest features. Second, the point-site diagram has a dual: if two cells share an edge, connect the two sites. Empty-circle certificates explain why that graph is the Delaunay structure we will use for triangulations and proximity reasoning.
