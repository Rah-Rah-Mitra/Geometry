Title And Problem: Supersampling Half-Plane Discrepancy

A pixel edge asks for a continuous area fraction; a renderer stores finitely many sample points. The chapter asks how bad the worst half-plane estimate can be.

Lecture Problem

For a finite sample set \(P\) in a pixel square, compare area \(\mu(h)\) with sample fraction \(|P\cap h|/|P|\) for every half-plane \(h\).

Why This Is Geometric

Each candidate image edge becomes a line. The chosen side is the locally visible part of the pixel.

Why This Is Algorithmic

The search is over infinitely many half-planes, but only finitely many combinatorial events change the sample count.

Ray tracing and supersampling motivate the discrepancy modelGoal: make area and count agree uniformly
Begin from the rendering motivation on printed pages 173-174: ray tracing estimates what fraction of a pixel is covered by a surface, and supersampling replaces a continuous area question by finitely many sample probes. The lecture problem is to place samples so every local edge half-plane is counted nearly in proportion to its area.

Local Approximation

Over one small pixel, a smooth visible boundary is treated as a line segment.

Half-Plane Query

The visible side is one of the two half-planes cut out by that line.

Design Target

Good sample patterns are judged against all edge positions and orientations, not one chosen image.

Half-planes encode local edge coverageThe pixel square supplies the bounded measure space
Use the pixel-edge picture to justify the abstraction rather than assuming it. At the scale of one pixel, a smooth boundary is approximated by a line, so the visible part of the pixel is one side of that line. The source discussion on printed pages 173-174 motivates why half-planes are the stress test for sample patterns.
Discrepancy
continuous area versus finite count

Continuous vs Discrete Measure: Area Versus Sample Count

Half-Plane Discrepancy

\(D(P)=\sup_h \left|\mu(Q\cap h)-\frac{|P\cap h|}{|P|}\right|\)

Continuous Side

\(\mu(Q\cap h)\) is the normalized area of the clipped pixel region.

Discrete Side

\(|P\cap h|/|P|\) is the fraction of sample probes that land on the same side.

The supremum ranges over all half-planesUniform accuracy is stronger than average accuracy
Now formalize the mismatch from printed pages 175-177. The continuous quantity is area fraction inside a region, while the discrete estimator is the fraction of stored samples in the same region. Discrepancy measures the worst absolute gap over all half-planes; low discrepancy means the sample set behaves like area for every edge position.
Finiteness
events happen when a line touches samples

Infinite Search Becomes Candidates

Count Is Piecewise Constant

Slide or rotate a boundary without crossing samples; \(|P\cap h|\) does not change.

Area Still Moves

Inside one count interval, the area term changes continuously, so endpoints and tangencies matter.

Candidate Principle

Worst cases can be sought among a finite set of boundary events.

Infinite geometric search becomes finite combinatoricsThis is the first step toward Theorem 8.2
Printed pages 175-177 show the first finiteness move. Although there are infinitely many half-planes, the sample count changes only when the boundary crosses a sample point. Between such events, the area changes continuously while the count is fixed, so maxima can be hunted among boundaries touching points and among extreme placements.

Duality
point (a,b) <-> line y = ax - b

Point-Line Duality Definition: \((a,b)\) To \(y=ax-b\)

Point To Line

\(p=(a,b)\quad\longmapsto\quad p^\ast:\ y=ax-b\)

Line To Point

\(\ell:y=mx+c\quad\longmapsto\quad \ell^\ast=(m,-c)\)

Scope

This version treats nonvertical primal lines directly; vertical cases are handled separately.

The slope of the dual line stores the primal x-coordinateThe intercept sign is chosen for clean order tests
Introduce the duality from printed pages 177-179 with the exact mapping used in the chapter. A primal point (a,b) becomes the nonvertical line y equals ax minus b. A primal nonvertical line y equals mx plus c becomes the dual point (m,-c). The sign convention is chosen so incidence and vertical order line up cleanly.
Duality Properties
incidence and order become algebraic

Incidence And Order Preservation

Incidence

\(p\in \ell\) in the primal plane exactly when \(p^\ast\) passes through \(\ell^\ast\) in the dual plane.

Order Signal

Above in the primal becomes below the dual point when evaluated at the corresponding slope coordinate.

Algorithmic Use

Side-of-line counts become above/below-line counts in a dual arrangement.

Duality preserves incidence exactlyVertical order is controlled, with a predictable reversal
Stay on printed pages 177-179 and emphasize what the transformation buys us. If a primal point lies on a primal line, the dual line passes through the dual point. If the primal point is above the primal line, then at the dual x-coordinate the dual line is below the dual point, giving a controlled reversal that still preserves order information.
Duality Caveat
vertical configurations require a wedge view

Vertical Lines And Segment Wedges

Problem

The mapping \(\ell:y=mx+c\mapsto(m,-c)\) does not represent vertical primal lines as ordinary finite dual points.

Replacement Picture

A same-\(x\) pair constrains a range of slopes; in the dual plane, that range is visible as a wedge.

Takeaway

Duality still organizes the candidates, but vertical boundary events need a local convention.

Vertical exceptions are handled geometrically, not ignoredWedges encode ranges of compatible slopes
Handle the caveat that ordinary point-line duality avoids vertical primal lines. For a vertical segment or a pair of points with the same x-coordinate, the set of compatible nonvertical lines appears as a wedge or interval of slopes in the dual plane. This is the geometric bridge needed before counting candidates from point pairs.

Discrepancy In The Dual Plane: Primal Pair Line To Dual Vertex Counts

Pair Boundary

A candidate primal line often passes through two samples.

Dual Vertex

The two corresponding dual lines meet at one arrangement vertex.

Count Translation

Number of samples on a side equals the number of dual lines above or below the associated vertex.

Discrepancy queries become arrangement level queriesVertices are the finite checkpoints
Move from duality to discrepancy. A primal line through two samples corresponds to the intersection vertex of their two dual lines. Counting how many primal points lie on one side becomes counting how many dual lines lie above or below that vertex. This is where the half-plane discrepancy problem becomes a level query in an arrangement.
Arrangements
simple position and degeneracy

Arrangement Complexity: Simple vs Degenerate Arrangements

Arrangement

The subdivision of the plane into vertices, edges, and faces induced by a set of lines.

Simple Position

\(n\) lines have \(\binom n2\) vertices when every pair crosses and no three are concurrent.

Degeneracy

Coincident or concurrent events change local counts and must be represented consistently.

Arrangement size is quadratic in the worst caseThe subdivision is the data object behind level queries
Use printed pages 179-182 to define arrangements as the subdivision induced by lines. In simple position, every pair meets once and no three lines meet at one point, giving a clean quadratic count of vertices, edges, and faces. Degeneracies collapse events and require consistent handling, but the subdivision viewpoint remains the same.
Data Structure

Why A Bounding Box And DCEL

Bounding Box

Clipping the arrangement makes every stored boundary finite.

DCEL

Directed half-edges encode adjacency: twin, next, previous, incident face, and origin.

Locality

A face split can be represented by pointer changes near the crossed boundary cycle.

The box is a storage device, not a mathematical restrictionDCEL turns topology into updateable records
The arrangement is easier to store when it is clipped to a large box, because every face boundary becomes finite. The DCEL records directed half-edges with twin, next, previous, incident face, and origin links. Printed pages 179-182 use this structure so local edits can update topology without rebuilding the whole subdivision.

Before

Runtime Lens

Zone Theorem As The Runtime Lens

Zone Theorem

For a line crossing an arrangement of \(n\) lines, the total complexity of the crossed faces is \(O(n)\).

Why It Matters

The whole arrangement can be quadratic, but one insertion touches only the zone of the inserted line.

Runtime View

Analyze the crossed strip, not every face in the subdivision.

The zone is the cost footprint of insertionLocal complexity controls the incremental construction
Printed pages 183-185 introduce the zone theorem as the lens for runtime. The zone of a line is the collection of arrangement faces the line crosses. Even though the whole arrangement may be quadratic, the total complexity of this crossed strip is linear in the number of existing lines, so one insertion can be charged locally.
Levels

Level Walk Along A Line

k-Level

The \(k\)-level is the set of points in the arrangement with exactly \(k\) lines strictly below them.

Walk Rule

Move left to right along a query line; at each crossing, the count changes by one.

Discrepancy Link

Those counts are the dual form of primal sample side counts.

Levels turn vertical order into a traversable objectEvery crossing updates the side count by one
Use printed pages 185-186 to connect arrangements to levels. The k-level consists of points that have exactly k lines strictly below them. Walking along a line from left to right, the level changes by one at every crossing, so the arrangement supplies the sequence of counts needed for discrepancy.
