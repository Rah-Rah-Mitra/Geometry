Motivation And Contract
Printed pages 307-308

Mesh Generation Problem

The board is a square domain with polygonal components; the mesh must support numerical simulation without exploding in size.

Mesh Goals
  • Conforming: no triangle edge contains another triangle vertex in its interior.
  • Input-respecting: polygonal component edges are unions of mesh edges.
  • Well-shaped: target triangles have angles in \([45^\circ,90^\circ]\).
  • Non-uniform: refinement follows component boundaries.

Why Steiner Points Matter

Mesh vertices need not be only input vertices. Extra points allow a mesh to repair shape and conformity while still respecting the domain.

Implementation Substrate

The final mesh is naturally stored as a DCEL: faces are cells or triangles, half-edges support local splits, and component diagonals become explicit edges.

Domain: \([0,U]\times[0,U]\), with \(U=2^j\)Finite element quality drives the geometry
Use the domain square as the contract. The mesh must be conforming, must respect component boundaries, must avoid badly shaped triangles, and must place fine detail only where it is needed. Emphasize that these goals compete: respecting input edges alone does not guarantee shape quality, and uniform refinement wastes elements.

Data Structure
Tree plus subdivision

Quadtree As Two Synchronized Objects

Every node stores a square; every internal node splits that square into four quadrant children.

Quadtree Node

A node \(\nu\) owns a square \(\sigma(\nu)\). If \(\nu\) is internal, its children own the NE, NW, SW, and SE quadrants of \(\sigma(\nu)\).

Leaf Subdivision

The leaves are disjoint in their interiors and exactly cover the initial square, so the tree encodes a planar subdivision.

Storage Choice

Storing each square makes queries simple. Storing only the root square saves memory but forces square coordinates to be recomputed during traversal.

Tree depth becomes geometric resolutionLeaves become mesh cells later
A quadtree is both a rooted four-child tree and a square subdivision. Students should keep these objects synchronized mentally: a tree edge means a geometric split, and a leaf means a square that remains in the subdivision. Algorithms will move between these views constantly.
Implementation Detail
Tie convention

Quadrant Rule And Boundary Ties

A deterministic split rule assigns every point to exactly one recursive child, even on median lines.

Routing Rule

For a square midpoint \((m_x,m_y)\), choose one fixed policy for points with \(x=m_x\) or \(y=m_y\). The deck uses east and north ownership for ties.

Why It Matters

Without a tie policy, a point on a split line could be duplicated, dropped, or routed differently by neighboring implementations.

Invariant

Each recursive call receives a disjoint subset, and the union of the four subsets is the parent set.

Boundary ties are algorithmic choices, not geometry changesDeterminism makes recursion testable
The quadrant convention is a small implementation detail that prevents large ambiguity later. Points on splitting lines must be routed deterministically, so every recursive call owns a disjoint subset. The exact less-than and greater-than choices are less important than the fact that they are fixed and used consistently.
Lemma Lens
Printed pages 311-312

Depth Bound

A deep internal cell must still contain two separated points, so its diagonal cannot be smaller than the minimum separation.

Depth Bound

If \(c\) is the smallest input-point distance and \(s\) is the root side length, then a point quadtree has depth \(O(\log(s/c))\).

Proof Sketch

An internal node at depth \(i\) contains at least two points. Their distance is at least \(c\), while any two points in the cell are at most the diagonal apart.

\(c \le s\sqrt{2}/2^i\)

Depth depends on geometry, not only on \(n\)Close points can force long chains
The depth argument is a packing argument inside one square. At depth i, a cell has side length s over 2 to the i, so its diagonal is s times square-root two over 2 to the i. If an internal cell still contains two points, that diagonal must be at least the minimum point separation c.
Theorem Lens
Size and construction

Size And Construction Cost

Even if the tree is geometrically deep, each depth layer has only linearly many internal nodes.

Layer Bound

For \(n\) input points and depth \(d\):

\(\#\text{nodes}=O((d+1)n)\)

\(\text{construction time}=O((d+1)n)\)

Counting Idea

At a fixed depth, cells are interior-disjoint. Every internal node has at least one input point in its cell, so at most \(n\) internal nodes appear on that layer.

Why Leaves Follow

Each split replaces one leaf by four children, so leaves and internal nodes differ by only a constant factor.

Layer sum: \((d+1)\) layers times at most \(n\)Construction partitions points by quadrants
Bound the tree one level at a time. At any fixed depth, internal nodes occupy disjoint cells and each contains at least one input point, so there are at most n internal nodes on that layer. Summing over d plus one layers gives the size and recursive construction cost.
Local Navigation
Neighbor finding

Neighbor Finding

To find a north neighbor, either cross to a sibling immediately or climb until crossing is possible.

North Neighbor Recipe
  • If \(\nu\) is a southern child, cross to the corresponding northern sibling.
  • If \(\nu\) is a northern child, recursively find the north neighbor of its parent.
  • If the found node is internal and a leaf is needed, descend through south children.
Runtime

Each climb reduces depth by one. The search takes \(O(d+1)\) time in a tree of depth \(d\).

Neighbor queries drive balancing and DCEL constructionSame idea rotates to east, south, and west
Neighbor finding is the first place where the tree view pays for the subdivision view. For a north neighbor, a southern child can cross to its sibling immediately. A northern child must climb until a crossing is possible, then descend on the opposite side if a leaf neighbor is required.
Mesh-Friendly Invariant
Balanced quadtree

Balanced Quadtree Invariant

Adjacent leaf squares may differ by at most one depth level, or by a factor two in side length.

Balance

For every pair of neighboring leaves \(a,b\):

\(|\operatorname{depth}(a)-\operatorname{depth}(b)| \le 1\)

Check Reflected In Notebook

The toy balanced leaves satisfy neighboring depth difference at most one.

Why It Helps

No side has more than one interior subdivision vertex after balancing, so cells can be triangulated with constant local templates.

Balanced cells are the bridge from squares to conforming trianglesLocal invariant, global usability
Balanced means adjacent leaves differ by at most one depth level, or equivalently by at most a factor two in side length. This prevents a large square from having a long side broken into many tiny pieces, which is exactly the situation that makes conforming triangulation awkward.
Algorithm State
Queue repair loop

Balancing Algorithm State

Balancing is a queue of local violations: split a leaf, then inspect the neighbors that the split exposed.

State Machine
  • Initialize \(L\) with leaves to inspect.
  • Pop a leaf \(\mu\).
  • If a neighbor is less than half its size, split \(\mu\).
  • Push new leaves and newly threatened neighbors.

Neighbor-Finding Role

The north, east, south, and west neighbor routines identify whether a smaller or larger adjacent leaf exists.

Present balancing as a local repair loop. The queue contains leaves that might violate the invariant with some neighbor. Splitting one square can fix a current violation but expose a new one nearby, so the algorithm checks neighbors after each split and pushes newly threatened leaves.
Proof Dashboard
Linear blowup

The Linear Blowup Proof

Balancing may propagate, but every new split can be charged to a constant-size neighborhood of old structure.

Balance Size

If the original quadtree has \(m\) nodes, its balanced version has \(O(m)\) nodes.

Contradiction Idea

Assume the first split cannot be charged. The local geometry forces a still-earlier nearby split of comparable size, contradicting the choice of the first unchargeable event.

Computational Check

A toy charge demonstration records split count bounded by \(8m\), matching the constant-neighborhood proof intuition.

Constant neighborhood charges imply linear blowupTime adds neighbor-query factors
The charging proof explains why local repair does not cascade into a superlinear explosion. A newly created split can be charged to one of a constant number of nearby old squares at a comparable scale. The contradiction idea is that the first unchargeable split would have needed an even earlier unchargeable neighbor.
From Point Sets To Components
Stopping criterion

From Points To Polygonal Components

For mesh generation, split cells near component boundaries until they reach unit size.

Polygon-Edge Stopping Rule

Split a square while it is larger than unit size and its closure intersects the boundary of at least one polygonal component.

Effect

Cells close to edges refine. Cells far from input boundaries remain coarse, preserving non-uniformity.

Boundary Check

The generated mesh must contain every component segment as a union of mesh edges after the triangulation step.

Depth becomes \(O(\log U)\) because splitting stops at unit cellsBoundary-driven refinement
For meshing, the stopping rule changes from point occupancy to boundary interaction. A square keeps splitting while it is larger than unit size and its closure intersects a component boundary. This concentrates cells around polygon edges and leaves large calm regions coarse.
Allowed Orientations
Unit cells

Why Diagonals Appear

The input orientation restriction makes edge-cell intersections behave like grid lines or diagonals inside unit cells.

Unit-Cell Consequence

At unit resolution, an allowed component edge cuts a cell as a full side segment or as a cell diagonal. Those cuts are valid mesh edges.

Diagonal Intersections

When a component boundary crosses a square diagonally, the DCEL can insert that diagonal as an edge of the mesh face.

Shape Payoff

A square cut by a diagonal yields two right isosceles triangles with angles \(45^\circ,45^\circ,90^\circ\).

Orientation restrictions simplify local topologyBoundary segments can be represented by mesh edges
The input restrictions make the final triangulation simple. With integer grid coordinates and component edges at horizontal, vertical, or forty-five degree orientations, any component segment crossing a unit cell runs as an allowed grid edge or diagonal. Those diagonals can become mesh edges rather than obstacles.
Local Templates
Triangulation cases

Balanced Cells Triangulate Cleanly

After balancing, each cell has only constant local complexity, so a small template set completes the mesh.

Angle Check

Every template uses right isosceles triangles; generated angles are in \(\{45^\circ,45^\circ,90^\circ\}\), up to numeric tolerance in computational checks.

Balance guarantees no side has more than one interior subdivision vertexDCEL updates are local
Once the balanced subdivision is stored in a DCEL, every face has only a small set of boundary configurations. A cell cut by a component diagonal already has its triangulating edge. An ordinary cell gets one diagonal. If side midpoints appear, balancing ensures at most one per side, and a center Steiner point creates only forty-five, forty-five, ninety triangles.
Complexity Pipeline
Mesh size and time

Complexity Pipeline

The construction cost is the product of boundary complexity, depth, and balancing overhead.

Mesh Bound

\(\#\text{triangles}=O(p(S)\log U)\)

\(\text{construction}=O(p(S)\log^2 U)\)

Pipeline

  • At any fixed grid level, component edges intersect \(O(p(S))\) cells.
  • There are \(O(\log U)\) levels before unit cells.
  • Balancing preserves size up to a constant factor.
  • Triangulation adds \(O(1)\) triangles per balanced cell.
\(p(S)\) is the total perimeter of polygonal componentsConstant triangulation per cell preserves asymptotic size
Walk through the bound as a pipeline rather than a theorem recital. Component edges intersect only O of p of S cells at each grid level. There are O log U levels down to unit cells. Balancing preserves asymptotic size, and triangulation adds only a constant number of triangles per balanced cell.
Recap And Extensions

Recap And Extensions

The algorithmic arc is simple: adaptive squares become useful after balancing, then templates turn them into a conforming triangular mesh.

Takeaway

Adaptive subdivision saves cells. Balance makes local topology tame. Triangulation templates produce conforming, input-respecting, well-shaped triangles.

Beyond This Chapter
