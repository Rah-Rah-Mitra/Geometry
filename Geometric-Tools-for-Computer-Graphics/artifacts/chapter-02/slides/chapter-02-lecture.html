Act on the plane

Linear maps

A Matrix Records Where Basis Directions Go

Definition
Matrix Representation
A linear map \(A:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m\) is represented by the \(m\times n\) matrix whose columns are \(A e_1,\ldots,A e_n\).
Graphics reading: the array is a compact description of a deformation, projection, frame change, or coordinate transfer.
\(A(\alpha u+\beta v)=\alpha A u+\beta A v\)
Present a matrix by its action on a basis. The grid deformation is a visual way to see all vectors move at once, while the two colored columns are enough to reconstruct the whole map by linearity. Make the determinant teaser concrete: area scale and orientation are visible before the symbolic formula is introduced.

Use this slide to slow down the notation. Tuples, rows, columns, and transposes are not just shapes on paper; in a graphics program they are type contracts. The product rule prevents impossible compositions, and the row-column dot product explains why matrix multiplication records many coupled linear measurements at once.
Linear systems

\(Ax=b\) As Agreement Among Constraints

Definition
A solution is a point \(x\) that satisfies every row equation \(a_i^T x=b_i\).
  • Unique: constraints meet at one point.
  • None: constraints are inconsistent.
  • Many: some constraints are dependent.
rank decides how many independent restrictions remain

Unique

Full independent information for the unknowns.

Inconsistent

Rows ask for incompatible geometry.

Many

Dependent rows leave a family of choices.

Frame a linear system as a question about whether several constraints agree on a common object. The three panels distinguish a unique intersection, inconsistent constraints, and a whole family of solutions. This prepares the rank discussion by making solution count a geometric property rather than a purely algebraic classification.
Elimination

Row Reduction Reveals Rank Without Changing Solutions

Invariant
Elementary row operations preserve the solution set of \(Ax=b\).
  • Pivots count independent row constraints.
  • Zero rows expose redundant information.
  • Pivot conflicts expose inconsistent data.
Rank is the algebraic name for how much independent geometry survives elimination.
Describe row reduction as a controlled rewrite of the same solution set. Pivots identify independent constraints; free columns identify degrees of freedom. The staircase diagram is deliberately schematic, because the invariant is not the numerical values in intermediate rows but the number and placement of pivots after elimination.
Square matrices

The Determinant Measures Oriented Scale

\(\det \begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}=ad-bc\)
Diagnostic
If \(\det A=0\), some nonzero direction collapses. If \(\det A\) is tiny, inversion may magnify noise.
  • positive determinant: orientation preserved
  • negative determinant: orientation reversed
  • zero determinant: rank loss and no inverse
Connect determinant to a visible area or volume scale. A positive value preserves orientation, a negative value reverses it, and a near-zero value signals that the inverse problem may amplify error. Stress that the determinant is both a geometric measurement and a diagnostic for code paths that assume invertibility.
Square-system toolkit

Invertibility Is A Geometric Promise

Equivalent exact conditions
For \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\): \(A^{-1}\) exists, \(\det A\ne0\), rank \(A=n\), and \(Ax=b\) has one solution for every \(b\).
Implementation caution
Exact equivalence does not remove tolerance decisions. Near singular matrices can return unstable coordinates while still passing a nonzero test.
Treat the inverse and Cramer's rule as part of the square-system toolkit, not as universal numerical advice. Students should hear that nonzero determinant, full rank, and unique solution are equivalent in the exact setting. Then add the graphics warning: near singular can be more dangerous than singular because it may still produce a number.
Linear spaces

Span, Independence, And Basis Separate Space From Coordinates

Definition
The span of \(v_1,\ldots,v_k\) is every linear combination \(\alpha_1v_1+\cdots+\alpha_kv_k\).
  • Subspace: closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication.
  • Independent: no vector is redundant.
  • Basis: independent spanning set with unique coordinates.
Use span, independence, and basis to separate the abstract space from the coordinates used to store it. The plane diagram makes a subspace tangible, but the important statement is that a basis lets every vector receive a unique coordinate description. This is the bridge toward coordinate-free vector reasoning in the next chapter.
Mappings

Rank-Nullity Bookkeeps What A Map Keeps And Loses

Rank-nullity
For \(A:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m\), \(\dim\ker A+\operatorname{rank}A=n\).
  • The image is what the map can actually produce.
  • The kernel is what becomes invisible after mapping.
  • A matrix representation changes when the chosen bases change.
This is the structural view behind projections, constraints, and degeneracies.
Make linear mappings feel structural. A map can stretch, collapse, rotate, or shear a space, and a matrix is only its basis-dependent record. Rank-nullity is the bookkeeping statement behind that picture: output dimension gained through the image is balanced by input directions lost in the kernel.
Eigenstructure

Eigenvectors Are Directions That The Map Does Not Turn

\(Av=\lambda v,\quad v\ne0\)
Reading
The scalar \(\lambda\) records stretch, shrink, or flip along an invariant direction.
  • principal axes of a transform
  • repeated transformation behavior
  • deformation modes and stability cues
Introduce eigenvectors as directions that survive the map as directions. They may stretch, shrink, or flip, but they do not turn away from their line. In graphics, this gives a compact language for principal axes, stable directions, repeated transforms, and deformation modes, while also warning that not every real matrix has enough real eigenvectors.
Euclidean space

Inner Products Add Length, Distance, And Orthogonality

Inner product
A positive definite symmetric bilinear measurement \( \langle u,v\rangle \) gives \( \|u\|=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle} \).
Projection certificate
\(u-\sum_i\langle u,q_i\rangle q_i\) is orthogonal to every orthonormal \(q_i\) already removed.
orthogonal means \( \langle u,v\rangle=0 \)
Euclidean structure adds measurements to a vector space. Inner products give length, distance, and perpendicularity, which are exactly the notions used by projections and fitting. Use the Gram-Schmidt sketch to show that orthonormal frames are constructed objects, not magic defaults, and that each subtraction removes an already explained direction.
Least squares

When Constraints Disagree, Project Onto The Column Space

\(A^T A x^* = A^T b\)
Geometric certificate
The residual \(r=b-Ax^*\) is orthogonal to every column of \(A\), so \(A^Tr=0\).
  • overdetermined systems usually cannot satisfy all rows
  • the best fit minimizes squared residual length
  • projection explains the normal equations
Least squares should land as geometry, not just optimization. When the equations disagree, the best fit is the point in the column space closest to the data vector. The residual is perpendicular to every column direction, which is why the normal equations are the algebraic certificate rather than an arbitrary trick.
Applied graphics lab

Turn Linear Algebra Into A Debuggable Geometry Workflow

Lab pattern
Choose a primitive, draw the active map or constraint, perturb the data, compute a residual, and decide what failure would look like in production.
  • matrix area scale matches determinant
  • least-squares residual is orthogonal to the column space
  • row-reduction rank matches a numerical rank routine
  • eigenvectors reproduce their scaled directions
A reliable implementation reports enough state to debug degeneracy, tolerance, and convention errors.
