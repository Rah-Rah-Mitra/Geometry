Trigonometry as a Graphics Contract

Angles and circular coordinates turn rotations, projections, inverse recovery, and curve motion into quantities a graphics program can test.

unit-circle coordinates identities as invariants branch-aware inverse functions calculus checks
$$p(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$$
$$s=r\theta$$
$$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$$
Begin by making the appendix feel operational rather than decorative. Angles, functions, identities, inverse branches, and calculus formulas are the small contracts behind rotation code, projection code, and intersection diagnostics. Ask students to watch for two recurring questions: what is the represented geometry, and what invariant checks the result?
Angle Model
standard position, sign, and radian measure

Angle Data Contract

Representation

An angle is a signed rotation from a reference ray to a terminal ray. In standard position, the reference ray is the positive x-axis.

Radian Measure

Radians tie the angle to distance traveled on a circle:

$$s=r\theta,\qquad \theta=\frac{s}{r}.$$
Conversion Checks
  • degrees to radians: multiply by \(\pi/180\)
  • radians to degrees: multiply by \(180/\pi\)
  • full turn: \(2\pi\) radians equals \(360^\circ\)
Use this slide to fix orientation before formulas start. The initial ray is a reference direction, the terminal ray names the result of rotation, and the sign convention is part of the data model. Radian measure is not just a unit conversion; it binds angle to arc length by s equals r theta.
Coordinates
the six functions from one point on the circle

Unit-Circle Functions

Definition View

For a terminal ray meeting the unit circle at \(p=(x,y)\), the primary graphics contract is

$$x=\cos\theta,\qquad y=\sin\theta.$$
$$\tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}$$
$$\sec\theta=\frac{1}{x}$$
$$\csc\theta=\frac{1}{y}$$
Undefined Is Information

Any quotient using \(x=0\) or \(y=0\) exposes a vertical or horizontal direction. Treat the undefined value as a branch or domain event, not as noise.

Connect the right-triangle mnemonic to a full circular definition. Once the terminal point is stored as x and y on the unit circle, sine and cosine become coordinates, while tangent, secant, cosecant, and cotangent become quotients with explicit domain exclusions. The check x squared plus y squared equals one should feel like a unit test.
Periodic Signals
graphs reveal range, phase, and excluded inputs

Graphs, Domains, Periods

Periodicity
$$\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta,\qquad \cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta$$
$$\tan(\theta+\pi)=\tan\theta$$
Phase As Geometry

A phase shift is a rotation of the parameter origin. It should move the graph without changing amplitude, period, or the circular path being sampled.

Domain Signal

Vertical asymptotes are not drawing artifacts. They mark directions where a quotient has a zero denominator and an implementation needs a branch policy.

The graphs are useful because they expose both periodic repetition and forbidden inputs. Sine and cosine are bounded coordinate projections, while tangent is a quotient and therefore develops vertical asymptotes. In graphics terms, a phase shift changes where sampling begins, but it should not change the geometric circle or the invariant amplitude.
Complex Rotation
Euler form compresses sine and cosine into one map

Exponential Form

Euler Coordinate Pair
$$e^{i\alpha}=\cos\alpha+i\sin\alpha$$

The real part stores the x-coordinate and the imaginary part stores the y-coordinate of the unit-circle point.

Rotation By Multiplication
$$e^{i\alpha}e^{i\beta}=e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}$$

Composition of rotations becomes addition of angles, a pattern echoed by rotation matrices and quaternions.

$$\sin\alpha=\frac{e^{i\alpha}-e^{-i\alpha}}{2i}$$
$$\cos\alpha=\frac{e^{i\alpha}+e^{-i\alpha}}{2}$$
Present Euler form as a compression of two coordinate functions into one rotation-valued expression. The formula is not an abstraction for its own sake: multiplying complex unit numbers composes rotations, and separating real and imaginary parts recovers cosine and sine. This gives students another way to recognize addition formulas.
Identity Checks
formulas become executable assertions

Identities as Invariants

Coordinate Norm
$$\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$$

A sampled rotation should stay on the unit circle unless the scale was intentionally changed.

Angle Addition
$$\cos(a+b)=\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b$$
$$\sin(a+b)=\sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b$$
Implementation Habit

Choose one identity as a residual. Draw the construction, perturb the input, and verify the residual stays near zero.

Treat the identities as tools for debugging, not a list to memorize. The Pythagorean identity checks whether a circular coordinate pair has drifted off the unit circle. The addition formulas check whether two rotations were composed in the intended order. The notebook habit is to turn each identity into a residual that can be asserted.
Triangle Recovery
side lengths, included angle, and robust reconstruction

Triangle Laws For Geometry Queries

Law Of Cosines
$$c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$$

Use it for angle recovery when all three side lengths are known or when dot products encode side lengths.

Law Of Sines
$$\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}=\frac{\sin C}{c}$$
Numerical Guard

Before applying \(\arccos\), clamp cosine data to \([-1,1]\). Roundoff can push a valid dot product barely outside the inverse domain.

Use the triangle laws as the bridge from reference trigonometry to geometric algorithms. A mesh routine, collision query, or camera calibration step often knows lengths or dot products before it knows the angle. The law of cosines turns those measurements into an angle, but numerical code must still clamp near-domain endpoints before applying arccos.
Branch Policy
principal values are conventions, not the full geometry

Inverse Branches

Principal Ranges
  • \(\arcsin u\) returns an angle in \([-\pi/2,\pi/2]\)
  • \(\arccos u\) returns an angle in \([0,\pi]\)
  • \(\arctan u\) alone cannot identify the quadrant
Quadrant-Aware Recovery
$$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$$

The ordered pair carries the signs needed to choose the intended branch.

Graphics Consequence

An inverse branch mistake can flip a normal, reverse a sweep, or make a rotation interpolate the long way around.

This is the most practical inverse-trigonometry slide in the lecture. Principal values are useful because they make inverse functions single-valued, but a graphics computation often needs the original quadrant. Stress the difference between arctan of a ratio and atan2 of an ordered pair; the second preserves the signs that encode orientation.
Motion Checks
derivatives and integrals explain tangency and accumulated angle

Calculus Of Circular Motion

Derivative Pair
$$\frac{d}{d\theta}\sin\theta=\cos\theta,\qquad \frac{d}{d\theta}\cos\theta=-\sin\theta$$

The derivative of unit-circle position is tangent to the circle.

Integral Pair
$$\int \cos\theta\,d\theta=\sin\theta,\qquad \int \sin\theta\,d\theta=-\cos\theta+C$$
Notebook Check

Finite differences should agree with analytic derivatives away from step-size extremes. The residual gives a numerical scale for trust.

Use the calculus formulas to interpret circular motion, not merely to fill a reference table. Differentiating the position p of theta gives a tangent velocity, so the derivative pair is visible in the diagram. Integrals reverse that relationship and appear whenever angular velocity or accumulated phase must be reconstructed from samples.
Worked Pattern
recover an oriented angle between two vectors

Robust Angle Recovery

Oriented 2D Formula
$$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(u_xv_y-u_yv_x,\;u_xv_x+u_yv_y)$$
normalize u, v
dot = clamp(u.x*v.x + u.y*v.y, -1, 1)
det = u.x*v.y - u.y*v.x
theta = atan2(det, dot)
residual = norm(R(theta) u - v)
Why This Pattern Works
  • dot product encodes \(\cos\theta\)
  • 2D determinant encodes signed area and orientation
  • \(\operatorname{atan2}\) keeps the quadrant instead of folding it away
This slide turns the appendix into a routine students can actually use. The dot product alone gives an unsigned angle through arccos, but the determinant supplies orientation. Feeding both numbers to atan2 avoids quadrant ambiguity, and the reconstruction residual R of theta applied to u minus v checks that the recovered angle matches the intended rotation.
Debugging
the errors are usually convention errors before they are formula errors

Failure Modes And Guards

Unit Mismatch

Degrees sent to a radian API create plausible but wrong rotations.

$$180^\circ=\pi$$

Guard: normalize units at every interface.

Quotient Blow-Up

Tangent, secant, cosecant, and cotangent can divide by zero.

$$\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$$

Guard: test denominator magnitude.

Branch Cut Jump

Principal inverse ranges can insert discontinuities in sweeps.

$$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$$

Guard: track orientation and unwrap when needed.

Invariant

\(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta\) should remain near one for normalized rotations.

Residual

Reconstruct points, sides, or vectors and measure the difference from the intended object.

Convention Log

Record radians versus degrees, orientation sign, principal range, and tolerance policy.

Frame these failures as production bugs students can recognize. A degree-radian mismatch often gives a smooth but incorrect animation. A quotient function can explode at a legitimate axis direction. A branch cut can make a continuous sweep jump. The repair strategy is always the same: name the convention, check the invariant, and report the residual.
Recap
from reference formulas to testable graphics code

Recap Exercise

In-Class Prompt

A tool receives two normalized 2D vectors, a desired clockwise sweep, and a display label in degrees. Design the angle recovery and verification path.

Input contract

Normalize or reject vectors; record radians internally.

Recovery

Use determinant and dot product with atan2.

Convention

Apply sign policy for clockwise versus counterclockwise.

Check

Rotate, reconstruct, and report the residual.

Takeaways
  • Trigonometry is circular geometry encoded as coordinates and ratios.
  • Identities become invariants for rotations, triangles, and sampled curves.
  • Inverse functions require explicit branch choices.
  • Derivatives and integrals explain tangent motion and accumulated phase.

$$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\det,\operatorname{dot})$$
Close by asking students to assemble the full workflow rather than recall isolated formulas. A strong answer names the unit convention, chooses atan2 because orientation matters, converts only at the display boundary, and checks the result by reconstructing the rotated vector. This recap mirrors the notebook pattern: model, draw, perturb, measure, and check.
