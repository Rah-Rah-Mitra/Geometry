Unifying Algorithm

Every Construction Starts by Moving the Unknown

Constraint Translation

  • Unknown point: usually the desired circle center or line origin.
  • Equal distance to two points: a perpendicular bisector.
  • Distance \(r\) from a line: one of two parallel offset lines.
  • Tangent to a circle: an auxiliary circle with signed radius shift.
constraint \(\longrightarrow\) locus, then \(L_0 \cap L_1\) or \(C_0 \cap C_1\)

Residual rule. After each candidate is produced, measure distances and dot products against the original constraints, not the transformed construction.

Model, draw, perturb, measure, checkReusable pattern
Use this slide to give the route students should apply all hour. Each geometric promise is rewritten as membership in a line, circle, or offset copy. Intersections then produce candidates, and residuals decide which candidates survive. This framing prevents the chapter from feeling like disconnected recipes.
Circle Centers from Points

Equal Distance Creates Bisectors and Auxiliary Circles

Three Points

For noncollinear \(P,Q,R\), the circle center is \(B(P,Q)\cap B(P,R)\), where each \(B\) is a perpendicular bisector.

Two Points and Radius

Centers are intersections of two radius-\(r\) circles centered at \(P\) and \(Q\).

real centers iff \(\lVert P-Q\rVert \le 2r\)

Degeneracy. Collinear triples, coincident points, and exactly tangent auxiliary circles should return explicit multiplicities.

Sections 8.1 and 8.6 orientationUnknown: center
For equal distance constraints, emphasize that the center is the unknown. Three noncollinear points determine a circle because two perpendicular bisectors meet at one point. Two fixed points plus a prescribed radius determine zero, one, or two centers depending on whether the auxiliary equal-radius circles intersect.
Tangent Lines

A Tangent Radius Is Orthogonal to the Tangent Line

Known Contact Point

If \(T\) lies on circle center \(C\), then a tangent line through \(T\) has direction perpendicular to \(T-C\).

\(d = (-(T_y-C_y),\,T_x-C_x)\), and \(d\cdot(T-C)=0\)

Unknown Contact from \(P\)

When \(P\) is outside the circle, the triangle \(C,T,P\) is right at \(T\), so the tangent length and angle are determined by \(r/\lVert P-C\rVert\).

Existence. Outside gives two tangents, on the circle gives one tangent, inside gives none.

Sections 8.3 and 8.4 orientationResidual: radius dot direction
Tangency at a known point is the cleanest case: the tangent direction is perpendicular to the radius. From an external point, the contact point is unknown, so the right triangle from center, query point, and contact point controls existence and produces symmetric directions.
Tangents to Two Circles

Common Tangents Are Controlled by Similarity Centers

Two Families

  • External tangents keep both circles on the same side of the tangent line.
  • Internal tangents pass between the circles and reverse side membership.
  • Equal radii push the external similarity center to infinity, giving parallel tangents.

Multiplicity Cases

Separate circles can have four tangents. Touching and containment thresholds collapse families to two, one, zero, or infinitely many coincident cases.

Section 8.5 orientationBranch by center distance and radii
Common tangents of two circles are best explained through homothety centers. External tangents meet at one similarity center and internal tangents at the other. The number of real tangent lines changes when one circle crosses the other, becomes nested, or touches at a threshold distance.
Distance to a Line

A Tangent Circle Center Lives on an Offset Line

Implicit Line Form

For \(L: ax+by+c=0\), a point \(X\) has signed distance \(s\) when

\(\displaystyle {aX_x+bX_y+c \over \sqrt{a^2+b^2}} = s\)

Thus the radius-\(r\) center locus is \(ax+by+c=\pm r\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).

Parametric Line Form

If \(L(t)=P+t\hat d\), offsets move by \(P \pm r\hat n\), with \(\hat n=(-\hat d_y,\hat d_x)\).

Offset methodNormalize before using distances
Now switch from tangent lines to tangent circles. A circle of radius r tangent to a line has its center on one of two parallel offset lines. This is the most important reduction in the chapter, because it turns a nonlinear contact condition into an ordinary line intersection problem.
Circle Through a Point and Tangent to a Line

One Offset Line Meets One Radius Circle

Candidate Centers

Given \(P\), \(L\), and radius \(r\), centers satisfy two equations:

\(C\in L_{\pm r}\), and \(\lVert C-P\rVert=r\)

Signed Side Logic

If \(P\) is on one side of \(L\), only the offset on that side can contain centers for circles tangent at distance \(r\) and passing through \(P\).

If \(\operatorname{dist}(P,L)>2r\), the auxiliary line misses the radius circle. If equality holds, the two centers collapse to one.

Section 8.7 orientationLine-circle intersection
For a circle through a point and tangent to a line, combine two loci: centers must lie on an offset line and also on a radius-r circle around the point. The signed distance from the point to the line predicts whether the two loci intersect in two, one, or no centers.
Circles Tangent to Two Lines

Four Sign Choices Become Four Candidate Centers

Offset-Offset Construction

For nonparallel \(L_0,L_1\), construct \(L_0^{+}, L_0^{-}\) and \(L_1^{+}, L_1^{-}\). Intersections of all sign pairs are centers.

\(C_{\sigma,\tau}=L_0^{\sigma r}\cap L_1^{\tau r}\), where \(\sigma,\tau\in\{-1,+1\}\)

What to Store

Keep the sign pair with the center. It identifies which side of each line the circle touches and makes residual debugging transparent.

Section 8.8 orientationGeneric maximum: 4
With two nonparallel lines, both constraints are offset lines. There are two offsets for each original line, so the generic construction has four candidate centers. The implementation lesson is that sign choices are not afterthoughts; they are the enumeration mechanism for all tangent configurations.
Circle Through a Point and Tangent to a Circle

Point Constraint Plus Signed Circle Offset

Two Loci

The requested center \(C_1\) lies on a radius-\(r_1\) circle centered at \(P\), and also on an offset circle around \(C_0\).

external: \(\lVert C_1-C_0\rVert=r_0+r_1\)
internal: \(\lVert C_1-C_0\rVert=|r_0-r_1|\)

Containment matters. The same drawing rule must distinguish external contact, internal contact, and the case where the point lies inside the given circle.

Section 8.9 orientationCircle-circle intersection
For point-circle tangency with prescribed radius, the point again creates a circle of possible centers. The given circle creates one or more offset circles depending on internal or external tangency. Intersecting those auxiliary circles returns the candidates, while containment and boundary cases explain lost or doubled solutions.
Circle Tangent to a Line and a Circle

Pair Line Sides with Tangency Sense

Batch of Intersections

For requested radius \(r_1\), centers satisfy a line offset and a circle offset.

\(C_1\in L_{\pm r_1}\cap \partial B(C_0, r_0+r_1)\)
or \(C_1\in L_{\pm r_1}\cap \partial B(C_0, |r_0-r_1|)\)

Each sign and tangency sense is a branch. The candidate list may contain duplicates at thresholds, so the result type should support merging with a tolerance.

Section 8.10 orientationFilter duplicates after generation
The line-and-circle problem combines the two recurring offsets. The line gives parallel center lines, while the circle gives enlarged or reduced auxiliary circles. Pairing both line sides with both tangency senses can create many candidates, so filtering by residuals and duplicate merging is part of the algorithm.
Circle Tangent to Two Circles

Signed Radius Shifts Reduce to Circle-Circle Intersections

External Pair

For target radius \(r_2\), external tangency to both inputs gives auxiliary radii \(r_0+r_2\) and \(r_1+r_2\).

Signed Pair

Internal tangency changes a plus to an absolute difference. Enumerating signed shifts gives all branches before residual filtering.

intersect \(\partial B(C_0,\rho_0)\) and \(\partial B(C_1,\rho_1)\)
Section 8.11 orientationSame primitive as chapter 07 circle intersections
For a circle tangent to two circles with a prescribed radius, each input circle becomes an auxiliary circle whose radius is shifted by the requested radius. External and internal tangencies correspond to different signed shifts. The construction is therefore just a small batch of circle-circle intersections plus validation.
Line Construction Kernels

Simple Lines Carry the Coordinate Burden

Normal Through a Point

If \(L(t)=P_0+t d\), the normal through \(Q\) uses direction \(d^\perp=(-d_y,d_x)\). If \(L\) is implicit, the normal direction is \((a,b)\).

Equidistant Line

The line between two points is the perpendicular bisector: origin \(M=(Q_0+Q_1)/2\), direction \((Q_0-Q_1)^\perp\).

Parallel Offsets

Distance offsets preserve direction but move the origin along a unit normal. Vertical or horizontal offsets must be converted to perpendicular distance before reuse.

Sections 8.12 through 8.16 orientationLine primitives feed circle constructions
The closing line kernels are not less important because they are simple. Normal lines, perpendicular bisectors, parallel offsets, and vertical or horizontal offsets provide the coordinate infrastructure for the earlier circle constructions. They are also where normalized directions and implicit line scaling mistakes most often enter.
Multiplicity and Degeneracy

Thresholds Are Part of the Specification

Typical Thresholds

  • Point to circle: \(\lVert P-C\rVert\) compared with \(r\).
  • Two-point radius circle: \(\lVert P-Q\rVert\) compared with \(2r\).
  • Two circles: center distance compared with radius sum and radius difference.
  • Offset line and circle: signed distance compared with auxiliary radius.
return count is geometry, not an error code

Robust Graphics Implementation

Return Candidates with Evidence

Candidate Record

  • geometry: center, radius, line origin, direction, or contact point
  • branch: sign choices, internal or external tangency, normalized input state
  • multiplicity: empty, single, double, crossing pair, duplicate merge
  • evidence: distance residuals, dot-product residuals, threshold margin

Chapter Sanity Checks

The notebook asserts equal radii to constraints, tangent-radius perpendicularity, threshold solution counts, and dot-product tests for constructed perpendicular and parallel lines.

distance residual: \(|\,\lVert C-P\rVert-r\,|\)
line residual: \(|aC_x+bC_y+c|/\sqrt{a^2+b^2}-r\)
tangent residual: \(|(T-C)\cdot \hat d|\)

Do not hide the branch. Downstream graphics code needs to know whether a missing solution is impossible geometry, near degeneracy, or a tolerance decision.

Engineering endpointOriginal deck prose and diagrams
This is the engineering contract for the chapter. A construction routine should return more than coordinates: it should report the branch, multiplicity, residuals, and degeneracy flags. That extra state makes the notebook sanity checks meaningful and gives downstream graphics code a chance to explain failures.
Lecture Recap

Classify Before Computing

1. Name the Unknown

Is the unknown a point center, a line direction, a contact point, or a signed branch? Choosing the unknown determines the loci.

2. Intersect Loci

Use the smallest primitive that fits: line-line, line-circle, or circle-circle. Enumerate sign choices before filtering.

3. Certify the Result

Measure residuals in the original geometry: equal distances, tangency perpendicularity, line side, and duplicate thresholds.
