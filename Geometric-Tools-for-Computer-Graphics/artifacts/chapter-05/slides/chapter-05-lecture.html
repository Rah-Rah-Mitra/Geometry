Geometric Primitives in 2D

Geometry Atlas

A primitive is not only a shape; it is a representation, a parameter domain, and a small set of invariants that later queries rely on.

Lecture Contract

  • Choose implicit, parametric, barycentric, or control-point data to match the query.
  • Track boundary conventions: infinite, half-infinite, closed, solid, or sampled.
  • Attach checks: side tests, signed area, residuals, support intervals, and bounding boxes.

Query-Driven Choice

A side test wants an implicit residual. A closest point on a segment wants a clamped parameter. A curve editor wants control data and basis functions.

Use this slide to establish the recurring vocabulary before formulas appear. Ask students to identify the stored data, legal parameter values, boundary interpretation, and invariant for each primitive. That vocabulary turns the chapter into a set of design decisions that a graphics engine can test.
Lines, Rays, Segments

Implicit Form Classifies Space; Parametric Form Enumerates Points

linear components

Implicit Residual

\(f(X)=\vec n\cdot X-d\)

  • \(f(X)=0\): on the line.
  • \(f(X)>0\): side pointed to by \(\vec n\).
  • \(f(X)<0\): opposite side.

Parametric Enumerator

\(X(t)=P+t\vec d,\quad t\in\mathbb R\)

A parameter value is a coordinate along the line direction. Later, restricting that coordinate creates rays and segments.

Contrast the two line forms by what each makes cheap. The implicit residual immediately answers sidedness and signed distance after normalization. The parametric expression gives a point generator along a direction, so it is natural for sampling, projection, and later clamping to ray or segment domains.
Domain Restrictions

A Ray Or Segment Is A Line With A Smaller Legal Parameter Set

boundary convention

Three Linear Components

  • Line: \(t\in\mathbb R\).
  • Ray: \(t\ge 0\), with origin \(P\).
  • Segment: \(t\in[0,1]\) for \(X(t)=(1-t)P_0+tP_1\).

Conversion Pattern

Given \(x=P_x+t d_x\) and \(y=P_y+t d_y\), one implicit residual is

\(-d_y x+d_x y+(P_xd_y-P_yd_x)=0\)

Treat domain restriction as the whole point of the ray and segment representations. Many algorithms first compute the unconstrained line parameter, then project it back into the legal set. That clamping view prepares students for distance formulas and intersection tests in the following chapters.
Triangles

Orientation And Barycentric Coordinates Turn Three Vertices Into A Region

simplex domain

Signed Area Test

\(\Delta= (P_1-P_0)^\perp\cdot(P_2-P_0)\)

Positive sign records counterclockwise order, negative sign records clockwise order, and zero indicates collinearity.

Barycentric Contract

\(X=c_0P_0+c_1P_1+c_2P_2\)

\(c_i\ge0\) and \(c_0+c_1+c_2=1\) describe exactly the solid triangle.

Emphasize that vertex order is not cosmetic. It supplies orientation, normals by convention, and a sign for area-like predicates. Then connect barycentric coordinates to containment: if all weights are nonnegative and sum to one, the point is inside the solid triangle with no separate drawing needed.
Rectangles

Axis Alignment Is A Special Case; The Primitive Is A Frame With Extents

oriented boxes in 2D

Vertex-Edge Form

\(X(t_0,t_1)=P+t_0e_0+t_1e_1\)

\(t_0,t_1\in[0,1]\), with perpendicular edge vectors.

Symmetric Form

\(X=C+t_0u_0+t_1u_1\)

\(|t_0|\le e_0\), \(|t_1|\le e_1\). This form makes containment a pair of coordinate bounds.

Area Check

Parametric area is \(\|e_0\|\,\|e_1\|\). Symmetric area is \(4e_0e_1\).

Use rectangles to reinforce the frame idea from the line slide. An axis-aligned rectangle is only the easiest coordinate frame. The more general oriented rectangle is still simple because points can be expressed in its local axes, where containment and area checks become coordinate tests.
Polylines And Polygons

Topology Metadata Matters Before Any Numeric Query Runs

classification gallery

Open Chain

Adjacent segments share endpoints; the chain has distinct start and end.

Simple Convex

Every segment between interior points stays in the solid polygon.

Simple Concave

A chord between interior points can leave the region, so convex tests fail.

Nonsimple

Nonadjacent edges intersect, changing what inside and traversal mean.

Monotone polygon idea: a simple polygon can often be split into two chains that each meet lines orthogonal to a chosen direction in at most one connected set.

Pause on the taxonomy rather than racing to formulas. The same vertex array can be open, closed, simple, concave, convex, or self-intersecting depending on its edge relationships. Those labels decide whether area, point containment, triangulation, and monotone decomposition routines have valid preconditions.
Quadratic Curves

A Change Of Basis Makes Conic Classification Inspectable

matrix form

General Quadratic

\(X^TAX+B^TX+c=0\)

With \(A=A^T\), rotate into an eigenbasis: \(A=R^TDR\), \(Y=RX\), and \(E=RB\).

Classification Inputs

  • Signs and zero entries of \(d_0,d_1\).
  • The completed-square residual \(r\).
  • Degenerate cases: point, line pair, no real locus, or all space.
Present conic classification as a linear algebra story. The raw coefficients are hard to read, but diagonalizing the symmetric quadratic part reveals principal directions. Completing squares then separates genuine ellipses and hyperbolas from degenerate outcomes such as points, lines, or an empty real set.
Circle And Ellipse Forms

Metric, Parametric, And Quadratic Views Are The Same Shape From Different Queries

special conics

Circle Equivalences

\(\|X-C\|=r,\quad X(t)=C+r(\cos t,\sin t)\)

Quadratic coefficients satisfy \(B=-2C\) and \(c=C^TC-r^2\).

Ellipse Equivalences

\((X-C)^TR^TDR(X-C)=1\)

Half-lengths live in \(D\), orientation lives in \(R\), and parameterization uses the unit circle as the source domain.

Use circles and ellipses as a friendly bridge from the general conic equation back to geometry. Distance form is best for membership intuition, parametric form is best for sampling, and quadratic form is best when a larger algorithm already stores coefficients or applies affine transformations.
Polynomial Curves

Bezier Curves Trade Coefficients For A Geometric Control Polygon

Bernstein basis

Vector Polynomial View

\(X(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_jt^j,\quad t\in[0,1]\)

Good for algebra, but coefficient changes are not visually local.

Bezier Blend

\(X(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\choose i}t^i(1-t)^{n-i}P_i\)

The basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one, so the curve is organized by its control points.

Explain why graphics courses prefer the control polygon story for Bezier curves. The curve is still a polynomial, but the Bernstein basis makes endpoints, tangent intuition, and convex-hull behavior visible. That geometric handle is more useful to an editor than raw monomial coefficients.
B-Splines And NURBS

Knots Localize Polynomial Pieces; Weights Add Rational Projection

free-form curves

B-Spline Curve

\(X(t)=\sum_i B_{i,j}(t)P_i\)

Degree \(j\), knot vector \((t_0,\ldots,t_n)\), and piecewise polynomial basis functions control local support.

NURBS Curve

\(X(t)=\sum_i R_{i,j}(t)P_i,\quad R_{i,j}=\frac{w_iB_{i,j}}{\sum_k w_kB_{k,j}}\)

Positive weights create a rational curve by homogeneous projection, giving conic-like precision inside the same control framework.

Separate three ideas that students often merge. B-splines are piecewise polynomial curves controlled by knots and local basis support. Nonuniform knots change the parameter spacing. NURBS adds positive weights and a homogeneous division, which lets rational curves represent shapes unavailable to ordinary polynomial splines.
Worked Query Design

Pick The Representation That Makes The Predicate A Small Residual

implementation lens

Representation Checklist

  1. State the query as a predicate, projection, distance, sampler, or editor operation.
  2. Choose the coordinate domain where legal points are easiest to characterize.
  3. Compute a residual or certificate that can be tested against tolerance.
  4. Attach a geometric invariant to catch scaling, orientation, or endpoint mistakes.
Side of a line

Use \(f(X)=\vec n\cdot X-d\). Normalize \(\vec n\) when signed distance magnitude matters.

Inside a triangle

Use barycentric weights and require nonnegative coordinates whose sum is one.

Sample a curve

Use a parametric curve basis and verify endpoints or bounding boxes from sampled points.

Make this the bridge from definitions to algorithms. Students should not memorize one representation per primitive; they should ask which representation turns the current query into a simple residual, parameter clamp, or domain membership test. That habit will pay off immediately in distance and intersection chapters.
Applied Lab

Stress-Test A Primitive By Varying One Domain, One Tolerance, And One Frame

student exercise

1. Choose A Primitive

Line component, triangle, rectangle, polygon, conic, Bezier, B-spline, or NURBS curve.

Record its stored data and legal parameter domain.

2. Perturb A Contract

Move one vertex, rotate one frame, change one knot, or adjust one NURBS weight.

Keep all other choices fixed so the effect is attributable.

3. Check A Certificate

Use residual zero, signed area, containment bounds, support intervals, or sampled bounding boxes.

Distinguish geometry changes from representation artifacts.

Assign the lab as controlled variation, not as free drawing. Students should change one aspect of the primitive contract and predict which invariant will move. For example, moving a triangle vertex changes signed area, while merely rotating an oriented rectangle frame should preserve its area.
Recap

The Chapter Is A Representation Atlas For Later Geometry Queries

takeaways

What To Remember

  • Implicit equations give residuals, signs, and algebraic classification.
  • Parametric domains turn infinite objects into rays, segments, regions, and curve samples.
  • Orientation, simplicity, convexity, and monotonicity are preconditions, not decorations.
  • Control-point curves encode shape through basis functions, knots, and weights.

Sanity Checks

Line residuals vanish on samples, polygon area reports orientation, Bezier endpoints match controls, and sampled curve boxes contain every sample.

Close by naming the transfer to later chapters. Distance and intersection algorithms will look less mysterious once students recognize which primitive contract they consume. The useful habit is to keep representation, domain, boundary convention, and invariant together whenever a new geometric object enters code.
