Linear Components

One Formula, Three Domains

Definition Pattern
\(X(t)=P+t d\), where \(d\ne 0\).
The primitive is incomplete until the admissible values of \(t\) are recorded.
Normal Form View
A 3D line can also be described as the intersection of two nonparallel planes: \(n_0\cdot X=c_0\) and \(n_1\cdot X=c_1\).
Implementation check. Store the domain with the representation. Otherwise a segment endpoint, a ray origin, and a whole line can be silently treated as the same object.
Source orientation: Section 9.1Parameter restrictions are geometric data
Treat the parameter interval as part of the primitive, not a later clipping detail. A line, ray, and segment can share the same formula, yet the domain determines closest-point, intersection, and containment behavior. The normal form is useful when a line is the intersection of two planes.
Planar Components

Plane Equations Become Local Coordinate Frames

Frame Contract
Given \(P\) and unit normal \(n\), choose unit vectors \(u,v\) so that \(u\cdot n=0\), \(v=n\times u\), and \(R=[u\ v\ n]\) is orthonormal.
normal\(n\cdot(X-P)=0\)
implicit\(a x+b y+c z+d=0\)
parametric\(X(s,t)=P+s u+t v\)
Numerical Habit
Choose the zero component of \(u\) using the largest safe normal components, then normalize once and assert the dot products.
Notebook check: basis vectors are orthonormal and perpendicular to normalPlane frames support every planar object
Slow down on the frame construction because many later algorithms depend on it. The robust choice of an in-plane vector avoids dividing by a nearly zero normal component. After choosing u, the cross product produces v, and the dot-product checks confirm that the basis is usable.
2D Objects Embedded in 3D

Use a Plane Basis as an Adapter

Embedding Map
For a planar set \(S\subset\mathbb{R}^2\), place it in 3D by \[ X=P+xu+yv,\qquad (x,y)\in S. \]
Recovery Map
For a point \(Q\) on the plane, \[ x=u\cdot(Q-P),\qquad y=v\cdot(Q-P). \] A 2D polygon algorithm can then operate on \((x,y)\).

Projection to a coordinate plane is cheaper when congruence is not needed, but the chosen normal component must avoid collapse.

Source orientation: Section 9.2.3Local coordinates transfer mature 2D tools into 3D
The key teaching move is to let two-dimensional algorithms work in a three-dimensional scene without pretending the object is flat in world coordinates. The embedding map builds points in space, while the recovery map projects them back into local coordinates where polygon, circle, or triangulation routines are simpler.
Polymeshes, Polyhedra, and Polytopes

Meshes Are Incidence Structures, Not Just Pictures

Validity Invariants
A graphics mesh records which vertices form edges, which edges bound faces, how face order orients normals, and whether the surface is closed or has boundary.
  • No isolated vertices in the active mesh.
  • No edges unused by faces when a surface mesh is expected.
  • Face intersections must be represented by shared vertices or edges.
  • Closed polyhedra support inside/outside and volume queries.
Notebook check: mesh Euler counts match the constructed exampleTriangle soup is not automatically a polyhedron
Meshes are not just arrays of triangles. They are incidence structures with assumptions about which vertices, edges, and faces are allowed to meet. Ask students to distinguish a drawable triangle soup from a valid mesh, then connect the distinction to manifoldness, closure, orientation, and Euler checks.

For quadrics, the matrix representation is the useful abstraction. Diagonalization rotates the coordinate system to expose the surface type, and completing the square locates centers or degeneracies. The residual is the practical check: sampled points should drive the implicit equation close to zero.
Torus

Parametric Grid for Rendering, Implicit Equation for Checking

Parameterization
\[ x=(r_0+r_1\cos v)\cos u,\quad y=(r_0+r_1\cos v)\sin u,\quad z=r_1\sin v. \]
Implicit Residual
\[ \rho(X)=\left(r_0-\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\right)^2+z^2-r_1^2. \] A sampled torus should make \(|\rho(X)|\) numerically tiny.

Shape regimes. Ring, horn, and spindle cases are controlled by the ratio \(r_0/r_1\); the same code path should expose the regime instead of hiding it.

Source orientation: Section 9.5Notebook final sanity: max residual below tolerance
Use the torus to compare representations of the same object. The parametric grid is ideal for rendering and normals, while the implicit equation is ideal for residual checks and containment-style tests. Major and minor radii also provide a clean way to discuss ring, horn, and spindle cases.
Polynomial Curves and Surfaces

Control Data Plus Basis Functions Over a Domain

Curve Template
\[ X(t)=\sum_i b_i(t) P_i,\qquad t\in D. \] Bezier curves use Bernstein bases; B-splines add knots and locality; NURBS add weights and homogeneous projection.
Surface Template
\[ X(s,t)=\sum_i\sum_j b_i(s)c_j(t)P_{ij} \] for rectangular patches; triangular patches use barycentric coordinates.

The representation records domain shape, control lattice topology, basis support, and whether rational weights are present.

Source orientation: Sections 9.6 and 9.7Basis, knots, weights, and domains are part of the primitive
The curve and surface families share a pattern: control data, basis functions, parameter domains, and sometimes homogeneous weights. Keep the focus on representation choices rather than memorizing every formula. Bezier, B-spline, and NURBS objects differ mainly in locality, knot structure, and rational weighting.
