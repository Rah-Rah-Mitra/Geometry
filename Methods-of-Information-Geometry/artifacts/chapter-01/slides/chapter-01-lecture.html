CoordinatesChapter 01

Manifolds and Charts

Smooth manifold

A set covered by charts whose overlap maps are smooth, so calculus is well-defined independent of coordinates.

Statistical model

$S=\{p(x;\theta):\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\}$

Parameters as coordinates

The parameter $\theta$ is a coordinate system on the model manifold.

Reparametrization

Changing parameters is a coordinate change; geometric quantities must remain invariant under it.

Printed pp. 1-24 · PDF 10-33Manifolds and Charts
Define a smooth manifold as a set covered by coordinate charts whose overlaps are smooth. Emphasize that a statistical model, a parametrized family of distributions, is exactly such a manifold with the parameter serving as a coordinate system. Coordinate changes correspond to reparametrizations of the model, which must leave geometric quantities invariant.
Directions of changeChapter 01

Tangent Vectors

Coordinate basis

$\partial_i=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta^i}$

Tangent vectors are directional derivatives; the coordinate partials span the tangent space.

Score function

$\partial_i\,\ell(\theta)=\partial_i\log p(x;\theta)$

Statistical meaning

Tangent vectors are identified with scores, linking geometry to estimation.

Printed pp. 1-24 · PDF 10-33Tangent Vectors
Introduce tangent vectors as directional derivatives and the tangent space at a point as their span. A basis is given by the coordinate partial derivatives. In the statistical setting tangent vectors are identified with score functions, the derivatives of the log-likelihood, which links geometry directly to estimation theory.
Length and angleChapter 01

Riemannian Metric

Metric tensor

$g_{ij}(\theta)=\langle\partial_i,\partial_j\rangle$

Fisher information

$g_{ij}=E[\partial_i\ell\,\partial_j\ell]$

Distinguishability

The Fisher metric measures how distinguishable nearby distributions are.

It is the canonical, reparametrization-invariant metric on a statistical model.

Printed pp. 1-24 · PDF 10-33Riemannian Metric
Define a Riemannian metric as a smoothly varying inner product on tangent spaces, giving lengths and angles. In information geometry the canonical metric is the Fisher information matrix, which measures how distinguishable nearby distributions are. This metric makes the manifold of distributions into a genuine geometric space.
Differentiation along the manifoldChapter 01

Affine Connections

Covariant derivative

$\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j=\Gamma_{ij}^k\,\partial_k$

A connection compares tangent vectors at different points and defines geodesics and parallel transport.

Christoffel symbols

$\Gamma_{ij}^k$ encode the connection in coordinates.

Looking ahead

Information geometry uses a whole family of connections, not just Levi-Civita.

Printed pp. 1-24 · PDF 10-33Affine Connections
Introduce affine connections and covariant differentiation, which let us compare tangent vectors at different points and define geodesics and parallel transport. The Christoffel symbols encode the connection in coordinates. Information geometry will exploit a whole family of connections, not just the Levi-Civita one, in the next chapters.
