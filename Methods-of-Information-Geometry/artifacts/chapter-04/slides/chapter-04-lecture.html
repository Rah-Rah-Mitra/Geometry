Frame an estimator as a map from samples to points on the model manifold. Unbiasedness, consistency, and efficiency all become geometric statements about how the estimator sits relative to the true distribution. The Fisher metric measures local error, and the dual connections describe how that error transports across the manifold during inference.

Maximum Likelihood as Projection

MLE objective

$\hat\theta=\arg\min_\theta \mathrm{KL}(\hat p\,\|\,p_\theta)$

The empirical distribution is projected onto the model along a mixture geodesic.

Uniqueness

On a dually flat model the projection is unique.

Optimality

The Pythagorean theorem makes its optimality transparent.

Printed pp. 81-114 · PDF 90-123Maximum Likelihood as Projection
Recast maximum-likelihood estimation as m-projection of the empirical distribution onto the model along a mixture geodesic. By the generalized Pythagorean theorem this projection minimizes the Kullback-Leibler divergence and is unique on a dually flat model. The geometric view makes the optimality of maximum likelihood transparent rather than computational.
The metric limitChapter 04

Cramer-Rao Bound

Lower bound

$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\theta)\succeq g^{-1}(\theta)$

No unbiased estimator beats the inverse Fisher information.

Geometric reading

The metric sets the finest distinguishable scale on the model.

Efficiency

Efficient estimators sit exactly at this limit.

Printed pp. 81-114 · PDF 90-123Cramer-Rao Bound
Derive the Cramer-Rao lower bound directly from the Fisher information metric: no unbiased estimator can have covariance smaller than the inverse Fisher matrix. Geometrically, the bound expresses that the metric sets the finest distinguishable scale on the model. Efficient estimators attain it, sitting exactly at the metric limit of precision.

Meaning

Printed pp. 81-114 · PDF 90-123Higher-Order Asymptotics
Go beyond first order using curvature. The exponential and mixture curvatures of the model and of the estimator control second-order bias and variance. A model that is e-flat and an estimator that is m-flat achieve higher-order efficiency. Curvature thus quantifies exactly how much information is lost beyond the Cramer-Rao limit.
