Printed pp. 133-144 · PDF 142-153Hypothesis Testing Exponents
Recall that in hypothesis testing the error probability decays exponentially, with the exponent given by a Kullback-Leibler divergence through Stein's lemma. In the multiterminal setting the achievable exponent shrinks because compression restricts the available information. The divergence geometry makes the optimal exponent a clean projection quantity.

Divergence Geometry of Testing

Optimal exponent

$E^*=\min_{q\in\mathcal{C}_R} D(p\,\|\,q)$

The best exponent is the divergence to the nearest feasible alternative.

m-projection

The minimizer is an m-projection onto the constraint set.

Loss split

Pythagoras separates statistical from communication loss.

Printed pp. 133-144 · PDF 142-153Divergence Geometry of Testing
Express the best testing exponent as a divergence from the true distribution to the nearest alternative consistent with the rate constraint. This is an m-projection onto the constraint set, and the generalized Pythagorean theorem decomposes the loss into a statistical part and a communication part. Geometry separates the two sources of degradation cleanly.

Trade-off

Printed pp. 133-144 · PDF 142-153Rate-Constrained Estimation
Turn to estimation under communication constraints. The achievable Fisher information is reduced by a factor reflecting the transmitted rate, and the constrained Cramer-Rao bound follows from the metric restricted to feasible encodings. The geometry quantifies precisely how compression trades away statistical precision in a distributed system.
