Quantum States

State

Pure and mixed

New difficulty

Printed pp. 145-166 · PDF 154-175Quantum States
Replace probability distributions with density operators: positive semidefinite, unit-trace matrices on a Hilbert space. Pure states are rank-one projectors and mixed states are their convex combinations. This state space is again a manifold, but non-commutativity of operators makes its geometry richer and more subtle than the classical case studied earlier.

Monotone Metrics

Petz classification

Metrics monotone under quantum channels correspond to operator-monotone functions.

There is a whole family, unlike the unique classical Fisher metric.

Extremes

$g_{\text{SLD}}\ \le\ g\ \le\ g_{\text{RLD}}$

Choice matters

The metric used affects achievable estimation precision.

Printed pp. 145-166 · PDF 154-175Monotone Metrics
Introduce quantum Fisher metrics. Unlike the classical case there is a whole family of metrics monotone under quantum channels, characterized by Petz through operator-monotone functions. The smallest is the SLD or symmetric-logarithmic-derivative metric and the largest is the right-logarithmic-derivative metric, and the choice matters for estimation.
Distinguishing statesChapter 07

Quantum Divergences

Relative entropy

$D(\rho\,\|\,\sigma)=\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)$

The operator analogue of Kullback-Leibler, monotone under channels.

Distinguishability

Divergences quantify how well states can be told apart.

Quantum Stein

They drive quantum hypothesis testing exponents.

Printed pp. 145-166 · PDF 154-175Quantum Divergences
Define quantum relative entropy and the broader family of quantum divergences between density operators. Quantum relative entropy is the operator analogue of Kullback-Leibler and shares its monotonicity under channels. These divergences quantify distinguishability of quantum states and underlie quantum hypothesis testing through the quantum Stein lemma.
Ultimate precisionChapter 07

Quantum Cramer-Rao Bound

Bound

$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\ge \dfrac{1}{J_{\text{SLD}}(\theta)}$

Covariance is bounded by the inverse quantum Fisher information.

Optimal measurement

Attaining the bound requires the right measurement.

Metrology

Sets the ultimate limit of quantum estimation.

Printed pp. 145-166 · PDF 154-175Quantum Cramer-Rao Bound
State the quantum Cramer-Rao bound: estimator covariance is bounded below by the inverse quantum Fisher information, with the SLD metric giving the tightest single-parameter bound. Achieving it requires an optimal measurement. The bound sets the ultimate precision of quantum metrology and shows where classical and quantum estimation diverge.
