Natural parametersChapter 02

Exponential Families

Density

$p(x;\theta)=\exp\!\big(\theta^i F_i(x)-\psi(\theta)\big)$

The log-density is affine in $\theta$, so the natural parameters form a flat coordinate system, the e-coordinates.

Examples

Normal, Poisson, Bernoulli, and many other standard models are exponential families.

Why it matters

Affine log-densities give the geometry a clean, broadly applicable flat structure.

Printed pp. 25-50 · PDF 34-59Exponential Families
Introduce the exponential family as the central example: the log-density is affine in the natural parameter. This affine structure makes the natural parameters a flat coordinate system, the so-called e-coordinates. Many familiar models, including the normal, Poisson, and Bernoulli distributions, are exponential families, which gives the geometry broad reach.
Mixture parametersChapter 02

Mixture Families

Density

$p(x)=\textstyle\sum_i \eta^i\,p_i(x)$

The density is an affine combination of fixed distributions, so the mixture weights form the flat m-coordinates.

Two kinds of flatness

Exponential families are e-flat; mixture families are m-flat.

Looking ahead

Duality will tie these complementary flat structures together.

Printed pp. 25-50 · PDF 34-59Mixture Families
Introduce the mixture family, where the density itself is an affine combination of fixed distributions. Its mixture parameters form a second flat coordinate system, the m-coordinates. Exponential and mixture families illustrate the two complementary kinds of flatness that information geometry weaves together through duality in the chapters ahead.
Legendre dualityChapter 02

Dual Coordinates

Expectation parameter

$\eta_i=\partial_i\psi(\theta)=E[F_i]$

Two charts

The natural parameter $\theta$ and the expectation parameter $\eta$ describe the same model.

Legendre transform

$\varphi(\eta)=\theta^i\eta_i-\psi(\theta)$

Seed of duality

This pairing yields a connection flat in $\theta$ and a dual flat in $\eta$.

Printed pp. 25-50 · PDF 34-59Dual Coordinates
Present the two coordinate systems side by side: the natural parameter and the expectation parameter. They are related by the Legendre transform of a convex potential. This pairing is the seed of the dually flat structure, where one connection is flat in one chart and its dual is flat in the other.
Convex generatorsChapter 02

Potential Functions

Metric from potential

$g_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j\psi(\theta)$

The log-partition function $\psi$ is convex; its Hessian is the Fisher information metric.

Dual potential

$\varphi$, the negative entropy, is the Legendre dual of $\psi$ and generates the dual chart.

One function, all structure

A single convex potential yields metric and dual connections.

Printed pp. 25-50 · PDF 34-59Potential Functions
Define the potential functions: the log-partition function and its Legendre dual, the negative entropy. Their second derivatives recover the Fisher information metric, and their Legendre relationship encodes the dual coordinates. Potentials thus generate the entire metric and connection structure from a single convex function on the model.
