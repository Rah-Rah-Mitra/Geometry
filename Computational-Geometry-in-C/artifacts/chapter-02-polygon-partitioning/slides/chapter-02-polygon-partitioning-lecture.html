Cut a difficult polygon into monotone, trapezoidal, mountain, and convex pieces whose structure makes later algorithms simpler.

Motivation

Whole-Polygon Triangulation Versus Cut-First Strategy

Whole polygon

  • Every local notch must be handled by the triangulation procedure.
  • Algorithm invariants mix topology, visibility, and ordering.
  • One global failure can corrupt the whole decomposition.
Risk
The hard events are still present when the final algorithm begins.

Cut first

  • Find structural diagonals with a sweep or local test.
  • Run simpler algorithms on monotone or convex subproblems.
  • Attach correctness to each preprocessing invariant.
Strategy
Pay \(O(n \log n)\) once, then solve easy pieces with tight local rules.
Partitioning as preprocessingthe cuts simplify the invariants
Make the comparison concrete: partitioning is not competing with triangulation so much as preparing for it. A cut-first strategy isolates the difficult sweep events, then asks each resulting piece to satisfy a stronger geometric promise before triangulation or convex processing begins.
Definition

Y-Monotone Polygons: Horizontal Lines See One Interval

Definition 2.1
A polygon is y-monotone when every horizontal line intersects its interior in the empty set, one point, or one line segment.
Equivalent Picture
The boundary splits into two chains from the top vertex to the bottom vertex. Each chain is monotone in \(y\).
  • Horizontal-line test: connected slice, never two separated intervals.
  • Top-to-bottom ordering gives a natural vertex order for triangulation.
  • Rotating the coordinate direction gives monotonicity in other directions.
Notebook checkmonotone example max horizontal intersections = 2
Spend time on the horizontal-line test because it is the chapter's most useful mental model. A monotone polygon can still be nonconvex, but the sweep never has to maintain two disconnected interior intervals for the same piece.

Symptom

Local Criterion

Reflex Vertices Whose Neighbors Lie On One Side

Cusp Criterion
For a chosen sweep direction, an interior cusp is a reflex vertex whose two boundary neighbors lie locally on the same side of the sweep level.

Reflex test

The internal angle exceeds \(180^\circ\), so the polygon turns inward at \(v\). Cusps are the reflex vertices that also create split or merge events for the sweep.

Notebook Ledger
The notched example records reflex vertices 4, 5, 8, and 9. These are the local obstructions later counted by convex partition bounds.
Local rulereflex plus same-side neighbors signals a cusp event
Separate two ideas that students often merge. Reflex means the internal angle bends inward; cusp means that inward bend matters to the chosen sweep direction. The same vertex can be harmless for one direction and troublesome for another.
Algorithmic Payoff

Monotone Triangulation: Sorted Vertices And A Stack

Stack Intuition
Process vertices from top to bottom. The stack stores the current visible chain; when visibility changes, pop vertices and draw diagonals.

Sweep Construction

Trapezoidalization: Supports And Active Edge Order

Trapezoidalization
Extend horizontal supports from event vertices until they hit the nearest visible polygon edge. The resulting cells are trapezoid-like vertical-order regions.
  • Sort vertices by sweep coordinate.
  • Maintain active edges in left-to-right order.
  • At cusp events, query the neighboring active edge.
Check
The notebook support level is \(y=1.8\), matching the cusp level in the synthetic notched polygon.
Sweep-line rolebalanced active edge order gives O(n log n)
Explain the data-structure role of trapezoidalization. Sorting supplies the event order, while the active edge structure answers the geometric question: if a cusp shoots a support ray horizontally, which boundary edge does it encounter first?

Algorithm Box

Sort, Sweep, Connect Cusps, Triangulate Pieces

1
Sort. Order vertices by the sweep coordinate.
2
Sweep. Maintain the active edge order and classify events.
3
Connect cusps. Insert support diagonals that destroy split and merge events.
4
Triangulate. Run the monotone stack procedure on each piece.
Running Time
The accessible sweep-based route is \(O(n \log n)\): sorting plus balanced active-edge updates dominate the local geometry.
Implementation Caution
Degenerate equal-height vertices need tie handling, but the conceptual invariant is unchanged: active edges are ordered by current sweep intersection.
Chapter-level algorithmO(n log n) preprocessing plus monotone triangulation
This is the operational slide. The chapter is not asking students to memorize every low-level case; it asks them to see why sorting and active-edge order locate the supports, and why the resulting monotone pieces make the remaining triangulation routine simple.
Special Structure

Monotone Mountains: A Base Plus One Convex Chain

Definition 2.2
A monotone mountain has one distinguished base edge and a remaining boundary chain that is convex from one base endpoint to the other.
  • It is y-monotone after orienting the base below the chain.
  • The convex chain exposes ears in a predictable order.
  • The structure is strong enough for a linear triangulation argument.
Notebook Check
The synthetic mountain has 6 vertices and triangulates into 4 triangles, matching \(n-2\).
Mountain structurebase edge plus convex chain
Describe mountains as a useful extreme of monotonicity. The base anchors the polygon, and the upper chain is convex enough that ear choices become visually forced. That makes mountains a clean bridge between monotone partitioning and linear triangulation.
Lemma

Mountain Ear Lemma: Convex Chain Vertices Can Be Removed

Mountain Ear Lemma
In a monotone mountain, at least one internal vertex of the convex chain is an ear tip: its adjacent vertices see each other through the polygon.
Proof Roadmap
Convexity of the chain keeps the candidate triangle inside the mountain. Removing that triangle leaves a smaller mountain, so induction can continue.

The lemma is the structural reason a mountain can be triangulated without returning to a global search for arbitrary ears.

Ear structurevisible ears support induction
Use the colored triangles to make the lemma feel geometric rather than magical. The convex chain bends the right way, so an ear triangle does not poke outside the polygon. After clipping it, the remaining polygon is again a smaller mountain.

Context

Linear-Time Triangulation: Chazelle And Seidel

Where this chapter sits

  • Sweep partitioning gives a clear \(O(n \log n)\) route.
  • Mountains show why special structure can be triangulated linearly.
  • The broader literature asks whether arbitrary simple polygons can be triangulated in linear time.
Role Of History
The named algorithms are context for the asymptotic frontier, not prerequisites for implementing the chapter's sweep construction.
Asymptotic storyaccessible sweep versus linear-time frontier
Keep the history balanced. Chazelle proves that linear triangulation is possible, while Seidel gives a randomized path that is important in computational geometry practice. For this lecture, their role is to show that partitioning is part of a larger search for exploitable polygon structure.
Convex Partitioning

Approximation And Recap

Hertel-Mehlhorn: Triangulate, Then Remove Inessential Diagonals

Essential Versus Inessential
A diagonal is inessential when removing it merges two adjacent pieces and the union remains convex. Essential diagonals protect a reflex obstruction.
  • Start from any valid triangulation.
  • Scan diagonals and remove those certified as inessential.
  • Keep a convex partition that is fast and near the right scale.
