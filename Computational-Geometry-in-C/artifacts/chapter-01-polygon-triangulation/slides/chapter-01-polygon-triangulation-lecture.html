Definition Contract
simple closed chain

What Counts As A Polygon

Simple Polygon

A cyclic list of vertices joined by boundary segments, where nonadjacent boundary segments do not cross.

Boundary Convention

The boundary is part of the polygonal region. Algorithmic tests still distinguish interior, boundary, and exterior events.

Why It Matters

Triangulation counts, visibility, ears, parity, and guard coverage all assume a well-defined inside.

Representation: cyclic previous and next verticesTopology: inside/outside is meaningful only for simple input
Slow down here because many later tests silently assume this contract. A simple polygon is a closed cyclic boundary that does not cross itself, so every edge has a previous and next edge and the curve separates inside from outside. The self-intersecting example is not a different input style; it violates the object the triangulation theorem is about.
Visibility
segment-inside-polygon question

Visibility And Guarding

Visibility

Points \(p\) and \(q\) are visible when the open segment \(pq\) stays inside the polygonal region.

Guard Problem

Choose a set of guard points whose visibility regions cover the entire polygon.

Triangulation makes the problem finite: if each triangle is guarded, the polygon is guarded.

Visibility is a predicate, not merely a drawingBlocked sight lines motivate segment intersection tests
Introduce visibility as a segment-inside-polygon question. Two points see each other when the open segment between them stays in the polygon's interior, with boundary endpoint conventions handled separately. The guard formulation is then natural: choose vertices or points whose visibility regions cover the polygon, and triangulation will soon make that coverage finite.
Art Gallery Intuition
worst-case ceiling

Why \(\lfloor n/3 \rfloor\) Appears

Art Gallery Bound

Every simple polygon with \(n\) vertices can be guarded by at most \(\lfloor n/3 \rfloor\) vertex guards.

Small Cases

Triangles and quadrilaterals need one guard; the formula gives \(1\).

Comb Intuition

Each deep notch can hide from guards placed too far away, so the bound cannot be improved in general.

The theorem is an upper bound for every simple polygonSome families make the bound tight
The art gallery bound should feel surprising before it becomes formal. The comb picture suggests why every third notch can force a new guard, while the small n cases remind students that the formula is a worst-case ceiling, not a claim that every polygon needs that many guards. This prepares the class for Fisk's coloring proof.
Proof Roadmap
triangulate, color, choose, cover

Fisk's Proof In Four Moves

1. Triangulate

Decompose the polygon into \(n-2\) triangles using noncrossing diagonals.

2. 3-Color

Color the triangulation graph so every triangle has three different colors.

3. Choose Smallest

At least one color class has size at most \(\lfloor n/3 \rfloor\).

4. Guard Triangles

Every triangle contains one selected-color vertex, so every triangle is seen.

The proof uses triangulation as a cover by certified trianglesPigeonhole principle supplies the guard count
Present Fisk's proof as a compressed algorithmic certificate. Triangulate the polygon, 3-color the triangulation graph, choose the smallest color class, and observe that every triangle has all three colors. Since the triangles cover the polygon, the chosen color class sees the whole polygon, and pigeonhole gives the floor n over 3 bound.
Diagonals
legal versus crossing

Diagonals And Triangulations

Diagonal

A segment between nonadjacent polygon vertices that lies in the polygon and does not cross the boundary.

Triangulation

A maximal set of noncrossing diagonals that decomposes the polygon into triangles.

Invalid Candidate

A segment can connect two vertices and still fail by exiting the polygon or crossing an edge.

A diagonal is certified by visibility plus endpoint localityA triangulation is a noncrossing certificate
Define a legal diagonal with both global and local responsibilities. It connects nonadjacent vertices, avoids crossing boundary edges, and lies inside the polygon near both endpoints. The contrast between the blue diagonal and the amber crossing segment sets up why a triangulation is a maximal noncrossing collection, not merely several attractive chords.
Existence Lemma
convex vertex and blocked chord

Existence Of A Diagonal

Proof Move

Choose a convex vertex \(v\). Test the segment between its two neighbors.

If Clear

The neighbor chord is a diagonal and splits the polygon.

If Blocked

A polygon vertex inside the candidate triangle provides a smaller local configuration that yields a diagonal.

The triangulation theorem starts with one certified splitExistence is constructive enough to guide algorithms
Sketch the proof move rather than treating existence as magic. Pick a convex vertex and test the chord between its neighbors. If that chord is clear, it is a diagonal. If it is blocked, the blocking vertex inside the candidate triangle gives a smaller local choice that yields a diagonal. This is the proof's constructive heartbeat.
Counting
split recurrence

Counting Triangles And Diagonals

Triangulation Counts

Every triangulation of a simple \(n\)-gon has \(T(n)=n-2\) triangles and \(D(n)=n-3\) internal diagonals.

Split Accounting

A diagonal creates subpolygons with sizes \(a\) and \(b\), where \(a+b=n+2\) because the diagonal endpoints are counted twice.

Notebook Check

For \(n=8\): \(T=6\), \(D=5\), and triangle areas sum to \(5.44\).

Counts are independent of which triangulation is chosenThey become executable sanity checks
Make the two counting formulas feel inevitable from a split. One diagonal divides an n-gon into two smaller polygons whose sizes overlap at the diagonal endpoints. Induction then gives T(n)=n-2 triangles and D(n)=n-3 internal diagonals. The notebook's checked eight-vertex polygon produces six triangles and five diagonals, matching the formulas.
Structure
weak dual tree

Dual Tree And Two Ears

Weak Dual

Make one node per triangle; connect two nodes when the triangles share an internal diagonal.

Tree Consequence

The weak dual of a polygon triangulation is a tree, so it has leaves.

Two-Ears Theorem

Leaf triangles correspond to ears. The checked example has 6 dual nodes, 5 dual edges, and 4 ear leaves.

Graph leaves identify removable trianglesEar clipping follows the dual-tree intuition
Translate the triangulation into graph language. Each triangle becomes a node, and shared diagonals become edges of the weak dual. Because the dual is a tree, it has leaves; leaf triangles touch the rest of the triangulation along one diagonal and therefore are ears. The notebook example has six dual nodes, five dual edges, and four leaf ears.
Fisk Coloring
guards from smallest color class

Three-Coloring And Fisk Guards

Invariant

Every triangle in the triangulation graph has one vertex of each color.

Guard Choice

Choose the smallest color class. It has size at most \(\lfloor n/3 \rfloor\).

Checked Example

For \(n=8\), selected guards are vertices \(1\) and \(5\); \(2 \le \lfloor 8/3 \rfloor\).

Coloring is possible because triangulation graphs are maximal outerplanarCoverage follows triangle by triangle
Show the coloring as a maintained invariant on the triangulation graph. Every triangle receives one vertex of each color, so placing guards on any one color class covers every triangle. The checked notebook polygon selects color 2 with vertices 1 and 5, which is within floor of eight over three and makes the guard theorem concrete.
Determinants

Signed Area Is The Predicate Engine

Shoelace

\(2A(P)=\sum_i (x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\)

Orientation Predicate

\(\operatorname{area2}(a,b,c)=\det(b-a,\;c-a)\)

Checked Invariant

CCW orientation, shoelace area \(5.44\), triangle-fan area \(5.44\), and odd ray parity for the inside query.

Positive signed area fixes the counterclockwise conventionThe same determinant powers local branch decisions
Connect area, orientation, and implementation in one formula. The shoelace sum gives the polygon's signed area and confirms counterclockwise orientation when positive. The same determinant, restricted to three points, drives left-turn tests. The notebook check reports area 5.44 and equal triangle-fan area, making area conservation a testable invariant.
Predicate Design
avoid slope special cases

From Slopes To Orientation Predicates

Slope Trap

Vertical and nearly parallel segments force special cases before the algorithm has even reached geometry.

One Sign Test

\(\operatorname{sign}\big((b_x-a_x)(c_y-a_y)-(b_y-a_y)(c_x-a_x)\big)\)

C Vocabulary

area2, left, left_on, collinear, between turn determinant signs into named decisions.

Predicates make geometric intent explicitRobust code branches on signs, not pictures
Warn students away from slope comparisons without making the point only numerical. Slopes need special vertical cases and become fragile near parallel lines, while the determinant gives one sign convention for left, right, and collinear triples. This is why the C vocabulary in the chapter is built around area2, left, left_on, collinear, and between.

Diagonal Predicate
edge scan plus InCone

Diagonal Testing: Global Edge Tests Plus Local InCone

Global Test

The candidate \(v_i v_j\) must not properly intersect any polygon edge except those incident to its endpoints.

Local Test

\(\operatorname{InCone}(i,j)\) checks whether \(v_j\) lies inside the interior wedge at \(v_i\).

Reflex Vertex

At a reflex vertex, the acceptable cone is the complement of the visually smaller exterior wedge.

Local interior wedge is necessary but not sufficientGlobal edge scan is necessary but not sufficient
Separate the diagonal test into the two questions the implementation asks. Globally, the candidate segment must avoid all unrelated polygon edges. Locally, the InCone test checks that the chord leaves each endpoint through the interior wedge, with reflex vertices reversing the intuitive picture. Both parts are needed before an ear is safe to clip.

Ear Clipping As Algorithmic Proof

Loop

while more than 3 vertices: find convex empty ear (prev, tip, next) record diagonal prev-next delete tip from the boundary record final triangle

Theorem-Level Checks

\(n-2\) triangles, \(n-3\) diagonals, conserved area, and a tree-shaped dual.

Cost

A straightforward scan may test \(O(n)\) candidate ears across \(O(n)\) removals, giving \(O(n^2)\).

Table source: ear-clipping-removal-order.csvThe proof becomes a terminating mutation of the boundary list
Close by making the algorithm look like the proof running in a loop. Find a convex empty ear, record its diagonal or final triangle, remove the ear tip, and repeat. A straightforward implementation scans candidates at each removal, giving O(n^2) time. The chapter's theorem-level checks then become program checks: n-2 triangles, n-3 diagonals, and conserved area.
