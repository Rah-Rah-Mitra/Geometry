The Absolute Conic and Its Sign Test

Absolute

$$Q(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2-z^2=0$$

Interior — material

$Q<0$: genuine hyperbolic points inside the disk.

Boundary — ideal

$Q=0$: ideal points at infinity on the circle.

Exterior — bookkeeping

$Q>0$: projective lines still meet here, keeping intersections defined.

Printed pp. 483-504The Absolute Conic
Introduce the quadratic form x squared plus y squared minus z squared. Interior points give negative values, boundary points vanish, exterior points are positive. The interior is the material hyperbolic plane; the boundary is ideal; the exterior is projective bookkeeping that keeps intersections alive. This sign test underlies everything else.

The Beltrami-Klein Model

Points

The open unit disk; the circle is the absolute boundary.

Geodesics

Straight chords. Incidence is ordinary projective join and meet.

Why it helps

Lines are linear, so collinearity and intersection are easy.

Printed pp. 483-504Beltrami-Klein Model
Present the open unit disk with straight chords as geodesics. The big selling point is that incidence is linear, so joins and meets are ordinary projective operations. Note that two chords can fail to meet inside the disk while their projective lines still meet outside, which removes case distinctions.

Distance from the Boundary Cross-Ratio

Construction

For interior $P,Q$, extend their chord to meet the circle at ideal points $X,Y$.

Klein distance

$$d(P,Q)=\tfrac12\big|\log(P,Q;X,Y)\big|$$

Projective invariance

Cross-ratio is preserved by every absolute-fixing projectivity.

Ideal = infinite

As $Q\!\to\!X$ the cross-ratio diverges, so $d\!\to\!\infty$.

Printed pp. 483-504Distance by Cross-Ratio
Derive the Klein distance from the cross-ratio of P and Q with the two boundary points X and Y where their chord meets the circle. The one-half log absolute value form is forced by additivity and the requirement that ideal points sit infinitely far away. Emphasize projective invariance.

Many Parallels Through a Point

Failure of Euclid

Through an outside point $P$ there are infinitely many lines missing a given line $\ell$.

Limiting parallels

Two boundary cases share an ideal endpoint with $\ell$ on the circle.

Ultraparallels

Lines strictly between them are disjoint and admit a common perpendicular.

Printed pp. 483-504Many Parallels
Contrast with Euclid. Through an outside point P there is a whole fan of chords missing a given line, bounded by two limiting parallels that share an ideal endpoint with the line. Everything between is ultraparallel. This visibly breaks the parallel postulate while keeping the other axioms.

The Angle of Parallelism

Setup

Drop a perpendicular of length $a$ from $P$ to a line; measure the limiting-parallel angle $\Pi(a)$.

Lobachevsky

$$\tan\!\frac{\Pi(a)}{2}=e^{-a}$$

Limits

$a\!\to\!0$ gives $\Pi\!\to\!\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ (Euclidean); $a\!\to\!\infty$ gives $\Pi\!\to\!0$.

Printed pp. 483-504Angle of Parallelism
Define the angle of parallelism as the angle between the perpendicular dropped from a point and a limiting parallel. Lobachevsky's formula ties it to the distance from the point to the line. As distance grows the angle shrinks to zero, which is the quantitative signature of hyperbolic divergence.

The Poincare Disk Model

Conformal

Angles equal ordinary Euclidean angles; the model is angle-faithful.

Geodesics

Circular arcs meeting the boundary circle orthogonally.

Klein ↔ Poincare

$$w=\frac{2z}{1+|z|^2},\quad z=\frac{w}{1+\sqrt{1-|w|^2}}$$

Printed pp. 483-504Poincare Disk Model
Switch to the conformal disk. Geodesics are circular arcs meeting the boundary orthogonally, and angles are ordinary Euclidean angles. The radial map relates the Poincare coordinate z to the Klein coordinate w. Same ideal boundary, different drawing, chosen so angle measurement becomes painless.

The Upper Half-Plane Model

Domain

$\{z:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$; the real axis is the ideal boundary.

Geodesics

Vertical rays and semicircles centered on the real axis.

Disk to half-plane

$$z\mapsto i\,\frac{1+z}{1-z}$$

Isometries

Real Mobius maps $\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}$ with $ad-bc>0$.

Printed pp. 483-504Upper Half-Plane
Present the upper half-plane as a Mobius image of the disk. Geodesics are vertical lines and semicircles centered on the real axis, which is the ideal boundary. Mobius maps with real coefficients are the isometries. Useful because many computations linearize on the real boundary line.

Isometries Preserve the Absolute

Projective view

Isometries are exactly the projectivities fixing the absolute conic.

Complex view

Disk-preserving Mobius and anti-Mobius maps of $\mathbb{CP}^1$.

Disk automorphism

$$z\mapsto e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar a z},\quad |a|<1$$

Normal form

Choose $a$ to send any point to the center; the boundary stays fixed.

Printed pp. 483-504Isometries
State the unifying principle: hyperbolic isometries are exactly the projectivities preserving the absolute conic, equivalently the disk-preserving Mobius and anti-Mobius maps. The normal form sends a chosen point to the center with the boundary fixed, which is the standard trick for simplifying distance and angle calculations.

Triangles: Angle Sum and Defect

Angle sum

$$\alpha+\beta+\gamma<\pi$$

Defect = area

$$\text{Area}=\pi-(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)$$

Gauss-Bonnet

Curvature $-1$ makes area proportional to the angular defect.

Ideal triangle

All vertices on the boundary: angles $0$, maximal area $\pi$.

Printed pp. 483-504Triangles and Defect
State the angle-sum theorem: every hyperbolic triangle has angle sum strictly less than pi. The defect, pi minus the sum, is positive and, by Gauss-Bonnet on a curvature minus-one surface, equals the area. Larger triangles are thinner; an ideal triangle has all angles zero and area pi.

Hyperbolic Trigonometry

Law of cosines

$$\cosh c=\cosh a\,\cosh b-\sinh a\,\sinh b\,\cos\gamma$$

Law of sines

$$\frac{\sinh a}{\sin\alpha}=\frac{\sinh b}{\sin\beta}=\frac{\sinh c}{\sin\gamma}$$

AAA congruence

Angles fix a triangle up to isometry — there is an absolute length scale.

Printed pp. 483-504Hyperbolic Trigonometry
Replace circular functions with hyperbolic ones. Give the law of cosines with cosh of the sides and the law of sines using sinh. Note the AAA congruence consequence: angles determine a triangle up to isometry, because there is an absolute length scale set by the curvature.

Synthesis: One Conic, Many Models

Klein

Linear incidence; distance is the chord cross-ratio.

Poincare & half-plane

Conformal; angles are Euclidean; Mobius maps connect them.

Isometries

Absolute-preserving projectivities, equivalently disk Mobius maps.

Defect law

Triangle area equals $\pi$ minus the angle sum.

Printed pp. 483-504Synthesis
Recap the through-line. One real absolute conic generates the whole hyperbolic plane. The Klein model linearizes incidence, the Poincare and half-plane models linearize angles, and Mobius maps move between them. Distance and angle are cross-ratios, and isometries are the conic-preserving projectivities. Point to the notebook checks.
