The Determinant Vector $\Gamma(P)$

Bracket

$[\,i,j,k\,] = \det\big[\,p_i\;p_j\;p_k\,\big]$

Projective scaling

Under $P \mapsto H P$, every bracket scales by one common factor: $[\,i,j,k\,] \mapsto \det(H)\,[\,i,j,k\,]$.

Column scaling

Rescaling column $i$ by $\lambda$ multiplies only brackets containing the letter $i$.

Alternation

Odd permutations flip sign; a repeated letter gives $0$; a zero bracket detects collinearity.

Determinant vector

$\Gamma(P) = \big([\,i,j,k\,]\big)_{i

Why useful

Coordinates change wildly, but $\Gamma$ changes by a single predictable scale — ideal projective bookkeeping.

Define the bracket as a three-by-three determinant and assemble all of them into the determinant vector Gamma of P. Stress the two transformation laws: a projective map multiplies every bracket by one common determinant factor, while rescaling a column multiplies only brackets containing that letter. These laws drive everything later.

The Ring of Bracket Polynomials

Abstract brackets

Treat each $[\,i,j,k\,]$ as a formal variable, alternating in its three indices.

Expansion map

$\Phi:\; \mathbb{R}[\text{brackets}] \to \mathbb{R}[x_i,y_i,z_i]$

Not free

Random bracket numbers are usually inconsistent: no point set realizes them.

The bracket ring

The image of $\Phi$ is the bracket ring; equality there means equal coordinate polynomials.

Ideal of relations

$\ker\Phi = I_{\mathrm{GP}}$

Goal

Describe $\ker\Phi$ generators and a basis of the quotient — the next three slides.

Grassmann–Plücker Relations

Defining syzygy ($\mathbb{RP}^2$)

$\displaystyle\sum_{t} (-1)^{t}\,[\,a,b,c_t\,]\,[\,c_0,\dots,\widehat{c_t},\dots\,] = 0$

Worked instance

$[abc][def]-[abd][cef]$
$+\,[abe][cdf]-[abf][cde]=0$

Meaning

These quadratics are the consistency conditions that make bracket numbers behave like real determinants.

Generators

They generate $\ker\Phi$: every bracket relation is a consequence of Grassmann–Plücker.

Use

Each relation is a rewrite rule — the substitution that powers straightening.

The Standard Tableaux Basis

Monomial as tableau

A product of $k$ brackets is a $k\times 3$ array, one sorted bracket per row.

Standard

Rows sorted left-to-right, and columns weakly increase down the tableau.

Theorem

Standard tableaux are a vector-space basis of the bracket ring — a unique normal form.

Printed pp. 109-126Standard Tableaux Basis
Explain that bracket monomials map to Young tableaux, and that monomials whose rows are sorted and whose columns weakly increase are called standard. The standard tableaux form a vector-space basis for the bracket ring, giving every bracket expression a unique canonical normal form once we can reach it.

The Straightening Algorithm

Step 1

Find a column inversion in a non-standard monomial — a witness that it is not yet canonical.

Step 2

Apply the matching Grassmann–Plücker relation as a rewrite, producing a signed sum.

Step 3

Each new monomial is lexicographically smaller; iterate until all are standard.

Termination & decision

$M \xrightarrow{\;\text{GP}\;} \sum_j c_j\,S_j$

Reaches the standard basis in finitely many steps; two expressions are equal iff their difference straightens to $0$.

Consequence

A complete, terminating decision procedure for equality of bracket expressions.

Printed pp. 109-126The Straightening Algorithm
Walk through straightening: any non-standard monomial has a violating column, and a Grassmann-Plucker relation rewrites it as a signed sum of lexicographically smaller monomials. Iterating terminates in the standard basis. This is the engine that decides equality of bracket expressions by reducing their difference to zero.

Invariants as Balanced Bracket Ratios

Balance rule

A bracket-monomial ratio is invariant when numerator and denominator share total degree and per-point weight.

Cross-ratio (anchor $e$)

$(a,b;c,d) = \dfrac{[\,a,c,e\,]\,[\,b,d,e\,]}{[\,a,d,e\,]\,[\,b,c,e\,]}$

Why it cancels

Each point appears once above and once below, so all $\det(H)$ and column scalings vanish.

Final Polynomials & Recap

Non-realizability

Some combinatorial configurations admit no real point coordinates. How to prove it?

Final polynomial

$0 < \sum_k c_k\,[\cdots] \equiv 0 \pmod{I_{\mathrm{GP}}}$

The contradiction

Sign constraints force a sum positive, while Grassmann–Plücker force it to vanish — impossible.

A certificate

The final polynomial is a short, checkable proof of non-realizability — no exhaustive search needed.
