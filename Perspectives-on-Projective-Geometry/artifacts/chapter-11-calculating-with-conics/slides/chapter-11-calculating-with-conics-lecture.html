The Matrix Representation

Quadratic form

A conic is the zero set of a homogeneous quadratic in $x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)$.

Symmetric encoding

The six coefficients sit in a symmetric matrix $A=A^{\mathsf T}$.

Projective scaling

$A$ and $\lambda A$, $x$ and $\mu x$, describe the same conic and point.

Defining equation

$$x^{\mathsf T} A\,x = 0,\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix} a & b & d\\ b & c & e\\ d & e & f\end{pmatrix}$$

Why it computes

Incidence, tangency, and intersection all become matrix algebra on $A$.

Printed pp. 189-208The Matrix Representation
Anchor the algebra. A conic is the zero set of a quadratic form, encoded by a symmetric three-by-three matrix A. Points and lines are homogeneous vectors. Stress that scaling A or scaling a point leaves the equation invariant, so the conic is really a point in projective matrix space.

The Conic Through Five Points

Counting freedom

$A$ has six entries, one killed by scaling, leaving five degrees of freedom.

One equation each

Each $P_i$ on the conic gives a linear equation $P_i^{\mathsf T} A\,P_i = 0$.

General position

No three points collinear, so the five equations are independent.

Linear system

$$M\,\mathrm{vec}(A)=0,\quad M\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times 6}$$

The one-dimensional kernel is the conic, recovered up to scale.

Solve

A null-space computation returns $\mathrm{vec}(A)$; reshape into $A$.

Printed pp. 189-208The Conic Through Five Points
Five points in general position fix a conic uniquely. Each incidence is one linear equation in the six independent matrix entries, so five equations leave a one-dimensional kernel. Emphasize that the homogeneity removes the sixth degree of freedom, and that general position rules out three collinear points.

The Bracket Determinant Formula

No solve needed

The conic through five points has a closed determinant form in the running point $x$.

Coordinate-free

Written with brackets $[\,\cdot\,]$, it respects projective transformations.

Link

Equivalent to the cross-ratio characterization of a conic.

Bracket form

$$[xP_1P_2][xP_3P_4][P_1P_3P_5][P_2P_4P_5]$$ $$=[xP_1P_4][xP_2P_3][P_1P_2P_5][P_3P_4P_5]$$

Reading it

Setting the difference to zero is a degree-two condition on $x$: the conic itself.

Printed pp. 189-208The Bracket Determinant Formula
Show the closed form. Instead of solving the linear system, expand a six-by-six determinant whose vanishing forces the running point x onto the conic through the five data points. The bracket form is coordinate-free and connects directly to the cross-ratio characterization of conics from earlier chapters.

Poles, Polars, and Tangents

Polar line

The matrix sends a point $p$ to a line $\ell = A\,p$, its polar.

Tangent case

If $p$ lies on the conic, $A\,p$ is exactly the tangent at $p$.

Duality

Pole and polar are inverse maps through $A$ and $A^{-1}$.

Printed pp. 189-208Poles, Polars, and Tangents
The matrix does more than test membership. Multiplying a point by A produces its polar line; for a point on the conic that polar is the tangent. Pole and polar are dual through A inverse. Mention that polarity is the projective engine behind tangent constructions and conjugate points.

Pencils of Conics

Linear family

Given conics $C_1,C_2$, every $\lambda C_1+\mu C_2$ is again a conic.

Four base points

All members pass through the four common points of $C_1$ and $C_2$.

One parameter

The ratio $\lambda{:}\mu$ sweeps the entire one-parameter family.

Printed pp. 189-208Pencils of Conics
Introduce the pencil: every linear combination lambda C1 plus mu C2 is again a conic, and all of them pass through the four common base points of C1 and C2. Stress that one real parameter sweeps an entire family, and that the four base points are exactly the intersection of the two generators.

The Three Degenerate Members

Where rank drops

A member degenerates when $\det(\lambda C_1+\mu C_2)=0$.

Cubic in the ratio

This determinant is a homogeneous cubic in $\lambda{:}\mu$ with three roots.

Three line-pairs

Each root is a pair of lines joining the four base points in one pairing.

Degeneracy condition

$$\det(\lambda C_1+\mu C_2)=0$$

Printed pp. 189-208The Three Degenerate Members
A pencil generically contains three degenerate members, each a pair of lines joining the four base points in one of the three pairings. They are found where the determinant of lambda C1 plus mu C2 vanishes, a homogeneous cubic in lambda and mu. These line-pairs are the workhorses of intersection.

Intersecting Two Conics

Build the pencil

Form $\lambda C_1+\mu C_2$ from the two conics to intersect.

Find a line-pair

Solve the cubic $\det(\lambda C_1+\mu C_2)=0$ for one degenerate member.

Split & intersect

Factor it into two lines; intersect each with $C_1$.

Reduction pipeline

$$C_1\cap C_2\;\longrightarrow\;(\ell_1\cup\ell_2)\cap C_1$$

A conic-conic problem becomes two conic-line solves.

Why it works

The degenerate member shares all four intersection points with both conics.

Printed pp. 189-208Intersecting Two Conics
Here is the central algorithm. To intersect two conics, build their pencil, solve the determinant cubic for a degenerate member, split that member into two lines, and intersect each line with one original conic. The hard conic-conic problem collapses into easy conic-line solves. Walk the pipeline slowly.

The Primitive: Conic Meets Line

Parametrize the line

Write points on $\ell$ as $x(t)=p+t\,q$ for two points $p,q$ on $\ell$.

Quadratic in $t$

$x(t)^{\mathsf T}A\,x(t)=0$ is a scalar quadratic; its roots are the hits.

Discriminant decides

Two real points, one tangent double point, or a conjugate complex pair.

Printed pp. 189-208The Primitive: Conic Meets Line
Explain the primitive that the pencil method reduces to. Restricting the quadratic form to a parametrized line gives a quadratic in one parameter; its discriminant decides two real points, one tangent double point, or a conjugate complex pair. The dual splitting via the skew matrix gives the same two points.

Points with Prescribed Tangents

Tangency = merge

A line touches a conic when its two intersections coincide.

Vanishing discriminant

Force the line-restricted quadratic to have a double root.

Four points, one tangent

Use the pencil through four points; the tangent condition selects members.

Tangency condition

$$\operatorname{disc}_t\big(x(t)^{\mathsf T}(\lambda C_1+\mu C_2)\,x(t)\big)=0$$

Result

Two fixed points on the line yield the two conics through the four points tangent to it.

Printed pp. 189-208Points with Prescribed Tangents
Tangency is a limiting incidence: two intersection points merging. Using a pencil through given points, we can demand the conic touch a chosen line by forcing the line-restricted discriminant to vanish. This builds the conic through points with prescribed tangents, the four-points-and-a-tangent construction.

Complex Roots, Real Geometry

Misses are conjugate

A real line missing a real conic meets it in two complex conjugate points.

Real join

The line through a conjugate pair is real after removing one complex scalar.

Carry it honestly

Complex intermediates are legitimate steps, not numerical noise.

Conjugate intersection

$$x_\pm = p + t_\pm q,\qquad t_\pm=\alpha\pm i\beta$$

$x_+ , x_-$ are conjugate; their join $x_+\!\times x_-$ is real.

Lesson

Real projective computation lives over $\mathbb{C}$ in the middle and returns to $\mathbb{R}$ at the end.

Printed pp. 189-208Complex Roots, Real Geometry
Reassure students that complex roots are not failures. When a real line misses a real conic the two intersections are conjugate complex points, yet their join is a real line once the shared complex scalar is removed. Real projective computation must carry complex intermediate values honestly.
