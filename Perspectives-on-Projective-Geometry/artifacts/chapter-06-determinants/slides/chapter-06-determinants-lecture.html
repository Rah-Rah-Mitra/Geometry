Three Properties Pin It Down

Multilinear

Linear in each column separately, holding the others fixed.

Alternating

Swapping two columns flips the sign; a repeated column gives zero.

Normalized

The determinant of the identity is one.

Unique characterization

$\det(\dots,\,\alpha u+\beta v,\,\dots)=\alpha\det(\dots,u,\dots)+\beta\det(\dots,v,\dots)$

Sign & degeneracy

$\det(\dots,u,\dots,u,\dots)=0,\qquad \det(I)=1$

Why it matters

Every later identity is forced by these axioms — not by coordinate grinding.

Printed pp. 93-108Three Defining Properties
Stress that multilinearity, the alternating property, and normalization pin the determinant down uniquely. Every later identity follows from these three axioms alone, so when a sign flips or a term cancels, point back to which axiom is doing the work rather than to a coordinate computation.

Oriented Area and Volume

Geometric meaning

The determinant of column vectors is the signed area (2D) or volume (3D) of the spanned parallelepiped.

Sign is orientation

Positive for counter-clockwise, negative for clockwise; zero when the vectors collapse.

Plane

$\text{area}(a,b,c)=\tfrac12\,[a,b,c]$

Printed pp. 93-108Oriented Area and Volume
Connect the alternating axiom to geometry: the determinant measures signed area in the plane and signed volume in space. The sign is orientation, and a zero value is degeneracy. Have students predict the sign before computing so the geometry leads the algebra.

The Bracket [a,b,c]

Definition

For homogeneous points stacked as columns, the bracket is their $3\times3$ determinant.

Bracket

$[a,b,c]=\det\!\begin{pmatrix}a_1&b_1&c_1\\a_2&b_2&c_2\\a_3&b_3&c_3\end{pmatrix}$

Affine reading

Normalize $w=1$: the bracket is twice the signed triangle area.

Printed pp. 93-108The Bracket of Three Points
Introduce bracket notation as the determinant of three homogeneous column vectors. Emphasize it is twice the signed triangle area in the affine chart with last coordinate one. This single scalar simultaneously records area magnitude, orientation, and the degeneracy we care about most.

Collinearity = Vanishing Bracket

Incidence test

$[a,b,x]=0 \iff x \text{ lies on the join } a\vee b$

Signed-distance proxy

As $x$ crosses the line, the bracket passes through zero and flips sign.

No coordinates needed

The bracket, not its expansion, is the conceptual object.

Dual Statement: Concurrence

Points ↔ lines

In the projective plane the roles are interchangeable, so each bracket theorem dualizes verbatim.

Concurrence

$[\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3]=0 \iff \ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3 \text{ meet in one point}$

Free theorems

Every incidence result arrives with a dual partner at no extra cost.

Printed pp. 93-108Duality: Concurrent Lines

Cross Product & Cramer, Re-derived

Join and meet

$a\vee b = a\times b,\qquad \ell_1\wedge\ell_2 = \ell_1\times\ell_2$

Bracket as triple product

$[a,b,c]=\langle a\times b,\;c\rangle$

Cramer's rule

Each coordinate of a solution is a ratio of brackets.

Solve $Mx=v$

$x_i=\dfrac{[\dots v \dots]}{[\,m_1,m_2,m_3\,]}$

Printed pp. 93-108Cross Product and Cramer's Rule
Show that join and meet are cross products and that the bracket is the triple product dotting one point against the cross product of the other two. Cramer's rule then reads each coordinate as a ratio of brackets, so solving linear systems becomes pure bracket bookkeeping.

Plucker's $\mu$: A Construction Rule

Idea

Combine two equations so their shared zeros survive, then force the result through a new point $q$.

Constructed line

$\langle \ell_2,q\rangle\,\ell_1 - \langle \ell_1,q\rangle\,\ell_2$

Guarantee

Passes through $\ell_1\wedge\ell_2$ and through $q$: two residuals vanish.

Printed pp. 93-108Plucker's Mu Construction
Explain Plucker's mu as a recipe: combine two equations so their shared zeros survive while a new point is forced. Evaluating each line at the target supplies the coefficients. This turns the abstract pencil of lines into a concrete construction students can compute by hand.

Brackets as Invariant Building Blocks

Transformation law

$[Ta,Tb,Tc]=\det(T)\,[a,b,c]$

Rescaling

$[\lambda_a a,\lambda_b b,\lambda_c c]=\lambda_a\lambda_b\lambda_c\,[a,b,c]$

Multihomogeneity

Build each term so every summand inherits the same nonzero factor — the zero set is invariant.

Printed pp. 93-108Brackets Build Invariants
Make the invariance argument concrete: a projective map multiplies every three-bracket by the determinant of the transformation, and rescaling a point multiplies its brackets by that scale. A multihomogeneous polynomial is engineered so every term inherits the identical nonzero factor, leaving its zero set fixed.

Grassmann-Plucker Relations

Universal syzygies

Quadratic identities among brackets that vanish for every configuration.

Three-bracket relation

$[abc][ade]-[abd][ace]+[abe][acd]=0$

Two-bracket relation

$[ab][cd]-[ac][bd]+[ad][bc]=0$

Not special cases

These are algebraic identities in the entries, true regardless of where the points sit.

Why we care

They explain why different-looking bracket formulas encode the same incidence theorem.

Six vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$

$[abc][def]-[abd][cef]+[abe][cdf]-[abf][cde]=0$

Printed pp. 93-108Grassmann-Plucker Relations
Present the Grassmann-Plucker relations as universal syzygies among brackets that vanish for every configuration. They are algebraic identities, not special cases, and they explain why different looking bracket formulas can certify the same incidence theorem behind the scenes.

A Syzygy Certifies an Incidence

Area cancellation

The four-term relation reads as four oriented triangle areas summing to zero identically.

Identity at work

$[cdb][efa]-[cda][efb]=0$

From syzygy to theorem

Rearranged, the relation is exactly the condition that three constructed joins concur.
