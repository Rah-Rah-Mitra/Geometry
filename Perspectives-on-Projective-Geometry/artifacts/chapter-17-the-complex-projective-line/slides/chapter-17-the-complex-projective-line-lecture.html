Finite and infinity charts

$z=\dfrac{z_0}{z_1},\qquad w=\dfrac{z_1}{z_0}=\dfrac1z$

Finite point

The class of $(z,1)$; the chart $z=z_0/z_1$ covers all of $\mathbb{C}$.

Point at infinity

The class $(1,0)$ sits at $w=0$ in the reciprocal chart.

The Riemann Sphere via Stereographic Projection

Tangent sphere

Sphere centered above the origin, tangent to the plane; project from the north pole $N$.

Projection

$z\mapsto\dfrac{(4x,\,4y,\,2|z|^2)}{|z|^2+4}$

Infinity is the pole

Large $|z|$ crowds toward $N$, so $\infty$ completes the surface: $\mathbb{CP}^1\cong\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$.

Printed pp. 311-328The Riemann Sphere
Walk through stereographic projection slowly. The sphere sits tangent to the plane, projection runs from the north pole, and every finite complex number lands on the sphere. Large modulus points crowd toward the pole, so infinity becomes the north pole and completes the surface. This is the global picture of CP1.

Mobius Transformations

Matrix action, then dehomogenize

$\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\!\!:\;z\mapsto\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}$

Nondegenerate

Require $ad-bc\neq 0$; otherwise the map collapses $\mathbb{CP}^1$ to a point.

Generalized circles

Finite circles or lines through $\infty$ map to generalized circles.

Printed pp. 311-328Mobius Transformations
Present the Mobius map as a matrix acting on homogeneous coordinates, then dehomogenized to the familiar fraction. Insist on the nondegeneracy condition a d minus b c nonzero. Grid lines bend into arcs, but the projective content is invariant: generalized circles map to generalized circles under every such map.

The Projectivities Form $\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})$

Scale invariance

The matrix is defined only up to nonzero scalar $\lambda$: $(a{:}b{:}c{:}d)\sim(\lambda a{:}\dots)$.

Three complex DOF

Four entries minus one scale gives exactly three complex degrees of freedom.

A group

Composition is matrix multiplication; symmetry reduces to linear algebra.

Group structure

$\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{C}^\times$

Explain why the matrix is defined only up to nonzero scale, which collapses four complex entries into three complex degrees of freedom. The projectivities of CP1 form the group PGL of two over C. Composition is matrix multiplication, so the whole symmetry group reduces to clean linear algebra.

The Complex Cross-Ratio

Definition

$(A,B;C,D)=\dfrac{(A-C)(B-D)}{(A-D)(B-C)}$

Mobius invariant

Every Mobius map preserves the cross-ratio of four points.

Normal form

Send $A,B,C\to 0,1,\infty$; then the cross-ratio is just the image of $D$.

Printed pp. 311-328The Complex Cross-Ratio
Define the cross-ratio of four points as a ratio of differences and state its central property: it is invariant under every Mobius transformation. Connect this to slide five, since three degrees of freedom let us send any three points to zero, one, and infinity, after which the cross-ratio is the image of the fourth.

Real Cross-Ratio $\Leftrightarrow$ Concyclic or Collinear

Theorem

$(A,B;C,D)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\iff A,B,C,D\text{ concyclic/collinear}$

Side predicate

$\operatorname{Im}(A,B;C,p)$ is zero on the circle, with opposite signs inside and outside.

Collinearity

A real quotient $(B-A)/(C-A)$ is the degenerate, line-through-$\infty$ case.

Printed pp. 311-328Real Cross-Ratio Detects Circles
State the key theorem crisply: four points are concyclic or collinear exactly when their cross-ratio is real or infinite. Demonstrate with the imaginary part as a side predicate, which is zero on the boundary circle and opposite signs inside and outside. Cocircularity becomes a projective fact on CP1.

Lines and Circles Are One Family

Generalized circles

Finite circles and straight lines are a single family of “circles on the sphere”.

Line $=$ circle through $\infty$

On the sphere, a line lifts to a circle that passes through the north pole.

Mobius-stable

Mobius maps permute this family, so line-versus-circle is not fundamental.

Printed pp. 311-328Circles and Lines Unified
Bring lines and circles together as one family of generalized circles. On the sphere a line is simply a circle that passes through the north pole, while a finite circle avoids it. Mobius maps permute this single family, which is why distinguishing line from circle is no longer fundamental.

Inversions and Anti-Mobius Reflections

Unit-circle inversion

$z\mapsto\dfrac{1}{\overline{z}}$

Anti-Mobius

Conjugation is a field automorphism with no projective matrix; it reverses oriented sides.

Inversion facts

Fixes the unit circle pointwise, swaps $0\leftrightarrow\infty$, exchanges inside and outside.

Printed pp. 311-328Inversions and Reflections
Contrast Mobius and anti-Mobius maps. Conjugation is a field automorphism with no projective matrix, so it preserves the circle family but reverses oriented sidedness. Unit-circle inversion fixes the unit circle pointwise, swaps origin with infinity, and exchanges inside with outside. These are the orientation-reversing symmetries.

Grassmann-Plucker and Ptolemy's Shadow

Rank-2 relation

$[AB][CD]-[AC][BD]+[AD][BC]=0$

Brackets

For finite $(z,1)$ the bracket $[AB]$ is the complex difference $A-B$.

Take magnitudes

The triangle inequality on three vectors yields Ptolemy's inequality.

Equality

Concyclic in cyclic order makes the summands collinear, so equality holds.

Printed pp. 311-328Grassmann-Plucker and Ptolemy
Show the two-coordinate bracket as a complex determinant and write the rank-two Grassmann-Plucker relation. For finite points the bracket is just a difference, so the three summands are complex vectors. Taking magnitudes and applying the triangle inequality casts a Euclidean shadow that is exactly Ptolemy's inequality.

Intersection Angles from Cross-Ratios

Signed angle

$\angle(C_1,C_2)=\arg\,(Q_1,Q_2;P_1,P_2)$

Encoding

Circles $C_1=(P_1,Q_1,P_2)$ and $C_2=(P_1,Q_2,P_2)$ share the points $P_1,P_2$.

Conformal

Mobius maps preserve cross-ratios, hence the signed tangent angle.

Orientation

Reversing either circle flips the sign of the angle.

Printed pp. 311-328Intersection Angles
Encode two oriented circles through their shared points using auxiliary points, then read the argument of a cross-ratio as the signed angle between their oriented tangents. Reversing either orientation flips the sign. Because Mobius maps preserve cross-ratios, they preserve this signed angle, so the geometry is conformal.

Recap: A Chain of Translations

Coordinates

Homogeneous pairs over $\mathbb{C}$ define $\infty$; two charts cover $\mathbb{CP}^1$.

Cross-ratio

Real $\iff$ concyclic/collinear; its imaginary part is a side test, its argument an angle.

Symmetry

$\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ preserves all of it; anti-Mobius maps reverse orientation.

Ptolemy

Grassmann-Plucker brackets, read as distances, yield Ptolemy's theorem.

Printed pp. 311-328Recap
Recap the chain of translations. Homogeneous coordinates define infinity, cross-ratios make the circle test projective and record sidedness and angle, Mobius maps preserve all of it while anti-Mobius maps flip orientation, Grassmann-Plucker yields Ptolemy, and stereographic projection reveals CP1 as a sphere for the audience.
