The Complete Quadrilateral

Definition

Four lines in general position plus their six pairwise intersection points.

Dual companion

The complete quadrangle: four points and their six joining lines.

Diagonals

Three diagonals join the three pairs of opposite vertices.

Printed pp. 129-144The Complete Quadrilateral
A complete quadrilateral is four lines in general position together with their six pairwise intersection points. Stress the count: four lines, six vertices, and three diagonals joining opposite vertices. This is the dual companion of the complete quadrangle, four points and their six joining lines, and both will feed the same six point invariant.

Six Points on a Transversal

The cut

A transversal missing the vertices meets each side once: six marked points $a,b,c,d,e,f$.

Three opposite pairs

Group as $(a,d)$, $(b,e)$, $(c,f)$ from opposite sides.

Claim

These six points are never arbitrary: one relation always binds them.

Printed pp. 129-144Six Points on a Transversal
Cut the figure with any line that misses the vertices. Each of the four lines meets it once and each of the two diagonals meets it once, giving six marked points. Grouped into three opposite pairs these six points are the quadrilateral set. The key claim is that they are not arbitrary; one algebraic relation always binds them.

The Bracket Condition

Brackets

$[u\,v]$ is the $2\times2$ determinant of two homogeneous line coordinates.

Quadrilateral set

Opposite pairs $(a,d),(b,e),(c,f)$ obey the balance below.

Projective

Invariant under every projective map, so independent of the transversal.

Defining relation

$$[a\,e]\,[b\,f]\,[c\,d] \;=\; [a\,f]\,[b\,d]\,[c\,e]$$

Reading

$\mathrm{qs}(a,b,c;d,e,f)$ — six points, one identity.

Printed pp. 129-144The Bracket Condition
Here is the defining equation. Using two by two brackets of homogeneous line coordinates, opposite pairs a-d, b-e, c-f satisfy the product of three brackets on the left equal to the product on the right. Point out that this is a projective statement, invariant under any projective map of the line, so it survives every change of transversal.

Affine Slope Form

Pick a chart

In an affine coordinate the bracket $[u\,v]$ becomes the difference $u-v$.

Computable

The notebook builds six points from slopes of lines through four finite points.

Guaranteed

Such slopes automatically satisfy the relation; we perturb it in the lab.

Affine condition

$$(a-e)(b-f)(c-d) = (a-f)(b-d)(c-e)$$

Printed pp. 129-144Affine Slope Form
Choosing an affine chart turns brackets into coordinate differences, so the condition reads as a product of three factors equal to another. The notebook builds the six points from slopes of the lines through four finite points, which guarantees the relation. This is the form we actually compute with and perturb in the lab.

Generalizing the Harmonic Relation

Degenerate case

Let two of the three pairs coincide: the six-point relation collapses.

Harmonic four

What remains is the classical harmonic relation, cross ratio $-1$.

von Staudt

The same complete quadrangle drives addition and multiplication on a line.

Harmonic limit

$$(A,B;C,D) = -1$$

Printed pp. 129-144Generalizing the Harmonic Relation
When two of the three pairs coincide the quadrilateral set degenerates to the classical harmonic relation with cross ratio minus one. So a quadrilateral set is the natural six point cousin of a four point harmonic quadruple. Emphasize that the same incidence machinery, the complete quadrangle, produces both, which is why von Staudt arithmetic reappears here.

Five Points Force the Sixth

One equation

The condition is a single relation, so five coordinates determine the last.

Recognition test

Six points come from a planar quadrilateral iff the bracket balance holds.

Degeneracies

Failures are exactly a vanishing denominator or colliding points.

Solve for $f$

$$f = \frac{a(b-d)(c-e) - \dots}{(b-d)(c-e)-(b-f_0)\dots}$$

Design lab

Move $e$, recompute the forced $f$, and watch the lifting rank respond.

Printed pp. 129-144Five Points Force the Sixth
Because the condition is a single equation, fixing five of the six coordinates determines the last one whenever the denominator is nonzero. This is the recognition test: given six points on a line, they come from a planar quadrilateral exactly when the bracket balance holds. Degeneracies are precisely vanishing denominators or collisions, nothing mysterious.

Liftings: Shadows from Higher Space

Idea

Realize a flat incidence figure as the projection — the shadow — of points in higher space.

Heights

Raise line points to heights $(x,h)$ so the requested triples become truly collinear.

Existence

A nontrivial lift exists exactly when a rank condition — the bracket balance — holds.

Printed pp. 129-144Liftings: Shadows from Higher Space
A lifting realizes a flat incidence figure as the projection, the shadow, of points and lines living in higher dimensional space. Collinear points on the line are raised to heights so that the requested triples become genuinely collinear up in the plane or space. The lifting exists exactly when a rank condition holds, the same bracket balance in disguise.

The Lifting Matrix

One equation per triple

Each required collinear triple is a linear equation on the unknown heights.

Stack

Assemble the equations into a lifting matrix $M$ acting on the height vector.

Signature

Rank $3$ with a genuine, non-affine null vector means a real lift exists.

Rank / nullspace test

$$M\,\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{0},\quad \operatorname{rank}M=3,\ \mathbf{h}\notin\{\text{const},\text{affine}\}$$

Printed pp. 129-144The Lifting Matrix
Encode each required collinear triple as a linear equation on the unknown heights. Stack them into a matrix. A nontrivial, non-affine lift exists when this matrix drops rank, leaving a null vector that is not simply a constant or linear height. Rank three with a genuine null direction is the computational signature the notebook checks.

Desargues in Three Dimensions

Two triangles, one center

Triangles $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ are perspective from a point $O$.

Lift to space

Place them in two distinct planes; $O$ is the apex of the perspective.

The axis is real

The two planes meet in a line; corresponding sides meet on it.

Printed pp. 129-144Desargues in Three Dimensions
Two triangles perspective from a point are the shadow of two triangles lying in two distinct planes of space. Those planes meet in a line, and the three pairs of corresponding sides meet on that line of intersection. Projecting down, that line becomes the Desargues axis. In space the theorem is just two planes sharing a line.

Why the 3D View Makes It Obvious

Common planes

Each pair of corresponding sides lies in one plane through the center $O$.

Must meet

Two coplanar lines intersect; that point lies in both triangle planes.

Hence collinear

All three meets lie on the planes' shared line — the axis.

Spatial Desargues

$$\pi_1 \cap \pi_2 = \ell \;\Rightarrow\; \text{axis is a true line}$$

Project down

Collapsing the extra dimension sends a genuine spatial incidence to the planar theorem — no casework.

Printed pp. 129-144Why the 3D View Makes It Obvious
The space picture removes all casework. Corresponding sides lie in a common plane through the center, so each pair must meet, and every such meeting lies in both triangle planes, hence on their shared line. Collapsing the third dimension projects a true spatial incidence to a planar theorem. Liftings turn hard plane proofs into easy space observations.

Synthesis and Takeaways

One invariant

The bracket balance underlies the quadrilateral set and its many readings.

Special cases

Harmonic quadruples, von Staudt arithmetic, and involution pairs all fall out.

Liftings

A rank test recognizes which line data lifts to a planar incidence figure.

Shadows explain theorems

Habit to keep

Ask: what higher figure casts this plane configuration as its shadow?

Printed pp. 129-144Synthesis and Takeaways
Recap the through line: one bracket balance underlies the quadrilateral set, the harmonic special case, von Staudt arithmetic, the five forces six reconstruction, and the rank test for liftings.
