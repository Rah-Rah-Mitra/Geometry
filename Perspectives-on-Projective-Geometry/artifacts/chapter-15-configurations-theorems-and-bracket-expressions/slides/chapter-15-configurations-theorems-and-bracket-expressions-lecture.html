Configurations as Incidence Patterns

Combinatorial first

A configuration is a set of points and lines with a prescribed incidence relation, before coordinates exist.

Two questions

Which abstract patterns are realizable? Which incidences are then forced?

Why it matters

Theorems are exactly the forced incidences of a partial configuration.

Printed pp. 269-294Configurations as Incidence Patterns
A configuration is pure combinatorics first: a set of points and lines with a fixed incidence count, before any coordinates are chosen. Stripping away the picture lets us ask which abstract patterns can be realized by actual points and lines, and which incidence relations any realization is forced to obey.

Balanced $n_k$ Configurations

Definition

$n$ points, $n$ lines; each point on $k$ lines, each line on $k$ points.

Double counting

Flags counted two ways: $n\cdot k = n\cdot k$, forcing the same $n$ on both sides.

The stars

Pappos is $9_3$; Desargues is $10_3$.

Printed pp. 269-294n_k Configurations
The notation n underscore k records a balanced configuration: n points and n lines, each point on k lines, each line through k points. Double counting forces the same n on both sides. Pappos is nine three and Desargues is ten three; these small symmetric patterns are the workhorses of the whole chapter.

The Desargues $10_3$ Configuration

Perspective from a point

Triangle $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ have join lines $AA',BB',CC'$ all through centre $P$.

Perspective from a line

Corresponding sides meet in three points on one axis.

Census

$10$ points, $10$ lines, three at each → $10_3$.

Printed pp. 269-294The Desargues 10_3 Configuration
Desargues two triangles perspective from a centre have their corresponding sides meeting on a common axis. Counting the centre, six vertices, and three axis points gives ten points; the perspective rays, the six sides, and the axis give ten lines, three through each. The figure is the canonical ten three configuration.

Brackets: Incidence as a Determinant

The bracket

$$[A\,B\,C]=\det\begin{pmatrix}a_1&b_1&c_1\\ a_2&b_2&c_2\\ a_3&b_3&c_3\end{pmatrix}$$

Collinearity

$A,B,C$ collinear $\iff [A\,B\,C]=0$.

Sign = orientation

Nonzero brackets record the orientation needed for ratios.

Printed pp. 269-294Brackets and Determinants
A bracket is the determinant of three homogeneous point columns. It vanishes exactly when the three points are collinear, so every incidence in a configuration becomes a single algebraic equation. Signs of brackets record orientation, which we will need when ratios and cross-ratios enter the bookkeeping later.

The Grassmann-Plucker Relations

Not independent

Brackets of a common point set satisfy quadratic syzygies for free.

Three-term relation

$$[ZAB][ZQR]-[ZAQ][ZBR]+[ZAR][ZBQ]=0$$

The engine

Trade one bracket product for another to manufacture the conclusion factor.

Printed pp. 269-294Grassmann-Plucker Relations
Brackets are not independent: the Grassmann-Plucker relations are quadratic identities that hold among them for any point set. The three term relation shown here is the engine of every proof. It lets us trade a product of hypothesis brackets for another product, manufacturing the conclusion factor we want.

A Theorem as a Bracket Identity

Hypotheses

$[H_1]=0,\ [H_2]=0,\ \dots,\ [H_m]=0$

Conclusion

$[X\,Y\,Z]=0$

The claim

The theorem holds projectively $\iff$ the hypothesis brackets force the target bracket to vanish.

Printed pp. 269-294A Theorem as a Bracket Identity
Translating a theorem means writing the hypotheses as vanishing brackets and the conclusion as one target bracket. The theorem is true projectively exactly when that target bracket is forced to vanish whenever the hypothesis brackets do. The whole statement now lives inside the ring of bracket polynomials.

Final Polynomials: The Certificate

Cancellation ledger

Multiply hypothesis rows; interior brackets cancel; the survivor is the conclusion.

Binomial proof row

$\underbrace{[\cdots][\cdots]}_{\text{left}}=\underbrace{[\cdots][\cdots]}_{\text{right}}$

Biquadratic final polynomial

An explicit sum of G-P relations reducing to $[X\,Y\,Z]$ — a checkable certificate.

Printed pp. 269-294Final Polynomials and Certificates
A final polynomial, or biquadratic final polynomial, is an explicit combination of Grassmann-Plucker relations that reduces to the conclusion bracket. It is a cancellation ledger: multiply the hypothesis rows, cancel interior brackets, and the survivor is the conclusion equals zero. That ledger is a machine-checkable proof certificate.

Cross-Ratio Chains and Cycles

Cross-ratio

$$(A,B;C,D)=\frac{[ACX]\,[BDX]}{[ADX]\,[BCX]}$$

Perspectivity = Mobius map

On a line each perspectivity is a projective map; the cross-ratio is its invariant passenger.

Closing a cycle

A cycle returning three of four marked points must return the fourth.

Printed pp. 269-294Cross-Ratio Chains
On a line a perspectivity is a Mobius map, so the cross-ratio rides through unchanged. Chaining perspectivities composes these maps; a closed cycle that returns three of four marked points must return the fourth. This one dimensional proof machine is the cross-ratio counterpart of the bracket cancellation argument.

Ceva, Menelaus, and Gluing

Same product, two signs

$$\frac{\overline{AX}}{\overline{XB}}\cdot\frac{\overline{BY}}{\overline{YC}}\cdot\frac{\overline{CZ}}{\overline{ZA}}=\pm 1$$

Bracket form

Each directed ratio is a quotient of brackets — the product is projective.

Gluing

Shared edges cancel: interior factors vanish, leaving a boundary condition.

Printed pp. 269-294Ceva, Menelaus, and Gluing
Ceva and Menelaus are the same product of directed ratios, each ratio rewritten as a quotient of brackets, with value plus one for concurrence and minus one for a transversal. Gluing such faces along shared edges cancels interior factors, exactly the bracket cancellation seen at the level of whole configurations.

Realizability over the Reals

Not all are drawable

Some abstract configurations admit no realization by real points and lines.

Non-realizability certificate

A final polynomial deriving a contradiction from the incidence brackets proves it cannot be drawn.

Decidable

The bracket machinery turns realizability into an algebraic decision; it also powers automatic theorem proving.

Printed pp. 269-294Realizability over the Reals
Not every abstract configuration can be drawn. A final polynomial that derives a contradiction from the incidence brackets is a non-realizability certificate over the reals. Conversely many small symmetric configurations like Pappos and Desargues are realizable, and the bracket machinery is what decides the question algorithmically.
