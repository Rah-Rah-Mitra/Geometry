Five Points Determine a Conic

Six coefficients, one scale

A conic $x^\top M x = 0$ has six entries but only five degrees of freedom.

Two routes, one curve

The linear nullspace of the design matrix and the bracket pencil agree up to scale.

Why four leave a family

Perspectivity versus Projectivity

Perspectivity

A single projection from a center; corresponding meets all land on one fixed axis line.

Projectivity

A composition of perspectivities; the axis need not be preserved.

The conic case

The curve appears precisely when the map between pencils is not a perspectivity.

Printed pp. 167-188Perspectivity versus Projectivity
Draw the key distinction sharply. A perspectivity between two pencils fixes the cross axis and forces all meets onto one line. A projectivity is a composition of perspectivities and need not fix that axis. The whole chapter hinges on the non perspective case, where the meets escape a line and curve.

Steiner's Generation of a Conic

Steiner's theorem

$$\ell \mapsto \pi(\ell): \quad \{\ell \cap \pi(\ell)\}\ \text{is a conic through } A,B$$

Two pencils, one projectivity

Pair each line through $A$ with a line through $B$ by a projectivity $\pi$.

Degenerate guard

If $\pi$ is a perspectivity, the locus collapses to a line.

Printed pp. 167-188Steiner's Generation of a Conic
State Steiner's theorem as the heart of the chapter: pair the lines of a pencil at A with the lines of a pencil at B by a projectivity, and the intersection points trace a conic through A and B. Walk the diagram, matching colored rays and showing the locus. Note a perspectivity would collapse this to a straight line.

The Generation Sweep

Shared parameter

A single $t$ on the circle induces the projective pairing of the two bundles.

Per-frame certificate

Each meet $m(t)$ lies on both lines and has conic residual $\approx 0$.

Locus check

$$m(t) = \big(A\vee \ell_p(t)\big)\wedge\big(B\vee \ell_q(t)\big),\quad m(t)^\top M\, m(t)\approx 0$$

Degenerate contrast

Under a perspectivity, $m(t)$ would slide along one line instead of circling the conic.

Printed pp. 167-188The Generation Sweep

A shared parameter on the circle induces the projective pairing between the two bundles, so each frame produces one meet with a near zero conic residual. Tell students the residual check is what certifies the locus is genuinely the conic and not an artifact.

Cross-Ratio From a Conic Point

Conic cross-ratio

$$(1,2;3,4)_p = \frac{[p,1,3]\,[p,2,4]}{[p,1,4]\,[p,2,3]}$$

Four secants

From a viewpoint $p$ on the conic, draw secants to four fixed conic points.

Bracket = determinant

Each $[p,i,j]$ is a $3\times3$ homogeneous determinant.

Printed pp. 167-188Cross-Ratio From a Conic Point
Introduce the cross ratio of four conic points measured by the four secants drawn from a fifth point on the curve. Write the bracket ratio explicitly. The theorem to feature: this value is independent of the vantage point, so the conic carries a well defined projective coordinate just like a line does.

Why the Value Is Invariant

Move the viewpoint

Slide $p$ to $p'$ along the conic; the four secant directions all change projectively.

Ratio unchanged

$(1,2;3,4)_p = (1,2;3,4)_{p'}$ wherever the brackets are defined.

Consequence

The conic carries an intrinsic $\mathbb{RP}^1$ coordinate.

Printed pp. 167-188Why the Value Is Invariant
Explain the invariance as the engine of the chapter. Because the four secant angles change projectively as the viewpoint slides, their cross ratio stays fixed, and this is exactly the statement that the conic is a model of the real projective line. Mention the notebook's flat invariant tracker as numerical evidence.

Conics as Projective Lines

Veronese parameterization

$$[u:v]\ \longmapsto\ [\,u^2 : uv : v^2\,]$$

Lift a Mobius map

A $2\times2$ matrix $B$ on $[u:v]$ lifts to a $3\times3$ map on the parabola.

Group statement

Conic-preserving maps act on the curve as $\mathrm{PGL}(2)$.

Form preserved up to scale

$$x_0 x_2 - x_1^2 \ \longmapsto\ (\det B)^2\,(x_0 x_2 - x_1^2)$$

Why it matters

The symmetries are not arbitrary; on the conic they are exactly line projectivities, so cross-ratio is preserved.

Printed pp. 167-188Conics as Projective Lines
Make the identification precise: parameterize the parabola by the Veronese map sending a parameter pair to its squares. A Mobius matrix on the parameter lifts to a three by three matrix preserving the conic form up to det squared. So conic preserving maps act like PGL of two on the curve. This is the group statement.

Hesse's Transfer Principle

Line becomes a pair

Record a line by its two intersections with a fixed conic.

Parabola device

$$y=x^2:\quad \text{chord}(a,b)\ \text{meets the axis at } y=-ab$$

Concurrence as a relation

Equal axis intercepts $\Leftrightarrow$ equal products $\Leftrightarrow$ a quadrilateral-set equation.

Printed pp. 167-188Hesse's Transfer Principle
Present Hesse transfer as the bridge to incidence theorems. A line is recorded by its two meets with the conic, turning a line into a point pair. On the parabola the chord through parameters a and b crosses the axis at minus their product, so equal products encode concurrence as a quadrilateral set relation.

Pascal's Mystic Hexagon

Pascal's theorem

$$\{14\cap 25,\ 25\cap 36,\ 36\cap 14\}\ \text{are collinear}$$

Inscribed hexagon

Take any six points $1\ldots6$ on a conic and join them as a hexagon.

The Pascal line

The three opposite-side meets line up; different orderings give different Pascal lines.

Printed pp. 167-188Pascal's Mystic Hexagon
State Pascal: inscribe any hexagon in a conic, intersect the three pairs of opposite sides, and the three meets are collinear on the Pascal line. Trace the hexagon in the figure, color the three opposite side pairs, and mark the line. Stress this holds for any hexagon ordering, giving many Pascal lines.

Brianchon and Duality

Brianchon's theorem (dual)

$$\text{diagonals } \overline{14},\ \overline{25},\ \overline{36}\ \text{are concurrent}$$

Circumscribed hexagon

Six tangent lines to a conic form a hexagon of tangency vertices.

Same arithmetic

One join/meet bracket identity tests both Pascal and Brianchon.

Printed pp. 167-188Brianchon and Duality
Give Brianchon as the exact point line dual of Pascal: circumscribe a hexagon of six tangents, and the three diagonals joining opposite vertices are concurrent. Emphasize the same join and meet bracket arithmetic tests both, so duality is not decorative — it halves the proof work and reuses one incidence equation.

Synthesis and Takeaways

Generation

Five points fix a conic; projectively paired pencils trace it out (Steiner).

Projective character

Cross-ratio from any conic point is the same — the curve is an $\mathbb{RP}^1$.

Symmetry

Conic-preserving maps restrict to $\mathrm{PGL}(2)$ on the curve.

Incidence by transfer

Hesse transfer turns Pascal and Brianchon into one-dimensional bracket identities.

One slogan

$$\text{conic}\ \cong\ \mathbb{RP}^1\ \text{with}\ \mathrm{PGL}(2)\ \text{symmetry}$$

Replay it

Each claim has a numerical certificate in the chapter notebook.
