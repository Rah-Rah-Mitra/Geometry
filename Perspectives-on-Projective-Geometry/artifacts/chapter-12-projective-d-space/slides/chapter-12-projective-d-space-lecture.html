Homogeneous Coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$

Definition

$$\mathbb{RP}^d = \big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{0\}\big)\big/\!\sim,\quad x\sim\lambda x$$

Points are rays

A projective point is a $1$-dim subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$: a nonzero vector up to scale.

Affine chart

The slice $x_{d+1}=1$ recovers ordinary $\mathbb{R}^d$; $x_{d+1}=0$ holds the directions at infinity.

Printed pp. 209-226Homogeneous coordinates in R^{d+1}
Anchor the construction: a point of d-dimensional projective space is a one-dimensional subspace of the (d plus one)-dimensional vector space, equivalently a nonzero vector up to nonzero scale. Stress that scale freedom is the whole point, and that the finite affine chart is just the slice where the last coordinate equals one.

Subspaces and Hyperplanes

$k$-flats

A $k$-dim projective subspace is a $(k{+}1)$-dim linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$.

Hyperplanes

Codimension-one duals, written as covectors $h$ with $h\cdot x=0$.

Rank dictionary in $\mathbb{RP}^3$

$\text{point}=1,\ \text{line}=2,\ \text{plane}=3$

Printed pp. 209-226Subspaces and hyperplanes
Walk through the rank dictionary in three-space: rank one points, rank two lines, rank three planes, rank four numbers. Emphasise that a k-dimensional projective subspace comes from a (k plus one)-dimensional linear subspace, and that hyperplanes are the codimension-one dual objects represented by covectors.

Join and Meet

Join $U\vee V$

The smallest subspace containing both: combine disjoint labels, rank goes up.

Meet $U\wedge V$

The intersection: the dual operation, rank comes down by the ambient $d$.

Examples in $\mathbb{RP}^3$

$\text{pt}\vee\text{pt}=\text{line},\quad \text{plane}\wedge\text{plane}=\text{line}$

Printed pp. 209-226Join and meet
Introduce join as the smallest subspace containing both operands and meet as their intersection. Connect each to the exterior calculus: join combines disjoint label sets and raises rank, meet is the dual operation that lowers rank once the ambient dimension is subtracted. Promise the dimension formula on the next slide ties them together.

The Dimension Formula

Theorem

$$\dim(U\vee V)+\dim(U\wedge V)=\dim U+\dim V$$

Two skew lines

$\dim=1$ each; they span $\mathbb{RP}^3$ ($\dim 3$) and meet emptily ($\dim -1$).

Two coplanar lines

They span a plane ($\dim 2$) and share a point ($\dim 0$).

Printed pp. 209-226The dimension formula
This is the central theorem. Walk students through the two regimes in three-space: two skew lines span everything and meet emptily, while two coplanar lines drop their span to a plane and gain a common point. The formula is the projective analogue of inclusion-exclusion for vector subspace dimensions.

Incidence and Duality

Incidence pairing

$$x\in H \iff h\cdot x = 0$$

Dual swap

Points $\leftrightarrow$ hyperplanes, and $\vee\leftrightarrow\wedge$. In $\mathbb{RP}^3$ the line is self-dual.

Transform rule

Points move by $T$, hyperplanes by $T^{-\top}$, so incidence is preserved.

Printed pp. 209-226Incidence and duality
Duality exchanges points and hyperplanes while reversing join and meet. Show that point lies on hyperplane exactly when the bracket pairing vanishes, and that this incidence is preserved by the inverse-transpose action on covectors. In three-space the self-dual middle object is the line.

$\mathbb{RP}^3$: Points, Lines, Planes

Points

Rank-1 vectors $(x,y,z,w)$; finite when $w\neq0$.

Planes

Rank-3 duals. For $ax+by+cz+d=0$ use labels $123,124,134,234$.

Incidence as a scalar

$p\vee H = ax+by+cz+d$

Printed pp. 209-226RP^3: points, lines, planes
Make three-space concrete. Points are rank one, planes are the dual rank three objects, and lines sit in the self-dual middle. Highlight that a plane through the affine equation a x plus b y plus c z plus d uses the four triple labels, and that joining a point with it returns the equation value as a single scalar.

Plücker Coordinates of a Line

Six minors

$$\ell_{ij}=p_i q_j - p_j q_i$$

From two points

The $2\times2$ minors of the $4\times2$ matrix $[\,p\mid q\,]$ give the line $p\vee q$.

Homogeneous

A different spanning pair only rescales the six-vector; labels with $4$ track the chart offset.

Printed pp. 209-226Plucker coordinates of a line
Define the six Plucker coordinates as the two-by-two minors of the four-by-two matrix of two point representatives. Stress homogeneity: resampling the same line by a different spanning pair only rescales the six-vector. The labels involving index four record how the line sits relative to the plane at infinity.

Grassmann–Plücker & the Klein Quadric

Decomposability test

$$\ell_{12}\ell_{34}-\ell_{13}\ell_{24}+\ell_{14}\ell_{23}=0$$

Not every six-vector

It is a line only when this single quadric vanishes; an identity for genuine joins.

Klein quadric

The relation cuts a quadric in $\mathbb{RP}^5$; lines of $\mathbb{RP}^3$ are exactly its points.

Printed pp. 209-226Grassmann-Plucker and the Klein quadric
Not every six-vector is a line. Decomposability is detected by the single quadratic Grassmann-Plucker relation, an identity for genuine joins and a working test otherwise. Geometrically the relation cuts out the Klein quadric in five-dimensional projective space, so lines of three-space are exactly its points.

The Bracket $[a,b,c,d]$

Definition

$$[a,b,c,d]=\det\big[\,a\ b\ c\ d\,\big]$$

Coplanarity

$[a,b,c,d]=0$ exactly when the four points lie in one plane.

Oriented volume

Its sign and magnitude give the signed volume of the spanned simplex.

Printed pp. 209-226The bracket in 3-space
The bracket of four points is the four-by-four determinant of their representatives. It vanishes precisely when the four points are coplanar, and its sign and magnitude encode oriented volume. Frame the bracket as the universal incidence and dependence detector that powers every join and meet computation in the chapter.

Parallel Planes Meet at Infinity

No exceptions

Parallel planes differ only in the constant label; their meet is a real line.

Line at infinity

$H_1\wedge H_2:\ \ell_{14}=\ell_{24}=\ell_{34}=0$

Same as a join

Equal up to scale to the join of two direction points with $w=0$.

Printed pp. 209-226Parallel planes meet at infinity
Projective space removes the parallel exception. Two parallel planes differ only in their constant label, and their meet is a genuine line whose coordinates with index four vanish, identical up to scale to the join of two direction points. This is the payoff of treating points at infinity as ordinary elements.
