Conics and Their Duals

One symmetric matrix, two geometries: a locus of points and an envelope of lines

Point view

A conic is the zero set $x^\top A x = 0$ of a quadratic form.

Line view

The same conic is the envelope of its tangent lines, governed by $A^{-1}$.

Bridge

Poles and polars tie points to lines through $A$.

A Conic as a Quadratic Form

Definition

A conic is $\{\,x : x^\top A x = 0\,\}$ with $A=A^\top$ a symmetric $3\times3$ matrix.

Symmetric part only

Any skew component cancels in the form, so we may always symmetrize $A$.

Scale invariance

$A\!\to\!\lambda A$ and $x\!\to\!\mu x$ leave the curve fixed.

Homogeneous quadratic form

$$x^\top A x = a x^2 + c y^2 + f z^2 + 2bxy + 2dxz + 2eyz = 0$$

Membership test

$$x \in \text{conic} \iff x^\top A x = 0$$

Printed pp. 145-166A Conic as a Quadratic Form
Define the conic as the zero set of the homogeneous quadratic form x transpose A x with A symmetric. Emphasize that only the symmetric part survives, that scaling A or x changes nothing projectively, and that homogenizing a familiar circle or ellipse equation immediately produces this matrix shape.

Reading the Matrix Entries

The conic matrix

$$A=\begin{bmatrix} a & b & d \\ b & c & e \\ d & e & f \end{bmatrix}$$

Affine type cue

The sign of $\det\!\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{bmatrix}$ flags ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola.

Projective Classification by Rank and Signature

Invariants

Under $A \to T^{-\top} A T^{-1}$ only the rank and the signature of $A$ survive.

Rank 3

Nondegenerate: real conic $(+,+,-)$ or empty real locus $(+,+,+)$.

Rank 2

A line pair: real $(+,-,0)$ or complex-conjugate $(+,+,0)$.

Rank 1

A double line, signature $(+,0,0)$.

Transformation law

$$p' = Tp \;\Longrightarrow\; A' = T^{-\top} A\, T^{-1}$$

Normal form

$$\text{every real nondegenerate conic} \;\sim\; \operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)$$

Printed pp. 145-166Projective Classification
State the central classification: rank and signature are the only projective invariants of a symmetric matrix, so every nondegenerate real conic is projectively equivalent to a single normal form. Contrast the full-rank cases with the rank-two line pairs and the rank-one double line, listing each signature.

All Nondegenerate Conics Are Projectively One

Same object

Ellipse, parabola, hyperbola are one projective conic seen in different affine charts.

Only difference

How the curve meets the line at infinity: $0$, $1$, or $2$ real points.

Consequence

A homography can carry any one to any other.

Printed pp. 145-166All Nondegenerate Conics Agree
Drive home that ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola differ only by how they meet the line at infinity, not as projective objects. A homography can carry any one to any other. The affine names are chart artifacts; the projective truth is one conic up to equivalence.

Poles and Polars

Polar map

A point $x$ has polar line $\ell = A x$; the line $\ell$ has pole $A^{-1}\ell$.

Conjugacy

Points $x,y$ are conjugate when $x^\top A y = 0$.

Involution

For invertible $A$ the pole-polar map is its own inverse.

Pole-polar duality

$$x \;\xrightarrow{\;A\;}\; \ell = Ax, \qquad \ell \;\xrightarrow{\;A^{-1}\;}\; x$$

Self-conjugate points

$$x^\top A x = 0 \iff x \text{ lies on its own polar}$$

Printed pp. 145-166Poles and Polars
Introduce polarity as the map sending a point x to the line A x. Define conjugacy through the symmetric pairing x transpose A y. Explain that the map is an involution when A is invertible, since the inverse matrix sends the polar line back to its pole. This duality is the engine of the chapter.

The Polar Dictionary: Inside, On, Outside

Inside

Polar misses the real conic; discriminant $<0$.

On

Polar is the tangent; discriminant $=0$.

Outside

Polar is a secant through both tangency points; discriminant $>0$.

Printed pp. 145-166The Polar Dictionary
Give the inside, on, and outside dictionary for an ellipse. A point on the conic has its tangent as polar. An exterior point has a polar secant cutting the conic at the two points where its tangents touch. An interior point has a polar that misses the real curve entirely. Tie each to the discriminant sign.

Tangents Are Polars of Conic Points

Tangent at a conic point

$$p^\top A p = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{tangent} = A p$$

Why double root

A line through $p$ meets the conic in a repeated point exactly when it is $Ap$.

Two tangents from outside

Join the exterior point to the two points its polar cuts from the conic.

Printed pp. 145-166Tangents as Polars
Show that the tangent at a conic point is precisely the polar of that point, because the line through it meets the conic in a double root. Then construct the two tangents from an exterior point as the joins to the two intersection points its polar carves out of the curve.

The Dual Conic: A Set of Tangent Lines

Lines, not points

Each tangent has coordinates $\ell = Ap$; these lines form the dual conic.

Dual equation

Tangent coordinates satisfy a quadratic form in the inverse matrix.

Two readings

$A$ governs the point locus; $A^{-1}$ governs the line envelope.

Primal and dual

$$x^\top A x = 0 \qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad \ell^\top A^{-1} \ell = 0$$

Adjugate form (any rank)

$$\ell^\top \operatorname{adj}(A)\, \ell = 0$$

Printed pp. 145-166The Dual Conic
Define the dual conic as the set of tangent lines, governed by the adjugate or inverse matrix through l transpose A inverse l equals zero. Stress the duality of viewpoint: the same conic is simultaneously a locus of points and an envelope of lines, and the tangent family wraps the curve.

Locus and Envelope Together

Same conic

The blue dashed curve is never drawn directly; the tangents reveal it.

Envelope

The conic is the boundary the tangent family is tangent to.

Duality of viewpoint

$$\text{point locus} \;\equiv\; \text{line envelope}$$

Use the envelope picture to make the point-line symmetry concrete: dozens of tangent lines, none drawn on the curve, yet together they outline it. The dual conic A inverse lives in line space exactly as A lives in point space, and tangency is the bridge between the two.

Degenerate Conics and the Adjugate

Rank 2: line pair

$A = g h^\top + h g^\top$; the adjugate stores the singular meeting point.

Rank 1: double line

$A = \ell\,\ell^\top$ and $\operatorname{adj}(A) = 0$.

Why primal-dual pairs

When $\operatorname{adj}(A)$ vanishes a second matrix $B$ must carry the tangent data.

Adjugate of a line pair

$$\operatorname{adj}(g h^\top + h g^\top) = -(g\times h)(g\times h)^\top$$

Pair condition

$$A B = \lambda I, \quad \operatorname{rank}A + \operatorname{rank}B \;\text{constrained}$$

Printed pp. 145-166Degenerate Conics and the Adjugate
Treat degeneracy as legitimate projective content. A rank-two conic is a line pair whose adjugate stores its singular point; a rank-one double line has vanishing adjugate. This loss is why the chapter introduces primal-dual pairs, where a second matrix carries the tangent data the primal can no longer hold.

Synthesis: One Matrix, Every Operation

Point equation

$x^\top A x = 0$ tests membership.

Polar

$x \mapsto Ax$ sends points to lines; tangents are polars of conic points.

Dual

$A^{-1}$ or $\operatorname{adj}(A)$ gives the tangent envelope.

Transform

$A' = T^{-\top} A T^{-1}$ pulls the form back along $T$.

Classify

Rank and signature are the complete projective invariants.

Primal-dual closure

$$A B = \lambda I$$

Printed pp. 145-166Synthesis
Close by collecting the dictionary: one symmetric matrix gives the point equation, sends points to polars, produces tangents, transforms by the inverse, classifies by rank and signature, and pairs with a dual through a product condition. Ask students to recite which matrix operation realizes each geometric act.
