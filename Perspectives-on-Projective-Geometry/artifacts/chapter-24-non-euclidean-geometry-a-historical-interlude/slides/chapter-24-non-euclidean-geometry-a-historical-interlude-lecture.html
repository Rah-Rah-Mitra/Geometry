Euclid's Five Postulates

The first four

Draw a segment between two points; extend a segment; draw a circle from any center and radius; all right angles are equal. Simple, self-evident, uncontested.

Background

These give incidence, extension, congruence of circles, and a notion of right angle.

The fifth (parallel) postulate

If a transversal makes the interior angles on one side sum to less than $\pi$, the two lines meet on that side.

Why suspicious

Long, conditional, and far from obvious. Geometers felt it should follow from the other four, not stand beside them.

Printed pp. 465-482Euclid's Five Postulates
Walk through the first four postulates as the uncontroversial backbone, then dwell on the fifth. Stress that its wordiness and non-self-evidence are exactly why, for two thousand years, geometers believed it ought to be a theorem rather than an assumption, and tried to derive it from the other four.

A web of equivalents

Once the first four postulates hold, the fifth is logically equivalent to many familiar facts. Assume any one, and the rest follow.

What we will test

Playfair's axiom: one parallel through a point. Triangle angle sum equals $\pi$. Existence of rectangles.

Centuries of Failed Proofs

The pattern

Every attempt to derive the fifth from the other four secretly assumed something equivalent to it. The circle was hidden, never broken.

Saccheri's gambit

Assume the fifth is false and chase a contradiction. He produced many strange but consistent theorems.

The near miss

From the acute hypothesis Saccheri found no contradiction, only an unfamiliar geometry.

Why he stopped

He rejected the results as repugnant to the nature of the straight line, an aesthetic verdict, not a logical one. He missed the discovery.

Printed pp. 465-482Centuries of Failed Proofs
Tell the story of the failed proofs as productive failures. Each attempt smuggled in an assumption equivalent to the postulate. Saccheri's reductio is the turning point: he derived strange but contradiction-free theorems from the acute hypothesis and only rejected them on aesthetic grounds, missing the discovery by a hair.

Saccheri and Lambert Quadrilaterals

Construction

Two equal sides rise perpendicular from a base. The two summit angles at the top are then forced to be equal to each other.

The whole question

The entire parallel debate reduces to one trichotomy: are those summit angles right, obtuse, or acute?

Three Hypotheses, Three Geometries

Right angle

$\alpha = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$. Flat, Euclidean geometry. Triangle angle sum is exactly $\pi$; the fifth postulate holds.

Obtuse angle

$\alpha > \tfrac{\pi}{2}$. Elliptic geometry. Angle sum exceeds $\pi$; lines always meet, no parallels exist.

Acute angle

$\alpha < \tfrac{\pi}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ $ hyperbolic geometry, $\ \sum\text{angles} < \pi$

The verdict

Saccheri killed the obtuse case but the acute case is internally consistent. That consistent acute world is hyperbolic geometry.

Printed pp. 465-482Three Hypotheses, Three Geometries
Connect the trichotomy to angle sums and curvature. Right summit angles give Euclidean flatness and angle sum exactly pi; obtuse angles give elliptic geometry where lines always meet; acute angles give hyperbolic geometry with angle sum below pi. Emphasize that the acute case is internally consistent, not absurd.

Three Discoverers, One Geometry

Gauss

Privately convinced of a consistent non-Euclidean geometry, but kept his notes unpublished, wary of the clamor it would raise.

Bolyai

Published a bold appendix building a geometry from the negated postulate. His father had warned him away from the very problem.

Lobachevsky

Developed the new system most fully and in print, earning it the name Lobachevskian geometry.

Shared move

Accept the acute hypothesis as a real geometry and reason deductively, decades before any consistency proof existed.

The Angle of Parallelism

Two limiting parallels

From a point at distance $d$ off a line, two rays just barely fail to meet it. Each makes an acute angle $\Pi(d)$ with the perpendicular.

The signature

$\Pi(d)$ shrinks toward zero as $d$ grows: far away, the cone of non-meeting directions widens.

Lobachevsky's formula

$$\tan\!\left(\tfrac{\Pi(d)}{2}\right) = e^{-d}$$

Contrast with Euclid

In Euclidean geometry $\Pi(d)=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ always. Any departure from a right angle is the parallel postulate failing.

Printed pp. 465-482The Angle of Parallelism
Introduce Lobachevsky's angle of parallelism as the quantitative signature of the new geometry. Through a point off a line there are two limiting parallels making an acute angle that depends on distance and shrinks toward zero far away. In Euclid this angle is always a right angle, so this formula encodes the postulate's failure.

The Beltrami-Klein Disk

Points and lines

Points are interior to the unit circle; lines are ordinary straight chords clipped by the circle.

The absolute conic

The boundary circle is the absolute. Its points are infinitely far away to an inhabitant.

Parallels

Two chords sharing an ideal endpoint are limiting parallels; chords that share no endpoint are ultraparallel.

Printed pp. 465-482The Beltrami-Klein Disk
Now build the model that ends the debate. Points are interior to the unit circle, lines are straight chords, and the circle itself is the absolute conic representing points at infinity. Show many chords through a point all missing a given chord, making Playfair's axiom visibly false inside an ordinary Euclidean picture.

Distance from the Absolute

Boundary supplies scale

Take points $P,Q$ on a chord with ideal endpoints $A,B$ on the boundary. Their cross-ratio fixes the hyperbolic distance.

Why infinite

As $Q$ slides toward $B$, the cross-ratio diverges, so the distance grows without bound. The edge is truly at infinity.

Cayley-Klein distance

$$d_K(P,Q) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\bigl|\log[A,P,Q,B]\bigr|$$

The projective idea

Distance is measured relative to the chosen absolute conic. This is the Cayley-Klein metric of the previous chapters at work.

Printed pp. 465-482Distance from the Absolute
Explain how the boundary supplies a metric. The two ideal endpoints of a chord, together with the two interior points, form a cross-ratio whose logarithm is the hyperbolic distance. This is the Cayley-Klein construction: distance is measured projectively relative to the chosen absolute conic, and the boundary sits infinitely far away.

Consistency via Models

The translation

Every hyperbolic primitive becomes a verified Euclidean statement about the disk: points, chords, cross-ratio distances.

Poincare's companion

A second disk model where geodesics are circular arcs but Euclidean angles equal the true angles, linking the subject to complex analysis.

Relative consistency

A contradiction in hyperbolic geometry $\Rightarrow$ a contradiction in Euclidean geometry.

Independence proved

Hyperbolic and Euclidean geometry stand or fall together. So the parallel postulate cannot be derived from the other four.

Printed pp. 465-482Consistency via Models
State the punchline as a logician would. Because every hyperbolic primitive translates into a verified statement about the Euclidean disk, any contradiction in hyperbolic geometry would force a contradiction in Euclidean geometry. So the two stand or fall together, and the parallel postulate is proven genuinely independent. Mention Poincare's conformal companion model.

The unifying view

The Cayley-Klein program of the preceding chapters reads every classical geometry as projective measurement relative to a chosen absolute conic.

The dictionary

real absolute $\to$ hyperbolic; degenerate $\to$ Euclidean; imaginary $\to$ elliptic.
