Slots Replace Rows and Columns

Points and lines

Both are triples. A point slot is outgoing, a line slot incoming. Joining opposite slots is the incidence test $\langle p,\ell\rangle$.

Grade-2 objects

A transformation $T$ has one in, one out. A quadratic form $A$ has two incoming slots and eats two points.

Typed slots

$\langle p,\ell\rangle = p^i\,\ell_i, \qquad A(p,q)=A_{ij}\,p^i q^j$

Why it matters

Variance, not layout, fixes geometric role. Slot bookkeeping replaces fragile row-versus-column reasoning.

Printed pp. 227-246Slots Replace Rows and Columns
Explain that points and lines are both triples of numbers, distinguished only by slot variance. A point slot is outgoing, a line slot incoming, and joining opposite slots performs the incidence test. Transformations carry one in and one out slot; quadratic forms accept two incoming points. The slot bookkeeping replaces fragile row-versus-column reasoning.

Transformation Halos Cancel

Dual transforms

Points map by $T$, lines by $T^{-\top}$. Draw a halo of $T$ on each point slot, $T^{-1}$ on each line slot.

Internal cancellation

$T\,T^{-1}=\mathbb{1}=\delta$

Consequence

Closed invariant expressions survive coordinate change because internal halos annihilate.

Printed pp. 227-246Transformation Halos Cancel
Under a projective matrix points map by T while lines map by the inverse transpose. Diagrammatically a halo of T sits on every outgoing point slot and an inverse halo on every incoming line slot. On an internal wire these neighbours meet and annihilate, leaving a bare identity. This is why closed invariant expressions survive coordinate change.

The Delta Wire

Identity wire

$\delta_{ij}\,p^j = p^i, \qquad \delta = \mathbb{1}$

Free to add or drop

A delta node can be slid onto any wire or removed from it; it does no geometric work.

Checked symbolically

The notebook confirms $\mathbb{1}\,(x_0,x_1,x_2)^{\top}=(x_0,x_1,x_2)^{\top}$ exactly.

Printed pp. 227-246The Delta Wire

Stress the symbolic check from the notebook: the identity matrix times a vector returns the same vector, confirming delta does no geometric work.

The Epsilon Node and the Bracket

Antisymmetric node

In the plane the epsilon tensor $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is the fully antisymmetric node with $d+1=3$ legs.

Bracket = closed epsilon

$[a,b,c]=\varepsilon_{ijk}\,a^i b^j c^k=\det[a\,b\,c]$

Built-in signs

Swapping two legs flips sign; a repeated leg forces $0$ — the collinearity test, drawn.

Printed pp. 227-246The Epsilon Node and the Bracket
This is the heart of the calculus. The epsilon tensor is the fully antisymmetric node with three legs in the plane. Attach three point-vectors and you contract every leg, producing the determinant bracket. Swapping any two legs flips the sign, and a repeated leg forces zero, which is exactly the collinearity condition made visual.

Joins and Meets: One Node, Two Readings

Join (points → line)

$(a\times b)^k=\varepsilon^{ijk}a_i b_j$

Meet (lines → point)

$(\ell\times m)_k=\varepsilon_{ijk}\,\ell^i m^j$

Duality, made visual

Join and meet are the same epsilon node read with a different free slot — projective duality in one picture.

Printed pp. 227-246Joins and Meets
Leave one epsilon leg free and the node outputs a vector instead of a scalar. Two point legs leave a line leg, giving the join as a cross product. Feed two lines and the free leg is their meet point. Emphasise that join and meet are the same epsilon node read with different free slots, exposing projective duality directly.

The Epsilon–Delta Rewrite

Workhorse identity

$\displaystyle\sum_k \varepsilon_{ijk}\,\varepsilon_{lmk}=\delta_{il}\delta_{jm}-\delta_{im}\delta_{jl}$

The minus is mandatory

It records the crossed pairing of the surviving legs. Drop it and antisymmetry is lost.

Verified

The notebook checks the identity over all index choices symbolically.

Printed pp. 227-246The Epsilon-Delta Rewrite
Present the workhorse identity: a covariant epsilon contracted with a contravariant epsilon along one leg becomes a difference of two delta pairings. The minus sign records the crossed pairing and is never optional. Diagrammatically two three-legged nodes joined by a wire untie into a parallel pair of identity wires minus a crossed pair.

Meet of Two Joins

Apply the rewrite

Open the epsilon wire joining line $a\times b$ to line $c\times d$ and the meet expands into brackets.

Two equal splits

$(a\times b)\times(c\times d)$

$=[a,c,d]\,b-[b,c,d]\,a$

$=[a,b,d]\,c-[a,b,c]\,d$

Same point, two ways

Which epsilon you untie first chooses the split; both land on the identical intersection point.

Cramer, diagrammed

This is the classical meet formula obtained as a picture, and the notebook verifies both splits symbolically.

Reading

$[a,c,d]\,b-[b,c,d]\,a \in \text{line } ab$

Invariants and the First Fundamental Theorem

First fundamental theorem

Every projective invariant is a contraction of epsilon tensors with point and line vectors — a closed diagram.

Bracket scaling

$[Tp,Tq,Tr]=\det(T)\,[p,q,r]$

Weight = epsilon count

Each epsilon node contributes one $\det T$. Count nodes to read the relative weight.

A closed configuration

$I=[r,q,s]\,\langle p,\ell\rangle-[r,p,s]\,\langle q,\ell\rangle$

Relative invariant

Brackets scale by $\det T$; incidence factors are unchanged under the dual line transform. Net weight one.

Lab confirms it

The Plotly determinant lab shows the bracket ratio tracking $\det(T)$ frame by frame.

Printed pp. 227-246Invariants and First Fundamental Theorem
Connect to the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory: every projective invariant is a contraction of epsilon tensors with point and line vectors. A closed configuration diagram reduces to brackets and incidences, and each epsilon contributes one factor of det T under transformation, fixing the relative weight. Diagrams reveal the weight by counting epsilon nodes.

Recap and Practice

The rule set

  • Contraction is a wire.
  • $\delta$ is the identity wire.
  • $\varepsilon$ is the antisymmetric node.
  • Epsilon–delta untying $\to$ bracket algebra.

Diagrams reveal structure

Hidden symmetries and the invariant's weight become visible as soon as the wiring is drawn.

Lab discipline

Attach a numeric check to every move: $\delta$ relabels, $\varepsilon$ alternates, brackets scale by $\det T$, incidences hold under dual transforms.

Practice

$(a\times b)\times(c\times d)\;\overset{?}{=}\;[a,c,d]b-[b,c,d]a$

Fail-fast

A diagram proof fails exactly when one local relabel, sign, or scaling check fails.

Recap the rule set: contraction is a wire, delta is the identity wire, epsilon is the antisymmetric node, and the epsilon-delta rewrite converts networks into bracket algebra.
