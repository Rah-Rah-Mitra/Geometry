Complex Geometry: An Introduction · Chapter 03

Kähler Manifolds: The Metric Condition That Unlocks Hodge Theory

Printed pages 113-144; PDF pages 124-155. The chapter turns the compatibility condition \(d\omega=0\) into the engine behind Hodge decomposition, Lefschetz theory, and Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations.

Use printed page 113, PDF page 124, as the orientation point. The chapter is not merely adding a Riemannian metric to a complex manifold; it singles out metrics whose fundamental form is closed. Tell students that this one closedness condition is the hinge: it gives examples through positive closed forms, analytic control through the Kahler identities, and global topological consequences through Hodge and Lefschetz theory.

The Compatibility Triad \(I,g,\omega\)

Local Matrix Picture

\(h_{\alpha\bar\beta}=g_{\alpha\bar\beta}\), \(\omega=i\sum h_{\alpha\bar\beta}\,dz^\alpha\wedge d\bar z^\beta\)

  • \(I\) splits complexified tangent directions.
  • \(g\) is Hermitian when it respects \(I\).
  • \(\omega\) is a real \((1,1)\)-form encoding the same metric.
Anchor this in printed pages 114-116, PDF pages 125-127. The lecture should make the dictionary explicit: a Hermitian metric can be read as a positive Hermitian matrix in holomorphic coordinates, and its imaginary part gives the fundamental real two-form. The point of the triad diagram is that no one object is decorative; \(I\) tells us what complex directions mean, \(g\) measures them, and \(\omega(u,v)=g(Iu,v)\) is the form that will be tested by \(d\).

Hermitian Is Pointwise; Kähler Adds \(d\omega=0\)

Hermitian Metric

A smooth positive \((1,1)\)-form can encode a metric at every point.

\(\omega=i h_{\alpha\bar\beta}dz^\alpha\wedge d\bar z^\beta,\quad h>0\)

Failure Mode

When \(d\omega\neq 0\), the metric is compatible with \(I\) pointwise but not symplectic.

local positivity \(\not\Rightarrow\) closedness

Use printed pages 116-118, PDF pages 127-129, to distinguish the local and differential requirements. Every Kahler metric is Hermitian, but a Hermitian metric is only a candidate until its fundamental form is closed. Emphasize that \(d\omega=0\) is not a cosmetic regularity assumption; it removes torsion-like failure terms and is what lets the complex, Riemannian, and symplectic languages speak to each other.

Kähler Forms Live In An Open Cone

Definition View

A Kähler form is a real, closed, positive \((1,1)\)-form.

\(\mathcal K_X=\{[\omega]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R): \omega>0,\ d\omega=0\}\)

The cone viewpoint separates the differential representative from its cohomology class.

realclosedpositivetype \((1,1)\)
Continue through printed pages 118-120, PDF pages 129-131. Present the Kahler cone as an organizing object rather than a new technical burden. Positivity is open, closedness fixes a de Rham class, and type \((1,1)\) places the class in the Hodge-theoretic part of cohomology once compact theory is available. The boundary matters because many later questions ask when a limiting class is nef, ample, or genuinely represented by a Kahler form.

Fubini-Study Is The Local Model From A Potential

Local Formula

\(\omega_{FS}=i\partial\bar\partial\log(1+\sum |z_j|^2)\)

The form glues on projective space because chart changes alter the potential by terms killed by \(\partial\bar\partial\).

Use the Fubini-Study discussion on printed pages 120-122, PDF pages 131-133. The teaching move is to separate the potential from the form: the logarithmic potential is chart-dependent, but the \(\partial\bar\partial\) of the potential is globally meaningful on projective space. Students should see why this model is both explicit and universal for projective examples: it supplies the positive closed \((1,1)\)-form that projective geometry keeps restricting.

\(\mathbb{CP}^1\) Normalization: Area Is A Cohomology Choice

Affine Coordinate

\(\omega_{FS}=\frac{i\,dz\wedge d\bar z}{(1+|z|^2)^2}\)

Different conventions place \(2\pi\) either in the form or in the integral.

Normalization Reminder

\(\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega_{FS}\) fixes the generator of \(H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1)\).

State the convention before comparing curvature, Chern classes, or hyperplane classes.

Still in the examples around printed pages 120-123, PDF pages 131-134, pause on \(\mathbb{CP}^1\). This is where normalization mistakes often begin. The same geometric sphere picture may be paired with conventions differing by \(2\pi\), so the instructor should say which generator of \(H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1)\) the form represents. The important conceptual point is that the area integral is not an afterthought; it identifies the class used later for Chern classes and Lefschetz operators.

Projective And Submanifold Examples Come By Restriction

Restriction Principle

If \(X\subset \mathbb{CP}^N\) is a complex submanifold, then \(\omega_{FS}|_X\) remains real, closed, positive, and of type \((1,1)\).

d(\omega_{FS}|_X)=(d\omega_{FS})|_X=0

Projective Space

Explicit global Kähler form from Fubini-Study.

Smooth Submanifold

Restriction preserves closedness and positivity.

Products

Sums of pullback Kähler forms stay Kähler.

Finish the introductory examples on printed pages 122-123, PDF pages 133-134. The restriction argument is short but powerful: closedness commutes with pullback, type is preserved by holomorphic inclusion, and positivity survives on nonzero complex tangent vectors. This is the bridge from one explicit projective model to a large class of compact Kahler manifolds, and it also previews why projective geometry will later impose rationality and integrality conditions beyond being merely Kahler.

Lefschetz Operators Organize Forms By Powers Of \(\omega\)

Decomposition

A^k=\bigoplus_{r\ge 0} L^r P^{k-2r}

The algebra of \(L\), its adjoint \(\Lambda\), and the grading operator is the linear skeleton behind Hard Lefschetz.

This slide starts the Lefschetz vocabulary used later in the chapter, tied to the metric material on printed pages 114-123 and the later theorems on printed pages 138-141, PDF pages 149-152. Define \(L\) as wedging with the Kahler form and \(\Lambda\) as its adjoint. Primitive forms are the pieces not already obtained by wedging lower-degree material with \(\omega\). The ladder diagram should be read as linear algebra on each tangent space before it becomes a cohomological theorem.

Why The Grid Matters

The identities convert \(d\)-harmonic information into \(\partial\)- and \(\bar\partial\)-harmonic information without changing representatives.

Use the Kahler identities from printed pages 123-124, PDF pages 134-135, as the analytic turning point. Do not ask students to memorize every sign in the first pass. The \(\partial\bar\partial\) Lemma Is An Equivalence Square

Lemma Shape

On compact Kähler manifolds, several notions of exactness collapse to \(\partial\bar\partial\)-exactness under the usual closedness hypotheses.

im \(d\cap ker\partial\cap ker\bar\partial = im\,\partial\bar\partial\)

Use printed pages 127-130, PDF pages 138-141. The \(\partial\bar\partial\) lemma is often the first result that feels disproportionately strong, so frame it as an equivalence square among exactness conditions. The hypotheses matter: compact Kahler geometry and closedness under the relevant operators are doing the work. The payoff is that de Rham, Dolbeault, and Bott-Chern style representatives can be compared with far less ambiguity.

Hodge Decomposition: Diamond Symmetries And Betti Sums

Decomposition

H^k(X,\mathbb C)=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}H^{p,q}(X)

b_k=\sum_{p+q=k} h^{p,q}

Conjugation and Poincare duality impose visible symmetries on the diamond.

Use printed pages 130-131, PDF pages 141-142, for the cohomology decomposition. The Hodge diamond is a memory device, but insist that it is also an accounting table: \(h^{p,q}\) counts harmonic representatives of type \((p,q)\), conjugation reflects the real structure, and diagonal sums recover Betti numbers. This is a good place to remind students that the decomposition is a compact Kahler conclusion, not a feature of every complex manifold.

\((1,1)\) Classes Connect Cohomology To Picard Data

Lefschetz \((1,1)\) Theme

Integral \((1,1)\)-classes are the cohomological shadow of holomorphic line bundles in the projective/Kähler setting.

c_1: Pic(X)\to H^2(X,\mathbb Z)

Move into printed pages 132-135, PDF pages 143-146. The first Chern class map lets line bundles enter cohomology, and the \((1,1)\) condition identifies which degree-two classes can arise in the holomorphic story. Keep Picard, Picard-zero, and Neron-Severi visually separated: Picard records line bundles, Picard-zero records the topologically trivial part, and Neron-Severi records the discrete divisor-like classes after quotienting by Picard-zero.

Pic\(^0\) And Albanese Are Dual Torus Constructions

Pic\(^0(X)\) and Alb\((X)\) package the continuous part of line bundles and period integrals.

Use printed pages 135-138, PDF pages 146-149. Picard-zero is not a finite correction term; it is a complex torus measuring topologically trivial holomorphic line bundles. The Albanese torus is built from periods of holomorphic one-forms, and it is naturally dual to the Picard-zero story. In lecture, the lattice quotient pictures should help students see why these objects are global, analytic, and still controlled by Hodge-theoretic data.

Hard Lefschetz Leads To Hodge-Riemann Positivity

Hodge-Riemann Form

Q(\alpha,\bar\alpha)=i^{p-q}(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\int_X \alpha\wedge\bar\alpha\wedge\omega^{n-k}

On primitive \((p,q)\)-classes, this form has the prescribed positivity.

Use printed pages 138-142, PDF pages 149-153. Hard Lefschetz is the global version of the local Lefschetz ladder: wedging by powers of the Kahler class gives isomorphisms across complementary degrees. Hodge-Riemann then refines this by saying the natural bilinear form has controlled signs on primitive classes. The instructor should stress that the theorem is both algebraic-looking and analytic in origin, resting on the Kahler identities and compact Hodge theory already built.

Hodge Index, Signature, And The Hodge Conjecture Boundary

Projective Caution

The Hodge conjecture concerns rational Hodge classes on smooth projective varieties, not arbitrary Kahler classes.

H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{p,p}(X)\quad ? \quad \text{algebraic cycles}

Index

Primitive decomposition controls signatures of intersection forms.

Signature

Hodge numbers encode topological signature formulas in even dimensions.

Boundary

Rationality and projectivity are extra hypotheses, not automatic from Kahlerness.

Close with printed pages 142-144, PDF pages 153-155. The Hodge index theorem is a concrete signature consequence of Hodge-Riemann, especially memorable for surfaces where the intersection form has one positive direction along the ample or Kahler class and negative directions on primitive \((1,1)\)-classes. Then draw the boundary carefully: the Hodge conjecture is a statement about rational Hodge classes on smooth projective varieties and algebraic cycles. Do not let students collapse compact Kahler, projective, rational, and algebraic into the same word.
