Algebraic base layer

Supercommutative Differential Algebra

Parity Grading

A supercommutative algebra has \(A=A^0\oplus A^1\). A homogeneous element \(a\) has parity \(|a|\in \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

\(a\beta=(-1)^{|a||\beta|}\beta a\)
Odd Differential

The differential switches parity, satisfies \(d^2=0\), and obeys

\(d(a\beta)=d(a)\beta+(-1)^{|a|}a\,d(\beta)\).

Definition before BV: only a \(\mathbb Z/2\)-grading is requiredRole in geometry: parity is \(p+q\)
Start at the smallest algebraic level: the appendix only needs a parity grading, not a full integer grading. Every sign comes from moving odd objects past each other. The differential is odd, squares to zero, and obeys the graded Leibniz rule, so students should hear this as the algebraic shadow of the Dolbeault differential.
Definition 6.A.1

BV, GBV, And dGBV Operators

BV Operator

An odd linear operator \(\Delta:A\to A\) is BV when the operation

\(\Theta_a(\beta)=(-1)^{|a|}\Delta(a\beta)-(-1)^{|a|}\Delta(a)\beta-a\Delta(\beta)\)

is a derivation of parity \(|a|+1\).

BV\(\Theta_a\) is a derivation
GBVBV plus \(\Delta^2=0\)
dGBVGBV plus \(d\Delta+\Delta d=0\)
Delta is odd and second-order in spiritTheta notation records the derived operation
Emphasize that Delta is not introduced as another derivation. It is an odd linear operator whose second-order failure produces derivations theta_a. If Delta also squares to zero, the structure is GBV; if an existing differential d anticommutes with Delta, the resulting package is dGBV. That compatibility is what later survives deformation.
Proposition 6.A.2

The Derived Odd Bracket

\([a\bullet \beta] := \Theta_a(\beta)\)
Odd Lie Behavior
  • Odd skew: \([a,\beta]=-(-1)^{(|a|+1)(|\beta|+1)}[\beta,a]\).
  • Odd Jacobi: nesting distributes with the shifted parity sign.
  • Compatibility: \([d,\Theta_a]=\Theta_{d(a)}\).

Reading The Signs

The bracket is Lie after a parity shift, so the exponent uses \(|a|+1\) and \(|\beta|+1\).

Derived bracket notation: \([a\bullet\beta]\)The proof is sign computation; the structure is the lesson
The bracket is not an extra primitive in the abstract definition; it is derived from the BV operator. The displayed identities say it behaves like a Lie bracket after shifting parity by one. The important teaching point is that d differentiates the bracket, so the bracket can measure whether a perturbed differential still squares to zero.
Proposition 6.A.3

Deforming The Differential

Even Perturbation

For \(\varphi\in A^0\), set

\(d_{\varphi}=d+\Theta_{\varphi}\).

Both summands are odd derivations.

Master Equation

The condition \(d_{\varphi}^2=0\) is governed by

\(d\varphi+\frac12[\varphi,\varphi]=0\).

Same shape as Maurer-Cartan: linear plus quadratic\(\varphi\) is even in the abstract dGBV statement
This is the first payoff slide. An even element phi produces a new odd derivation d_phi by adding the inner operation theta_phi. Squaring d_phi gives exactly the same shape as the deformation equation from complex structures: a linear term plus a quadratic bracket term. The master equation is therefore an obstruction-cancellation equation.
Return to complex manifolds

The Algebra Of Polyvector-Valued Forms

Geometric Algebra

\(A_X=\bigoplus_{p,q}A^{0,q}(X,\wedge^pT_X)\)

with parity \(p+q\), exterior product, and differential \(\bar\partial\).

Sign Convention

Forms and tangent vectors anticommute in the total parity, so \(dz_i\wedge \partial_{z_j}=-\partial_{z_j}\wedge dz_i\).

Here \(T_X\) is holomorphic, so \(\bar\partial\) acts on the coefficientsCentral object: \(A^{0,*}(X,\wedge^*T_X)\)
Now move from abstract algebra back to geometry. The algebra A_X consists of (0,q)-forms with values in exterior powers of the holomorphic tangent bundle. Its parity is p plus q, the product combines exterior multiplication and polyvector wedge, and the Dolbeault operator is the differential because the tangent bundle powers are holomorphic.
Lemma 6.A.4

Schouten Bracket Before Delta

Schouten-Nijenhuis Bracket

The bracket extends the Lie bracket of vector fields to polyvector-valued forms.

Properties Used
  • \([\alpha,\,]\) is a derivation on \(A_X\).
  • \([\alpha,[\alpha,\,]]=\frac12[[\alpha,\alpha],\,]\).
  • \([\bar\partial,\Theta_\alpha]=\Theta_{\bar\partial\alpha}\).

Missing Piece

For a general complex manifold, this bracket is present, but there is no automatic BV operator \(\Delta\).

Corollary 6.A.5 repeats the master-equation criterion for \(A_X\)The Calabi-Yau step adds \(\Delta\)
For any complex manifold, the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket is already present on polyvector fields and extends to polyvector-valued forms. It has the odd skew symmetry and derivation properties needed for the master equation, but it need not come from a BV operator. The Calabi-Yau condition will supply that missing origin.
Remark 6.A.6

Maurer-Cartan Versus Master Equation

Complex-Structure Side

For a Beltrami differential \(\varphi(t)\in A^{0,1}(T_X)\), integrability is encoded by

\(\bar\partial\varphi+\frac12[\varphi,\varphi]=0\).

dGBV Side

The same expression is the master equation for the deformed differential \(\bar\partial_\varphi=\bar\partial+\Theta_\varphi\) on \(A_X\).

The normalization by \(1/2\) is bookkeeping, not geometry\(\bar\partial(t)\) and \(\bar\partial_\varphi\) live in different viewpoints
Connect the appendix directly to Chapter 6. A Beltrami differential phi in A^{0,1}(T_X) satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation precisely when the corresponding element in the larger algebra satisfies the master equation, up to the harmless normalization used in the appendix. The distinction is conceptual: we deform the differential on the same algebraic model.
Calabi-Yau input

A Calabi-Yau Volume Form Creates Delta

Contraction Isomorphism

Fix a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic \(n\)-form \(\Omega\). Contraction gives

\(\eta_\Omega:A^{0,q}(\wedge^pT_X)\to A^{n-p,q}\).

\(\Delta=\eta_\Omega^{-1}\circ\partial\circ\eta_\Omega\)

Consequences

\(\Delta^2=0\), and holomorphicity of \(\Omega\) gives \(\bar\partial\Delta+\Delta\bar\partial=0\).

Trivial \(K_X\) is the source of the operatorThis is where Calabi-Yau geometry enters the dGBV story
This is the geometric source of the BV operator. A nowhere-vanishing holomorphic volume form converts polyvectors into ordinary forms by contraction. Transporting the partial operator across that identification gives Delta. Because partial squares to zero and the volume form is holomorphic, Delta squares to zero and anticommutes with the Dolbeault differential.
Proposition 6.A.7 and Corollary 6.A.8

Generalized Tian-Todorov Lemma

Bracket Generated By Delta

For \(\alpha,\beta\in A_X\), the Schouten bracket is generated by \(\Delta\) in the BV sense:

\(-[\alpha,\beta]=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\Delta(\alpha\beta)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\Delta(\alpha)\beta-\alpha\Delta(\beta)\).

Immediate Outcome

For a Calabi-Yau manifold, \(\left(A^{0,*}(X,\wedge^*T_X),\bar\partial,\Delta\right)\) is a dGBV algebra.

Generalizes the earlier Tian-Todorov identitySign conventions differ, but the derived-bracket role is fixed
Present the lemma as the bridge between the abstract and geometric stories. The Schouten bracket on A_X is generated by Delta in the BV sense, up to the sign convention. Therefore, for a Calabi-Yau manifold, the polyvector-valued Dolbeault algebra is not merely bracketed; it is a full dGBV algebra.
Remark 6.A.9

Keeping dGBV Under Deformation

Two Conditions
  1. Master equation: \(d_\varphi^2=0\).
  2. Gauge condition: \(\Delta(\varphi)=0\).
Then

\((A,d_\varphi,\Delta)\) is again a dGBV algebra because \([d_\varphi,\Delta]=0\).

Calabi-Yau Reading

\(\Delta(\varphi)=0\) means \(\eta_\Omega(\varphi)\) is \(\partial\)-closed.

The gauge condition is the algebraic companion to unobstructednessSame \(\Delta\), changed \(d\)
The deformation d_phi solves only half the problem: it becomes a differential when the master equation holds. To remain a dGBV algebra with the same Delta, we also need Delta phi equal to zero. In the Calabi-Yau picture, that condition says the contracted form eta(phi) is partial-closed, matching the earlier Tian-Todorov gauge choice.
Definition 6.A.10 and Proposition 6.A.12 setup

Formal Solutions In A General dGBV Algebra

Cohomology

\(H(A)=\ker(d:A\to A)/\operatorname{im}(d:A\to A)\).

The appendix assumes \(H^1(A)=0\), so nontrivial cohomology classes are even.

Formal Unknown

\(\Phi=\Phi_1+\Phi_2+\Phi_3+\cdots\in A\otimes\mathbb C[H(A)^*]\)

with \(d\Phi+\frac12[\Phi,\Phi]=0\).

\(\Phi_0=0\); \(\Phi_1\) represents cohomologyFor \(k\ge 2\), the construction keeps \(\Phi_k\in\ker\Delta\)
After the geometric example, the appendix abstracts the Tian-Todorov recursion. The unknown is a formal power series Phi with coefficients in the algebra and polynomial coordinates on cohomology. Phi_1 chooses representatives for cohomology classes, and higher Phi_k terms are correction terms added so the master equation holds order by order.
Proposition 6.A.11

The Two Hypotheses

Assumption (1)

\(H(A)\) is finite-dimensional.

Assumption (2)

\(\operatorname{im}(d\Delta)=(\ker d\cap\ker\Delta)\cap(\operatorname{im}d+\operatorname{im}\Delta)\).

Calabi-Yau Compact Kahler Case

Finite Dolbeault cohomology gives (1), and the \(\partial\bar\partial\)-lemma gives the image identity (2).

The finite-dimensional parameter space is \(H(A)\)Condition (2) is the exactness engine for recursion
Make the two extra assumptions feel familiar rather than technical. Finite-dimensional cohomology gives a finite parameter space for formal coordinates. The image identity is the dGBV version of the ddbar lemma: if an element is closed for both d and Delta and is separately d-exact or Delta-exact, it actually comes from dDelta.
Proposition 6.A.12

Recursion: Why Obstructions Disappear

Inductive Obstruction

At homogeneous degree \(k\), previous terms create

\(R_k=\frac12\sum_{i+j=k}[\Phi_i,\Phi_j]\).

Recursion Move

Lower-order equations make \(dR_k=0\). The Delta-gauge and bracket formula put \(R_k\) in \(\operatorname{im}\Delta\). Hypothesis (2) solves \(R_k=d\Delta\Psi_k\), so choose \(\Phi_k=-\Delta\Psi_k\).

Final Output

\(\Phi_0=0\), \(\Phi_1\) splits cohomology, \(\Phi_k\in\ker\Delta\) for \(k\ge2\), and \(d\Phi+\frac12[\Phi,\Phi]=0\).

Formal unobstructedness is an exactness-and-gauge recursionAppendix 61 closes the Chapter 6 deformation mechanism
Close with the mechanism, not just the statement. At each degree, previous choices create a quadratic obstruction term. The bracket identity and Delta-closed gauge put that term in the exact rectangle, hypothesis (2) solves it as dDelta of something, and the next correction kills it while staying Delta-closed. This is the abstract Tian-Todorov engine.
