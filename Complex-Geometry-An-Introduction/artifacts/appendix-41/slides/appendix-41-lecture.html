The central comparison

Two Connections Act on the Same Geometry

Comparison Problem
Identify the real and complex tangent pictures
Use the isomorphism \(TX \to T^{1,0}X\), then compare the connection induced by a Hermitian connection with the Levi-Civita connection of \(g\).
The answer is not automatic. Hermitian connections are plentiful, while the Levi-Civita connection is unique. The Chern connection becomes the Levi-Civita connection precisely under the Kahler compatibility.
This is the orientation slide. Students should see that the appendix compares not two unrelated gadgets, but two ways of transporting tangent vectors on the same manifold. The map u to one half of u minus i Iu identifies the real tangent bundle with the (1,0) tangent bundle. The question is whether the canonical complex connection and the canonical real connection line up under that identification.
Riemannian warm-up

Metric Connections and Torsion

Definition
Metric connection and torsion
A connection \(D\) on \(TM\) is metric when \(Dg=0\). Its torsion is the tensor
$$T_D(u,v)=D_u v-D_v u-[u,v].$$

Local diagnostic

If \(D=d+A\), then constant-coordinate terms cancel and

$$T_D(u,v)=A(u)v-A(v)u.$$
The first technical move is to separate metric compatibility from torsion. Metric compatibility says the connection preserves inner products under transport. Torsion, by contrast, measures the skew part of covariant differentiation after the Lie bracket has removed the intrinsic noncommutativity of vector fields. In coordinates, the formula A(u)v minus A(v)u is the practical test: torsion is a real tensor, not an artifact of the chosen functions.
The unique real baseline

Levi-Civita Is the Intersection of Two Constraints

Theorem 4.A.3
Levi-Civita uniqueness
For every Riemannian manifold \((M,g)\), there exists exactly one connection on \(TM\) satisfying
$$Dg=0,\qquad T_D=0.$$
This connection is the Levi-Civita connection.
This theorem makes Levi-Civita the standard of comparison for every later connection induced from complex geometry.
The uniqueness statement is the anchor for the whole appendix. Once a connection is both metric and torsion-free, there is no further freedom left. That is why a Hermitian connection cannot usually become Levi-Civita merely by forgetting complex structure: there are many Hermitian connections. The later propositions succeed only when the induced real connection also lands inside this tiny intersection.
Why torsion-free matters

Torsion-Free Connections Compute Exterior Differentials Cleanly

Formula
Exterior derivative through \(D\)
When \(T_D=0\), the induced connection on forms gives
$$d\alpha(v_0,\ldots,v_k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^i(D_{v_i}\alpha)(v_0,\ldots,\widehat{v_i},\ldots,v_k).$$

Immediate corollary

If \(D\alpha=0\) and \(T_D=0\), then \(d\alpha=0\). Parallel forms become closed forms.

This result explains why torsion-free is more than a formal condition. In the torsion-free case, the covariant derivative formula for d has no extra correction terms. The important downstream use is for the fundamental two-form omega. Once a connection is metric and commutes with the complex structure, omega is parallel. Torsion-free then promotes parallelness to closedness, which is the Kahler condition.
Enter complex geometry

Hermitian Manifolds Tie \(g\), \(I\), and \(\omega\) Together

Hermitian Structure
Riemannian metric compatible with \(I\)
The complex structure splits \(T_{\mathbb C}X\), while the metric gives the two-form \(\omega=g(I\,\cdot,\cdot)\).
$$\Phi:TX\longrightarrow T^{1,0}X,\qquad \Phi(u)=\frac12(u-iIu).$$

This identification is the bridge used to move connections back and forth between the real and complex tangent bundles.

Now the real tangent bundle is given complex structure. A Hermitian metric is not an extra metric floating above the Riemannian one; it is the same real metric made compatible with I. The fundamental form omega packages the metric and I together. The map Phi is the practical bridge: it turns a real tangent vector into its (1,0) component, so a connection on T^{1,0}X can be read as a real connection when appropriate.
First bridge lemma

A Hermitian Connection Induces a Metric Real Connection

Lemma 4.A.6
Taking real parts preserves metricity
Under \(TX\simeq T^{1,0}X\), a Hermitian connection on \((T^{1,0}X,g_{\mathbb C})\) induces a connection \(D\) on \(TX\) satisfying \(Dg=0\).
$$d g_{\mathbb C}(s,t)=g_{\mathbb C}(\nabla s,t)+g_{\mathbb C}(s,\nabla t)\quad \Rightarrow\quad dg(u,v)=g(Du,v)+g(u,Dv).$$

But not enough

Metricity alone does not force Levi-Civita. The remaining question is whether torsion vanishes.

This lemma is deliberately modest. If a connection preserves the Hermitian metric on the complex tangent bundle, then its real part preserves the Riemannian metric. This explains why the Chern connection is a plausible candidate for comparison with Levi-Civita. However, Levi-Civita uniqueness has two hypotheses. The bridge lemma supplies metricity, but the torsion calculation has to do the remaining work.
The key implication

Torsion-Free Hermitian Transport Forces the Kahler Package

Proposition 4.A.7
Torsion-free Hermitian connection
If a Hermitian connection on \(T^{1,0}X\) has zero induced torsion, then it is the Chern connection, the induced real connection is Levi-Civita, and \((X,g)\) is Kahler.
$$T^\nabla=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nabla=\nabla^{\mathrm{Ch}},\quad D=\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}},\quad d\omega=0.$$

The proof uses the torsion formula to force the connection form into type \((1,0)\), then uses parallelness of \(\omega\).

This proposition is the first major bridge theorem. The proof has a local calculation hidden inside it: zero torsion forces the connection form to have the type condition expected of the Chern connection. Once the induced real connection is metric and torsion-free, Levi-Civita uniqueness applies. Finally, since the connection preserves both g and I, it preserves omega, and the torsion-free exterior derivative formula makes omega closed.
Four equivalent viewpoints

The Kahler Condition Is a Compatibility Loop

In the appendix, these equivalences license a notational simplification: on a Kahler manifold, \(D^{LC}\) and \(\nabla^{Ch}\) may be treated as the same tangent connection.
This is the summary theorem the lecturer should pause on. The source span phrases the conclusion as four equivalent conditions: Levi-Civita makes I parallel, the metric is Kahler, Levi-Civita and Chern are identified by the tangent isomorphism, and the Chern connection is torsion-free. The point is not memorizing a chain of implications; it is recognizing the same compatibility appearing as a differential form condition, a connection condition, and a torsion condition.
Curvature comparison

Riemannian Curvature Matches Connection Curvature

Riemannian Curvature
Operator convention
The Levi-Civita curvature acts on vector fields by
$$R(u,v)s=D_uD_v s-D_vD_u s-D_{[u,v]}s.$$

Bundle curvature viewpoint

The same expression is the curvature \(F^D\) of the connection on \(TM\). The appendix verifies this by expanding \(Ds=\alpha\otimes t\).

After the Kahler equivalence, the appendix turns to curvature. The Riemannian curvature formula is the commutator of covariant derivatives corrected by the derivative along the Lie bracket. For a tangent bundle connection, this is exactly the usual bundle curvature. This matters because the later Ricci formula can be read either through Riemannian curvature tensors or through the Chern curvature of T^{1,0}X.
Ricci in complex language

Kahler Ricci Curvature Has Two Equivalent Traces

Proposition 4.A.11
Ricci comparison
For a Kahler manifold, using the common Levi-Civita/Chern connection,
$$\operatorname{Ric}(X,g)=i\,\omega(\Lambda_\omega F^\nabla)=i\,\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C}(F^\nabla).$$

Ricci-flat

The Kahler metric is Ricci-flat exactly when this real two-form vanishes.

The Ricci comparison is where the two languages become computationally interchangeable. The Riemannian Ricci tensor is converted to a real two-form by inserting I in the first slot. On a Kahler manifold, the curvature of the common tangent connection is of type (1,1), and the Ricci form can be obtained either by contracting with the Kahler form or by taking the complex trace of the endomorphism-valued curvature.
From curvature to holonomy

Parallel Transport Preserves the Riemannian Metric

Parallel Transport
Transport along a path
For a path \(\gamma:[0,1]\to M\), a vector field \(v(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}M\) is parallel when it is flat for the pulled-back connection \(\gamma^*D\).
$$P_\gamma:T_{\gamma(0)}M\longrightarrow T_{\gamma(1)}M,\qquad P_\gamma(v)=v(1).$$

Isometry and closed loops

Since \(Dg=0\), \(P_\gamma\) preserves inner products. Closed paths based at \(x\) generate \(\operatorname{Hol}_x(M,g)\subset O(T_xM)\).

Parallel transport is the mechanism by which a connection becomes group-theoretic. Pull the tangent bundle back to the interval, solve the flat-section equation, and the initial vector determines a final vector. Metric compatibility says lengths and inner products do not change. When the path closes, the resulting operators form the holonomy group, a subgroup of the orthogonal group at the base point.
Classification snapshot

Berger's List Highlights the Complex Cases

General Riemannian

$$SO(m)$$

No extra parallel tensor is forced beyond orientation in the irreducible non-symmetric setting.

Complex geometry

$$U(n),\quad SU(n)$$

These preserve a complex structure; \(SU(n)\) also preserves a complex volume form.

Further special cases

$$Sp(n),\quad Sp(n)Sp(1),\quad G_2,\quad Spin(7)$$

The appendix flags these as important but keeps the lecture path centered on \(U(n)\) and \(SU(n)\).

The appendix invokes Berger's theorem only as a snapshot, not as a proof target. Under standard hypotheses such as simply connected, irreducible, and not locally symmetric, the possible holonomy groups are highly restricted. For this course, the important entries are U(n) and SU(n), with Sp(n) nearby because of hyperkahler geometry. The classification tells us that holonomy reduction is a major geometric event, not a cosmetic change of notation.
Holonomy principle

Invariant Tensors Extend to Parallel Tensors

Holonomy Principle
From one tangent space to the manifold
If a tensor \(a_x\) on \(T_xM\) is invariant under \(\operatorname{Hol}_x(M,g)\), then \(a_x\) extends uniquely by parallel transport to a parallel tensor field \(a\) on \(M\).
$$\operatorname{Hol}(M,g)\subset U(n)\quad\Rightarrow\quad \exists\, I\ \text{parallel},\quad I^2=-\mathrm{id}.$$

Complex consequence

The parallel complex structure gives a Kahler form \(\omega=g(I\cdot,\cdot)\) that is parallel and therefore closed.

The holonomy principle is the conceptual payoff. If a tensor at one tangent space is fixed by all closed-loop transports, then parallel transport defines the same tensor everywhere with no path ambiguity. For a U(n) holonomy reduction, the invariant tensor is an almost complex structure I_x. Its parallel extension has I squared equal to minus the identity everywhere, and the torsion-free connection ensures the resulting complex structure is integrable, so the metric is Kahler.
Complex holonomy consequences

\(U(n)\) Gives Kahler; \(SU(n)\) Adds Ricci-Flatness

Holonomy inside \(U(n)\)

$$\operatorname{Hol}(M,g)\subset U(n)$$

There is a parallel complex structure \(I\). The form \(\omega=g(I\cdot,\cdot)\) is parallel, hence closed, and \(g\) is Kahler for that complex structure.

Holonomy inside \(SU(n)\)

$$\operatorname{Hol}(M,g)\subset SU(n)$$

A parallel complex volume form trivializes \(K_X\). With the Kahler metric, the Ricci form vanishes, so the metric is Ricci-flat.

This final mathematical slide states the two propositions at the end of the appendix in teaching form. U(n) holonomy gives an invariant complex structure, hence a parallel complex structure and a Kahler metric. SU(n) holonomy strengthens the invariant data: the determinant representation is trivial, so a parallel holomorphic volume form appears. Combined with the Ricci formula for the canonical bundle, this yields Ricci-flatness.
