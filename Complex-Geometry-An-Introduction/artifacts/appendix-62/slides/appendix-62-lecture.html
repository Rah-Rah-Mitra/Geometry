Definitions B.0.19

Presheaves Are Restriction Machines

Open sets are ordered by inclusion
Sections move in the opposite direction

Presheaf Data

For every open \(U\subset M\), an algebraic object \(\mathcal F(U)\). For \(U\subset V\), a restriction \(r_{U,V}:\mathcal F(V)\to\mathcal F(U)\).

Functorial Rules

\(r_{U,U}=\mathrm{id}\), and whenever \(U\subset V\subset W\), the two restriction paths agree:

\(r_{U,V}\circ r_{V,W}=r_{U,W}\).

Sections restrict; open sets include. The direction reversal is the first local-to-global habit.

Introduce a presheaf as a disciplined bookkeeping device. The open sets become a category by inclusion, while the assigned groups or rings move contravariantly through restriction maps. The identity and composition rules mean that restricting in one step or several steps gives the same answer. This is still only bookkeeping; no gluing has been promised yet.

Definition B.0.21

The Two Sheaf Axioms

A sheaf is a presheaf that can recognize
and reconstruct local agreement

Separatedness

If two sections over \(U\) restrict to the same section on every member \(U_i\) of an open cover, then they were equal on \(U\).

Gluing

If \(s_i\in\mathcal F(U_i)\) agree on all pairwise overlaps \(U_i\cap U_j\), then there is a unique \(s\in\mathcal F(U)\) with \(s|_{U_i}=s_i\).

Running Example

Continuous functions form a sheaf because equality and continuity can both be tested locally across an open cover.

The key distinction from a presheaf is that local data can be trusted. Separatedness says local tests detect equality, while gluing says compatible local pieces assemble into a unique global piece. Use continuous functions as the sanity check: if the functions agree on overlaps, the pasted function is well defined, and continuity remains local.

Examples B.0.20 and B.0.22

Examples: Functions, Constants, Sections

The same word can hide different local behavior

Continuous Functions

\(\mathcal C_M(U)\) is the ring of continuous maps \(U\to\mathbb R\). Restriction is ordinary restriction of functions.

Constant Presheaf

The rule \(U\mapsto\mathbb R\) for every nonempty \(U\) fails on disconnected open sets: two different constants can live on different components.

Constant Sheaf

The corrected sheaf assigns locally constant functions into the discrete set \(\mathbb R\), so each component may choose its own value.

This comparison is where students often repair their intuition. A constant presheaf remembers one number per open set, so it cannot glue different constants across disconnected components. The constant sheaf instead means locally constant functions. Then move to vector bundles: the sheaf of sections is not extra decoration, because for many purposes the bundle can be recovered from its section sheaf.

Definitions B.0.23 and B.0.26

Homomorphisms and the Image-Cokernel Trap

Kernels behave well; images and cokernels need sheafification

Compatibility Square

A morphism \(\varphi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G\) is a family \(\varphi_U:\mathcal F(U)\to\mathcal G(U)\) commuting with all restrictions.

Subtle Point

\(\ker\varphi\) is already a sheaf. The pointwise image and cokernel are generally only presheaves; their sheaf versions are obtained by sheafification.

Surjectivity Means Local Lifting

A sheaf morphism may be surjective even when \(\varphi_U\) is not onto for a chosen open set \(U\).

The commutative square gives a clean test for a sheaf homomorphism: map first and restrict later, or restrict first and map later, and the result must match. Then stress the asymmetric behavior. Kernels are local equalizers and stay sheaves. Images and cokernels can fail gluing, so the source defines image and cokernel sheaves by sheafifying the naive presheaves.

Definition B.0.24

Stalks: Germs Seen Under a Microscope

A stalk forgets the open set but remembers behavior near one point

Stalk at \(x\)

Pairs \((U,s)\), with \(x\in U\) and \(s\in\mathcal F(U)\), are identified when they agree after restricting to some smaller neighborhood of \(x\).

\(\mathcal F_x=\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal F(U)\)

Local Detection

A sheaf morphism is injective or surjective exactly when every induced stalk map \(\mathcal F_x\to\mathcal G_x\) has that property.

A stalk is the formal version of looking only near one point. The representative section and the representative neighborhood may change, as long as the two representatives coincide after passing to a smaller neighborhood of the point. This is why stalks detect sheaf exactness and surjectivity: every question has been reduced to a sufficiently small local lifting problem.

Definition B.0.25

Sheafification Repairs a Presheaf

Local germs become genuine local sections

Associated Sheaf

\(\mathcal F^+(U)\) consists of maps \(s\) assigning to each \(x\in U\) a germ \(s(x)\in\mathcal F_x\), locally represented by a section of the original presheaf.

Why It Matters

The image sheaf, cokernel sheaf, and tensor product of sheaves are often defined by first forming a natural presheaf and then sheafifying it.

Sheafification is best presented as the universal repair operation. It does not invent arbitrary functions into stalks; every germ-valued section has to be locally represented by a genuine section of the original presheaf. If the presheaf was already a sheaf, this construction changes nothing. Otherwise it adds precisely the missing gluing behavior.

Definition B.0.27 and Corollary B.0.28

Exactness Is Stalkwise, But Global Sections Are Fragile

This is the doorway to cohomology

Exact Complex of Sheaves

A complex \(\cdots\to\mathcal F^i\to\mathcal F^{i+1}\to\cdots\) is exact if \(\ker\varphi^{i+1}=\operatorname{im}\varphi^i\) as sheaves.

Stalk Criterion

Exactness can be checked at every point:

\(\cdots\to\mathcal F^0_x\to\mathcal F^1_x\to\mathcal F^2_x\to\cdots\)

Global Sections Need Not Stay Exact

A short exact sequence of sheaves need not give a short exact sequence after evaluating on \(M\).

Exactness for sheaves is local in the strongest possible way: after passing to stalks, exactness can be checked point by point. The surprise is that taking global sections does not preserve the right end of a short exact sequence. Cohomology is introduced precisely to measure this failure of global lifting, not as an arbitrary new invariant.

Definitions B.0.29-B.0.30

Flasque Resolutions Supply Cohomology-Free Objects

Use the standard spelling flasque

Resolution

A resolution of \(\mathcal F\) is an exact sequence \(0\to\mathcal F\to I^0\to I^1\to\cdots\).

Flasque Sheaf

\(\mathcal I\) is flasque if each restriction \(\mathcal I(M)\to\mathcal I(U)\) is surjective.

Reason for the Choice

Flasque sheaves are acyclic and make global sections exact enough to define cohomology for arbitrary sheaves.

This slide corrects the common OCR noise in the source span: the intended term is flasque. The intuition is that a section over a smaller open set can be extended all the way to the whole space. The source uses flasque sheaves as the chosen acyclic class, then states that every sheaf admits a flasque resolution, so the definition applies generally.

Definition B.0.33 and Proposition B.0.34

Defining \(H^i(M,\mathcal F)\)

Resolve first, then take global sections

Definition

Choose a flasque resolution \(\mathcal F\to I^\bullet\). The cohomology of \(\mathcal F\) is the cohomology of the complex of global sections.

\(H^i(M,\mathcal F)=H^i\big(I^\bullet(M)\big)\)

Immediate Checks
  • \(H^0(M,\mathcal F)=\mathcal F(M)\).
  • If \(\mathcal F\) is flasque, then \(H^i(M,\mathcal F)=0\) for \(i>0\).
Independence

Different flasque resolutions produce naturally isomorphic cohomology groups.

The definition is deliberately procedural. Replace the sheaf by a flasque resolution, apply global sections to get an honest complex of groups or vector spaces, and take ordinary cohomology. Point out the two calibration facts: degree zero recovers global sections, and a flasque sheaf has no higher cohomology. Independence of the chosen resolution is what makes the procedure an invariant.

Proposition B.0.35

Short Exact Sequences Produce Long Exact Sequences

The connecting map names the obstruction

Global Sections Are Left Exact

The map \(H^0(F^1)\to H^0(F^2)\) may miss global targets.

Cohomology Completes the Story

The missed target maps by \(\delta\) into \(H^1(F^0)\), and exactness continues in all degrees.

Present the long exact sequence as the central payoff. A short exact sequence of sheaves may lose surjectivity on global sections, but the connecting homomorphism records exactly where the obstruction lives. For later complex geometry, this is the computational mechanism behind many extension, deformation, and classification arguments.

Definition B.0.36 and Corollary B.0.37

Euler-Poincare Characteristic

Alternating dimensions make exact sequences additive

Finite Cohomology

If the groups are finite-dimensional and only finitely many are nonzero, set \(h^i(M,\mathcal F)=\dim H^i(M,\mathcal F)\).

Characteristic

\(\chi(M,\mathcal F)=\sum_i(-1)^i h^i(M,\mathcal F)\)

Additivity

For \(0\to\mathcal F^0\to\mathcal F^1\to\mathcal F^2\to0\), finite cohomology gives \(\chi(F^1)=\chi(F^0)+\chi(F^2)\).

Euler-Poincare characteristic is a compressed numerical shadow of all cohomology groups. The alternating signs are not decoration; in a long exact sequence, dimensions cancel in adjacent degrees, which gives additivity for short exact sequences. This makes the invariant useful even when individual cohomology groups are hard to compute.

Definition B.0.38 and Proposition B.0.39

Soft and Acyclic Sheaves

Flasque resolutions are not always the computational choice

Soft

\(\mathcal F\) is soft if every section over a closed subset \(K\subset M\) extends from \(K\) to all of \(M\).

Acyclicity

Soft sheaves are acyclic. Modules over a soft sheaf of commutative rings are soft, hence acyclic.

Context Matters

Continuous and differentiable functions on manifolds are soft; holomorphic functions on complex manifolds are not soft.

After the abstract flasque construction, the appendix points to more usable acyclic sheaves. Softness is phrased with closed subsets rather than open subsets, and on manifolds it aligns with extension tools such as partitions of unity. The warning about holomorphic functions is important for complex geometry: analytic rigidity prevents the same soft argument from applying.

Alternating Differential

The usual alternating restriction sum satisfies \(d^2=0\), so the Cech groups are cohomology groups of this complex.

Refinement

Passing to refinements and then to the direct limit removes dependence on a single coarse cover.

Cech cohomology is the cover-based version of the same local-to-global idea. A zero-cochain gives local sections, a one-cochain gives overlap data, and higher cochains live on higher intersections. The alternating differential is the algebraic way of saying that overlap corrections must be compatible. Refinement is how the construction avoids depending on one particular cover.

Examples B.0.41 and Proposition B.0.43

Basic Cech Computations

The cover turns topology into a finite algebraic complex

Circle Cover

The two-arc cover of \(S^1\) has an intersection with two components, so the Cech complex detects one independent loop class.

Reasonable Spaces

For paracompact spaces, the limiting Cech groups often agree with sheaf cohomology. In degree one, the natural map is always bijective.

Projective Space Pattern

For \(\mathbb P^n\), the standard affine cover leads to cohomology in even degrees \(0,2,\ldots,2n\).

Use the circle computation as a model of how a cover remembers topology. The two open arcs are individually simple, but their intersection has two pieces, and that split creates the one-dimensional cohomology class. Then connect this to the broader statements in the source: spheres and projective spaces follow similar patterns, and degree-one Cech cohomology is especially reliable.

Corollary B.0.44, Definitions B.0.45-B.0.46, Remark B.0.47

Line Bundles, Pullbacks, Stalks, and Fibres

Transition cocycles classify bundles;
pullbacks and fibres keep functoriality honest

Line Bundles via \(H^1\)

Local trivializations give nowhere-zero transition functions \(g_{ij}\). The triple-overlap rule makes \(\{g_{ij}\}\) a Cech one-cocycle, and changing trivializations changes it by a coboundary.

Pullback Reminder

For ringed spaces, \(f^\ast\mathcal F=f^{-1}\mathcal F\otimes_{f^{-1}R_N}R_M\), and \((f^{-1}\mathcal F)_x=\mathcal F_{f(x)}\).

Stalk Versus Fibre

The stalk \(\mathcal F_x\) stores germs. The fibre \(\mathcal F(x)\) is the quotient by the maximal ideal; for a vector bundle sheaf, it is the ordinary geometric fibre.

Close by combining the two final uses of the appendix. Degree-one Cech cohomology classifies line bundles by transition functions, while inverse image and ringed-space pullback explain how sheaves move along maps. End with the stalk-versus-fibre distinction: a stalk stores all local germs near the point, while the fibre is a quotient and matches the geometric fibre for vector bundles.
