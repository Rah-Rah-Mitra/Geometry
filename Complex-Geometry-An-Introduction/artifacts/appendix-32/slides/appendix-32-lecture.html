Definition
weight n decomposition

Pure Hodge Structure of Weight \(n\)

Data

A finite-dimensional \(\mathbb{Q}\)-vector space \(V\) and a decomposition \(V_{\mathbb{C}} = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} V^{p,q}.\)

Axiom

\(\overline{V^{p,q}} = V^{q,p}\)

(complex conjugation, induced by the real structure)

Hodge numbers

\(h^{p,q} = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} V^{p,q}\). Total dimension \(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V = \sum_{p+q=n} h^{p,q}\).

Geometric source

For \(X\) compact Kahler, \(V = H^n(X,\mathbb{Q})\) carries this structure with \(V^{p,q} = H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial}(X)\), the Dolbeault cohomology.

Functoriality

Morphisms of Hodge structures respect both the rational structure and the bigrading. Pure structures form an abelian category.

Definition is short; the geometric realization is the workThe diamond just visualizes the dimension data
Restate the definition cleanly. A pure Hodge structure of weight n on a finite-dimensional rational vector space V is a direct-sum decomposition of its complexification into bigraded pieces indexed by (p,q) with p+q=n, satisfying the conjugation symmetry exchanging (p,q) with (q,p). The integer h to the (p,q) is the Hodge number.
Bookkeeping
two symmetries

The Hodge Diamond and Its Symmetries

Conjugation

\(h^{p,q} = h^{q,p}\)

(mirror across the vertical axis of the diamond)

Serre Duality

\(h^{p,q}(X) = h^{n-p, n-q}(X)\)

(for \(X\) compact Kahler of complex dimension \(n\))

Useful identity

Betti numbers split: \(b_n = \sum_{p+q=n} h^{p,q}\); the Euler characteristic alternates the rows.

Two reflections constrain the diamondIndependent data: roughly the upper-left quadrant
Draw the Hodge diamond for a compact Kahler surface. The vertical axis indexes total weight; the rows index p plus q. Symmetries h to the (p,q) equals h to the (q,p) reflect across the vertical; Serre duality reflects across the horizontal. Each lattice point is a Hodge number, and the diamond is the bookkeeping tool for cohomology dimensions.
Equivalent data
decreasing filtration

From Decomposition to Filtration

Filtration

\(F^p V_{\mathbb{C}} = \bigoplus_{r \ge p} V^{r, n-r}\)

Recovery

\(V^{p,q} = F^p V_{\mathbb{C}} \cap \overline{F^{q} V_{\mathbb{C}}}\)

Why filtrations?

In a variation of Hodge structure the filtration deforms holomorphically; the bigrading does not. Filtrations are the right invariant for families.

Filtration is the holomorphic shadow of the decompositionUsed for periods, Griffiths transversality, mixed Hodge structures
Introduce the equivalent filtration data. Define F to the p as the direct sum over r at least p of H to the (r,n-r). The filtration is decreasing, and the original decomposition is recovered by intersecting F to the p with the conjugate F to the (n-p). For variations of Hodge structure the filtration is the holomorphic invariant; the decomposition is not.
Rigidity
Hodge-Riemann relations

Polarization Makes Hodge Structures Rigid

Pairing

A bilinear form \(Q: V \otimes V \to \mathbb{Q}\), symmetric for even \(n\), antisymmetric for odd \(n\).

First Relation

\(Q(F^p, F^{n-p+1}) = 0\)

Second Relation

\(i^{p-q} Q(v, \bar v) > 0\)

for \(0 \ne v \in V^{p,q}_{\mathrm{prim}}\)

Geometric source

For a polarized Kahler manifold, \(Q\) comes from cup product with the Kahler class to a power that lands in top degree.

Why rigidity

Hodge-Riemann positivity makes period domains into open subsets of flag varieties with finite isotropy — the moduli are well-behaved.

Consequence

Period maps for variations of polarized Hodge structures are highly constrained: this drives global Torelli theorems.

Polarization = first relation (orthogonality) + second relation (positivity)Without it: too much flexibility for moduli
Recall the polarization. A polarized Hodge structure of weight n carries a bilinear form Q satisfying the two Hodge-Riemann relations: orthogonality of distinct (p,q) pieces and positivity of the i-power factor on the primitive part. Polarization is what makes Hodge theory rigid; it produces positive-definite quadratic data.

Applied lab
perturb and inspect

Lab: Perturb a Hodge Diamond

Setup

Start from the K3 diamond. Increase \(h^{2,0}\) by one. Decide which axioms break.

Check conjugation

If only \(h^{2,0}\) changes, \(h^{0,2}\) must change too: conjugation forces the change to be symmetric.

Check Serre duality

For a surface this requires \(h^{2,0}=h^{0,2}\), which the symmetric change preserves.

Check polarization

The Hodge-Riemann relations require positivity on primitive parts; arbitrary diamonds need not be realized by any Kahler manifold.

Outcome

The lab teaches that the diamond is constrained, and that not every diamond is realizable as a polarized Hodge structure. Realizability is the appendix's structural punchline.
