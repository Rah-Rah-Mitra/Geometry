Complex Manifolds and Vector Bundles

Printed pages 51-112; PDF pages 62-123. The chapter builds the global language: charts, examples, bundles, divisors, projective models, blow-ups, and Dolbeault calculus.

Start with the chapter's architecture, using the introduction on printed pages 51-52 as orientation. Holomorphic Atlases Turn Real Dimension \(2n\) Into Complex Dimension \(n\)

Definition Pattern

A complex manifold is a Hausdorff, second countable real manifold with coordinate maps to \(\mathbb C^n\) whose transition functions are holomorphic.

\[ \dim_{\mathbb R} X = 2n,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C} X = n \]

Section 2.1 makes holomorphicity a compatibility condition, not an extra function attached afterward.

Use the overlap picture to slow down Definition 2.1 from Section 2.1, printed pages 52-65. The underlying space has the size of a real manifold of dimension 2n, but the admissible coordinates are complex n-tuples. The content is in the transition maps: once z_j composed with z_i inverse is holomorphic on every overlap, holomorphic functions, tangent vectors, and later forms can be tested locally and glued consistently.

Compact Rigidity: Holomorphic Functions Freeze, Meromorphic Functions Measure

Maximum Principle On Compact \(X\)

If \(X\) is compact and connected, every holomorphic map \(f:X\to\mathbb C\) is constant.

\[ \mathcal O(X)=\mathbb C \]

There are no global complex-valued coordinates on a compact connected complex manifold.

Meromorphic Field

Meromorphic functions may have poles, so they carry global geometry instead of being forced constant.

\[ a(X)=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C(X)\le \dim_{\mathbb C}X \]

The algebraic dimension records how many independent meromorphic coordinates exist.

After the definition, emphasize the first global shock in Section 2.1: compact connected complex manifolds have no nonconstant holomorphic functions to the affine line. This is just the maximum principle seen globally, but it explains why the chapter immediately cares about meromorphic functions. The algebraic dimension is introduced as a measurement of the meromorphic function field, and it separates compact complex manifolds that look algebraic from those with too few meromorphic coordinates.

Hypersurfaces and Grassmannians live inside projective geometry. Complex tori and quotients show that compact complex manifolds need not be projective.

Submanifolds And Subvarieties: Coordinates, Ideals, Singular Warnings

Ideal-Sheaf Sequence

\[ 0 \to \mathcal I_Y \to \mathcal O_X \to \mathcal O_Y \to 0 \]

A submanifold has local coordinates in which \(Y\) is a coordinate plane.

Warning

Subvarieties are cut out by holomorphic equations, but the local zero set may fail to be a manifold at singular points.

When Section 2.1 discusses submanifolds and analytic subvarieties, separate the smooth local coordinate theorem from the broader equation-cut-out viewpoint. A complex submanifold can be made into a coordinate plane after choosing suitable holomorphic coordinates. An analytic subvariety is more flexible and is tracked by an ideal sheaf, hence the exact sequence displayed here, but it may have singular points where the coordinate-plane picture breaks.

Holomorphic Vector Bundles Are Cocycles With Fiber Coordinates

Bundle Definition

A rank \(r\) holomorphic vector bundle \(E\to X\) is locally \(U_i\times\mathbb C^r\), with holomorphic transition matrices.

\[ g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki}=I,\qquad g_{ii}=I \]

The cocycle condition is exactly the consistency of changing fiber coordinates three ways.

Section 2.2, printed pages 66-76, begins with the local trivialization definition. Make students say aloud what is local and what is global: locally the bundle is just a product with C to the r, but on overlaps the fiber coordinates are transformed by holomorphic GL(r,C)-valued functions. The displayed cocycle relation is the whole gluing law. It is also the template for line bundles, tangent bundles, normal bundles, and later sheaf cohomology descriptions.

Bundle Operations Are Matrix Operations Done Holomorphically

Sum\(E\oplus F\) uses block diagonal cocycles.
Tensor\(E\otimes F\) multiplies fiber changes.
Dual\(E^\vee\) uses inverse transpose matrices.
Determinant\(\det E=\bigwedge^r E\) is a line bundle.
Exterior\(\bigwedge^kE\) tracks alternating tensors.
Symmetric\(S^kE\) tracks homogeneous fiber polynomials.
Pullback\(f^\ast E\) composes cocycles with \(f\).
SectionsHolomorphic maps into fibers transform compatibly.
The operations in Section 2.2 are not separate mysteries; they are functorial operations on the transition matrices. If the original changes of frame are holomorphic, then block sums, tensor products, exterior powers, symmetric powers, duals, determinants, and pullbacks are holomorphic as well. This viewpoint is efficient because the same local calculation will later give the tangent bundle, canonical bundle, normal bundle, and line bundles associated with divisors.

Tautological Bundles Connect Projective Geometry To \(c_1\)

Picard Group

\(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\) records holomorphic line bundles up to isomorphism, with tensor product as addition.

Exponential Sequence

\[ 0\to\mathbb Z\to\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_X^\ast\to0 \]

The connecting map sends a line bundle to its first Chern class \(c_1\).

Use projective space to make line bundles concrete before discussing the Picard group. The tautological bundle O(-1) over projective space has fiber equal to the actual line represented by the point [v], and O(1) is its dual. Section 2.2 introduces Pic(X), the exponential sequence, and the first Chern class as a bridge from holomorphic transition functions to topological cohomology classes. The point is not to compute every Picard group yet, but to see line bundles as measurable objects.

Tangent, Normal, Canonical: Three Bundles From One Chart Calculation

Jacobian Cocycles

For charts \(z_i,z_j\), the tangent bundle transitions by the holomorphic Jacobian matrix.

\[ \frac{\partial z_j}{\partial z_i} \]

Normal Bundle

\(N_{Y/X}=T_X|_Y/T_Y\) measures directions lost when restricting to \(Y\).

Adjunction

The canonical bundle of a smooth submanifold is related to \(K_X\) and \(\det N_{Y/X}\).

Kodaira Dimension

Pluricanonical sections measure the growth of \(K_X^{\otimes m}\).

The tangent bundle is the first nontrivial vector bundle that every complex manifold owns. Its transition matrices are Jacobians of holomorphic coordinate changes, so the bundle is holomorphic. From there, Section 2.2 introduces the normal bundle of a submanifold, the canonical bundle of holomorphic top forms, adjunction, and the Kodaira dimension. Present these as one family: tangent data, quotient normal directions, and determinant duals of tangent data.

Divisor Ledger: Orders Of Zeros And Poles Become Line Bundles

Divisor Accounting

An effective divisor has nonnegative coefficients. A principal divisor is the divisor of a meromorphic function.

\[ \operatorname{Div}(X)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}(X),\qquad D\mapsto\mathcal O(D) \]

Principal divisors map to the trivial line bundle, matching the idea that a global meromorphic function is a change of trivialization.

Section 2.3, printed pages 77-90, turns meromorphic behavior into bookkeeping. A divisor records codimension-one zero and pole orders, with effective divisors having only nonnegative entries and principal divisors coming from a meromorphic function. The map from Div(X) to Pic(X) sends D to O(D). Explain that the line bundle remembers how local equations for the divisor change from chart to chart, while principal divisors disappear because a global meromorphic function supplies the trivialization.

Sections To Projective Maps: No Common Zero, Then A Map

Basepoint-Free Linear System

Sections \(s_0,\ldots,s_N\in H^0(X,L)\) define a holomorphic map when they never vanish all at once.

\[ x\longmapsto [s_0(x):\cdots:s_N(x)]\in\mathbb P^N \]

Ample line bundles provide enough sections to embed after taking a positive power.

sectionzero divisorbase locusampleness
Continue Section 2.3 by linking divisors to maps. A nonzero section of a line bundle has a zero divisor, and a collection of sections gives projective coordinates whenever there is no base point. The formula is familiar, but the point is subtle: the values s_i(x) live in the same one-dimensional fiber L_x, so their ratios are well-defined after choosing any local trivialization. This prepares the projective-space computations and the definition of ampleness.

Hypersurface Adjunction Turns Degree Into Canonical Class

Adjunction Formula

For a smooth degree \(d\) hypersurface \(X\subset\mathbb P^n\), the normal bundle is \(\mathcal O_X(d)\).

\[ K_X = (K_{\mathbb P^n}\otimes \mathcal O(d))|_X = \mathcal O_X(d-n-1) \]

The sign of \(d-n-1\) foreshadows Fano, Calabi-Yau, and general-type behavior.

Now put the projective formulas to work. A smooth hypersurface defined by a homogeneous polynomial of degree d has normal bundle O_X(d), because the defining equation changes as a section of O(d). Combining this with K of projective space and adjunction gives K_X equal to O_X(d-n-1). This one line explains why quadrics, cubics, quintics, and higher-degree hypersurfaces land in very different birational worlds.

Blow-Ups Replace A Center By Its Normal Directions

Local Model

The blow-up \(\widetilde X\to X\) is an isomorphism away from \(Y\), and over \(Y\) inserts the projectivized normal directions.

\[ E \simeq \mathbb P(N_{Y/X}) \]

Canonical Shift

For a smooth center of codimension \(r\), the canonical bundle changes by a multiple of the exceptional divisor.

Section 2.5, printed pages 98-103, introduces blow-ups as a controlled surgery. Away from the center Y nothing changes. Over Y, the space remembers not just the point but the limiting normal direction, so the exceptional divisor is the projectivized normal bundle P(N_{Y/X}). Mention the canonical shift for a smooth center of codimension r, because it signals that blow-ups change birational geometry while leaving the complement of the center untouched.

Almost Complex To Dolbeault: Type Decomposition Becomes Cohomology

Linear Splitting

An almost complex structure satisfies \(I^2=-\operatorname{id}\), so after complexifying the tangent bundle splits.

\[ T_\mathbb C X = T^{1,0}X \oplus T^{0,1}X \]

Integrability is the condition that this almost complex structure comes from holomorphic charts.

Newlander-Nirenberg

Integrability is equivalent to the existence of holomorphic coordinates.

\((p,q)\)-Forms

Forms split by how many \(dz\) and \(d\bar z\) factors they contain.

Dolbeault Cohomology

\(H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial}(X)\) measures \(\bar\partial\)-closed forms modulo \(\bar\partial\)-exact ones.

Section 2.6, printed pages 104-112, brings the chapter back to differential calculus. An almost complex structure is a real endomorphism squaring to minus the identity, so the complexified tangent bundle has plus i and minus i eigenspaces. Integrability, by Newlander-Nirenberg, is the condition that these data come from holomorphic charts. Once integrable, differential forms split into (p,q)-types, d decomposes into partial plus dbar, and Dolbeault cohomology records the dbar complex.

Synthesis Exercise: Three Languages, One Compatibility Test

Divisors -> Line Bundles -> Maps

Given an effective divisor \(D\), identify \(\mathcal O(D)\), its canonical section, and when a set of sections defines a map to projective space.

Charts -> Integrable \(I\)

Explain how holomorphic transition functions induce \(T^{1,0}\oplus T^{0,1}\), and why integrability recovers charts.

Blow-Up -> Exceptional Divisor

For a smooth center \(Y\subset X\), describe the exceptional divisor and what happens on \(X\setminus Y\).

Close by making students connect the chapter's three languages without adding new definitions. First, divisors give line bundles, line bundles have sections, and basepoint-free sections produce projective maps. Second, holomorphic charts give an integrable almost complex structure and hence the Dolbeault splitting; Newlander-Nirenberg runs the logic back. Third, blow-ups replace a smooth center by an exceptional divisor of normal directions while preserving the complement. These are the tools the next chapter will use on Kahler manifolds.
