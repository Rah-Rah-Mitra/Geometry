Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch

The HRR Ledger: Integrate Only the Top Degree

Index Formula
$$\chi(X,E)=\int_X \operatorname{ch}(E)\operatorname{td}(X)$$

The integral means: multiply the formal characteristic-class series, keep the component in degree \(2n\), and evaluate it on the fundamental class of \(X\).

Present HRR as bookkeeping with geometric content, not as a black-box miracle. The left side is an alternating cohomology count for a holomorphic vector bundle or coherent sheaf in the appropriate version; the right side is built from Chern data. Students should hear that lower-degree terms matter only through products that land in top degree. This is why the formula is effective in examples: the ledger discards everything except the degree matching the complex dimension.
Examples

Curves, Surfaces, and Tori as Test Cases

Curves

\(\chi(L)=\deg L+1-g\)

Riemann-Roch for a line bundle is HRR after the top-degree extraction has only one Chern-class slot.

Surfaces

\(\chi(L)=\frac12 L\cdot(L-K_X)+\chi(\mathcal O_X)\)

Intersection arithmetic enters because degree-four components are products of divisor classes.

Complex Tori

\(\operatorname{td}(T)=1\)

Trivial tangent bundle collapses the Todd side, so the Chern character carries the count.

Move quickly through the examples as calibration points. On a curve, HRR reproduces the familiar Riemann-Roch expression, so students can recognize the new theorem inside an older formula. On a surface, the same mechanism becomes intersection theory because top degree is now degree four. For a complex torus, the tangent bundle is holomorphically trivial, so the Todd factor is trivial and the computation emphasizes how special the torus case is.

Additivity

Exact Sequences Make Euler Characteristics Behave Like Ledgers

Coherent Sheaf Additivity
$$0\to \mathcal F'\to\mathcal F\to\mathcal F''\to0$$

Long exact cohomology gives \(\chi(\mathcal F)=\chi(\mathcal F')+\chi(\mathcal F'')\).

Hypersurface Sequence
$$0\to \mathcal O_X(-Y)\to\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_Y\to0$$
Use the exact sequence slide to connect formal algebra with geometric examples. The key reason Euler characteristics are useful is that they are additive even when individual cohomology dimensions are not. The hypersurface sequence is the geometric model: the structure sheaf of a divisor is measured by subtracting a twisted ideal sheaf from the ambient structure sheaf. This supports later arguments where vanishing turns these ledger identities into actual section counts.
Positive Line Bundles

Curvature Is the Analytic Form of Positivity

Positive Line Bundle

A Hermitian metric on \(L\) gives curvature \(\Theta_L\). Positivity means the associated real \((1,1)\)-form is positive on complex tangent lines.

ample intuitionpositive curvaturemany sections

This is the analytic entrance to the second part of the chapter. A holomorphic line bundle becomes positive when a Hermitian metric has positive curvature in the appropriate (1,1)-sense. Stress the change of scale: positivity is a pointwise tangent-space condition, but the consequences are global. Kodaira Vanishing

Vanishing Region: Positivity Erases Higher Cohomology

Kodaira Vanishing
$$H^q(X,K_X\otimes L)=0\quad(q>0)$$

For \(L\) positive on compact Kahler \(X\), HRR for \(K_X\otimes L\) becomes a count of global sections.

The grid marks a proof scaffold, not a Hodge diamond classification.

State Kodaira vanishing in the form the chapter needs: positive L kills the higher cohomology of K_X tensor L. Then point back to HRR. Once the higher terms vanish, the Euler characteristic is no longer an alternating shadow; it is the actual dimension of H0. This is the decisive bridge from curvature to the existence of holomorphic sections. The (p,q) grid is only a visual scaffold for where the Bochner argument acts.
Bochner Mechanism

Nakano Identity Supplies the Energy Inequality

Proof Dependency

Kahler commutator identities convert curvature positivity into a lower bound for the Laplacian on bundle-valued forms.

Why It Vanishes

A harmonic representative with positive energy contribution can only satisfy the identity when the representative is zero.

Do not attempt a full analytic proof on this slide. Instead, identify the dependencies that make the proof work. The Kahler identities relate the Dolbeault Laplacian to curvature terms, and positivity gives the curvature term a sign. For a harmonic representative, the identity says a sum of nonnegative quantities is zero. The conclusion is that the representative vanishes, and Hodge theory then translates that analytic statement into cohomology vanishing.
Projective Space Example

Vanishing on \(\mathbf P^n\) Is the Model Calculation

Line Bundle Pattern

For \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^n}(k)\), cohomology is concentrated in predictable degrees. Positive twists have many sections and no higher cohomology.

  • Global sections are homogeneous polynomials of degree \(k\).
  • Higher cohomology disappears in the positive range.
  • Negative twists reappear by duality at the opposite end.
Use projective space as the example students can calculate with their hands. Positive twists of the hyperplane bundle have sections represented by homogeneous polynomials, and the higher cohomology vanishes in the expected range. The slide also warns that vanishing is not simply a statement that all twists are easy; negative twists reappear at the top by duality. This example anchors the later projective embedding discussion in a familiar coordinate model.
Weak Lefschetz

A Hyperplane Section Preserves Cohomology in a Range

Range Statement

For a smooth ample hypersurface \(Y\subset X\), restriction maps preserve cohomological information below the middle range and inject at the boundary.

The theorem says an ample divisor is not merely a subset; it is a reliable cohomological probe of \(X\).

Treat Weak Lefschetz as another payoff of positivity and vanishing, not as a disconnected theorem. A smooth ample hypersurface is cohomologically close to the ambient manifold in a precise range: below the middle dimensions the restriction map preserves information, and at the boundary it remains injective. The range matters because it prevents overstatement. The pedagogical message is that ample divisors can test global topology while being lower-dimensional and often easier to understand.
Serre to Grothendieck

From Serre Vanishing to the Lemma on \(\mathbf P^1\)

Serre Vanishing

For an ample line bundle, sufficiently high twists kill higher cohomology for any coherent sheaf.

Grothendieck on \(\mathbf P^1\)

Vanishing and generation arguments help split vector bundles into sums of line bundles.

Make this slide feel like a technique transfer. Serre vanishing says that after enough positive twisting, coherent sheaves behave as if higher cohomology is no longer obstructing global generation. On P1, this control is strong enough to feed the Grothendieck splitting lemma for vector bundles. The point is not to reprove the full splitting theorem, but to show how positivity, vanishing, and generation keep reappearing as a single method.
Linear Systems

Embedding Requires Three Separation Tests

Basepoints

For every \(x\), some section satisfies \(s(x)\ne0\). The map is defined everywhere.

Points

For \(x\ne y\), some section distinguishes their values. The map is injective.

Tangents

First-order jets separate tangent directions. The map is an immersion.

This is the practical criterion behind Kodaira embedding. First, basepoint freeness ensures the projective map is actually defined at every point. Second, point separation ensures two different points are not collapsed to the same projective coordinate vector. Third, tangent separation ensures the differential is injective, so the map is an immersion. Students should see that an embedding theorem is not magic; it is a checklist about sections and first-order data.
Blow-Up Bridge

Exceptional Divisors Turn Separation into Positivity Tests

Bridge Idea

To separate points or tangent directions, blow up the relevant locus and twist by the exceptional divisor. Vanishing on the blow-up gives surjectivity of restriction maps.

Exceptional geometry packages a local condition as a global line-bundle positivity question.

Explain why blow-ups appear in a proof about embeddings. Separating a point, a pair of points, or a tangent direction is a local condition, but Kodaira vanishing is a global theorem about line bundles. The blow-up converts the local condition into a statement involving an exceptional divisor. If the adjusted line bundle remains positive enough, vanishing gives the needed surjectivity of sections. This is the bridge from analytic positivity to the linear-system checklist.
Kodaira Embedding

Proof Flow: Positive Line Bundle Gives a Projective Embedding

State the theorem in the positive-line-bundle form: if a compact complex manifold has a positive holomorphic line bundle, then a high tensor power has enough holomorphic sections to define a projective embedding. The proof flow is the chapter's synthesis. Positivity feeds Kodaira-type vanishing; vanishing gives surjectivity to local data; surjectivity gives basepoint, point, and tangent separation; those three tests turn the section map into an embedding.
Tori and Projectivity

Kahler Cones, Integral Lattices, and Riemann Forms

Torus Criterion

A complex torus is projective precisely when it carries the right integral positive \((1,1)\)-class, equivalently a Riemann form compatible with the lattice.

The diagram shows the Kahler cone as a positive region and the lattice as arithmetic constraints inside it.

Use the torus criterion to separate Kahler geometry from algebraic geometry. Complex tori are naturally Kahler in many cases, but projectivity requires an integral positive class, expressed classically as a Riemann form on the lattice. The cone in the diagram represents positivity, while the lattice dots represent arithmetic integrality. Projectivity happens where the two conditions meet. This fits the chapter's larger theme: analytic positivity needs the right cohomological and integral packaging to become algebraic.
