The Lefschetz Operator and Hard Lefschetz

Operator

\(L : H^k(X) \to H^{k+2}(X), \quad L(\alpha) = \omega \smile \alpha\)

where \(\omega\) is the Kahler class.

Hard Lefschetz

\(L^{n-k}: H^{n-k}(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^{n+k}(X)\)

for \(0 \le k \le n\), \(\dim_{\mathbb{C}} X = n\).

Consequence

Betti numbers satisfy \(b_{n-k} = b_{n+k}\). The Hodge diamond is symmetric across its middle row.

L raises degree by two; powers reach symmetric levelsTopology meets Kahler geometry
Define the Lefschetz operator L as cup product with the Kahler class. On compact Kahler manifolds of complex dimension n, L raises degree by two. The hard Lefschetz theorem says that iterated L from degree n minus k to degree n plus k is an isomorphism. This is the structural rigidity that produces the diamond's reflection.
Primitive cohomology
kernel of next L power

Primitive Cohomology and Lefschetz Decomposition

Primitive part

\(P^k(X) = \ker\bigl(L^{n-k+1}: H^k \to H^{2n-k+2}\bigr)\)

Lefschetz Decomposition

\(H^k(X) = \bigoplus_{r \ge 0} L^r P^{k-2r}(X)\)

Why primitives

Polarization is positive (up to fixed signs) on primitive pieces. They are the basic blocks for Hodge-Riemann analysis.

Primitives are the building blocksL^r translates them up the cohomology ladder
Introduce primitive cohomology as the kernel of one more application of L beyond the top. Define P to the k equal to kernel of L to the (n-k+1) on H to the k. Cohomology decomposes as a sum of L-translates of primitive parts. The polarization is positive on each primitive piece up to a known sign.
Working form
PHS

Polarized Hodge Structures (PHS)

Data

A pure Hodge structure \((V, F^\bullet)\) of weight \(n\) plus a polarization form \(Q\) (symmetric for \(n\) even, alternating for \(n\) odd).

Hodge-Riemann I

\(Q(F^p, F^{n-p+1}) = 0\)

Hodge-Riemann II

\(i^{p-q} Q(v, \bar v) > 0,\quad 0 \ne v \in V^{p,q}_{\mathrm{prim}}\)

Geometric origin

On compact Kahler \(X\) of dimension \(n\), the form \(Q(\alpha,\beta) = \int_X \omega^{n-k} \wedge \alpha \wedge \beta\) on \(H^k(X)\) polarizes the Hodge structure (after sign corrections).

Rigidity payoff

Polarized Hodge structures form a category with finite-dimensional automorphism groups; moduli problems are well-posed.

PHS = HS + form satisfying HR relationsPositivity is concentrated on primitive pieces
Restate polarization in the working form. A polarized Hodge structure is a pure Hodge structure together with a bilinear form Q whose Hodge-Riemann relations are satisfied. On primitive cohomology Q is positive definite after multiplying by the standard i-to-the-power factor. The polarization makes period domains tractable.
Period domains

Period Domains: Where PHS Live

Definition

Fix Hodge numbers \(h^{p,q}\) and polarization type. \(D\) = all polarized Hodge structures with these data.

Structure

\(D\) is an open subset of a flag variety \(\check D\) (the compact dual) cut out by the Hodge-Riemann second-relation inequality.

Symmetry

Real Lie group \(G_{\mathbb{R}}\) (the isometry group of \(Q\)) acts transitively on \(D\); \(D = G_{\mathbb{R}} / V\) with \(V\) compact isotropy.

D is an open homogeneous spacePeriods of a family land here
Describe period domains. Fix the Hodge numbers and the polarization type. The period domain D is the space of all polarized Hodge structures with that combinatorial data. It sits as an open subset of a flag variety, and the map from a family of varieties to D records how the Hodge filtration moves with the parameter.
Constraint
horizontal motion

Griffiths Transversality

Period Map

For a family \(\pi: \mathcal{X} \to S\), each fiber \(X_s\) has a PHS; the period map \(P: S \to D / \Gamma\) records its filtration.

Transversality

\(dP(T_s S) \subset \bigoplus_p \mathrm{Hom}\bigl(F^p / F^{p+1},\; F^{p-1}/F^p\bigr)\)

Reading

The derivative of \(P\) shifts the Hodge filtration by at most one step. The period map factors through a horizontal distribution on \(D\).

Geometric source

Kodaira-Spencer + the Gauss-Manin connection produce the shift-by-one bound when you differentiate the filtration along the base.

Consequence

Variations of Hodge structure are not arbitrary maps to \(D\); they are constrained, and many classical rigidity theorems exploit this.

The deepest constraint in the basic toolkitDrives Torelli and rigidity statements
State Griffiths transversality. The derivative of the period map lands in F to the (p minus 1) modulo F to the p, not in arbitrary directions. This is a horizontality condition: the period map factors through a horizontal distribution on the period domain. This is the deepest constraint in the basic toolkit.

Working Examples: Elliptic Curves and K3

Elliptic curves

Weight one, rank two, alternating polarization. Period domain is the upper half plane \(\mathbb{H}\); modular group \(SL_2(\mathbb{Z})\) acts.

The j-invariant is the period map for the universal family.

K3 surfaces

Weight two, Hodge numbers \(1,20,1\). Period domain has complex dimension 20; primitive cohomology is rank 22 (or 21 algebraic + 1 transcendental for polarized K3s).

Period map is dominated by transversality; global Torelli holds.

Two clean illustrations of the toolkit in actionBoth period domains are quotients of Lie groups
Walk through working examples. Elliptic curve periods land in the upper half plane, which is the period domain for weight-one polarized structures of rank two. K3 surface periods land in a twenty-dimensional period domain. The next layer of the theory studies variations of these maps and their global properties.

Recap: A Working Toolkit for Hodge Theory

  • Lefschetz operator \(L\). Cup with Kahler class; iterated isomorphism by Hard Lefschetz.
  • Primitive cohomology \(P^k\). Kernel of the next L-power; building blocks of \(H^*\).
  • Polarized Hodge structure. HS + form satisfying Hodge-Riemann relations.
  • Period domain \(D\). Open homogeneous subset of a flag variety.
  • Griffiths transversality. Period maps shift filtration by at most one step.

Outcome: a complete vocabulary for the variation of cohomology in families of Kahler manifolds.
