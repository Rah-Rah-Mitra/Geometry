Boundary Coordinates Local model: a half-space, not a broken manifold

Boundary Charts Remember One Missing Side

Definition Lens

A boundary chart sends a neighborhood of \(p\) to the half-space

\(H^n=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n):x_n\ge 0\}\).

Interior points have \(x_n>0\). Boundary points have \(x_n=0\), with a distinguished inward normal direction.

tangent directions normal coordinate collar \( \partial M \times [0,\epsilon) \)
Stress that a boundary point is not a singularity. It has a smooth chart, but the local model is a half-space rather than all of Euclidean space. The inward normal coordinate gives the extra bookkeeping used later when a one-dimensional preimage meets the boundary of a parameter interval.
One-Dimensional Geometry Intervals contribute two endpoints; circles contribute none

Compact One-Manifolds Are Parity Devices

Parity Fact

If \(W\) is a compact one-manifold, then

\(\#\,\partial W \equiv 0 \pmod 2.\)

Interval component

Two boundary points: one at each end.

Circle component

No boundary points: the component closes on itself.

Every parity proof later in the chapter arranges a finite set as the boundary of a compact one-manifold.

This slide is the chapter's parity engine. A compact one-manifold is built from circle and interval components, so its boundary has an even number of points. Every later mod two invariance proof is a dressed-up use of this census.

Transversality Makes Intersections Regular

Transversality

For \(f:M\to N\) and \(Z\subset N\), write \(f\pitchfork Z\) when every \(p\in f^{-1}(Z)\) satisfies

\(df_p(T_pM)+T_{f(p)}Z=T_{f(p)}N.\)
Preimage Principle

If \(f\pitchfork Z\), then \(f^{-1}(Z)\) is a submanifold of dimension \(\dim M-\operatorname{codim} Z\).

Read the formula as a tangent-space spanning condition. At a point mapping into the target submanifold, the derivative of the map and the tangent directions of the submanifold together fill the ambient tangent space. When that happens, the inverse image is itself a clean submanifold of the expected dimension.
Local Movie Ordinary counts can jump; parity does not

Nontransverse Frame

A tangent line touches the circle with one unstable contact.

Transverse Frames

Move the line slightly: intersections appear or disappear two at a time.

ordinary count: \(0, 1, 2\)   but   \( \#\bmod 2: 0, ?, 0\)
Use the line and circle picture as the small model for nontransverse behavior. At tangency the count is unstable, but on the transverse sides intersection points are born or die in pairs. The ordinary count jumps by two, while the mod two count is unchanged.
Homotopy Invariance The comparison object is a compact one-manifold

A Homotopy Sweeps A One-Manifold

Proof Shape

Let \(F:M\times[0,1]\to N\) be transverse to \(Z\). Then \(W=F^{-1}(Z)\) is a compact one-manifold when the expected dimension is \(1\).

Boundary Accounting

The boundary of \(W\) is the endpoint data:

\(\partial W=f_0^{-1}(Z)\cup f_1^{-1}(Z)\)
Explain the homotopy proof geometrically. A one-parameter family of transverse maps defines a swept inverse image inside the product with the interval. Its boundary consists of the two endpoint intersection sets, and compact one-manifold parity says those endpoint counts agree modulo two.
Intersection Invariant Forget signs; keep homotopy-stable parity

Mod Two Intersection Number

Definition

When \(f\pitchfork Z\) and the inverse image is finite, define

\(I_2(f,Z)=\# f^{-1}(Z)\pmod 2.\)
Why It Works

A transverse homotopy compares two finite sets by the boundary of a compact one-manifold.

Present the invariant as intentionally modest. It forgets orientation and sign, but it keeps exactly the information that survives the chapter's homotopy argument. The hypotheses are not cosmetic: compactness, boundary control, and transversality are what make the finite count legal.

Boundary Control Blocks Retractions

Obstruction Template

If a proposed deformation fixes the boundary, its swept intersection set has controlled boundary. Parity cannot change from odd to even.

What To Inspect
  • Is the map transverse to the target after perturbation?
  • Is the sweep compact?
  • Can intersections escape through an uncontrolled boundary?

Winding Intersection data becomes an integer on the complement

Winding Number Counts Rotating Rays

Definition

For a closed curve \(\gamma\) and point \(a\notin \gamma\), unwrap the angle of \(\gamma(t)-a\). The total turn divided by \(2\pi\) is an integer.

Component Test

Move \(a\) without crossing the curve and the winding number stays fixed.

Winding number is the integer version of the same counting habit. Around a test point off the curve, track the changing argument of the vector from the point to the moving curve. The total angle change is constant on components of the complement and jumps only when a path crosses the curve.
Separation Codimension one turns crossing into a component change

Jordan-Brouwer As A Separation Test

Theorem Theme

An embedded \(S^{n-1}\subset S^n\) separates the ambient sphere into two regions with the embedded sphere as common boundary.

Counting Interpretation

A path crossing the hypersurface changes its side label. A path avoiding the hypersurface cannot change that label.

Frame Jordan-Brouwer as the higher-dimensional separation statement suggested by winding. An embedded codimension-one sphere has two complementary regions, and crossing the hypersurface changes the component label. The slide keeps the theorem visual while avoiding a full proof.
Antipodal Pairs The one-dimensional case is a sign-change argument

Borsuk-Ulam On The Circle

Residual

For \(f:S^1\to\mathbb R\), define

\(g(\theta)=f(\theta)-f(\theta+\pi).\)
Odd Symmetry

Then \(g(\theta+\pi)=-g(\theta)\). Continuity forces \(g(\theta)=0\) for some \(\theta\).

For the circle case, define the antipodal residual g(theta) equals f(theta) minus f(theta plus pi). The residual reverses sign under a half-turn, so continuity forces a zero. A zero is exactly an antipodal pair with the same scalar value.
Borsuk-Ulam Odd maps and parity pressure in higher dimension

Antipodal Coincidence In Higher Dimension

Theorem

Every continuous \(F:S^n\to\mathbb R^n\) has some \(x\) with \(F(x)=F(-x)\).

Parity Heuristic

The odd difference \(H(x)=F(x)-F(-x)\) cannot avoid \(0\) without creating an impossible antipodal degree pattern.

Now state the general idea without overloading the proof. If a continuous map from the n-sphere to n-space separated every antipodal pair, the odd difference map would avoid the origin. After normalization it would produce an antipodal self-map with the wrong parity behavior. That is the topological pressure behind Borsuk-Ulam.
Warnings The invariant is robust inside its hypotheses

Where The Counting Argument Breaks

Nontransverse contact

Finite intersections can merge, thicken, or fail to be regular. Perturb before counting.

\(df(T_pM)+T_yZ \ne T_yN\)

Noncompact sweep

Pairs can disappear at infinity, so the boundary of the swept one-manifold is not only endpoint data.

\(W=F^{-1}(Z)\) not compact

Boundary leakage

Intersection points can escape through an uncontrolled boundary face, changing parity by one.

\(\partial W\) has extra terms

The chapter's recurring discipline is to verify transversality, compactness, and boundary control before invoking a mod two count.

Pause on the failure modes because they are common student mistakes. A nontransverse intersection may not be finite or stable. A noncompact sweep can lose points at infinity. Boundary leakage can change the endpoint count. The mod two invariant is robust only inside the correct geometric fence.
