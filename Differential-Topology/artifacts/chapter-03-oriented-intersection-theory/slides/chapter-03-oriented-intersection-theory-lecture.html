Orientation Is A Determinant Choice

local algebra

Definition

An orientation of a real vector space chooses one of the two determinant sign classes of ordered bases.

positive if \(\det[v_1\ \cdots\ v_n] > 0\)

Convention alert

Swapping two basis vectors reverses orientation. Reordering tangent bases later changes intersection signs.

Original determinant comparator based on the chapter's orientation theme.

Emphasize that orientation is not an arrow drawn on the whole space. It is an equivalence class of ordered bases, and a chart is compatible with the orientation only when the relevant transition determinant preserves that sign.

Oriented Manifolds Need Compatible Charts

chart overlaps

Atlas Test

On an overlap \(U_i \cap U_j\), the transition map must have positive Jacobian determinant wherever both charts are used.

\(\det D(\varphi_j \circ \varphi_i^{-1})(x) > 0\)

Why this matters

The sign assigned to a crossing should not depend on which compatible chart was used to inspect it.

The deck uses orientation conventions to prepare all later signed counts.

Make the atlas condition concrete. A single chart can declare positive coordinates locally, but an oriented manifold requires these declarations to agree on overlaps. The determinant of every transition Jacobian is the local test that prevents sign contradictions.

A Transverse Crossing Has A Local Sign

oriented intersection

Local Sign

If \(X^k\) and \(Z^{n-k}\) meet transversely in \(M^n\), concatenate positive tangent bases:

\([T_pX, T_pZ]\quad\hbox{versus}\quad T_pM\)

Contribution

The point contributes \(+1\) if the concatenated basis is positive and \(-1\) if it is negative.

Original crossing diagram for complementary-dimensional oriented submanifolds.

Pause on the order of tangent spaces. At a complementary-dimensional transverse intersection, concatenate a positive basis for one submanifold with a positive basis for the other, compare it with the ambient orientation, and record plus one or minus one.

Homotopy Moves Crossings In Pairs

invariance

Generic Motion

Most times remain transverse, so each intersection point simply follows a track.

Only Event

At an isolated tangency, a pair of crossings can be born or die with opposite signs.

Invariant

Because pair contributions sum to zero, the total signed count survives the homotopy.

Original homotopy movie schematic for oriented intersection invariance.

The invariance story is a local movie. Under a generic deformation, crossings trace one-dimensional tracks, and births or deaths occur as opposite-sign pairs. That is why the signed sum is stable even though individual intersection points move.

Fixed Points Are Intersections

graph vs diagonal

Lefschetz Local Index

At a transverse fixed point, the graph-diagonal intersection contributes a sign computed from \(I-df_p\).

\(\operatorname{ind}_p(f)=\operatorname{sign}\det(I-df_p)\)

Global Count

The Lefschetz number is the algebraic sum of these local fixed-point contributions.

Fixed point theory is introduced here as an oriented intersection problem.

Translate fixed point theory into the intersection language. A fixed point of a map is exactly a point where the graph meets the diagonal, and transversality of that intersection is controlled by the invertibility of I minus the derivative.

Circle Formula

For a degree \(d\) map on \(S^1\), the Lefschetz count is modeled by

\(L(f)=1-d\)

Sign Check

Simple fixed points inherit the sign of the one-dimensional factor \(1-df\).

Poincare-Hopf Sums The Local Indices

global obstruction

Theorem

For a compact oriented manifold with isolated zeros of a vector field,

\(\sum_{p:V(p)=0}\operatorname{ind}_p(V)=\chi(M)\)

Consequence

A nonzero Euler characteristic obstructs a nowhere-zero vector field.

Poincare-Hopf is presented as a signed local-to-global theorem, not as a black box.

Frame Poincare-Hopf as the vector-field analogue of oriented intersection counting. Each zero contributes an integer index, and on a compact oriented manifold the total cannot be chosen freely; it must equal the Euler characteristic. Stress that this makes vector fields remember topology, even when the arrows are drawn locally.

Hopf Degree Is The Global Version Of Local Sign

regular value count

Degree By Fibers

For a regular value \(y\), every point \(x\in f^{-1}(y)\) receives a sign from \(df_x\).

\(\deg(f)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\operatorname{sign}\det(df_x)\)

Hopf Degree Theorem

For oriented compact manifolds of the same dimension, degree is a homotopy invariant and organizes maps into degree classes in the sphere model.

Original regular-fiber diagram for the chapter's Hopf degree theme.

Present degree as the same mechanism after replacing one crossing by a whole fiber over a regular value. A smooth map between oriented manifolds of the same dimension assigns signs to inverse images, and Hopf's theorem makes the resulting integer homotopy invariant.

Euler Characteristic Has Many Costumes

same integer

Cell Count

\(\chi=V-E+F-\cdots\), independent of the chosen triangulation or cell decomposition.

Index Count

Poincare-Hopf computes the same value by summing local vector-field indices.

Surface Checks

Sphere: \(2\). Torus: \(0\). Genus two surface: \(-2\). The vector field must match the topology.

Euler characteristic values are used as sanity checks for the chapter's index computations.

Connect the chapter's differential and combinatorial languages. Euler characteristic can be computed from cells, predicted by a vector field through Poincare-Hopf, and constrained by degree and fixed point information in related settings. The point is that different calculations are checking the same integer.
