Alternation, orientation, determinant

Exterior Algebra Is A Sign Machine

Top-form rule If \(A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\), then $$A^*(dx\wedge dy)=\det(A)\,du\wedge dv.$$
Slow down on the wedge table because many later mistakes are sign mistakes in disguise. Repeated covectors vanish because a degenerate parallelogram has no oriented area, while swapping the order reverses orientation. The determinant formula is the local ancestor of change of variables.
Differential Forms
Smoothly varying measurements

Forms Attach Algebra To Each Tangent Space

Definition lens A \(k\)-form assigns each \(p\in M\) an alternating \(k\)-linear map on \(T_pM\), smoothly in \(p\).
Coordinate expression $$\omega=\sum_I a_I(x)\,dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}.$$

A form is the object being integrated; coordinates are only a temporary language for evaluating it.

Present a differential form as a smoothly varying measuring device rather than as a decorated expression. At each point it eats tangent vectors and returns signed volume, and the smoothness condition ensures those measurements change coherently across charts and overlap regions.
Pullback
Maps forward, forms backward

Pullback Moves Forms Backward

Definition $$ (f^*\omega)_p(v_1,\ldots,v_k)=\omega_{f(p)}(df_pv_1,\ldots,df_pv_k). $$
Two-form change If \(F(u,v)=(x(u,v),y(u,v))\), then $$F^*(g\,dx\wedge dy)=g(F)\det(DF)\,du\wedge dv.$$

Pullback is the algebraic reason a source-domain computation can equal a target-domain integral.

Stress the contravariant direction. A map sends points and tangent vectors forward, but it lets forms be evaluated on the source by feeding forward the tangent vectors first. This makes integrals computable in source coordinates and packages the Jacobian determinant without separate bookkeeping.
Integration
Charts, signs, and local sums

Top Forms Are Integrands With Orientation

Local rule On an oriented \(n\)-chart, $$\omega = a(x)\,dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n,\qquad \int_U \omega=\int_{\phi(U)}a(x)\,dx.$$
Sign warning Reversing orientation changes \(\int_M\omega\) by a minus sign, even when the underlying volume is unchanged.
Integration of a top-degree form is not just area with a positive density. Orientation chooses which ordered bases count positively, so a chart with reversed orientation changes the sign. The partition sketch shows why local chart computations can be added into a global integral.
Exterior Derivative
A coordinate-free derivative for forms

The Exterior Derivative Measures Boundary-Relevant Change

Plane one-form For \(\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy\), $$d\omega=(Q_x-P_y)\,dx\wedge dy.$$
Structural identity $$d(d\eta)=0.$$ Exact forms are automatically closed.

The derivative raises degree and keeps the antisymmetric part of coefficient change.

Connect the formula for d omega to the familiar curl pattern without reducing the concept to vector calculus. The exterior derivative raises degree, is independent of the chosen chart, and satisfies d squared equals zero, which will later make exact forms automatically closed.
Stokes Theorem
Boundary integral equals interior derivative

Stokes Theorem Is The Master Conversion

Theorem For a compact oriented manifold with boundary, $$\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega.$$
Disk model With \(\omega=(-y/2)\,dx+(x/2)\,dy\), $$d\omega=dx\wedge dy,\qquad \int_{\partial D}\omega=\operatorname{area}(D).$$

The theorem simultaneously contains the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green theorem, and divergence/curl identities.

Treat Stokes theorem as a conversion rule between an interior derivative integral and a boundary integral. The disk example is deliberately simple: the rotational one-form has exterior derivative equal to area form, so the boundary circulation equals the area integral with the chosen orientation.
Orientation Diagnostics
The boundary inherits its sign

Boundary Orientation Carries The Sign

Manifold with boundary If \(M\) is oriented by outward-normal-first convention, \(\partial M\) receives the induced orientation.
  • Intervals: endpoints enter with opposite signs.
  • Regions in the plane: positive boundary is counterclockwise.
  • Chart flips: top-form integrals reverse sign.
Use this slide as a diagnostic for the most common source of wrong answers. Boundary orientation is not arbitrary decoration; it is induced from the orientation of the manifold. In dimension two the positive boundary direction keeps the region on the left as one walks along the boundary.
Closed Versus Exact
A hole detected by a period

Closed Need Not Mean Exact

Angular one-form $$\omega=\frac{-y\,dx+x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}\quad \text{on } \mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}.$$
Two facts $$d\omega=0,\qquad \int_{|z|=r}\omega=2\pi.$$

If \(\omega=df\) globally, every closed-loop integral would be zero. The nonzero period is the obstruction.

Make the punctured plane example the first genuine topological payoff. The angular form has zero exterior derivative away from the origin, but its period around a loop is nonzero. If it were globally exact, every closed-loop integral would vanish by Stokes, so the hole is detected by the form.
Form Cohomology
Closed forms modulo exact forms

Form Cohomology Records Period Obstructions

Definition $$H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)=\frac{\ker(d:\Omega^k\to\Omega^{k+1})}{\operatorname{im}(d:\Omega^{k-1}\to\Omega^k)}.$$

Closed forms define possible measurements on cycles. Exact forms define measurements that vanish on closed cycles.

Periods are the observable residues of cohomology classes.

Explain de Rham cohomology as a ledger of closed forms modulo those that are visibly derivatives. Exact forms integrate to zero on closed cycles, while closed non-exact forms can carry periods. The quotient language records which measurements survive all choices of primitive.
Degree Formula
Integration remembers signed covering count

Mappings Turn Integrals Into Degree

Degree by integration $$\int_X f^*\omega=\deg(f)\int_Y\omega.$$
Circle model For \(f_m(\theta)=m\theta\), $$f_m^*(d\theta)=m\,d\theta,\qquad \frac{\int f_m^*d\theta}{\int d\theta}=m.$$

The analytic integral returns the signed preimage count from oriented topology.

Relate this slide back to oriented intersection theory from the previous chapter. For compact oriented manifolds of the same dimension, pulling back a top form scales the total integral by the degree. On the circle, theta mapped to m theta makes the ratio visibly equal to m.
Change Of Variables
The determinant carries scale and sign

Change Of Variables Is Pullback Plus Orientation

Agreement $$\int_{F(U)} g\,dx\wedge dy=\int_U F^*(g\,dx\wedge dy).$$
Orientation reversal If \(\det(DF)<0\), the pulled-back top form records the sign.
Map behaviorIntegral sign
orientation preservingsame oriented total

Separate the analytic and geometric ingredients. The analytic expression is the determinant of the derivative inside the pulled-back top form. The geometric meaning is that the source integral and the target integral agree when orientation is respected, and reverse sign when the chart or map reverses orientation.
Gauss-Bonnet
A local curvature integral returns topology

Gauss-Bonnet Converts Curvature Into Euler Characteristic

Closed oriented surface $$\int_M K\,dA=2\pi\,\chi(M).$$
SurfaceCurvature budgetEuler signal
spherepositive everywhere, total \(4\pi\)\(\chi=2\)
toruspositive and negative regions cancel\(\chi=0\)
Present Gauss-Bonnet as the chapter finale rather than a separate theorem from another course. Curvature is a local two-form on an oriented surface, but its normalized integral returns a topological integer. The sphere and torus budget comparison makes the sign cancellation visible.
