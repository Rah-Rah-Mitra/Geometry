Gluing

The local inverse of bubbling: from a nodal configuration to a smooth family

Input

Two regular curves $u_0, u_\infty$ meeting at a node.

Output

A family $u^R$ of genuine glued curves, $R\to\infty$.

Payoff

Boundary structure of $\overline{\mathcal M}$ and the splitting axiom.

Printed pp. 369-416Gluing
Gluing is the local inverse of bubbling. Starting from two regular curves meeting at a node, this chapter builds a long-neck approximate curve, measures its Cauchy-Riemann defect in weighted norms, corrects it by a Newton argument, and uses the local model to prove the splitting axiom.

The Gluing Problem

Broken data

A nodal pair, or a broken Floer trajectory, in the limit set of $\overline{\mathcal M}$.

Goal

A one-parameter family of smooth solutions limiting onto it.

Matching node

$u_0(0) = u_\infty(\infty) = p$

Regularity

Each component is cut out transversally: $D_{u_0}, D_{u_\infty}$ surjective.

Printed pp. 369-416The Gluing Problem
The data is a broken or nodal configuration: two J-holomorphic curves sharing the node value, or a broken Floer trajectory. We want a one-parameter family of genuine smooth solutions whose limit is exactly that broken object, realising the boundary stratum of the compactified moduli space.

Five controlled steps, not one implicit-function call

1. Preglue

2. Weight

Printed pp. 369-416Proof Pipeline
The theorem is not a single implicit-function call. The Preglued Curve

Outer region

Agrees with $u_0$ for $|z|$ large.

Inner region

Agrees with the rescaled $u_\infty$ for $|z|\le 1/R$.

Neck

Flattened to the node value $p$ across the middle circle $|z|=1/R$.

Printed pp. 369-416The Preglued Curve
The preglued map agrees with the outer curve away from the node, with the rescaled inner curve near the node, and is flattened to the common intersection value across the central neck. The Cauchy-Riemann defect is concentrated only in the transition annuli where the cutoffs are active.

Weighted Norms

Why the tiny rescaled disk does not disappear

Problem

Ordinary area makes the disk of radius $1/R$ negligible.

Fix

A metric density $\theta_R$ that the neck involution preserves.

Density

$\theta_R(z)=\begin{cases}R^{-2}+R^2|z|^2,&|z|\le 1/R\\[2pt]1+|z|^2,&|z|\ge 1/R\end{cases}$

Symmetry

$z\mapsto 1/(R^2 z)$ exchanges the two sides; weighted areas match.

Printed pp. 369-416Weighted Norms
After rescaling, the inner component lives on a disk of radius one over R; ordinary area makes it negligible. The metric density theta_R restores balance so the involution exchanging the two sides of the neck makes the weighted inner area equal the weighted outer area.

Logarithmic Cutoff

Borderline estimate

The derivative is not pointwise small, but its $L^2$ energy is.

Consequence

Cutoff multiplication is a small operator on a long annulus.

Energy

$\|\nabla\beta\|_{L^2}^2 = \dfrac{2\pi}{\log(\varepsilon/\delta)}\xrightarrow[\text{long neck}]{}0$

Printed pp. 369-416Logarithmic Cutoff
The special cutoff is logarithmic on a long annulus. Its derivative is not pointwise small, but its L-squared energy equals two pi over log of epsilon over delta. This borderline two-dimensional estimate is what lets cutoff multiplication become a small operator on a long neck.

Approximate Solution and Error

Cutoff term

Weighted Sobolev size of the residual.

Cylinder term

Exponentially small as the neck lengthens.

Residual budget

$\big\|\bar\partial_{J^R} u^R\big\| \lesssim (\delta R)^{-2/p} + e^{-c\,\ell(R)}$

Reading

Longer neck $\Rightarrow$ smaller defect; the candidate nears the broken curve.

Printed pp. 369-416Approximate Solution and Error
The preglued map is not exactly holomorphic. Its residual splits into a cutoff-supported weighted Sobolev term of size delta R to the minus two over p, plus an exponentially small long-cylinder term. A longer neck makes the candidate geometrically closer to the broken curve.

Right Inverse and Newton

Approximate inverse

$\|D T\eta - \eta\|\le \tfrac12\|\eta\|$.

Exact inverse

Neumann series gives $Q = T(DT)^{-1}$, $DQ=\mathrm{id}$.

Quadratic estimate

Contraction sends residual to zero.

Newton step

$\xi_{n+1} = \xi_n - Q\,\mathcal F(\xi_n),\quad \mathcal F(\xi^\ast)=0$

Printed pp. 369-416Right Inverse and Newton
We first build an approximate inverse T with operator estimate one half. By a Neumann series D T is invertible, so Q equals T composed with D T inverse is a true right inverse. Newton iteration with quadratic estimate then contracts the residual to zero, producing the glued curve.

The Gluing Map and Its Derivative

Map

$\#: (u_0,u_\infty,R)\mapsto u^R_{\text{glued}}$.

Two effects

Pregluing derivative + Newton-correction derivative.

Derivative split

$d\# = d(\text{preglue}) + \underbrace{d(\text{correction})}_{O(\text{neck factor})}$

Outcome

Pregluing derivative governs immersion and orientation of the family.

Printed pp. 369-416The Gluing Map and Its Derivative
The gluing map sends a gluing parameter to the corrected curve. Its derivative separates into the pregluing derivative and the small Newton-correction derivative. Because the correction derivative is smaller by a neck factor, the pregluing derivative governs immersion and orientation of the glued family.

Surjectivity

Turning weak closeness into the Newton normal form

Given

A curve only $C^0$-close to the broken pair.

Upgrade to

$v=\exp_{u^R}(\xi)$ with $\xi\in\mathrm{im}\,Q$, $\|\xi\|_{W^{1,p}}$ small.

Normal slice

$v = \exp_{u^R}(\xi),\qquad \xi \in \operatorname{im} Q$

Tools

Successive approximation, compactness, long-cylinder estimates, uniqueness.

Printed pp. 369-416Surjectivity
Image of the gluing map is not automatic. A curve only C-zero close to the broken pair must be upgraded to the Newton normal form with correction in the chosen right inverse image and small weighted norm. Successive approximation plus long-cylinder estimates achieve this converse to compactness.

Splitting Axiom

The diagonal is the node

Node condition

Evaluation maps land on the diagonal $\Delta\subset M\times M$.

Split diagonal

$[\Delta]=\sum_i e_i\otimes e^i$ in a Poincare-dual basis.

Printed pp. 369-416Splitting Axiom
Near a one-node boundary stratum, gluing identifies smooth curves with pairs meeting at the node. The node condition is intersection with the diagonal in the product. Splitting the diagonal through a Poincare-dual basis converts a single Gromov-Witten count into a sum of paired counts.

Disk Coordinate for the Boundary

Four marked points

Normalized to $(1,\infty,\lambda,0)$ when $J$ is domain-independent.

Degeneration

$\lambda\to 0$ is the same as $R\to\infty$.

Gluing parameter

$\lambda = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}}{R^2}\;\in\;\mathbb C,\qquad \lambda\to 0$

Printed pp. 369-416Disk Coordinate for the Boundary
With domain-independent J, the four-marked one-node stratum has a two-real-dimensional gluing parameter. After normalization the smooth curve carries lambda equal to exp i theta over R squared. Thus lambda tending to zero is the same degeneration as R tending to infinity, giving a boundary chart.

Takeaways

Construction

  • Pregluing is a controlled connected sum with localized defect.
  • The weighted metric makes the tiny disk visible to Sobolev estimates.

Estimates

  • Logarithmic cutoff hits the borderline $L^2$ bound.
  • Neumann series upgrades $T$ to an exact right inverse.

Solution

  • Newton correction produces the honest $J^R$-holomorphic curve.
  • Surjectivity is the analytic converse of compactness.

Payoff

  • The gluing map gives manifold-with-boundary structure and orientations.
  • Splitting axiom turns the boundary into paired counts.
Printed pp. 369-416Takeaways
Pregluing is a controlled connected sum with localized defect; the weighted metric makes the tiny disk visible; the logarithmic cutoff hits the borderline estimate; Neumann plus Newton produce the exact curve; surjectivity is the analytic converse of compactness; and splitting is the counting payoff.
