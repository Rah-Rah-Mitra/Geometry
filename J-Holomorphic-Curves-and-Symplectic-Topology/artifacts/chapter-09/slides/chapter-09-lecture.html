Monotonicity and Area

Energy identity

Symplectic area equals harmonic energy, hence is nonnegative.

Minimal area

A curve through a point cannot be arbitrarily small.

Energy = area

$E(u)=\tfrac12\!\int|du|^2 = \int u^*\omega \ge 0$

Monotonicity

$\operatorname{Area}\big(u\cap B_r(p)\big)\ \ge\ \pi r^2$

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Monotonicity and Area
The energy identity makes symplectic area equal the harmonic energy of a J-holomorphic curve, so area is automatically nonnegative and vanishes only for constants. The monotonicity lemma then forces a minimal area for any curve passing through a point, the quantitative seed of nonsqueezing and most capacity bounds.

Gromov Nonsqueezing

Theorem

$B^{2n}(R)\hookrightarrow Z^{2n}(r)\ \Longrightarrow\ R\le r$

Proof idea

A J-holomorphic plane through the center has area $\ge\pi R^2$, but the cylinder bounds it by $\pi r^2$.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Gromov Nonsqueezing
Nonsqueezing says a ball of radius R embeds symplectically into the cylinder of radius r only when R does not exceed r. A symplectic embedding would carry a J-holomorphic plane through the image center; monotonicity bounds its area below by pi R squared, while the cylinder bounds it above by pi r squared.

The Capacity Picture

Capacities, not volume

$c(B_R)=\pi R^2$, $c(Z_r)=\pi r^2$; volume is blind to this.

Sharp boundary

The line $R=r$ is the exact equality of the two capacities.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383The Capacity Picture
Plotting the capacities pi R squared against pi r squared turns nonsqueezing into a sharp half-plane. The boundary line R equals r is not a grid artifact but the exact symbolic equality of the two capacities. Volume cannot detect this, since a long cylinder has unbounded volume yet a fixed symplectic width.

Action and Periodic Orbits

Action functional

$\mathcal A_H(x)=\!\int x^*\lambda-\!\int_0^1 H_t(x)\,dt$

Floer energy

A connecting trajectory carries energy equal to the action drop.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Action and Periodic Orbits
Hamiltonian periodic orbits are critical points of the symplectic action, and Floer trajectories connecting them carry energy equal to the action drop. Modeling orbits as nodes ordered by action, every connecting arrow strictly lowers action and index by one. Action bounds compactify trajectory spaces and yield existence of orbits.

Symplectic Four-Manifolds

Positivity

Distinct $J$-curves meet positively; each intersection contributes $+1$ or more.

Adjunction

Genus, self-intersection, and $c_1$ are rigidly linked.

Adjunction formula

$2g-2 = A\cdot A - c_1(A)$

Consequence

One embedded sphere of nonnegative self-intersection seeds a full family.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Symplectic Four-Manifolds
In dimension four, positivity of intersections and adjunction make J-holomorphic spheres behave almost like complex curves. A single embedded sphere of nonnegative self-intersection, combined with Gromov compactness, propagates into a whole family, the structural engine behind ruled-surface and rational-surface classification results.

Foliation by Spheres

Unique leaf

Through each point passes one $J$-sphere in the fixed class $A$.

No crossings

Positivity forbids two distinct leaves from meeting.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Foliation by Spheres
Through every point of a suitable symplectic four-manifold passes a unique J-holomorphic sphere in a fixed homology class. These spheres never cross, by positivity of intersections, so they foliate the manifold. The leaf space recovers the base, exhibiting the manifold as a sphere bundle and pinning down its symplectic form.

Classifying $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $S^2\!\times\!S^2$

Standard up to scale

The sphere foliation forces the form to be the standard one.

Uniqueness

Cohomologous symplectic forms are isotopic.

Result

$[\omega_0]=[\omega_1]\ \Longrightarrow\ \omega_0\simeq\omega_1$

Why it matters

No smooth invariant detects this rigidity; only curves do.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Classifying CP2 and S2xS2
For CP squared and the product of two spheres, the sphere foliation forces the symplectic form to be standard up to scale and diffeomorphism. Any two cohomologous symplectic forms are isotopic, so these manifolds carry an essentially unique symplectic structure, a uniqueness no smooth invariant could supply.

Symplectomorphism Groups

Gromov's theorem

$\mathrm{Symp}(S^2\!\times\!S^2,\omega)$ retracts onto an isometry group.

Method

The space of $J$-sphere configurations becomes a classifying object.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Symplectomorphism Groups
Gromov used spaces of J-holomorphic spheres to compute the homotopy type of the symplectomorphism group of the product of two spheres, showing it retracts onto an isometry group. The configuration space of curves becomes a classifying object, turning a soft topological group into something explicitly computable.

Rigidity and Hofer Geometry

Deformation invariants

Capacities distinguish forms smooth tests cannot, and obstruct ball packings.

Hofer norm

Action estimates make the norm nondegenerate.

Printed pp. 295-368 · PDF pp. 310-383Rigidity and Hofer Geometry
Curve-derived capacities are deformation invariants, so they distinguish symplectic forms that look identical to smooth or cohomological tests, and they obstruct ball packings. The same action estimates make the Hofer norm nondegenerate, giving the symplectomorphism group a genuine bi-invariant geometry detected by spectral length windows.
