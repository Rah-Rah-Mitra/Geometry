Sobolev Spaces and Weak Derivatives

Weak derivative

Integration by parts against every test function.

Completion

$W^{k,p}$ is a Banach space for the analysis of curves.

Defining identity

$\int_\Omega u\,\partial^\alpha\varphi = (-1)^{|\alpha|}\!\int_\Omega (D^\alpha u)\,\varphi$

Sobolev norm

$\|u\|_{W^{k,p}}^p = \sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\|D^\alpha u\|_{L^p}^p$

Printed pp. 549-578Sobolev Spaces and Weak Derivatives
A weak derivative is a promise that integration by parts holds against every compactly supported test function, not a new pointwise formula. The space W to the k,p collects functions whose weak derivatives through order k lie in L-p, giving us a Banach completion where the analysis of curves can safely happen.

Sobolev Embedding: Decay Buys Continuity

Subcritical

$kp<n$: gain integrability, $W^{k,p}\hookrightarrow L^{q}$.

Supercritical

$kp>n$: gain continuity, $W^{k,p}\hookrightarrow C^{0,\gamma}$.

Printed pp. 549-578Sobolev Embedding: Decay Buys Continuity
Embedding theorems trade Sobolev regularity for pointwise control. Once kp exceeds the dimension n, functions in W to the k,p are continuous, even Holder continuous. In the Fourier model the membership condition is tail decay: each extra derivative weights coefficients by frequency, so faster decay purchases more classical smoothness for free.

Elliptic Operators and Their Symbols

Laplacian

$-\Delta$ has symbol $|\xi|^2$, invertible off zero.

Cauchy-Riemann

$\bar\partial = \tfrac12(\partial_s + i\partial_t)$, first order.

Ellipticity

$\sigma(D)(\xi)\ \text{invertible for all}\ \xi\ne 0$

Consequence

Invertible symbol $\Rightarrow$ we can invert and gain derivatives.

Printed pp. 549-578Elliptic Operators and Their Symbols
An operator is elliptic when its principal symbol is invertible away from zero frequency. The Laplacian has symbol the squared length of xi, and the Cauchy-Riemann operator dbar has symbol linear and nonzero off the origin. Ellipticity is exactly the algebraic condition that lets us invert and gain derivatives.

The Fundamental Elliptic Estimate

A priori bound

$\|u\|_{W^{k+1,p}}\le C\big(\|Du\|_{W^{k,p}}+\|u\|_{L^p}\big)$

Reading it

Operator output plus a lower-order term controls all derivatives of $u$.

Printed pp. 549-578The Fundamental Elliptic Estimate
The a priori estimate is the engine of the whole appendix. It bounds the full W to the k plus one, p norm of u by the operator output Du in W to the k,p plus a harmless lower-order L-p term. Every derivative of u is controlled by what the elliptic operator produces.

Calderon-Zygmund: Hessian via the Laplacian

Second-derivative symbol

$\widehat{\partial_i\partial_j u}=\dfrac{\xi_i\xi_j}{|\xi|^2}\,\hat f,\quad -\Delta u=f$

Order zero

The multiplier is bounded, not order two.

Printed pp. 549-578Calderon-Zygmund for the Hessian
Solving minus Laplacian u equals f gives second-derivative multipliers xi-i xi-j over the squared length of xi. These are order-zero, bounded symbols, not order-two ones. For p equal to two the Hessian identity is exact; for general p the singular-integral Calderon-Zygmund theory supplies the bound that controls every second derivative.

The Calderon-Zygmund Inequality for $\bar\partial$

First-order estimate

$\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}\le C\big(\|\bar\partial u\|_{L^p}+\|u\|_{L^p}\big)$

Why it matters

Makes the curve equation elliptic and tractable.

Bogomolov-type kernel

$\bar\partial$ inverts via convolution with $\tfrac{1}{\pi z}$; differentiating gives a singular integral.

Range

Bounded on $L^p$ for $1<p<\infty$; the constant blows up at the endpoints.

Printed pp. 549-578Calderon-Zygmund for dbar
For the Cauchy-Riemann operator the same philosophy yields the basic estimate: the full W to the one,p norm of u is bounded by the L-p norm of dbar u plus a lower-order term. This first-order Calderon-Zygmund inequality is what makes the pseudoholomorphic curve equation elliptic and tractable.

Laplace Regularity: Two Free Derivatives

Localize

Cutoff so data are controlled on a small ball.

Compare

Subtract the fundamental-solution convolution.

Absorb

The remainder is harmonic; Weyl's lemma smooths it.

Inverse Laplacian in Fourier

$\hat u_k = \dfrac{\hat f_k}{|k|^2}\ \Rightarrow\ u\in W^{k+2,p}$

Net effect

The solution tail drops by $|k|^2$: two derivatives gained.

Printed pp. 549-578Laplace Regularity: Gaining Two Derivatives
Interior regularity localizes with a cutoff, compares against the fundamental-solution convolution, and absorbs the harmonic remainder via Weyl's lemma. A weak solution of a Laplace equation inherits two more derivatives than its right-hand side. In Fourier terms the inverse Laplacian divides every nonzero mode by the squared frequency.

Bootstrapping: Climbing the Sobolev Ladder

Idea

Feed the estimate its own output, one rung at a time.

Iteration

$u\in W^{k,p}\ \Rightarrow\ Du\in W^{k,p}\ \Rightarrow\ u\in W^{k+1,p}$

Printed pp. 549-578Bootstrapping: Climbing the Sobolev Ladder
Bootstrapping iterates the elliptic estimate. A weak solution in W to the one,p has data forcing one more derivative, landing it in W to the two,p; rerun and reach W to the three,p, and so on. With smooth coefficients the climb never stalls, so the solution is infinitely differentiable, hence smooth.

The Exponent Ladder for $(dJ)(du)$

The danger

If $dJ,du\in L^p$, the product can fall to $L^{p/2}$.

Holder bookkeeping

$\tfrac1r=\tfrac1p+\tfrac1q,\quad W^{1,r}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2r}{2-r}}$

Printed pp. 549-578The Exponent Ladder for the Product Term
The delicate step is the product of dJ and du. If both sit only in L-p the product can fall to L-p-over-two. Holder gives a source in L-r with one over r equal to one over p plus one over q; improving q by Sobolev embedding lifts r past two, where continuity returns the product to L-p.

Application: $\bar\partial_J$ Solutions Are Smooth

Theorem (informal)

$J\in C^\infty,\ u\in W^{1,p},\ p>2,\ \bar\partial_J u=0\ \Rightarrow\ u\in C^\infty$

Role of $p>2$

Puts two-dimensional embedding on the right side of the argument.

Printed pp. 549-578Application: J-holomorphic Curves Are Smooth
For an almost complex structure J of class C-infinity, any W to the one,p solution of the dbar-J equation with p above two is smooth. The dependency chain runs from the weak curve through coordinates, the Laplace form, Calderon-Zygmund, interior regularity, the embedding threshold, and the finite ladder to a smooth curve.

Recap: The Regularity Machine

Stage

Sobolev spaces encode weak derivatives for Banach analysis.

Tame

Calderon-Zygmund controls second derivatives via $\Delta$.

Gain

Laplace regularity adds two derivatives locally.

Iterate

Bootstrapping climbs the ladder to $C^\infty$.

Printed pp. 549-578Recap: The Regularity Machine
Four layers compose into one upgrade. Sobolev spaces stage weak derivatives, Calderon-Zygmund tames second derivatives through the Laplacian, Laplace regularity adds two derivatives locally, and bootstrapping iterates the gain. The geometric payoff is decisive: weak pseudoholomorphic curves are as smooth as the almost complex structure allows.
