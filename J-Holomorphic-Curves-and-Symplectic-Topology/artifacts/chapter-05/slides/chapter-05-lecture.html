The Stable Map

Definition

A J-holomorphic map from a nodal curve, continuous at nodes.

Energy

Total energy equals the area of the homology class.

Component maps

$u_\alpha:\, S^2 \to M, \quad \bar\partial_J u_\alpha = 0$

Node matching

$u_\alpha(z_{\alpha\beta}) = u_\beta(z_{\beta\alpha})$

Printed pp. 115-152The Stable Map
A stable map is a J-holomorphic map from a nodal curve whose components fit together continuously at nodes. The map carries energy on each component, and the total energy is the symplectic area of the homology class, conserved across every degeneration limit we study here.

The Stability Condition

Finite automorphisms

Stability means the map has finitely many symmetries.

Nonconstant

Image geometry already rigidifies the parametrization.

Constant

Needs special points to pin the Mobius group.

Stability rule

$E_\alpha = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; n_\alpha + m_\alpha \geq 3$

For each constant (ghost) component, nodes $n_\alpha$ plus marks $m_\alpha$ must be at least three.

Printed pp. 115-152The Stability Condition
Stability demands finitely many automorphisms. A nonconstant component is rigid through its image, but a ghost component is constant and only special points pin its Mobius group. The rule requires every constant component to carry at least three special points, nodes plus marks combined.

Ghost Components: Stable vs Unstable

When ghosts appear

A new mark collides with a mark or lands on a node during bubbling.

Zero energy

The component map is constant, $E_\alpha = 0$, yet structurally required.

Printed pp. 115-152Ghost Components
A ghost component carries zero energy yet is structurally necessary. It may appear when a marked point collides with another mark or lands on a node during bubbling. Stability forces it to have three special points, otherwise it would retain residual Mobius freedom and break compactness.

The Equivalence Relation

Tree isomorphism

Relabel vertices preserving incidence.

Reparametrization

One Mobius map per component.

Preserved data

Energy, nodal positions, marked values.

Equivalence

$(u_\alpha) \sim (u_\alpha \circ \phi_\alpha), \quad \phi_\alpha \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$

Quotient

Dividing by this relation removes the bounded automorphisms.

Printed pp. 115-152The Equivalence Relation
Two stable maps are equivalent when a tree isomorphism plus one Mobius reparametrization per component identifies them. Component labels may change, but incidence, energy, nodal positions, and marked values must remain compatible. Quotienting by this relation kills the infinitesimal automorphisms stability already bounded.

Gromov Convergence

Away from nodes

Maps converge smoothly on compact subsets.

At nodes

Rescalings match the two sides of each neck.

Energy axiom

No energy disappears in the limit.

Energy bookkeeping

$\lim_n E(u_n) = \sum_\alpha E(u_\alpha)$

Energy concentrating near a nodal point equals the energy carried by the separated subtree.

Printed pp. 115-152Gromov Convergence
Gromov convergence is a coordinated limit, not pointwise convergence on one sphere. Maps converge smoothly on compact sets away from nodes, rescalings match the two sides of each shrinking neck, and the energy axiom forces every bubble's energy to reappear exactly at its node.

Rescaling and Bubbling

Rescaling

Node match

Printed pp. 115-152Rescaling and Bubbling
Near a bubbling point we apply Mobius rescalings with parameter tending to zero. The parent chart sees a shrinking disc while the bubble chart magnifies it into a full sphere. The collapsing neck annulus becomes a node, and opposite source points match across the new edge.

Compactness of the Bubble Tree

Bubble extraction

Zoom at unresolved concentration points.

Energy threshold

Every bubble carries energy $\geq \hbar > 0$.

Termination

Bounded total energy bounds the number of edges.

Finite tree

$\#\{\text{vertices}\} \;\leq\; 1 + \dfrac{E_{\mathrm{tot}}}{\hbar}$

Outcome

The bubble-growth process ends with a stable tree limit.

Printed pp. 115-152Compactness
Bubble extraction grows a finite weighted tree by zooming at unresolved concentration points. It terminates because every nonconstant bubble carries energy above a positive threshold while the total energy is bounded. The resulting tree is stable, giving subsequential Gromov limits for any bounded sequence.

Uniqueness and Topology

Compare rescalings

Two systems of rescalings give the same tree.

No hidden bubble

Rule out an unaccounted intermediate component.

Rho neighborhoods

Asymmetric diagnostics, not a metric.

Convergence test

$x_n \to x \iff \rho_\epsilon(x, x_n) \to 0$

First countable

Small rho-sets form a countable neighborhood basis.

Printed pp. 115-152Uniqueness and Topology
Uniqueness compares two systems of rescalings and rules out an unaccounted intermediate bubble, recovering the tree isomorphism. Rho-style neighborhoods then translate Gromov convergence into a first-countable Hausdorff topology, so two candidate limits of one sequence must define equivalent decorated trees.

The Moduli Space is Compact and Hausdorff

Compact

Bounded sequences have convergent subsequences.

Hausdorff

Distinct stable maps have disjoint neighborhoods.

Metrizable

Countable basis yields a compact metrizable space.

Structure maps

$\mathrm{ev}_i:\, \overline{\mathcal{M}} \to M, \quad \pi:\, \overline{\mathcal{M}} \to \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}$

Continuity

Evaluation and forgetful maps survive Gromov convergence.

Printed pp. 115-152Compact and Hausdorff
The space of stable maps is compact, Hausdorff, and metrizable. Compactness supplies limits, uniqueness makes them well defined up to equivalence, and continuity of the evaluation and forgetful maps lets the moduli space support the intersection theory built in later chapters of the book.
