The Perturbed Cauchy-Riemann Equation

Inhomogeneous $\bar\partial$

Subtract a Hamiltonian-valued one-form before the anti-linear projection.

Local form

Write the perturbation as $H = F\,ds + G\,dt$ on the domain.

Perturbed operator

$\bar\partial_{J,H}(u) = \tfrac12\big(du - X_H\big) + \tfrac12\, J\,(du - X_H)\, j$

Local residual

$u_s + X_F(u) + J\big(u_t + X_G(u)\big) = 0$

Printed pp. 257-294The Perturbed Cauchy-Riemann Equation
The inhomogeneous operator subtracts a one-form valued in Hamiltonian vector fields before applying the standard anti-linear projection. Writing the perturbation locally as F ds plus G dt exposes exactly where it enters: it shifts both covariant derivatives in the section equation before the complex structure tests them.

The Floer Equation as Gradient Flow

On the cylinder

The perturbed equation on $\mathbb{R}\times S^1$ is gradient flow.

Loop space

Negative gradient of the action $\mathcal{A}_H$ on $\mathcal{L}M$.

Endpoints

Asymptotic to 1-periodic Hamiltonian orbits.

Floer equation

$\partial_s u + J(u)\big(\partial_t u - X_H(u)\big) = 0$

Gradient flow

$\partial_s u = -\nabla \mathcal{A}_H(u(s,\cdot))$

Critical points

Orbits $x$ with $\dot x = X_H(x)$, period one.

Printed pp. 257-294The Floer Equation
On the cylinder the perturbed equation is the negative gradient flow of the symplectic action functional on the free loop space. A finite-energy solution is a trajectory connecting two one-periodic Hamiltonian orbits, the critical points of the action functional, giving Floer theory its variational backbone and its grading by the Conley-Zehnder index.

Energy and the Action Functional

Action

$\mathcal{A}_H$ pairs the symplectic area against the Hamiltonian.

Energy

The Dirichlet energy of a perturbed cylinder.

Identity

Energy equals the drop in action across the trajectory.

Action functional

$\mathcal{A}_H(x) = -\!\int_{D^2}\! \bar u^*\omega - \int_0^1 H_t(x(t))\,dt$

Energy identity

$E(u) = \mathcal{A}_H(x^-) - \mathcal{A}_H(x^+)$

Printed pp. 257-294Energy and the Action Functional
The geometric energy of a perturbed cylinder equals the difference of action values at its two asymptotic orbits. This identity makes energy a topological quantity along gradient trajectories and is the source of the a priori bounds that underlie compactness and the broken-trajectory structure of Floer-type theories.
