Energy of a $J$-holomorphic Curve

Dirichlet energy

Half the squared gradient integrated over the domain.

Equals area

For $J$-holomorphic maps, energy is the symplectic area.

Why it matters

A uniform energy bound is a geometric, topological constraint.

Energy identity

$E(u)=\tfrac12\!\int_\Sigma |du|^2\,dvol=\int_\Sigma u^*\omega$

Consequence

Energy depends only on the homology class $[u]$, hence is bounded along the sequence.

Printed pp. 75-114 · PDF pp. 90-129Energy
Energy is the central currency of the chapter. For a J-holomorphic map it equals the symplectic area, so a uniform energy bound is geometric, not analytic. This identity is why bounded energy forces the strong conclusions of compactness rather than merely controlling some abstract norm.

Gromov Compactness Theorem

Theorem

If $E(u_\nu)\le C$, a subsequence converges modulo reparametrization to a cusp curve $u_\infty$: smoothly off finitely many bubble points $z_1,\dots,z_k$.

Modulo reparametrization

Mobius transformations are quotiented out before passing to the limit.

Off the bubble points

$u_\nu\to u_\infty$ in $C^\infty_{loc}$ on $\Sigma\setminus\{z_j\}$.

At the bubble points

Lost energy reappears on attached sphere bubbles.

Total

The limit is a connected tree of $J$-holomorphic spheres and the principal curve.

Printed pp. 75-114 · PDF pp. 90-129Gromov Compactness
Gromov's theorem is the destination. A sequence with a common energy bound has a subsequence converging modulo reparametrization to a cusp curve. Convergence is uniform away from finitely many bubble points, and at those points the geometry resolves into attached spheres carrying the missing energy.

Bubbling: Energy Concentration

Gradient blow-up

$\|du_\nu\|_\infty\to\infty$ at a point: smooth convergence fails.

Concentration

$\lim_{r\to0}\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\!\int_{B_r(z)}|du_\nu|^2 \ge \hbar$

Removal of Singularities

Theorem (4.5)

A $J$-holomorphic $u:D\setminus\{0\}\to M$ with $E(u)<\infty$ extends to a smooth $J$-holomorphic map on the full disk $D$.

Effect

The rescaled plane $\mathbb{C}=S^2\setminus\{\infty\}$ closes up into a sphere.

Why it works

Finite energy plus the isoperimetric estimate forces the puncture loop to shrink.

Without it

The bubble would be a map with a hole, not a closed curve.

Upshot

Every bubble is a genuine $J$-holomorphic sphere.

Printed pp. 75-114 · PDF pp. 90-129Removal of Singularities
Removal of singularities closes the construction. A J-holomorphic map on a punctured disk with finite energy extends smoothly across the puncture. Applied to the rescaled plane this fills the point at infinity, turning the limit into a genuine sphere rather than a map with a hole in it.

The Mean-Value Inequality

Estimate (4.3)

$|du(0)|^2 \le \frac{C}{r^2}\int_{B_r}|du|^2$

Reading

Small energy on a disk forces a small derivative at its center.

Use

Off the concentration set, energy is small, so convergence is smooth.

The Isoperimetric Inequality

Estimate (4.4)

$\Big|\int_\gamma u^*\lambda\Big| \le \tfrac{1}{4\pi}\,\ell(\gamma)^2$

Reading

A short boundary loop bounds only a small symplectic area.

Circular model

$L^2/(4\pi A)=1$ saturates the bound exactly.

The Energy Quantum: No Energy Loss

Monotonicity

$E(v)\ge \hbar > 0 \quad\text{for every nonconstant bubble } v$

Finiteness

Total energy $\le C$, so at most $\lfloor C/\hbar\rfloor$ bubbles can form.

No leakage

Energy cannot escape into infinitely many invisible pieces.

Printed pp. 75-114 · PDF pp. 90-129Energy Quantum
Monotonicity yields a minimal energy quantum: every nonconstant bubble carries at least hbar units of energy. With a finite total budget only finitely many bubbles can form, and no energy leaks into infinitely many invisible pieces. The threshold separates a genuine bubble from a harmless sharp peak.
