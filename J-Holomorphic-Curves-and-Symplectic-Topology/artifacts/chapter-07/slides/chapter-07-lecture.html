From Curves to Counts

Not a literal count

An oriented intersection number, not the visible set of curves.

Inputs

A class $A \in H_2(M)$ and constraint classes $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$.

Genus-zero invariant

$$\mathrm{GW}_{A,n}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) = \int_{[\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,n}(M,A)]^{\mathrm{vir}}} \prod_{i=1}^{n} \mathrm{ev}_i^{*}\alpha_i$$

Stable

Depends only on $A$ and the classes, not on choices.

Printed pp. 201-256From Curves to Counts
A Gromov-Witten invariant is not a literal count of visible curves but an oriented intersection number. We pull back chosen cohomology classes under the evaluation maps and integrate over the virtual fundamental class of the compactified moduli space, so the answer depends only on the homology class and degrees.

The Evaluation Map

Per marked point

$\mathrm{ev}_i$ records the image of the $i$-th marked point.

Pull back

Each $\mathrm{ev}_i^{*}\alpha_i$ encodes one geometric constraint.

Evaluation

$$\mathrm{ev}_i\colon \overline{\mathcal M}_{0,n}(M,A) \to M, \quad (u,z_1,\dots,z_n)\mapsto u(z_i)$$

Printed pp. 201-256The Evaluation Map
Each marked point gives an evaluation map sending a stable map to the image of that point in the target. Pulling back constraint classes and pairing the product against the moduli cycle turns geometry into intersection theory, and this construction is the technical heart of the genus-zero definition.

The Dimension Gate

Expected dimension

The count exists only when the constrained space is zero-dimensional.

$\mathbb{CP}^2$ case

Each point removes complex codimension two.

Dimension count

$$\dim_{\mathbb C}\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,k}(\mathbb{CP}^2,d) = 3d + k - 1$$

$$\text{residual} = 3d - k - 1 = 0 \iff k = 3d-1$$

Counting line

$k=3d-1$ isolates the rational curves.

Printed pp. 201-256The Dimension Gate
Before a moduli problem can be a number, its constrained dimension must vanish. For degree d spheres in the projective plane the stable-map dimension is 3d plus k minus one, and each point constraint removes complex codimension two, isolating curves exactly when k equals 3d minus one.

Constraints as Cycles

Poincare dual

Each $\alpha_i$ is dual to a generic submanifold $V_i \subset M$.

Meaning

Count curves whose $i$-th marked point lies on $V_i$.

Printed pp. 201-256Constraints as Cycles
We visualize a sphere meeting submanifolds Poincare dual to the constraint classes alpha_i. The evaluation map records where each marked point lands, and requiring those images to lie on fixed generic cycles cuts the moduli space down to the finite oriented set whose signed cardinality is the invariant.

Pseudocycles and Semipositivity

Pseudocycle

Boundary strata of codimension $\geq 2$ define a homology class.

Indecomposable $A$

No splitting into positive-area pieces, so no leaking count.

Semipositive bound

$$\dim \partial\,\mathrm{ev} \;\leq\; \dim \mathrm{ev} - 2$$

Why it works

The class avoids the full virtual cycle when $M$ is semipositive.

Printed pp. 201-256Pseudocycles and Semipositivity
For semipositive manifolds the evaluation map defines a pseudocycle whose boundary strata have codimension at least two, so it carries a well-defined homology class. Indecomposable classes prevent the count from leaking into lower-energy bubbles, giving a rigorous count without the full virtual cycle technology.

Invariance

Independent of $J$

Any two generic structures give the same count.

Independent of reps

Homologous constraint cycles give the same number.

Printed pp. 201-256Invariance
The invariant is independent of the generic almost complex structure and of the chosen geometric representatives of the constraints. A generic path of structures supplies an oriented cobordism between the two finite solution sets, so the signed count is preserved and the number is a genuine symplectic invariant.

The Obstruction Bundle

Nonregular $J_0$

A natural structure may give excess dimension.

Cokernel bundle

The defect is a finite-rank obstruction bundle.

Printed pp. 201-256The Obstruction Bundle
When a convenient almost complex structure is nonregular, excess dimension is absorbed by a finite-rank obstruction bundle whose fibers are cokernels of the linearized operator. A generic section vanishes on a set of expected dimension, and its Euler class replaces the direct count of regular curves.

The Gromov-Witten Axioms

Effective & grading

Negative-area classes vanish; the dimension test selects nonzero invariants.

Fundamental class

Inserting $1$ forces a count to vanish unless absorbed.

Divisor axiom

$$\mathrm{GW}_{A,n+1}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n,\beta) = (A\cdot\beta)\,\mathrm{GW}_{A,n}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$$

for a divisor class $\beta\in H^2(M)$.

Symmetry

Permuting marks only changes the sign by orientation.

Printed pp. 201-256The Gromov-Witten Axioms
The axioms make the invariants a structured intersection theory. The effective axiom kills negative-area classes, the fundamental-class and divisor axioms simplify special insertions, and grading enforces the dimension test. Together they constrain the invariants tightly enough to make many of them computable by hand.

Splitting and Gluing

Nodal boundary

Sum over $A = A_0 + A_1$ glued along the diagonal.

Domain degeneration

A node forms in $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}$.

Printed pp. 201-256Splitting and Gluing
The splitting axiom evaluates a nodal boundary by summing over decompositions of the class into two pieces glued along a Poincare-dual basis of the diagonal. A domain degeneration in the moduli of four marked spheres equates two boundary divisors and produces the fundamental gluing relation.

WDVV and Kontsevich Recursion

Keel's relation

Boundary divisors in $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}$ agree.

WDVV

Associativity of the quantum product of the potential.

Kontsevich recursion ($\mathbb{CP}^2$)

$$N_d = \!\!\sum_{d_1+d_2=d}\!\! N_{d_1}N_{d_2}\,d_1^2 d_2\Big[d_2\tbinom{3d-4}{3d_1-2} - d_1\tbinom{3d-4}{3d_1-1}\Big]$$

Output

Each count from lower-degree splittings.

Printed pp. 201-256WDVV and Kontsevich Recursion
Keel's description of boundary relations in the moduli of marked genus-zero curves yields the WDVV equation for the potential. Extracting the degree d coefficient for the projective plane gives Kontsevich's recursion, which assembles each count from products of lower-degree splittings along the diagonal.

Counting Rational Plane Curves

The classical numbers

Degree $d$ rational curves through $3d-1$ generic points.

Validation

The recursion exactly reproduces these integers.

Printed pp. 201-256Counting Rational Plane Curves
The recursion reproduces the classical enumerations of rational curves in the projective plane: one line through two points, one conic through five, twelve cubics through eight, and six hundred twenty quartics through eleven. These exact integers are the chapter's concrete payoff and validation.
