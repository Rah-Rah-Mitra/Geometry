The Nonlinear Cauchy-Riemann Operator

Definition

$\bar\partial_J(u)=\tfrac12\big(du + J(u)\circ du\circ j\big)$

Reading the formula

It compares $du$ with the same differential rotated by $j$ on the source and $J$ on the target.

Section, not function

$\bar\partial_J(u)$ is a one-form on $\Sigma$ with values in $u^*TM$, antilinear in $j,J$.

Curves are zeros

$u$ is $J$-holomorphic $\iff \bar\partial_J(u)=0$ pointwise.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53The dbar_J Operator
The nonlinear Cauchy-Riemann operator measures how far a map is from being J-holomorphic. It compares the differential with its own conjugate twisted by J on the target and j on the domain, returning a one-form valued section that vanishes exactly when the map is a pseudoholomorphic curve.

du Splits: Linear Plus Antilinear

Splitting

$du=\partial_J u+\bar\partial_J u$

Complex-linear part

$\partial_J u=\tfrac12(du-J\,du\,j)$ commutes with $J$ — what a holomorphic map keeps.

The obstruction

$\bar\partial_J u$ is the antilinear part; it is the whole error term.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Complex-Linear and Antilinear Split
Every real-linear differential splits canonically into a complex-linear part and a complex-antilinear part with respect to j and J. The complex-linear piece is what a holomorphic map keeps; the antilinear piece is precisely dbar_J. A curve is J-holomorphic exactly when that antilinear obstruction is zero.

Local Coordinate Form

In a holomorphic chart $s+it$

$\partial_s u+J(u)\,\partial_t u=0$

Standard structure

If $J=i$ this is the classical Cauchy-Riemann system $u_s+iu_t=0$.

A nonlinear deformation

$J(u)$ depends on the point, so the equation is genuinely nonlinear and elliptic.

Why it matters

Ellipticity gives the regularity and unique-continuation theorems used throughout.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Local Coordinate Form
In a holomorphic chart on the domain the equation becomes du_s plus J of u acting on du_t equals zero. With the standard structure this is the ordinary Cauchy-Riemann system, so J-holomorphic curves are an honest nonlinear deformation of classical holomorphic maps, controlled by how J varies over the target.

The Energy Functional

Definition

$E(u)=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_\Sigma |du|^2\,d\mathrm{vol}$

Conformally invariant

The Dirichlet energy of $u$ does not depend on the conformal scale of the domain metric.

Norm from $J$

$|du|^2$ is measured in $g_J(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)$, the metric tamed by $\omega$.

The control quantity

Bounded energy is what later forces Gromov compactness of curve sequences.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Energy of a Curve
The energy is half the integral of the squared norm of the differential measured in the metric that J and a compatible omega define. It is a conformally invariant Dirichlet-type functional, finite on compact domains, and it is the quantity whose boundedness later forces Gromov compactness of sequences of curves.

Energy Identity: Energy Equals Area

Energy identity (compatible $J$)

$E(u)=\displaystyle\int_\Sigma u^*\omega=\langle[\omega],u_*[\Sigma]\rangle$

A priori bound

The right side depends only on the homology class of $u$, so energy is fixed by topology.

No harmonic analogue

General harmonic maps have no such topological energy bound.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Energy Equals Symplectic Area
For a compatible structure the energy of a J-holomorphic curve equals the integral of the pulled-back symplectic form, its omega-area. Because that area depends only on the homology class of the curve, energy is bounded a priori by topology alone, a fact with no analogue for general harmonic maps.

Relation to Harmonic and Conformal Maps

Conformal

A $J$-holomorphic curve is a conformal map for the metric $g_J$ it induces.

Harmonic

It is harmonic, hence a critical point of the energy functional.

Minimal in its class

$\mathrm{Area}(u)=E(u)=\langle[\omega],A\rangle$

Calibrated geometry

$\omega$ calibrates the image: it minimizes area among maps in the homology class $A$.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Harmonic and Conformal Maps
J-holomorphic curves are conformal harmonic maps for the induced metric, so they are area-minimizing in their homology class. This links pseudoholomorphic curve theory to the classical calculus of minimal surfaces while the symplectic area identity supplies the energy bound that the harmonic-map viewpoint lacks.

Unique Continuation / Identity Theorem

Theorem

$u,v$ agree to $\infty$ order at $z_0 \;\Rightarrow\; u\equiv v$

Why

$\bar\partial_J$ is first-order elliptic; a Carleman estimate forbids infinite-order vanishing of a nonzero solution.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Unique Continuation
Because dbar_J is a first-order elliptic operator with a Carleman estimate, a J-holomorphic curve that vanishes to infinite order at a point, or two curves agreeing to infinite order, must coincide on the whole connected domain. This identity theorem mirrors the rigidity of ordinary holomorphic functions.

Critical Points and Zeros of du

Isolated zeros

For a non-constant curve, $du$ vanishes only at finitely many points of a compact $\Sigma$.

Local model

$u(z)\sim z^{m}\quad(m\ge 1)$ near a critical point

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Critical Points and Zeros
The differential of a non-constant J-holomorphic curve has only isolated zeros, and near each the map looks like a complex polynomial branch z to the m. Critical points are therefore finite in number on a compact domain, and away from them the curve is a local immersion onto its image.

Somewhere-Injective Curves vs Multiple Covers

Simple = somewhere injective

Some $z$ has $u^{-1}(u(z))=\{z\}$ and $du_z\ne0$. The non-injective set is countable.

Multiple cover

Otherwise $u=v\circ\varphi$ with $v$ simple and $\varphi$ a branched cover of degree $k\ge2$.

Dichotomy

$E(u)=k\cdot E(v),\qquad A=k\cdot A_0$

Why it matters

Moduli theory and transversality are stated for simple curves; covers are handled separately.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Simple Curves and Multiple Covers
A curve is somewhere injective, or simple, when some point has a single preimage and an injective differential. The set of non-injective points is then countable. Every curve is either simple or factors as a branched multiple cover of a simple curve composed with a holomorphic branched covering of the domain.

Local Structure of Solutions

Normal form

Isolated critical points, branch model $z^m$, immersion elsewhere.

Intersections

Two distinct curves meet in isolated points with positive local index.

Adjunction (dim 4, $\mathbb{CP}^2$)

$\delta+g\le \tfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}$

Local to global

Positivity of intersections turns the local data into a topological inequality.

Printed pp. 17-38 · PDF pp. 32-53Local Structure of Solutions
Combining the antilinear splitting, the Carleman estimate, and the branch model gives the local normal form for solutions: isolated critical points, somewhere injectivity for simple curves, and controlled intersections. In real dimension four positivity of intersections then converts this local data into the global adjunction inequality.
