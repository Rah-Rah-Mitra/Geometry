The Riemann-Roch Theorem

An analytic index controlled entirely by topology

Fredholm

A Cauchy-Riemann operator on $E \to \Sigma$ has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel.

Topological index

The index depends only on rank, genus, and the first Chern number.

Appendix C supplies the index bookkeeping behind every moduli dimension in the book. A real-linear Cauchy-Riemann operator on a bundle over a closed Riemann surface is Fredholm, and its index is fixed entirely by topology: the rank, the genus through the Euler characteristic, and the first Chern number of the bundle.

The Cauchy-Riemann Operator

Real-linear dbar

$D$ is a complex-linear $\bar\partial$ plus a real-linear zeroth-order term.

Lower order is harmless

The $0$-th order part is compact; it cannot change the index.

Operator

$$D u = \bar\partial u + A\,u,\qquad D:W^{k,p}(E)\to W^{k-1,p}(\Lambda^{0,1}\!\otimes E)$$

Elliptic estimates

Sobolev estimates give the Fredholm property of $D$.

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
We work with a complex bundle E over a closed Riemann surface and a real-linear operator that differs from a complex-linear dbar by a zeroth-order term. The lower-order term does not change the index, so the analytic problem reduces to a model whose Fredholm theory is controlled by elliptic estimates on Sobolev completions.

The Real Index Formula

Theorem

$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R} D = n\,\chi(\Sigma) + 2\langle c_1(E),[\Sigma]\rangle$$

Equivalent form

$$=\,2\big(n(1-g) + \deg E\big),\quad n=\operatorname{rk}E$$

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
The central identity states that the real index equals n times the Euler characteristic plus twice the pairing of the first Chern class with the fundamental class. Equivalently it is two times n times one minus g plus the degree. Degree raises the index by two; each handle lowers it by two.

Classical Holomorphic Statement

Line bundle

For a holomorphic line bundle $L$, kernel and cokernel are $H^0(L)$ and $H^1(L)$.

Complex index

Half the real index recovers the classical count.

Riemann-Roch (classical)

$$\dim H^0(L) - \dim H^1(L) = \deg L + 1 - g$$

Specialization

Holds when $D$ is complex-linear and integrable.

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
For a holomorphic line bundle L the kernel and cokernel become the sheaf cohomology groups H zero and H one. Their dimensions difference equals degree plus one minus genus. This is the form learned in algebraic geometry, and it specializes the analytic statement when the operator is honestly complex-linear and integrable.

Serre Duality

Dual problem

The cokernel on $L$ is a kernel on the twisted canonical bundle.

Canonical bundle

$\deg K = 2g-2$ sets the vanishing threshold.

Duality

$$H^1(L) \;\cong\; H^0(K\otimes L^{-1})^{*}$$

Consequence

Obstructions become a second section count.

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
Serre duality identifies the cokernel of the operator on L with the kernel of the dual problem on the canonical bundle twisted by L inverse. This turns obstruction counting into a second section count, makes the index formula symmetric, and explains why the canonical bundle of degree two g minus two governs vanishing thresholds.

Kernel Versus Cokernel

Stable difference

$\dim\ker - \dim\operatorname{coker}$ is pinned by topology.

High degree

For $\deg L > 2g-2$ the cokernel vanishes.

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
The index is a stable difference even though kernel and cokernel dimensions jump. As degree increases past two g minus two the cokernel vanishes and the kernel grows linearly. Holomorphic sections, the kernel, trade against obstructions, the cokernel, while their signed difference stays pinned by topology.

A Genus-g Surface with a Degree-d Bundle

Large degree

$$\deg L > 2g-2 \;\Rightarrow\; \dim H^0(L) = d+1-g$$

Unobstructed

The obstruction space $H^1(L)$ is then empty.

Lowering degree reintroduces cohomology in degree one.

The Determinant Line Bundle

Family of operators

Over a parameter space the kernel and cokernel may jump.

Patches smoothly

The determinant line is globally well defined.

Determinant line

$$\det D = \Lambda^{\max}\ker D \otimes \big(\Lambda^{\max}\operatorname{coker} D\big)^{*}$$

Orientations

Its triviality orients moduli spaces coherently.

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
For a family of Cauchy-Riemann operators the kernels and cokernels may jump, but the top exterior power of the kernel tensored with the dual top power of the cokernel patches into a smooth determinant line over the parameter space. Its orientability underwrites coherent orientations of moduli spaces.

Virtual Dimension of Moduli

Linearize

The linearized $\bar\partial$ at $u$ acts on the pullback bundle $u^*TM$.

Expected dimension

The index is the virtual dimension of the moduli space.

Virtual dimension

$$\dim^{\text{vir}}\mathcal M = n\chi(\Sigma) + 2\langle c_1(TM),u_*[\Sigma]\rangle$$

Here

$E = u^*TM$, $\;n = \tfrac12\dim_{\mathbb R} M$.

Linearizing the dbar equation at a J-holomorphic curve u gives a Cauchy-Riemann operator on the pullback bundle u star T M. Riemann-Roch computes its index, which is the expected or virtual dimension of the moduli space. With n now half the real dimension of the target, the formula reads two n one minus g plus two c one.

Boundary Problems and Maslov

Bordered index

$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R} D = n + \mu(E,F)$$

Disk model

One full turn of the boundary line gives $\mu = 2$.

When the surface has boundary mapped into a Lagrangian, totally real boundary conditions add a Maslov term. In the disk model one full rotation of the boundary line contributes Maslov index two, and the index becomes rank plus Maslov, generalizing the closed formula to bordered curves used in Floer theory.

Gluing and Index Additivity

Match at a node

Two pieces share one evaluation point.

Subtract the constraint

The matching condition removes its real dimension.

Additivity

$$\operatorname{ind} D_{A\#B} = \operatorname{ind} D_A + \operatorname{ind} D_B - \dim_{\mathbb R}T_pM$$

Proof engine

Disk cases plus gluing build the general theorem.

Printed pp. 579-618Riemann-Roch · Appendix C
Gluing two curves at a node is index bookkeeping: the glued index equals the sum of the piece indices minus the real dimension of the matching constraint at the node. This additivity, together with disk base cases and elliptic estimates, is exactly how the appendix assembles the general Riemann-Roch theorem.
