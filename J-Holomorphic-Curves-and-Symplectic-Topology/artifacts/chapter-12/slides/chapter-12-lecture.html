The Symplectic Action Functional

Loop space

Contractible loops with chosen capping disc $v$.

Unbounded

$\mathcal{A}_H$ has no top or bottom: classical Morse fails.

Action functional

$\mathcal{A}_H(x,v) = -\!\int_{D} v^*\omega - \int_0^1 H_t(x(t))\,dt$

Covering

The cap remembers topology; recapping shifts $\mathcal{A}_H$ by $\langle\omega,A\rangle$.

Printed pp. 487-530The Symplectic Action Functional
The action functional lives on the contractible loop space lifted with capping discs. Its critical points are exactly the one-periodic orbits of the Hamiltonian, so finding orbits becomes a variational problem. The action is unbounded above and below, which is why ordinary Morse theory fails and Floer's idea is needed.

Periodic Orbits as Critical Points

First variation

$d\mathcal{A}_H=0$ recovers Hamilton's equation.

Nondegenerate

Return map has no eigenvalue $1$: orbits isolated.

Critical point equation

$\dot{x}(t) = X_{H_t}\big(x(t)\big),\quad x(0)=x(1)$

Generators

Critical loops $\mathcal{P}(H)$ form the finite generating set.

Printed pp. 487-530Periodic Orbits as Critical Points
Setting the first variation of the action to zero recovers Hamilton's equation, so critical loops are precisely the closed orbits. Nondegeneracy of an orbit means the linearized return map has no eigenvalue one, guaranteeing that orbits are isolated and that the generating set of the complex is finite.

Floer Cylinders: The Gradient Equation

Perturbed Cauchy-Riemann

$\partial_s u + J(u)\big(\partial_t u - X_{H_t}(u)\big) = 0$

Asymptotics

Finite energy $\Rightarrow u(s,\cdot)\to x,y$ as $s\to\mp\infty$.

Printed pp. 487-530Floer Cylinders: The Gradient Equation
Negative gradient flow lines of the action with respect to the L-two metric are cylinders solving a perturbed Cauchy-Riemann equation. Finite energy forces each cylinder to converge at its two ends to periodic orbits, so the trajectories interpolate between critical points just like Morse flow lines connect critical points downhill.

The Conley-Zehnder Grading

Index

Built from the path of symplectic matrices of the linearized flow.

Spectral flow

Equals the Fredholm index of the linearized Floer operator.

$\dim \mathcal{M}(x,y) = \mu_{CZ}(x) - \mu_{CZ}(y)$

Printed pp. 487-530The Conley-Zehnder Grading
Each capped orbit carries a Conley-Zehnder index built from the path of symplectic matrices the linearized flow traces. This index grades the complex and equals the spectral flow of the linearized Floer operator, so the dimension of a cylinder moduli space is the difference of the two endpoint indices.

The Floer Chain Complex

Chain group

Free Novikov module on capped orbits, graded by $\mu_{CZ}$.

Rigidity

Differential counts index-1 cylinders modulo $\mathbb{R}$-translation.

Floer differential

$\partial x = \sum_{\mu(x)-\mu(y)=1} \#\big(\mathcal{M}(x,y)/\mathbb{R}\big)\, T^{E}\, y$

Energy

Each counted cylinder drops action: $E = \mathcal{A}_H(x)-\mathcal{A}_H(y)>0$.

Printed pp. 487-530The Floer Chain Complex
The chain groups are the free module on capped orbits over the Novikov ring, graded by the Conley-Zehnder index. The differential counts rigid index-one cylinders modulo the R translation action, weighting each by its energy. This packages all the orbit and cylinder data into one algebraic object.

Why $\partial^2 = 0$

Boundary identity

$\partial \overline{\mathcal{M}}_2(x,z) = \bigsqcup_y \mathcal{M}_1(x,y)\times\mathcal{M}_1(y,z)$

Conclusion

Even boundary count $\Rightarrow \partial^2=0$ via gluing.

Printed pp. 487-530Why d squared equals zero
The square of the differential counts ends of one-dimensional moduli spaces of index-two cylinders. Compactness and gluing show these spaces compactify by adding exactly the broken trajectories, pairs of index-one cylinders. Since a compact one-manifold has an even boundary, every broken pair cancels and the square vanishes.

Invariance Under the Hamiltonian

Continuation

Homotopy of $(H,J)$ gives a chain map between complexes.

Independence

$HF_*$ depends only on $M$, not on the chosen pair.

Continuation map

$\Phi_{H^0\to H^1}\circ\partial^0 = \partial^1\circ\Phi_{H^0\to H^1}$

Equivalence

Composing forward and back is chain homotopic to identity.

Printed pp. 487-530Invariance Under the Hamiltonian
Different regular Hamiltonian and almost complex choices give different complexes, but continuation maps built from homotopies of the data induce chain homotopy equivalences. Hence Floer homology depends only on the manifold, not on the auxiliary choices, and becomes a genuine symplectic invariant of the closed manifold.

Isomorphism with Morse Homology

Small $H$

Time-independent Morse function: orbits are critical points.

Degeneration

Floer cylinders collapse to Morse gradient lines.

Floer = Morse = singular

$HF_*(M) \cong HM_*(f) \cong H_*(M;\Lambda)$

Printed pp. 487-530Isomorphism with Morse Homology
For a small time-independent Hamiltonian the orbits are the critical points of a Morse function and the cylinders degenerate to Morse gradient lines. This identifies Floer homology with the Morse, hence singular, homology of the manifold, providing the bridge from dynamics to topology.

The Arnold Conjecture

Arnold lower bound

$\#\,\mathcal{P}(H) \;\ge\; \sum_{k} \dim H_k(M;\mathbb{Q}) = \sum_k b_k$

Sharper

Improves $\#\mathrm{Fix} \ge |\chi(M)|$ from Lefschetz.

Printed pp. 487-530The Arnold Conjecture
Counting generators bounds homology from below, so the number of nondegenerate one-periodic orbits is at least the total Betti number. This vastly improves the classical Lefschetz fixed point estimate, which only gives the Euler characteristic, and resolves the homological Arnold conjecture for closed symplectic manifolds.

The Pair-of-Pants Product

Three ends

Count solutions over a thrice-punctured sphere.

Algebra

Associative, unital ring structure on $HF_*$.

Printed pp. 487-530The Pair-of-Pants Product
Counting solutions over a three-punctured sphere defines a product on Floer homology. This pair-of-pants count is associative and unital and, transported through PSS, becomes the quantum cup product, deforming the classical cup product by counting rational curves weighted by Novikov variables.

PSS and Quantum Cohomology

PSS maps

Cap orbits with spiked half-cylinders carrying cycles.

Ring iso

Intertwines pair-of-pants with the quantum product.

$\mathrm{PSS}:\; QH^*(M) \;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\; HF^*(H)$

Printed pp. 487-530PSS and Quantum Cohomology
The Piunikhin-Salamon-Schwarz maps cap orbits with half-cylinders carrying spiked discs, giving a ring isomorphism between Floer homology and quantum cohomology. Through PSS the pair-of-pants product matches the quantum product, completing the dictionary between Hamiltonian dynamics and enumerative quantum geometry.
