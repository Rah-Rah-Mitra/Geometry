The Nonlinear Cauchy-Riemann Equation

The map

$u : (\Sigma, j) \to (M, J)$ from a Riemann surface.

Generalization

Reduces to classical holomorphic maps when $M=\mathbb{C}^n$.

Definition: J-holomorphic curve

$du \circ j = J \circ du$

Nonlinear

$J$ varies over $M$, so the operator is genuinely nonlinear.

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31The CR Equation
A J-holomorphic curve is a map u from a Riemann surface into an almost complex manifold whose differential commutes with the two complex structures. The equation du composed with j equals J composed with du is the nonlinear Cauchy-Riemann equation, generalizing classical holomorphic maps to curved nonintegrable targets.

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31Nonsqueezing
Gromov introduced pseudoholomorphic curves in 1985 and immediately proved nonsqueezing: a symplectic embedding of a ball of radius r into a cylinder forces the cylinder radius to be at least r. A holomorphic disc through any point produces the area bound, turning soft topology rigid.

Moduli Spaces of Curves

The space

Object of study

Geometry of the space, not any single curve.

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31Moduli Spaces
Fixing a homology class and marked points, the solutions of the Cauchy-Riemann equation form a moduli space. Studying symplectic manifolds means studying these spaces. Their geometry, not any single curve, encodes the invariants, so we must control their dimension, smoothness, and limiting behavior carefully.

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31Transversality
Transversality asks that the linearized Cauchy-Riemann operator be surjective, making the moduli space a smooth manifold of the Fredholm index dimension. For generic almost complex structures regularity holds for simple curves, so the predicted dimension is real and the count well defined rather than illusory.

Compactness: Controlling Limits

Energy bound

Energy equals symplectic area; it is topological.

Bubbling

Energy concentrates and spheres pinch off in limits.

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31Compactness
Compactness controls how sequences of curves degenerate. Bounded energy forces convergence up to bubbling, where energy concentrates and spheres pinch off. Gromov compactness replaces missing limits by stable maps, so the moduli space has a closed, countable boundary structure suitable for intersection theory.

Evaluation Maps and Pseudocycles

Evaluation

Send a marked curve to the image of its points.

Pseudocycle

Controlled boundary gives a cycle to intersect.

Evaluation map

$\mathrm{ev} : \mathcal{M}_{0,k}(A,J) \to M^k, \quad u \mapsto (u(z_1),\dots,u(z_k))$

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31Evaluation
Evaluation at marked points sends a curve to its image points, turning the moduli space into a geometric cycle in the target. Because of the controlled boundary, this evaluation defines a pseudocycle that can be intersected with constraints, the bridge from analysis to enumerative geometry.

Gromov-Witten Invariants

Intersection count

Intersect the evaluation cycle with constraints.

Dimension gate

Count only when the dimension budget cancels to zero.

Printed pp. 1-16 / PDF pp. 16-31GW Invariants
Gromov-Witten invariants are intersection numbers of the evaluation pseudocycle against chosen constraints once the dimension budget cancels to zero. They are deformation invariant and satisfy splitting axioms, making them robust symplectic invariants rather than artifacts of a chosen almost complex structure or metric.
