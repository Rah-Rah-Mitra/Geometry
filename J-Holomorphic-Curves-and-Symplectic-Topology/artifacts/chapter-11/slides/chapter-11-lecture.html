From Cup Product to Quantum Product

Classical

$a\cup b$ is dual to the intersection of cycles dual to $a$ and $b$.

Quantum

$a*b$ adds curve-corrected terms weighted by $q^A$.

Quantum product

$$a * b = \sum_{A} (a*b)_A\, q^A$$

The $A=0$ term equals the classical $a\cup b$.

Slogan

A ring that remembers holomorphic spheres.

Printed pp. 417-486From Cup Product to Quantum Product
Ordinary cohomology multiplies cocycles by intersecting the Poincare duals of their cycles. The quantum product adds correction terms that count rational curves passing through three cycles. Each correction carries a formal Novikov variable tracking the curve class, so the classical intersection answer survives intact as the leading term when those variables vanish.

Definition

$\mathrm{GW}_A(a,b,c)$ counts spheres in class $A$ meeting three cycles.

Dimension

A degree constraint selects which $(a,b,c,A)$ contribute.

Structure constants

$$\langle a*b, c\rangle = \sum_A \mathrm{GW}_A(a,b,c)\, q^A$$

Source

Built from the evaluation maps on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(M,A)$.

The structure constants of the quantum product are the genus-zero three-point Gromov-Witten invariants. They count J-holomorphic spheres in a fixed homology class meeting three cycles dual to the inserted classes. A dimension count over the moduli space forces a degree constraint that pins down exactly which classes and which curve classes can contribute a nonzero number.

The Novikov Ring

Formal series

Elements are series in $q^A$ over effective classes $A$.

Energy filtration

Finiteness below each energy level makes products converge.

Novikov ring

$$\Lambda = \Big\{ \textstyle\sum_A \lambda_A q^A : \#\{A:\lambda_A\neq 0,\ \omega(A)\le c\}<\infty \Big\}$$

Classical limit

The map $q\to 0$ projects $\Lambda$ onto the ground ring.

Printed pp. 417-486The Novikov Ring
Infinitely many curve classes can contribute, so we book-keep them in the Novikov ring of formal series in variables q raised to homology classes. The finiteness condition controlling how many classes appear below each energy level makes products well defined, and setting q to zero recovers ordinary cohomology cleanly as the degree-zero piece.

The Small Quantum Product

Domain

Defined on $QH^*(M)=H^*(M)\otimes\Lambda$.

Unit

$\mathbb{1}=1\in H^0(M)$ is a two-sided unit.

Grading

The product respects a shifted degree.

Defining relation

$$\langle a*b,\, c\rangle \;=\; \sum_A \mathrm{GW}_A(a,b,c)\, q^A$$

$A=0$ gives $\langle a\cup b, c\rangle$.

Recovery

$q\to 0$ returns the classical cup product.

Printed pp. 417-486The Small Quantum Product
The small quantum product uses only three-point invariants and lands in cohomology tensored with the Novikov ring. The fundamental class stays a two-sided unit, the product respects a shifted grading coming from the Chern number of the curve class, and the degree-zero curve class reproduces the classical cup product exactly under the limit sending q to zero.

Associativity and WDVV

Key property

$(a*b)*c = a*(b*c)$ for all classes.

Equivalent to

The WDVV system on the potential $\Phi$.

WDVV equations

$$\sum_{e,f}\Phi_{abe}\,g^{ef}\,\Phi_{fcd} = \sum_{e,f}\Phi_{ade}\,g^{ef}\,\Phi_{fcb}$$

Why hard

Each product mixes infinitely many curve corrections.

Printed pp. 417-486Associativity and WDVV
The deepest property of the quantum product is associativity. It is equivalent to the Witten-Dijkgraaf-Verlinde-Verlinde equations, a nonlinear system of partial differential equations on the Gromov-Witten potential. Associativity is far from obvious because each product mixes infinitely many curve-class corrections together, so the identity is genuinely a statement about how those counts interlock.

The Gromov-Witten Potential

Generating function

One series $\Phi$ stores all genus-zero invariants.

Third derivatives

$\Phi_{abc}$ are the quantum structure constants.

Potential

$$\Phi(t)=\sum_{n,A}\frac{1}{n!}\,\mathrm{GW}_A(t,\dots,t)\,q^A,\qquad \langle a*b,c\rangle=\Phi_{abc}$$

Payoff

WDVV becomes a PDE on the single function $\Phi$.

Printed pp. 417-486The Gromov-Witten Potential
Packaging all genus-zero invariants into one generating function gives the Gromov-Witten potential. Its third partial derivatives in the cohomology coordinates are precisely the quantum structure constants, so the WDVV constraint becomes a clean differential equation on this single potential function, directly foreshadowing the Frobenius-manifold viewpoint developed in the final section of the chapter.

Quantum Cohomology of $\mathbb{CP}^n$

Classical ring

$H^*(\mathbb{CP}^n)=\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{n+1})$.

Quantum relation

The single relation deforms to $x^{n+1}=q$.

Quantum ring

$$QH^*(\mathbb{CP}^n)=\Lambda[x]\big/\big(x^{n+1}-q\big)$$

Reading it

One $q$-correction generates the whole quantum ring.

Printed pp. 417-486Quantum Cohomology of CP^n
The cleanest example is complex projective space. Classically the hyperplane class x satisfies x to the n plus one equals zero. Quantum corrections deform this single relation to x to the n plus one equals q, and that one change generates the entire quantum ring, turning the nilpotent classical algebra into a far richer deformed structure.

The $\mathbb{CP}^1$ Multiplication Table

Basis

$\{1, H\}$ with $H$ the hyperplane class.

Quantum entry

$H*H=q$ replaces the classical $H^2=0$.

Eigenvalues

Multiplication by $H$ has eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{q}$.

Printed pp. 417-486The CP^1 Multiplication Table
For the projective line the whole ring fits in a tiny table. The only nonclassical entry is H times H equals q, replacing the classical nilpotent relation H squared equals zero. Setting q to zero recovers the classical table, confirming the deformation is consistent, while for positive q multiplication by H becomes semisimple with eigenvalues plus and minus square root of q.

Frobenius Structure and Seidel Units

Frobenius

$\langle a*b,c\rangle=\langle a,b*c\rangle$ against the Poincare pairing.

Seidel map

Hamiltonian loops $\to$ invertible quantum classes.

Invertibility (CP^1)

$$H * \tfrac{H}{q} = \tfrac{1}{q}(H*H)=\tfrac{1}{q}\,q = 1$$

Package

Metric, product, and potential $\Phi$ cohere as one structure.

Printed pp. 417-486Frobenius Structure and Seidel Units
The Poincare pairing, the quantum product, and the potential together form a Frobenius manifold in which the product is symmetric against the metric. Seidel's representation sends Hamiltonian loops to invertible quantum cohomology classes, and in the projective line model the inverse of H is simply H over q, since H times H over q equals one.
