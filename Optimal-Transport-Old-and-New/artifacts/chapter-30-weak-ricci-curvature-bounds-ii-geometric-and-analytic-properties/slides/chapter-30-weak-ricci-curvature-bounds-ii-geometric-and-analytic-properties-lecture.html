The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality

Setup

Transport set $A_0$ onto $A_1$; track the midpoint set $A_t$.

Claim

Flat limit

$K=0$ recovers the classical Brunn-Minkowski inequality.

Distorted Brunn-Minkowski

$$\nu(A_t)^{1/N} \ge \tau_{K,N}^{(1-t)}\,\nu(A_0)^{1/N} + \tau_{K,N}^{(t)}\,\nu(A_1)^{1/N}$$

Interpolating between two sets by transporting one onto the other yields a synthetic Brunn-Minkowski inequality. The measure of the midpoint set dominates a distortion-weighted mean of the endpoint measures. Flat space recovers the classical inequality, while positive curvature improves it through the comparison coefficients.

The Poincare Inequality

Source

$K>0$ makes entropy uniformly $K$-convex along geodesics.

Linearize

Second-order expansion at the uniform measure $\nu$.

Meaning

Variance of any observable is controlled by its energy.

Spectral-gap form

$$\mathrm{Var}_\nu(f) \le \frac{N-1}{NK}\int |\nabla f|^2\, d\nu$$

With K positive the entropy is uniformly convex, and linearizing that convexity around the uniform measure produces a spectral-gap Poincare inequality. The variance of any function is bounded by a curvature-dimension constant times the Dirichlet energy, controlling fluctuations of every observable on the space.

Uniform displacement convexity of entropy is exactly the hypothesis that yields a logarithmic Sobolev inequality. This sharpens Poincare, governs exponential convergence of the heat semigroup, and encodes Gaussian-type concentration of measure.
