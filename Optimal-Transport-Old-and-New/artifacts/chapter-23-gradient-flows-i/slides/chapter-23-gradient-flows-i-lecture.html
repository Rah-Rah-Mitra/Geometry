Steepest Descent Reconsidered

Finite dimensions

On a Riemannian manifold, descent follows minus the gradient.

Gradient flow ODE

$$\dot{x}(t) = -\nabla_g \mathcal{F}\big(x(t)\big)$$

Lift

Replace $x$ by a measure $\rho$ and $g$ by the Wasserstein metric.

Printed pp. 629-692Steepest Descent Reconsidered
Before lifting to measures, recall how steepest descent works on a manifold. The velocity points opposite to the Riemannian gradient, so the energy falls as fast as the metric allows. Wasserstein gradient flows copy this picture exactly, but the manifold is now an infinite-dimensional space of densities equipped with a transport metric.

The Free-Energy Functional

Energy

$$\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\int U(\rho)\,dx+\int V\,\rho\,dx+\tfrac12\!\iint W\,\rho\,\rho$$

Internal

$U$ convex; the Boltzmann choice $U(\rho)=\rho\log\rho$ gives entropy.

Potential

$V$ confines mass and selects the equilibrium profile.

Interaction

$W$ couples mass to itself; aggregation or repulsion.

Key fact

The choice of $U,V,W$ fixes which diffusion equation appears.

Printed pp. 629-692The Free-Energy Functional
Gradient flows need an energy to descend. We focus on free-energy functionals built from an internal entropy term, a potential, and an interaction. The internal term uses a convex density function; choosing the Boltzmann entropy makes the resulting flow the linear heat equation. Different energies select different evolution equations from the same scheme.

Otto's Riemannian Picture

Tangent vectors

Infinitesimal mass moves obeying a continuity equation $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\!\cdot(\rho v)=0$.

Metric tensor

$$\|\dot\rho\|_\rho^2=\inf_v\int |v|^2\,\rho\,dx$$

Benamou-Brenier

$W_2^2$ is the least kinetic energy of a transporting flow.

Printed pp. 629-692Otto's Riemannian Picture
Otto's calculus reads the Wasserstein space as formally Riemannian. Tangent vectors are infinitesimal mass displacements written as continuity-equation velocity fields, and the metric measures their kinetic energy. This viewpoint lets us define gradients of functionals and gives meaning to the phrase steepest descent in the space of probability measures.

The JKO Minimizing-Movement Scheme

One step, time step $\tau$

$$\rho^{n+1}=\arg\min_{\rho}\;\Big\{\mathcal{F}(\rho)+\frac{W_2^2(\rho,\rho^{n})}{2\tau}\Big\}$$

Two pulls

Energy term drags downhill; penalty term anchors to the previous density.

Proximal point

Exactly the implicit Euler / proximal method in the metric $W_2$.

Well-posed

Each minimization has a unique solution under mild convexity.

Goal

Let $\tau\to0$ and recover a continuous gradient flow.

Printed pp. 629-692The JKO Minimizing-Movement Scheme
Jordan, Kinderlehrer, and Otto proposed building the flow one small step at a time. Each step minimizes the energy plus a quadratic penalty equal to the squared Wasserstein distance to the previous density, divided by twice the time step. This is the proximal point method, transplanted from Euclidean optimization into the transport metric.

Reading the Step as a Proximal Operator

Proximal operator

$$\rho^{n+1}=\operatorname{prox}^{W_2}_{\tau\mathcal{F}}(\rho^{n})$$

Trust region

The penalty $W_2^2/2\tau$ forbids moving mass too far in one step.

Small $\tau$

Short steps stay near $\rho^n$, tracking the true descent direction.

Printed pp. 629-692Reading the Step as a Proximal Operator
Read the JKO update as a proximal operator. The penalty keeps the new measure close to the old one in transport distance, while the energy term pulls it downhill. As the step size shrinks the penalty dominates short moves, so the discrete trajectory hugs the true gradient-flow curve more and more tightly.

Discrete Steps Descending the Landscape

Staircase

JKO iterates $\rho^0,\rho^1,\rho^2,\dots$ step down the energy surface.

Each tread

One Wasserstein proximal move, balancing $\mathcal{F}$ against $W_2^2/2\tau$.

Refinement

As $\tau\to0$ the staircase straightens into a smooth descent curve.

Printed pp. 629-692Discrete Steps Descending the Landscape
Here is the geometric heart of the scheme. The minimizing-movement iterates form a staircase descending the energy surface, each tread a single Wasserstein proximal step. Refining the time step turns the staircase into a smooth curve of steepest descent, the continuous gradient flow we ultimately want to construct.

Heat and Fokker-Planck Equations

Entropy gradient flow

$$\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\;\Rightarrow\;\partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho$$

Add a potential $V$

$$\partial_t\rho=\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\rho\,\nabla V\big)+\Delta\rho$$

Fokker-Planck

The free energy $\int\rho\log\rho+\int V\rho$ drives the same flow with drift.

Printed pp. 629-692Heat and Fokker-Planck Equations
The headline identity: the heat equation is the gradient flow of the Boltzmann entropy in Wasserstein space. Adding a confining potential turns it into the Fokker-Planck equation, the gradient flow of the corresponding free energy. A single variational principle thus produces a whole family of diffusion equations.

Optimality of a Single JKO Step

First variation

Perturbing $\rho^{n+1}$ by a transport velocity must not lower the objective.

Euler-Lagrange

$$\frac{\varphi}{\tau}+U'(\rho^{n+1})+V=\text{const}$$

Kantorovich $\varphi$

$\nabla\varphi$ is the optimal velocity from $\rho^{n+1}$ back to $\rho^n$.

Discrete continuity

$$\frac{\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n}}{\tau}\approx-\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\rho^{n+1}\nabla(\tfrac{\varphi}{\tau})\big)$$

Limit

Sending $\tau\to0$ this becomes $\partial_t\rho=\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\,\nabla(U'+V))$.

Reading

The discrete optimality already carries the diffusion equation.

Printed pp. 629-692Optimality of a Single JKO Step
Differentiating a single JKO step gives its optimality condition. The first variation of the Wasserstein penalty produces a Kantorovich potential, whose gradient is the optimal transport velocity. Setting the total variation to zero recovers a discrete continuity equation that, in the limit, becomes the continuous diffusion equation we expect.

The Energy-Dissipation Estimate

Telescoping the steps

$$\sum_{n}\frac{W_2^2(\rho^{n+1},\rho^{n})}{2\tau}\le\mathcal{F}(\rho^0)-\inf\mathcal{F}$$

Monotone energy

Each step lowers $\mathcal{F}$, so the total drop is bounded.

Uniform bound

The discrete curve has controlled length, independent of $\tau$.

Compactness

Bounded length plus tightness yields a convergent subsequence.

Why it matters

This single inequality powers the entire existence proof.

Printed pp. 629-692The Energy-Dissipation Estimate
The scheme comes with a free a-priori bound. Summing the optimality of each step telescopes into an energy-dissipation inequality controlling the total squared distance travelled by the discrete curve. This uniform estimate is exactly the compactness we need to pass to the limit and extract a continuous trajectory.

Existence via the Minimizing-Movement Limit

Interpolate

Glue the iterates into piecewise-constant curves $\rho^\tau_t$.

Pass to the limit

Compactness gives $\rho^\tau\to\rho$ uniformly as $\tau\to0$.

Lower semicontinuity

$\mathcal{F}$ does not jump up under the limit, so estimates survive.

Limit solves

$$\partial_t\rho=\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\rho\,\nabla(U'(\rho)+V)\big)$$

Conclusion

An absolutely continuous curve in $\mathcal{P}_2$ solving the gradient flow exists.

Printed pp. 629-692Existence via the Minimizing-Movement Limit
Compactness plus lower semicontinuity finishes the construction. The piecewise-constant and interpolated JKO curves converge to an absolutely continuous curve in Wasserstein space that solves the gradient-flow equation. Minimizing movements thus prove existence for the heat and Fokker-Planck flows without ever differentiating the equation directly.
