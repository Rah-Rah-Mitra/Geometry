From steepest descent to a flow

The setting

$\mathcal{F}$ is a functional on $(\mathcal{P}_2(M), W_2)$. Its gradient flow is steepest descent in this metric.

The gap

Minimizing-movement schemes give existence but no rates. We need a sharper differential law.

Minimizing movement

$\mu^{\tau}_{n+1}=\arg\min_{\mu}\;\mathcal{F}(\mu)+\tfrac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\mu,\mu^{\tau}_{n})$

Curve of maximal slope

The discrete steps converge, as $\tau\to 0$, to a continuous curve dissipating $\mathcal{F}$ as fast as possible.

We need a sharper differential characterization that survives the lack of smoothness in this metric setting.

$K$-displacement convexity

Along geodesics

Convexity is tested on displacement interpolations $(\mu_s)_{s\in[0,1]}$, not on linear mixtures.

Sign of $K$

$K>0$: a genuinely convex landscape. $K\le 0$: still controlled, with weaker estimates.

Definition

$\mathcal{F}(\mu_s)\le (1-s)\mathcal{F}(\mu_0)+s\,\mathcal{F}(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}s(1-s)\,W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1)$

Printed pp. 693-718K-displacement convexity
The single hypothesis driving every result is lambda-convexity along displacement geodesics. With constant K, the functional lies above each chord by a quadratic correction tuned by the squared Wasserstein distance. Positive K means a genuinely convex landscape; negative K still controls the flow, only with weaker, possibly growing, estimates.

The Evolution Variational Inequality

The statement

For every test measure $\sigma$ and almost every $t$, the flow $(\mu_t)$ obeys the EVI below.

Reading it

Squared distance to $\sigma$ shrinks at a rate set by the functional gap plus the $K$ term.

$\mathrm{EVI}_K$

$\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\,W_2^2(\mu_t,\sigma)\;\le\;\mathcal{F}(\sigma)-\mathcal{F}(\mu_t)-\dfrac{K}{2}\,W_2^2(\mu_t,\sigma)$

All at once

Because it must hold for all $\sigma$, this one inequality encodes the complete qualitative theory.

Printed pp. 693-718The Evolution Variational Inequality
The EVI is the heart of the chapter. It states that along the flow the half time-derivative of squared distance to any test measure is bounded by the functional gap minus a K-quadratic term. This single inequality, holding for all comparison measures, encodes the whole qualitative theory at once.

Why EVI is the right notion

Stronger than slope

An $\mathrm{EVI}_K$ solution is a curve of maximal slope, but the converse needs convexity.

Stable under limits

The inequality passes to weak limits, so it survives approximation and discretization.

Characterizes the flow

A semigroup satisfying $\mathrm{EVI}_K$ is the gradient flow of a $K$-convex $\mathcal{F}$; nothing else qualifies.

Rigidity

$\mathrm{EVI}_K \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{unique semigroup } S_t,\quad S_0=\mathrm{id}$

Printed pp. 693-718Why EVI is the right notion
EVI is stronger than being a curve of maximal slope: it is rigid enough to pin down a unique solution yet flexible enough to pass to limits. A flow satisfying EVI with parameter K is automatically a gradient flow of a K-convex functional, so EVI characterizes the dynamics completely.

Contraction between two solutions

Test each against the other

Write $\mathrm{EVI}_K$ for $\mu_t$ with $\sigma=\nu_t$, and for $\nu_t$ with $\sigma=\mu_t$.

Add and cancel

The functional terms $\mathcal{F}(\mu_t),\mathcal{F}(\nu_t)$ appear with opposite signs and vanish.

Differential inequality

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\,W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\;\le\;-2K\,W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu_t)$

Then Gronwall

Integrating this scalar inequality delivers the exponential contraction bound on the next slide.

Printed pp. 693-718Contraction between two solutions
Apply EVI to each of two solutions, using the other as the test measure, and add. The functional terms cancel by symmetry, leaving a clean differential inequality for the squared distance between the trajectories. Gronwall then yields lambda-contraction: distinct flows can never spread apart faster than the exponential rate allows.

The contraction estimate

Exponential comparison

Distance between any two flows decays (or grows) at the rate fixed by $K$.

Sign reading

$K>0$: trajectories are pulled together. $K<0$: at most controlled separation.

$\lambda$-contraction

$W_2\big(\mu_t,\nu_t\big)\;\le\;e^{-K t}\,W_2\big(\mu_0,\nu_0\big)$

Printed pp. 693-718The contraction estimate
The contraction estimate reads as exponential comparison of the two trajectories: their Wasserstein distance at time t is at most the initial distance times e to the minus K t. When K is positive the two flows are pulled together; when K is negative they may separate, but only at a controlled exponential rate.

Uniqueness and stability

Uniqueness

Put $\mu_0=\nu_0$. Contraction forces $W_2(\mu_t,\nu_t)=0$ for all $t$: one flow per datum.

Stability

Vary the data: the solution map $S_t$ is Lipschitz in $W_2$ with an explicit constant.

Lipschitz semigroup

$W_2\big(S_t\mu_0,\,S_t\nu_0\big)\;\le\;e^{-Kt}\,W_2\big(\mu_0,\nu_0\big)$

Well-posedness

Existence (Chapter 23) plus uniqueness and stability gives a fully well-posed flow.

Printed pp. 693-718Uniqueness and stability
Contraction is not just elegant; it is the workhorse. Setting the initial data equal forces the two solutions to coincide, giving uniqueness. Letting initial data vary makes the solution map Lipschitz in the Wasserstein distance, so the flow depends continuously and stably on its starting measure with an explicit modulus.

Convergence to equilibrium

The minimizer

If $K>0$ then $\mathcal{F}$ is uniformly convex, so it has a unique minimizer $\mu_\infty$.

Fix $\sigma=\mu_\infty$

The equilibrium is stationary; using it as test measure converts contraction into decay.

Exponential trend at rate $K$

$W_2(\mu_t,\mu_\infty)\le e^{-Kt}W_2(\mu_0,\mu_\infty),\quad \mathcal{F}(\mu_t)-\mathcal{F}(\mu_\infty)\le e^{-2Kt}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_0)-\mathcal{F}(\mu_\infty)\big)$

Both levels decay

Distance falls like $e^{-Kt}$; the functional gap falls twice as fast, like $e^{-2Kt}$.

Printed pp. 693-718Convergence to equilibrium
When K is strictly positive the functional has a unique minimizer, the equilibrium measure. Taking that minimizer as the fixed test point in EVI turns contraction into exponential convergence: every flow relaxes to equilibrium in Wasserstein distance at rate K, with a matching exponential decay of the functional toward its minimum.

Synthesis and outlook

The logical chain

$K$-displacement convexity $\Rightarrow$ $\mathrm{EVI}_K$ $\Rightarrow$ contraction $\Rightarrow$ everything else.

What you get

Uniqueness, Lipschitz stability, and exponential equilibration at the explicit rate $K$.

$\mathrm{EVI}_K \;\Rightarrow\; W_2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\le e^{-Kt}W_2(\mu_0,\nu_0)$
