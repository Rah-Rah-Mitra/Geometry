Tightness Keeps Plans Compact

Marginals are tight

Weakly convergent marginals are uniformly tight by Prokhorov's theorem.

Plans inherit tightness

A coupling lives over the product; tight marginals force a tight family of plans.

Compactness

$$\pi_n\in\Pi(\mu_n,\nu_n)\ \Rightarrow\ \pi_{n_k}\rightharpoonup\pi_\infty$$

Subsequence limit

Every optimal sequence has a weak limit that is again a valid coupling.

Printed pp. 773-794Tightness Keeps Plans Compact
Convergent marginals are tight, and tightness of marginals forces tightness of the couplings that join them. By Prokhorov, any sequence of optimal plans then has a weakly convergent subsequence whose limit is again a coupling of the limiting marginals. Compactness is the first pillar of every stability proof.

Lower Semicontinuity of Cost

Cost hypothesis

The cost $c$ is lower semicontinuous and bounded below.

Functional inherits it

Then $\pi\mapsto\int c\,d\pi$ is lower semicontinuous for weak convergence.

liminf inequality

$$\int c\,d\pi_\infty\ \le\ \liminf_{n}\int c_n\,d\pi_n$$

Reading

The limit plan never costs more than the limit of the costs.

Printed pp. 773-794Lower Semicontinuity of Cost
A lower semicontinuous, bounded below cost makes the total transport functional lower semicontinuous under weak convergence of plans. So the limit plan cannot cost more than the limit of the costs. Pairing this with compactness lets us pass to the limit inside the Kantorovich minimization safely.

The Limit Plan Is Optimal

Admissible

Tightness puts $\pi_\infty\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)$.

Lower bound

liminf gives cost below the limiting infimum.

Upper bound

A recovery sequence matches the infimum from above.

Stability theorem

$$\pi_\infty\ \text{optimal},\qquad C(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to C(\mu,\nu)$$

Printed pp. 773-794The Limit Plan Is Optimal
Combine the two pillars. A limit of optimal plans is admissible by tightness, and lower semicontinuity bounds its cost below the limiting infimum. A recovery-sequence argument supplies the matching upper bound, so the limit is optimal and the costs converge. This is the central stability theorem of the chapter.

The Gamma-Convergence Frame

liminf condition

Every recovering family bounds the limit functional from below.

limsup condition

A recovery sequence attains the limit value from above.

Gamma-convergence

$$C_n\ \xrightarrow{\ \Gamma\ }\ C\ \Rightarrow\ \min C_n\to\min C$$

Payoff

Minimizers and minimum values pass to the limit by construction.

Printed pp. 773-794Gamma-Convergence Frame
The two inequalities are exactly the liminf and limsup conditions of Gamma-convergence. Reading transport functionals as a Gamma-converging family explains why minimizers and minimum values pass to the limit automatically. It is the cleanest language for stating that optimal transport is stable under perturbation of its data.
