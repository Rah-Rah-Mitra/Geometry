Wasserstein Space as a Manifold

Points

Each density $\rho$ with finite second moment is one point of the space.

Curves

A path $t \mapsto \rho_t$ is a continuous redistribution of unit mass.

Goal

Length of the shortest curve should equal $W_2(\rho_0,\rho_1)$.

Configuration space

$$P_2(M) = \Big\{ \rho \geq 0 : \int \rho = 1,\ \int |x|^2 \rho < \infty \Big\}$$

Benamou–Brenier

$W_2$ is rewritten as a least-action problem over flowing densities — the seed of the metric.

Printed pp. 421-434Wasserstein Space as a Manifold
We regard P two of M, the densities of finite second moment, as the configuration space. A point is an entire density rho, and a curve is a flow of mass. The aim is to equip this space with a metric so that the Wasserstein distance becomes the length of the shortest path.

Tangent Vectors via the Continuity Equation

Mass is conserved

An admissible perturbation $\dot\rho$ must be a flux divergence, never a source.

Continuity equation

$$\partial_t \rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\, v) = 0$$

Identification

The tangent vector is the velocity field $v$, not the bare $\dot\rho$.

Printed pp. 421-434Tangent Vectors via Continuity
An infinitesimal change of a density is constrained: mass is conserved. The continuity equation records that any admissible perturbation is the divergence of a flux rho v. So a tangent vector at rho is identified with the velocity field v that pushes the mass, not with the raw time derivative of rho.

Velocity Fields Are Gradients of Potentials

Many fields, one change

Adding a divergence-free field to $v$ leaves $\dot\rho$ unchanged but costs energy.

Minimal representative

The cheapest $v$ is curl-free: a pure gradient $v = \nabla\phi$.

Coordinates

Tangent vectors $\leftrightarrow$ potentials $\phi$, taken modulo constants.

Helmholtz selection

$$v = \nabla\phi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\,\nabla\phi) = 0$$

Elliptic link

$$\dot\rho = -\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\,\nabla\phi)$$
solves for $\phi$ given $\dot\rho$ — a weighted Laplace equation.

Printed pp. 421-434Gradient Potentials
Among all velocity fields realizing a given change of density, the most efficient one is a gradient of a potential. This Helmholtz selection picks the minimal kinetic energy representative, so tangent vectors are parametrized by potentials phi with v equal to grad phi, modulo additive constants.

The Otto Metric

Kinetic energy

The norm of a tangent vector is the kinetic energy of the flow carrying it.

Metric tensor

$$\|\dot\rho\|_\rho^2 = \int_M \rho\,|\nabla\phi|^2\, dx$$

Inner product

$\langle \dot\rho_1,\dot\rho_2\rangle_\rho = \int \rho\,\nabla\phi_1\!\cdot\!\nabla\phi_2$.

Printed pp. 421-434The Otto Metric
The Otto metric measures a tangent vector by the kinetic energy of the flow that carries it. Pairing two potentials integrates the product of their gradients weighted by rho. This is precisely the Riemannian tensor whose geodesic distance equals the quadratic Wasserstein distance W two.

Geodesics Are Displacement Interpolations

Shortest paths

$W_2$ geodesics minimize the integrated Otto kinetic energy.

McCann interpolation

Mass flows along straight transport rays at constant speed.

Action equals distance

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^1\!\!\int \rho_t|\nabla\phi_t|^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}W_2^2$$

Printed pp. 421-434Geodesics Are Displacement Interpolation
With the Otto metric in place, the shortest paths between two densities are the displacement interpolations of McCann. Mass travels along transport geodesics at constant speed, and the energy of that path equals one half W two squared, certifying that the formal metric reproduces optimal transport.

The Entropy Functional on $P_2(M)$

Boltzmann entropy

The functional $H(\rho)$ is the chapter's distinguished height function.

Definition

$$H(\rho) = \int_M \rho \log \rho \; dx$$

Why entropy

Its gradient and Hessian carry all the analytic information we want.

The Boltzmann entropy, the integral of rho log rho, is the central functional living on the Wasserstein manifold. We study it as a height function whose landscape, read through the Otto metric, governs how densities relax. Its gradient and Hessian carry the chapter's analytic payload.

Heat Equation = Gradient Flow of Entropy

Steepest descent

Follow $-\,\mathrm{grad}_{W_2} H$ in the Otto geometry.

Wasserstein gradient

$$\mathrm{grad}_{W_2} H(\rho) = -\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\rho\,\nabla \log\rho\big)$$

Collapses to heat

$$\partial_t \rho = \Delta\rho$$

The computation

Since $\rho\,\nabla\log\rho = \nabla\rho$, the continuity equation with $v=\nabla\log\rho$ is exactly diffusion.

Reading

Heat flow is entropy dissipation: the density relaxes by descending $H$ as fast as the metric allows.

JKO scheme

Implicit Euler steps in $W_2$ discretize this flow — minimizing movements.

Printed pp. 421-434Heat Equation as Gradient Flow
Otto's key discovery is that the steepest descent of entropy in the Wasserstein geometry is exactly the heat equation. The gradient of entropy points along grad log rho, and the induced continuity flow collapses to the diffusion equation, identifying heat flow with entropy dissipation.

Computing Gradients and Hessians

Differentiate along geodesics

Evaluate $\tfrac{d^2}{dt^2}H(\rho_t)$ on a displacement interpolation.

Hessian of entropy

$$\mathrm{Hess}\,H = \int \rho\Big(\|\nabla^2\phi\|^2 + \mathrm{Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\Big)$$

Bochner

The first term is non-negative; the second is curvature.

Printed pp. 421-434Gradients and Hessians
The formalism gives recipes for the gradient and Hessian of functionals on P two. Differentiating along transport geodesics, the second derivative of entropy combines a Bochner term with the Ricci curvature of the base manifold. This is the engine that converts geometry into functional inequalities.

Displacement Convexity Becomes Curvature

Equivalence

$H$ is $K$-displacement-convex along $W_2$ geodesics iff $\mathrm{Ric}\geq K$.

Convexity inequality

$$H(\rho_t) \leq (1-t)H(\rho_0) + t\,H(\rho_1) - \tfrac{K}{2}t(1-t)W_2^2$$

One Hessian, many corollaries

The curvature term drives the estimates.

Functional inequalities

Log-Sobolev, Talagrand transport, and HWI inequalities all descend from $K$-convexity.

Contraction

The heat semigroup contracts $W_2$ at rate $K$: $W_2(\rho_t,\sigma_t)\leq e^{-Kt}W_2(\rho_0,\sigma_0)$.

Synthetic Ricci

This equivalence even defines curvature bounds on non-smooth metric measure spaces.

Printed pp. 421-434Convexity Becomes Curvature
Displacement convexity of entropy along Wasserstein geodesics is equivalent to a lower Ricci bound on the underlying space. The Hessian computation makes this precise, so convexity of a functional encodes curvature, and from it flow logarithmic Sobolev, Talagrand, and contraction estimates.

Recap: Otto's Dictionary

tangent vector $\leftrightarrow$ potential $\phi$, $v=\nabla\phi$
metric $\leftrightarrow$ $\int \rho|\nabla\phi|^2$

What we gained

A formal Riemannian calculus on $P_2(M)$ that recovers $W_2$ and turns PDE into geometry.

What it powers

Functional inequalities, gradient-flow PDE, and synthetic Ricci bounds.

Caveat

The structure is formal: rigor needs the metric-space and JKO machinery developed later.
