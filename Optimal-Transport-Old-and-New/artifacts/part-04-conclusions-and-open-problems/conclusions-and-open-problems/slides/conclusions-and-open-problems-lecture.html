Couplings and Kantorovich Duality

Relax maps to plans

Allow mass to split; existence is restored as a linear program.

Duality

Cost equals a supremum over potentials with a cost-constraint.

Kantorovich problem

$$\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)} \int c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)$$

Dual

$$\sup_{\varphi\oplus\psi\le c}\ \int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu$$

Printed pp. 903-914Pillar One: Couplings and Kantorovich
Everything starts with the relaxation from maps to plans. Allowing mass to split makes the problem a linear program with a clean dual, restoring existence where Monge's formulation failed. Kantorovich duality turns transport cost into a supremum over potential functions, the device reused throughout the book to prove sharp statements.
Optimal Transport: Old and NewPillar 2

Wasserstein Geometry

A metric on measures

Quadratic cost yields a distance on $P_2(X)$.

Benamou-Brenier

Distance as least action of a flow.

Geodesics

Displacement interpolation moves mass along straight lines.

Printed pp. 903-914Pillar Two: Wasserstein Geometry
The optimal cost with quadratic price defines a genuine distance on probability measures with finite second moment. Benamou and Brenier recast it as a least-action problem, so the metric space carries geodesics, a formal Riemannian structure, and a notion of velocity field. This is the stage on which the remaining pillars are erected.
Optimal Transport: Old and NewPillar 3

Synthetic Ricci Curvature

Curvature without smoothness

A lower Ricci bound becomes convexity of entropy along geodesics.

$\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$

Defined on any metric measure space, stable under limits.

Displacement convexity

$$\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_t)\le (1-t)\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_0)+t\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}t(1-t)\,W_2^2$$

Why it matters

Curvature survives in singular spaces the classical tensor cannot reach.

Printed pp. 903-914Pillar Three: Synthetic Ricci Curvature
Lott, Sturm and Villani encode a lower Ricci bound as convexity of entropy along Wasserstein geodesics. The curvature-dimension condition needs no smooth manifold, only a metric measure space, so curvature survives limits and singular spaces. This is the book's deepest structural payoff and the seed of much current research.

Diffusions as Gradient Flows

Steepest descent

The heat equation is the gradient flow of entropy in $W_2$.

Minimizing movements

The JKO scheme makes the descent rigorous step by step.

JKO step

$$\mu^{n+1}=\arg\min_{\mu}\ \mathcal{F}(\mu)+\frac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\mu,\mu^{n})$$

Reach

Heat, Fokker-Planck and porous-medium flows all fit this template.

Printed pp. 903-914Pillar Four: Gradient Flows
Many diffusion equations are steepest descent of an energy in Wasserstein geometry. The heat equation descends entropy; porous-medium and Fokker-Planck flows descend their own functionals. The minimizing-movement scheme makes this rigorous, giving existence, contraction estimates, and a variational reading of irreversible dynamics.

Displacement convexity converts geometric curvature bounds into analytic inequalities. Log-Sobolev, Talagrand transport-entropy, Brunn-Minkowski and isoperimetric statements all emerge from one convexity principle along geodesics. The chapter highlights this unification: a single geometric input produces a whole family of sharp analytic outputs.

Sharp Regularity and the MTW Tensor

When is the map smooth?

Brenier gives existence; smoothness is far more delicate.

Ma-Trudinger-Wang

A fourth-order tensor of the cost controls regularity by its sign.

On manifolds

Smoothness ties to the geometry of cut loci.

MTW condition (schematic)

$$\mathfrak{S}(x,p)(\xi,\eta)\ge 0\ \text{ for }\ \xi\perp\eta$$

Still open

Sharp thresholds and regularity for general costs remain unresolved.

Printed pp. 903-914Open Frontier: Sharp Regularity and MTW
Smoothness of the optimal map depends delicately on the cost. Ma, Trudinger and Wang isolated a fourth-order curvature tensor of the cost whose sign governs regularity. On manifolds it ties transport smoothness to the geometry of cut loci, leaving sharp thresholds and general cost regularity wide open.
Optimal Transport: Old and NewFrontier 2

RCD: Adding Infinitesimal Hilbertianity

$\mathrm{CD}$ is too broad

It admits Finsler spaces with a nonlinear heat flow.

The refinement

Demand the heat flow be linear: infinitesimal Hilbertianity.

Result

$\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)$ singles out the Riemannian-like spaces.

Definition sketch

$$\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)=\mathrm{CD}(K,N)\ +\ \text{linear heat flow}$$

Open

Structure theory, stability, and the branching vs non-branching boundary.

Printed pp. 903-914Open Frontier: RCD and Hilbertian Refinement
The curvature-dimension condition allows Finsler behaviour, which the heat flow should not see. Adding infinitesimal Hilbertianity, that the heat flow is linear, singles out the Riemannian-like RCD spaces. Stability, structure theory, and the boundary between branching and non-branching geometry remain very active questions.

$$\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\ \langle C,\pi\rangle+\varepsilon\sum_{ij}\pi_{ij}\log\pi_{ij}$$

Open

Printed pp. 903-914Open Frontier: Numerics and Applications
The analytic theory leaves computation underdeveloped. Entropic regularization and Sinkhorn iteration made large transport problems tractable and connect to machine learning, imaging, and economics. Sharp rates, debiasing, scaling to high dimension, and faithful discretization of curvature and gradient flows form a fast-moving applied frontier.
