Two definitions of a lower Ricci bound — and why we want the second

Analytic

A tensor differentiated from a smooth metric. Local, computable, but fragile.

Synthetic

Convexity of entropy along transport. Coordinate-free and stable under limits.

This chapter opens Part III by contrasting two readings of lower Ricci bounds. The analytic reading lives on smooth manifolds and computes a tensor in coordinates. The synthetic reading lives on any metric-measure space and tests convexity of entropy. The whole part shows the two agree where both apply.

The Analytic Definition

From the connection

Contract Riemann curvature to get the Ricci tensor.

The bound

Dominate the metric pointwise by a constant $K$.

Ricci lower bound

$$\mathrm{Ric}_x(v,v)\;\ge\;K\,g_x(v,v)\quad \forall\,x\in M,\ v\in T_xM$$

Prerequisite

The metric $g$ must be at least $C^2$ to differentiate.

Printed pp. 735-742The Analytic Definition
Analytically the Ricci tensor is built from the Levi-Civita connection by contracting the Riemann curvature. A lower bound asks that this symmetric tensor dominate K times the metric at every point. The definition is local, coordinate-dependent, and demands the metric be at least twice differentiable to even write down.

What the Analytic View Buys

Checkable

A pointwise tensor: verify it by differentiation in any chart.

Powerful machinery

Bochner formula, Laplacian and volume comparison, Bonnet–Myers.

Bochner identity

$$\tfrac12\Delta|\nabla f|^2=|\nabla^2 f|^2+\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta f\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)$$

The cost

Every formula presupposes smoothness; nothing survives a singular limit.

Printed pp. 735-742What the Analytic View Buys
Because the Ricci tensor is a pointwise object it is checkable by differentiation and feeds directly into Bochner formulas, comparison theorems, and volume bounds. These are the classical tools of Riemannian geometry. The cost is rigidity: the formula presupposes smoothness and says nothing about singular or limiting spaces.

Where Smoothness Fails

Limits leave the category

Manifolds with $\mathrm{Ric}\ge K$ can converge to non-manifolds.

Singular limits

Cones, collapsed and weighted spaces have no chart at every point.

Printed pp. 735-742Where Smoothness Fails
Sequences of manifolds with a fixed Ricci bound can converge in Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a limit that is no longer a manifold. Cones, collapsed spaces, and weighted spaces appear. The analytic tensor cannot be evaluated on such a limit, so a bound expressed only through derivatives simply does not survive the passage.

The Synthetic Stage: Metric-Measure Spaces

The data

A complete separable metric space with a reference measure — no charts, no tensor.

The arena

Pass to the Wasserstein space $P_2(X)$ of probability measures.

Object

$$(X,d,\nu)\ \rightsquigarrow\ \big(P_2(X),\,W_2\big)$$

Why transport

$W_2$-geodesics give an intrinsic notion of "moving mass" without coordinates.

Printed pp. 735-742Metric-Measure Spaces
The synthetic stage is a metric-measure space: a complete separable metric space carrying a reference measure. No charts, no tensor, no differentiability are assumed. The Wasserstein space of probability measures over this base becomes the arena where curvature is reformulated through the geometry of optimal transport.

Entropy: the Central Observable

Boltzmann entropy

Integrate the density against its logarithm, relative to $\nu$.

Filter

If $\mu$ has no density w.r.t. $\nu$, set $H=+\infty$.

Relative entropy

$$H_\nu(\mu)=\int_X \rho\,\log\rho\ \,d\nu,\qquad \mu=\rho\,\nu$$

The idea

How $H$ behaves along a geodesic will encode curvature.

Printed pp. 735-742Entropy on Wasserstein Space
The Boltzmann entropy functional, the integral of the density times its logarithm against the reference measure, is the central observable. Its behaviour along Wasserstein geodesics encodes curvature. Where the measure has no density relative to the reference, the entropy is set to plus infinity, which already filters admissible interpolations.

Displacement Convexity Is the Bound

The definition

$(X,d,\nu)$ has Ricci $\ge K$ when $H_\nu$ is $K$-convex along every $W_2$-geodesic.

Tensor → functional

Positivity of a tensor is replaced by convexity of one functional.

K-convexity along $(\mu_t)$

$$H_\nu(\mu_t)\le (1-t)H_\nu(\mu_0)+t\,H_\nu(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}t(1-t)W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2$$

Coordinate-free

Only distances and measures appear — valid verbatim on any $(X,d,\nu)$.

Printed pp. 735-742Displacement Convexity Is the Bound
The synthetic definition declares a lower Ricci bound K by requiring the entropy to be K-convex along every Wasserstein geodesic. Convexity of one functional replaces positivity of a tensor. The inequality involves only distances and measures, so it makes sense verbatim on any metric-measure space.

The Two Views Agree on Smooth Spaces

Bridge

The Hessian of entropy along transport is a Ricci integral — via Bochner.

Equivalence

$K$-displacement convexity $\iff \mathrm{Ric}\ge K g$ on a weighted manifold.

Otto–Villani / Cordero–McCann–Schmuckenschläger

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H_\nu(\mu_t)\;=\;\int \mathrm{Ric}_N\big(\nabla\varphi_t\big)\,d\mu_t\;\ge\;K\,W_2^2$$

Anchor

The synthetic test reproduces the analytic one with no loss.

Printed pp. 735-742The Two Views Agree
On a smooth weighted manifold the Bochner identity converts the Hessian of entropy along transport into a Ricci integral. So K-displacement convexity of entropy holds exactly when the Ricci tensor dominates K times the metric. The synthetic test reproduces the analytic one with no loss, anchoring the new definition.

Stability: the Decisive Advantage

Closed condition

The inequality uses only $d$ and $\nu$ — quantities that pass to limits.

Consequence

If $X_n\to X$ in measured Gromov–Hausdorff and each $X_n$ has Ric $\ge K$, so does $X$.

Printed pp. 735-742Stability Under Limits
The synthetic inequality is phrased in distances and entropies, quantities that pass to Gromov-Hausdorff and measured limits. So a lower bound that holds along a sequence holds in the limit. This is the decisive advantage: the curvature condition is closed, exactly the property the analytic tensor lacked.

Lab: Hessian Samples vs Chord Inequalities

Analytic test

Finite-difference Hessian: unstable at a kink, where $f''$ does not exist.

Synthetic test

Chord inequality: midpoint below the average of endpoints — still valid.

Printed pp. 735-742Analytic Hessian vs Synthetic Chords
The notebook's lab makes the contrast tactile. A finite-difference Hessian of a convex profile becomes unstable at a kink, while the chord inequality, comparing a midpoint value to the average of endpoints, stays valid. Checkability and stability are different virtues; the synthetic chord test keeps the second one.

Lineage: Alexandrov to Lott–Sturm–Villani

Alexandrov geometry

Sectional bounds via triangle comparison, not a curvature tensor.

Same philosophy

Replace a differentiated object by an inequality between distances.

Triangle comparison (curvature $\ge 0$)

$$d(p,m)^2\ \ge\ \tfrac12 d(p,x)^2+\tfrac12 d(p,y)^2-\tfrac14 d(x,y)^2$$

The translation

Lott–Villani and Sturm carry this to Ricci via entropy: the CD$(K,N)$ spaces.

Printed pp. 735-742Alexandrov and the Lineage
The move from tensors to chord inequalities echoes Alexandrov geometry, where sectional curvature bounds are encoded by triangle comparison rather than by a curvature tensor. Lott, Villani, Sturm carry the same philosophy to Ricci bounds via entropy convexity, giving the CD spaces studied through the rest of the part.
