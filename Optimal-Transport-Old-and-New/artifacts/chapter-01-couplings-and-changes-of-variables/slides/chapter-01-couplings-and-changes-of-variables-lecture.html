What Is a Coupling

Definition

A measure $\pi$ on $X\times Y$ whose two projections return $\mu$ and $\nu$.

Always exists

The product $\mu\otimes\nu$ is a coupling, so the set is never empty.

Marginal conditions

$(\mathrm{proj}_X)\#\pi=\mu,\quad (\mathrm{proj}_Y)\#\pi=\nu$

In sets

$\pi(A\times Y)=\mu(A),\ \ \pi(X\times B)=\nu(B)$

Set of couplings

Write $\Pi(\mu,\nu)$ for all such joint laws; it is convex.

Printed pp. 5-20What Is a Coupling
A coupling glues two measures into a joint law whose projections recover each one. Stress that couplings always exist because the product measure is one, so the coupling set is never empty. This non-emptiness is exactly what makes the Kantorovich relaxation a well-posed minimization rather than a possibly infeasible problem.

Marginals and Transport Plans

Plan reading

$\pi(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)$ is mass shipped from near $x$ to near $y$.

Conservation

Row sums give $\mu$, column sums give $\nu$.

Printed pp. 5-20Marginals and Transport Plans
Here the coupling becomes a transport plan: pi of dx dy is how much mass leaves a region of x and arrives in a region of y. The marginal constraints are conservation laws, total mass out equals mu, total mass in equals nu. The heatmap with edge histograms is the picture students should carry forward.

Three Classic Couplings

Same margins, very different joint structure

Independent

$\mu\otimes\nu$: no correlation, mass spread everywhere.

Deterministic

Supported on a graph $\{(x,T(x))\}$, no splitting.

Monotone

On $\mathbb{R}$: sort mass, push small to small.

Printed pp. 5-20Three Classic Couplings
Survey the canonical couplings before formalizing any one. The independent coupling spreads mass with no correlation; the deterministic coupling concentrates all mass on a graph; the monotone coupling on the line sorts mass increasingly. Each answers the same marginal constraints differently, which is the whole point of studying the coupling set.

The Independent Coupling

Product measure

$X$ and $Y$ drawn independently; knowing one says nothing about the other.

Maximum entropy

The most disordered coupling, the baseline of zero structure.

Density factorizes

$\pi(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)=\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}y)$

Printed pp. 5-20The Independent Coupling
The product measure is the maximum-entropy coupling: knowing x tells you nothing about y. Emphasize it as the baseline of zero structure against which every optimal coupling is a departure. Its density factorizes, so the joint heatmap is an outer product of the two marginal profiles, visibly rank one.

Deterministic Couplings and Maps

On a graph

Mass at $x$ all goes to $T(x)$; the coupling sits on $\{(x,T(x))\}$.

Monge picture

No splitting of mass: each source point has one destination.

Caveat

A map need not exist; e.g. a Dirac cannot map onto two atoms.

Deterministic coupling

$\pi=(\mathrm{Id}\times T)\#\,\mu,\qquad T\#\mu=\nu$

Printed pp. 5-20Deterministic Couplings and Maps
A deterministic coupling lives on the graph of a map T, so randomness in y is entirely determined by x. This is the Monge picture: no splitting of mass. Make clear that not every pair of measures admits such a map, which is precisely why Kantorovich relaxed maps to plans.

Pushforward and Change of Variables

Pushforward

Mass of a target set equals source mass of its preimage.

Density rule

For smooth invertible $T$, densities relate through the Jacobian.

Pushforward (set form)

$T\#\mu(B)=\mu\big(T^{-1}(B)\big)$

Change of variables

$f(x)=g\big(T(x)\big)\,\big|\det \nabla T(x)\big|$

Why the determinant

$|\det\nabla T|$ is the local volume distortion of $T$.

Printed pp. 5-20Pushforward and Change of Variables
The pushforward records where T sends mass: the measure of a target set equals the source measure of its preimage. For densities this becomes the change-of-variables formula with a Jacobian factor. Walk through why the determinant appears, it is the local volume distortion of the map.

The Monge-Ampere Relation

Gradient maps

When $T=\nabla\varphi$ with $\varphi$ convex, the rule becomes a PDE.

Heart of the theory

This nonlinear equation underlies Brenier's theorem ahead.

Monge-Ampere equation

$g\big(\nabla\varphi(x)\big)\,\det\!\big(\nabla^2\varphi(x)\big)=f(x)$

Printed pp. 5-20The Monge-Ampere Relation
When the transport map is a gradient of a convex function, the density constraint becomes a Monge-Ampere equation: the Hessian determinant balances the source and target densities. Preview that this nonlinear PDE is the analytic heart of Brenier's theorem, met later, and explain each symbol in the ratio.

Monotone Coupling on the Line

Quantile maps

Push the source quantile to the target quantile.

Comonotone

The unique increasing rearrangement of $\mu$ onto $\nu$.

Optimal

Best coupling for every convex cost on $\mathbb{R}$.

Increasing rearrangement

$T=F_\nu^{-1}\circ F_\mu,\qquad T\#\mu=\nu$

Printed pp. 5-20Monotone Coupling on the Line
On the real line the optimal coupling is explicit: push quantiles to quantiles. The increasing rearrangement T equals the target quantile composed with the source distribution function. Emphasize this is the comonotone coupling and it is optimal for every convex cost, a rare closed form we will reuse constantly.

Knothe-Rosenblatt Coupling

Coordinate by coordinate

Apply the 1-D monotone rule to each variable in turn.

Triangular

Each $T_k$ depends only on $x_1,\dots,x_k$.

Constructive

Explicit and always exists, but not cost-optimal in general.

Triangular structure

$T_k(x)=T_k(x_1,\dots,x_k),\quad k=1,\dots,n$

Printed pp. 5-20Knothe-Rosenblatt Coupling
In higher dimensions the Knothe-Rosenblatt coupling builds a map coordinate by coordinate, conditioning on previous coordinates and applying the one-dimensional monotone rule at each step. It is triangular and explicit but not optimal in general. It is a constructive existence tool and a degeneration limit of optimal maps.

The Moriarty Coupling

The villain

A coupling engineered to be as costly as possible.

Lesson

Coupling alone imposes no structure; optimization does.

Same margins

It satisfies $\Pi(\mu,\nu)$ exactly like the good couplings.

Printed pp. 5-20The Moriarty Coupling
The Moriarty coupling is Villani's cautionary villain: a coupling engineered to be as bad as possible, spreading mass to maximize cost. It exists for the same reason the good ones do. Use it to stress that the coupling set is large and that optimization, not mere coupling, is what selects structure.

Gluing and Recap

Gluing lemma

Two couplings sharing a middle marginal glue into a joint law on the triple.

Glued law

$\pi_{12}\in\Pi(\mu_1,\mu_2),\ \pi_{23}\in\Pi(\mu_2,\mu_3)\Rightarrow \pi_{123}$

Payoff

Drives the triangle inequality for Wasserstein distance.

Printed pp. 5-20Gluing and Recap
Close with the gluing lemma: two couplings sharing a middle marginal can be glued into a joint law on the triple, the device behind the triangle inequality for Wasserstein distance. Then recap the ladder from coupling to plan to map to Jacobian that this chapter has built.
