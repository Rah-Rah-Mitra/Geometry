The Monge-Kantorovich Problem

Couplings

Plans $\pi$ on $X\times Y$ with marginals $\mu$ and $\nu$ form a convex, nonempty set.

Relaxation

Mass may split, so a minimizer always exists where a Monge map might not.

Why it matters

Linearity in $\pi$ is exactly what enables a clean duality theory.

Primal cost

$$\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times Y} c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)$$

Marginal constraints

$$(\mathrm{proj}_X)_\#\pi=\mu,\quad (\mathrm{proj}_Y)_\#\pi=\nu$$

Printed pp. 51-92The Monge-Kantorovich Problem
Recall the relaxed problem: minimize the average cost over couplings whose marginals are the prescribed source and target measures. The plan may split mass, so the feasible set is convex and never empty. This linear structure is exactly what makes a clean duality theory possible, and we exploit it throughout the chapter.

c-Cyclical Monotonicity

Definition

$$\sum_{i=1}^{N} c(x_i,y_i)\;\le\;\sum_{i=1}^{N} c(x_i,y_{\sigma(i)})$$

for every $N$ and every permutation $\sigma$ of the matched targets.

Reading

No finite cyclic reshuffle of matched pairs can lower total cost.

Combinatorial heart

The condition is local to the matched set, independent of densities.

Certificate of failure

Find one improving cycle and the plan cannot be optimal.

Printed pp. 51-92c-Cyclical Monotonicity
A set is c-cyclically monotone when no finite cyclic permutation of its matched targets lowers total cost. Spell out the inequality with students: sum the costs along the original pairs, compare to any reshuffle around a cycle, and the original must win. This is the combinatorial heart of optimality.

Support of an Optimal Plan

Theorem

If $c$ is continuous and the cost is finite, the support of any optimal $\pi$ is $c$-cyclically monotone.

Local from global

A global minimization becomes a pointwise, checkable geometric condition.

Support-level

The statement is about where mass may travel, not about densities.

Converse

Under mild assumptions, c-cyclical monotonicity of the support also implies optimality.

Kantorovich Duality

Duality theorem

$$\inf_{\pi}\int c\,d\pi=\sup_{\varphi+\psi\le c}\Big(\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu\Big)$$

No duality gap

The primal infimum equals the dual supremum exactly.

Potentials and the No-Arbitrage Bound

Admissibility

$$\varphi(x)+\psi(y)\le c(x,y)\quad\forall\,(x,y)$$

Weak duality

Any feasible pair yields a lower bound on the transport cost.

Bound

$$\int\varphi\,d\mu+\int\psi\,d\nu\le\int c\,d\pi$$

Shipping intuition

$\varphi$ is paid at the origin, $\psi$ at the destination; the ceiling forbids arbitrage.

The c-Transform and c-Concavity

c-transform

$$\psi^{c}(x)=\inf_{y}\big(c(x,y)-\psi(y)\big)$$

Best partner

Given $\psi$, the optimal compatible $\varphi$ is exactly $\psi^{c}$.

c-concave

$$\varphi=\varphi^{cc}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\varphi \text{ is }c\text{-concave}$$

Single variable

Pointwise optimization is automatic, so the dual searches over one c-concave function.

Printed pp. 51-92The c-Transform
Define the c-transform: given psi, the best compatible phi is the infimum over y of c minus psi. Iterating once more yields a c-concave function, and the pair stabilizes. Explain that optimizing one potential pointwise against the other is automatic, so the dual really searches over a single c-concave function.

Complementary Slackness

On the support

At the optimum the inequality phi plus psi at most c becomes equality exactly on the support of the optimal plan. This is complementary slackness: active transported pairs carry zero dual slack, inactive pairs carry nonnegative slack. The notebook makes this visible as a slack matrix whose zeros mark the optimal edges.

Kantorovich Potentials

Maximizers

A pair $(\varphi,\psi)$ attaining the dual supremum is a pair of Kantorovich potentials.

Uniqueness

Unique up to an additive constant on a connected support.

Equality set

$$\{\varphi(x)+\psi(y)=c(x,y)\}=\operatorname{supp}\pi$$

Geometry encoded

The potentials' equality set tells the plan exactly where mass may move.

Printed pp. 51-92Kantorovich Potentials
A maximizing pair is called Kantorovich potentials. They are the prices realizing the duality, unique up to an additive constant on connected supports. Tie them to slide nine: the equality set of the potentials is precisely where mass is allowed to travel, so the potentials encode the optimal plan's geometry directly.
