From Transport Cost to Distance

Kantorovich problem

Minimize expected cost over couplings $\pi$ sharing the marginals $\mu,\nu$.

What is missing

A bare cost gives no symmetry and no additivity along chains.

The fix

Pick cost $=d(x,y)^p$. Metric axioms then follow from the metric on $X$.

Coupling set

$$\Pi(\mu,\nu)=\{\pi:\ (\mathrm{proj}_x)_\#\pi=\mu,\ (\mathrm{proj}_y)_\#\pi=\nu\}$$

Optimal cost

$$C(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X} c(x,y)\,d\pi$$

Printed pp. 93-112From Transport Cost to Distance
Recall that the Kantorovich problem minimizes expected cost over couplings sharing the prescribed marginals. Stress that nothing yet makes the optimal value symmetric or additive; the Wasserstein construction is exactly the choice of cost that forces those metric properties to hold.

Definition of the Wasserstein Distance

Definition

$$W_p(\mu,\nu)=\Big(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X} d(x,y)^p\,d\pi\Big)^{1/p}$$

Why the root

The $1/p$ power restores length units, so $W_p$ scales like a distance, not an energy.

Special cases

$W_1$ rewards short moves; $W_2$ penalizes long moves quadratically.

Printed pp. 93-112Definition of W_p
Give the definition slowly. The cost is the ground distance raised to power p, we minimize its integral over couplings, then take a p-th root to restore the right units. Emphasize that the root is what makes W_p scale like a length rather than like an energy.

The Space of Finite p-th Moment

Why restrict

Without a finite $p$-th moment the infimum can be $+\infty$, so $W_p$ is not a distance.

Base point free

Finiteness of the moment does not depend on which base point $x_0$ is chosen.

Reading

Moments are part of the space, not a technical afterthought.

Definition

$$P_p(X)=\Big\{\mu:\ \int_X d(x_0,x)^p\,d\mu(x)<\infty\Big\}$$

Finite distance

$$W_p(\mu,\nu)<\infty\quad\text{for all }\mu,\nu\in P_p(X)$$

Printed pp. 93-112The Space P_p(X)
Explain why we must restrict the universe of measures. Without a finite p-th moment the infimum can be infinite, so W_p lives on the set of measures whose p-th moment against a base point converges. Note that finiteness is independent of which base point we pick.

Symmetry and Separation

Nonnegative

$d^p\ge 0$, so the infimum and its root are nonnegative.

Symmetric

Swapping the two coordinates of $\pi$ swaps the marginals: $W_p(\mu,\nu)=W_p(\nu,\mu)$.

Separation

$W_p(\mu,\nu)=0$ forces $\mu=\nu$; the diagonal coupling is the only zero-cost one.

Identity of indiscernibles

$$W_p(\mu,\nu)=0\iff \mu=\nu$$

Zero attained by the diagonal

$$\mu=\nu\ \Rightarrow\ \pi=(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})_\#\mu,\quad \int d^p\,d\pi=0$$

Printed pp. 93-112Symmetry and Separation
Walk through the easy metric axioms. Symmetry comes from swapping the two coordinates of a coupling, and nonnegativity is immediate. The separation axiom uses that the diagonal coupling costs zero only when the two measures coincide, so distance zero forces equality.

Gluing Couplings

The lemma

Two couplings sharing a middle marginal combine into one measure on the triple.

Method

Disintegrate each coupling over the shared marginal $\nu$, then recombine.

Outcome

The outer marginals of the glued measure are exactly $\mu$ and $\lambda$.

Printed pp. 93-112Gluing Couplings
Introduce the gluing lemma as the engine of the triangle inequality. Given a coupling of the first pair and a coupling of the second sharing the middle marginal, disintegrate and recombine to build a single measure on the triple whose two outer marginals are the ones we need.

The Triangle Inequality

Step 1

Glue optimal couplings of $(\mu,\nu)$ and $(\nu,\lambda)$ on a triple $(x,y,z)$.

Step 2

Bound $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$ pointwise.

Step 3

Apply Minkowski's inequality in $L^p(\pi)$ to the two distance terms.

Triangle inequality

$$W_p(\mu,\lambda)\le W_p(\mu,\nu)+W_p(\nu,\lambda)$$

Minkowski step

$$\Big(\!\int d(x,z)^p d\pi\Big)^{1/p}\!\!\le\Big(\!\int d(x,y)^p\Big)^{1/p}\!\!+\Big(\!\int d(y,z)^p\Big)^{1/p}$$

Printed pp. 93-112The Triangle Inequality
Now cash in the gluing. Project the three-fold measure to the outer pair, bound the ground distance by the path through the middle, and apply the Minkowski inequality in L-p. The result is W_p of the outer pair at most the sum of the two leg distances.

The Topology Induced by W_p

Convergence theorem

$W_p(\mu_k,\mu)\to 0$ iff weak convergence plus $p$-th moments converge.

Test functions

Equivalently, $\int\varphi\,d\mu_k\to\int\varphi\,d\mu$ for $\varphi$ growing at most like $d^p$.

Stronger than weak

Weak convergence alone may lose mass to infinity; $W_p$ controls it.

Characterization

$$W_p(\mu_k,\mu)\to 0\iff \mu_k\rightharpoonup\mu\ \text{and}\ \int d(x_0,x)^p d\mu_k\to\int d(x_0,x)^p d\mu$$

Printed pp. 93-112The Topology of W_p
State the convergence theorem carefully. Convergence in W_p is equivalent to weak convergence together with convergence of p-th moments, equivalently convergence of integrals of every continuous function growing no faster than the p-th power. This is strictly stronger than plain weak convergence.

Nesting of the Orders

Monotone in p

For $p\le q$, the $p$-th root of a probability average is monotone: $W_p\le W_q$.

Mechanism

Apply Jensen or Holder to the same optimal coupling raised to two powers.

Consequence

Larger $p$ weighs long moves more, metrizing a finer, stronger topology.

Order inequality

$$1\le p\le q\ \Longrightarrow\ W_p(\mu,\nu)\le W_q(\mu,\nu)$$

Printed pp. 93-112Ordering: W_p ≤ W_q
Use Jensen or Holder on the coupling to compare orders. Because the p-th root of an average is monotone in p, a smaller exponent yields a smaller or equal distance. Larger p weighs long-range mass transport more heavily and therefore metrizes a finer, stronger topology.

P_p(X) is a Polish Space

Inherited structure

If $X$ is complete and separable, so is $(P_p(X),W_p)$.

Completeness

Every $W_p$-Cauchy sequence of measures converges inside $P_p(X)$.

Separability

Finitely supported measures on rational atoms form a countable dense set.

Theorem

$$X\ \text{Polish}\ \Longrightarrow\ (P_p(X),W_p)\ \text{Polish}$$

Dense set

$$D=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i} a_i\,\delta_{x_i}:\ a_i\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0},\ x_i\ \text{rational}\Big\}$$

Printed pp. 93-112Polish Space Structure
Summarize the structural payoff. When the ground space is complete and separable, so is the metric space of measures with finite p-th moment under W_p. Completeness lets Cauchy sequences converge, and separability supplies a countable dense set of finitely supported measures.

Synthesis and Outlook

Four claims

  • $W_p$ is a metric on $P_p(X)$.
  • $P_p(X)$ is Polish.
  • Topology $=$ weak $+$ moments.
  • Orders nest: $W_p\le W_q$.

The engine

Gluing of couplings drove every metric axiom that was not immediate.

Looking ahead

Curvature
