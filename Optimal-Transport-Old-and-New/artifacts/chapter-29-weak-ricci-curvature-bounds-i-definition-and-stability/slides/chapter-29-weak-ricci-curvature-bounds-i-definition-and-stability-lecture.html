From Smooth Ricci to a Synthetic Idea

Riemannian fact

On a manifold, $\mathrm{Ric}\ge K$ is equivalent to $K$-convexity of the Boltzmann entropy along optimal transport.

Promote to a definition

Take that equivalence as the meaning of curvature where no tensor exists.

Smooth equivalence

$$\mathrm{Ric}\ge K \iff \operatorname{Ent}\ \text{is }K\text{-convex along }(\mu_t)$$

Why it works

The Bochner identity converts the curvature lower bound into a convexity statement about spreading mass.

Curvature becomes a statement about how mass spreads, not about the curvature tensor, which need not exist in a singular space.

Entropy Functionals on a Space

Boltzmann entropy

Governs the dimension-free regime $N=\infty$.

$$\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(\mu)=\!\int \rho\log\rho\,\,d\nu,\quad \mu=\rho\,\nu$$

Rényi-type functionals

Govern finite dimension $N<\infty$.

$$\mathcal{S}_N(\mu)=-\!\int \rho^{1-1/N}\,d\nu$$

Shared structure

Both are lower semicontinuous in the weak topology and measured against the reference $\nu$.

Printed pp. 795-846Entropy Functionals on a Space
Two functionals carry the curvature signal. The Boltzmann entropy integrates the density times its logarithm and governs the dimension free case. The Renyi style functionals integrate a power of the density and govern finite dimension N. Both are defined relative to the reference measure and are lower semicontinuous in the weak topology.

Distortion Coefficients

What they measure

How a unit of mass is stretched at fraction $t$ of the way along a geodesic of length $d$.

Model-space weight

$$\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}(d)=t^{1/N}\Big(\tfrac{\sin(t\alpha)}{\sin\alpha}\Big)^{1-1/N},\ \ \alpha=d\sqrt{\tfrac{K}{N-1}}$$

Printed pp. 795-846Distortion Coefficients
Distortion coefficients measure how a unit volume of mass is stretched as it is transported a fraction of the way along a geodesic. They are built from the model space of constant curvature K over dimension N and reduce to simple sine or hyperbolic expressions. They are the precise weights that correct naive linear interpolation.

The Curvature–Dimension Condition $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$

Definition

A geodesic space $(X,d,\nu)$ satisfies $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ if, for every $W_2$-geodesic $(\mu_t)$ joining $\mu_0=\rho_0\nu$ and $\mu_1=\rho_1\nu$ with optimal plan $\pi$,

$$\mathcal{S}_N(\mu_t)\ \le\ -\!\int \Big[\tau_{K,N}^{(1-t)}(d)\,\rho_0^{-1/N}+\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}(d)\,\rho_1^{-1/N}\Big]\,d\pi(x_0,x_1).$$

Equivalently, the Rényi entropy is convex along $(\mu_t)$ once each endpoint is reweighted by its distortion coefficient. The pair $(K,N)$ records a lower Ricci bound and an upper dimension bound at once.

Printed pp. 795-846The CD(K,N) Definition
A space satisfies the curvature dimension condition when, along every Wasserstein geodesic, the Renyi entropy obeys a convexity inequality whose interpolation weights are the distortion coefficients. This single inequality encodes a lower Ricci bound K and an upper dimension bound N simultaneously, and it requires no smooth structure to state.

Reading the Inequality Term by Term

Interior value

$\mathcal{S}_N(\mu_t)$ is what we control at an intermediate time $t$.

Endpoint mix

The right side blends $\mathcal{S}_N(\mu_0)$ and $\mathcal{S}_N(\mu_1)$ with distortion weights.

Sign of $K$

When $K>0$ the weights exceed $1-t$ and $t$, pushing entropy down: mass concentrates, as positive Ricci demands.

Printed pp. 795-846The Convexity Inequality in Detail
Reading the inequality term by term shows why it is sharp. The value of entropy at an interior time is bounded by a distortion weighted combination of the endpoint values. When curvature is positive the coefficients exceed the linear weights, forcing entropy down and concentrating mass exactly as positive Ricci should.

The Dimension-Free Case $\mathrm{CD}(K,\infty)$

Limit $N\to\infty$

Distortion coefficients collapse to the linear weights, leaving plain $K$-convexity.

Clean condition

$$\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(\mu_t)\le (1-t)\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(\mu_0)+t\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}\,t(1-t)\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2$$

Isolates $K$

Only the lower Ricci bound survives; dimension drops out entirely.

Bakry–Émery link

Matches the weighted-manifold theory and is the first bound to test on a candidate space.

Geometry

$K$-convexity is exactly geodesic convexity in $(\mathcal{P}_2,W_2)$ with modulus $K$.

Printed pp. 795-846The Dimension Free Case
Letting N tend to infinity collapses the distortion coefficients to one and leaves plain K convexity of the Boltzmann entropy. This cleaner condition isolates the lower bound K alone, matches Bakry Emery theory on weighted manifolds, and is often the first synthetic bound one checks on a candidate space.

Examples and Sanity Checks

Weighted manifolds

$(M,g,e^{-V}\mathrm{vol})$ is $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ exactly when its Bakry–Émery tensor $\mathrm{Ric}_{N,V}\ge K$.

Flat space

$(\mathbb{R}^n,|\cdot|,\mathcal{L}^n)$ satisfies $\mathrm{CD}(0,N)$ for every $N\ge n$.

Sphere

$\mathbb{S}^n$ realises $\mathrm{CD}(n-1,n)$, the sharp model for positive curvature.

Printed pp. 795-846Examples and Sanity Checks
Smooth weighted manifolds satisfy CD(K,N) exactly when their Bakry Emery Ricci tensor is bounded below by K. Euclidean space with Lebesgue measure is CD(0,N). The discrete transport laboratory in the notebook makes the corrected convexity residual visible and shows it turning negative once K is pushed too high.

Stability: $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ is Closed under mGH Limits

Theorem

If $(X_k,d_k,\nu_k)$ each satisfy $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ and converge in the measured Gromov–Hausdorff sense to $(X,d,\nu)$, then the limit again satisfies $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$.

Engine

Lower semicontinuity of entropy plus convergence of optimal plans pushes the inequality to the limit.

Printed pp. 795-846Stability under mGH Convergence
The central theorem says the curvature dimension condition is closed. If a sequence of CD(K,N) spaces converges in the measured Gromov Hausdorff sense, the limit again satisfies CD(K,N). Lower semicontinuity of entropy and convergence of optimal plans pass the inequality to the limit measure and metric.

Why Stability Matters

Limits keep the bound

Gromov–Hausdorff limits of manifolds with uniform $\mathrm{Ric}\ge K$ retain a synthetic bound, even when smoothness is lost.

Compactness

Families of $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ spaces with bounded diameter are precompact and closed, an essential tool.

Right notion certified

Closure under limits shows the definition captures genuine curvature, not an artefact of the smooth setting.

Consequences inherited

Functional inequalities proved from $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ then hold on the singular limits too.

Printed pp. 795-846Why Stability Matters
Stability is what makes the synthetic definition useful rather than merely consistent. It guarantees that limits of manifolds with uniform Ricci bounds keep that bound, supplies compactness for families of such spaces, and certifies that the definition captures the right notion rather than an accident of smoothness.

Synthesis and Outlook

What we built

Curvature as $(K,N)$-displacement convexity of entropy along $W_2$ geodesics.

Distortion coefficients $\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}$ weight the inequality and encode $N$.

The class $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ is closed under measured Gromov–Hausdorff limits.

$$\mathrm{Ric}\ge K \ \rightsquigarrow\ \text{entropy is }(K,N)\text{-convex along }(\mu_t)$$
