Solution of the Monge Problem I

The global approach: from a Kantorovich plan to a transport map

Goal

Turn an optimal coupling into a deterministic map $T$ with $T_\#\mu=\nu$.

Tool

The dual potential, $c$-convex, and its $c$-subdifferential.

Hypothesis

The source $\mu$ is absolutely continuous (no atoms).

Monge versus Kantorovich

Monge problem

$\inf_{T_\#\mu=\nu}\ \int c\big(x,T(x)\big)\,d\mu(x)$

Kantorovich relaxation

$\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\ \int c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)$

The gap

Monge can be empty or non-attained; Kantorovich always has a minimizer.

The bridge

If the optimal $\pi$ concentrates on a graph $y=T(x)$, the two problems coincide.

This chapter

Proves that concentration under mild assumptions.

Printed pp. 205-214Monge versus Kantorovich
Recall the two formulations. Monge asks for a map pushing mu to nu and minimizing average cost; Kantorovich relaxes to couplings. Kantorovich always has a minimizer, Monge may not. The global approach shows the optimal coupling secretly concentrates on the graph of a map, so the relaxed solution answers the original question.

The Global Strategy in Three Moves

1. Solve the dual

Maximize $\int\psi\,d\mu+\int\phi\,d\nu$ to get a $c$-concave potential.

2. Restrict to support

On $\mathrm{supp}\,\pi$, the dual constraint is an equality.

3. Differentiate

Show $\partial_c\psi$ is single valued $\mu$-a.e., giving a map.

Printed pp. 205-214The Global Strategy
Lay out the three moves the chapter executes. First, solve the dual Kantorovich problem to obtain a c-concave potential psi. Second, restrict to the support of the optimal plan, where psi attains equality. Third, prove the c-subdifferential of psi is single valued for almost every source point, which turns the plan into a graph.

$c$-Convexity and Potentials

$c$-convex function

$\psi(x)=\sup_{y}\ \big[\zeta(y)-c(x,y)\big]$

Reading

A supremum of cost-translated pieces, one per candidate destination $y$.

Why it appears

The optimal dual potential is automatically $c$-convex.

Printed pp. 205-214c-Convexity and Potentials
Introduce the engine of the proof. A function is c-convex when it is a supremum of cost-translated affine pieces. The optimal dual potential is c-convex, and its c-subdifferential collects, for each x, every y that achieves the defining supremum. Single valuedness of this set is exactly what makes a transport map exist.

The $c$-Subdifferential

Definition

$\partial_c\psi(x)=\{\,y:\ \psi(x)+\psi^c(y)=c(x,y)\,\}$

Where the plan lives

$\mathrm{supp}\,\pi\subseteq\{(x,y):y\in\partial_c\psi(x)\}$.

The dichotomy

One point per $x$ gives a map; several points give branching.

Printed pp. 205-214The c-Subdifferential
Define the central object precisely. The c-subdifferential at x is the set of points y for which the dual constraint is tight. The optimal plan must live inside this set viewed as a relation in the product space. If for almost every x that set is a single point, the relation is the graph of a map T.

Quadratic Cost and Brenier's Theorem

Squared distance

$c(x,y)=\tfrac12|x-y|^2$

Brenier

$T=\nabla\psi,\qquad \psi\ \text{convex}$

Translation

$c$-convexity becomes ordinary convexity after a quadratic shift.

Printed pp. 205-214Quadratic Cost and Brenier
Specialize to the squared distance. Then c-convexity becomes ordinary convexity after a quadratic shift, and the potential psi can be taken convex. Brenier's theorem says the optimal map is the gradient of that convex function. The c-subdifferential becomes the ordinary subdifferential, single valued wherever the convex potential is differentiable.

The Map Is a Gradient Field

Monotonicity

Gradients of convex functions are cyclically monotone, so mass never folds back.

Order preserved

$\langle\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(x'),\,x-x'\rangle\ge 0$

Consequence

Transport is geometrically rigid, not an arbitrary rearrangement.

Printed pp. 205-214The Map is a Gradient
Make the picture concrete. A convex potential has a gradient field, and that field carries mu onto nu. Convexity forbids the field from folding mass back on itself, so transport is monotone. This is the geometric heart of the chapter: the optimal map is not arbitrary, it is the slope of a convex landscape.

Differentiability Almost Everywhere

Rademacher

A locally Lipschitz function is differentiable Lebesgue-almost everywhere.

Convex regularity

Convex potentials are locally Lipschitz, hence differentiable off a null set.

Single valued where smooth

$\psi\ \text{differentiable at}\ x \;\Rightarrow\; \partial_c\psi(x)=\{\nabla\psi(x)\}$

Printed pp. 205-214Differentiability Almost Everywhere
Explain why single valuedness holds. A convex function is differentiable outside a set of measure zero, by Rademacher's theorem applied to its local Lipschitz property. Points of non-differentiability are where the subdifferential branches into several directions. That branch set is Lebesgue negligible, hence invisible to an absolutely continuous source.

The Exceptional Set and Absolute Continuity

Exceptional set $E$

Points where $\partial_c\psi$ is multivalued; $E$ has Lebesgue measure zero.

Invisible to $\mu$

$\mu\ll\mathcal L^n \;\Rightarrow\; \mu(E)=0$

Necessity

Drop absolute continuity and a Monge map may genuinely fail to exist.

Printed pp. 205-214The Exceptional Set
Highlight the role of absolute continuity. The exceptional set, where the subdifferential is multivalued, has measure zero. If mu charges no such set, that is, if mu is absolutely continuous, then almost every source point has a unique image. Drop absolute continuity and a map may genuinely fail to exist, which is why the hypothesis is essential.

Existence and Uniqueness of the Map

Theorem (Brenier-type)

$\exists!\ T=\nabla\psi:\ T_\#\mu=\nu$

$\psi$ convex, valid $\mu$-a.e.

Existence

From differentiability almost everywhere of $\psi$.

Uniqueness

Strict convexity of $c$ pins down the gradient.

Hypotheses that matter

  • $\mu$ absolutely continuous (no atoms).
  • Finite transport cost between $\mu$ and $\nu$.
  • Cost smooth and strictly convex in $x-y$.

Bonus

$T$ is also the unique optimal coupling: the plan and the map agree.

Printed pp. 205-214Existence and Uniqueness
State the payoff theorem. For squared distance with mu absolutely continuous, there is a unique optimal map T equal to the gradient of a convex potential, and it is the unique optimal coupling. Existence comes from differentiability almost everywhere; uniqueness comes from strict convexity of the cost forcing the gradient to be determined.

From Dual Potential to Transport Map

Recovery pipeline

  • Solve dual $\to$ potential $\psi$.
  • $\psi$ is $c$-convex; restrict to $\mathrm{supp}\,\pi$.
  • Differentiate $\psi$ where possible.
  • Read off $T=\nabla\psi$ $\mu$-a.e.
For quadratic cost

$T(x)=\nabla\psi(x)$

Printed pp. 205-214From Dual Potential to Map
Trace the full recovery pipeline once more, end to end. Solve the dual, obtain psi c-convex, restrict to the support, differentiate, and read off T equals grad psi almost everywhere. Each arrow is a theorem in the chapter. The global approach is exactly this chain from a number, the dual value, to a function, the map.

Recap and Outlook

What we proved

The optimal plan concentrates on the graph of $\nabla\psi$ for a $c$-convex $\psi$.

Key mechanism

$\partial_c\psi$ is single valued $\mu$-a.e. once $\mu\ll\mathcal L^n$.

One-line summary

Dual value $\rightsquigarrow$ convex potential $\rightsquigarrow$ gradient map.

Printed pp. 205-214Recap and Outlook
Summarize and look ahead. The global approach proves a Monge map exists by showing the optimal plan concentrates on the graph of the gradient of a c-convex potential, single valued almost everywhere when the source has no atoms.
