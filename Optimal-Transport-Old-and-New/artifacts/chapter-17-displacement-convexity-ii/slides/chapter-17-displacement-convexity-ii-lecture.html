From Flat to Curved

Flat baseline

On $\mathbb{R}^n$ McCann interpolation is affine in the variable and volumes are unchanged.

Curved correction

A pencil of geodesics spreads or focuses; the Jacobian is no longer linear in $t$.

Interpolation

$\mu_t = (T_t)_\#\,\mu_0,\quad T_t(x)=\exp_x\!\big(t\,\nabla\varphi(x)\big)$

Printed pp. 449-492From Flat to Curved
On Euclidean space McCann interpolation moves mass along straight lines and volumes are preserved, so convexity of the entropy reduces to a one-dimensional fact. On a manifold the same interpolation rides geodesics, and a thin bundle of geodesics spreads or focuses depending on curvature. That focusing is precisely what the distortion coefficients must record.

Distortion Coefficients

Definition

The ratio of the geodesic Jacobian to its flat counterpart along the transport.

Volume Jacobian

$\beta_t(x,y)=\lim_{s\to t}\frac{\text{vol of pushed cell at }s}{\text{vol of flat cell at }s}$

Reading

$\beta_t>1$ where geodesics spread; $\beta_t<1$ where curvature focuses them.

Printed pp. 449-492Distortion Coefficients
The distortion coefficient compares the actual Jacobian of the geodesic interpolation to the model space of constant curvature K and dimension N. When the bundle of geodesics spreads more than the flat baseline the coefficient exceeds one; when curvature focuses it, the coefficient drops below one. These coefficients are the curved replacement for the trivial flat factor.

The Role of the Reference Measure

Entropy is relative

Boltzmann entropy is computed against a fixed reference $\nu$, here the volume.

Boltzmann $H$-functional

$H_\nu(\mu)=\int \rho\log\rho\,\,d\nu,\qquad \mu=\rho\,\nu$

Weighted manifolds

Choosing $\nu=e^{-V}\mathrm{vol}$ shifts curvature to the Bakry-Emery tensor.

Effective curvature

$\mathrm{Ric}_{N,\nu}=\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla^2 V-\dfrac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{N-n}$

Why it matters

The reference fixes which density enters $H$, hence which curvature the entropy feels.

Consequence

The same inequality holds with $\mathrm{Ric}_{N,\nu}\ge K g$ on a weighted space.

Printed pp. 449-492The Reference Measure
Entropy is never absolute; it is measured against a fixed reference measure, here the Riemannian volume. The density of a transported measure with respect to that volume is what enters the entropy functional. Changing the reference measure changes the effective curvature seen by the entropy, which is the seed of weighted manifolds and the Bakry-Emery tensor.

The Classes $\mathrm{DC}_N$

Pressure form

Encode $U$ through its pressure $p(r)=rU'(r)-U(r)$ and a rescaled potential.

Membership condition

$U\in\mathrm{DC}_N \iff r\mapsto r^{N}\,U\!\big(r^{-N}\big)\ \text{is convex}$

Why $N$ enters

The exponent matches $U$ to the $N$-dimensional distortion coefficients.

Canonical members

Boltzmann $U(r)=r\log r$ lies in every $\mathrm{DC}_N$; Renyi $U(r)=\tfrac{r^{1-1/N}-r}{1/N-1}$ marks the boundary.

Printed pp. 449-492Displacement Convexity Classes DC_N
A nonlinearity U lies in the class DC_N when its pressure-rescaled form is convex in the appropriate variable. This single algebraic condition is exactly what lets U interact correctly with the distortion coefficients of dimension N. Boltzmann entropy and the Renyi-type functionals are the canonical members that drive the geometric inequalities.

Two Geometric Inputs

Curvature from below

$\mathrm{Ric}\ge K\,g$ caps how fast geodesics may focus.

Dimension from above

$\dim M\le N$ caps how many directions focus at once.

Combined hypothesis

$\mathrm{Ric}\ge K g\ \ \text{and}\ \ N\ge n=\dim M$

Printed pp. 449-492Ricci Lower Bound Meets Dimension Upper Bound
Two inputs combine: a Ricci curvature bounded below by K times the metric, and a manifold dimension at most N. The lower curvature bound controls how much geodesics may focus, while the dimension cap limits how many independent directions can focus at once. Together they pin down the model distortion coefficients beta from below.

The Coefficients $\beta_t^{K,N}$

Model-space coefficient

$\beta_t^{K,N}(d)=\left(\dfrac{\sin\!\big(t\,\alpha\big)}{t\,\sin\alpha}\right)^{N-1},\ \ \alpha=d\sqrt{\tfrac{K}{N-1}}$

Negative $K$

Replace $\sin$ by $\sinh$ when $K<0$; geodesics spread, so $\beta_t^{K,N}\ge 1$.

Flat limit

As $K\to 0$ the coefficient collapses to the trivial factor: $\beta_t^{0,N}=1$.

Printed pp. 449-492The Distortion Coefficients beta_t^{K,N}
For positive K the coefficient uses a sine profile whose argument scales with the geodesic length and the square root of K over N minus one. For negative K it uses the hyperbolic sine. At K equal to zero it collapses to the flat factor t. These closed forms are the model-space yardstick the inequality measures against.

The Convexity Inequality

Distorted displacement convexity

$U_\nu(\mu_t)\le \int\Big[(1-t)\,\beta_{1-t}^{K,N}\,U\!\big(\tfrac{\rho_0}{\beta}\big)+t\,\beta_{t}^{K,N}\,U\!\big(\tfrac{\rho_1}{\beta}\big)\Big]$

Reading

The endpoint energies are reweighted by the distortion coefficients, not just by $t$ and $1-t$.

Flat check

Setting $K=0$, $\beta=1$ recovers the Chapter 16 linear interpolation bound.

Printed pp. 449-492The Convexity Inequality
The central estimate bounds the entropy at the midpoint, and more generally along the geodesic, by a beta-weighted combination of the endpoint entropies. The distortion coefficients enter inside the convexity, replacing the naive linear interpolation. This is the precise curved analogue of the flat displacement convexity inequality from the previous chapter.

$K$-Convexity of the Entropy

$K$-convexity (geodesic form)

$H_\nu(\mu_t)\le (1-t)H_\nu(\mu_0)+t\,H_\nu(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}\,t(1-t)\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2$

Second difference

$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H_\nu(\mu_t)\ge K\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2$ along every geodesic.

Consequences

$K>0$ forces heat-flow contraction, log-Sobolev, and Talagrand inequalities.

Printed pp. 449-492K-Convexity of the Entropy
When the distortion correction is absorbed, the entropy is K-convex along every Wasserstein geodesic: its second difference is bounded below by K times the squared transport distance. A positive K makes the entropy uniformly convex, which forces contraction of the heat flow and underlies functional inequalities like log-Sobolev and Talagrand.

The Condition $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$

Synthetic definition

A metric measure space is $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ when entropy is $\beta^{K,N}$-displacement convex.

Equivalence on manifolds

$\mathrm{CD}(K,N)\iff \mathrm{Ric}\ge K g\ \text{and}\ \dim\le N$

No smoothness

The transport formulation needs only geodesics and a measure, not a metric tensor.

Stability

$\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ is stable under measured Gromov-Hausdorff limits, so curvature passes to singular spaces.

Printed pp. 449-492The Curvature-Dimension Condition CD(K,N)
Lott, Villani, and Sturm turn the inequality into a definition: a metric measure space satisfies CD(K,N) when the entropy is displacement convex with the beta coefficients for those parameters. On a smooth manifold this is equivalent to the Ricci and dimension bounds, but it requires no smoothness, so it transports curvature to singular spaces.
