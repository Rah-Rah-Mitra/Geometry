$K$-convexity

The functional dips below the chord by a curvature penalty.

Integrated chord form

$$U(\mu_t)\le (1-t)U(\mu_0)+t\,U(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}t(1-t)\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2$$

The Entropy Along a Geodesic

Model functional

Boltzmann entropy $\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)=\int \rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{dvol}$.

Continuity equation

Density transported by a velocity field $\nabla\psi$.

Transport dynamics

$$\partial_t\rho_t+\nabla\!\cdot(\rho_t\,\nabla\psi_t)=0,\qquad \partial_t\psi_t+\tfrac12|\nabla\psi_t|^2=0$$

Why entropy

Its convexity encodes the Ricci lower bound we want to detect.

Boltzmann entropy is the model functional. Along a displacement interpolation driven by a Kantorovich potential, the density evolves by a continuity equation. We set up the time-dependent density and velocity field so that the first and second time derivatives of entropy become tractable transport integrals over the moving measure.

First Derivative: The Velocity Field

One differentiation

Pair $\partial_t\rho$ against $\log\rho+1$ and integrate by parts.

Clean form

The boundary terms vanish; only a divergence integral survives.

First time derivative

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_t)=-\int \nabla\!\cdot(\rho_t\nabla\psi_t)\,\log\rho_t\,\mathrm{dvol}=\int \nabla\psi_t\!\cdot\nabla\rho_t\,\mathrm{dvol}$$

Second Derivative and Bochner

Bochner formula

The commutator of $\nabla$ and $\Delta$ exposes the Ricci tensor.

Two children

A Hessian square and a curvature quadratic form.

Bochner identity

$$\tfrac12\Delta|\nabla\psi|^2-\nabla\psi\!\cdot\nabla\Delta\psi=\|\nabla^2\psi\|^2+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi)$$

Reading

The left side is dynamical; the right side is geometric.

Printed pp. 525-544Second derivative and Bochner
The heart of the chapter: differentiating a second time and integrating by parts yields the Bochner formula. The commutator of gradient and Laplacian along the flow splits the result into the squared Hessian of the potential plus a Ricci quadratic form acting on its gradient, the curvature signature we seek.

The Curvature-Weighted Integral

Master identity

The second derivative is one integral over the moving measure.

Density weight

Each contribution is averaged against $\rho_t$.

Second time derivative

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_t)=\int\!\Big(\|\nabla^2\psi_t\|^2+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla\psi_t,\nabla\psi_t)\Big)\rho_t\,\mathrm{dvol}$$

Interpretation

Convexity of entropy = nonnegativity of this average.

Printed pp. 525-544The curvature-weighted integral
Collecting terms, the second derivative of entropy equals the integral over the moving measure of the Hessian energy plus the Ricci form evaluated on the velocity. Every contribution is weighted by the density, so the identity is genuinely a curvature-weighted average rather than a single pointwise number.

Decomposition: Dissipation Plus Curvature

Dissipation term

$\|\nabla^2\psi\|^2\ge 0$ always: a Fisher-type Hessian energy.

Curvature term

$\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi)$ carries the sign of geometry.

Lower bound

If $\mathrm{Ric}\ge K g$, the average is $\ge K\!\int|\nabla\psi|^2\rho$.

Printed pp. 525-544Two nonnegative pieces
We dissect the integrand into a manifestly nonnegative dissipation term, the squared Frobenius norm of the Hessian, and a curvature term carrying the Ricci tensor's sign. Under a Ricci lower bound the second piece is controlled, and the whole second derivative is bounded below by the curvature constant times kinetic energy.

Local Criterion Equals Integrated Bound

Forward

$g''(t)\ge K|\dot\mu_t|^2$ integrates to the chord inequality.

Backward

The chord bound forces $g''\ge K|\dot\mu|^2$ a.e. in $t$.

Equivalence

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}U(\mu_t)\ge K\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; U \text{ is } K\text{-convex}$$

Analogy

This mirrors the $\Gamma_2$ calculus of Bakry and Emery.

Printed pp. 525-544Local equals integrated
We prove the equivalence: the pointwise lower bound on the second derivative integrates to the K-convexity chord inequality, and conversely the chord bound forces the infinitesimal inequality at almost every time. This two-way passage is the chapter's organizing principle, mirroring the Gamma-two calculus of Bakry and Emery.

The Dimensional Refinement: CD(K,N)

Isolate the trace

$\|\nabla^2\psi\|^2\ge\frac{(\Delta\psi)^2}{N}$ by Cauchy-Schwarz.

Dimension N

The Bochner remainder tightens the pure Ricci estimate.

Curvature-dimension bound

$$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\,\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_t)\ge K\!\int|\nabla\psi|^2\rho+\frac{1}{N}\!\int(\Delta\psi)^2\rho$$

Outcome

The sharp $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ condition for the entropy functional.

This yields the dimensional curvature-dimension inequality, where a Bochner remainder of the squared Laplacian divided by N tightens the pure Ricci estimate into the sharp CD(K,N) condition.

Synthesis and Outlook

What we proved

$d^2/dt^2$ of entropy = Hessian energy + Ricci form, weighted by $\rho$.

Why it matters

Gradient flows, functional inequalities, contraction estimates.

The bridge

From smooth Bochner calculus to synthetic Ricci bounds on metric measure spaces.

One line to remember

$$\ddot{\mathrm{Ent}}=\underbrace{\textstyle\int\|\nabla^2\psi\|^2\rho}_{\text{dissipation}\,\ge 0}+\underbrace{\textstyle\int\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi)\rho}_{\text{curvature}}$$
