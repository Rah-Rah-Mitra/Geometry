What is a metric-measure space?

Triple

A complete separable metric space carrying a Borel probability measure of full support.

Normalize

Total mass one removes scaling ambiguity in the measure.

Identify

Measure-preserving isometries are treated as equalities: compare shapes, not labels.

Definition

$X = (X,\, d,\, \nu), \quad \nu(X)=1,\ \operatorname{supp}\nu = X$

Isomorphism

$X \cong Y \iff \exists\, \phi:X\to Y,\ \phi_\#\nu_X=\nu_Y,\ d\text{-preserving}$

Why both

Metric alone misses where mass lives; the pair fixes the geometry of probability.

Printed pp. 743-772What is a metric-measure space
A metric-measure space bundles a complete separable metric with a reference probability measure of full support. Normalizing the total mass to one removes a scaling ambiguity, and treating isomorphic spaces as identical means we compare shapes, not labelings, which is exactly what a convergence theory for geometries must do.

Need convergence first

"The limit inherits the bound" is empty until $X_n \to X$ has meaning.

Synthetic Ricci bounds are stated for fixed spaces, yet their value lies in stability. If a sequence of spaces with a common lower bound converges, the limit should inherit the bound. To make sense of that sentence we first need a precise topology in which the sequence converges at all.

Gromov-Hausdorff distance

Embed and compare

Put both spaces isometrically into one host $Z$, take Hausdorff distance, minimize over hosts.

Definition

$d_{GH}(X,Y)=\inf_{Z,\,\iota_X,\iota_Y} d_H^Z\big(\iota_X(X),\,\iota_Y(Y)\big)$

Coordinate-free

No shared chart needed; only intrinsic distances matter.

Printed pp. 743-772Gromov-Hausdorff distance
The Gromov-Hausdorff distance measures geometry without coordinates. We embed two spaces isometrically into a common host and take the Hausdorff distance of their images, then minimize over all embeddings. Equivalently we look for an approximate isometry, a correspondence that distorts pairwise distances by at most a small amount.

Measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology

Geometry plus mass

Near-isometry of supports is not enough; the measures must also match.

Weak push-forward

The approximate isometry sends $\nu_n$ close to $\nu$ in weak convergence.

No mass escape

Densities cannot drift to infinity while supports appear to align.

mGH convergence

$X_n \xrightarrow{mGH} X \iff d_{GH}\to 0 \ \text{and}\ (\phi_n)_\#\nu_n \rightharpoonup \nu$

Reading

Two layers move together: shapes converge and weighted mass converges weakly.

Printed pp. 743-772Measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology
The measured version asks for more than near-isometry of the supports. The approximate isometry must also push the reference measure of one space close to that of the other in a weak sense. Mass and metric move together, so densities cannot quietly escape to infinity while the shapes appear to match.

Correspondences and distortion

Correspondence

A relation $R \subseteq X\times Y$ where every point on each side has a partner.

Distortion

$\operatorname{dis}(R)=\sup_{(x,y),(x',y')\in R}\big|\,d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')\,\big|$

Certificate

Small $\operatorname{dis}(R)$ plus near-matching of $\nu_X,\nu_Y$ certifies closeness.

Printed pp. 743-772Correspondences and approximate isometries
A correspondence is a relation pairing points of two spaces so that every point on each side has a partner. Its distortion is the largest discrepancy between paired distances. Small distortion plus near matching of the two measures is the concrete, checkable certificate that two metric-measure spaces are close.

Sturm's $\mathbb{D}$-distance

Couple the measures

Choose a transport plan $q$ on $X\times Y$ with marginals $\nu_X,\nu_Y$.

Compare distances

Measure the $L^2$ gap between $d_X$ and $d_Y$ under the coupling.

Optimize

Minimize over all couplings: a Gromov-Wasserstein quantity.

Definition

$\mathbb{D}(X,Y)^2=\inf_{q}\!\int\!\!\int \big(d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')\big)^2\, dq\, dq$

Outcome

The space of spaces becomes itself a metric space under $\mathbb{D}$.

Printed pp. 743-772Sturm's D-distance
Sturm builds an honest metric on isomorphism classes. Couple the two reference measures by a transport plan on the product, compare the two distance functions under that coupling in an L-squared sense, and minimize over couplings. This is a Gromov-Wasserstein quantity, and it makes the space of spaces itself a metric space.

$\mathbb{D}$ is a genuine metric

Identity

$\mathbb{D}(X,Y)=0$ exactly when $X$ and $Y$ are isomorphic.

Triangle inequality

A gluing lemma pastes optimal couplings across three spaces.

Comparable topology

On nice families $\mathbb{D}$ and the mGH topology agree.

Gluing

$\mathbb{D}(X,Z)\le \mathbb{D}(X,Y)+\mathbb{D}(Y,Z)$

Printed pp. 743-772D-distance as a Gromov-Wasserstein metric
The D-distance satisfies the triangle inequality through a gluing lemma that pastes optimal couplings across three spaces. It vanishes exactly on isomorphic pairs and metrizes a topology comparable, on nice families, to the measured Gromov-Hausdorff one. We now have a genuine distance, not merely a convergence notion.

Precompactness theorems

Uniform diameter

A common cap $\operatorname{diam}(X_n)\le D$ on every space in the family.

Uniform covering

A shared doubling or volume-growth control on the measures.

Conclusion

The family is precompact: every sequence has a $\mathbb{D}$-convergent subsequence.

Theorem (schematic)

$\sup_n \operatorname{diam}X_n\le D,\ \ \nu_n\big(B_r\big)\ge \beta(r) \ \Rightarrow\ \{X_n\}\ \text{precompact}$

Why it matters

This is the workhorse: bounds in, convergent subsequence out.

Printed pp. 743-772Precompactness theorems
The payoff is compactness. A family of normalized spaces that is uniformly totally bounded, with a common diameter cap and a uniform doubling or covering control on the measures, is precompact in the D-distance. Uniform bounds therefore force convergent subsequences, the workhorse of every limiting argument.

Curvature bounds pass to the limit

Entropy functional

The curvature-dimension condition is a convexity of entropy along geodesics.

Lower semicontinuity

Entropy is lower semicontinuous under measured convergence.

Closed class

The family of spaces with a fixed bound is closed in the topology.

Stability

$X_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{D}} X,\ \ X_n\in\mathrm{CD}(K,N)\ \Longrightarrow\ X\in\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$

Consequence

Synthetic Ricci bounds are robust: this is why the whole theory is useful.

Printed pp. 743-772Passing curvature bounds to the limit
Because the entropy functionals defining curvature-dimension bounds are lower semicontinuous along measured convergence, a lower Ricci bound shared by every space in a convergent sequence transfers to the limit. The class of spaces with a fixed bound is closed, which is precisely what makes the synthetic theory robust.
