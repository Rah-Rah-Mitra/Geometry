Optimal Transport: Old and NewChapter 04

The Kantorovich Problem

Couplings

$\Pi(\mu,\nu)$ has marginals $\mu$ and $\nu$.

Always nonempty

The product $\mu\otimes\nu$ is admissible.

Goal

Minimize the integrated cost over $\Pi(\mu,\nu)$.

Total cost functional

$$I[\pi]=\int_{X\times Y} c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)$$

Optimal value

$$C(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)} I[\pi]$$

Printed pp. 43-50The Kantorovich Problem
Recall the relaxed problem: minimize the integral of the cost against a coupling whose marginals are the prescribed measures mu and nu. The admissible set is never empty because the product measure always qualifies. We fix vocabulary, the transport cost functional and the optimal total cost, before studying when a minimizer is attained.

Standing Hypotheses

Spaces

$X,Y$ are Polish (complete separable metric).

Cost

$c$ is lower semicontinuous and bounded below.

Normalization

Often $c\ge 0$; subtracting a constant is harmless.

Lower semicontinuity

$$(x_n,y_n)\to(x,y)\ \Rightarrow\ c(x,y)\le\liminf_n c(x_n,y_n)$$

Why mild

Covers $c=d(x,y)^2$ and most natural costs.

Printed pp. 43-50Standing Hypotheses
The existence theorem rests on two mild hypotheses. The cost function is lower semicontinuous and bounded below, typically by zero on a Polish space. These are weak enough to cover squared distance and most natural costs, yet strong enough to make the cost functional behave well under weak limits of couplings.

The Admissible Set is Convex

Linear constraints

Marginal conditions are linear in $\pi$.

Linear objective

$I[\pi]$ is linear, so this is a linear program.

Convexity

$$\pi_0,\pi_1\in\Pi \Rightarrow (1-t)\pi_0+t\pi_1\in\Pi$$

Printed pp. 43-50The Set of Couplings is Convex
The set of couplings with fixed marginals is convex: any mixture of two couplings is again a coupling, since the marginal constraints are linear. The cost functional is linear in the plan. So Kantorovich is an infinite-dimensional linear program, and we expect minima at extreme points of the admissible set.

Tightness and Prokhorov

Tight marginals

For each $\varepsilon$, compacts hold most of $\mu$ and $\nu$.

Uniform control

$K_\varepsilon\times L_\varepsilon$ holds joint mass for every $\pi$.

Tightness of plans

$$\pi\big((K_\varepsilon\times L_\varepsilon)^c\big)\le 2\varepsilon\quad\forall\,\pi\in\Pi$$

Prokhorov's theorem

$$\text{tight family}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{weakly precompact}$$

Consequence

A minimizing sequence $(\pi_n)$ has a weak limit $\pi_\star$.

Printed pp. 43-50Tightness and Prokhorov
Each marginal is tight, so given epsilon we find compacts capturing mass in each factor. The product compact then captures the joint mass uniformly over all couplings. By Prokhorov, the family of couplings is precompact for weak convergence, and a minimizing sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence.

Lower Semicontinuity of the Cost

Weak limits

Integrals are not continuous under weak convergence.

One-sided is enough

We only need a $\liminf$ inequality.

Lifting l.s.c.

$c$ l.s.c. $\Rightarrow$ $I$ weakly l.s.c. in $\pi$.

Key inequality

$$\pi_n\rightharpoonup\pi_\star\ \Rightarrow\ I[\pi_\star]\le\liminf_n I[\pi_n]$$

Sandwich

$$I[\pi_\star]\le\liminf_n I[\pi_n]=C(\mu,\nu)$$

Printed pp. 43-50Lower Semicontinuity of the Cost
Weak convergence alone does not preserve integrals, but lower semicontinuity of the cost gives the one-sided inequality we need. The total cost is weakly lower semicontinuous in the plan, so the limit of a minimizing sequence does no worse than the infimum. Combined with compactness this delivers a minimizer.

Existence of an Optimal Coupling

Direct method

Compactness + lower semicontinuity $\Rightarrow$ a minimizer.

No convex cost needed

$c$ need not be convex in $(x,y)$.

Theorem (existence)

$$\exists\,\pi_\star\in\Pi(\mu,\nu):\ I[\pi_\star]=C(\mu,\nu)$$

Printed pp. 43-50Existence of an Optimal Coupling
Assemble the pieces by the direct method of the calculus of variations. The admissible set is weakly compact by Prokhorov, the cost is weakly lower semicontinuous, so the infimum is attained. Under a lower semicontinuous cost bounded below on Polish spaces, an optimal coupling always exists, with no convexity of the cost required.

Optimal Cost as a Function of Marginals

A new map

Fix $c$; read $C(\mu,\nu)$ as a function of $(\mu,\nu)$.

Convexity

Jointly convex in the pair of marginals.

Stability

Lower semicontinuous under weak convergence of marginals.

Lower semicontinuity in marginals

$$\mu_n\rightharpoonup\mu,\ \nu_n\rightharpoonup\nu\ \Rightarrow\ C(\mu,\nu)\le\liminf_n C(\mu_n,\nu_n)$$

Why it matters

Foundation for Wasserstein distances and their topology.

Printed pp. 43-50Optimal Cost as a Function of Marginals
Fixing the cost, the optimal total cost becomes a function of the pair of marginals. It inherits convexity from the linear structure and is itself lower semicontinuous: if the marginals converge weakly, the optimal cost does not jump up. This stability is what lets us later define Wasserstein distances and study their topology.

The Restriction Property

Statement

A sub-plan of an optimal plan stays optimal.

Restriction

$$\tilde\pi=\tfrac{1}{\pi[Z]}\,\pi\restriction_Z,\quad Z\subseteq X\times Y$$

Proof idea

A cheaper sub-plan pastes back to beat $\pi$ — contradiction.

Printed pp. 43-50The Restriction Property
Restriction is the chapter's most useful lemma. If a plan is optimal and we restrict it to a measurable subset of the product, then after renormalizing, the restriction is optimal for its own marginals. A cheaper sub-plan could be pasted back to beat the original, contradicting global optimality. Optimality is hereditary.

Cyclical Monotonicity Appears

Localize

Restriction pushes optimality to finite subsets of $\mathrm{supp}\,\pi$.

No swap helps

No finite cyclic reshuffle of pairs can lower the cost.

Name

$c$-cyclical monotonicity of the support.

$c$-cyclical monotonicity

$$\sum_{i=1}^{N} c(x_i,y_i)\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^{N} c(x_i,y_{\sigma(i)})$$

Outlook

The bridge to Kantorovich duality (next chapter).

Printed pp. 43-50Cyclical Monotonicity Appears
Restriction localizes optimality down to finite subsets of the support, and there it forces a combinatorial condition: no finite cyclic reshuffle of assigned pairs can lower the total cost. This is c-cyclical monotonicity, the structural signature of an optimal support, and the bridge to Kantorovich duality in the next chapter.

Polish Space Technicalities

Radon measures

On Polish spaces, Borel probability measures are inner regular.

Disintegration

Plans factor as $d\pi=d\mu(x)\,\pi_x(dy)$.

Gluing

Compatible plans can be glued on a common middle space.

Disintegration of a plan

$$\pi(dx\,dy)=\mu(dx)\,\pi_x(dy)$$

Payoff

These make tightness, Prokhorov, and restriction rigorous.

Printed pp. 43-50Polish Space Technicalities
The measurability scaffolding matters. Working on Polish spaces keeps measures Radon and inner regular, lets us disintegrate plans, and ensures the restriction and gluing operations are well defined. These technical hypotheses are not decoration; they are exactly what makes tightness, Prokhorov, and the direct method legitimately applicable.
