Entropy Drives the Flow

The functional

Relative entropy $H_\nu$ measures disorder against a reference $\nu = e^{-V}$.

Its gradient flow

Steepest descent in Wasserstein space is the Fokker–Planck equation.

Boltzmann entropy

$$H_\nu(\rho)=\int \rho\,\log\!\frac{\rho}{\nu}\,dx$$

Gradient flow = diffusion

$$\partial_t\rho=\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\rho\,\nabla(\log\rho+V)\big)$$

We fix the Boltzmann entropy relative to a reference measure as the energy that the flow minimizes. Its Wasserstein gradient flow is exactly the Fokker-Planck or heat equation, so the abstract machinery from the previous chapters now drives a concrete diffusion whose decay we can quantify.

Dissipation = Fisher Information

The identity

The rate at which entropy falls is exactly the Fisher information $I$.

Why it matters

Dissipation is computable: an integral of the squared score $\nabla\log(\rho/\nu)$.

Energy dissipation identity

$$\frac{d}{dt}H_\nu(\rho)=-I_\nu(\rho)\le 0$$

Fisher information

$$I_\nu(\rho)=\int \rho\,\big|\nabla\log\tfrac{\rho}{\nu}\big|^2\,dx$$

Printed pp. 719-730Entropy Dissipation Equals Fisher Information
The key bookkeeping identity says the time derivative of entropy along the flow is minus the Fisher information. Dissipation is therefore not abstract: it is a computable integral of the squared gradient of the log-density, the quantity that the lab shades as an inequality gap.

The HWI Inequality

Statement

Entropy is bounded by a transport–dissipation product, with a curvature correction.

Proof idea

Interpolate along the displacement geodesic from $\rho$ to $\nu$; read convexity off the second derivative.

Otto–Villani HWI

$$H_\nu(\rho)\le W_2(\rho,\nu)\sqrt{I_\nu(\rho)}-\tfrac{K}{2}\,W_2(\rho,\nu)^2$$

Reading

Names: H entropy, W distance, I information — an interpolation inequality.

Printed pp. 719-730The HWI Inequality
The HWI inequality bounds the entropy gap by a product of Wasserstein distance and the square root of Fisher information, corrected by a curvature term. Proved by Otto and Villani, it interpolates along the displacement geodesic between the current density and equilibrium, reading convexity off the second derivative.

Log-Sobolev Falls Out

The trick

Apply Young's inequality $ab\le \tfrac{a^2}{2K}+\tfrac{K b^2}{2}$ to the product $W\sqrt{I}$.

Outcome

The $W$ terms cancel; entropy is controlled by Fisher information alone.

Logarithmic Sobolev inequality

$$H_\nu(\rho)\le \frac{1}{2K}\,I_\nu(\rho)$$

Meaning

Entropy never exceeds $1/(2K)$ times its own dissipation rate.

Printed pp. 719-730Log-Sobolev from HWI
Setting the target to equilibrium and using Young's inequality on the HWI product collapses the W term, leaving entropy bounded by Fisher information over twice K. That is the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, the exact statement that entropy decays exponentially at rate two K along the flow.

Talagrand Falls Out Too

The trick

At equilibrium $I_\nu(\nu)=0$; drop the nonnegative Fisher term in HWI.

Outcome

Transport distance to equilibrium is bounded by entropy.

Talagrand transport inequality

$$W_2(\rho,\nu)^2\le \frac{2}{K}\,H_\nu(\rho)$$

Meaning

Small entropy forces the density to sit close to $\nu$ — concentration of measure.

Printed pp. 719-730Talagrand from HWI
Dropping the nonnegative Fisher term at equilibrium turns HWI into Talagrand's transport inequality: the squared Wasserstein distance to equilibrium is controlled by twice the entropy over K. Thus a single curvature bound delivers both concentration of measure and the spectral-gap-style decay simultaneously.

Exponential Relaxation

Gronwall

Dissipation $\dot H=-I$ plus log-Sobolev $I\ge 2K H$ gives $\dot H\le -2K H$.

Consequence

Entropy, and hence $W_2$, decays geometrically to zero.

Printed pp. 719-730Exponential Decay of Entropy
Combining the dissipation identity with the log-Sobolev inequality gives a Gronwall differential inequality for entropy. Its solution is exponential decay at rate two K, so the density relaxes to equilibrium geometrically. The Wasserstein distance inherits the same exponential rate through Talagrand.

The Otto–Villani Theorem

The implications

Under $K>0$: HWI $\Rightarrow$ log-Sobolev $\Rightarrow$ Talagrand.

The lesson

Wasserstein geometry, not ad hoc analysis, drives these implications.

Implication chain

$$\text{HWI}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{LSI}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{Talagrand}$$

Dimension-free

Constants depend on $K$ only, the curvature lower bound — no ambient dimension.

Printed pp. 719-730The Otto-Villani Theorem
Otto and Villani's theorem packages the logic: under a curvature-dimension lower bound, log-Sobolev implies Talagrand, and HWI implies both. The Riemannian geometry of Wasserstein space, not ad hoc analysis, is what makes these implications transparent and dimension-free in the right coordinates.
