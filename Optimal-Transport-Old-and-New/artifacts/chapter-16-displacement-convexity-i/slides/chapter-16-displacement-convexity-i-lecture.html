Two Ways to Interpolate Between Measures

Linear mixture

$\mu_t^{\mathrm{lin}}=(1-t)\mu_0+t\mu_1$. Averages densities pointwise — one peak splits into two.

Displacement

$\mu_t=\big((1-t)\,\mathrm{id}+t\,T\big)_\#\mu_0$. Slides mass along optimal segments — a single travelling bump.

Why it matters

The internal energy is convex on the second path, not the first.

Printed pp. 435-448Two Ways to Interpolate
Linear interpolation averages two densities pointwise, so a single peak typically splits into two. Displacement interpolation instead slides mass along optimal geodesics, producing one travelling bump. The internal energy behaves very differently on these two paths, and that contrast is the engine of the whole chapter.

McCann's Displacement Interpolation

Geodesic of measures

$$T_t=(1-t)\,\mathrm{id}+t\,T,\qquad \mu_t=(T_t)_\#\mu_0$$

Constant speed

Each particle moves on a straight segment at fixed speed.

Uniqueness

In flat $\mathbb{R}^n$ this is the unique $W_2$-geodesic between its endpoints.

Printed pp. 435-448McCann Displacement Interpolation
McCann's theorem builds the geodesic mu_t by pushing the source forward under the map that interpolates the optimal transport. Each particle travels a straight Euclidean segment at constant speed. The resulting curve of measures is the unique constant-speed Wasserstein geodesic between its endpoints in flat space.

Internal Energy Functionals

Definition

$$U_\nu(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} u\big(\rho(x)\big)\,d\nu(x)$$

Boltzmann entropy

$u(r)=r\log r$, giving $U(\rho)=\int \rho\log\rho$.

Power energies

$u(r)=\dfrac{r^{m}-r}{m-1}$ generate the Rényi family.

Printed pp. 435-448Internal Energy Functionals
Internal energy integrates a nonlinearity u of the density against the reference volume. Boltzmann entropy uses u of r equal to r log r; Renyi-type energies use power laws. These functionals measure concentration, and their convexity along displacement is the property we want to characterise sharply.

What Displacement Convexity Means

Definition

$$U(\mu_t)\le(1-t)\,U(\mu_0)+t\,U(\mu_1)$$

for every $W_2$-geodesic $(\mu_t)$ and all $t\in[0,1]$.

$\lambda$-convexity

$U(\mu_t)\le(1-t)U(\mu_0)+tU(\mu_1)-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)W_2^2$.

Key shift

Convexity along transport paths, not linear mixtures.

Reading the inequality

Restrict $U$ to a single Wasserstein geodesic; the restriction is an ordinary convex function of one variable $t$.

Modulus $\lambda$

A positive $\lambda$ gives strong convexity, controlling contraction and uniqueness of gradient-flow minimisers.

Why "right paths"

Many natural energies fail on mixtures yet remain convex along displacement — the geometry lives in the choice of path.

Printed pp. 435-448Definition of Displacement Convexity
A functional is displacement convex when its restriction to every Wasserstein geodesic is a convex function of the interpolation parameter. Lambda-convexity strengthens this with a quadratic modulus. This is convexity along the right paths, not along linear mixtures, and that distinction is everything here.

McCann's Condition on the Nonlinearity

Flat-space criterion ($\mathbb{R}^n$)

$$r\;\longmapsto\;r^{N}\,u\!\left(r^{-N}\right)$$

is convex and nonincreasing, where $N=n$ is the dimension.

Consequence

$U_\nu$ is displacement convex for every pair $\mu_0,\mu_1$.

Pressure form

Equivalent to convexity of the pressure $p(r)=r\,u'(r)-u(r)$ in $r^{-1/N}$.

Printed pp. 435-448McCann's Condition
McCann found the exact algebraic test in flat n-dimensional space. Rewrite the nonlinearity through the map r maps to r to the N times u of r to the minus N, where N is the dimension. If this transformed function is convex and nonincreasing, the internal energy is displacement convex for every pair of measures.

Where the Substitution Comes From

Change of variables

$$\rho_t(T_t x)=\frac{\rho_0(x)}{\det\big(\nabla T_t(x)\big)}$$

Jacobian dilation

$\det\nabla T_t = \prod(1-t+t\,\lambda_i)$ along eigenvalues of $\nabla T$.

Concavity of $\det^{1/N}$

The map $t\mapsto(\det\nabla T_t)^{1/N}$ is concave — the geometric heart.

Printed pp. 435-448Why the Substitution Appears
Along a geodesic the Jacobian of the transport map controls how volume dilates. Writing the density as mass over a determinant turns the integral into a function of that determinant raised to the power one over N. The convexity of the pressure profile in this variable is exactly McCann's condition.

Examples: Entropy and Rényi Energies

Boltzmann entropy

$$H(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\,dx$$

Passes McCann's test in every dimension $n$.

Rényi / power energies

$u(r)=\dfrac{r^m-r}{m-1}$ is displacement convex iff $m\ge 1-\tfrac1n$.

Below threshold

For $m<1-\tfrac1n$ convexity can fail; concentration is no longer penalised enough.

Printed pp. 435-448Entropy and Rényi Examples
Boltzmann entropy passes McCann's test in every dimension, so it is displacement convex in flat space. The Renyi entropies given by power-law nonlinearities are displacement convex precisely when their exponent lies above the dimensional threshold one minus one over n. Below it convexity can fail.

The Headline Link: Entropy and Ricci

Equivalence (manifold)

$$\mathrm{Ric}\ge 0 \iff H \text{ displacement convex}$$

Flat space

McCann's algebraic condition is the $\mathrm{Ric}=0$ case in disguise.

Curved space

A lower bound $\mathrm{Ric}\ge K$ gives $\lambda=K$ in $\lambda$-convexity.

Synthetic curvature

This turns into the Lott–Sturm–Villani definition for metric measure spaces.

Printed pp. 435-448The Headline Link to Ricci
On a manifold the flat criterion is no longer enough. Displacement convexity of the Boltzmann entropy along Wasserstein geodesics becomes equivalent to nonnegative Ricci curvature. Lower Ricci bounds translate into lambda-convexity moduli, opening the synthetic Lott-Sturm-Villani definition of curvature for metric measure spaces.
