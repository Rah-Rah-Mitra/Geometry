From a Static Plan to Moving Mass

What we had

An optimal coupling $\pi$ or map $T$ between two fixed measures $\mu_0,\mu_1$ minimizing the transport cost.

The new question

How does mass actually travel in time? We want a curve $t\mapsto\mu_t$, not just the two endpoints.

The answer is already there

The optimal plan tells each grain where to go; let it move at constant speed along the straight ray.

Static problem

$\displaystyle \min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_0,\mu_1)}\int |x-y|^2\,d\pi(x,y)$

Dynamic upgrade

$\text{grain at }x \;\longrightarrow\; (1-t)\,x + t\,T(x)$

Reading

The displacement interpolation is the law of that moving grain at each time $t\in[0,1]$.

The Interpolation Formula

Map form (Euclidean)

$\mu_t = \big((1-t)\,\mathrm{id} + t\,T\big)_\# \mu_0$

Plan form (general)

$\mu_t = \big(e_t\big)_\#\,\pi,\quad e_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty$

Why straight

The quadratic cost makes minimizing geodesics straight segments, so the motion is explicit.

Brenier map

For absolutely continuous $\mu_0$, the optimal $T=\nabla\varphi$ is the gradient of a convex potential, and the displacement map $T_t = (1-t)\,\mathrm{id}+t\,\nabla\varphi$ stays a gradient of a convex function.

No crossings

Optimal rays for quadratic cost do not cross transversally, so $T_t$ is injective for $t<1$ and $\mu_t$ has a density.

These Paths Are Wasserstein Geodesics

Theorem (McCann)

The displacement interpolation $(\mu_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ is a constant-speed geodesic of $(\mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^n),W_2)$ joining $\mu_0$ to $\mu_1$, and every such geodesic arises this way.

Constant speed

$W_2(\mu_s,\mu_t)=|t-s|\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)$

Displacement vs Linear Interpolation

Displacement (moves mass)

One bump slides across the domain, staying a single travelling peak. This is the geodesic $\mu_t$.

Linear (fades mass)

$\rho_t=(1-t)\rho_0+t\rho_1$ shrinks one bump while a second grows: two peaks, mass teleports.

Verdict

Linear interpolation is not a $W_2$-geodesic; its length strictly exceeds $W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)$.

The Benamou-Brenier Action

Dynamic formulation

$W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2=\inf\int_0^1\!\!\int |v_t|^2\,d\mu_t\,dt$

Constraint

The infimum runs over all $(\mu_t,v_t)$ solving the continuity equation with the prescribed endpoints.

Least action

The geodesic is the curve of minimal kinetic energy: a least-action principle for measures.

Manifolds and the Lagrangian Action

Beyond Euclidean

Recap and Outlook

Formula

$\mu_t=((1-t)\,\mathrm{id}+tT)_\#\mu_0$ moves mass along optimal rays.

Geodesic

It is the constant-speed $W_2$-geodesic; speed equals $W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)$.

Least action

Benamou-Brenier recasts $W_2$ as minimal kinetic action under the continuity equation.

One picture to keep

$\mu_t = (e_t)_\#\pi,\qquad W_2(\mu_s,\mu_t)=|t-s|\,W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)$

What comes next

This length-space structure opens the door to displacement convexity, gradient flows, and Ricci curvature bounds in later chapters.

Contrast to remember

Displacement moves mass; linear interpolation merely fades it and is never a geodesic.
