Talagrand's $T_2$ Inequality

Definition

$$W_2(\mu,\nu)^2 \;\le\; \frac{2}{K}\,H(\nu\,|\,\mu)$$

Left side

$W_2$: quadratic Wasserstein distance, a geometric transport cost.

Right side

$H(\nu|\mu)$: relative entropy of any competitor $\nu$.

One constant, two roles

The same $K$ controls transport cost and the strength of concentration.

Holds for all $\nu$

The bound is uniform over every probability measure $\nu\ll\mu$.

Equivalent form

$W_2(\mu,\nu)\le\sqrt{2\,H(\nu|\mu)/K}$.

Printed pp. 567-628Talagrand's T2 Inequality
Talagrand's transport-entropy inequality bounds the squared quadratic Wasserstein distance from any probability measure to the reference by twice its relative entropy divided by the curvature constant. We read each symbol carefully and stress that the same constant K controls both the transport cost and the eventual strength of concentration we will derive.

Entropy Controls Transport

Information distance

$H(\nu|\mu)$ measures how far $\nu$ sits from equilibrium in bits.

Geometric distance

$W_2$ measures the physical cost of moving $\mu$ onto $\nu$.

The bargain

Cheap entropy forbids expensive transport.

Printed pp. 567-628Entropy Controls Transport
The right side measures how far a competitor density sits from equilibrium in information; the left side measures the geometric cost of physically transporting equilibrium onto it. T2 asserts that cheap entropy forbids expensive transport, so distributions that are close in entropy simply cannot drift far away in Wasserstein distance.

The Logarithmic Sobolev Inequality

Definition (LSI)

$$H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\;\le\;\frac{1}{2K}\,I(\nu\,|\,\mu)$$

Entropy

$H(\nu|\mu)$ on the left, as before.

Fisher information

$I(\nu|\mu)=\int|\nabla\log\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}|^2\,d\nu$.

One rung up

LSI is a stronger statement than $T_2$.

The engine

Differentiating along the heat flow, Fisher information drives entropy decay to equilibrium.

Same constant

The curvature $K$ reappears as $1/2K$.

Printed pp. 567-628The Logarithmic Sobolev Inequality
The log-Sobolev inequality bounds the entropy of a density by its Fisher information up to the constant one over two K. It sits one rung above Talagrand: differentiating along the heat flow turns Fisher information into the engine that drives entropy decay, and ultimately yields the transport bound we want.

From LSI down to $T_2$

Otto-Villani

LSI implies Talagrand's $T_2$ with the identical constant $K$.

The mechanism

Integrate the Hamilton-Jacobi semigroup along a $W_2$ geodesic.

One way only

The converse fails in general — $T_2$ is strictly weaker.

The hierarchy

$$\text{LSI}\;\Rightarrow\;T_2\;\Rightarrow\;\text{Poincaré}$$

Printed pp. 567-628From LSI down to T2
Otto and Villani showed the logarithmic Sobolev inequality implies Talagrand's T2 with the identical constant, by integrating the Hamilton-Jacobi semigroup along a Wasserstein geodesic. The converse generally fails, so T2 is strictly weaker, sitting between the Poincaré inequality and the full log-Sobolev inequality in the standard hierarchy.

Marton's Argument

Coupling, not calculus

Convert any $T_2$ measure directly into concentration, no derivatives needed.

Two conditioned measures

Restrict $\mu$ to a set $A$ and to the complement of its enlargement.

Pinsker bridge

Entropy of a conditioning equals $-\log\mu(A)$.

Marton's bound

$$W_2(\mu_A,\mu_{B^c})\ge r,\quad \mu(B^c)\le e^{-\,\tfrac{K}{2}\,(r-r_0)^2}$$

Outcome

An exponential bound on the complement of an $r$-neighbourhood, straight from $T_2$.

Printed pp. 567-628Marton's Argument
Marton's coupling argument converts any T2 measure directly into concentration. Bounding the transport distance between the conditioned measure on a set and the conditioned measure on its enlargement's complement, then applying Pinsker-type entropy control, yields an exponential bound on the complement of a neighbourhood without ever differentiating a single function.

Bobkov-Götze Duality

Kantorovich duality

Recast $T_2$ as a statement about Lipschitz test functions.

Equivalent form

$T_2$ holds iff every $1$-Lipschitz $f$ has a sub-Gaussian Laplace transform.

Why it matters

The median tail estimate now falls out mechanically.

Dual characterisation

$$\int e^{\lambda(f-\mathbb{E}f)}\,d\mu\;\le\;e^{\lambda^2/(2K)}$$

Reading it

The moment generating function is bounded by a Gaussian with variance $1/K$ — a single curvature constant.

Printed pp. 567-628Bobkov-Gotze Duality
Bobkov and Götze recast T2 through Kantorovich duality: the transport inequality is equivalent to a sub-Gaussian bound on the Laplace transform of every one-Lipschitz function. This dual reading is exactly what makes the median tail estimate fall out mechanically from a single curvature constant, with no further geometric input required.

Gaussian-Thin Tails

Headline estimate

$$\mu\big(f-\mathbb{E}f\ge r\big)\;\le\;e^{-K r^2/2}$$

Derivation

Sub-Gaussian Laplace bound + Chebyshev's exponential trick, optimised in $\lambda$.

Genuinely Gaussian

Quadratic exponent, not merely exponential decay.

Printed pp. 567-628Gaussian-Thin Tails
Combining the dual bound with Chebyshev's exponential trick gives the headline estimate: a one-Lipschitz function exceeds its mean by r with probability at most the exponential of minus K r squared over two. The tails are genuinely Gaussian in shape, with their width governed by the curvature K rather than by dimension.
