Action-Minimizing Curves

Transport side

Displacement interpolation moves mass along minimizing geodesics $\gamma$.

Mather side

Action-minimizing curves of a Lagrangian $L(x,v,t)$.

Shared property

Each curve realizes the infimum of an action between its endpoints.

Action functional

$$\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1} L\big(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t),t\big)\,dt$$

Minimality

$$\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=\min_{\sigma:\,\sigma(0)=\gamma(0),\,\sigma(1)=\gamma(1)}\mathcal{A}(\sigma)$$

Only hypothesis

Minimality is all the shortening argument ever uses.

Recall that displacement interpolation moves mass along minimizing geodesics, and Mather theory studies action-minimizing curves of a Lagrangian. Both share one defining property: each curve realizes the infimum of an action functional between its endpoints. That minimality is the only hypothesis the shortening argument will ever exploit.

The Reconnection Move

Contradiction

Rewired pair is strictly cheaper, but originals were minimizers.

Triangle-type gain

$$\mathcal{A}(\tilde\gamma_1)+\mathcal{A}(\tilde\gamma_2)<\mathcal{A}(\gamma_1)+\mathcal{A}(\gamma_2)$$

Read it as

Total action strictly drops under reconnection.

Conclusion

Near-crossings of true minimizers are impossible.

Printed pp. 163-204The Reconnection Move
Here is the heart of the chapter. Swap the tails of two near-crossing curves at the meeting point, then smooth the corner. Triangle-type inequalities for the action say the rewired pair is strictly cheaper. Because the originals were minimizers, this contradiction shows near-crossings are impossible.

The Shortening Estimate

From no-crossing to a bound

Closeness at two times forces closeness throughout.

Monge-Mather estimate

Controls how far minimizers can drift apart.

Role

The precise inequality invoked by every later regularity result.

Uniform closeness

$$\sup_{r\in[0,1]}|\gamma_1(r)-\gamma_2(r)|\;\le\;C\big(|\gamma_1(0)-\gamma_2(0)|+|\gamma_1(1)-\gamma_2(1)|\big)$$

Quantitative shortening

$$|\gamma_1(s)-\gamma_2(t)|\le\varepsilon \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{pair stays within } K\varepsilon$$

Printed pp. 163-204The Shortening Estimate
The qualitative no-crossing statement upgrades to a quantitative bound: if two minimizers are close at two times, the whole pair stays uniformly close. This Monge-Mather estimate controls how far trajectories can drift, and it is the precise inequality that all later regularity results invoke.
