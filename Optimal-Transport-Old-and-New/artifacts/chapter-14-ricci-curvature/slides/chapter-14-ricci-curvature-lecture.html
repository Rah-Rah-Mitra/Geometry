From Sectional to Ricci Curvature

Sectional $K(u,v)$

Curvature of the 2-plane spanned by two directions $u,v$.

Ricci $\mathrm{Ric}(v,v)$

Trace over an orthonormal frame transverse to $v$ — an average of sectionals.

Averaging

$\mathrm{Ric}(v,v)=\sum_{i}K(e_i,v)$

Printed pp. 357-420From Sectional to Ricci
Sectional curvature lives on a plane; it tells you how two geodesics in that plane converge or diverge. Ricci curvature averages sectional curvature over all planes containing a fixed direction. So Ricci in direction v measures the net focusing felt by a whole transverse bundle of geodesics travelling alongside v.

Geodesic Deviation & Jacobi Fields

Jacobi equation

$\ddot J + R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma = 0$

What $J$ is

The infinitesimal separation between neighbouring geodesics.

Sign rule

Curvature $>0$ pulls $J$ inward (focusing); $<0$ pushes it out (spreading).

Printed pp. 357-420Geodesic Deviation and Jacobi Fields
A Jacobi field is the infinitesimal separation between neighbouring geodesics. Its acceleration obeys the Jacobi equation, governed by the curvature operator. Positive curvature makes the separation accelerate inward, focusing the family; negative curvature drives it apart. This is the mechanical heart of every curvature estimate in the chapter.

The Bochner Formula

The analytic identity where Ricci enters calculus

Bochner–Weitzenböck

$\tfrac12\Delta|\nabla f|^2 = \|\nabla^2 f\|^2 + \langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta f\rangle + \mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)$

Hessian term

$\|\nabla^2 f\|^2 \ge \tfrac{(\Delta f)^2}{n}$ injects the dimension $n$.

Ricci term

The only place curvature appears — a lower bound makes it controllable.

Why it matters

Replacing $\mathrm{Ric}$ by $K\,g$ and the Hessian by its dimensional bound turns Bochner into the curvature-dimension inequality $\Gamma_2 \ge K\,\Gamma + \tfrac1N(\Delta f)^2$.

Printed pp. 357-420The Bochner Formula
Bochner's identity is the analytic engine. It expresses the Laplacian of half the squared gradient as the Hessian norm plus a gradient-of-Laplacian term plus a Ricci term acting on the gradient. The Ricci term is exactly where curvature enters analysis, and it is what makes lower Ricci bounds controllable through inequalities.

Ricci as Volume Distortion

Flow a small ball

Push a tiny geodesic ball along geodesics; its volume evolves by the Jacobian of the exponential map.

Volume rate

$\mathrm{vol}(B_r)=\omega_n r^n\Big(1-\tfrac{\mathrm{Ric}(v,v)}{6(n+2)}r^2+\cdots\Big)$

Reading the sign

$\mathrm{Ric}>0$ shrinks the ball below flat volume; $\mathrm{Ric}<0$ swells it.

Printed pp. 357-420Volume Distortion of a Ball
Track a small geodesic ball as it flows along geodesics. Its volume changes at a rate set by Ricci curvature. Positive Ricci compresses the ball faster than flat space, negative Ricci lets it swell. The Jacobian of the exponential map is the bookkeeping device, and its determinant is the volume the gas must occupy.

Distortion Coefficients

Packaging a Ricci bound $K$ and a dimension bound $N$

Coefficient (model space)

$\beta_t^{(K,N)}(x,y)=\Big(\tfrac{\sin(t\,\alpha)}{t\,\sin\alpha}\Big)^{N-1},\;\; \alpha=d\sqrt{\tfrac{K}{N-1}}$

Interpolation in $t$

Compares the volume at the displacement parameter $t$ to the flat value $t$.

Notebook lab

Orders the $K>0,\,K=0,\,K<0$ curves and checks small-distance behaviour.

Printed pp. 357-420Distortion Coefficients
Distortion coefficients package a Ricci lower bound K together with a dimension upper bound N into explicit comparison functions. They interpolate the volume of intermediate points along a geodesic against the flat reference. The notebook orders the positive, zero, and negative curvature curves and checks numerically that small-distance behaviour matches the sign of curvature.

The Lazy Gas Experiment

Villani's heuristic for why curvature controls entropy

The setup

A gas redistributes from density $\rho_0$ to $\rho_1$ at least transport cost.

The path

It moves along displacement interpolation $(\mu_t)$, the Wasserstein geodesic.

The laziness

Positive curvature focuses the geodesics, so the gas may pass through compressed, low-entropy states.

Printed pp. 357-420The Lazy Gas Experiment
Here is Villani's signature heuristic. A gas of particles must redistribute itself from one density to another at least cost. Along the optimal displacement interpolation it passes through intermediate configurations. In positive curvature the geodesics focus, so the gas can momentarily occupy lower-volume, lower-entropy states. The gas is lazy and exploits that focusing.

Entropy Along Displacement

Boltzmann entropy

$H(\mu)=\int \rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{dvol}$

$K$-convexity along $(\mu_t)$

$H(\mu_t)\le (1-t)H(\mu_0)+t\,H(\mu_1)-\tfrac{K}{2}t(1-t)\,W_2^2$

Larger $K$

A bigger Ricci bound bends the entropy curve down more strongly.

Printed pp. 357-420Entropy Along Displacement
Translate laziness into a formula. The Boltzmann entropy evaluated along the displacement interpolation is more convex when Ricci is larger. A lower bound Ric at least K g forces a quantitative convexity inequality for the entropy functional. This is the reformulation that turns a tensor statement into a statement about a functional on probability measures.

Lower Ricci $\equiv$ Entropy Convexity

Equivalence (smooth $M$)

$\mathrm{Ric}\ge K\,g \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; H \text{ is } K\text{-convex along } W_2\text{-geodesics}$

$\Rightarrow$ direction

Bochner and Jacobi fields turn the tensor bound into the entropy inequality.

$\Leftarrow$ direction

Probing with sharply concentrated measures recovers $\mathrm{Ric}(v,v)$ pointwise.

Printed pp. 357-420Lower Ricci Equals Entropy Convexity
State the equivalence cleanly. On a smooth manifold, Ric greater than or equal to K g holds if and only if relative entropy is K-displacement-convex along every Wasserstein geodesic. The forward direction uses Bochner and Jacobi fields; the converse recovers the tensor by probing with concentrated measures. Both readings describe the same curvature.

The Curvature-Dimension Condition $\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$

Two inputs

$K$ — a Ricci lower bound. $N$ — a dimension upper bound.

Dimensional entropy convexity

$\mathcal{U}_N(\mu_t)\le (1-t)\,\mathcal{U}_N(\mu_0)+t\,\mathcal{U}_N(\mu_1)$ with $\beta_t^{(K,N)}$ weights

Sharper

Distortion coefficients $\beta_t^{(K,N)}$ refine plain $K$-convexity.

Written in transport

$\mathrm{CD}(K,N)$ uses only a metric $d$, a reference measure $\mathrm{vol}$, and the entropy along $W_2$-geodesics — no charts, no Christoffel symbols.

Printed pp. 357-420The Curvature-Dimension Condition
Refine the bound by adding dimension. The condition CD(K,N) couples a Ricci lower bound K with a dimension upper bound N through the distortion coefficients in the entropy inequality. It sharpens displacement convexity and, crucially, it is written entirely in transport language, so it survives where charts and tensors do not.
