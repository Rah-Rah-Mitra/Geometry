Six Smooth Length Mechanisms

1. Riemannian cost

Every tangent space carries an inner product \(g_p\); length is accumulated continuously.

2. Exponential map

Initial velocity data launches geodesics and builds normal coordinates near a point.

3. Hyperbolic model

The disk looks Euclidean but measures distance with boundary blow-up.

4. Horizontal motion

Sub-Riemannian curves are allowed to move only in selected directions.

5. Metric volume

Riemannian determinants and Finsler unit balls define competing density rules.

6. Besikovitch lock

Opposite-face distances force a lower bound on volume.

Visible grounding: sections 5.1-5.6All prose and diagrams are course-artifact based, not textbook copied.
Use the six mechanisms as a map, not as a list. The same sentence should keep returning: choose admissible tangent vectors, assign an infinitesimal cost, integrate along curves, and then extract metric consequences. This prepares students to compare Riemannian, hyperbolic, sub-Riemannian, and Finsler behavior.
Section 5.1

Riemannian Length Is Integrated Tangent Cost

Definition

A smooth metric tensor assigns each \(p \in M\) an inner product \(g_p\) on \(T_pM\).

\(L_g(\gamma)=\int_a^b \sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))}\,dt\)

Distance Recovery

  • Choose a curve \(\gamma\) joining \(x\) to \(y\).
  • Compute its infinitesimal speed by \(g\).
  • Take the infimum over all connecting curves.

The metric space appears after a variational step.

Source span: printed pp. 135-208; PDF pp. 150-223Formula rendered with KaTeX
Make the definition operational. A Riemannian metric is a smoothly varying inner product, and the length of a curve is the integral of its instantaneous norm. Stress that the distance is not specified directly; it is recovered by minimizing lengths of connecting curves.

The Exponential Map Launches Geodesics

Local Recipe

Given \(v \in T_pM\), follow the geodesic with initial velocity \(v\) for time \(1\). The endpoint is \(\exp_p(v)\).

\(\exp_p(tv)=\gamma_v(t)\)

Introduce the exponential map as the geodesic launcher. A vector at p records initial direction and speed; following the geodesic for unit time lands on the manifold. The point is local coordinate production, not global one-to-one behavior.

Geometric Reading

This makes the radius parameter a first-order distance coordinate.

Section 5.3

Poincare Disk Warning: Euclidean Pictures Can Mislead

Warning

The disk is visually bounded, but the hyperbolic boundary is infinitely far away.

Euclidean straightness is not the criterion for hyperbolic geodesics.

Metric

\(\displaystyle ds=\frac{2\,|dz|}{1-|z|^2}\)
  • Angles are preserved by the conformal model.
  • Lengths blow up near the ideal boundary.
Interactive artifact: ../html/hyperbolic-poincare-lab.htmlCheck: hyperbolic-model-checks.json
The Poincare disk is a warning against trusting Euclidean appearance. The boundary is infinitely far away in the hyperbolic metric, geodesics are boundary-orthogonal arcs, and angles are Euclidean-looking because the model is conformal. Ask students to separate three ideas that the drawing overlays: topology of an open disk, Euclidean screen coordinates, and the hyperbolic length element.

Section 5.4

Sub-Riemannian Metrics Restrict The Legal Directions

Admissible Curves

A curve is measured only when its velocity lies in a prescribed horizontal distribution \(H \subset TM\).

\(\dot\gamma(t)\in H_{\gamma(t)}\)

Metric Consequence

  • Length is still integrated along curves.
  • The allowed path family is smaller.
  • Brackets may create access to missing directions.
Source span: Chapter 5, section 5.4Next slide reads the ball-box artifact.
Sub-Riemannian geometry changes the admissible directions rather than merely changing their weights. Curves must stay horizontal, so the metric is defined through constrained controls. This makes reachability a bracket question. Emphasize that forbidden instantaneous motion may still be produced indirectly by short loops, which is why local geometry becomes anisotropic at different scales.

Section 5.5

Riemannian Volume Versus Finsler Volume

Artifact: ../figures/volume-comparison.png

Riemannian

Volume density is governed by \(\sqrt{\det g}\).

Finsler

A normed tangent space has a unit ball; volume conventions read either that ball or its polar.

area integral 5.206l2 density errors 0, 0
Check: volume-comparison-checks.jsonTable: ../tables/finsler-volume-table.csv
Separate the two volume stories. Riemannian volume comes from the determinant of the metric tensor. Finsler volume needs a convention because the unit ball can be non-elliptic; Busemann and Holmes-Thompson encode different normalizations. Point out that both conventions agree on the Euclidean disk but respond differently to diamond and square tangent balls.

Section 5.6

Besikovitch Inequality: Face Distances Force Area

Artifact: ../figures/besikovitch-cube-map.png

Two-Dimensional Reading

A square-like surface with opposite faces separated by \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) must carry area at least \(d_1d_2\).

area 0.6888product 0.5666margin 0.1221
Check: besikovitch-checks.jsonInequality verified for model: true
State Besikovitch as a lower-bound principle. If a cube-like region keeps opposite faces separated by prescribed distances, then its volume cannot collapse below the product scale. The model verifies a positive margin between area and the product bound.

The proof roadmap is the topological lock. Distance-to-face functions give nonexpanding coordinates, boundary behavior forces a degree-one map, and degree one forces coverage of the target box. Analysis then turns nonexpansion into a Jacobian bound.

Recap and exit exercise

From Tangent Cost To Global Constraint

Three Takeaways

  • Smooth length begins with a tangent norm, not a distance table.
  • Geodesics and normal coordinates translate infinitesimal data into local metric structure.
  • Volume inequalities detect global constraints that local pictures alone can miss.

Exit Exercise
