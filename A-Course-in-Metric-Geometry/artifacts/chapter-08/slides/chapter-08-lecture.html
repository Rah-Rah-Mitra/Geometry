Section 8.1

Pointed Balls Turn a Finite Cycle Into the Integer Line

Inspection Window

Fix radius \(R=4\) around a basepoint and compare the induced distance matrix.

Check

Cycles with 20 and 40 vertices match the integer interval; the 8-cycle still sees wraparound.

Limit Signal

For each fixed radius, sufficiently large cycles look locally like \(\mathbb{Z}\).

artifact: pointed-cycle-balls-limit.pngeventual local stability: true
The cycle example is intentionally finite but noncompact in the limit. At radius four, the small cycle still wraps around and fails the interval test, while larger cycles have pointed balls that match the integer line. Students should see convergence as eventual agreement of every fixed inspection window.
Definition pattern

Pointed Convergence Is Approximate Matching on Every Fixed Ball

Quantifier Order

For every fixed radius \(R\) and tolerance \(\varepsilon\), the large \(n\) balls admit an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate comparison.

Basepoint Role

The marked point prevents the comparison from drifting away to a different part of the space.

\((X_n,p_n) \to (X,p)\)
printed pages 271-274pointed Gromov-Hausdorff viewpoint
Present the definition as a controlled matching problem. A basepoint anchors the comparison, a radius chooses the visible window, and an error tolerance allows approximate distance preservation. The order of quantifiers matters: fix the window first, then go far enough along the sequence.

Section 8.2

Rescaling Brings Infinity Into a Fixed Window

Asymptotic Cone Idea

Study \((X,\lambda_n d,p)\) with \(\lambda_n \to 0\). Large original distances become visible at bounded scale.

Lattice Signal

The sampled \(\lambda\mathbb{Z}^2\) grid fills the same display window more densely as \(\lambda\) decreases.

far away + rescale = cone view
artifact: cone-rescaling-snapshots.pngPDF pages 290-291
After pointed limits, rescaling asks a complementary question: instead of increasing the object, shrink the metric so farther and farther regions fit into a fixed window. For the lattice, each scale halves the mesh and the sampled window begins to look like a continuum.
Metric rescaling

The Formula Is Simple; the Limit Is the Geometry

\(d_\lambda(x,y)=\lambda d(x,y)\)

Ball Conversion

\(B_{(X,\lambda d)}(p,R)=B_{(X,d)}(p,R/\lambda)\). A bounded rescaled ball sees a larger original ball.

Covering Radius

For \(\lambda\mathbb{Z}^2\), the Euclidean covering bound scales like \(\lambda/\sqrt{2}\).

Section 8.3

Quasi-Isometries Keep Geometry After Bounded Distortion

Distance Control

\(\lambda^{-1}d_X(x,x')-C \le d_Y(fx,fx') \le \lambda d_X(x,x')+C\)

Coarse Onto

Every point of the target lies within a bounded distance of the image.

Meaning

Local wrinkles may disappear, but large-scale dimension, ends, and growth type can remain detectable.

PDF pages 292-298coarse equivalence relation
Quasi-isometry is the chapter's coarse equivalence relation. Exact equality of distances is replaced by multiplicative and additive control, and a coarse-surjectivity condition prevents the target from having unseen regions. Emphasize that bounded local damage is allowed because the theory studies the far field.

Section 8.4

Hyperbolicity Is a Tree-Like Diagnostic for Geodesic Triangles

Tree Model

In a tree, geodesic triangles collapse onto a tripod. Thinness residuals vanish.

Failure Mode

Large rectangles and cycles create points on one side that sit far from the other two sides.

\(\delta=0\) for the sampled binary tree
artifact: hyperbolicity-diagnostics.pngPDF pages 299-312
The tree-like diagnostic should be explained before the word hyperbolic becomes formal. In a tree, geodesic triangles are forced through shared branches; in grids and cycles, rectangles and loops create thick triangles. Hyperbolicity measures how large that thickness must be.

Recap and exercise

What Survives at Infinity?

Limits

Pointed balls and rescaled cones turn asymptotic behavior into finite inspection windows.

Equivalence

Quasi-isometries preserve coarse structure while forgiving bounded local errors.

Diagnostics

Growth, hyperbolicity residuals, and stable-norm areas expose different large-scale features.
