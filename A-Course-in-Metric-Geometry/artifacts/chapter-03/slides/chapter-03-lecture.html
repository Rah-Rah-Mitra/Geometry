Section 3.1

Local Agreement Can Hide Global Disagreement

Jump Metric

Allow chains whose original jumps satisfy \(d(x_i,x_{i+1})\le \varepsilon\), then minimize the sum of the jump lengths.

Lesson

A complete non-intrinsic metric can imitate the local distances yet produce a different global travel metric.

\(d_\varepsilon(x,y)=\inf \sum_i d(x_i,x_{i+1})\)
artifact: locality-epsilon-jumps.pnglocal error check: 0.0
Use the jump metric as the first warning. Two metrics can agree locally yet disagree globally when one is not intrinsic. On the sampled circle, small chord jumps force travel around the circle, so the epsilon-jump distance remembers path length that the chord distance hides.
Section 3.1

Gluing Is the Maximal Semimetric Compatible With the Rules

Closure Principle

Declare upper bounds and zero identifications; then take the largest semimetric below all polygonal-route bounds.

Computation

The notebook models this as all-pairs shortest-path closure over the declared bounds.

Invariant

Recorded triangle violations after closure: 0.

artifact: gluing-maximal-semimetric.pngmaximal metric after identification
Gluing should be presented as a metric closure operation, not merely as identifying points. Once a zero-cost edge is declared, triangle inequality propagates new upper bounds through every polygonal route, and the quotient distance is the largest semimetric compatible with those bounds.

Section 3.2

Polyhedral Spaces Are Glued Pieces Plus Local Links

Pieces

Euclidean faces supply local metrics; face isometries say which boundary points are the same.

Links

A link records the local cone of directions: flat inside a face, graph-like along edges, and richer at vertices.

interior link length \(=2\pi\)
artifact: polyhedral-links-and-word-ball.pnglinks encode local cone models
Polyhedral spaces package gluing in Euclidean pieces. A face point looks locally flat, an edge point has a link recording adjacent sectors, and a vertex can have a cone over a more complicated link. The link is the local data that tells us what kind of metric neighborhood we built.
Combinatorial constructions

Metric Graphs and Word Metrics Are Shortest-Route Machines

One-Dimensional Polyhedra

Edges are length pieces. Vertices are gluing sites. The metric is shortest path length through the graph.

Cayley Ball

The notebook records radius 3, 25 vertices, and 36 edges for a sample \(\mathbb{Z}^2\) word ball.

section 3.2combinatorial gluing as route minimization
Metric graphs and word metrics are the one-dimensional and combinatorial face of the same idea. Edges are pieces, vertices are gluing sites, and distance is shortest route length. In a Cayley graph, the route count becomes algebraic word length with respect to chosen generators.
Section 3.3

Quotient Metrics Compare Orbits, Not Just Points

Orbit Formula

\(d([x],[y])=\inf_{g,h} d(gx,hy)\)

Free Action

Translations have no fixed point, so small neighborhoods project regularly.

Fixed Point

A stabilizer can collapse directions and produce singular quotient behavior.

artifact: quotients-coverings-local-isometry.pngtranslation action has fixed point: false
Quotients by isometries are safe when orbit representatives can be compared without creating fixed-point singularities. The orbit formula minimizes over representatives. Free translation actions preserve local geometry, while fixed-point actions compress directions into a singular quotient point.
Section 3.4

Coverings Repeat Local Geometry Sheet by Sheet

Local Sheet Test

For small enough base neighborhoods, each component upstairs maps isometrically onto the same neighborhood.

Deck Motion

Deck transformations move sheets to sheets and preserve the lifted length metric.

\(\mathbb{R}\to S^1\), deck step \(2\pi\)
local isometry plus repeated sheetscovering projection
Coverings are the local-isometry version of repeated geometry. Around a small enough arc in the base, the covering splits into separate sheets upstairs, and each sheet maps isometrically to the same base neighborhood. Deck transformations move sheets without changing length.
Section 3.5

Arcwise Isometries Preserve Path Length, Not Necessarily Points

Path-Length Claim

Every curve has the same length after mapping, so the length structure is preserved.

But Not Injective

The pleat folds source intervals onto overlapping target intervals.

piecewise \(|f'|=1\)
artifact: arcwise-isometry-pleat.pngsample interval length errors near zero
Arcwise isometry is a length-structure statement, not an injectivity statement. The pleat map folds, so different source points can land together, but each path interval has the same length because the absolute slope is one on each linear piece.
Section 3.6

Product Metrics Split Geodesic Bookkeeping by Factors

Formula

\(d^2=d_X(x_1,x_2)^2+d_Y(y_1,y_2)^2\)

Midpoint Check

The notebook records left and right distances agreeing up to \(2.3\cdot10^{-16}\).

product midpoint splits by factorssection 3.6
The product metric is the Pythagorean construction for length spaces. Distances in the factors combine by a square root formula, and a midpoint in the product splits into midpoint choices in each factor. The notebook's numerical check verifies this split.
Section 3.6

Cone Distances Use Angular Data, Then Clip at \(\pi\)

Cone Law of Cosines

\(r^2+s^2-2rs\cos(\min\{d_X,\pi\})\)

Truncation

Large base separations no longer make wider angles; after \(\pi\), the radial route through the apex can dominate.

artifact: product-cone-metrics.pngcone truncation beyond pi
The cone construction turns a base metric into angular data. The law of cosines controls distance between radial points, but the base separation is clipped at pi. When the angular gap is large enough, the shortest route can prefer the apex.

Construction quality control

Ask for the Invariant That Certifies the Construction

Locality

Small-jump distance agrees locally but may change global travel.

Gluing

Triangle inequality holds after maximal semimetric closure.

Polyhedra

Links record the local cone model at faces, edges, and vertices.

Quotients

Orbit distance minimizes over representatives and exposes stabilizers.

Coverings

Small neighborhoods lift to isometric sheets.

Products/Cones

Midpoints split in products; cone angles truncate at \(\pi\).

artifact checks: all PNGs nonblankfinal assertions all true
The invariant checklist is the quality-control habit for constructions. For each recipe, ask which identity or inequality proves the metric has survived: triangle inequality, local agreement, orbit minimization, sheetwise local isometry, path-length preservation, product midpoint, or cone truncation.
Common traps

Four Mistakes This Chapter Trains Us to Avoid

Topology Alone Does Not Set Quotient Distance

Triangle inequality can force additional shrinkage after gluing.

Local Agreement Is Not Always Global Control

Non-intrinsic metrics can pass local tests and fail over long routes.

Quotients Need Stabilizer Awareness

Fixed points can create singular behavior even when the action is by isometries.

Length Preservation Does Not Force Injectivity

Arcwise isometries may fold while preserving path length.

use examples as diagnostic testsnot just definitions to memorize
The most common mistakes are assuming topology determines metric gluing, assuming local agreement determines global distance, assuming quotient maps are always locally regular, and assuming length preservation forces injectivity. These are exactly the traps the chapter's examples are designed to expose.
