Local CBB test

The Quadruple Condition Reads Curvature as an Angle Fan

Local Test

Choose \(p,x,y,z\) close together and compare the three model angles at \(p\).

\(\tilde\angle xpy+\tilde\angle ypz+\tilde\angle zpx \le 2\pi\)

Interpretation

The space may be singular, but it cannot pack more angular opening around \(p\) than the lower-bound model permits.

artifact: quadruple-toponogov-comparison.pngsource span: 351-404
The local quadruple condition is the first working test. Given four nearby points, compare the model angles that would be seen from one vertex and ask whether the angle fan is too large. For lower curvature bounds, the geometry refuses to fold inward beyond the model allowance. Emphasize that this is a local diagnostic, not yet the full Toponogov theorem.
From local to global

Hinge Comparison Turns Curvature Into a Measurable Inequality

Hinge Data

Two geodesic sides and an included angle determine a model hinge in \(M^2_\kappa\).

Comparison Move

Measure the third side in the space, then compare it with the side in the constant-curvature model.

Local lab: toponogov-hinge-lab.html

hinges are triangle comparisons in minimal forminteractive artifact linked locally
A hinge is the simplest global comparison unit: two geodesic sides with an included angle. In a curvature-bounded-below space, the side opposite the hinge is controlled by the corresponding model triangle. The point of the slide is to turn the abstract curvature hypothesis into a concrete measurement game: fix two sides and an angle, then compare the third side.

Smooth

Riemannian manifolds with sectional curvature \(\ge \kappa\).

Polyhedral

Piecewise Euclidean or spherical spaces with link conditions.

Convex

Boundaries of convex bodies carry lower-curvature comparison behavior.

artifact: constructions-cones-quotients.pngexamples prepare singular local models
Use examples to keep the definition from becoming too ethereal. Smooth Riemannian manifolds with sectional curvature bounded below fit the theory, but so do many singular length spaces such as suitable polyhedra, convex surfaces, cones with valid links, and quotients by isometric group actions. The examples are not decoration; they tell students where singular tangent cones will come from later.
Local cone models

Cone Links Tell Us Which Singularities Respect the Lower Bound

Metric Cone

A neighborhood may look like \(C(\Sigma_p)\), where \(\Sigma_p\) is the link or space of directions.

\(T_pX = C(\Sigma_pX)\)

Link Criterion

The angular space controls whether the cone has lower curvature behavior. Bad links create too much angular room or the wrong triangle comparison.

cone geometry foreshadows spaces of directionslink first, cone second
Cone links are the chapter's local microscope. A cone is lower-curvature controlled when its angular link has the right diameter and comparison behavior. This is why spaces of directions matter: they are not an extra object added after the fact, but the data that controls the first-order cone model around a point.

Global theorem

Toponogov Is the Engine That Converts Local CBB Into Global Control

Input

A geodesic triangle or hinge in a length space with curvature bounded below by \(\kappa\).

Output

Side and angle data are constrained by the triangle in the model plane \(M_\kappa^2\).

Teaching Use

Every later rigidity theorem asks: what does Toponogov force if a comparison inequality is sharp?

Toponogov theorem as proof enginehinge lab supports live inspection
Toponogov is the global engine of the chapter. Local comparison information is upgraded to a statement about geodesic triangles and hinges across the space. The theorem is the reason lower curvature becomes a machine rather than a definition: once triangle sides and angles are comparable to the model space, many global consequences can be pushed through.

Rigidity I

Positive Lower Curvature Bounds Put a Ceiling on Diameter

Comparison Message

Large triangles cannot spread farther apart than their model-space analogues when the lower bound is positive.

Rigidity Message

When the diameter reaches the model threshold, the space is forced toward a spherical suspension or another model-like shape.

artifact: diameter-splitting-models.pnginequality first, equality second
Diameter control is the first rigidity consequence to emphasize. A positive lower curvature bound makes very large spaces impossible under the right hypotheses, and equality cases force model-like behavior. The important teaching move is to separate the inequality from the rigidity statement: the number bounds size, while equality describes geometry.
Rigidity II

A Line in Nonnegative Curvature Forces a Product Structure

Geodesic Witness

A line is a complete geodesic that remains minimizing on every subinterval. It is a global certificate, not a local accident.

Splitting Conclusion

Under nonnegative lower curvature, the line organizes the space as an \(\mathbb{R}\)-factor times a transverse metric space.

line plus comparison yields product behaviorglobal witness, global theorem
Splitting is the opposite kind of rigidity: a line in a nonnegatively curved lower-bound setting forces a product decomposition. Present the line as a global geodesic witness that forbids hidden bending. Once the line exists, Busemann-type functions and comparison inequalities organize the space into a factor along the line and a transverse factor.
Regularity

Dimension Is Detected by Comparison Configurations, Not Smooth Charts

Regular Points

Many independent directions behave like Euclidean coordinate axes at the chosen scale.

Singular Points

The direction space loses Euclidean shape, but still has metric structure.

Strainer Test

Almost opposite pairs provide a practical dimension detector.

Lecture checkpoint. The chapter replaces smooth coordinates with comparison-based coordinates that survive singularities and limits.

dimension is stable enough to teach through strainerssmooth intuition needs replacement language
Dimension in Alexandrov spaces is subtle because points can be singular, but it is still robust. Strainers provide coordinate-like data, and spaces of directions keep local dimension under control. Tell students that dimension here is not merely topological bookkeeping; it is detected by comparison configurations that behave like almost orthogonal directions.
Quantitative comparison

Bishop Comparison Measures How Curvature Restricts Volume Growth

Volume Ratio

\(\frac{\operatorname{vol} B(p,r)}{\operatorname{vol}_{\kappa,n} B(r)}\)

Monotonicity

Lower curvature bounds prevent excessive spreading, so ball volumes are compared against the model ball.

artifact: volume-gh-limit-collapse.pngvolume is comparison made quantitative
Bishop-type volume comparison translates curvature lower bounds into monotonicity of volume ratios. The slide should be taught as a quantitative companion to the qualitative rigidity theorems. Curvature prevents too much spreading, and equality or near equality signals that the metric balls are behaving like their model counterparts.
Limits

Gromov-Hausdorff Limits Preserve Comparison While Allowing Collapse

Stable

Curvature lower bounds survive appropriate Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.

Unstable

Dimension, smoothness, and local topology may change in the limit.

Teaching Moral

Comparison geometry is robust enough for limits, but the limit may be singular.

collapse is allowed; comparison remains meaningfulsets up tangent-cone language
Gromov-Hausdorff limits show why the category matters. Lower curvature bounds are stable under appropriate limits, but dimension may collapse and singular sets may appear. The figure lets students see the main tension: comparison survives, while ordinary smooth intuition can fail dramatically under limiting processes.
Local coordinates without charts

Strainers Act Like Approximate Coordinate Axes in a Singular Space

Strainer Pair

Points \(a_i,b_i\) are chosen so the comparison angles at \(p\) behave like opposite coordinate directions.

Regularity Signal

Enough good pairs mean \(p\) has an almost Euclidean neighborhood at that scale.

artifact: directions-tangent-cone-strainers.pngcomparison angles become coordinates
Strainers are the local coordinate technology of the chapter. A collection of pairs acts like approximate coordinate axes when comparison angles are close to the Euclidean pattern. Students should understand them as a measurable way to say that a point has enough independent directions to look regular at a chosen scale.
Angular tangent data

The Space of Directions Records All First-Order Geodesic Germs

Definition Idea

Start with geodesic germs from \(p\). Measure the angle between two germs, then complete the resulting angular metric space.

Singularity Detector

A smooth point has a sphere of directions. A singular point has a different link, and that link changes the tangent cone.

direction spaces are metric objects in their own rightlocal geometry is angular geometry
Spaces of directions collect germs of geodesics issuing from a point, with angular distance between them. They are themselves metric spaces with curvature behavior inherited from the original space. The pedagogical point is that singularity is visible in the direction space before it is visible in the whole neighborhood.
Blow-up limit

Tangent Cones Are the Local Limits Seen Under Infinite Magnification

Blow Up

Rescale \(d\) by factors tending to infinity around \(p\). The limit is a cone over \(\Sigma_pX\).

Smooth vs Singular

Euclidean tangent spaces are special tangent cones. Singular points keep the cone shape but change the link.

\(T_pX=C(\Sigma_pX)\)the chapter ends with a microscope
The tangent cone is the blow-up limit: rescale distances near a point and watch the local geometry become conical. At a smooth point the cone is Euclidean, while at a singular point the cone records the link and any missing directions. Connect this slide back to cones, strainers, and spaces of directions so the last definitions feel inevitable.
