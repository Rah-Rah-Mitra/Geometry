Orientation Is a Sign Choice for Top Forms

Section 4.1

Definition

An orientation on an n-dimensional tangent space chooses one of the two components of ΛnV - {0}.

b1 ∧ ... ∧ bn > 0

A frame is positive when its dual top form lies in the chosen component. The determinant is the transition test.

Define orientation as a sign convention that lives in the top exterior power, not as an arrow drawn on the page. The three frames make the determinant test visible: swapping basis vectors reverses orientation, while a skew frame can remain positive if its determinant stays positive.

Three Equivalent Ways to Recognize Orientability

Proposition 4.2 and examples

Local Test

Use charts whose transition Jacobians all have positive determinant on overlaps.

Global Test

Find a smooth nowhere-vanishing n-form. It selects the positive component everywhere.

Examples

Lie groups and spheres are orientable; real projective space depends on parity.

Teaching Cue

The theorem lets students switch between algebra, charts, and geometry without changing the object.

Present the equivalence as a translation table. An orientable atlas is useful for calculations, a nowhere-zero top form is useful for integration, and the component definition is the invariant object. The examples show why orientation is not automatic on every manifold.

Pullback Is the Integration Machine

Change of variables for forms

∫φ(A) ω = ± ∫A φ*ω

The sign is positive when the chart map preserves orientation and negative when it reverses orientation.

Jacobian determinant orientation sign top form ordinary integral
Move slowly through the formula because it is the operational heart of the chapter. The integral is defined by bringing a form back to a domain where ordinary calculus applies. The Jacobian is not an extra decoration; it is already contained in the pulled-back top form.

Integrating Forms Over Singular Chains

Section 4.6

Definition pattern

∫Σ aiσi ω = Σ ai ∫Δp σi*ω

What the Definition Buys

  • Parametrizations become the source of integration.
  • Orientation signs are carried by the simplex maps.
  • The boundary operator is ready for Stokes theorem.
Explain that singular chains are deliberately flexible: a chain is not a submanifold with perfect edges, but a finite signed collection of parametrized simplices. This is why pullback is the natural definition. Each simplex contributes an ordinary integral on the standard domain.

Stokes I: Exterior Derivative Is Dual to Boundary

Section 4.7

c dω interior derivative ∫∂c ω p = 1 fundamental theorem
Stokes theorem for chains

∫c dω = ∫∂c ω

The first version is stated for differentiable singular chains. It already contains the fundamental theorem of calculus when the chain is an interval.

Proof roadmap

Reduce to a simplex, use the Euclidean theorem on the standard simplex, and track the alternating signs of its faces.

State the theorem as a pairing identity between two operations: exterior derivative on forms and boundary on chains. The proof is not just a calculation; it is a compatibility statement. The interval case is the anchor students already know from one-variable calculus.

Regular Domains Need Boundary Orientation

Section 4.8

Boundary convention

If v points outward, then v1,...,vn-1 is positive on ∂D when v,v1,...,vn-1 is positive in M.

Why This Matters

The induced orientation is what makes the boundary integral in Stokes carry the correct sign. It also explains the familiar counterclockwise direction around an oriented disk.

Pause on the convention because many sign errors in integration on manifolds begin here. The outward vector is placed first, not last. Once that convention is fixed, the orientation on the boundary is not a matter of taste; it is forced by the ambient orientation.

Riemannian Metrics Supply Volume Forms

Section 4.10

Volume form

ωg = θ1 ∧ ... ∧ θn

For an oriented orthonormal coframe, this top form defines volume. Then ∫M f ωg is the integral of a function.

Hodge viewpoint

The metric also identifies forms with complementary degree forms through the star operator, a bridge toward divergence identities.

Explain that the metric adds measurement, while orientation supplies the sign needed for a top form. In an oriented orthonormal coframe the volume form looks simple, but its meaning is global: it packages all local metric scale factors into a coordinate-free integrand.

Divergence Theorem Is Stokes in Metric Clothing

Section 4.10

Flux identity

∫D div(V) dV = ∫∂D <V,n> dA

Translation Dictionary

  • Vector field becomes a form by metric duality.
  • Exterior derivative becomes divergence times volume.
  • Boundary orientation becomes outward unit normal.
Use this slide to show how classical vector calculus is recovered without changing the theorem. The metric turns a vector field into a form and supplies normals and area measures. After that translation, the divergence theorem is just Stokes with familiar names attached.

de Rham Cohomology Keeps Closed Forms Modulo Exact Forms

Section 4.13

Definition

HpdR(M) = ker(d: Ωp → Ωp+1) / im(d: Ωp-1 → Ωp)

Closed means dα = 0. Exact means α = dβ. Cohomology measures closed forms not explained by primitives.

Introduce de Rham cohomology as bookkeeping for what Stokes cannot erase. Exact forms are invisible on cycles because Stokes turns them into integrals over empty boundaries. Closed forms that still have nonzero periods therefore carry information about the global shape of the manifold.

A Period Can Detect a Missing Primitive

Example around S1 and punctured plane

Diagnostic period

∫S1 ω = 2π

A nonzero integral over a cycle rules out global exactness, even when the form is closed away from the puncture.

Local Versus Global

Small contractible patches admit angle primitives; a loop around the puncture cannot choose one consistently.

Use the angular form as the chapter's most concrete cohomology example. Locally, students can imagine an angle function whose differential gives the form. Globally, the angle fails to be single valued around the puncture, and the nonzero period proves that failure.

Closed Forms Pair With Cycles

Sections 4.16-4.17

de Rham pairing

[α], [z] &mapsto ∫z α

The value is unchanged if a closed form is replaced by a cohomologous form or a cycle is replaced by a homologous cycle.

Why it is well-defined

Stokes kills exact changes on cycles and kills closed forms on boundaries.

This slide prepares the de Rham theorem without proving it. The pairing is the bridge from differential forms to real singular homology. Stokes is again the reason it descends to equivalence classes, because exact and boundary ambiguities integrate away under the right hypotheses.

The Poincare Lemma Says the Obstruction Is Global

Section 4.18

Local exactness

On a ball, every closed k-form with k ≥ 1 is exact.

dω = 0   implies locally   ω = dη

The lemma explains why the angular example is topological: the problem is not local differentiation, but the global hole that prevents a single primitive.

Make the local-global distinction explicit. The Poincare lemma says closed forms on a ball have primitives, so local computations cannot by themselves detect cohomology. Nontrivial de Rham classes appear when the manifold prevents those local primitives from being patched into one global object.

Instructor Checklist: How to Read an Integral on a Manifold

Recap

1. Sign

Which orientation is fixed, and does the chosen parametrization preserve it?

2. Pullback

What form is being integrated on the parameter domain, including the Jacobian contribution?

3. Boundary

Does the problem call for Stokes, and what is the induced boundary orientation?

4. Metric

If the integrand is a function or flux, where does the volume or area measure come from?

5. Period

Is a nonzero cycle integral detecting a global obstruction to a primitive?

6. Class

Which equivalence class survives after exact forms and boundaries are ignored?
