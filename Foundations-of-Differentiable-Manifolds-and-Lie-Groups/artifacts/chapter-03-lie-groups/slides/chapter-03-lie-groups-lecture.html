Definition 3.1

What Makes A Group Into A Lie Group?

Smooth multiplication
smooth inverse
manifold topology
Definition

A Lie group is a differentiable manifold \(G\) with a group law for which the combined map \((a,b)\mapsto ab^{-1}\) is smooth.

Immediate Consequence

Inversion and multiplication are each smooth. Left and right translations are diffeomorphisms, so every point of the group looks like every other point after translating by a group element.

\(\mathbb{R}^n\) \(\mathbb{C}^{*}\) \(GL(n,\mathbb{R})\) \(O(n)\), \(U(n)\) products \(G\times H\)

at the identity
Proposition 3.7

Left invariant vector fields form a finite-dimensional Lie algebra under the vector-field bracket. Evaluation at the identity identifies this algebra with \(T_eG\).

Working Model

\(X(a)=d\ell_a(X(e))\), where \(\ell_a(b)=ab\).

  • Global vector field from one identity tangent vector.
  • Bracket remembers noncommuting infinitesimal flows.
  • Classical matrix groups get concrete tangent matrix algebras.

The important move is that a Lie group has enough translation symmetry to make tangent data global. A left invariant vector field is completely determined by its value at the identity, and the bracket of such fields stays left invariant, giving the tangent space its algebra structure.

The Exponential Map Turns A Tangent Vector Into A Subgroup

local algebra
global curve
Definition

For \(X\in\mathfrak{g}\), \(\exp(tX)\) is the unique one-parameter subgroup whose tangent vector at \(t=0\) is \(X(e)\).

Theorem 3.31

\( \exp((s+t)X)=\exp(sX)\exp(tX) \)

SO(2) Covering

The map \(t\mapsto R(t)\) covers the circle: the algebra is a line, while the group records periodicity.

Theorem 3.14

Homomorphisms Differentiate To Lie Algebra Homomorphisms

smooth map
bracket preserving
Statement

If \(\phi:G\to H\) is a Lie group homomorphism, then \(d\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}\) preserves brackets and relates left invariant vector fields.

Why It Works

The identity is respected, and the homomorphism commutes with left translations. That compatibility transports the derivative at \(e\) across the entire group.

  • Group map first, algebra map second.
  • Brackets are preserved because related vector fields have related brackets.
  • Connectedness will later make the derivative determine the homomorphism.
Homomorphisms are rigid once the algebra map is fixed on a connected sourceSlide 6 / 17
Present this as the first direction of the global-local dictionary. A smooth group homomorphism necessarily respects the infinitesimal algebra. The proof is not a computation in coordinates; it uses the fact that the homomorphism intertwines left translations and therefore carries left invariant fields to left invariant fields.
Theorems 3.16 and 3.27

Simply Connected Sources Let Algebra Maps Integrate Back

existence
uniqueness
global hypothesis
Uniqueness

If \(G\) is connected, two homomorphisms \(G\to H\) with the same derivative are equal.

Existence

If \(G\) is simply connected, every Lie algebra homomorphism \(\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}\) integrates to a unique Lie group homomorphism \(G\to H\).

Teaching Caution

Without simple connectivity, the algebra may describe a local homomorphism whose analytic continuation fails to descend globally.

The source topology is not decorative; it controls integrationSlide 7 / 17
This is a good place to slow down and name the hypotheses. Connectedness gives uniqueness because a homomorphism is generated from any neighborhood of the identity. Simple connectivity gives existence by letting the integral graph of a differential ideal project without monodromy obstruction.
Theorem 3.19

Subalgebras Integrate To Connected Lie Subgroups

Frobenius
integral manifold
subgroup closure
Correspondence

For a Lie group \(G\), each subalgebra \(\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}\) determines a unique connected Lie subgroup \(H\) whose algebra is \(\mathfrak{h}\).

Proof Roadmap
  1. Translate \(\mathfrak{h}\) to a left invariant distribution.
  2. Use bracket closure to get involutivity.
  3. Apply Frobenius through the identity.
  4. Use translation invariance to prove the leaf is a subgroup.
Connected subgroups correspond to subalgebrasSlide 8 / 17
Tie this theorem back to Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 tools. The subalgebra is converted into a distribution by left translation, and Frobenius supplies an integral manifold. The group structure of the leaf is not automatic from Frobenius alone; it follows from left invariance and maximality.
Coverings And Global Topology

The Same Algebra Can Sit Above Different Global Groups

covering homomorphisms
simply connected cover
Theorem 3.25

Every connected Lie group has a simply connected covering space that is itself a Lie group, and the covering map is a Lie group homomorphism.

Proposition 3.26

A homomorphism of connected Lie groups is a covering map exactly when its derivative at the identity is an isomorphism.

The exponential \( \mathbb{R}\to SO(2) \) is the running example: locally an isomorphism of tangent lines, globally periodic.

Use the line over the circle as the mental model for this part of the chapter. The derivative can be an isomorphism while the global map still wraps several times. Covering groups repair that mismatch by replacing the source with a simply connected Lie group upstairs.

Theorem 3.42

Closed Abstract Subgroups Are Automatically Lie Subgroups

closedness
relative topology
local exponential chart
Closed Subgroup Theorem

If \(A\) is a closed abstract subgroup of a Lie group \(G\), then \(A\) has a unique manifold structure making it a Lie subgroup of \(G\).

Algebra From Exponentials

\(\mathfrak{a}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}: \exp(tX)\in A\ \text{for all }t\}\)

Related Rigidity

Continuous homomorphisms between Lie groups are automatically smooth, so the differentiable structure is more rigid than it first appears.

Closedness upgrades an abstract subgroup to a smooth oneSlide 13 / 17
Stress that this is one of the chapter's strongest structural results. A subgroup is initially only an algebraic subset with the relative topology. Closedness supplies enough compactness and limit control for exponential coordinates to identify a tangent subspace and build the smooth subgroup structure.
Theorems 3.58 and 3.62

Homogeneous Manifolds Are Smooth Quotients \(G/H\)

closed subgroup
cosets
transitive action
Definition

If \(H\) is a closed subgroup of \(G\), the coset space \(G/H\) has a unique manifold structure making the projection \(G\to G/H\) smooth and locally modeled by slices.

Orbit-Stabilizer Geometry

A transitive action of \(G\) on \(M\) identifies \(M\) with \(G/H\), where \(H\) is the isotropy group of a chosen base point.

Model Example

\(S^{n-1}\cong O(n)/O(n-1)\)

Homogeneous means the group action moves any point to any otherSlide 14 / 17
This slide ties together closed subgroups and group actions. The quotient is smooth because closed subgroups have the right embedded structure. Conversely, a transitive action turns the manifold into a quotient by the stabilizer, so homogeneous spaces are the geometric form of orbit-stabilizer reasoning.

Common Pitfalls

The Hypotheses Are Not Cosmetic

what each condition buys
Connected

A neighborhood of the identity generates the group. This is why a derivative can determine a homomorphism on all of \(G\).

Simply Connected

Algebra homomorphisms integrate without monodromy. Without it, the same local data can wrap around a quotient obstruction.

Closed Subgroup

The quotient \(G/H\) becomes a manifold with the intended topology, and the subgroup inherits a Lie structure.

Precise hypotheses keep local and global statements honestSlide 16 / 17
Use this warning slide to prevent overgeneralization. Students often remember the slogan that Lie groups are determined by Lie algebras, but the chapter is careful about connectedness, simple connectivity, closedness, and normality. Each hypothesis pays for a specific global conclusion.
