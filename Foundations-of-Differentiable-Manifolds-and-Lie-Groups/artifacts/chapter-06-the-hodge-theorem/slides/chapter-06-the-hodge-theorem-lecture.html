The Hodge Theorem1 / 17
Open by framing the theorem as a bridge, not as a single isolated statement. Students have seen de Rham cohomology as closed forms modulo exact forms; this chapter adds a Riemannian metric and asks for one preferred representative in each class. That preference is analytic, but the payoff is topological.

Metric Input

The Hodge Star Converts Degree And Measures Forms

Local Sign Model
In an oriented Euclidean 2-plane, the useful memory aid is *1 = dx ∧ dy, *dx = dy, *dy = -dx, and *(dx ∧ dy) = 1. Degree controls the sign of applying star twice.
Inner Product On Forms
The metric pairing becomes global by integrating α ∧ *β over the compact oriented manifold.
Hodge star and inner product3 / 17
Make the star feel concrete before introducing the formal adjoint. In two dimensions it rotates basis forms and records orientation, while in general it is the metric-dependent device that lets a wedge product become a scalar integral. The inner product on forms is the hinge that later defines delta as the adjoint of d.
Adjoint Structure

The Codifferential Is The Backward Arrow

Laplace-Beltrami Operator
For p-forms the positive operator is Delta = d delta + delta d. It is self-adjoint and nonnegative, so its kernel is detected by an energy identity rather than by guessing coordinates.

Forward

The exterior derivative tests closedness and raises degree.

Backward

The codifferential tests coclosedness and lowers degree.

Balanced

The Laplacian sees failure in both directions at once.

d, delta, and Delta4 / 17
Emphasize that delta is not a new topological operation; it is forced by the Riemannian inner product. The exterior derivative moves forward in degree, and the codifferential is its adjoint motion backward. The Laplacian combines these two failures, so a harmonic form is simultaneously closed and coclosed on a compact oriented Riemannian manifold.
Kernel

Harmonic Means No Differential Energy Remains

Energy Test
On a compact oriented Riemannian manifold, (Delta ω, ω) = ||dω||^2 + ||deltaω||^2. Thus Delta ω = 0 exactly when dω = 0 and deltaω = 0.
Low-Degree Check
For functions, harmonicity collapses to the familiar compactness result: on a compact connected manifold, harmonic functions are constant.
Harmonic forms5 / 17
This is the slide where the kernel becomes meaningful. Instead of saying harmonic forms are mysterious solutions of a partial differential equation, identify them as forms with zero d-energy and zero delta-energy. The compactness hypothesis is doing real work, and the function case gives students a familiar boundary for the idea.
Why Analysis Enters

First Solve Weakly, Then Prove Smoothness

Weak Equation
A candidate solution can be encoded by testing against smooth forms: l(Delta^* phi) = (alpha, phi). This is easier to construct than a smooth form at the start.
Regularity Theorem
Elliptic regularity upgrades the weak functional to a smooth p-form omega satisfying Delta omega = alpha.
Regularity theorem6 / 17
Students may wonder why the chapter spends so much time on Fourier series and Sobolev estimates. This slide gives the reason: the proof can manufacture a weak solution through functional analysis, but Hodge theory needs an honest smooth differential form. Regularity is the analytic bridge from test equations back to geometry.
Hodge Decomposition

Every Form Splits Into Harmonic And Laplacian Parts

Theorem Shape
For compact oriented Riemannian M, the space of harmonic p-forms is finite dimensional and E^p(M) = H^p ⊕ Delta(E^p(M)) orthogonally.

Kernel

Harmonic forms carry the finite-dimensional obstruction.

Image

The Laplacian accounts for everything orthogonal to the kernel.

Projection

The operator H records the harmonic component of a form.

Orthogonal direct sum7 / 17
Present the decomposition as linear algebra with an analytic proof. The picture is a finite-dimensional shadow, but it captures the logical target: separate the kernel from its orthogonal complement and show that the complement is exactly hit by the Laplacian. The hard estimates justify this clean projection picture in infinite dimensions.
Solving Away The Obstruction

The Green Operator Inverts Delta Off The Kernel

Definition By Orthogonality
Given alpha, define G alpha as the unique solution in the orthogonal complement of harmonic forms to Delta w = alpha - H alpha.
Commutation Payoff
Because the Green operator commutes with operators that commute with the Laplacian, it helps move from decomposition of forms to statements about closed forms and de Rham classes.
Green operator8 / 17
The Green operator should be taught as the inverse that is allowed only after the harmonic obstruction has been removed. This language helps avoid the mistaken phrase that the Laplacian is simply invertible. It is invertible on the orthogonal complement, and that restricted inverse is exactly what makes the projection onto harmonic forms operational.
Topological Payoff

Each de Rham Class Has One Harmonic Representative

Hodge Theorem Consequence
The map from harmonic p-forms to de Rham cohomology is an isomorphism. This gives finite-dimensional cohomology and leads directly to Poincare duality for compact oriented manifolds.

Existence

Subtract an exact correction to reach a harmonic representative.

Uniqueness

A harmonic exact form is forced to have zero energy.

Duality

The star sends a nonzero harmonic class to a complementary pairing partner.

de Rham representatives9 / 17
This is the moment to reconnect the analysis to the previous chapter on de Rham cohomology. The theorem does not merely say harmonic forms are interesting; it says they are a canonical model for cohomology. Existence comes from decomposition and the Green operator, while uniqueness comes from orthogonality and the energy identity.
Periodic Model

Fourier Modes Turn Derivatives Into Multipliers

Circle Memory Aid
For the positive scalar Laplacian on the circle, -d^2/dx^2 cos(kx) = k^2 cos(kx). Higher frequency costs more energy, so estimates can measure smoothness by penalizing large k.
Why Periodic?
Local elliptic problems are reduced to periodic ones because Fourier coefficients make differential inequalities visible and computable.
Fourier series and periodic reduction10 / 17
The proof becomes easier to follow once students see why periodic functions appear. Fourier series convert differentiation into multiplication by frequency, so analytic estimates become weighted inequalities on coefficients. The circle picture is deliberately simple; it is the same inspection habit the proof later applies to vector-valued periodic functions.
Sobolev Accounting

Smoothness Is Rapid Decay Of High Frequencies

Weighted Norm Idea
The Sobolev norm weights the Fourier coefficient at frequency xi by a power of 1 + |xi|^2. A function is smoother when these weighted sums remain finite for higher exponents.

Sobolev Lemma

Enough weighted control implies uniform convergence and visible continuity.

Difference Quotients

Bounded translated differences detect an extra derivative.

Rellich Compactness

A stronger norm gives compactness when viewed in a weaker norm.

Sobolev estimates11 / 17
Avoid presenting Sobolev spaces as a notation storm. Their job here is bookkeeping for high frequencies. The Sobolev lemma converts enough coefficient decay into pointwise control, difference quotients detect derivative gain, and Rellich compactness supplies subsequences. These are the analytic tools that later make the harmonic space finite dimensional.
Fundamental Inequality

Elliptic Operators Control More Derivatives Than They Show

Estimate Schematic
For an elliptic operator L of order l, the proof obtains an inequality of the form ||u||_{s+l} ≤ C(||Lu||_s + ||u||_s), first in periodic coordinates and then locally.
Pedagogical Warning
The lower norm term is not a nuisance to erase casually; it reflects compactness and kernel behavior, exactly the phenomena that harmonic forms encode.
Fundamental elliptic estimate12 / 17
Use the estimate as the analytic backbone of the proof. Ellipticity says the highest-order part of L sees every cotangent direction, so knowing Lu controls more regularity of u. The lower norm term should be interpreted honestly: it prevents the estimate from pretending that kernel directions have disappeared.
Principal Symbol

Ellipticity Is A Cotangent-Space Test

Definition In Practice
Keep only the top-order part of L and replace each derivative by a covector component. The resulting matrix sigma_L(x, xi) must be invertible for every nonzero xi.

Local

The test is performed in coordinates and local trivializations.

Top-Order

Lower-order terms cannot repair a failed principal symbol.

Uniform

Compactness of the unit sphere turns pointwise invertibility into estimates.

Elliptic symbols13 / 17
This slide should demystify the word elliptic. The condition is not that solutions look like ellipses; it is an algebraic test on the highest-order part in every nonzero cotangent direction. For the Laplacian the model symbol is positive like the squared length of xi, which is why all directions are controlled.
Reduction Strategy

Local Problems Borrow Periodic Estimates

Local-To-Periodic
Near one point, replace the operator by a periodic elliptic operator that agrees on a smaller neighborhood.
Periodic-To-Global
Use cutoffs and a finite cover to assemble local regularity and compactness back on the manifold.
Reduction to periodic case14 / 17
The reduction is technical, but its shape is teachable. First make a local coordinate problem agree with a periodic one where Fourier analysis is available. Then use cutoffs and a finite cover to bring the estimate back to the manifold. Students should see this as a proof transport method, not as an arbitrary change of setting.
Completing The Proof

The Laplace-Beltrami Operator Is Elliptic

Finite-Dimensional Algebra
At a point and nonzero covector, wedge by the covector and its adjoint form an exact algebraic complex. The symbol of the Laplacian is the positive operator built from those two maps, hence invertible.
Proof Closure
Once ellipticity is established, the regularity and compactness theorems assumed earlier apply to the Laplace-Beltrami operator itself.
Ellipticity of Delta15 / 17
The final ellipticity argument is compact but important. At each cotangent vector, the exterior derivative symbol is wedge multiplication, and the adjoint symbol points back. Exactness of the algebraic complex makes the combined positive operator invertible. That finishes the analytic prerequisites promised near the beginning of the chapter.
In-Class Exercise

Audit A Toy Hodge Decomposition

Prompt
Let L = diag(0, 1, 4, 9) and v = (2, -1, 3, 1/2). Identify the harmonic projection, the nonharmonic component, and the obstruction to solving Lw = v.
Instructor Target
Students should say that the zero-eigenvalue coordinate is the harmonic part. A solution exists only after subtracting that component, mirroring alpha - H alpha in the Green operator definition.
Finite spectral model16 / 17
This exercise gives students a small linear algebra version of the theorem. They should not try to invert the zero eigenvalue. Instead, they should project away the harmonic component, solve on the positive eigenspaces, and recognize the same obstruction-removal logic used in the Green operator. This keeps the theorem computationally inspectable.

End of lecture17 / 17
Close by reminding students that the theorem changes the way cohomology can be studied. A de Rham class is no longer just an equivalence class; after choosing a metric it has a distinguished harmonic representative. The representative depends on the metric, but the dimension and duality information recovered from it are topological.
