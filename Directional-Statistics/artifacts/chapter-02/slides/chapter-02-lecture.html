Mean Direction
average in the plane, then read polar coordinates

Resultant Vector As Center Of Mass

Definition

\(z_j=e^{i\theta_j}\),   \(\bar z=C+iS={1\over n}\sum_{j=1}^n z_j\)

Mean direction: \(\bar\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(S,C)\) when \(|\bar z|>0\).

Mean resultant length: \(\bar R=|\bar z|\), with \(0\leq \bar R\leq 1\).

Present the resultant as an average in the plane, not as an average of degrees. The coordinates C and S are mean cosine and mean sine. When the resultant is nonzero, atan2 returns the mean direction and the length Rbar measures how strongly the sample agrees.
Invariant Check
summaries must rotate with the data

Rotation Equivariance Is The Test

A valid circular summary is not tied to north, east, or a plotting convention.

Two Algebraic Diagnostics

Rotation: \(\bar\theta(\theta+\delta)=\bar\theta(\theta)+\delta\), while \(\bar R\) is unchanged.

Balance: \(\sum_j \sin(\theta_j-\bar\theta)=0\).

The notebook check gives rotation error \(0\) and sine residual about \(1.1\times 10^{-15}\).

The invariance check is the proof habit students should keep. Rotating every observation by a fixed delta rotates the mean direction by that delta and preserves Rbar. The sine residual around the mean vanishes, which is the circular analogue of a first-order balance condition.
Concentration

Resultant Length Measures Concentration

Variance From The Same Vector

\(V=1-\bar R\)

Long resultant: concentrated sample and small circular variance.

Short resultant: directions cancel and location may be unstable.

Explain concentration with cancellation. Samples pointing together have a long average vector; samples spread around the circle cancel toward the origin. The statistic V equals one minus Rbar, so low variance means the resultant nearly reaches the boundary of the unit disk.
Dispersion
candidate directions pay a chord-loss penalty

Dispersion Is A Loss Landscape

Identity Behind The Curve

\(D(\alpha)={1\over n}\sum_j\{1-\cos(\theta_j-\alpha)\}\)

\(D(\bar\theta)=V=1-\bar R\)

The notebook verifies the identity over a grid with maximum error below \(10^{-15}\).

Use the graph as a loss landscape rather than a black-box formula. Chord dispersion is minimized at the mean direction, and the notebook verifies the identity D(alpha) equals V plus a penalty depending only on the angular gap from the mean. That identity is why the mean is geometrically natural.
Robust Location
arc distance changes the optimizer

Circular Median Uses Arc Loss

The median minimizes shortest-arc distance rather than squared or chord-like loss.

L1 Geometry On The Circle

Median direction \(\alpha_m\) minimizes

\({1\over n}\sum_j d_{\mathbb S^1}(\theta_j,\alpha)\)

The robust sample has a cluster around zero degrees and one point near the opposite side. The vector mean and circular median are close, but they answer different optimization questions.

Separate robustness from concentration. The circular median minimizes average shortest-arc distance, so one observation on the opposite side can pull the vector mean while leaving the median near the dense cluster. The median is a location summary under an L1 geometry on the circle.
Moments
fold first, then average

Trigonometric Moments Fold The Circle

Why Fold?

The first moment is location and concentration.

The second moment makes two-fold or axial structure visible because opposing directions can become aligned after doubling.

length \(R_p=|m'_p|\), phase \(\arg(m'_p)/p\)

Introduce trigonometric moments as repeated wrapping before averaging. The first moment is the resultant; the second moment doubles angles so opposing directions can agree. Moment length records strength, while the phase records the axis or direction after undoing the fold.

Grouped Observations
midpoints hide within-bin angular spread

Grouped Data Shortens Resultants

Equal-width bins turn exact directions into midpoint representatives. That compression changes the average vector length.

Bias Pattern In The Notebook Simulation

Grouping is a measurement operation, not merely a storage format. Replacing every observation in a bin by the midpoint compresses within-bin angular variation, so resultant lengths tend to be biased downward as the bins become coarse. Equal-width grouping lets the bias be approximately undone.
Grouping Correction
small bins, small multiplicative adjustment

The Correction Factor Is Local

Equal-Width Midpoint Correction

\(a(h)={h/2\over \sin(h/2)}\)

\(a(h)=1+{h^2\over 24}+{7h^4\over 5760}+O(h^6)\)

For the first resultant, multiply the grouped midpoint \(\bar R\) by \(a(h)\) when equal-width grouping assumptions are credible.

The correction factor should be read as a small-angle expansion. For small h it is nearly one, which explains why fine grouping barely changes the answer. At forty-five degrees the factor is about 1.026, large enough to matter but still not a cure for every circular data problem.
Diagnostic Split
do not confuse coordinate failure with grouped-data bias

Two Mistakes, Two Remedies

Bad Cut Point

Symptom: a linear mean changes when the zero direction moves.

Remedy: use \(e^{i\theta}\) and the resultant.

Grouped Midpoints

Symptom: coarse bins shorten the resultant length.

Remedy: apply \(a(h)\) only under equal-width grouping.

Draw a firm distinction between the two failure modes. Grouping correction repairs a known loss of concentration caused by midpoint bins. It does not legitimize a linear mean that depends on an arbitrary cut point; the coordinate-free representation is still the first line of defense.
