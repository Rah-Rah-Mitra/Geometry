Tests Of Uniformity And Goodness-Of-Fit

A circular null model is not just flat density. It is the claim that no direction, cut point, gap pattern, semicircle, or fitted-model residual carries structure.

Rayleigh Kuiper Watson Spacing Ajne PIT
Open the lecture by naming uniformity as the circular null model that everything else has to respect. The important geometric constraint is that the zero angle is a coordinate choice, not a property of the data. Preview the chapter as a set of diagnostic lenses: mean vectors, circular empirical distribution functions, spacings, semicircles, pairwise kernels, and probability integral transforms.
Null Geometry
Rotation invariance before calculation

Uniformity Means No Direction Survives Rotation

A circular uniform null makes every chosen zero angle disposable and makes every nonzero harmonic vanish in expectation.

Null contract Uniformity means \(P\{\theta \in A\}=|A|/(2\pi)\) for every arc \(A\), regardless of where the circle is cut.
All nonzero Fourier resultants satisfy \(E(e^{im\theta})=0\).
A valid circular statistic must be rotation invariant or state its chosen harmonic.
Graphical checks should reveal whether the evidence is a direction, a gap, a semicircle, or a model residual.
Use the unit-circle schematic to separate the statistical claim from the coordinate system. Under uniformity, rotating the origin should not change the evidence, and every nonzero harmonic has zero population resultant. This is why circular tests must either be explicitly invariant or make clear which geometric feature they are designed to detect.
Chapter Route
From graphical checks to model diagnostics

A Test Family Is A Diagnostic Map

The tests are best organized by the geometry of nonuniformity they can see.

Graphical checksUnit-circle point clouds, resultant arrows, CDF steps, spacing bars, and PIT plots reveal the likely alternative before a statistic is chosen.
RayleighFirst-harmonic evidence for a single preferred direction, summarized by \(2n|\bar z|^2\).
Kuiper / WatsonOrigin-invariant empirical CDF discrepancies for general circular departures from a flat distribution.
Spacing / AjneGap lengths and moving semicircle counts for clusters, empty arcs, and hemispheric imbalance.
Hermans-Rasson / BeranOmnibus energy-style summaries that compare the whole pairwise or integrated discrepancy fingerprint with uniformity.
PIT diagnosticsGoodness-of-fit workflow: transform by a model CDF, then ask whether the transformed angles are uniform.
Frame the chapter as a map rather than a ranking of tests. Rayleigh is local to the first harmonic, Kuiper and Watson are empirical-distribution scans, spacing and Ajne tests look at gaps and semicircles, Hermans-Rasson and Beran use pairwise or integrated discrepancies, and PIT diagnostics convert model checking back into uniformity checking.
Rayleigh
Notebook artifact: first harmonic panels

Rayleigh Reads The First Harmonic

Statistic $$\bar z = {1 \over n}\sum_{j=1}^n e^{i\theta_j}, \qquad Z_R = 2n|\bar z|^2.$$
A long resultant is evidence for one dominant direction.
The notebook unimodal sample has \(Z_R \approx 37.66\) and \(p \approx 6.63\times 10^{-9}\).
The antipodal sample has \(|\bar z|\approx 0\) even though its second harmonic is large.
Present Rayleigh as the cleanest geometry in the chapter: add the unit vectors and ask whether the resultant is too long. The notebook example makes the limitation visible. A unimodal sample gives a large first resultant, while a symmetric antipodal pattern can be strikingly nonuniform and still cancel almost perfectly in the first harmonic.

Use the harmonic panel to explain why Rayleigh is powerful but not omnibus. The first resultant is tuned to one preferred direction. When mass appears in opposite lobes, the first vectors cancel but the doubled angles align. That observation motivates second-harmonic diagnostics and warns students not to equate a large Rayleigh p-value with uniformity.
Kuiper And Watson
Origin-invariant CDF diagnostics

CDF Tests Must Survive The Cut

A circular empirical CDF is drawn only after choosing a zero angle.
\(D^+\) and \(D^-\) move with the chosen origin.
Kuiper \(V\) and Watson \(U^2\) are designed to stay stable under rotation.
Notebook invariance check \(D^+\) and \(D^-\) each vary by about \(0.248\) across origins, while the recorded ranges for \(V\) and \(U^2\) are numerical roundoff.
Move from vectors to empirical distribution functions by emphasizing the artificial cut. The step function depends on where zero is placed, so raw one-sided discrepancies move when the origin moves. Kuiper combines the positive and negative excursions, and Watson recenters the squared discrepancy, leaving the statistic stable under rotation.
Two CDF Energies
Extremes versus integrated discrepancy

Kuiper And Watson Keep Different Shape Memory

Kuiper \(V = D^+ + D^-\)
Keeps the largest upward and downward separation from the uniform diagonal.
Watson \(U^2 = \int_0^1 \{G_n(t)-t-\overline{G_n-t}\}^2\,dt\)
Measures the centered global CDF discrepancy around the circle.
Kuiper behaves like a circular Kolmogorov statistic with both tails kept.
Watson smooths the evidence into an origin-free quadratic score.
Use both when a visual CDF discrepancy is visible but the type of departure is not obvious.
The two formulas should be read as design choices. Kuiper keeps the largest upward and downward gaps, so it is sensitive to localized departures anywhere around the circle. Watson integrates squared discrepancy after removing the mean offset, so it behaves like a smooth global CDF energy. Both avoid rewarding a convenient origin.

Ajne And Semicircles
Hemispheric imbalance as a moving count

Ajne Scans Every Semicircle

A half-circle should not repeatedly capture far more or far fewer than \(n/2\) observations under uniformity.

Move a semicircle through every center and record the contained count.
Uniform samples fluctuate around \(n/2\); structured samples create persistent high or low arcs.
Ajne-style evidence is especially natural when the departure is hemispheric rather than a single mode.
Notebook check The synthetic clustered sample has maximum absolute deviation \(5.0\) from the half-sample count.
Use Ajne-style semicircle scanning as the bridge between spacing and distribution-function thinking. If the data are uniform, a moving half-circle should usually contain about half the observations. Too many or too few points in some semicircle signals hemispheric imbalance, and the scan stays circular because every possible center is considered.
Omnibus Energy Tests
Pairwise and integrated discrepancy fingerprints

Hermans-Rasson And Beran Use Pairwise Energy

Omnibus idea Compare the observed angular-separation fingerprint with the fingerprint expected from independent uniform angles.
Hermans-Rasson style tests use pairwise angular kernels to detect broad alternatives.
Beran style tests integrate discrepancy over arcs or harmonics, giving a global energy.
These tests complement Rayleigh when multimodal or axial structure is plausible.
Introduce these omnibus tests qualitatively because their value is not a new picture of the origin but a new object of comparison. They score the whole interpoint-separation fingerprint against what uniformity would produce. Pairwise kernels can see multimodal or axial structure that a first-harmonic resultant may miss, while integrated discrepancy tests average evidence around the circle.
Goodness-Of-Fit
Probability integral transform diagnostics

PIT Turns Model Fit Into Uniformity

Circular PIT $$\phi_j = 2\pi F_{\widehat\eta}(\theta_j) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \phi_j \sim \operatorname{Unif}(0,2\pi) \text{ if the model is adequate.}$$
The fitted von Mises transform reduces the first resultant from \(0.641\) to \(0.064\).
Kuiper drops from \(0.517\) under the uniform-model scale to \(0.127\) under the fitted model PIT.
When parameters are estimated, calibration must account for that estimation step.
For goodness-of-fit, shift the null from a uniform circle to a specified continuous circular model. The probability integral transform maps each observation through the fitted or hypothesized circular CDF, then wraps the result back to an angle. If the model is adequate, those transformed angles should pass the same uniformity diagnostics.

Decision Workflow
Match statistic geometry to alternative geometry

Choose The Test From The Alternative

1
Single preferred directionStart with Rayleigh and inspect the mean vector.
2
General CDF departureUse Kuiper or Watson and verify origin invariance.
3
Cluster or empty arcInspect spacings, Rao spacing, and semicircle scans.
4
Multimodal or axial signalAdd harmonics, Hermans-Rasson, Beran, or another omnibus energy.
5
Specified circular modelTransform by the model CDF and test PIT angles for uniformity.
Turn the lecture into a decision workflow. A single preferred direction calls for Rayleigh. General CDF deviations call for Kuiper or Watson. Empty arcs and clustered spacings call for Rao, Ajne, or related spacing scans. Symmetric multimodal structure calls for higher harmonics or energy tests. Model adequacy calls for PIT followed by uniformity diagnostics.
Warnings
What the tests do not say

Common Misreads

Rayleigh Non-rejection means no detected first-harmonic direction, not verified uniformity.
CDF plots A step plot drawn from one cut is a coordinate view, not yet a circular statistic.
PIT with fitted parameters A fitted-model PIT changes the null calibration because the data helped choose the transform.
Pause on mistakes because this chapter is full of plausible but wrong shortcuts. A nonsignificant Rayleigh statistic is not proof of uniformity. A CDF plot drawn from one origin is not a circular statistic by itself. A fitted PIT diagnostic must remember that parameters were estimated. A useful test report names the geometry it is capable of seeing.
