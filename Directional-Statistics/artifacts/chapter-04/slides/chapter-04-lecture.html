Characteristic Functions

A Circular Law Is A Harmonic Fingerprint

Definition

\(\displaystyle \phi_p = E\{\exp(i p\Theta)\}, \quad p\in\mathbb Z.\)

  • The zero coefficient is fixed: \(\phi_0=1\).
  • For a real circular law, \(\phi_{-p}=\overline{\phi_p}\).
  • All distributional information lives on integer harmonics, not a continuum of frequencies.

The theorem-level idea: convergence and identity of circular laws can be tested through this coefficient sequence.

Emphasize the difference from the line. The natural frequency grid is the set of integers, so a distribution is probed by a countable coefficient sequence. The picture shows that magnitude records how much harmonic structure survives while phase records the angular alignment of that structure.
Fourier Rules

Operations Become Coefficient Algebra

Rotation

If \(\Theta'=\Theta+\alpha\), then

\(\phi'_p=e^{ip\alpha}\phi_p\)

same magnitudes, shifted phases.

Circular Sum

If \(\Theta=X+Y \pmod{2\pi}\) and \(X,Y\) are independent, then

\(\phi_p=\phi^X_p\phi^Y_p\)

convolution becomes multiplication.

Reality Check

Coefficient arrays must obey

\(\phi_0=1,\quad \phi_{-p}=\overline{\phi_p}\)

or they cannot describe a probability law on the circle.

Present this as the reason characteristic functions are more than a diagnostic plot. Rotation multiplies each harmonic by a phase factor, convolution multiplies matching coefficients, and conjugate symmetry remembers that the original measure is real. These rules are the computational form of the fundamental theorems.
Distribution Families

Model Fingerprints Look Different

Recognition Principle

A family is often easier to compare by the decay, phase, and sparsity of \(\{\phi_p\}\) than by a plotted density alone.

Walk through the strip from left to right. Uniformity kills every nonzero harmonic, a lattice law keeps exact spikes, a cardioid only perturbs the first harmonic, wrapped normal coefficients decay quadratically in frequency, and von Mises coefficients decay through Bessel ratios. The goal is recognition, not memorization.
Sample Geometry

Resultants Turn Angles Into A Random Walk

Statistic

\(\displaystyle C=\sum_j \cos\theta_j,\quad S=\sum_j \sin\theta_j,\quad R=\sqrt{C^2+S^2}.\)

  • \(\hat\mu=\operatorname{atan2}(S,C)\) is meaningful only when \(R>0\).
  • \(\bar R=R/n\) measures concentration of the sample directions.
  • Uniform angles create an isotropic unit-step random walk.
Move from distribution theory to sample geometry. Each observation contributes a unit vector, and the endpoint contains both the mean direction and the resultant length. Under isotropy the direction of the endpoint is not informative, but the radius remains a statistic with a nontrivial distribution.
Resultant Distribution

Uniform Resultants Have A Rayleigh Scale

Large \(n\) Uniform Limit

\(\displaystyle T_n=2n\bar R^2=\frac{2R^2}{n}\;\Rightarrow\;\chi^2_2.\)

The endpoint cloud becomes nearly bivariate normal with equal coordinate variance, so squared radius has a chi-square reference law.

Small samples keep exact boundaries and should not be treated as already asymptotic.

The key normalization is not the raw length but two n times the squared mean resultant length. For many independent uniform angles, the endpoint coordinates behave like a planar normal, so the squared radius approaches chi-square with two degrees of freedom. Stress that small samples still keep exact circular geometry.
Coordinate Warning

The Mean Direction Is A Derived Coordinate

Map

\(\displaystyle \hat\mu=\operatorname{atan2}(\bar S,\bar C)\)

  • The vector mean \((\bar C,\bar S)\) has ordinary planar coordinates.
  • The angular coordinate folds those coordinates around the origin.
  • The delta method needs a base point away from the singularity.
This slide is about a common failure mode. The planar average has a central limit theorem even when its endpoint lies near the origin, but the angle coordinate is unstable at zero length. Tangent normal approximations require a nonzero population resultant so the atan map can be linearized.
Circular Limit Theorem

Planar CLT First, Tangent CLT Second

Two-Step Approximation

\(\sqrt n\{(\bar C,\bar S)-E(\cos\Theta,\sin\Theta)\}\Rightarrow N_2(0,\Sigma)\)

\(\sqrt n(\hat\mu-\mu)\approx \text{projection of that ellipse onto the tangent line.}\)

The tangent coordinate is local. It is a derivative of the angle map, not a separate sampling theorem detached from the vector mean.

State the order of operations clearly. First apply the multivariate central limit theorem to the cosine-sine average. Then project the resulting ellipse onto the tangent line at the population mean direction. This is why the angular variance is a delta-method quantity rather than a primitive scalar variance.
Von Mises Coordinates

Von Mises Gives Explicit Variance Pieces

Bessel Ratios

\(\displaystyle A(\kappa)=I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa),\quad A_2(\kappa)=I_2(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa).\)

After Rotating \(\mu=0\)

\(\operatorname{var}(\cos\Theta)=\frac{1+A_2}{2}-A^2,\quad \operatorname{var}(\sin\Theta)=\frac{1-A_2}{2}.\)

The perpendicular component is the one that perturbs the estimated direction.

For the von Mises model, Bessel ratios provide concrete covariance entries for the cosine and sine coordinates after rotating the mean to zero. The perpendicular variance controls mean-direction error, while the parallel variance controls resultant-length fluctuation. Keeping the two axes separate avoids overusing a one-dimensional normal story.
Local Approximation

High Concentration Makes The Circle Locally Flat

Large \(\kappa\) Heuristic

\(\displaystyle u=\sqrt{\kappa}(\Theta-\mu)_{\mathrm{wrap}}\approx N(0,1).\)

  • Arc length and tangent length become nearly the same.
  • Cosine loss behaves quadratically: \(1-\cos x\approx x^2/2\).
  • Quadratic angular scatter leads to chi-square references.
When concentration is large, most mass lives in a narrow arc near the mean direction. Rescaling angular deviations by the square root of kappa turns the arc into an approximately normal coordinate. The visual target is the transition from curved circle geometry to an almost straight tangent calculation.
High-Concentration Distribution Theory

Quadratic Scatter Splits Into Two Pieces

Corrected Decomposition

\(\displaystyle 2n\gamma(1-\bar C)=2n\gamma(1-\bar R)+2n\gamma(\bar R-\bar C),\qquad \gamma^{-1}=\kappa^{-1}+\frac{3}{8\kappa^2}.\)

Use the brace diagram to connect the approximation to ordinary normal sums of squares. The total centered scatter around the true direction separates into variation around the fitted mean and movement of the fitted mean itself. The corrected concentration gamma keeps the approximation accurate deeper into finite samples.
Choosing References

Approximation Regimes Are Not Interchangeable

Uniform random walk Needs enough independent steps for the endpoint cloud to look planar-normal.
Tangent CLT Needs nonzero population resultant and enough observations for linearization.
High concentration Needs a tight arc so wrapped deviations behave like line-normal deviations.
Finite-sample exactness Still matters near boundaries, low n, or weak concentration.
This is the applied warning slide. Increasing n helps the uniform random-walk chi-square limit, while increasing kappa helps the local normal and high-concentration chi-square limits. A procedure can be asymptotically correct and still be a poor numerical approximation for the regime in front of us.
Notebook Lab

A Diagnostic Workflow

Applied Lab Prompt

Before using a reference distribution, choose the statistic, match the regime, and inspect a simulation-based distance to the reference law.

Frame the workflow as a reproducible habit. Identify whether the statistic is radial, angular, or high-concentration quadratic; simulate at the planned sample size and concentration; compare against the reference law; and only then decide whether the approximation is accurate enough for inference.
Failure Modes

Common Mistakes To Catch Early

Line Thinking

Averaging raw angles near the cut can create artificial jumps. Use unit-vector summaries before choosing coordinates.

Zero Resultant

A normal approximation for \(\hat\mu\) is not meaningful when the population endpoint is at the origin.

Wrong Regime

High-concentration chi-square formulas are local arc approximations, not generic circular sample-size approximations.

Use this slide to slow down before the recap. Most mistakes come from treating angles as line variables, applying an angle normal approximation when the resultant is near zero, or borrowing a high-concentration formula in a diffuse sample. Each mistake has a visible diagnostic in the preceding slides.
