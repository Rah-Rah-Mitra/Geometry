The Contract: Work On The Sample Space

Directional constraint

Observations are unit vectors \(x_i\in S^2\). Procedures may compute in \(\mathbb R^3\), but their summaries must be interpreted through angles, caps, axes, or surface density.

Audit habit

Every modern method needs an invariant: unit norm, angular residual, density mass, bootstrap cone radius, or geodesic smoothing error.

Before moving into individual methods, establish the quality-control rule. A tangent-plane calculation can be useful locally, but the final object must be a direction, cone, density, or curve on the sphere, with a check that makes the constraint visible.

Outliers Are Angular Influence Problems

Diagnostic shift

A spherical outlier has no large radius. It has leverage because its angle from the cluster changes \(R=\|\sum_i x_i\|\) and rotates \(\hat\mu\).

$$I_j=\angle\!\left(\hat\mu,\hat\mu_{(-j)}\right),\qquad \bar R=\frac{\|R\|}{n}$$

Inspection target: separate direction shift from concentration loss. The antipodal point can make the data look less concentrated even when the cluster remains visually obvious.

Explain that outliers on the sphere are not points with unusual magnitude, because every observation has unit length. The diagnostic question is how much one direction changes the resultant vector, either by rotating the fitted mean direction or by shortening the concentration summary.

Influence Sweep: Move One Contaminant

Experiment

Hold a clean cluster fixed. Move one contaminating direction around a great circle and recompute the ordinary mean after each placement.

Read both panels

The angular shift and mean resultant length need not peak at the same location. A robust analysis watches both.

Use this slide as a mental version of the notebook's contaminant sweep. Students should learn to ask which observation would change the direction estimate if removed, and which observation mostly reduces the evidence for concentration without rotating the pole much.

Robust Direction: Weight Angular Residuals

Huber-style update

Start from a current direction \(m\), compute angular residuals \(\theta_i=\angle(x_i,m)\), and downweight observations beyond a cutoff \(c\).

$$w_i=\min\!\left(1,\frac{c}{\theta_i}\right),\qquad m_{\text{new}}=\frac{\sum_i w_i x_i}{\|\sum_i w_i x_i\|}$$
Robustness statement

The estimator is not just "less sensitive." It specifies which angular residuals lose influence and still returns a unit direction.

Present the robust estimator as an algorithm with a geometric loss, not as a black-box replacement for the mean. The cutoff has an angular interpretation, and the normalization step is part of the definition rather than an afterthought.

Bootstrap Confidence Cone

Bootstrap statistic

For each resample \(b\), compute a direction \(\hat\mu^{*(b)}\) and record \(a_b=\angle(\hat\mu^{*(b)},\hat\mu)\).

$$\alpha_{0.95}=\operatorname{quantile}_{0.95}\{a_b\},\qquad C=\{u\in S^2:\angle(u,\hat\mu)\leq \alpha_{0.95}\}$$
Interpretation

The interval is a spherical cap. It is not a coordinatewise error bar attached to three Euclidean components.

Emphasize that the bootstrap preserves the original sample space by resampling directions, not coordinates from a tangent chart unless a tangent method has been explicitly justified. The uncertainty summary is angular, so the final report is immediately readable on the sphere.

Bootstrap Workflow: Resample, Refit, Reproject, Report

Sample Use observed directions \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) with replacement.
Refit Compute the chosen estimator on each resampled data set.
Angle Measure \(\angle(\hat\mu^*,\hat\mu)\), not componentwise spread.
Report Convert an empirical quantile into a confidence cone radius.
Low concentration warning

When the sample resultant is short, bootstrap directions can spread widely. That is information, not an algorithm failure.

Walk through the workflow slowly because it prevents two common mistakes. Learners should see that resampling happens among the original directions, and that the bootstrap distribution is summarized by angles away from the fitted pole rather than by separate coordinate standard errors.

Density Estimation: Kernels Must Integrate On The Sphere

$$\hat f(u)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} c_3(\kappa)\exp\{\kappa u^\top x_i\},\qquad u\in S^2$$
Smoothing parameter

The concentration \(\kappa\) controls bump width. Large \(\kappa\) gives sharp caps; small \(\kappa\) spreads mass broadly.

Density check

A heatmap earns the word density only after \(\int_{S^2}\hat f(u)\,d\omega(u)\approx 1\).

Introduce spherical kernel density estimation by comparing it with ordinary KDE only at the conceptual level. The important directional feature is normalization with respect to surface area, so the map and the sphere view are both representations of the same curved density.

Bandwidth Tradeoff: Detail Against Stability

Too small kappa

The estimate can erase real modes and make separated directional clusters look like one broad cap.

Too large kappa

The estimate can become a constellation of observation-sized spikes rather than a population-level surface.

Practical check

Report the chosen \(\kappa\), inspect mass normalization, and compare density features against raw spherical scatter.

Frame bandwidth choice as a scientific judgment with numerical checks, not as a purely aesthetic preference. A directional density estimate should reveal stable angular structure while still integrating properly and agreeing with the visible scatter pattern.

Bayesian Idea: Prior As Pseudo-Resultant

Geometric core

A prior direction \(m_0\) with strength \(\tau\) behaves like an additional resultant vector.

$$m_{\text{post}}\propto R+\tau m_0,\qquad R=\sum_i x_i$$
Reading the prior

The prior is not an unconstrained coordinate vector. It has a direction and a visible strength relative to the observed resultant.

Treat the Bayesian material as a geometric doorway. Without deriving a full posterior, students can understand why a prior direction and prior strength act like a pseudo-resultant that shrinks the sample mean toward a stated directional belief.

Prior Strength Is A Sensitivity Axis

Sensitivity plot

Vary \(\tau\). If the fitted direction changes dramatically over plausible strengths, the data are not dominating the prior.

Reporting rule

Never hide directional shrinkage inside coordinates. Report the angular movement from the sample mean and from the prior pole.

Ask the audience to read the plot as a robustness check for the prior. A good report does not merely state that a Bayesian estimate was used; it explains how many degrees the answer moved as the prior pseudo-count became more influential.

Smoothing A Spherical Response

Extrinsic local average

For predictor \(t\), average nearby response vectors with kernel weights, then project the fitted vector back to the unit sphere.

$$\tilde m(t)=\sum_i K_h(t-t_i)x_i,\qquad \hat m(t)=\frac{\tilde m(t)}{\|\tilde m(t)\|}$$
Evaluation

Use angular residuals \(\angle(x_i,\hat m(t_i))\) and unit-norm checks, not ordinary vertical errors.

Connect smoothing to regression without flattening the response. The fitted curve can be computed through ambient weighted averages, but the final curve has to be renormalized, and model quality should be stated in angular residuals along the sphere.

Method Audit: The Invariants To Save

A reproducible modern analysis records checks
  • input vectors have norm one;
  • robust direction moves less under contamination;
  • bootstrap quantiles are ordered and angular;
  • KDE mass is near one on \(S^2\);
  • smoothing output remains unit length.
This slide translates the notebook's numerical sanity checks into habits students can use in their own projects. Modern methods are flexible, so the audit trail matters: every flexible choice should be paired with a geometry-preserving invariant.

Common Pitfalls In Modern Directional Work

Outliers

Looking for radial extremes misses antipodal leverage and off-cluster angular leverage.

Bootstrap

Coordinate intervals can imply impossible fitted points inside or outside the sphere.

Density

A rectangular heatmap can hide polar distortion unless surface area normalization is checked.

Bayes

A prior with no stated directional strength is hard to audit or communicate.

Use the pitfalls slide as a practical warning before the recap. The mistakes all share the same structure: a familiar method is applied in coordinates, but the report forgets to return to angular quantities, surface-area normalization, or unit-norm fitted values.

Recap And Instructor Prompt

Takeaways
