Open by reframing shape analysis as directional statistics after several quotient operations. The data begin as landmark coordinates, but the object of inference is not the drawing on the page. It is the configuration after translation, scale, and rotation have been treated as nuisance directions.
Route

Six Geometric Decisions Behind Shape Statistics

1. Register Represent planar landmarks as a complex vector with a declared landmark order.
2. Remove Pose Center, Helmertize, and divide by size to reach the preshape sphere.
3. Quotient Phase Identify all representatives \(e^{i\theta}z\) as the same planar shape.
4. Compare Use Procrustes alignment, distance, and mean shapes as invariant summaries.
5. Linearize Use horizontal tangent coordinates only for concentrated shape samples.
6. Model Choose sphere, projective, or tangent models by the sample-space geometry.

The chapter's statistical question is always paired with a geometric question: which transformations should leave the answer unchanged?

Use the route slide to keep the chapter from becoming a catalog of methods. Every stop removes or models a specific geometric feature: centering removes location, normalization removes size, phase quotienting removes planar rotation, Procrustes summaries compare shapes, tangent coordinates approximate local variation, and complex directional families provide global models.
Representation

Planar Landmarks Form A Complex Vector

Configuration

A landmark configuration with \(k\) planar points is stored as \(z_0=(x_1+iy_1,\ldots,x_k+iy_k)\in\mathbb{C}^k\).

\(z_0 \mapsto a\,e^{i\phi} z_0 + b\mathbf{1}\), where \(a>0\), \(\phi\in[0,2\pi)\), and \(b\in\mathbb{C}\).
Statistician's Choice

Translation, scale, and rotation may be nuisance transformations; landmark relabeling is not removed unless the scientific design says it should be.

Establish the computational representation before any statistics appear. A planar landmark configuration is a complex vector, one entry per landmark. Similarity transformations move the entire vector in predictable ways, so the statistical task is to decide which parts of that movement are irrelevant to shape.
Preshape Construction

Center, Helmertize, Normalize

Translation Filter

The Helmert submatrix \(H\) has orthonormal rows and satisfies \(H\mathbf{1}=0\), so common translations vanish.

\(u=Hz_0,\qquad z=\dfrac{u}{\|u\|}\in S^{2k-3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{k-1}\)
Notebook Check

Helmert orthogonality error is approximately \(4.23\times 10^{-16}\), and the base preshape norm is \(1.0\).

Explain the pipeline as a sequence of invariant filters. Subtracting the centroid and multiplying by a Helmert submatrix both remove the all-ones translation direction, while division by norm removes scale. The resulting preshape lies on a complex unit sphere, where rotations still act as a common phase.
Quotient Geometry

A Shape Is A Phase Orbit, Not A Drawing

\([z]=\{e^{i\theta}z:\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}\), so \(z\) and \(e^{i\theta}z\) represent the same planar shape.
Sample Space

For \(k\) planar landmarks, preshapes lie on a complex unit sphere and shapes live in \(\mathbb{CP}^{k-2}\).

Invariant Statistic

A valid shape statistic must be unchanged by common phase multiplication of every preshape representative.

Pause on the quotient because it is the conceptual hinge of the chapter. A preshape is one representative, but a shape is the entire circle of representatives obtained by common phase multiplication. Statistics on shape space must give the same answer anywhere along that orbit.
Comparison

Procrustes Distance Uses The Best Phase Alignment

\(d_P([z_1],[z_2])=\sqrt{1-|\langle z_1,z_2\rangle|^2}\)
Alignment

The optimal common phase makes \(\langle z_1,e^{i\theta}z_2\rangle\) real and nonnegative.

0.04349 Procrustes shape distance in the notebook sample
2.55e-15 distance invariance error under pose changes
Introduce Procrustes distance through the modulus of the complex inner product. The phase of the inner product tells how to align one preshape to the other, but its absolute value tells how close the shapes are after the best alignment. This is why the formula is invariant under separate similarity transformations.
Mean Shape

A Mean Shape Is An Aligned Average On The Quotient

Optimization View

Choose a shape class \([m]\) that minimizes total squared Procrustes distance to the sample.

\(\widehat{[m]}=\arg\min_{[m]}\sum_i d_P([z_i],[m])^2\)
Algorithmic Loop

Align each preshape to the current mean representative, average the aligned preshapes, renormalize, and repeat.

Describe the Procrustes mean as an averaging problem with alignment built in. Each observation is first rotated into the current frame, then the aligned representatives are averaged and renormalized. The mean is a shape class, so the displayed representative is only a convenient gauge.
Triangle Shapes

For Three Landmarks, The Quotient Is A Sphere

\((x,y,z)=\left(2\operatorname{Re}(z_1\overline{z_2}),\, |z_1|^2-|z_2|^2,\, 2\operatorname{Im}(z_1\overline{z_2})\right)\)
Hopf/Kendall Coordinates

Common phase multiplication leaves all three coordinates unchanged, so the map belongs to shape space rather than pose space.

Inspection Targets

Flat triangles land on the equator; mirror-image triangles cross hemispheres; equilateral shapes sit near opposite poles.

Use triangle shapes as the place where the quotient becomes visible. For three landmarks, complex projective shape space is an ordinary sphere under the Hopf coordinates. Flat triangles sit on an equator, mirror images live on opposite sides, and common phase rotations leave the plotted point fixed.
Local Linearization

Tangent Coordinates Are A Concentrated-Sample Approximation

Horizontal Residual

Align each preshape to the mean, take a logarithmic residual, then remove the vertical phase direction.

\(v_i \in T_{\widehat{[m]}}\mathcal{S},\qquad \langle v_i,m\rangle=0\)
Regime Check

Use tangent coordinates only when the sample cloud is concentrated enough that curvature is a small local effect.

Make the tangent approximation conditional rather than automatic. When shapes are concentrated near a Procrustes mean, aligned logarithmic residuals can be treated as horizontal tangent vectors. Horizontal means the vector changes shape rather than merely sliding along the phase orbit.
Local Variation

Tangent PCA Should Recover Shape Deformations

Teaching Target

Principal directions should alter the landmark geometry itself: bending, elongation, or local bulging, not a pose transformation.

Why Alignment Matters

The notebook hides each synthetic shape under random translations, scales, and rotations before recovering deformation modes.

tangent PCA: shape residuals \(\longrightarrow\) covariance \(\longrightarrow\) interpretable deformation paths
Interpret tangent PCA as a local deformation tool. Principal components in the tangent space should describe interpretable changes in landmark geometry, not translations, rotations, or scale. The notebook sample deliberately hides nuisance transformations so the recovery of deformation modes becomes a check on the geometry.
Model Families

Model Choice Follows The Shape Space

Triangle Shapes

Use the Kendall sphere and spherical directional tools when \(k=3\).

General Planar Shapes

Use phase-invariant families on \(\mathbb{CP}^{k-2}\): complex Bingham, complex Watson, and complex angular Gaussian models.

Local Approximation

Tangent normal models are useful for tight clouds, but they are not global distributions on shape space.

Route model choice by sample space. Triangle shapes can be analyzed on the Kendall sphere, while general planar landmark shapes require phase-invariant models on complex projective space. Complex Bingham, Watson, and angular Gaussian families differ in how much anisotropy and robustness they allow.

Pitfalls

Four Ways Shape Analysis Goes Off Geometry

Pose As Shape

Comparing raw coordinate arrays lets translation, scale, or drawing angle drive the answer.

Unaligned Averaging

Averaging preshapes without phase alignment blurs rotations into false deformation.

Global Tangent PCA

A tangent plane is local. Spread-out shape samples need global shape-space methods.

Wrong Density Space

A complex directional model must respect the phase quotient or it models representatives rather than shapes.

Warn against the shortcuts that make shape analysis look easier than it is. Comparing raw coordinate arrays confuses pose with shape, averaging unaligned preshapes blurs rotations, using tangent PCA globally ignores curvature, and fitting a directional model before naming the quotient can put density on the wrong space.

Present the workflow as an instructor-ready checklist. Start from the landmark registration convention, remove translation and scale, work with shape classes, choose Procrustes or tangent summaries according to concentration, then select complex directional models only when their symmetry matches the scientific question.
Recap

Shape Analysis Is Statistics On Quotient Sample Spaces

Core Chain

Landmarks \(\rightarrow\) centered Helmert coordinates \(\rightarrow\) preshapes \(\rightarrow\) phase orbits \(\rightarrow\) shape-space statistics.

Reliable Summaries

Procrustes distances and means use phase-invariant inner products; tangent PCA uses local horizontal coordinates.

Model Discipline

Sphere, complex projective, and tangent models are not interchangeable; each assumes a different sample space.

Close by tying the chapter back to the course. Shape analysis is directional statistics on quotient sample spaces. The successful analysis is not the one with the most elaborate model, but the one whose summaries, approximations, and diagnostics respect the invariances of the measured shapes.
