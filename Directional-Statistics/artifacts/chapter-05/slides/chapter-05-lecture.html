Open by saying that point estimation on a circle starts with a contract, not with a formula. The zero angle is arbitrary, so any estimator worth using must rotate with the data. This chapter asks how that contract shapes von Mises location, concentration, wrapped Cauchy weighting, and mixture fitting.
Geometric Requirement
A point estimate must commute with rotation.

The Estimator Must Respect Rotation

Equivariance Test

For a common rotation \( \delta \), a circular estimator should satisfy

\( \hat\mu(\theta_1+\delta,\ldots,\theta_n+\delta) = \hat\mu(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)+\delta \pmod{2\pi}. \)

The concentration estimate should be invariant: \( \hat\kappa(\theta+\delta)=\hat\kappa(\theta) \).

The whole chapter can be read as a search for estimating equations that satisfy this diagram while still learning concentration and component structure from the sample.

Estimator contractLocation equivariant; concentration invariant.
Use the commutative diagram as the guiding rule for the whole lecture. Rotating the sample before fitting should give the same result as fitting first and then rotating the fitted direction. Concentration is different: it measures clustering strength, so it should remain unchanged under a common rotation.
Sample Geometry

The Resultant Is The Data Geometry

Statistic

\( \bar z = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n e^{i\theta_j} = \bar C + i\bar S \)
\( \hat\mu = \arg(\bar z), \qquad \bar R = |\bar z| \)
  • \( \hat\mu \) is a direction, not an arithmetic angle average.
  • \( \bar R \) measures how strongly the unit vectors align.
  • For the notebook sample: n = 32, Rbar = 0.7797, mu-hat = 0.5528.
Unit-vector averageResultant length becomes the concentration signal.
Explain that the sample is first moved to unit vectors, then summarized by the average complex vector. Its direction is the natural circular mean, and its length is the concentration signal. The diagram intentionally avoids an angle line, because the circle, not the cut coordinate, carries the statistic.
Von Mises MLE
Location and concentration are coupled but separable.

Von Mises Likelihood Splits The Job

Log Likelihood Up To Constants

\( \ell(\mu,\kappa) = \kappa\sum_{j=1}^n \cos(\theta_j-\mu) - n\log I_0(\kappa) \)
1. Align

For fixed \( \kappa \), maximize the cosine sum.

2. Direction

\( \hat\mu = \arg\sum e^{i\theta_j} \).

3. Strength

Solve \( A(\hat\kappa)=\bar R \).

The notebook verifies the score at the fit: score_mu = -2.35e-15 and score_kappa = -1.42e-14.

Von Mises estimating equations\(A(\kappa)=I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)\).
Walk through the log likelihood as two linked problems. For a fixed concentration, maximizing over direction means aligning with the resultant vector. After that alignment, the concentration score asks for the model mean resultant length A(kappa) to match the observed Rbar.
Concentration Equation
Root finding replaces a line-variance shortcut.

Concentration Comes From Inverting \(A\)

MLE Equation

\( A(\kappa)=\frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)}, \qquad A(\hat\kappa)=\bar R. \)
  • \( A(0)=0 \), and \( A(\kappa) \) increases toward 1.
  • Large \( \bar R \) requires larger \( \hat\kappa \).
  • Notebook fit: kappa-hat = 2.6430 and A(kappa-hat) = 0.7797.
Bessel-ratio inversionPiecewise starts are useful, but the target is still \(A(\hat\kappa)=\bar R\).
Make the Bessel-ratio curve the main object. Students often expect concentration to be a sample variance formula, but the von Mises model uses the monotone relationship between kappa and expected resultant length. Numerical inversion is not a nuisance here; it is the statistical definition made executable.

Bias On A Circle
The branch cut should not decide whether an estimator is centered.

Circular Unbiasedness Is Not Linear Unbiasedness

Diagnostic

\( E\{\sin(\hat\mu-\mu)\}=0 \)

The sine of the wrapped error records signed tangential imbalance without choosing a privileged cut point.

  • Linear error \( \hat\mu-\mu \) can jump near \( \pm\pi \).
  • Wrapped error lives in a chosen interval but still respects the circle.
  • Sine error checks centering in the tangent direction.
Circular centeringMean sine error is stable under the angular coordinate choice.
Pause on the sine-error diagnostic because it prevents a common mistake. A signed angle error depends on where the branch cut is placed, so it is not a stable criterion. Taking the sine of the wrapped error asks whether the estimator is centered on the circle without pretending angles form a line.
Weighted Circular Updates
Wrapped Cauchy behavior is a disk-valued reweighting problem.

Wrapped Cauchy Estimation Reweights Vectors

Weighted Equation

A wrapped Cauchy location update can be read as a weighted resultant:

\( \bar z_w = \frac{\sum_j w_j e^{i\theta_j}}{\sum_j w_j} \)
  • Weights depend on the current disk parameter.
  • The update is still a unit-vector calculation.
  • The notebook check keeps weights positive and the path inside the unit disk.
Wrapped Cauchy fittingFinal parameter norm in notebook check: 0.5549.
Frame wrapped Cauchy fitting as another version of the same geometry. The current parameter sits inside the unit disk, and the observations receive weights determined by their circular relationship to it. The update is a weighted vector average, so the computation stays on circular objects.
Mixture Estimation
Responsibilities turn each component fit into a weighted circular MLE.

Mixtures Replace Hard Assignment With Responsibilities

EM Responsibilities

\( r_{ij} = \frac{\pi_j f_{\mathrm{vM}}(\theta_i;\mu_j,\kappa_j)}{\sum_m \pi_m f_{\mathrm{vM}}(\theta_i;\mu_m,\kappa_m)} \)

For each observation, the row of responsibilities sums to one. The M step then recomputes \( \mu_j \) and \( \kappa_j \) from weighted resultants.

Notebook mixture weights: 0.526381 and 0.473619.

Von Mises mixture EMResponsibility row-sum max error: 2.22e-16.
Describe the EM loop without reducing it to generic clustering. Each component has a circular density, and each observation receives responsibilities that sum to one. The M step then performs weighted circular estimation for each component, which keeps the fit invariant to the angular cut.

Implementation Pattern
A geometric estimator is a sequence of invariance-preserving moves.

Estimator Pipeline

1

Wrap Before Comparing

Every angular difference is reduced modulo \(2\pi\), so the coordinate cut cannot create artificial residuals.

2

Average Unit Vectors

Use \(e^{i\theta_j}\) and the resultant \( \bar z \) for direction and concentration evidence.

3

Solve Or Weight

For von Mises, solve \(A(\kappa)=\bar R\). For wrapped Cauchy and mixtures, update weighted resultants.

4

Check Geometry

Verify score equations, small-sample behavior, responsibility sums, and rotation equivariance.

Warning
Linear instincts can quietly break circular inference.

Mistakes To Catch Early

Angle Mean

Averaging raw degrees can put the estimate across the cut from every observation.

Line Variance

Von Mises concentration is not a plug-in sample variance on unwrapped angles.

Unwrapped Residual

Likelihood and score calculations need wrapped angular differences.

Rigid Labels

Mixture components are learned through responsibilities, not permanent early assignments.

Common failure modesThe fix is always to return to unit-vector geometry.
Use this warning slide to make students skeptical of familiar linear shortcuts. Averaging degree values across a branch cut, plugging a line variance into concentration, forgetting to wrap residuals, or treating mixture labels as fixed can all give plausible-looking but geometrically wrong estimates.
