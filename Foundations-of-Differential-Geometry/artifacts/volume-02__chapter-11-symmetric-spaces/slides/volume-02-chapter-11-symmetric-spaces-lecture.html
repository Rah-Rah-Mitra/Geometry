Affine geometry first

Local Symmetry At A Point

The reflection is defined through the exponential map.

Definition Surface

A local symmetry s_x is the map near x that reverses every geodesic issuing from x.

s_x(exp_x X) = exp_x(-X)
  • Differential at the center: d s_x = -I.
  • Involutive near the center.
  • Affine local symmetry means every s_x preserves the affine connection.
Emphasize that the first object is local and geometric. In normal coordinates, the symmetry at a point reverses tangent vectors through the exponential map. This is not yet a group action on the whole manifold; it is a test for whether the affine connection looks identical in opposite geodesic directions.
Invariant test

The Affine Criterion

Local point symmetries force the connection data to stabilize.

Criterion

A manifold with affine connection is affine locally symmetric exactly when the obstruction tensors satisfy

T = 0    and    ∇R = 0

The first condition removes torsion. The second says curvature is transported unchanged.

Instructor cue: this is the affine version of a familiar idea from constant-curvature models, but the hypothesis is parallel curvature rather than numerically constant curvature.
Explain the criterion as a compression of many coordinate identities into two invariant statements. Torsion must vanish because a point reflection reverses odd first-order asymmetry. Curvature must be parallel because the same curvature model is seen after moving by affine symmetries from point to point.
Homogeneous encoding

From Symmetries To A Triple

A global symmetric space is recorded as (G,H,σ).

Symmetric Triple

A symmetric space is encoded by a connected Lie group G, a closed subgroup H, and an involutive automorphism σ.

σ^2 = id,    G^σ_0 ⊂ H ⊂ G^σ

The quotient G/H carries the point symmetries.

EffectiveNo nontrivial normal subgroup of G hides inside H.
Almost EffectiveThe hidden normal subgroup is allowed only if discrete.
The homogeneous triple is the chapter's first major translation. Choose a base point, collect connected affine transformations, record the isotropy subgroup, and let the base-point symmetry conjugate the transformation group. The fixed subgroup condition says that isotropy is exactly the group-level shadow of reflection.
Algebra under reflection

The Lie Algebra Split

The differential of the involution has plus and minus eigenspaces.

Bracket Table

BracketWhere it lands
[h,h]h
[h,m]m
[m,m]h

The tangent space at the origin is identified with m.

Reading habit: whenever a theorem names a geometric condition, ask which row of the bracket table is doing the work.
Pause on the eigenspace decomposition because it drives nearly every later formula. The plus part is infinitesimal isotropy and the minus part identifies with tangent vectors at the base point. The bracket table is the algebraic replacement for geometric reflection rules.
Reverse construction

Canonical Connection

The symmetric triple builds an affine symmetric geometry on G/H.

Canonical Connection On G/H

For the canonical decomposition g = h + m, choose the invariant connection whose tangent part has no extra bilinear correction at the origin.

T = 0,    ∇R = 0,    R(X,Y)Z = -[[X,Y],Z]
  • The connection is invariant under every point symmetry.
  • The curvature is algebraic: bracket, project, act.
  • Any invariant metric compatible with the quotient uses this same Levi-Civita connection.
The canonical connection is the reverse construction: start from the symmetric triple and recover an affine symmetric geometry on the quotient. At the origin, the connection is as simple as possible on the tangent part, while curvature is still present through the isotropy bracket.
Metric specialization

Riemannian Symmetric Spaces

Symmetries are isometries, and the canonical connection becomes Levi-Civita.

Riemannian Upgrade

  • Each point symmetry extends to a global isometry.
  • The identity component of the isometry group acts transitively.
  • The isotropy representation is compact for a positive definite metric.
  • Simply connected complete spaces split by de Rham into symmetric factors.
∇g = 0    and    ∇ = ∇LC
Specialize carefully. A Riemannian symmetric space is not just a symmetric space with a metric attached; its geodesic symmetries are isometries and the canonical connection becomes Levi-Civita. Completeness and transitivity make local reflection data into global geometry.
Subgeometry

Totally Geodesic Submanifolds

A geometric containment condition becomes a triple bracket test.

Closure Test

For a tangent subspace m' inside m, the symmetric-space test is

[[m',m'],m'] ⊂ m'

When it holds, exponentiating the corresponding symmetric subalgebra produces a totally geodesic submanifold.

Geometric readingGeodesics that begin in the submanifold never bend out of it.
Algebraic readingCurvature generated by m' maps m' back into itself.
This slide is the main bridge from global space to subspace testing. A submanifold is totally geodesic when geodesics that start tangent remain inside. In the symmetric algebra, that geometric condition becomes a triple bracket closure, which is much easier to check in examples.
Algebraic reduction

Structure Of Symmetric Lie Algebras

The involution respects the pieces used to simplify the algebra.

Reduction Moves

  • The radical is invariant under every automorphism, hence under the symmetry.
  • Levi decomposition can be chosen compatibly with the involution.
  • Semisimple pieces split into symmetric ideals.
  • Direct sums correspond to product behavior in the associated geometry.
Frame this as algebraic bookkeeping with geometric consequences. Radical and semisimple pieces must respect the involution, and direct-sum decompositions of symmetric Lie algebras mirror product decompositions of spaces. The point is not classification yet, but a controlled reduction of complexity.
Product geometry

Irreducible Factors

The de Rham split reduces the Riemannian problem to basic factors.

Why This Matters

For simply connected Riemannian symmetric spaces, each de Rham factor is itself symmetric.

  • Euclidean factor: flat and elementary.
  • Irreducible factors: classification targets.
  • Product factors: handled by direct sums of symmetric Lie algebras.
Connect the algebraic reductions to de Rham decomposition. A simply connected Riemannian symmetric space splits into Euclidean and irreducible factors, and each factor is again symmetric. Classification can therefore focus on irreducible orthogonal symmetric Lie algebras rather than arbitrary products.
Orthogonal symmetric Lie algebras

Compact And Noncompact Types

Metric signs sort the irreducible Riemannian families.

Sign Pattern

TypeAlgebraic signalGeometric behavior
CompactKilling form negative on gcompact model, nonnegative sectional curvature
Noncompactdual sign on the tangent directionssimply connected, often realized as a cell-like space
Euclideancurvature bracket vanishesflat factor
The Killing form supplies a sign test for the two major Riemannian families. Compact type has curvature with nonnegative behavior and compact models, while noncompact type has the dual sign and often Euclidean-like topology. Stress that the same bracket data is being read with different metric signs.
Compact paired with noncompact

Duality

Keep h, rotate m, and the curvature sign changes.

Dual Algebra

The dual construction keeps the isotropy algebra and replaces tangent directions by an imaginary copy.

g* = h + i m

The bracket table survives, but curvature signs are reversed between dual models.

Duality is the chapter's elegant mechanism for pairing examples. The isotropy algebra is kept fixed while tangent directions are multiplied by an imaginary unit at the algebraic level. In geometric terms, this flips the curvature sign and pairs compact spaces with noncompact companions.
Rank-one anchors

Sphere And Hyperbolic Models

The classic models show the same quotient grammar with opposite signs.

Two Quotients

S^n = SO(n+1)/SO(n)
H^n = SO^0(1,n)/SO(n)
  • The isotropy group acts on tangent directions as rotations.
  • Geodesic subspaces are selected by lower-dimensional invariant tangent subspaces.
  • The compact and noncompact versions are dual.
Use the two rank-one models as an anchor before introducing larger families. Both are quotients by the same isotropy type, and both have large symmetry groups. Their geodesic subspaces show why the bracket closure test is not abstract: it literally selects lower-dimensional copies inside the model.
Matrix quotient examples

Grassmann And Projective Families

Block matrices provide a readable way to see tangent directions and isotropy.

Example Grammar

  • Oriented real Grassmannians use orthogonal block decompositions.
  • Complex projective space uses unitary isotropy and a central complex structure.
  • Complex quadrics and Grassmannians add Hermitian curvature structure.
  • Subspaces satisfying the triple bracket criterion give totally geodesic examples.
The examples section broadens the catalog from rank-one spaces to matrix quotients. Oriented Grassmannians, complex projective spaces, quadrics, and complex Grassmannians all carry the same pattern: a block matrix decomposition, an invariant metric, and symmetric subspaces detected algebraically.
Complex plus metric

Hermitian Symmetric Spaces

A point symmetry now preserves the affine connection, metric, and complex structure.

Hermitian Signal

An invariant complex structure on m that is compatible with the metric upgrades the symmetric space to a Hermitian symmetric space.

I = ad_m(Z)   for central   Z in h
  • The canonical connection preserves I.
  • The metric and complex structure form a Kahler geometry.
  • Noncompact Hermitian examples appear as bounded symmetric domains.
Make the complex structure feel structural rather than decorative. In the Hermitian case, the tangent representation admits an invariant complex operator, often coming from a central isotropy element. The symmetries preserve both the metric and complex structure, so the geometry is automatically Kahler.
Endgame

Classification Outline

The chapter reduces geometry to algebraic lists without asking students to memorize the lists today.

1. CompleteWork with simply connected complete Riemannian symmetric spaces.
2. SplitUse de Rham to separate Euclidean and irreducible factors.
3. EncodeReplace each factor by an orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra.
4. DualizePair compact and noncompact types through the dual construction.
5. ClassifyUse Cartan's classification of real simple Lie algebras.
The final section is an outline, so present it as a pipeline rather than a table to memorize. Reduce to simply connected irreducible spaces, pass to orthogonal symmetric Lie algebras, use duality to focus on noncompact type, and then invoke the classification of real simple Lie algebras.
