Adapted Frames Separate What Moves

frame bundle of a submanifold

Definition

An adapted orthonormal frame at x lists a basis of T_xM followed by a basis of the normal space.

Why it matters

  • The ambient connection form breaks into tangent, normal, and mixed blocks.
  • The normal bundle is not extra decoration; it records exactly where tangent derivatives leave M.
  • Changing adapted frames rotates tangent and normal blocks separately.
Spend time on the word adapted. The frame is not arbitrary; it is chosen so the first entries know the submanifold and the later entries know the normal directions. Once that split is made, every connection coefficient has a job: intrinsic motion, normal motion, or the mixed bending term.

The Ambient Connection Splits

tangent plus normal bookkeeping

Gauss Formula
\(\bar\nabla_XY=\nabla_XY+\alpha(X,Y)\)

The tangential part is the induced Levi-Civita connection; the normal part is the second fundamental form.

Weingarten Formula
\(\bar\nabla_X\xi=-A_\xi X+D_X\xi\)

Normal fields also differentiate into tangent and normal pieces.

Pairing Rule
\(g(A_\xi X,Y)=g(\alpha(X,Y),\xi)\)
Present these formulas as bookkeeping before proving anything. If two tangent vector fields are differentiated using the ambient connection, the result need not be tangent. The induced connection keeps the tangential component, while the normal component measures bending. The Weingarten formula gives the dual story for normal fields.

The Gauss Map Sends Points to Planes

Grassmannian viewpoint

Two equivalent readings

  • Tangent reading: x maps to the oriented or unoriented tangent n-plane.
  • Normal reading: x maps to the complementary p-plane of normal directions.
  • Bundle reading: adapted frame bundles cover the map to the Grassmann manifold.
For hypersurfaces in Euclidean space

The Gauss map can be read as the unit normal field \(\nu:M\to S^n\).

The Gauss map is where the chapter turns a moving tangent plane into an object that can be differentiated. For a hypersurface students already know the normal vector field picture. The Grassmannian version is the same idea without assuming the normal space is one-dimensional.

The Second Fundamental Form Is a Bending Meter

normal component of tangent differentiation

Bilinear tensor
\(\alpha:T_xM\times T_xM\to T_xM^\perp\)

In Riemannian submanifold theory, symmetry follows from torsion-free ambient connection.

How to inspect it

  • Zero \(\alpha\): the submanifold is locally as straight as the ambient connection allows.
  • Large \(\alpha(X,X)\): acceleration of curves tangent to X points strongly normal.
  • Changing normal frames changes components, not the tensor itself.
Define the second fundamental form through a question students can test: if we differentiate a tangent field in the ambient manifold, how much of the answer points out of the submanifold. The answer is normal-valued, bilinear, and symmetric in the Riemannian torsion-free setting, so it behaves like a genuine tensor rather than a coordinate artifact.

Shape Operators Turn Normals into Tangent Endomorphisms

Weingarten map

Shape operator
\(A_\xi:T_xM\to T_xM\)

For each normal vector \(\xi\), A_\xi\) records how the normal field changes along tangent directions.

Duality with alpha
\(g(A_\xi X,Y)=g(\alpha(X,Y),\xi)\)

Special gain for hypersurfaces

A single unit normal field makes A a self-adjoint operator with real eigenvalues, the principal curvatures.

The shape operator is often easier for computations than the normal-valued second fundamental form. Fix a normal vector and the bending tensor becomes a self-adjoint tangent operator. For a hypersurface this single operator diagonalizes, and its eigenvalues become the principal curvatures used throughout the later rigidity discussion.

Gauss Equation: Curvature Has an Extrinsic Term

intrinsic curvature audited by bending

Compatibility identity
\(\langle \bar R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle=\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle+\langle\alpha(X,Z),\alpha(Y,W)\rangle-\langle\alpha(Y,Z),\alpha(X,W)\rangle\)

Reading the formula

  • The ambient curvature is compared with the curvature induced on M.
  • The difference is quadratic in the second fundamental form.
  • For Euclidean ambient space, intrinsic curvature can be entirely produced by bending.
Explain the Gauss equation as an accounting identity. If a tiny parallelogram is transported using ambient geometry and then viewed inside the submanifold, part of the discrepancy is intrinsic curvature and part is created by bending. In Euclidean ambient space, the ambient term disappears, making the quadratic alpha term especially visible.

Codazzi and Ricci Equations Check Consistency

differentiating the bending data

Codazzi

The covariant derivative of \(\alpha\) must have the right skew part.

\((D_X\alpha)(Y,Z)-(D_Y\alpha)(X,Z)\)

Ricci Normal Equation

The curvature of the normal connection is linked to ambient curvature and commutators of shape operators.

\(R^\perp\) sees \([A_\xi,A_\eta]\)
Principle

These equations are not optional consequences; they are the integrability tests that say local immersion data can fit together.

After the Gauss equation, students may think only curvature values matter. Codazzi and Ricci correct that impression. They say the bending tensor and the normal connection must vary coherently. In later existence theorems, these are precisely the checks that stop a formally plausible collection of tensors from being fake immersion data.

Hypersurfaces Package Bending into Principal Curvatures

codimension one

Principal curvatures
\(A e_i=\lambda_i e_i\)

Mean curvature

\(H={1\over n}\sum_i\lambda_i\)

Gauss-Kronecker

\(K_G=\prod_i\lambda_i\)

Orientation warning

Changing the unit normal changes signs of the \(\lambda_i\), but symmetric combinations can have orientation-independent meanings in the right dimensions.

When codimension is one, the machinery becomes much more concrete. One unit normal gives one shape operator, and self-adjointness lets us diagonalize it. Principal curvatures become the observable spectrum of bending, while mean curvature and Gauss-Kronecker curvature package that spectrum into scalar diagnostics.

Convexity Is Visible Through the Gauss Map

hypersurfaces in Euclidean space

Geometric tests

  • Definite second fundamental form: all principal curvatures have the same sign after choosing a normal.
  • Nonzero Gauss-Kronecker curvature: the Gauss map has nondegenerate differential.
  • Strict convexity connects local bending to a global supporting-plane picture.
Inspection phrase

The normal directions sweep the sphere without folding at points where the shape operator is nonsingular.

For convex hypersurfaces, the Gauss map becomes more than a local differential gadget. It records the direction of supporting hyperplanes. The chapter uses this world to connect definiteness, nonvanishing curvature, and global rigidity statements. Make clear that these are strong hypotheses, not generic properties of every hypersurface.

Type Number Measures How Much Bending Is Present

rigidity diagnostics

Type number

At a point, the type number is the rank-like dimension detected by the second fundamental form or shape operator data.

Rigidity intuition

  • If too few directions bend, an isometric deformation can hide inside the flat directions.
  • If enough directions bend, the Gauss and Codazzi equations leave less room for a second immersion.
  • The chapter's rigidity results turn this local rank information into global restrictions under hypotheses.
Frame type number as an information count. It asks how many tangent directions are genuinely seen by the extrinsic curvature data. Rigidity theorems use this count because a metric-preserving deformation has to preserve the compatibility equations; when the bending data has enough rank, the equations overdetermine possible alternatives.

The Fundamental Theorem Is an Integrability Statement

hypersurface reconstruction

Local reconstruction

A Riemannian metric and self-adjoint shape operator satisfying the Gauss and Codazzi equations locally determine a Euclidean hypersurface immersion, up to rigid motion.

Why the theorem is believable

  • The metric supplies intrinsic connection data.
  • The shape operator supplies normal change.
  • The compatibility equations are exactly the mixed partial checks for the moving frame system.
Do not present the fundamental theorem as magic reconstruction. It is an integrability theorem for a moving-frame differential system. The data g and A propose what the tangent and normal frames should do. Gauss and Codazzi say the proposed first-order system has consistent mixed derivatives, and uniqueness follows up to ambient Euclidean motions.

Totally Geodesic Means No Normal Acceleration

autoparallel submanifolds

Riemannian criterion
\(\alpha\equiv 0\)

All tangent accelerations stay tangent, so geodesics starting tangent to M remain in M.

Affine connection caveat

With torsion present, autoparallel and totally geodesic conditions must be compared carefully; in the torsion-free case they coincide in the expected way.

The last section of the chapter broadens the discussion from Riemannian submanifolds to affine connections. The clean mental model is still alpha equals zero: tangent geodesic acceleration has no normal component. Mention the torsion caveat because it explains why autoparallel and totally geodesic are stated separately before being identified under torsion-free hypotheses.

Worked Diagnostic: Paraboloid Versus Saddle

shape spectrum as a quick readout

Local checklist
  • Diagonalize A at the point.
  • Read principal curvature signs and multiplicities.
  • Use \(\det A\) to decide whether the Gauss map is locally singular.
  • Use Gauss equation to compare with intrinsic sectional curvature.
Surface in Euclidean three-space
\(K=\lambda_1\lambda_2\), \(H={1\over2}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)\)
Use the paraboloid and saddle as a diagnostic, not as a textbook example to memorize. At an elliptic point the principal curvatures share a sign; at a hyperbolic point they do not. The determinant of the shape operator then tells both Gaussian curvature and local behavior of the Gauss map.

Common Misreadings to Correct Early

lecture guardrails

Misreading 1

The second fundamental form is not just a coordinate matrix. Its components change with frames, but the normal-valued tensor is invariant.

Misreading 2

The Gauss map is not merely a picture of normals. In higher codimension it lands in a Grassmannian.

Misreading 3

Gauss-Codazzi equations are not after-the-fact formulas. They are integrability conditions for immersion data.

Projection discipline

Every ambient derivative should be split before interpretation: tangent part, normal part, and the connection used for each.

Hypothesis discipline

Rigidity, convexity, and fundamental theorem statements depend on rank, connectedness, torsion, and compatibility assumptions.

Use this as an intervention slide before moving to exercises or proofs. Most mistakes come from reading a component formula without asking which bundle it lives in. Require students to say tangent part, normal part, ambient connection, induced connection, and normal connection whenever they explain a calculation.
