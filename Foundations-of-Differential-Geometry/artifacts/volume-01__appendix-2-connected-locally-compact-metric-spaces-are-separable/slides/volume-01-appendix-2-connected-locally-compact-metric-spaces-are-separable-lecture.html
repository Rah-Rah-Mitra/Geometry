A Countability Engine for Metric Spaces

Appendix 2 proves that a connected locally compact metric space is separable. Source span: printed pages 269-271.

Lecture Thesis

Local compactness supplies countable local data; connectedness forces that data to propagate across the whole metric space.

Why It Matters

This small appendix powers countability arguments later used for metrizable manifolds, Riemannian metrics, and paracompactness.

Metric local data becomes global countability. 1 / 17
Open by positioning the appendix as a compact but important tool, not as a side calculation. Students should hear the main promise first: local compactness gives countable material near every point, and connectedness prevents those local countable regions from remaining isolated from one another.

Separable

dense set D countable
Locally Separable

Second Countable

Metric structure lets dense sets and bases talk to each other. 3 / 17
Pause here and ask students to separate local and global statements. Local separability does not yet give one countable dense set for the whole space. The proof must explain how local countable witnesses can be transported through the space without losing countability.
Proof Architecture

The Statement Is A Three-Lemma Relay

Lemma 1

compact metric space \( \Longrightarrow \) separable

Lemma 2

locally compact metric space \( \Longrightarrow \) locally separable

Lemma 3

connected locally separable metric space \( \Longrightarrow \) separable

The difficult step is Lemma 3, where local pieces become one global piece. 4 / 17
Give students the whole proof map before entering details. Lemma 1 is a standard compact metric fact, Lemma 2 is the local compactness conversion, and Lemma 3 is the real engine. The lecture will spend most of its time unpacking the third lemma.
Lemma 1 Intuition

Compact Metric Spaces Have Countable Nets

Finite At Each Scale

For radius \(1/n\), compactness gives finitely many balls covering the space.

Countable Across Scales

Choose one center from each ball at each scale. The union over \(n = 1,2,3,\ldots\) is countable and dense.

The source cites this standard fact; the lecture uses it as a tool. 5 / 17
Explain the compact metric input through finite nets rather than through a reference. At a fixed scale, compactness converts an open cover by equal-radius balls into a finite subcover. Taking all positive integer scales gives a countable family whose centers approximate every point.
Lemma 2 Intuition

Local Compactness Gives Local Separability

Local Compactness

near \(x\), choose a metric neighborhood with compact closure.

Apply Lemma 1

compact closure \( \Rightarrow \) countable dense set nearby.

Result

Every point has a separable neighborhood.

Local compactness is the bridge from topology to local countable data. 6 / 17
Make the local compactness step concrete. Around each point, choose a neighborhood whose closure is compact. The compact closure has a countable dense subset, and the smaller neighborhood inherits separability. Nothing global has happened yet; we have only built countable data locally.
Lemma 3 Setup

An R-Relation Measures Mutual Local Reach

Definition

Write \(x R y\) when each point lies inside a separable metric ball centered at the other.

  • Reflexive: every point reaches itself.
  • Symmetric: the condition is built in both directions.
  • Not assumed transitive: chains will handle propagation.
R is a one-step local compatibility relation. 7 / 17
Define the relation slowly because the rest of the proof depends on it. The point is not that R is an equivalence relation at once. It only records whether two points can see one another through separable balls, and longer chains will supply the missing transitivity.
Reachability Operator

S(A) Adds One Layer Of R-Neighbors

Operator

\(S(A) = \{y \in M : y R x \text{ for some } x \in A\}\)

Iteration

Starting from one point, repeatedly apply S to collect points reachable in finitely many local steps.

The proof studies what S does to openness and separability. 8 / 17
Introduce S as an operator rather than as notation to memorize. Given a set A, S gathers all points that are R-related to something already in A. The proof then asks two operational questions: does S preserve separability, and does it produce open pieces?
Statement (a)

Each Sx Is Open By A Triangle-Inequality Cushion

Move

If \(y \in Sx\), choose a small ball around \(y\) that stays inside the two separable balls used by \(x R y\).

  • Nearby z still lies in the separable ball around x.
  • The ball around z sits inside a separable ball around y.
  • Therefore z R x, so z remains in Sx.
Openness is obtained by leaving margin in the metric inequalities. 9 / 17
Walk through the cushion idea geometrically. Once y is related to x, the separable balls have spare radius around the containment statements. Choose a smaller ball around y using that spare radius. Every nearby z then remains related to x, so Sx contains a neighborhood of y.
Statement (b)

S Sends Separable Sets To Separable Sets

Countable Data

Use a countable dense subset \(D \subset A\) and rational radii.

Key Cover

Every point in S(A) lies in one separable ball centered at a point of D with rational radius.

Why Countable

Countably many centers times countably many rational radii remains countable.

This is where rational radii prevent an uncountable indexing problem. 10 / 17
Emphasize the bookkeeping. If A has a countable dense set D, then every one-step point can be caught by a separable ball whose center is moved to D and whose radius is chosen rationally. The cover is countable, and a countable union of separable metric subspaces is separable here.
Iterating S

The Reachable Class U(x) Is Countably Built

Finite Chains

\(S^n x\) is the set reachable from x in n local R-steps.

Reachable Class

\(U(x) = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} S^n x\)

Countability

Statement (b) makes each layer separable, and countably many layers remain separable.

U(x) is the local-reachability component of x. 11 / 17
Treat U(x) as a component built from finite local moves. The base point is separable as a one-point set, statement (b) propagates separability to the next layer, and induction handles all finite layers. The final union is countable over n, so U(x) stays separable.
Statement (c)

Reachable Classes Are Either Disjoint Or Equal

Overlap Test

If \(z \in U(x) \cap U(y)\), then finite chains connect x to z and z to y.

Concatenate Chains

Every finite chain from x can be extended through z to reach y, and conversely.

One shared point collapses two reachable classes into one. 12 / 17
Explain this as the familiar component argument, but with R-chains replacing paths. If U(x) and U(y) share z, then x reaches z in finitely many steps and z reaches y in finitely many steps. Chaining these moves proves every reachable layer for one base point sits inside the other.
Connectedness

Connectedness Leaves Only One Reachable Class

Open Classes

Statement (a) makes every \(U(x)\) open.

Disjoint Or Equal

Statement (c) prevents partial overlaps.

Conclusion

A connected M cannot be a union of multiple disjoint nonempty open reachable classes, so M equals U(x).

Since U(x) is separable, M is separable. 13 / 17
This is the global punch line. The sets U(x) are open, separable, and arranged like equivalence classes: two of them either agree or do not meet. A connected space cannot split into several such open pieces, so the one containing the chosen point is the whole space.
Hypothesis Stress Tests

Which Assumptions Are Doing Work?

Drop Connectedness

An uncountable discrete metric space is locally compact and locally separable, but no countable subset is dense.

Drop Local Compactness

Local separable neighborhoods are no longer automatic; the proof has no first local countable witness.

Drop Metric Control

The triangle-inequality cushions and rational-radius catalogue are no longer available in this form.

The theorem is not only a positive result; it diagnoses the proof's required machinery. 14 / 17
Use the examples as diagnostic tests rather than as detours. Connectedness prevents many isolated countable pieces from staying separate. Local compactness supplies local separability through compact metric neighborhoods. The metric itself gives radii, rational approximations, and triangle inequalities used throughout the proof.
Back To Differential Geometry

The Lemma Powers The Manifold Equivalence Theorem

Appendix Contribution

On a connected manifold, a metric space structure is locally compact in charts; Appendix 2 then gives separability.

Metric Fact

For metric spaces, separability is equivalent to the second axiom of countability.

The appendix is a small hinge in a larger equivalence theorem. 15 / 17
Tie the appendix back to the theorem at the end of the source span. Once a connected differentiable manifold is metrizable, its local Euclidean structure supplies local compactness. The appendix then gives separability, and in metric spaces separability is the same countability condition needed for the equivalence theorem.
Instructor Lab

Turn The Proof Into A Ball-Graph Experiment

Data

Vertices are points. Draw an edge when two points are R-related by verified separable balls.

Algorithm

Start at x, apply S by adding one edge-neighborhood, and record which layers are reached.

Check

If the graph has multiple open components, identify which hypothesis has been weakened.

Students can test the argument by changing the graph assumptions. 16 / 17
This lab makes the abstract proof inspectable. Give students a finite proxy graph whose edges stand for verified R-relations. They should compute the S-layers, decide whether all vertices fall into one reachable class, and then explain which topological hypothesis the finite model is mimicking.
