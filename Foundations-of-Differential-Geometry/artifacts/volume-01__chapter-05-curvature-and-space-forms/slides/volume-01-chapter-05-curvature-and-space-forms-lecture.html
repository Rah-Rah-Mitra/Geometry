Algebraic Curvature Starts Before Manifolds

Section 1

Definition Pattern

R(v1, v2, v3, v4) is multilinear, skew in the first pair, skew in the second pair, and satisfies the first Bianchi cyclic sum.

Consequence

Those three rules force pair symmetry: R(v1, v2, v3, v4) = R(v3, v4, v1, v2).

Instructor Cue

The point is not the proof trick; it is the reduction of possible four-linear data before any coordinates appear.

Stress that the chapter begins in a vector space because the curvature tensor at a point has algebraic life of its own. The skew rules and Bianchi identity are not decorative signs; they are filters that remove impossible components before geometry enters, making later sectional reconstruction believable.

Planes Determine The Tensor

Propositions 1.2-1.4

Reconstruction Principle

If two algebraic curvature tensors agree on R(v, w, v, w) for every pair v, w, then they agree everywhere.

Constant Plane Value

When every plane has K(P) = c, the tensor is c times the model tensor R1.

Why This Matters

Sectional curvature is a scalar interface for a tensor with many components. The interface is small, but the collection of all planes is not.

Present this as the chapter's first surprise. A single plane gives only one number, but the family of all planes is rich enough to reconstruct the algebraic tensor. This explains why the later definition of sectional curvature is not a weak substitute for R; it is a carefully chosen sampling device.

Sectional Curvature On A Riemannian Manifold

Section 2

At x in M

K(P) = g(R(X, Y)Y, X)

for an orthonormal basis X, Y of the two-plane P inside T_xM.

Independence

Rotating the orthonormal basis inside P does not change K(P), because the algebraic curvature symmetries absorb the rotation.

Recovery

At each point, the set of all values K(P) determines the Riemannian curvature tensor.

Connect the abstract section to the manifold by freezing the geometry at one point. The Riemannian metric lets us choose orthonormal vectors and turn R into a scalar. Students should leave with the habit that sectional curvature is not attached to a direction alone, but to a two-dimensional tangent plane.

Ricci Curvature Is A Sum Of Sectional Views

Proposition 1.5

Trace View

S(v, v) = K(span(v, e2)) + ... + K(span(v, en))

Interpretation

Ricci curvature averages the sectional curvatures of all coordinate planes that contain the same unit direction.

Teaching Move

When the class later meets volume comparison, this slide is the algebraic preface: direction-wise spreading is a trace of plane-wise bending.

Use Ricci curvature as a bridge rather than a detour. The chapter introduces the symmetric bilinear form obtained by tracing the curvature tensor, and the proposition says exactly what that trace means for a unit vector: add the sectional curvatures of all planes that contain it.

Constant Curvature Collapses R To One Function

Schur setup

Model Tensor

R(W, Z, X, Y) = k { g(W, X) g(Z, Y) - g(Z, X) g(Y, W) }

Input

Every two-plane at the same point reports the same scalar k(x).

Output

The full curvature tensor at that point is forced to be k(x) times the constant-curvature algebraic pattern.

Pause here because this formula is the hinge of the chapter. Algebraic Proposition 1.4 is being globalized: if the sectional curvature is independent of the plane at a point, then the tensor cannot hide any additional information. All remaining variation is the scalar function k on the manifold.

Schur's Theorem Turns Pointwise Isotropy Into Constancy

Theorem 2.2

Schur Principle

If dim M > 2, M is connected, and K(P) depends only on the base point, then k is constant.

Proof Roadmap

  • Write R = k R1.
  • Use that the metric is parallel.
  • Apply the second Bianchi identity.
  • Choose mutually orthogonal directions to force every derivative of k to vanish.
State the theorem carefully, including connectedness and the dimension restriction. The proof is a good example of differential geometry using room: in dimension at least three, one can choose enough orthogonal vectors for the Bianchi identity to isolate directional derivatives of k. That is why the local scalar becomes global.

Curvature Forms Record Constant Curvature Compactly

Proposition 2.4

On O(M)

Ωij = k θi ∧ θj

Reading The Formula

The curvature form sends an infinitesimal frame parallelogram to the signed area form, scaled by the common sectional curvature.

Connection To Earlier Chapters

The canonical form θ and connection form ω are still the active instruments; the chapter now says exactly what Ω looks like in space forms.

This slide ties the chapter back to the principal bundle language of earlier sections. In a space of constant curvature, the curvature form is not an arbitrary matrix of two-forms. Each entry is the wedge of canonical coframe components, multiplied by the same scalar k.

Constructing The Simply Connected Models

Theorem 3.1

Positive

K = 1/a^2, compact model, full isotropy.

Negative

K = -1/a^2, noncompact model, complete.

Zero

K = 0, trivial curvature form, flat connection.

Frame Theorem 3.1 as a construction theorem, not merely a catalog. For every constant curvature sign, the chapter builds a simply connected complete model. The proof uses quadrics, invariant quadratic forms, and the orthonormal frame bundle to compute the curvature form.

The Quadric Formula Unifies Sphere And Hyperbolic Space

Theorem 3.1

Ambient Equation

(x1)^2 + ... + (xn)^2 + r t^2 = r

Metric Restriction

g comes from dx1^2 + ... + dxn^2 + r dt^2 restricted to the hypersurface.

Sign Of r

Positive r gives a sphere after rescaling the last coordinate. Negative r gives two isometric hyperbolic components.

The quadric equation is the chapter's efficient model builder. Rather than memorize separate constructions, students can track the sign of r. Positive r produces the round sphere after a coordinate rescaling, while negative r produces hyperbolic components with a metric inherited from the indefinite ambient form.

Homogeneity Comes From Preserving A Quadratic Form

Space forms

Transitive Isometries

The linear group preserving the defining quadratic form acts transitively on the quadric model.

Why The Proof Uses Frames

The bundle map from the group to orthonormal frames lets the Maurer-Cartan equations compute the Riemannian connection and its curvature.

Consequence

These models are homogeneous; no point has special local curvature data.

Explain that homogeneity is not assumed after the models are written down; it follows from the group that preserves the quadratic form. The proof identifies this group with the orthonormal frame bundle in the model case, so the constant curvature calculation becomes a Maurer-Cartan computation.

Space Forms Are Sorted By The Sign Of K

Theorem 3.2

Elliptic

K > 0

Locally modeled by the round sphere; geodesics refocus and triangles carry excess angle.

Hyperbolic

K < 0

Locally modeled by hyperbolic space; geodesics spread and triangles carry angle deficit.

Flat

K = 0

Locally Euclidean; curvature vanishes but topology may still be visible through holonomy.

This is the classification language students should remember from the chapter's model section. Elliptic, hyperbolic, and flat are not just names for familiar pictures; they are sign classes for constant sectional curvature. Emphasize that the simply connected complete representatives are the local models for later space-form discussions.

Flat Affine Connections Have Three Equivalent Faces

Theorem 4.1

Local Trivialization

The affine frame bundle locally matches the canonical flat connection on a product U x A(n).

Tensor Vanishing

The torsion and curvature of the associated linear connection vanish identically.

Discrete Holonomy

The affine holonomy group is zero-dimensional, so local parallel displacement has no infinitesimal curvature.

Make clear that flat affine does not initially mean Riemannian. The theorem gives three equivalent tests: a local model connection, vanishing torsion and curvature, and discrete affine holonomy. This is a good place to recall that the affine frame bundle contains translational information beyond the linear frame bundle.

Flat Riemannian Examples Can Still Have Topology

Examples 4.1-4.4

Euclidean Cylinders

R^n modulo a discrete translation subgroup carries an induced flat Riemannian metric.

Affine But Not Riemannian

Some flat affine torus connections do not arise from a parallel Riemannian metric.

Lesson

Curvature can vanish locally while global quotient data remains geometrically meaningful.

Use the examples to separate local curvature from global topology. Euclidean cylinders and tori inherit flat metrics from Euclidean space, but the chapter also includes a flat affine torus that is not Riemannian. This distinction prepares students for the covering-space theorem that follows.

Complete Flat Affine Manifolds Lift To Ordinary Affine Space

Theorem 4.2

Universal Cover

For a complete flat affine connection, the affine holonomy bundle M* is the universal cover and is affine n-space.

Linear Holonomy Cover

The corresponding linear holonomy cover M' is a Euclidean cylinder.

Kernel

The first homotopy group of M' is the kernel of affine holonomy projected to linear holonomy.

The theorem is easiest to teach as a covering ladder. Completeness lets the universal flat affine model be ordinary affine space. Factoring by the translation kernel produces a Euclidean cylinder, while the remaining linear holonomy accounts for the passage down to the original manifold.

Compact Flat Riemannian Manifolds Have Torus Covers

Bieberbach direction

Compact Flat Criterion

M' is a Euclidean torus if and only if M is compact flat Riemannian.

Group Picture

Write M as R^n divided by a discrete group of Euclidean motions. Pure translations form the torus cover; the quotient linear part is finite.

Corollary

A flat affine manifold has a Euclidean torus cover exactly when it is compact flat Riemannian.

Keep the proof details at the level of architecture unless the course wants a group-theory digression. The compactness assumption forces enough translations to create a torus cover, and the remaining linear holonomy is finite. This is the chapter's compact flat Riemannian classification theme.

Two-Dimensional Flat Manifolds: Four Quotient Patterns

End of Section 4

Orientable

Ordinary cylinder and ordinary torus come from translation actions on the Euclidean plane.

Nonorientable

Twisted cylinder and Klein bottle add an orientation-reversing motion to the quotient pattern.

Reading The List

Every complete flat surface other than the plane is, up to scale, one of these quotient types.

Use the surface list as an accessible finish to a highly abstract chapter. The universal cover is the Euclidean plane, but the allowed deck transformations determine the surface type. Orientation-preserving translations produce cylinders and tori, while adding a flip produces the twisted cylinder and Klein bottle.
