Homogeneous Model

A Point Is A Coset, And Symmetry Moves It

Base point o = H

Definition Lens

A homogeneous space is modeled as M = K/H with K acting transitively and H fixing the origin.

Why The Origin Matters

  • Every point is k o for some k in K.
  • The isotropy group H records the symmetries that stay at o.
  • Invariant objects are determined by data compatible with H.
T_o M ≅ k / h
The lecture uses k, h, and m for Lie algebra data. Slide 3 / 18
Emphasize that the quotient notation is not decorative. It tells students where all local data can be stored: at the coset H, modulo the directions that only move inside the stabilizer. This is the reason later connection and metric conditions become algebraic compatibility tests.

Invariant G-Structures

Frames Carry The Symmetry Upstairs

P inside L(M)

Automorphism Test

A transformation preserves a G-structure when its lifted action maps the chosen frame subbundle P into itself.

Infinitesimal Version

The natural lift of a vector field is tangent to P exactly when the corresponding local symmetry preserves the structure.

frame u at o identifies T_oM with R^n
The frame viewpoint prepares Wang's correspondence for invariant connections. Slide 4 / 18
This slide connects the chapter to the principal bundle language from earlier chapters. Students should see that a G-structure is a controlled set of frames, not a new kind of point. Symmetry upstairs is the bookkeeping device that makes invariant connection data well-defined.

Invariant Affine Connections

Wang Data Replaces A Moving Connection Form

A: k -> g

A(X) = lambda(X), X in h
A(Ad(h)X) = Ad(lambda(h)) A(X)

Teaching Translation

The connection is invariant when one linear map at the origin already predicts all transported connection values.

The theorem turns invariant connection forms into linear algebra constrained by isotropy. Slide 5 / 18
Present Wang's correspondence as the main compression theorem for the first section. The map A is not arbitrary: on isotropy directions it must reproduce the isotropy representation, and under H it must transform equivariantly. These two rules are the price of encoding a global connection at one point.

Downstairs Interpretation

The Same Linear Map Controls Covariant Differentiation

A_X = L_X - nabla_X

A_X = L_X - nabla_X

What It Computes

At the origin, the algebraic map A recovers the infinitesimal correction between symmetry motion and covariant differentiation.

Torsion And Curvature

Once A is fixed, torsion and curvature can be written using brackets and the chosen connection data rather than by differentiating in every chart.

This slide paraphrases the covariant-differentiation form of the correspondence. Slide 6 / 18
The goal here is to prevent A from feeling like an abstract artifact. It tells how a symmetry-generated vector field differs from the covariant derivative chosen by the connection. This difference becomes the practical bridge to torsion, curvature, and later holonomy calculations.

Reductive Homogeneous Spaces

A Complement m Becomes The Model Tangent Space

k = h + m

Reductive Condition

Choose an Ad(H)-invariant decomposition k = h + m. The subspace m represents T_oM in a way that H respects.

Why This Is Useful

  • Connection data restricts to a map A_m: m -> g.
  • Brackets split into h-parts and m-parts.
  • Canonical and natural connections can be compared cleanly.
Reductivity is the chapter's gateway from quotient geometry to formulas. Slide 7 / 18
Spend time on the word reductive because it does much of the chapter's work. The complement m is not merely a vector-space convenience. Its invariance under isotropy means that algebra performed in m remains meaningful after changing the representative of the base frame.

Canonical Connection

The Zero m-Map Choice Exposes Bracket Geometry

A_m = 0

T(X,Y) = -[X,Y]_m
R(X,Y) = -lambda([X,Y]_h)

Diagnostic Point

The canonical connection is simple because the complement m supplies horizontal directions. Its torsion and curvature reveal how m fails to close under the Lie bracket.

Formula presentation is original and condensed for lecture use. Slide 8 / 18
Connect the picture to the formulas slowly. In the canonical connection, m is declared horizontal, so the bracket of two m-directions is the experiment. The m-component contributes torsion, while the h-component rotates frames through the isotropy representation and contributes curvature.

H-compatible

Parallel Test

For the canonical connection on a reductive space, invariant tensor fields are parallel.

This is a practical reading of the invariant tensor result in the reductive section. Slide 9 / 18
Make the classroom payoff explicit. If a tensor is invariant, students do not need to check it point by point. They can inspect its value at the origin and the isotropy action. The canonical connection then upgrades this invariance into parallelism for reductive homogeneous spaces.

Natural Torsion-Free Connection

A Symmetric Correction Keeps The Canonical Geodesies

same unparameterized paths

Existence Statement

Every reductive homogeneous space admits a unique K-invariant torsion-free affine connection with the same geodesies as the canonical connection.

A_m(X)Y = 1/2 [X,Y]_m + symmetric correction

Lecture Warning

Canonical, natural torsion-free, and metric Levi-Civita connections coincide only under additional hypotheses.

The distinction is essential before introducing invariant metrics. Slide 10 / 18
This is a good place to slow down because names can mislead. The canonical connection is not automatically torsion-free. The natural torsion-free connection is chosen to preserve the canonical geodesic family, while later metric compatibility may impose a different or coincident choice.

Invariant Metrics

A Metric Is A Bilinear Form That Survives Isotropy

B on m

Metric Correspondence

K-invariant indefinite Riemannian metrics on K/H correspond to non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms fixed by isotropy.

B(Ad(h)X, Ad(h)Y) = B(X,Y)

Positive Definite Case

When B is positive definite, the resulting invariant metric is Riemannian rather than indefinite.

This slide uses the reductive identification T_oM = m. Slide 11 / 18
The invariant metric result should feel parallel to the invariant tensor result. A metric is determined by one bilinear form at the origin, but that form must be insensitive to the isotropy action. This provides a concrete algebraic test for whether a proposed metric descends to K/H.

Metric Connection

Levi-Civita Data Adds A Symmetric Correction

Nomizu formula

nabla_X Y = 1/2 [X,Y]_m + U(X,Y)
2B(U(X,Y),Z) = B(X,[Z,Y]_m) + B([Z,X]_m,Y)

When U Vanishes

Natural reductivity forces the symmetric correction to disappear, so geodesic and curvature formulas simplify sharply.

The formula is included as a lecture-safe summary rather than a copied proof. Slide 12 / 18
Use this slide to show how the Riemannian connection is still algebraic on a reductive homogeneous space. The bracket gives the skew part, but metric compatibility and zero torsion require the symmetric term U. Naturally reductive spaces are special because this extra correction vanishes.

Naturally Reductive Geometry

Curvature Simplifies When The Metric And Bracket Cooperate

geodesic orbit intuition

Natural Reductivity Test

The metric pairing makes certain bracket terms skew in the right way, so one-parameter group orbits behave like geodesics.

Examples To Mention

  • Compact groups with bi-invariant metrics.
  • Quotients whose Lie algebra carries an invariant inner product.
  • Settings where sectional curvature becomes nonnegative.
The slide links the metric section to examples and curvature consequences. Slide 13 / 18
This slide should give students a reason to care about the naturally reductive condition. It is not just a naming convention; it says the symmetry orbits are compatible with the Riemannian geodesic structure. That compatibility makes curvature and decomposition statements easier to read from Lie algebra data.

Holonomy Of Invariant Connections

Curvature Starts The Algebra, Stability Closes It

smallest stable subalgebra

Computational Reading

The holonomy algebra is the smallest subalgebra containing all curvature values and stable under brackets with connection data.

R(X,Y) in h*, and [A(Z), h*] inside h*

Canonical Reductive Case

For the canonical connection, curvature seeds are built from the h-component of [X,Y] for X and Y in m.

The closure diagram is an original visual summary of the holonomy section. Slide 14 / 18
Frame holonomy as an algorithm. Curvature supplies the first infinitesimal rotations, but those rotations must be closed under the connection's own infinitesimal transport. The theorem's smallest subalgebra language is exactly this iterative closure process, specialized to invariant data.

Normality

A Normal Invariant Connection Makes The Holonomy Bundle Visible

P(u) as reachable geometry

Holonomy Bundle

P(u) is the set of frames reachable from u by horizontal lifting of curves.

Normality Reading

An invariant connection is normal when K acts transitively on this reachable bundle.

Why It Matters

Parallel tensor fields can be tested through K-invariance when the holonomy bundle has enough symmetry.

Normality links invariant geometry to the reduction theorem and parallel tensors. Slide 15 / 18
Use this picture to make the holonomy bundle less mysterious. It is not chosen by hand; it is the subset of frames that horizontal motion can actually reach. Normality says the symmetry group K still sees this smaller bundle transitively, so invariant and parallel behavior become tightly linked.

de Rham And Irreducibility

Homogeneity Organizes The Product Factors

simply connected metric case

Homogeneous de Rham Reading

For simply connected homogeneous spaces with invariant Riemannian metrics, the product decomposition can be realized by homogeneous factors.

Natural Reductive Strengthening

Kostant's result relates irreducibility of naturally reductive spaces to ideals and holonomy behavior in the Lie algebra.

T_oM = T_0 + T_1 + ... + T_r
The deck keeps the decomposition theorem as a structural checkpoint, not a proof transcript. Slide 16 / 18
The de Rham section answers a structural question. If the metric geometry splits into factors, homogeneity should not disappear from the pieces. Students should connect this with holonomy: invariant subspaces and irreducible factors are detected by how the holonomy and isotropy actions preserve tangent decompositions.

Invariant Almost Complex Structures

Integrability Becomes An Algebraic Bracket Test

J on k/h or on m

J^2 = -I
J commutes with isotropy action

Reductive Version

On a reductive K/H, an invariant almost complex structure is a linear endomorphism of m satisfying the isotropy and square-minus-identity tests.

Integrability

The vanishing of the torsion obstruction is checked by a bracket identity among X, Y, JX, and JY.

The final source section ties homogeneous spaces back to complex manifolds. Slide 17 / 18
Close the mathematical arc by showing that complex geometry also follows the same pattern. An invariant almost complex structure is first a linear operator at the origin that squares to minus identity and respects isotropy. Integrability then asks whether the Lie bracket obstruction vanishes.
