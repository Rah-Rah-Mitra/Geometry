Foundations of Differential Geometry II

Characteristic Classes

Chern-Weil forms turn curvature into cohomology classes that remember bundles after the connection has been forgotten.

Chapter 12, printed pages 293-320 Canonical HTML lecture deck
Open by framing characteristic classes as a memory device for bundles. The connection is deliberately visible on the title slide, but the promised invariant is what survives after changing that connection. Emphasize that the lecture studies a machine, not a list of names: curvature enters, invariant polynomials process it, and cohomology records the output.

The Chapter's Machine

Reading map

BundleA principal or vector bundle carries local twisting data over the base.
ConnectionA choice of horizontal directions turns twisting into differential forms.
CurvatureThe curvature form measures the failure of horizontal motion to close.
PolynomialAn Ad-invariant polynomial eats curvature without seeing the chosen frame.
ClassThe resulting closed form defines a cohomology class of the bundle.

\( f \in I^k(G), \quad \Omega \in \Omega^2(P,\mathfrak g) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad f(\Omega,\ldots,\Omega) \)

The same architecture later specializes to Chern classes, Pontrjagin classes, Euler classes, and the Chern character.

Use this slide as the agenda for the whole session. Each later construction is a refinement of one arrow in the machine. Ask students to keep track of which choices are temporary, especially the connection, and which outputs are designed to be intrinsic, especially the final cohomology class.

Connections Supply the Input Form

Principal bundle geometry

A connection form \(\omega\) splits tangent vectors on \(P\) into horizontal and vertical parts.

\(\Omega = d\omega + \tfrac12[\omega,\omega]\)

Instructor Cue

  • Horizontal motion is a choice.
  • Curvature is the obstruction exposed by that choice.
  • Characteristic classes are built from curvature but must outlive the choice.
Pause here to separate geometric data from topological output. A connection is not unique, and that is a feature rather than a defect. Its curvature gives concrete differential forms that can be calculated, while the eventual class must be stable under changing the horizontal distribution.

Invariant Polynomials Remove the Gauge

Algebraic filter

An invariant polynomial satisfies \(f(\operatorname{Ad}_g X_1,\ldots,\operatorname{Ad}_g X_k)=f(X_1,\ldots,X_k)\).

Why This Matters

The curvature form changes by conjugation when frames change. The polynomial must be blind to that conjugation, or the expression will not descend from the bundle to the base.

If \(f\) sees the frame, \(f(\Omega)\) is not a characteristic form. It is just a gauge-dependent calculation.

This is the algebraic heart of the chapter. Make the gauge transformation visible as movement around an adjoint orbit. Then make the invariant polynomial a measuring device whose reading does not change along that orbit. That single requirement explains why traces, determinants, elementary symmetric functions, and Pfaffians appear naturally.

The Weil Homomorphism

Chern-Weil theorem

\( w_P : I(G) \longrightarrow H^\ast(M;\mathbb R), \qquad f \longmapsto [f(\Omega)] \)

What Must Be True

  • The form \(f(\Omega)\) is horizontal and \(G\)-invariant, hence basic.
  • The Bianchi identity forces it to be closed.
  • A path of connections changes it by an exact form.
Present the theorem as a three-part guarantee: descent to the base, closedness, and independence from connection. The proof in the chapter is technical, but the conceptual burden is modest. Invariance handles frame changes, Bianchi handles closedness, and the homotopy of connections handles independence.

Proof Roadmap, Not Proof Fog

Three checks

Basicness

Descend From \(P\) to \(M\)

Horizontal behavior and equivariance ensure the constructed form is not trapped upstairs on the principal bundle.

\(f(\Omega)\) is horizontal and invariant.

Closedness

Bianchi Kills the Derivative

The exterior covariant derivative of curvature vanishes, and the invariant polynomial packages that fact into an ordinary closed form.

\(D\Omega = 0 \Rightarrow d f(\Omega)=0\)

Independence

Change Connection, Same Class

A one-parameter family of connections produces a transgression form; the difference of endpoints is exact.

\(f(\Omega_1)-f(\Omega_0)=dT_f\)

This slide is meant to prevent students from drowning in indices. The detailed lemmas in the source support these three checks. Have the class name which hypothesis is used at each stage: polynomial invariance for basicness, Bianchi for closedness, and a path of connections for independence.

Chern Classes From Complex Curvature

Complex vector bundles

\( c(E)=\det\!\left(I+\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega\right) = 1+c_1+c_2+\cdots \)

Instructor Emphasis

For a rank \(r\) complex vector bundle, the degree \(2k\) component is represented by the \(k\)-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the curvature roots.

For a line bundle, the whole story begins with one form: \(c_1(L)=[\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega]\).

Introduce Chern roots as a teaching fiction that organizes the formulas without pretending the bundle globally splits. The determinant expression is the invariant polynomial package. In examples, students should first understand line bundles, then treat higher rank as elementary symmetric bookkeeping in formal roots.

The Chern Character Linearizes Products

Trace exponential

\( \operatorname{ch}(E)=\operatorname{tr}\exp\!\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega\right) \)

With formal roots \(x_j\): \(\operatorname{ch}(E)=\sum_j e^{x_j}\)

Teaching Payoff

  • Direct sum becomes addition.
  • Tensor product becomes multiplication.
  • Rational cohomology calculations become cleaner.
The Chern character is a good place to remind students that characteristic classes are not all equally convenient. Chern classes are integral and geometrically fundamental, while the character sacrifices some arithmetic information to gain algebraic simplicity. Instructors can use this contrast to motivate later index-theoretic appearances.

Pontrjagin Classes Detect Real Twisting

Even-degree invariants

\( p_k(E)=(-1)^k c_{2k}(E\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C) \)

Why Even Powers Appear

For orthogonal structure groups, curvature eigenvalues occur in sign-paired imaginary directions. The invariant polynomials that survive are symmetric in the squares of the roots.

\(p(E)=\prod_j (1+x_j^2)\)

Explain Pontrjagin classes as the real-bundle counterpart of the Chern story, but do not reduce them to a mere afterthought. The sign-pairing picture tells students why degrees jump by four and why complexification is a natural bridge from real curvature to familiar Chern formulas.

Euler Class Is the Oriented Top Test

Pfaffian representative

\( e(E)=\left[\operatorname{Pf}\!\left(\frac{\Omega}{2\pi}\right)\right] \in H^{2m}(M;\mathbb R) \)

Conditions to Say Out Loud

  • The real bundle must be oriented.
  • The rank must be even for a top-degree Pfaffian formula.
  • The connection is chosen compatible with the fibre metric.

Changing orientation flips the Euler class sign; Chern and Pontrjagin classes do not carry that same sign choice.

Treat the Euler class as the most orientation-sensitive member of the trio. The Pfaffian is the correct invariant polynomial for skew-symmetric curvature matrices in even rank. Be explicit that metric compatibility is part of the representative formula, even though the resulting class is topological.

Naturality and Whitney Formulas

How classes behave

\(c(f^\ast E)=f^\ast c(E), \qquad p(f^\ast E)=f^\ast p(E)\)

\(c(E\oplus F)=c(E)c(F)\)

\(p(E\oplus F)=p(E)p(F)\)

Reading Strategy

These formulas are not decorative identities. They are the operational rules that let characteristic classes classify examples by reducing complicated bundles to smaller pieces.

After the formulas are introduced, students need behavior rules. Naturality says the construction commutes with pulling bundles back along maps. Whitney formulas say direct sums multiply total classes. These two rules turn the theory from a definition into a calculation system for examples.

A Micro-Model: Line Bundle Curvature

Concrete calculation

\(c_1(L)=\left[\frac{i}{2\pi}\Omega\right]\)

\(\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} c_1(L)=1\)

What the Number Means

The integral is a global twisting count. It is computed from a local curvature form, but it cannot be erased by changing the connection.

This model keeps the calculation honest without copying any textbook example. The line bundle case is where students first see curvature become a number after integration. Stress that the representative form changes under connection changes, but its cohomology class and normalized integral remain the same.

Common Failure Modes

Diagnostic slide

Gauge-Dependent Polynomial

A polynomial that is not Ad-invariant may produce a form, but it will not descend to the base as a characteristic form.

Forgotten Orientation

The Euler class needs an orientation. Without it, the Pfaffian sign is not a globally consistent datum.

Flat Connection Shortcut

Vanishing curvature kills Chern-Weil representatives, but hypotheses decide which Euler conclusion is legitimate.

Use this as a mid-lecture assessment prompt. Ask students to identify which part of the machine fails in each panel. The right answer should name a mechanism, not only a class family: descent fails without invariance, Euler sign fails without orientation, and flatness needs care when translating representatives to topological conclusions.

Lab Board: Chern Roots as Data

Instructor exercise

Assume a rank-three complex bundle has formal roots \(x_1,x_2,x_3\). Compute \(c_1,c_2,c_3\), then predict what survives after replacing each root by its negative.

Chern

\(c_1=x_1+x_2+x_3\)

Second class

\(c_2=x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3\)

Top class

\(c_3=x_1x_2x_3\)

Pontrjagin shadow

even expressions in the roots resist sign changes.

This exercise is intentionally algebraic and short. It lets the instructor check whether students understand why roots are useful and why sign changes matter for real bundles. Do not let the exercise become a formal splitting principle lecture; keep the focus on invariant polynomial bookkeeping.

How to Read a Chern-Weil Formula

Four-line checklist

1. Identify the structure group and the Lie algebra where curvature lives.

2. Name the invariant polynomial before evaluating it.

3. Normalize constants consistently, especially \(2\pi\) and \(i\).

4. Separate the representative form from the cohomology class.

This is the slide students can use when studying independently. Many errors in characteristic class computations come from mixing layers: a group is confused with a bundle, a representative with a class, or a normalization convention with a theorem. The checklist gives a disciplined reading order.

Closing Synthesis

What should remain

Characteristic classes are cohomological shadows cast by curvature through invariant polynomials.

The connection computes; the polynomial descends; the cohomology class remembers.

After-Class Prompts

  • Explain why the polynomial must be invariant.
  • Compare the degree patterns of \(c_k\), \(p_k\), and \(e\).
  • Give one example where changing the connection changes the form but not the class.
End by returning to the machine from the first slide. Students should leave with one coherent sentence rather than several disconnected definitions. The best final answer is that curvature becomes topological information only after an invariant polynomial removes gauge dependence and cohomology removes connection dependence.
