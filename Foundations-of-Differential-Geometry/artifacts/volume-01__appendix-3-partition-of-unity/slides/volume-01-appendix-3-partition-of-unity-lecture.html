Partition Of Unity

A controlled smooth weighting system that turns local constructions into global differential-geometric objects.

local finiteness supports normalization extension
Lecture Problem

Given local data on a paracompact manifold, build a global smooth object without asking the local formulas to agree first.

Open by framing the appendix as a compact tool that makes global differential geometry possible. The source span is only printed pages 272 through 274, but it supplies the mechanism used elsewhere to build metrics, connections, and extensions from local ingredients.
Motivation
Local formulas need global glue

The Problem It Solves

Local Ease

Charts and trivializations give formulas on neighborhoods:

local metrics, local sections, local forms, local extensions.

Global Demand

A manifold-wide object must be smooth at every point and must be defined by a single rule, not by uncoordinated patches.

Partition Strategy

Weight each local answer by a smooth coefficient whose support stays inside the neighborhood where that answer makes sense.

Emphasize the mismatch that partitions of unity repair. Local formulas are easy because charts, trivializations, and closed-set extensions live in neighborhoods. Global objects must agree everywhere. The partition assigns compatible weights so the local answers can be averaged without losing smoothness.
Definition
Subordinate to an open cover

Subordinate Partition

Definition

For a locally finite open cover \(\{U_i\}_{i\in I}\) of \(M\), a partition of unity subordinate to it is a family of smooth functions \(\{\phi_i\}_{i\in I}\) satisfying

\[ 0 \le \phi_i \le 1,\quad \operatorname{supp}(\phi_i)\subset U_i,\quad \sum_i \phi_i(x)=1. \]

Three Checks

  • smooth, nonnegative coefficients
  • support controlled by the assigned open set
  • finite sum near each point, equal to one
State the definition as three separate checks students can run. Each weight is smooth and nonnegative, each weight lives inside its assigned open set by support containment, and local finiteness makes the sum at a point a genuinely finite sum equal to one.
Mechanism
Why infinite families behave locally like finite ones

Local Finiteness Is The Engine

Finite Near A Point

There is a neighborhood \(N_x\) such that only finitely many \(\phi_i\) are nonzero on \(N_x\).

Smoothness Consequence

\[ d\left(\sum_i \phi_i a_i\right) = \sum_{i\in F_x} d(\phi_i a_i) \]

near \(x\), with \(F_x\) finite.

Without It

Pointwise sums may exist but fail to define a smooth object that can be differentiated term by term.

Slow down on local finiteness because it is easy to treat as housekeeping. It is the analytic reason an indexed sum defines a smooth function. Near any point only finitely many terms survive, so differentiation passes through the sum without convergence arguments.
Lemma 1
Local bump inside a chosen neighborhood

Lemma 1: Make A Local Bump

Point Version

Given \(x\in M\) and an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(x\), choose a smooth \(b:M\to[0,1]\) with \(b(x)>0\) and \(\operatorname{supp}(b)\subset U\).

Coordinate Reduction

Work in a chart around \(x\), choose one-variable cutoffs in each coordinate, and multiply them to obtain a compactly supported local bump.

Role

This is the smallest smooth function that can both detect a point and stay out of places where a local formula is not valid.

Present the first lemma as the atomic move. Around a chosen point inside a chosen neighborhood, build a smooth function that is positive at the point and vanishes outside that neighborhood. Coordinates reduce the construction to a product of one-variable cutoff functions.
Lemma 2
From one point to a compact set

Lemma 2: Protect A Compact Set

Compact Version

If \(K\subset U\), with \(K\) compact and \(U\) open, then there is a smooth \(b\) such that \(b>0\) on \(K\) and \(b=0\) outside \(U\).

This lemma will create the raw functions that are later normalized.

The second lemma upgrades a point bump to a compact-set shield. Cover the compact set by finitely many positive neighborhoods, add the corresponding bumps, and obtain one smooth function that remains positive on the compact set while still vanishing outside the chosen larger neighborhood.
Lemma 3
A refinement with room for support

Lemma 3: Shrink The Cover

Shrinking Statement

For a locally finite open cover \(\{U_i\}\), choose a locally finite refinement \(\{V_i\}\) so that the closure of each \(V_i\) stays inside \(U_i\).

Geometric Meaning

The construction needs breathing room: positive on the smaller set, support inside the larger set, and no accidental contact with the boundary.

Notation For The Proof

\(V_i\subset W_i\subset \overline{W_i}\subset U_i\)

The third lemma supplies room for supports. Starting from a locally finite open cover, choose a locally finite refinement whose closed pieces sit inside the original open sets. This gap is what lets later functions be positive where needed and zero before they leak outside.
Theorem 1
Existence of subordinate partitions

The Existence Proof As A Pipeline

1. Shrink choose \(V_i\) and \(W_i\) with closure room inside \(U_i\)
2. Bump construct \(g_i\) positive on \(V_i\) and zero outside \(W_i\)
3. Sum form \(g=\sum_i g_i\), a smooth positive denominator
4. Normalize set \(\phi_i=g_i/g\), preserving supports and creating unity
Now assemble the three lemmas into the theorem. Shrink the cover, place an intermediate open neighborhood, use the compact-set bump lemma to build raw functions, add them into a positive smooth denominator, and normalize each raw function by that denominator.
Key Formula
The denominator is smooth and positive

Normalization Turns Weights Into Unity

Construction

\[ g(x)=\sum_i g_i(x) > 0,\qquad \phi_i(x)=\frac{g_i(x)}{g(x)}. \]

Why It Works

\[ \sum_i \phi_i(x) = \frac{\sum_i g_i(x)}{g(x)} =1. \]

Local finiteness keeps \(g\) smooth; positivity of the covering raw bumps keeps division legal.

The normalization step is the punchline. The raw functions already have the correct supports and cover every point positively, but their sum is not one. Dividing each raw function by the total preserves supports and forces the weights to add to one.
Support Control
Nothing is used outside its domain

Why Supports Stay Inside

Support Identity

If \(g_i=0\) outside \(W_i\), then \(\phi_i=g_i/g\) is also zero outside \(W_i\).

Subordination

\[ \operatorname{supp}(\phi_i)\subset \overline{W_i}\subset U_i. \]

Pedagogical Check

Ask: where is the local formula allowed to be evaluated? The support condition answers exactly that question.

Use the nested sets visually. The refined set marks where positivity is required, the intermediate set is where the raw function may live, and the original open set is the permitted support container. Local finiteness ensures this picture occurs only finitely often near each point.
Theorem 2
Closed-set smooth extension

Theorem 2: Extend From A Closed Set

Statement

If \(F\) is closed in a paracompact manifold \(M\), and a function \(f\) on \(F\) is differentiable in the local-extension sense, then \(f\) has a smooth extension to all of \(M\).

Softness Viewpoint

The sheaf of smooth functions on a paracompact manifold has enough global sections to extend closed-set data.

Shift from existence of weights to the first major application. A function defined on a closed subset is differentiable there when it has local smooth representatives. The theorem says paracompactness lets those local representatives be glued into a smooth function on the whole manifold.
Application Formula
Weighted local representatives

Extension Formula

Local Pieces

Choose \(g_i\) on \(U_i\): a representative of \(f\) when \(U_i\) touches \(F\), and zero for open sets lying away from \(F\).

Global Extension

\[ G=\sum_i \phi_i g_i. \]

Why \(G|_F=f\)

At points of \(F\), all active representatives agree with \(f\), and the partition weights sum to one.

Explain the formula as weighted consensus. On each small open set choose either a local representative near the closed subset or zero away from it. Multiplying by subordinate weights makes the expression local, and on the closed set all contributing representatives agree with the original function.
Differential Geometry
What the appendix enables elsewhere

Globalizing Differential-Geometric Data

Riemannian Metrics

Patch local positive definite inner products using nonnegative weights; positivity survives because the weights sum to one.

\(g=\sum_i \phi_i g_i\)

Fibre Metrics

Choose trivialized fibre inner products and blend them into a globally defined smooth fibre metric.

\(h=\sum_i \phi_i h_i\)

Connections And Forms

Local connection data and ordinary differential forms can be combined when the affine structure of the space of choices allows weighted sums.

\(\omega=\sum_i \phi_i \omega_i\)

Connect the appendix back to the course. The same gluing device builds Riemannian metrics from chartwise metrics, fibre metrics from trivializations, and connection forms from local choices. Partition weights convert local differential-geometric data into a coherent global object.
Worked Model
Two intervals on a line

A Concrete One-Dimensional Model

Blend

Let \(a_1\) be a local formula on \(U_1\) and \(a_2\) a local formula on \(U_2\). The global candidate is

\[ A=\phi_1 a_1+\phi_2 a_2. \]

Interpretation

Outside the overlap, one weight dominates. Inside the overlap, both formulas contribute smoothly.

This model captures the same logic used for forms, metrics, sections, and closed-set extensions.

Give students a small mental model before the abstract theorem fades. Two overlapping intervals carry two raw bumps. Their normalized versions cross-fade smoothly across the overlap, so a local left formula and a local right formula can be blended without a seam.
Warning
Where the construction can break

Common Failure Modes

  • Not locally finite: the visible formula may become an uncontrolled infinite sum.
  • Support not contained: a local object is evaluated where it was never defined.
  • No positive denominator: normalization by \(g=\sum_i g_i\) is impossible at some point.
  • Closed-set representatives disagree: extension formula cannot preserve the original value.
Practical Diagnostic

When a gluing proof fails, ask which of finiteness, support, positivity, or agreement has been lost.

Use the warning slide to separate necessary hypotheses from decorative assumptions. Without local finiteness, sums may stop being locally finite. Without support containment, local formulas may be used where they are undefined. Without positivity of the denominator, normalization breaks.

What To Carry Forward

Core Memory

A partition of unity is a smooth weighted voting system controlled by an open cover.

  • local finiteness makes the sums smooth
  • support containment keeps formulas inside their domains
  • normalization makes all weights add to one
  • paracompactness supplies the room needed for the construction
Close by identifying the portable lesson. A partition of unity is a smooth weighted voting system controlled by open sets. It turns local existence into global existence on paracompact manifolds, which is why it quietly appears behind many constructions in differential geometry.
