Appendix 4: Arcwise Connected Subgroups Of Lie Groups

A two-page theorem that turns path access inside a subgroup into a genuine Lie subgroup for holonomy and transformation arguments.

Source Span

Printed pages 275-276 prove the theorem through a tangent set S, a left-invariant distribution, and two inclusions H ⊂ K and K ⊂ H.

Lecture Aim

Make the proof usable: identify the path hypothesis, translate it into infinitesimal data, and see why a local product chart forces the subgroup to be smooth.

Motivation

Why This Appendix Appears After Partitions Of Unity

The Problem

A subgroup of a Lie group may be given as a set produced by geometry, not as a closed embedded matrix group with equations already available.

The Promise

If every element can be reached from the identity by piecewise differentiable motion staying in the subgroup, then the subgroup carries a Lie structure.

Later Use

Holonomy and transformation groups often arrive by composing geometric motions. This theorem converts those accessible motions into a smooth subgroup object.

Geometric subgroups often come from motion, not equationsPath access is the usable hypothesis
Frame the theorem as a repair tool for naturally occurring subgroups. In differential geometry, a subgroup may be generated by parallel transport or local transformations before anyone has written equations for it. The appendix gives a practical criterion: enough controlled paths from the identity force the subgroup to be smooth in the Lie-theoretic sense.
Theorem Input

The Hypothesis Is A Path Condition Inside H

Theorem Statement

Let G be a Lie group and H ≤ G a subgroup. If each h ∈ H is joined to e by a piecewise C1 curve contained in H, then H is a Lie subgroup.

What Is Not Assumed

No embedded equations, no closedness, and no prior smooth manifold structure on H are part of the input.

Input: subgroup plus path accessibilityOutput: a Lie subgroup structure
Slow down on the hypothesis because it is easy to overread. The theorem does not begin with a manifold H, and it does not require H to be closed in G. What it receives is a subgroup and a supply of paths lying completely inside that subgroup, one path for every element.
Infinitesimal Extraction

Build S From Curves Through The Identity

Definition Of S

S is the set of velocity vectors at e obtained from differentiable curves of class C1 that stay inside H.

Teaching Translation

Paths provide the missing smooth data. Instead of guessing equations for H, inspect every first-order direction that H visibly allows at the identity.

The proof starts at Te(G)All smooth structure is recovered from curve velocities
This slide converts a topological-sounding assumption into differential data. The set S is not declared to be a vector space at first; it is only the collection of tangent velocities realized by curves in H through the identity. The next proof steps are exactly the checks that S behaves like a Lie algebra.
Lemma, Part One

S Is Closed Under Scalars And Sums

Scalar Multiples

Reparameterize a curve x(t) as x(rt). Its initial velocity scales by r, so X ∈ S implies rX ∈ S.

Sums

Multiply two curves in the subgroup. The product curve remains in H, and its initial velocity is the sum of the two initial velocities.

Conclusion So Far

The curve calculus at the identity makes S a vector subspace before any Frobenius machinery appears.

Curve operations happen inside H because H is a subgroupFirst-order behavior is linear
Present this lemma as the first payoff from combining subgroup algebra with differentiability. Reparameterization supplies scalar multiples, while multiplying two H-valued curves supplies sums. The exact derivative formula is less important pedagogically than the source of the operations: they are legal because H is closed under group multiplication.
Lemma, Part Two

The Commutator Curve Produces Brackets

Bracket Witness

The group commutator made from curves with initial velocities X and Y has second-order drift governed by [X,Y].

Why It Stays In H

Products and inverses of elements of H remain in H. The commutator path therefore stays inside H whenever the original curves do.

Lemma Conclusion

S is closed under scalar multiplication, addition, and Lie brackets. Thus S is a Lie subalgebra of g.

Commutators turn group multiplication into Lie algebra bracketsS is a Lie subalgebra
Use the commutator picture to explain why Lie brackets are unavoidable, not decorative. The proof uses a standard curve construction whose leading nontrivial displacement records the bracket. Since the construction is built from multiplication and inversion, subgroup membership keeps it inside H and therefore puts the bracket back in S.
From Algebra To Geometry

Translate S Across The Lie Group

Distribution

Assign to each x ∈ G the subspace LxS ⊂ TxG, where Lx is left translation by x.

Involutive

Because S is closed under brackets, the associated left-invariant vector fields close under brackets.

Geometric Meaning

The allowed directions now form a smooth field of tangent subspaces across G, ready for Frobenius integration.

S becomes a left-invariant distributionBracket closure is the involutivity condition
Connect this step to the main body of differential geometry. A Lie subalgebra is not merely algebraic data; left translation spreads it into a smooth distribution on the whole group. Because brackets of left-invariant fields correspond to brackets in S, the distribution satisfies the exact closure condition needed for integral manifolds.
Frobenius Step

The Integral Manifold Through e Is K

Construct K

Let K be the maximal integral manifold through e for the distribution x ↦ LxS.

Lie Subgroup

The standard Lie subgroup theorem associates a connected subgroup K to the Lie subalgebra S.

What Remains

The source proof must now identify the original set H with the constructed smooth object K.

Constructed object: connected Lie subgroup KRemaining task: prove H = K
Students should see K as the smooth candidate forced by S. The theorem has now manufactured a connected Lie subgroup from the tangent directions that H visibly allows. The rest of the proof is not abstract existence; it is a comparison argument showing that the manufactured K and the original subgroup H are the same subset.
First Inclusion

Every H-Path Is Tangent To The K Distribution

Take Any a In H

By hypothesis, choose a piecewise C1 curve x(t) in H with x(0)=e and x(1)=a.

Velocity Check

Left-translate the velocity at x(t) back to the identity. The resulting tangent vector is realized by an H-curve, so it lies in S.

Therefore

The whole curve is an integral curve of x ↦ LxS starting at e. It stays in K, so H ⊂ K.

Path hypothesis proves H is inside the constructed leafFirst inclusion: H subset K
For the first inclusion, ask students to track one point a of H rather than the whole subgroup at once. The chosen path to a has each velocity in the translated subspace LxS after left translation back to the identity. Therefore it cannot leave the maximal integral manifold K through e.
Second Inclusion, Local Step

A Product Chart Places A Neighborhood Of e Inside H

Choose Basis Curves

Let e1,...,ek be a basis for S. For each basis vector, choose an H-curve whose initial velocity is that vector.

Product Map

Define a local map by multiplying those curves with parameters t1,...,tk. Its differential at the origin sends coordinate vectors to the basis of S.

Inverse Function Theorem

After shrinking the parameter box, this product map is a diffeomorphism onto an open neighborhood in K, and its image lies in H.

The inverse function theorem enters at the identityA neighborhood of e in K lies inside H
The second inclusion starts locally. Since S has a basis realized by H-curves, multiplying those curves gives a coordinate map into H. Its derivative at the origin is nonsingular because the selected velocities form a basis. Thus a small open neighborhood of the identity in K is already contained in H.
Second Inclusion, Global Step

Connectedness Spreads The Local Neighborhood

Local Input

There is an open neighborhood U of e in K with U ⊂ H.

Subgroup Propagation

Because H is a subgroup, products of elements from this neighborhood remain in H.

Connected K

A connected Lie group is generated by any neighborhood of its identity. Hence every point of K belongs to H.

Second inclusion: K subset HCombined with H subset K, the proof is complete
Now make the final argument feel inevitable. A small identity neighborhood of K lies inside H, and H is a subgroup, so products of small pieces remain in H. Since K is connected, those small pieces generate all of K. This changes a local inverse-function argument into the global inclusion K subset H.

Example

An Arcwise Connected Subgroup Need Not Be Closed

Model

In T2, the image of t ↦ (exp(it), exp(i alpha t)) with irrational alpha is a subgroup reached by one continuous parameter.

Lesson

The theorem is about Lie subgroup structure, not about closedness as a subset. Dense immersed examples explain why closed-subgroup intuition is too narrow.

Arcwise connected does not mean closedThe smooth structure may be immersed rather than closed embedded
The torus example keeps students from silently replacing the theorem with the closed subgroup theorem. A one-parameter irrational winding is path accessible from the identity and carries a one-dimensional Lie group structure, yet its image is dense in the torus. This illustrates why the appendix emphasizes paths and tangent directions rather than closed equations.
Scope

What The Theorem Does And Does Not Say

It Does Say
  • Path access to every element gives enough tangent directions.
  • Those directions assemble into a Lie algebra.
  • The original subgroup equals the integral subgroup generated by that algebra.
It Does Not Say
  • The subgroup must be closed in the ambient group.
  • The subset topology is automatically the final classroom picture.
  • Every connected-looking subgroup has the required differentiable paths.
Best Reading Habit

Whenever a geometric construction produces a subgroup, ask whether its elements can be connected to the identity by controlled curves inside the subgroup.

The path hypothesis is doing real workClosedness belongs to a different theorem
This is a misconception-control slide. Students often conflate several subgroup theorems: closed subgroups, immersed subgroups, and connected generated subgroups. Here the key assumption is differentiable path access inside H. The proof never asks H to be closed; it asks H to reveal enough first-order directions at the identity.
Use In The Course

How This Lemma Supports Holonomy Arguments

Holonomy Pattern

Parallel transport around loops produces transformations in a structure group. Composing loops produces a subgroup of that Lie group.

Path To Identity

Loops that deform to the constant loop often give families of transformations that move continuously back to the identity element.

Appendix 4 Payoff

Once these transformations are joined to identity by admissible curves, the holonomy component becomes a Lie subgroup and its Lie algebra can be studied.

Holonomy groups are geometric sources of subgroupsAppendix 4 makes smooth structure available
Tie the appendix back to the main narrative of the book. Holonomy elements arise from parallel transport around loops, so the group is initially generated by geometric procedures. When loop families connect transformations back to identity, Appendix 4 supplies the Lie subgroup conclusion needed before discussing infinitesimal holonomy and Lie algebras.
Instructor Lab

A Reusable Diagnostic For New Subgroups

Classroom Task

Given a subgroup H ≤ G produced by geometry, decide whether Appendix 4 can be applied without first finding equations for H.

  1. Find paths inside H from e to representative elements.
  2. Collect initial velocities at e to form candidate S.
  3. Check whether sums and brackets are explained by legal curve operations.
  4. Use basis curves and a product chart near the identity.
Diagnostic mirrors the proofUseful for holonomy, transformation, and generated subgroup examples
This lab turns the proof into a usable workflow. Students should not memorize Appendix 4 as a black box; they should learn to test a new subgroup by finding paths, reading tangent velocities, checking bracket closure, and building a local product chart. That workflow is what later chapters actually reuse.
Common Mistakes

Three Ways To Misread The Appendix

Mistake 1

Assuming topological connectedness alone provides the differentiable paths required by the theorem.

Mistake 2

Thinking the proof starts with equations for H. It starts with curves and extracts equations indirectly through S.

Mistake 3

Replacing the final connected-generation step with a vague appeal to intuition. The subgroup property and connectedness of K are both essential.

Correct Mental Model

Path access creates tangent directions; tangent directions create a Lie algebra; the Lie algebra creates K; comparison proves H=K.

Quick Test

Can the student explain why a velocity of an H-path at x lies in LxS? If yes, the first inclusion is understood.

Recap

What Appendix 4 Gives The Rest Of The Book

A Criterion

A subgroup with piecewise differentiable paths from identity to every element is a Lie subgroup.

A Proof Pattern

Extract S, prove it is a Lie algebra, integrate it to K, and then show H=K.

A Geometric Use

Holonomy and transformation groups can be treated as smooth Lie subgroups once their elements are connected to identity by admissible geometric motions.
