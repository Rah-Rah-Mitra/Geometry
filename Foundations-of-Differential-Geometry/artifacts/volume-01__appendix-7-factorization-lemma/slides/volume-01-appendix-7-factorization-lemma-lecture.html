Factorization Lemma: Local Loops from a Null-Homotopy

A proof mechanism for replacing one global contraction by finitely many loops that live inside chosen coordinate or covering neighborhoods.

The lecture question: how does a square contraction factor into local lassos?

Core Result

A closed curve that contracts to the base point can be rewritten, up to elementary backtrack equivalence, as a finite product of loops that each live inside one open set after conjugation by a path.

Motivation

Why a Global Homotopy Is Too Coarse

02 / 17
source span
284-304

Problem

A null-homotopy lives on the whole square I x I. A connection, frame, or local trivialization usually behaves cleanly only on small coordinate patches.

Desired Replacement

Instead of one large contraction, use a finite word of conjugated local loops. Each loop is small enough to fit inside a chosen member of the open covering.

The lemma makes local trivializations compatible with a global contraction.

Global inputLocal output
Emphasize the mismatch of scale. A homotopy proves that the loop dies globally, but most differential-geometric constructions are patched together locally. The factorization lemma bridges that mismatch by replacing the global square with a finite inventory of patch-sized loops and harmless backtracking pieces.

Endpoint Convention

Two curves x(t) and y(t) are compared by a map F(t,s) whose bottom and top edges are the curves, while the left and right edges remain fixed.

Why It Matters

With endpoints fixed, the boundary of the square carries a single based loop. That based viewpoint is what lets later loops multiply in a well-defined order.

The square remembers both deformation and basepoint discipline.

Homotopy relative endpointsBased loop multiplication comes later
Set up the square carefully because the rest of the proof reads its boundary. The vertical sides are not decorative; they keep the endpoints locked. That lock is what allows the boundary pieces to be inserted, canceled, and multiplied without changing the based homotopy class being analyzed.

First Lemma

Continuous Homotopy Can Be Made Piecewise Ck

04 / 17
regularity
by subdivision

Regularity Upgrade

For piecewise Ck boundary curves, a continuous homotopy can be replaced by a homotopy that is piecewise Ck on small rectangles.

Proof Move

  1. Subdivide the square.
  2. Keep each small image in one coordinate neighborhood.
  3. Modify locally without moving the boundary endpoints.
Regularity is localizedLater lassos inherit differentiability
Present this as the technical cleaning step before the main factorization. The proof does not magically smooth a wild global object all at once. It refines the square until every small rectangle is chart-controlled, then performs local replacements that preserve the boundary information needed for the next argument.

Open Cover

The Covering Chooses the Resolution

05 / 17
arbitrary cover
finite grid

Scale Rule

Given any open covering U, subdivide the homotopy square finely enough that every small square maps into one cover element.

Teaching Emphasis

The cover is arbitrary, so the lemma is useful precisely when later geometry dictates the neighborhoods.

Input: an open coveringOutput: local loops adapted to that covering
Stress that the open cover is not chosen for convenience after the proof. It is part of the input. If the later argument needs bundle charts, normal neighborhoods, or domains of a local equivalence, the subdivision adapts the homotopy square to exactly that prescribed local scale.

Definition

A U-Lasso Is a Conjugated Local Loop

06 / 17
base path
local loop

Lasso Form

γ = μ-1 · α · μ

The path μ walks from the base point to a point in a cover element; the loop α closes inside that cover element.

Interpretation

A lasso records local information but transports it back to the common base point so products can be compared.

Local loop, global base point.

Conjugation is bookkeepingThe loop itself is local
Use the lasso picture to separate two roles. The small loop alpha is where the local geometric calculation happens. The path mu is bookkeeping: it moves the loop to the chosen base point and moves back again, making it possible to multiply many local contributions as based loops.

Equivalence

Backtracking Pieces Carry No Net Curve

07 / 17
insert or delete
mu inverse mu

Equivalence Is Not Equality of Drawings

Curves are allowed to gain or lose a finite number of immediate out-and-back trips. These insertions change the parametrized path but not the based curve class used by the lemma.

Why the Proof Needs It

The grid boundary creates many duplicate edges with opposite orientations. Backtrack equivalence lets those duplicates cancel cleanly.

Allowed move: insert a backtrackAllowed move: delete a backtrack
Pause here because students often confuse the equivalence relation with ordinary equality of paths. The proof is algebraic about words in path segments. Opposite traversals of the same small edge appear constantly, and the equivalence relation gives permission to erase those adjacent out-and-back factors.

Main Lemma

Statement in Working Form

08 / 17
factorization
finite product

Factorization Lemma

For any open covering U of M, every based closed curve that is homotopic to the constant loop is equivalent to a finite product of U-lassos.

Piecewise Ck Version

If the original curve is piecewise Ck, the base paths can be chosen piecewise Ck and the small loops can be chosen Ck.

Input: contractible based curveOutput: product of local lassos
Give the theorem in the form an instructor will actually use. The conclusion is not merely that the curve is trivial. It says the triviality can be witnessed by finitely many local loop terms, each transported to the base point and each compatible with the prescribed open covering.

Proof Geometry

Divide the Homotopy Square into Small Cells

09 / 17
m by m grid
cover-controlled

Grid Choice

Compactness lets one choose m so the image of each cell lies in some cover element. This is the finite source of the finite lasso product.

Boundary Reading

The lower boundary is the original curve, the upper boundary has collapsed to the base point, and the vertical sides stay at the base point.

Fine subdivisionOne small cell, one local loop candidate
The grid is the heart of the proof, so linger on it. The homotopy square is compact and the cover is open, so a sufficiently fine mesh has images small enough for local control. The finite number of cells is exactly why the final factorization contains a finite word.

Cell Loop

One Cell Produces One Local Lasso

Cell Lasso

The path first moves from the square base corner to the chosen cell, loops once around the cell boundary, and then returns along the same corridor.

After Applying the Homotopy

The small cell boundary maps into one cover element, so its image is the local loop α; the corridor is the conjugating path μ.

A(i,j) is the combinatorial lassoF(A(i,j)) is the geometric lasso
Describe the lasso before naming formulas. In the parameter square, the path walks from the origin to a selected grid cell, goes around that cell, and returns. Applying the homotopy sends the small cell boundary into one open set, producing exactly the conjugated local loop needed in the theorem.

Word Algebra

Product Order Makes Interior Edges Cancel

11 / 17
finite product
edge cancellation

Ordering Idea

List the cell lassos row by row. Adjacent cells contribute shared grid edges in opposite directions, so the product reduces by repeated backtrack deletion.

What Remains

After the interior edges cancel, the word that remains follows the boundary of the full square. That boundary is equivalent to the original closed curve.

Combinatorics of pathsThe proof is a controlled cancellation argument
Make the algebraic nature explicit. Each cell lasso contributes a small boundary word plus a corridor to the base point. The chosen product order aligns shared edges with the reverse orientation supplied by neighboring cells, so repeated backtrack cancellations leave only the outer boundary of the homotopy square.

Regularity Again

Piecewise Ck Boundaries Give Smooth Local Loops

12 / 17
Ck cells
Ck loops

Differentiable Factorization

After the initial regularity upgrade, choose the grid so the homotopy is Ck on every small square. The local loop around each small square is then Ck.

Conjugating Path

The path back to the base point may remain piecewise Ck, which is exactly enough for products of curves in applications.

Smoothness is local, not globalThe theorem keeps the regularity needed later
Connect the technical lemma with the main theorem. Once the homotopy has been made piecewise Ck, choosing a finer mesh ensures every cell lies in a smooth patch of the homotopy. The lasso loop around the cell then has the smoothness required for differential-geometric operations.

Differential-Geometric Use

Local Lassos Feed Bundle and Holonomy Arguments

Bundle Reading

A lasso contained in one trivializing set is the part of the loop where connection forms, transition functions, or local equivalence data can be evaluated without changing charts.

Why Products Matter

If every null loop factors into local lassos, then a local rule that behaves correctly on lassos can often be extended to the original global loop.

Local computationGlobal curve class
Relate the appendix back to the course. In bundle arguments, calculations often require a single trivialization or a single coordinate neighborhood. The lemma says a globally null loop can be tested through a finite set of such local pieces, making it a quiet but powerful support result.

Common Mistakes

Failure Modes to Watch

14 / 17
diagnostics
proof hygiene

Grid Too Coarse

A cell whose image crosses several cover elements is not a local loop. Refine the mesh before naming lassos.

Lost Base Point

Local loops must be transported back to the same base point. Otherwise their product is not the original based word.

No Cancellation Rule

The product proof depends on deleting immediate reversals. Without backtrack equivalence, the boundary word will not simplify.

Check localityCheck basepoint discipline
Use these as a diagnostic checklist while students reconstruct the proof. The lasso terminology can hide three requirements: the cells must be small enough, the loop must return to the chosen base point, and the algebra must permit deletion of immediate reverse traversals.

Miniature Argument

Four cell lassos already display the whole proof. The exact indexing is less important than the principle: multiply cells so shared edges meet their reverse.

Classroom Use

Ask students to draw the boundary word before and after deleting the middle vertical and horizontal backtracks.

Run this as a board exercise. With only four cells, everyone can see the path word and the reverse edge cancellations. After that concrete case, the m by m proof feels like iteration of the same local move rather than a mysterious indexing formula.

Recap

The Factorization Machine

Checklist

  1. Start with a based loop and a null-homotopy.
  2. Refine the square to match the open covering.
  3. Attach one based lasso to each small cell.
  4. Multiply in an order that cancels shared edges.
  5. Preserve Ck regularity when the input has it.

Takeaway

The lemma converts global null-homotopy information into finitely many local curve tests.

A compact tool for moving between global homotopy and local geometry.
