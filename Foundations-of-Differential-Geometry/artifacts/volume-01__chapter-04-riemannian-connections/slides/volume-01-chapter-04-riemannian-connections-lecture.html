Riemannian Metrics As Moving Inner Products

section 1

Metric Data

A Riemannian metric assigns each tangent space TxM a positive definite inner product gx.

g = gij dxi dxj in a chart.

gij is the inverse matrix for raising indices.

Make the metric concrete before discussing connections. Students should see the metric as a smoothly varying ruler on tangent spaces, not merely as a matrix. The coordinate matrix is useful, but the invariant object is the inner product and the geometric measurements it supports.

Length And Distance Enter The Story

metric geometry

From Local Rulers To Global Distance

Length integrates the speed of a curve; distance is the infimum over all piecewise smooth connecting curves.

L(c) = ∫ ||c'(t)|| dt

d(x,y) = inf L(c) over paths from x to y.

Present distance as the first global object built from the metric. At this stage it is defined by an infimum, so it is not yet obvious that geodesics realize it or that it gives the manifold topology. The normal-coordinate section supplies the local proof mechanism.

The Levi-Civita Connection

section 2

Existence And Uniqueness

There is a unique connection satisfying ∇g = 0 and T = 0.

Metric: parallel transport preserves inner products.

Torsion-free: skew covariant differentiation agrees with the Lie bracket.

This is the chapter's central selection theorem. The previous chapter made connections flexible; the metric imposes two geometric demands that determine one connection. Explain metric compatibility as preserving measuring devices, and torsion-free behavior as aligning infinitesimal parallelograms with the bracket structure.

Koszul Formula As A Recipe

recovering the connection

Operational Reading

The Levi-Civita derivative is determined by differentiating pairings and correcting by brackets.

2g(∇XY,Z) = Xg(Y,Z) + Yg(X,Z) - Zg(X,Y) + bracket terms.

Differentiate Pairings

Measure how lengths and angles change along vector fields.

Correct Noncommuting Fields

Lie brackets account for moving bases that do not close as coordinate grids.

Test Against All Z

Pairing with every vector field determines the unknown derivative.

Uniqueness Falls Out

Any torsion-free metric connection must satisfy the same identity.

Do not ask students to memorize the full displayed identity as a first move. The important idea is that the metric lets one solve for the unknown covariant derivative by testing it against every vector field. The bracket corrections are exactly the price of non-coordinate motion.

Christoffel Symbols From The Metric

coordinate formula

Coordinate Payoff

The symbols Γijk are built from first derivatives of gij and the inverse matrix gij.

They are excellent for computation.

They are not tensor components by themselves.

This is where the abstract uniqueness theorem becomes a calculational tool. The metric coefficients and their first derivatives determine the Christoffel symbols. Emphasize the standard warning: symbols help compute geodesics and curvature, but the symbols themselves depend on the chosen coordinates.

Isometries Commute With Riemannian Transport

Uniqueness Argument

Pull back the Riemannian connection by an isometry. It is still metric and torsion-free, so uniqueness forces it to be the original one.

geodesics map to geodesics covering metrics lift holonomy is respected
This slide is a good place to show the power of uniqueness. Instead of computing transported vectors directly, pull the connection through an isometry and notice that the two defining properties survive. The conclusion automatically carries geodesics, parallel transport, and holonomy information across the map.

Normal Coordinates Spend Geodesics As Coordinates

section 3

Normal Coordinate Payoff

At the center, the frame is orthonormal and radial coordinate lines are geodesics parameterized by arc length.

The distance from the center is the tangent-vector norm.

Metric coefficients simplify at the base point.

Normal coordinates should feel like a controlled laboratory rather than a trick. The exponential map converts a small tangent ball into a neighborhood of the manifold, and straight radial lines become geodesics. This gives local access to distance, spheres, and minimizing behavior.

Convex Neighborhoods Localize Minimizing Geometry

section 3

1

Use normal coordinates to create local geodesic balls.

2

Shrink the ball until nearby endpoints are joined by one geodesic lying inside.

3

In that region, geodesic length realizes distance.

4

The squared distance becomes a differentiable two-point function.

Convex neighborhoods are the local control device for the rest of the chapter. They certify uniqueness and minimizing behavior without assuming global completeness. Stress that the theorem is not saying all geodesics minimize forever, only that sufficiently small regions have reliable geodesic coordinates.

Distance Remembers The Riemannian Metric

Myers-Steenrod direction

Distance Rigidity

A distance-preserving bijection between Riemannian manifolds is forced to be an isometry.

Segments are geodesics as point sets.

Normal coordinates recover first-order metric data.

This theorem is a conceptual checkpoint: the distance function is not an external decoration but a complete carrier of the Riemannian metric. Convex neighborhoods and normal coordinates let one turn preservation of distances into preservation of geodesics and then into preservation of inner products.

Completeness: Four Faces Of The Same Condition

section 4

Connected Manifold Theorem

Geodesic completeness, metric completeness, compactness of bounded sets, and global development behavior become equivalent.

minimizing geodesics exist compact manifolds complete homogeneous manifolds complete
Treat completeness as the moment local geodesic control becomes global. The theorem ties together four languages: ordinary metric spaces, geodesic differential equations, compactness of balls, and development in tangent space. The existence of minimizing geodesics between any two points is the visible payoff.

Riemannian Holonomy Is Orthogonal

section 5

Reducible Or Irreducible

Holonomy acts on TxM. If it preserves a proper orthogonal splitting, the geometry carries parallel distributions.

Irreducible: no nontrivial invariant subspace.

Reducible: tangent space splits into invariant directions.

Bring holonomy back as the memory of parallel transport around loops. Because the Levi-Civita connection preserves the metric, the holonomy group acts by orthogonal transformations. Reducibility is therefore a metric statement about invariant orthogonal subspaces in a single tangent space.

Parallel Distributions Create Local Products

section 5

1

Start with an invariant subspace of one tangent space.

2

Parallel transport it to every point to form a distribution.

3

Orthogonality supplies the complementary distribution.

4

Integral leaves are totally geodesic and the metric locally factors.

This is the local bridge from representation theory to geometry. A holonomy-invariant subspace is not just algebra at one point; parallel transport spreads it into a distribution. Orthogonality gives a partner distribution, and the torsion-free metric connection makes the corresponding leaves behave like product factors.

The de Rham Decomposition Theorem

section 6

Global Product Statement

A connected, simply connected, complete Riemannian manifold splits as a product of a Euclidean factor and irreducible complete factors.

M ≅ M0 × M1 × ... × Mk, with M0 Euclidean.

State the theorem as the global payoff of the holonomy discussion. Completeness and simple connectedness are the hypotheses that let local product geometry integrate without monodromy. The Euclidean factor records directions fixed by holonomy, while the other factors are irreducible pieces.

Affine Holonomy Adds Translations

section 7

Irreducible Alternative

When restricted linear holonomy is irreducible, affine holonomy either contains all translations or has a fixed point.

Fixed point in the complete simply connected case forces Euclidean geometry.

Nonflat irreducible complete cases therefore carry full translations.

Affine holonomy extends the linear picture by allowing translations of the affine tangent space. The chapter's dichotomy says that irreducibility leaves little room: either translations fill the tangent space, or a fixed point collapses the geometry toward the Euclidean case under completeness and simple connectedness.
