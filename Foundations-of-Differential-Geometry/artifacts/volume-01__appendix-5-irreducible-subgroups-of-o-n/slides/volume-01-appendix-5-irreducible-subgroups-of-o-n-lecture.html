Irreducible Subgroups of O(n)

A compact representation-theoretic tool for invariant metrics, holonomy decomposition, and closedness inside the orthogonal group.

Guiding QuestionWhat can an orthogonal group action preserve when it has no nonzero proper invariant subspace?
The appendix proves two workhorse results: invariant symmetric forms are scalar multiples of the standard metric, and connected irreducible subgroups of SO(n) are closed.

Proof Architecture

Two Theorems, One Commutant Engine

Instructor route: algebra first, geometry secondAppendix 5
Present the appendix as a dependency map. The same commutant idea controls both conclusions: first it limits invariant symmetric bilinear forms, then it limits the possible center and closure behavior of the connected Lie subgroup.
Definition Layer

Irreducible Means No Hidden Coordinate Block

DefinitionA subgroup G < O(n) acts irreducibly on R^n when the only G-invariant real linear subspaces are 0 and all of R^n.
  • Orthogonality supplies a fixed positive definite inner product.
  • Irreducibility prevents algebraic kernels from becoming stable subspaces.
  • The appendix repeatedly converts an operator statement into an invariant-subspace contradiction.
Core language: representation, invariant subspace, commutantSlide 3
Pause on the word irreducible, because it is the hypothesis doing the work. The action may have many orbits, but it cannot leave a smaller linear coordinate block stable, so any construction producing such a block contradicts the setup.
Algebraic Engine

The Commutant Is Forced To Be Small

Commuting OperatorAn operator A belongs to the commutant when Ag = gA for every g in G.
If a polynomial in A has a kernel, that kernel is automatically stable under G. Irreducibility then says the kernel is either trivial or everything.
ker p(A) is G-stable
The commutant turns operator algebra into invariant-subspace testsSlide 4
Explain that every later argument begins with an operator commuting with the group. Once the operator commutes, its kernels, images, and polynomial eigenspaces are also respected by the group, so irreducibility turns linear algebra into a severe dichotomy.
Lemma 1

Minimal Polynomials Have Only Two Real Shapes

ConclusionFor a commuting operator A, the real algebra it generates is either scalar, or it carries a complex-structure generator J with J^2 = -I.
  • Nilpotent commuting operators vanish.
  • A reducible minimal polynomial would split R^n.
  • The only irreducible real polynomial options are linear or quadratic.
Linear type or complex typeSlide 5
Use this slide to make Lemma 1 feel concrete. The proof does not classify all representations; it classifies the algebra seen by a single commuting operator. Irreducibility removes nilpotent and split-polynomial possibilities before they can grow.
Why Splitting Fails

A Polynomial Factor Would Draw Forbidden Subspaces

Contradiction PatternIf the minimal polynomial had coprime factors, each kernel would be stable under G. A nontrivial direct-sum decomposition would violate irreducibility.
This is the appendix's recurring proof gesture: find a candidate subspace naturally produced by algebra, then use the group action to test whether the candidate can exist.
Polynomial algebra draws geometrySlide 6
This is the proof move students should remember. A factorization is not merely a symbolic event; through the kernels of the factors it tries to create an invariant direct sum. Irreducibility rejects that picture unless one side is trivial.
Lemma 2, Symmetric Case

Invariant Self-Adjoint Operators Become Scalars

Self-Adjoint TestIf A is symmetric and commutes with an irreducible orthogonal action, then A = aI.
The complex-structure alternative J^2 = -I is incompatible with symmetry in a positive definite inner product: it would turn a vector into one with negative squared length.
symmetric + commuting + irreducible → scalar
The standard metric is unique up to scaleSlide 7
Emphasize the geometric meaning of the symmetric case. A self-adjoint commuting operator would encode a new invariant ellipsoid. Lemma 2 says irreducibility and orthogonality round that ellipsoid into a scalar multiple of the original metric.
Lemma 2, Skew Case

The Only Skew Alternative Is A Complex Structure

Skew CommutantA nonzero skew-symmetric commuting operator has the form bJ, where J^2 = -I. Therefore the dimension is even.
If two nonzero skew operators commute with each other and with G, the appendix compares their complex structures and concludes they are scalar multiples.
One complex direction at mostSlide 8
The skew-symmetric part explains why a one-dimensional center can appear later. A nonzero skew operator behaves like multiplication by the imaginary unit on paired planes, and the irreducibility argument prevents two independent such rotations from coexisting.
Theorem 1

Invariant Symmetric Bilinear Forms Are Scalar Metrics

StatementFor an irreducible subgroup G < O(n), every G-invariant symmetric bilinear form on R^n is a scalar multiple of the standard inner product.
f(x,y) = <Ax,y>, and invariance gives Ag = gA
  • Represent the form by a symmetric operator A.
  • Use invariance to place A in the commutant.
  • Apply Lemma 2 to get A = aI.
This is the appendix result used by metric uniqueness argumentsSlide 9
Walk through Theorem 1 as a three-line proof with a lot of meaning. The only representation theorem needed is the commutant lemma; once the form is represented by a symmetric operator, invariant geometry becomes scalar linear algebra.
Differential-Geometric Use

Why This Appendix Belongs In A Holonomy Course

Holonomy TranslationA parallel symmetric tensor is fixed by the holonomy group on each tangent space. If that holonomy action is irreducible, Theorem 1 says the tensor is a scalar multiple of the metric.
This is why the appendix supports de Rham-type decomposition statements and rigidity arguments elsewhere in the volume.
Parallel data becomes invariant representation dataSlide 10
Connect the algebra back to geometry. At a point, holonomy acts by orthogonal transformations on the tangent space. Parallel tensors are exactly the objects transported consistently around loops, so Theorem 1 says irreducible holonomy leaves no new symmetric tensor beyond the metric scale.
Theorem 2

Connected Irreducible Subgroups of SO(n) Are Closed

StatementIf G is a connected Lie subgroup of SO(n) acting irreducibly on R^n, then G is closed in SO(n).
The proof splits the Lie algebra into a small center and a semisimple part. The center produces at most a circle; the semisimple part is closed by a trace and complete reducibility argument inside the compact orthogonal group.
Closedness matters for treating holonomy as an honest Lie subgroupSlide 11
State Theorem 2 without making it sound automatic. A connected Lie subgroup of a compact group need not be visibly closed from its parametrization. The proof shows that irreducibility eliminates the extra closure directions that could otherwise appear.
First Step Toward Closedness

The Center Is Zero Or One-Dimensional

Key BoundCentral Lie algebra elements are skew-symmetric and commute with G. Lemma 2 forces any two nonzero central elements to be scalar multiples.
When the center is one-dimensional, its connected subgroup is a usual rotation circle generated by J, hence closed.
Lemma 2 controls the Lie algebra centerSlide 12
This slide is the bridge between the two theorems. The skew-operator conclusion from Lemma 2 returns as a statement about the center of the Lie algebra. There is room for no center, or for one circle of rotations, but not for a larger torus.
Lie Algebra Decomposition

Separate The Circle From The Semisimple Core

g = z + s, with s = s1 + ... + sk
Compact Inner ProductThe form (A,B) = -tr(AB) on skew matrices is positive definite and invariant under the adjoint action.
The orthogonal complement to the center is an ideal. Repeating the same complement argument decomposes it into simple ideals.
The compact metric makes ideal complements visibleSlide 13
Keep the Lie algebra notation simple. The invariant trace inner product lets the proof take orthogonal complements without losing ideal structure. Once the center is isolated, the remaining part splits into simple pieces that behave semisimply.
Semisimple Closedness

No Extra Lie Algebra Can Hide In The Closure

Closure TestLet the closure have a larger Lie algebra. Its complement acts on the complex irreducible summands. Schur-type reasoning makes that action scalar; the trace condition makes the scalar zero.
Therefore the closure contributes no new tangent direction. The subgroup and its closure have the same Lie algebra, so the connected subgroup is closed.
The proof is a trace argument disguised as topologySlide 14
Do not overload the audience with every historical reference. The main mechanism is that a hypothetical extra closure direction acts on each complex irreducible summand as a scalar. Semisimplicity supplies trace zero, forcing every such scalar to vanish.
Contrast Example

Reducibility Allows Many Invariant Forms

Toy ModelIf R^n = U + W is a stable orthogonal direct sum, then forms can weight U and W differently while remaining group-invariant.
f = a metric on U + b metric on W
Theorem 1 is therefore not a statement about orthogonal groups alone; it is a statement about orthogonal groups with irreducible representation behavior.
A useful misconception checkSlide 15
Use the contrast to protect students from overgeneralizing Theorem 1. If the representation splits, each stable block can carry its own scale. Irreducibility is exactly what removes those independent weights and forces a single scalar.
Board Plan

How To Test A Candidate Irreducible Subgroup

1. Check SplittingLook for nonzero proper invariant subspaces. If one exists, Theorem 1 is not available in scalar form.
2. Probe CommutantsFind operators commuting with the action. Kernels and eigenspaces should not produce new stable blocks.
3. Read InvariantsSymmetric invariant forms collapse to scale; skew commuting elements reveal at most one complex-structure direction.
Use as a lecture board sequenceSlide 16
This slide turns the appendix into a reusable classroom checklist. For any proposed holonomy or symmetry example, first test reducibility, then inspect commuting operators, and finally translate the result into metric uniqueness or the possible circle in the center.
