Connections In A Vector Bundle

covariant differentiation

Definition Pattern

A connection gives each section s a directional derivative ∇Xs along vector fields X.

∇fX+gYs = f∇Xs + g∇Ys

∇X(fs) = X(f)s + f∇Xs

Emphasize that the derivative is not obtained by subtracting vectors in unrelated fibers. A connection supplies the comparison rule first, and only then does the Leibniz formula make sense. This is the vector-bundle version of the horizontal-lift story already built for principal bundles.

Parallel Transport And A Bundle Metric

compatibility as preserved measurement

Metric-Compatible Connection

For a fiber metric g, compatibility means

X g(s,t) = g(∇Xs,t) + g(s,∇Xt).

Along a curve, parallel sections keep their fiber length and mutual angle. This turns transport into a controlled measurement process rather than a purely algebraic lift.

The chapter proves existence results for connections and then singles out metric-preserving behavior as a useful refinement. Present this as a design choice: many connections differentiate sections, but a metric-compatible one also promises that parallel transport does not distort fiber lengths or pairings.

The Frame Bundle Encodes Linear Connections

connection form plus solder form

Two Forms On L(M)

Connection form ω: vertical correction in gl(n)

Solder form θ: base velocity expressed in the current frame

A tangent vector to the frame bundle is readable when its vertical part and base-projected part are both recorded.

Move students from the abstract phrase linear connection to a concrete bookkeeping device. A point of the frame bundle is a choice of basis in a tangent space. The connection form detects how that basis changes vertically, while the solder form remembers the base velocity written in that basis.

Structure Equations

torsion and curvature as exterior derivatives

Cartan Equations For A Linear Connection

dθ + ω ∧ θ = Θ

dω + 1/2[ω,ω] = Ω

Torsion form

Θ measures failure of horizontal displacement to close in translation.

Curvature form

Ω measures failure of horizontal displacement to close in frame orientation.

Treat the equations as diagnostics, not as formal decorations. The solder form turns base motion into vector components, so its derivative exposes torsion. The connection form records frame change, so its derivative and bracket term expose curvature. This is the algebraic heart of the chapter.

Bianchi Identities As Consistency Checks

differentiate the structure equations

First Identity

DΘ = Ω ∧ θ

The torsion defect cannot vary independently of curvature.

Second Identity

DΩ = 0

The curvature form obeys a covariant closedness law.

Teaching Use

Whenever a proposed connection produces torsion and curvature, these identities are the first invariant audit.

Students often memorize Bianchi identities after seeing them in Riemannian geometry, but here they arise directly from the frame-bundle structure equations. Explain them as compatibility audits: once torsion and curvature are produced by a single connection, their covariant derivatives must satisfy these linked constraints.

Covariant Derivatives Of Tensor Fields

the connection acts as a derivation

Extension Principle

∇X(A ⊗ B) = (∇XA) ⊗ B + A ⊗ (∇XB)

∇X commutes with contraction.

A tensor is parallel when all directional covariant derivatives vanish, equivalently when parallel transport preserves its components along every curve.

This slide is the algebraic payoff of the earlier definitions. Once the connection differentiates vector fields and covectors, the product rule and contraction rule determine its behavior on all tensors. Parallel tensor fields then become invariant objects transported unchanged by the connection.

From Linear To Affine Connections

add translation to frame motion

Affine Warning

Affine frames remember both a point and a basis.

The homomorphism from affine frames to linear frames forgets translation. An affine connection is compatible with a linear connection when that forgetting sends horizontal directions to horizontal directions.

Make the affine step geometrically concrete. A linear frame knows how to compare directions; an affine frame also remembers where the origin of the model space has been placed. The chapter relates these by projecting affine information back to the linear frame bundle.

Development Of A Curve

roll the connection into R^n

Development Test

Lift the curve horizontally to frames, read velocity through θ, then integrate those components in R^n.

A geodesic is exactly a curve whose development is a straight line with affine parameter.

Development is the lecture's most visual construction. The curve is not copied into Euclidean space by coordinates; it is rolled out using horizontal frames. This is why the result detects the connection itself. A straight developed curve is the clean signature of a geodesic.

Torsion And Curvature Tensors

two failures of covariant differentiation

Tensor Formulas

T(X,Y) = ∇XY - ∇YX - [X,Y]

R(X,Y)Z = ∇X∇YZ - ∇Y∇XZ - ∇[X,Y]Z

Distinguish the two failures carefully. Torsion compares the skew part of covariant differentiation with the actual Lie bracket of vector fields. Curvature compares the two orders of second covariant differentiation. Both are tensors, so their values can be checked at a point using convenient extensions.

Bianchi Identities With Torsion

cyclic bookkeeping

Cyclic Forms

Σcyc R(X,Y)Z = Σcyc (∇XT)(Y,Z) + Σcyc T(T(X,Y),Z)

Σcyc (∇XR)(Y,Z) = Σcyc R(T(X,Y),Z)

When torsion is present, the familiar Riemannian identities acquire correction terms. Do not let the notation hide the idea: the cyclic sum is comparing three ways to travel around an infinitesimal triangle. Torsion contributes because the triangle itself fails to close in the naive way.

Geodesics As Horizontal Dynamics

autoparallel curves

Geodesic Equation Invariantly

∇˙γ˙γ = 0

The source treats geodesics through horizontal lifts, standard horizontal vector fields, and local existence for the connection-driven ordinary differential equation.

Frame geodesics as dynamics determined by the connection, not as shortest paths. A curve is geodesic when its velocity is transported parallel to itself. The development criterion gives students an immediate visual test: under the chosen connection, the curve rolls out to a straight line.

Coordinate Expressions

Christoffel symbols as local data

Local Frame

∇∂j∂k = Σi Γijk∂i

Tijk = Γijk - Γikj

geodesic: d2xi/dt2 + Γijk dxj/dt dxk/dt = 0

Coordinates are useful but dangerous. The Christoffel symbols are the local coefficients of a connection, not tensor components by themselves. Torsion and curvature are built from them in combinations that transform tensorially, while the geodesic equation exposes the ordinary differential equation behind autoparallel curves.

Normal Coordinates

the exponential map as a chart

Normal Coordinate Payoff

expx(tX) gives the geodesic with initial velocity X.

For torsion-free connections, normal coordinates make the connection coefficients vanish at the base point, leaving curvature in the next-order behavior.

Explain normal coordinates as a way to spend geodesics as coordinates. The exponential map sends tangent vectors to points reached by geodesics, so radial lines become geodesic paths. In the torsion-free case, the first-order Christoffel data can be erased at the base point.

Linear Infinitesimal Holonomy

curvature generates the local algebra

Holonomy Reading

The infinitesimal holonomy algebra is built from curvature operators and their covariant derivatives.

This is the local version of the idea that parallel transport around loops remembers curvature.

Connect this section back to Chapter 2 without assuming the audience remembers every holonomy theorem. Holonomy is what parallel transport can do around loops. Infinitesimally, the algebra is generated by curvature and its covariant derivatives, so curvature is not only a defect but a generator.
