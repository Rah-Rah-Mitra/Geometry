The Appendix Proves A Stronger Local Case

Lecture formulation

If a real analytic almost complex structure J is integrable, then near every point it is induced by a genuine complex coordinate system.

The proof is local: produce enough complex-valued functions, then use them as coordinate charts.

This slide isolates the theorem in lecture language. Students should hear that there are two separate upgrades: integrability removes the geometric obstruction, while real analyticity gives access to holomorphic extension. Neither ingredient alone is the whole proof. The flow diagram previews the proof order so later lemmas have a visible place in the argument.

Almost Complex Data Splits Forms Into Types

local linear algebra

Type (1,0)

alpha(Jv) = i alpha(v)

Type (0,1)

beta(Jv) = -i beta(v)

The complexified cotangent space separates into the two eigenspaces. A local coframe omega^1, ..., omega^n of type (1,0) is the main object to integrate.

Review the local linear algebra before discussing differential equations. The almost complex structure behaves like multiplication by i on tangent vectors after complexification, so covectors split into type (1,0) and type (0,1). The proof will not try to integrate arbitrary real forms; it focuses on a real analytic coframe of type (1,0).

Integrability Removes The Barred-Barred Component

Lemma 2 role

For a type (1,0) coframe omega^j, exterior differentiation can create three wedge types.

omega ∧ omegaallowed
omega ∧ bar omegaallowed
bar omega ∧ bar omegaobstruction

Integrable case

d omega^j is generated by omega^h ∧ omega^k and omega^h ∧ bar omega^k, with no pure barred wedge.

Connect integrability to a concrete algebraic filter. The appendix uses a formula from Chapter IX saying that torsion-free, or integrable, is equivalent to the absence of the barred-barred component in the exterior derivatives of a type (1,0) coframe. This is the exact condition needed before applying Frobenius after holomorphic extension.

Lemma 1: Enough Type (1,0) Functions Give Charts

coordinate criterion

Local chart criterion

If every point has functions f^1, ..., f^n whose differentials are type (1,0) and linearly independent, then these functions define complex coordinates for J.

The transition maps are almost complex maps between domains in C^n, hence holomorphic by the earlier Chapter IX result.

Lemma 1 is the bridge from differential forms to atlas construction. Once type (1,0) functions with independent differentials exist, they are local coordinates. On overlaps, the transition map preserves the standard complex structure because both coordinate maps respect J. A previous proposition then promotes almost complex maps between complex domains to holomorphic maps.

The Real Work Is Integrating A Local Coframe

after Lemma 1

Local input

Choose n real analytic type (1,0) forms omega^1, ..., omega^n forming a coframe.

Desired output

Find functions f^1, ..., f^n so the span of df^k equals the span of omega^j.

The proof is therefore a Pfaffian integration problem, not merely a coordinate notation problem.

After Lemma 1, the problem has a precise local target. We already have a coframe of analytic type (1,0) forms, but coframes need not be exact. The proof must show that, after shrinking the neighborhood and changing basis by an invertible matrix of functions, the coframe comes from differentials of coordinate functions.

Real Analyticity Opens A Holomorphic Neighborhood

complexification step

Write each coframe component in real coordinates:

omega^j = sum_A f_A^j(x) dx^A

Because f_A^j is real analytic, its power series extends to holomorphic coefficients F_A^j(z) on a small neighborhood in C^{2n}.

Important distinction

The extended barred symbols are holomorphic continuations of conjugate coefficient functions; they are not conjugates on the complexified neighborhood.

This is where the stronger hypothesis enters decisively. Smooth functions do not automatically extend to holomorphic functions, but real analytic functions have convergent power series. The proof replaces real variables x by complex variables z and works on a small complex neighborhood, while remembering that the barred extension is a separate holomorphic object off the real slice.

The Extended Forms Stay Independent After Shrinking

nonzero determinant

The 2n forms Omega^1, ..., Omega^n, bar Omega^1, ..., bar Omega^n form a holomorphic coframe near the origin.

Continuity move

The determinant is nonzero on the real slice at the base point; after shrinking U*, it remains nonzero.

This gives the holomorphic Frobenius theorem an independent system to act on.

Independence is not a decorative condition. Frobenius needs independent forms, and Lemma 1 later needs independent differentials. The appendix uses the nonzero determinant of the real coframe and its conjugate partners, then shrinks the complex neighborhood so the holomorphic determinant stays nonzero. This is a standard but essential local compactness and continuity move.

The Missing Component Vanishes By Analytic Continuation

from U to U*

In U*, expand the exterior derivative of Omega^j in the holomorphic coframe.

d Omega^j = A terms + B terms + C terms

On the real slice, integrability says the C terms, the bar Omega ∧ bar Omega coefficients, are zero.

Identity theorem

Holomorphic coefficients that vanish on the real analytic slice vanish throughout the smaller complex neighborhood.

Here the two main hypotheses meet. Integrability gives the vanishing of the barred-barred coefficient on the real neighborhood. Real analyticity turns that coefficient into a holomorphic function on U star. Since it vanishes along the real slice from which it was analytically continued, the identity theorem forces it to vanish in the complexified neighborhood.

Holomorphic Frobenius Integrates The Pfaffian System

the omitted engine

Holomorphic Frobenius

If phi^1, ..., phi^r are independent and d phi^j lies in the ideal they generate, then locally phi^j = sum_k P_k^j d g^k.

The appendix invokes this theorem for Omega^1, ..., Omega^n once the barred-barred obstruction has been removed.

The source span does not reprove Frobenius in detail, so the lecture should clarify its role. Frobenius says the differential system is closed under exterior differentiation in precisely the way required for local first integrals or coordinates to exist. In this proof the theorem is applied in the holomorphic category, where the forms and the resulting functions are holomorphic.

Restrict Holomorphic Functions Back To The Real Manifold

closing the loop

Frobenius gives holomorphic functions F^1, ..., F^n on U* and an invertible coefficient matrix P.

Omega^j = sum_k P_k^j dF^k

Restrict F^k to the real slice U and call the result f^k.

Result on U

df^1, ..., df^n are type (1,0) and independent, so Lemma 1 supplies the complex coordinate chart.

Do not let students lose the real manifold at the end of the holomorphic detour. Frobenius is applied in U star, but the final coordinate functions are the restrictions of those holomorphic functions to U. The equality expressing Omega in terms of dF restricts to an equality expressing omega in terms of df, preserving type and independence.

Proof Dependency Map

what depends on what?

Use this map as a mid-lecture checkpoint. The analytic hypothesis feeds the extension step, while integrability feeds the vanishing of the forbidden component. Those two strands combine before Frobenius can be invoked. Frobenius then supplies functions, and Lemma 1 converts those functions into complex charts. Each arrow is a dependency, not just a narrative transition.

Dimension One Has No Room For The Same Obstruction

n = 1 perspective

End note in the appendix

For n = 1, the theorem is essentially the existence of isothermal coordinates.

There is only one barred direction, so a skew wedge of two independent barred one-forms cannot carry the same obstruction.

The high-dimensional proof is a disciplined version of finding coordinates compatible with the rotating tangent planes.

The source closes with the one-dimensional comparison. In real dimension two, the obstruction term has no room to appear in the same way, and the result relates to isothermal coordinates. This helps students interpret the theorem geometrically: the question is whether the local rotation field J can be straightened into the standard complex coordinate rotation.

What The Analytic Hypothesis Buys

why this is not the full theorem

Real analytic proof
  • Coefficients have convergent power series.
  • The real slice has a holomorphic thickening.
  • Identity theorem propagates vanishing.
  • Holomorphic Frobenius supplies functions.
Differentiable theorem
  • No automatic holomorphic extension.
  • Requires deeper PDE analysis.
  • Newlander-Nirenberg handles much lower regularity.
  • The appendix deliberately avoids that machinery.
The analytic case is a clean laboratory: it shows the geometric obstruction and the integration mechanism with minimal analytic overhead.
This contrast prevents a common misconception. The appendix does not prove the full differentiable Newlander-Nirenberg theorem. It proves a stronger regularity case where power series and holomorphic Frobenius can do the work. The smooth case needs analytic estimates for a nonlinear partial differential equation, which is why the main theorem is only cited in the chapter.

Three Failure Modes To Watch

diagnostic slide

Rank failure

If the coframe determinant vanishes, the would-be functions cannot form coordinates.

Torsion residual

A nonzero bar omega ∧ bar omega term blocks the Frobenius ideal condition.

No analytic bridge

Smooth data alone cannot be complexified by this power-series argument.

Use this diagnostic slide to make the proof testable. A learner should be able to point to the exact place each failure breaks the argument. Rank failure breaks Lemma 1 and Frobenius input. Torsion residual breaks integrability. Lack of analyticity breaks the passage to a holomorphic neighborhood and therefore the identity theorem step.

This lab gives instructors an interactive thought experiment without relying on live code. The parameter c stands for the barred-barred coefficient family. When c is zero, the Pfaffian ideal generated by the Omega forms is closed in the required sense. When c is nonzero, the same formal expression shows exactly why integrability is not a cosmetic assumption.

A Compact Teaching Script For The Proof

board order

1

Choose a real analytic type (1,0) coframe omega near the point.

2

Use integrability to remove the pure barred wedge in d omega.

3

Complexify the coefficient functions to holomorphic forms Omega on U*.

4

Use the identity theorem to keep the forbidden term zero on U*.

5

Apply holomorphic Frobenius, restrict the resulting functions to U, and invoke Lemma 1.

Every step either preserves type, preserves independence, or converts a closed Pfaffian system into coordinates.
This is the board-ready version of the lecture. It is intentionally compressed so an instructor can reproduce the proof without re-reading every line of the source span. Ask students after each line which hypothesis is being used. That question reveals the architecture: analyticity is used in line three and four, while integrability is used in line two and carried into Frobenius.

Takeaways

Appendix 8

Core idea

Integrability is the statement that the type (1,0) Pfaffian system is closed enough to be integrated.

  • Lemma 1 reduces complex charts to finding enough type (1,0) functions.
  • Real analyticity permits a holomorphic extension of the coframe.
  • The identity theorem transports the vanished obstruction into the complexified neighborhood.
  • Holomorphic Frobenius produces local holomorphic functions whose restrictions finish the proof.
Close by returning to the geometric meaning: the almost complex structure becomes standard in suitable local coordinates. The proof is short because the analytic case lets the algebraic obstruction be moved into a holomorphic setting where Frobenius applies cleanly. Students should leave able to identify the role of Lemma 1, integrability, analyticity, the identity theorem, and Frobenius.
