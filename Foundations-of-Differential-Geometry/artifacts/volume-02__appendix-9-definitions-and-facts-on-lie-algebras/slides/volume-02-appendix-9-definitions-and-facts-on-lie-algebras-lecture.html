Two Ways To Descend By Brackets

Derived series versus central series

Derived Series

D0g = g, and Dp+1g = [Dpg, Dpg].

Descending Central Series

C0g = g, and Cp+1g = [g, Cpg].

The inclusion Dpg ⊂ Cpg says central descent is at least as large: it lets every element of g keep acting.

Spend time distinguishing the two recursions. The derived series brackets a stage with itself, so it asks how non-abelian that stage remains. The central series brackets the whole algebra with the current stage, so it measures persistence under all adjoint actions from the original algebra.

Taxonomy From Vanishing

When bracket towers stop

Abelian

The first derived term vanishes: [g,g] = 0.

Nilpotent

Some central-series term vanishes: Cpg = 0.

Solvable

Some derived-series term vanishes: Dpg = 0.

Distinguished Ideals

  • The maximum nilpotent ideal is the nilradical.
  • The maximum solvable ideal is the radical.
  • Semi-simple means the radical is zero.
This slide turns the bracket series into vocabulary. Make clear that the radical and nilradical are not arbitrary choices; they are maximum ideals with the stated behavior. This maximum property is what lets later arguments speak about the radical invariantly under automorphisms and quotients.

Radical, Semi-Simple, Simple

Removing the solvable shadow

0 ⟶ r ⟶ g ⟶ g/r ⟶ 0
Structural Reading

The quotient by the radical is semi-simple. A simple Lie algebra is non-abelian and has no non-zero proper ideals.

The appendix uses the radical as the obstruction that Levi theory splits off.

Explain the radical as the largest solvable piece that cannot be ignored if one wants a faithful structure theorem. Semi-simple does not mean every subalgebra is simple; it means the solvable radical has disappeared. Simple is the indecomposable non-abelian case inside the semi-simple world.

The Killing-Cartan Form

An invariant bilinear probe

B(X,Y) = trace(ad(X) ad(Y))
Invariance

Automorphisms preserve B, and derivations are skew with respect to it.

Orthogonal Ideals

For an ideal a, its B-orthogonal complement is again an ideal.

Present the Killing-Cartan form as a way of turning adjoint action into measurable linear algebra. The formula is simple, but its power comes from invariance. Orthogonal complements of ideals remain ideals, so nondegeneracy is not just metric language; it is structural information.

Reductive Means Split Enough

Adjoint action controls the representation

Reductive Algebra

A Lie algebra is reductive when its adjoint representation is semi-simple. Equivalently, it is a direct sum of a semi-simple Lie algebra and an abelian Lie algebra.

Compact Source

The Lie algebra of a compact Lie group is reductive. Compact subgroups act by averaging inner products.

Connect reductivity to compact geometry. When a compact group acts linearly, one can average an inner product and get invariant orthogonal complements. That is why compact isotropy algebras and compact transformation groups repeatedly produce reductive subalgebras in the geometric chapters.

Compact Subgroups Give Reductive Subalgebras

Orthogonal complements do the splitting

The Averaging Move

  • Start with any positive inner product on g.
  • Average it over the compact group.
  • Invariant subspaces acquire invariant orthogonal complements.
  • The restricted adjoint action becomes completely reducible.
This is the geometric proof pattern that students should remember. Compactness is not used abstractly; it supplies averaging. Once an invariant inner product exists, invariant subspaces have invariant complements, and that is exactly the linear-algebra mechanism behind reductivity in compact isotropy situations.

Levi Subalgebras Split The Radical

The semi-simple quotient comes back inside

g = r + s
Levi Theorem

Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra has a Levi subalgebra s, and s is isomorphic to g/r.

This is a vector-space direct sum, not a claim that the radical disappears as an ideal-free direct product.

Stress the direction of the theorem. The quotient by the radical is semi-simple automatically, but Levi says one can choose a semi-simple subalgebra inside the original algebra that maps isomorphically onto that quotient. Later symmetric-space arguments need this internal representative.

Compact Symmetry Can Preserve A Levi Choice

Averaging enters again, now affinely

Invariant Levi Fact

If a compact group A acts by automorphisms of g, then g has an A-invariant Levi subalgebra.

The proof reduces by induction on the radical. The abelian-radical case identifies all Levi choices with an affine space of cocycles, then averages a point of that affine space over A.

This slide explains why the appendix proves more than ordinary Levi decomposition. In Chapter XI, an involution or compact automorphism group must preserve the chosen semi-simple complement. The key image is affine averaging: compactness turns a moving family of complements into one invariant complement.

The Abelian-Radical Case Is A Cocycle Picture

Every tilted Levi subalgebra is a graph

X_s = X - f(X),   f([X,Y]) = [X,f(Y)] + [f(X),Y]
Meaning

A linear map f:s → r satisfying the displayed identity tilts a fixed Levi subalgebra into another one.

Automorphisms act affinely on these maps; a compact group action has a fixed averaged map.

Do not dwell on cohomology terminology, but let students see the graph construction. When the radical is abelian, changing a Levi complement is the same as choosing a linear correction into the radical. The identity displayed is exactly what makes the graph closed under the bracket.

Real Simple Algebras And Complexification

A dichotomy used in symmetric-space structure

Dichotomy

If g is real simple, then either it admits a compatible complex structure, or its complexification is simple.

Compatible Means

A linear operator J with J2 = -I and J[X,Y] = [X,JY].

Present the dichotomy as a structural fork. If complexification is not simple, a complex ideal projects isomorphically onto the real algebra and produces the operator J. If no compatible J exists, the complexified algebra must remain simple, which matters when classifying real symmetric Lie algebras.

Compact Type Is A Sign Condition

Killing form detects compact semi-simple behavior

Compact Type

A real semi-simple Lie algebra is of compact type when its Killing-Cartan form is negative-definite.

  • Compact Lie groups give negative semi-definite Killing form.
  • Semi-simple plus compact group gives negative-definite form.
  • A compatible complex structure prevents negative-definiteness.
Make the sign condition concrete. Compact adjoint orbits allow an invariant positive inner product, and the adjoint maps become skew-symmetric, making traces of squares non-positive. In the semi-simple case nondegeneracy removes the zero directions, yielding compact type.

Real Forms Of Complex Semi-Simple Algebras

Compact real forms anchor the classification

Working Vocabulary

  • A real form is a real subalgebra whose complexification recovers the complex algebra.
  • Every complex semi-simple Lie algebra has a compact real form.
  • The proof uses root systems, but the geometric takeaway is the existence of a compact model.
Keep this slide at the level needed for the differential-geometry course. The appendix does not develop root systems in detail here, but it records the existence of compact real forms because symmetric-space duality and compact versus noncompact types repeatedly depend on choosing the right real slice.

Where The Appendix Enters Geometry

Three recurring applications

Symmetric Spaces

Levi complements can be chosen invariant under involutions, so symmetric Lie algebra decompositions respect the symmetry.

Compact Isotropy

Compact isotropy groups produce reductive subalgebras and invariant complements in tangent representations.

Automorphism Groups

Reductivity and nilpotent obstructions help decide when transformation groups can act transitively or rigidly.

This is the bridge slide back to the main course. The appendix supplies facts that make later proofs shorter: invariant Levi subalgebras for symmetric decompositions, reductive subalgebras from compact isotropy, and sign or nilpotency restrictions for transformation groups of Riemannian and Kahler structures.

Instructor Exercise: Preserve The Split

A compact-action mini-lab

Prompt

Suppose a finite group generated by an involution acts on g by automorphisms. Explain why an invariant Levi subalgebra exists, and identify where compactness enters.

Expected Discussion

The group is compact because it is finite. Its affine action on the space of Levi complements admits a fixed point obtained by averaging.

Use this exercise to rehearse the proof strategy without drowning in notation. A finite group generated by an involution is compact, so averaging is legal. The students should say that the action on Levi complements is affine and that the averaged point gives an invariant complement.
