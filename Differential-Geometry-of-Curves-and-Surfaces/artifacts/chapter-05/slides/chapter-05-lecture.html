From Local Data To A Global Ledger

Interior

\(K\,dA\)

Gaussian curvature is the infinitesimal charge stored in area.

Boundary

Geodesic curvature records how the boundary turns inside the surface.

Singular Turns

\(\sum_i \theta_i\)

Corner angles and vector-field indices keep track of discrete rotation.

Use the ledger metaphor carefully. Gaussian curvature, geodesic curvature, corner turning, and vector-field rotation are local readings, but integration and compactness make them global quantities. The aim is to show how these local readings balance against Euler characteristic.
CompactnessNo ends where curvature can escape

Compact Surfaces Make Curvature Finite

What compactness buys

  • Continuous curvature \(K\) attains maxima and minima.
  • The total curvature \(\int_M K\,dA\) is a finite number.
  • Every sequence has a convergent subsequence, so limiting geodesic and field arguments have somewhere to land.
  • Closed surfaces have no boundary term, leaving topology exposed.
A global theorem usually needs a compactness or completeness hypothesis to stop local data from disappearing at infinity.
Stress that compactness is not merely a topological adjective here. It guarantees finite total area for smooth metrics, makes continuous curvature attain extrema, and prevents curvature contributions from escaping to an end. That is why closed surfaces support clean integral identities.
Gauss-BonnetThe main balance law

Gauss-Bonnet With Boundary

Region \(R\) with piecewise smooth boundary

\[ \int_R K\,dA + \int_{\partial R} k_g\,ds + \sum_i \theta_i = 2\pi\chi(R) \]

Interpretation

  • Interior charge: how tangent planes rotate inside the region.
  • Boundary charge: how the boundary turns relative to geodesics.
  • Corner charge: finite jumps in tangent direction.
  • Right side: only the Euler characteristic survives.
Present the formula as an accounting identity for a region. Interior Gaussian curvature, boundary geodesic curvature, and corner exterior angles combine to produce a purely topological number. Each term can change under deformation, but the total remains fixed.
Closed surfacesBoundary terms vanish

Closed Surfaces: Curvature Reads Genus

Closed oriented surface of genus \(g\)

\[ \int_M K\,dA = 2\pi\chi(M) = 2\pi(2-2g) \]

Sphere

\(g=0,\ \chi=2\)

\(\int K\,dA=4\pi\). Positive total curvature is forced.

Torus

\(g=1,\ \chi=0\)

Positive and negative regions must balance in any embedded torus.

Higher Genus

\(g\ge 2,\ \chi<0\)

Total curvature is negative, regardless of local coordinates.

For a closed oriented surface, all boundary terms vanish and Gauss-Bonnet becomes a direct bridge from geometry to topology. The sphere, torus, and higher genus examples are not just illustrations; they show that the sign of average curvature is constrained by genus.

Boundary termCurves pay for not being geodesics

Geodesic Curvature Is The Boundary Payment

Two useful extremes

  • If \(\partial R\) is a geodesic polygon, then \(k_g=0\) along each smooth side.
  • If the boundary bends inside the surface, \(k_g\) records the missing turning.
  • The same surface region can trade interior area against boundary turning while \(\chi(R)\) stays fixed.
Cap check

For a topological disk, the total ledger must return \(2\pi\).

A geodesic boundary contributes no geodesic curvature, so the interior curvature and corner terms must carry the whole topological charge. On a spherical cap, the boundary circle usually is not a geodesic, and its turning exactly compensates the cap area.
IndexRotation counts become topological

Index Ideas: Turning Of A Field

Index of an isolated zero

Follow a small loop around a zero of a tangent vector field. Normalize the field to unit length and measure how many times its direction winds.

Poincare-Hopf form

\[ \sum_{p:X(p)=0}\operatorname{ind}_p(X)=\chi(M) \]

Introduce index as a rotation count made intrinsic by a moving orthonormal frame. Away from zeros the field has a well-defined angle, and around a zero the angle can wind. Poincare-Hopf says the total winding is forced by topology.
Connection formCurvature is infinitesimal frame holonomy

A Local Frame Reveals Curvature

Moving frame identity

\[ d\omega_{12}=-K\,dA \]

The connection form \(\omega_{12}\) measures tangent-frame rotation; its exterior derivative is curvature area.

Gauss-Bonnet turns a local holonomy calculation into an integral identity over a region.
The connection form measures how a tangent frame turns under parallel transport. Its exterior derivative is curvature area. Integrating around a small loop therefore compares boundary rotation with enclosed curvature, which is the infinitesimal engine behind Gauss-Bonnet and index formulas.
CompletenessGeodesics no longer stop at finite time

Completeness Turns Geodesics Into Global Tools

Hopf-Rinow lens

  • Metric completeness and geodesic completeness agree for connected Riemannian surfaces.
  • Any two points can be joined by a minimizing geodesic.
  • Closed bounded subsets are compact in the induced metric.
Use

Local geodesic equations become global distance probes.

Move from compact surfaces to complete metrics. Hopf-Rinow lets geodesics exist for all time and realizes distances by minimizing geodesics. This changes local geodesic equations into global comparison tools for cut loci, shortest loops, and covering maps.
Geodesic variationCurvature focuses or spreads nearby geodesics

Jacobi Fields Detect Conjugate Points

Surface Jacobi equation

\[ j''(t)+K(\gamma(t))\,j(t)=0 \]

  • \(K>0\): neighboring geodesics tend to refocus.
  • \(K=0\): separation is linear in flat models.
  • \(K<0\): geodesics tend to spread apart.
Explain Jacobi fields as separation vectors between nearby geodesics. On a surface, the scalar equation j double prime plus K j equals zero shows how curvature focuses or defocuses geodesic families. Conjugate points record the failure of geodesic variation to remain locally minimizing.
Covering ideaCompleteness plus \(K\le 0\)

Hadamard: Nonpositive Curvature Unwraps

Global exponential map

For a complete simply connected surface with nonpositive curvature, the exponential map at any point has no conjugate folding. In the standard Hadamard conclusion, it gives global coordinates.

Model conclusion

\(\exp_p:T_pM\to M\) is a global diffeomorphism under the hypotheses.

The Hadamard principle is the chapter's cleanest global upgrade. Completeness prevents geodesics from ending, simple connectivity removes deck ambiguity, and nonpositive curvature prevents conjugate focusing. Together these conditions make the exponential map a global coordinate system.
Curve obstructionTotal turning sees the whole curve

Total Curvature Of Curves

Global curve measurement

\[ \operatorname{TC}(\gamma)=\int_\gamma \kappa\,ds \]

  • Closed curve: total curvature is at least \(2\pi\).
  • Nontrivial knot: total curvature is at least \(4\pi\).
  • The tangent indicatrix on \(S^2\) records the swept turning.
Pause to show a one-dimensional global phenomenon inside the surface chapter. The integral of curvature along a closed space curve controls how much the tangent direction must sweep on the unit sphere. This gives obstruction-style information that is invisible from any short arc.
Intrinsic versus extrinsicZero \(K\) does not settle global shape

Flat And Developable Models

Plane

\(K=0\), simply connected, geodesics are straight lines.

Cylinder

\(K=0\) intrinsically, but one direction wraps globally.

Flat Torus

\(K=0\) with two independent identifications in the metric model.

Use flat surfaces to separate intrinsic geometry from embedding intuition. A plane, cylinder, and flat torus can all have zero Gaussian curvature intrinsically, yet their global identifications and embeddings behave differently. Curvature zero does not mean globally identical.
Global obstructionLocal negative curvature cannot always be completed

Hilbert Obstruction

The collision of requirements

  • Local patches with constant \(K=-1\) can be built.
  • Completeness demands the metric extend indefinitely without missing endpoints.
  • Regular embedding in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) demands a smooth surface without singular edges.
  • Hilbert's theorem says the complete regular embedded model cannot exist.
A local coordinate model is not a global surface until completeness and regularity survive.
Frame Hilbert's theorem as a warning about local models. Patches of constant negative curvature exist, but a complete regular surface in Euclidean three-space cannot realize that metric everywhere. Global completeness and extrinsic regularity collide with the curvature demand.

Sanity Checks And Takeaways

Checks worth running

  • Sphere: numerical \(\int K\,dA\) should approach \(4\pi\).
  • Torus mesh: positive and negative angle defects should sum near zero.
  • Spherical cap: area charge plus boundary charge should return \(2\pi\).
  • Jacobi fan: positive curvature should focus nearby geodesics sooner than flat geometry.
  • Developable model: unroll a cylinder and preserve intrinsic distances.
Takeaway

Global differential geometry is the art of proving that local curvature data must obey the topology and metric completion of the whole surface.
