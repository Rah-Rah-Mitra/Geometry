Regular surface certificate I

A Patch Is Regular When Its Coordinate Velocities Span a Plane

Patch Test
$$x:U\subset R^2\to R^3,\qquad x_u\times x_v\ne 0.$$

What Fails

  • A smooth formula can still have rank loss.
  • A picture can hide a singular point.
  • The cross product is a local certificate, not decoration.
rank two is the local two-dimensional testartifact: regularity-rank-certificates.png
Stress that a parametrization is only a surface chart where its two velocity vectors are independent. The cross product is the visible diagnostic in R3: when it vanishes, the coordinate grid has collapsed and no tangent plane has been certified.
Regular surface certificate II

Implicit Surfaces Are Certified by a Nonzero Gradient

Regular Value Criterion
$$S=F^{-1}(c),\qquad \nabla F(p)\ne 0\quad \text{for }p\in S.$$

Why This Is a Surface

The gradient gives a normal direction; the implicit function theorem then solves one coordinate as a function of the other two.

implicit modelnormal is produced before a parametrization is chosen
Compare the patch certificate with the implicit certificate. A level set F equals c becomes a surface when the gradient does not vanish on that level, because the gradient supplies a normal direction and the implicit function theorem supplies local coordinates.
Coordinate overlap

Chart Changes Make Surface Calculus Independent of Notation

Transition Map
$$x^{-1}\circ y:\ y^{-1}(x(U)\cap y(V))\to U.$$

Atlas Rule

  • Use only overlaps where both charts describe the same surface points.
  • Require the transition and its inverse to be differentiable.
  • Use the Jacobian determinant as a local invertibility audit.
chart overlap as a computable testartifact: stereographic-coordinate-change.png
Coordinate changes are the honesty test for a surface atlas. If two charts describe the same patch of surface, the transition map must be differentiable with an invertible Jacobian, so computations made in one coordinate system can be translated to the other.
Differentiable functions on surfaces

A Surface Function Is Tested Through Charts, Not by a Single Formula

Chart Test
$$f:S\to R\quad \text{is differentiable near }p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\circ x \text{ is differentiable.}$$

Independence Check

If \(y=x\circ \phi\), then \(f\circ y=(f\circ x)\circ \phi\). The transition map carries differentiability between coordinate views.

surface calculus is local but chart-independentappendix: continuity and differentiability tools
A function on a surface is differentiable when it becomes a differentiable ordinary function after composing with every chart. The point is not to privilege a chart, but to make sure the function has the same differentiability status in all compatible coordinates.
Tangent plane

The Same Plane Appears from Coordinates and from Curves

Coordinate Basis
$$T_pS=\operatorname{span}\{x_u(u_0,v_0),x_v(u_0,v_0)\}.$$
Curve Velocity Model

Every tangent vector is \(\alpha'(0)\) for some surface curve \(\alpha(t)\) with \(\alpha(0)=p\).

coordinate span equals curve velocity spaceartifact: regular-surface-patch-normals.png
Present the tangent plane in its two equivalent languages. In coordinates it is spanned by x_u and x_v; intrinsically it is the set of velocities of curves on the surface through the point. The equivalence is what lets geometric arguments and coordinate computations talk to each other.
Differential of a surface map

The Differential Pushes First-Order Motion from One Surface to Another

Curve Definition
$$d\varphi_p(\alpha'(0))=(\varphi\circ \alpha)'(0).$$

Coordinate Computation

In charts, the same map is represented by a Jacobian matrix; chart changes ensure the vector it produces is the same tangent vector.

linear approximation between tangent planeschain rule lives here
Frame the differential as the linear map seen by first-order observers riding on curves. If a surface map sends alpha on S to beta on S prime, then the derivative sends alpha prime at zero to beta prime at zero, and the definition must be independent of the chosen curve.
Metric in coordinates

The First Fundamental Form Turns Tangent Coordinates into Measurements

Metric Matrix
$$I=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix},\quad E=\langle x_u,x_u\rangle,\ F=\langle x_u,x_v\rangle,\ G=\langle x_v,x_v\rangle.$$
QuestionCoordinate Answer
Length\(I(w,w)\)
Angle\(I(w,z)/\sqrt{I(w,w)I(z,z)}\)
Area density\(\sqrt{EG-F^2}\)
ambient dot product pulled back to coordinatesartifact: first-fundamental-form-metric-ellipse.png
The first fundamental form is the imported dot product on each tangent plane. In a chart it becomes a symmetric matrix with entries E, F, and G, and this matrix is the local machine for lengths, angles, orthogonality, and area density.
Reading the metric

Metric Ellipses Show Which Tangent Directions the Surface Stretches

Helicoid Patch
$$x(u,v)=(v\cos u,\ v\sin u,\ au).$$
Metric Coefficients
$$E=v^2+a^2,\qquad F=0,\qquad G=1.$$

The coordinate direction \(u\) sweeps a circle and climbs; its metric cost grows with \(|v|\) and pitch \(a\).

measurement changes before curvature appearsartifact: first-fundamental-form-helicoid-ellipses.png
Use the ellipse as a visual audit of anisotropic stretching. Unit coordinate vectors do not usually remain unit on the surface; the metric ellipse records which tangent directions are stretched more strongly and which directions are closer to orthogonal.
Area from the metric determinant

Area Is the Integrated Size of the Coordinate Parallelogram

Area Formula
$$A(x(R))=\int_R \sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv=\int_R \|x_u\times x_v\|\,du\,dv.$$

Mesh Audit

The torus refinement check verifies that discrete triangle area approaches the analytic value while preserving the expected topology.

area density equals the rank certificate normartifact: torus-area-mesh-refinement.png
Move from measurement of vectors to measurement of patches. The density square root of EG minus F squared is the norm of x_u cross x_v, so the area formula is the same rank certificate integrated over a region with metric scale included.
Local normals and global compatibility

Orientation Asks Whether Local Normal Choices Can Be Glued

Orientation

A regular surface is orientable when one can choose a continuous unit normal \(N(p)\) on the entire surface.

Overlap Test

On chart overlaps, compatible parametrizations must preserve the chosen side of the surface rather than flipping it unpredictably.

local normals exist; global coherence is extraartifact: orientation-normal-coherence.png
Orientation is a global compatibility question, not a local existence question. Every regular patch gives a local normal, but an orientable surface allows those choices to be made coherently across overlaps so that walking around loops does not reverse the chosen normal.
Using orientation

On Compact Oriented Surfaces, Local Area Elements Assemble Without Sign Ambiguity

Bookkeeping Payoff

Consistent normals choose a signed side of the surface, so local integrals can be summed with a common orientation convention.

Boundary Convention

When a boundary is present, the chosen surface orientation determines the positive boundary direction used in later integral theorems.

compactness helps finite patch assemblyorientation prepares intrinsic geometry
Connect orientation to integral bookkeeping. On a compact oriented surface, consistent normal choices let local area elements be assembled without sign ambiguity. When a boundary is present, that normal choice also fixes the positive direction used along the boundary.

Common pitfalls

Most Errors Come from Skipping One Certificate in the Chain

Drawing Instead of Rank

A smooth-looking mesh does not certify a regular surface. Check \(x_u\times x_v\).

One Chart as Global

Coordinates can miss points or distort overlaps. Transition maps do the atlas work.

Wrong Area Element

Coordinate rectangles do not have physical area \(du\,dv\); the metric determinant supplies the scale.

debug the proof by finding the missing certificatechapter-02 audit habit
Make the warning slide practical. Most mistakes in this chapter come from treating a drawing as a proof, using a single chart as if it were global, pushing tangent vectors without checking chart independence, or integrating area as though coordinates were already physical distances.
