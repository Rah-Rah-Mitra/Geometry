Local CertificateVelocity cannot vanish

Regularity Certifies Tangent Geometry

Definition A parametrized curve is regular at \(t_0\) when \[ \alpha'(t_0)\neq 0. \] The tangent line is then \[ \alpha(t_0)+\lambda\,\alpha'(t_0). \]

The derivative is both a geometric direction and a numerical certificate.

Stress that regularity is not a decorative hypothesis. It is the certificate that the tangent line is actually defined at the parameter value under discussion. Once alpha prime is nonzero, the velocity vector gives both a direction on the trace and a denominator for arclength differentiation.
ArclengthMonotone distance along the curve

Arclength Turns Motion Into A Ruler

Construction \[ s(t)=\int_{t_0}^{t}\|\alpha'(u)\|\,du,\qquad ds=\|\alpha'(t)\|\,dt. \] If \(\alpha\) is regular, then \(s(t)\) strictly increases.

Equal increments in \(s\) mean equal distance on the curve, not equal time on the old clock.

Present arclength as a cumulative measuring device attached to the curve. The graph of s against t is the useful object: strict increase comes from positive speed, and that strict increase is exactly what lets the old clock be replaced by distance traveled.
Clock ChangesChain rule versus invariant length

Reparametrization Changes Speed, Not Length

Clock Change Let \(\beta(u)=\alpha(\phi(u))\), with \(\phi'(u)>0\). \[ \beta'(u)=\alpha'(\phi(u))\phi'(u). \] Speed is a clock signal.
Length Check \[ \int\|\beta'(u)\|\,du =\int\|\alpha'(\phi(u))\|\phi'(u)\,du =\int\|\alpha'(t)\|\,dt. \] Length is geometric.
Connect the chain rule to the geometric invariant. A valid clock change rescales velocity by phi prime, so instantaneous speed changes, but the accumulated length does not. This slide prepares the habit of checking which quantities are clock signals and which are geometric signals.
Unit-Speed RepresentativeArclength as parameter

Unit Speed Makes The First Formula Quiet

Unit-Speed Consequence If \(\|\alpha'(s)\|=1\), write \(T(s)=\alpha'(s)\). Then \[ \langle T,T\rangle=1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \langle T',T\rangle=0. \] The tangent can only change in a normal direction.
Once the curve is reparametrized by arclength, the tangent vector has norm one. Differentiate that identity to show why the change in tangent is perpendicular to the tangent itself. This is the clean doorway into normal direction and curvature.
Space CurvesMoving orthonormal frame

Frenet Data Packages Local Motion

Frenet Recipe \[ T=\frac{d\alpha}{ds},\qquad \kappa=\left\|\frac{dT}{ds}\right\|,\qquad N=\frac{T'}{\|T'\|},\qquad B=T\times N. \] For \(\kappa>0\), the frame follows the curve as an adapted coordinate system.
Build the frame in the order a computation would use: tangent from velocity, normal from the derivative of tangent, and binormal from the oriented cross product. The point is not memorizing a triad; it is seeing how local motion becomes an orthonormal coordinate system carried by the curve.
Helix CalibrationConstant local invariants

Curvature And Torsion Are Calibration Signals

For a helix \(\alpha(t)=(a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\) \[ \kappa=\frac{a}{a^2+b^2},\qquad \tau=\frac{b}{a^2+b^2}. \]
Curvature
turning of \(T\) per unit length
Torsion
twisting of the osculating plane
Calibration
constant values should remain constant in samples
Use the helix as the calibration object. Its curvature and torsion are constant, so it gives an immediate sanity check for formulas and numerical samples. Curvature measures tangent turning per unit length, while torsion measures rotation of the osculating plane through the binormal.
Local ShapeTaylor expansion in the Frenet frame

Local Canonical Form Is A Microscope

Frame Coordinates Near \(s=0\) With origin at the point and axes \(T,N,B\), \[ \alpha(s)= \bigg(s,\frac{\kappa(0)}{2}s^2, -\frac{\kappa(0)\tau(0)}{6}s^3\bigg) +\text{higher terms}. \] The first three nonzero orders encode tangent, normal bending, and binormal twist.
Frame coordinates turn Taylor expansion into geometry. The s term records tangent displacement, the s squared term records curvature toward the normal, and the s cubed term begins to reveal torsion through binormal displacement. This is local shape information, not a new parametrization trick.
Second-Order ContactRadius is reciprocal curvature

The Osculating Circle Reads Kappa

Contact Interpretation The osculating circle at a plane point has center \[ C=\alpha(s_0)+\frac{1}{\kappa(s_0)}\,N(s_0). \] It agrees with the curve through second order data: point, tangent, and curvature.
Explain the osculating circle as the circle with second order contact at the chosen point. It shares position, tangent, and curvature direction. A large radius means small bending, and an infinite limiting radius is the geometric picture behind zero curvature.
Plane CurvesLocal curvature integrates to global turning

Plane Curves Carry A Turning Ledger

Tangent Angle For a unit-speed plane curve, \[ T(s)=(\cos\theta(s),\sin\theta(s)),\qquad \kappa_s(s)=\theta'(s). \] The total signed turn is \(\int \kappa_s\,ds\).
For plane curves, the unit tangent can be encoded by a tangent angle theta. Integrating the signed rate of change gives a global turning count. The local curvature signal therefore becomes a ledger for how the whole closed curve winds its tangent direction.
Global Plane-Curve DiagnosticsIntegrate and compare

Global Tests Catch More Than Local Smoothness

Checklist For A Closed Plane Curve

Track rotation index from the tangent angle.
Compare total curvature with expected turning.
Inspect convexity by signed curvature.
Mark vertices where curvature has local extrema.
Move from local definitions to diagnostics that inspect an entire trace. Rotation index, total curvature, convexity, and vertex counts ask whether local bending data is organized globally. The notebook examples are chosen to make these tests visible without relying on copied exercises.
WarningDo not divide by speed before checking speed

Bad Clocks And Singular Samples

Diagnostic Before Geometry \[ T=\frac{\alpha'}{\|\alpha'\|},\qquad \frac{d}{ds}=\frac{1}{\|\alpha'\|}\frac{d}{dt}. \] These expressions are meaningful only where \(\|\alpha'\|>0\).

The speed plot is a safety check for tangent, curvature, and numerical derivatives.

Return to the warning that the same plotted image can hide different parameter behavior. A clock that stops creates zero speed even when the trace looks familiar. The numerical habit is to inspect speed before trusting tangent, curvature, or any derivative divided by speed.
Notebook ArtifactInteractive helix frame

Inspect The 3D Frame, Not Just The Trace

Inspection Targets

  • Rotate until the osculating plane is edge-on.
  • Watch whether \(B\) stays fixed or twists.
  • Compare constant helix invariants with sampled frame residuals.
  • Use the artifact as a geometric debugger for formulas.
The helix frame is useful because torsion is difficult to see in a fixed two dimensional projection. Ask students to rotate the interactive artifact and track how T, N, and B move together. The visual target is the twisting osculating plane, not the helix shape alone.
In-Class AuditShort prompt, not a long exercise

A Short Board Audit

Given a new parametrized curve, classify each statement before computing: clock signal, local invariant, or global diagnostic.

1. Regularity

Where is \(\alpha'(t)\neq 0\)? Mark any parameter values where tangent geometry is not certified.

2. Local Data

After arclength normalization, compute \(T\), \(\kappa\), and, in space, \(\tau\).

3. Global Test

For a closed plane curve, compare total turning, convexity, and curvature extrema.

Use this slide as a compact in-class audit rather than a long exercise. Students should identify which claims depend only on the trace and which depend on the clock. The goal is to make regularity, arclength, curvature, and total turning feel like a checklist for a new curve.
RecapChapter 01 dependency chain

Recap: Local Data, Global Tests

Dependency Chain

  1. Start with \(\alpha(t)\), not only the trace.
  2. Check \(\alpha'(t)\neq 0\).
  3. Use \(s(t)=\int\|\alpha'\|dt\) as distance.
  4. Build \(T,N,B,\kappa,\tau\) when defined.
  5. Read local contact and global turning from those signals.
Close by walking through the dependency chain one last time. Start with a parametrized map, certify regularity, replace the clock by arclength when useful, build Frenet data, read local shape through curvature and torsion, and finally integrate or compare those signals to make global conclusions.
