Gauss Map

A Surface Sends Each Point To Its Unit Normal

Definition

For an oriented regular surface \(S\subset\mathbb{R}^3\), the Gauss map is

\(N:S\to S^2,\quad p\mapsto N(p)\).

The normal field becomes a measurable map into a fixed reference surface.

Stress that the Gauss map does not draw the original surface again; it records the direction of the oriented normal. A small patch whose normals rotate quickly sweeps a larger patch on the sphere, so area distortion becomes a curvature measurement.
Differentiating The Normal

The Normal Changes In Tangent Directions

Key observation

Since \(\langle N,N\rangle=1\), differentiating gives

\(\langle dN_p(v),N(p)\rangle=0.\)

Thus \(dN_p(v)\) lies in the tangent plane at the normal point, which is canonically parallel to \(T_pS\).

Convention

The chapter reads the Weingarten map as \(S_p(v)=-dN_p(v)\), so convex outward normals give positive principal curvature.

Explain the minus sign in the shape operator as a convention that aligns convex surfaces with positive principal curvatures for the outward normal. The derivative of the normal is tangent-valued because the normal has constant length, so all bending information returns to the tangent plane.
Shape Operator

A Linear Bending Map On Each Tangent Plane

Coordinate ledger

First form: \(I=[g_{ij}]\)

Second form: \(II=[b_{ij}]\)

\(S=g^{-1}b\)

The metric lowers and raises indices; normal acceleration supplies the entries of \(b\).

Interpretation

\(S_p(v)\) is large when a small move in direction \(v\) produces a rapid rotation of the normal.

Present the shape operator as the linear map that compares how tangent directions change the normal. In coordinates it is obtained by raising an index of the second fundamental form with the first fundamental form, which separates metric measurement from normal acceleration.
Self-Adjointness

Principal Directions Are Orthogonal Eigen-Axes

Spectral theorem in the tangent plane

\(\langle S(u),v\rangle=\langle u,S(v)\rangle\)

\(S(e_i)=k_i e_i\)

At non-umbilic points the two eigendirections are distinguished and perpendicular.

This slide is the linear algebra hinge of the chapter. Because the shape operator is self-adjoint for the first fundamental form, its eigenvectors can be chosen orthogonal, and the surface receives two principal directions except at umbilic points.
Curvature Invariants

Determinant And Trace Survive The Coordinate Choice

Eigenvalue summary

\(K=k_1k_2=\det S\)

\(H=\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}S\)

One number measures signed area distortion; the other measures average normal bending.

\(K>0\)same-sign bending
\(K<0\)opposite-sign bending
\(H=0\)balanced principal curvatures
Move from eigenvalues to invariant summaries. Gaussian curvature is the determinant of the shape operator and records signed area distortion of the Gauss map, while mean curvature is half the trace and records the average normal bending.
Sign Diagnostics

Read The Local Shape From The Signs Of \(k_1\) And \(k_2\)

Elliptic

Both principal curvatures have the same sign.

Hyperbolic

Principal curvatures have opposite signs.

Parabolic

One principal curvature vanishes to first order.

Use the three panels as a visual taxonomy rather than a proof. Positive Gaussian curvature means both principal curvatures have the same sign, negative curvature means the surface bends in opposite senses, and zero curvature means one principal direction is locally flat to first order.
Normal Curvature

The Dupin Indicatrix Shows Which Directions Bend

Quadratic form

\(k_n(v)=\frac{II(v,v)}{I(v,v)}\)

In principal coordinates, \(k_n(\theta)=k_1\cos^2\theta+k_2\sin^2\theta\).

Zeroes of \(k_n\) are asymptotic directions.

The Dupin indicatrix translates the quadratic normal curvature form into a plane picture. Elliptic points produce closed level curves, hyperbolic points produce crossed branches with asymptotic directions, and parabolic points sit at the boundary between these behaviors.
Coordinate Formulas

A Graph Patch Turns Curvature Into A Matrix Ledger

For \(X(u,v)=(u,v,f(u,v))\)
\(I=\begin{bmatrix}1+f_u^2&f_uf_v\\ f_uf_v&1+f_v^2\end{bmatrix}\)
\(II=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f|^2}}\begin{bmatrix}f_{uu}&f_{uv}\\ f_{uv}&f_{vv}\end{bmatrix}\)

At a critical point of \(f\), \(I\) is the identity and \(S\) is the Hessian.

For a graph patch, the formulas expose what the notebook computations are checking. First derivatives control the metric denominator, second derivatives control normal acceleration, and at a critical point the Hessian eigenvalues directly become the principal curvatures.
Umbilics

When \(k_1=k_2\), Every Direction Is Principal

Degenerate eigen-data

\(S_p=kI\)

Curvature may be nonzero, but the eigenbasis is not unique. Principal-line diagrams need special care near these points.

At an umbilic the shape operator is a scalar multiple of the identity, so every tangent direction is principal. The difficulty is not that curvature disappears, but that the principal-direction line field loses a preferred pair of axes and needs a separate local analysis.
Calibration Examples

Four Models Prevent Formula Drift

Sphere

\(k_1=k_2=1/r\)

Cylinder

\(k_1=1/r,\ k_2=0\)

Saddle

\(k_1k_2<0\)

Minimal

\(H=0\)

Compare the sphere, cylinder, saddle, and minimal-surface model as calibration tests. The same shape-operator ledger explains constant positive curvature, one zero principal curvature, opposite-sign curvatures, and vanishing mean curvature without requiring unrelated memorized cases or special notation.
Direction Fields

Eigenvectors Integrate Into Surface Curves

Curve-level reading

Principal curves track maximum and minimum normal curvature directions.

Asymptotic curves exist on hyperbolic regions and keep normal curvature zero along their tangent direction.

Once principal directions are found at each point, they form line fields that can be integrated where no degeneracy intervenes. Lines of curvature follow eigenvectors, while asymptotic lines follow directions of zero normal curvature on hyperbolic regions.
Computational Diagnostics

Checks That Catch Curvature Mistakes

Unit normal: verify \(\|N\|=1\) before differentiating.
Tangent derivative: check \(\langle dN(v),N\rangle=0\).
Metric symmetry: test \(\langle Su,v\rangle=\langle u,Sv\rangle\).
Invariant match: compare eigenvalues with \(\det S\) and \(\operatorname{tr}S\).
Sign stability: perturb samples and reclassify \(K\).
Visual audit: compare principal axes with observed normal rotation.

Applied Lab

Design A Curvature Sensor For Sampled Surface Data

Task
  1. Estimate tangent planes and normals from local samples.
  2. Fit first and second fundamental forms in local coordinates.
  3. Build \(S=g^{-1}b\) and enforce metric self-adjointness.
  4. Report \(K\), \(H\), principal axes, and confidence flags.
Failure mode

A nonsymmetric numerical \(S\) is often a coordinate or metric bug, not new geometry.

Invite students to treat curvature as an instrument design problem. Given sampled graph data, estimate the first and second fundamental forms, symmetrize the shape operator with respect to the metric, and compare the resulting signs with geometric inspection of the mesh.
Recap

The Gauss Map Turns Bending Into Linear Algebra

Core chain

\(N \longrightarrow -dN=S \longrightarrow (k_1,k_2) \longrightarrow K,H\)

Normal rotation becomes an operator; the operator becomes eigen-data; eigen-data becomes curvature classification.

What to remember
  • The Gauss map is a spherical record of surface normals.
  • The shape operator is self-adjoint for the surface metric.
  • Principal directions are eigenvectors, except at umbilics.
  • \(K\), \(H\), and \(II(v,v)\) are complementary diagnostics.
Close by tracing the continuous story. The Gauss map measures normal rotation, the shape operator differentiates it inside the tangent plane, self-adjointness produces principal axes, and determinant, trace, and quadratic forms turn bending into diagnostics for points and curves.
