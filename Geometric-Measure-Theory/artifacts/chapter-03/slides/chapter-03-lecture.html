Definition Pattern

Ordinary Differentiability Is A Vanishing Error Ratio

first order data
Definition

For \(f:A\subset X\to Y\), differentiability at an interior point \(a\) means there is a continuous linear map \(L:X\to Y\) such that

\(\displaystyle \lim_{x\to a}\frac{\|f(x)-f(a)-L(x-a)\|}{\|x-a\|}=0.\)
Interpretation
  • The numerator is the nonlinear residue after subtracting the candidate linear map.
  • The denominator makes the test invariant under first-order zoom.
  • The limit must hold through the whole ambient neighborhood.
Spend time on the quotient in the formula: the error is measured against the distance to the base point. A derivative is not just a best linear map, but a stable limit seen after rescaling the graph around a point while the nonlinear part disappears.
Measure-Theoretic Upgrade

Approximate Limits Ignore Null Dust, Not Bad Geometry

density decides typicality
Approximate Limit

\(\operatorname{ap}\lim_{x\to a} g(x)=b\) means the values that do not approach \(b\) occupy density zero at \(a\).

Approximate Differential

A map \(f:A\subset \mathbb{R}^m\to Y\) has approximate differential \(L\) at \(a\) when

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{ap}\lim_{x\to a}\frac{\|f(x)-f(a)-L(x-a)\|}{\|x-a\|}=0.\)
Warning

Approximate differentiability still asserts a first-order linear blow-up. It only changes which nearby samples are allowed to be negligible.

Approximate limits are the measure-theoretic compromise that makes differentiation useful on rough domains. The exceptional dust can be ignored only when it has density zero at the point. Stress that the word approximate is rigorous, not informal, because the density test decides which samples count.
Theorem Orientation

Coordinate Data Reassembles The Approximate Differential

slicing + measurability
Almost-Everywhere Assembly

For a measurable \(f:\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n\), approximate partial derivatives are measurable on their domains, and at almost every point where all coordinate pieces exist they assemble into

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{ap}Df(a)v=\sum_{i=1}^{m} v_i\,\operatorname{ap}D_i f(a).\)
Proof Scaffold
  1. Use one-dimensional slices to locate coordinate approximate limits.
  2. Control exceptional fibers through product measure estimates.
  3. Recover a linear map by testing every direction \(v\).
The important theorem orientation is that coordinate information can be reassembled into a linear map at almost every point where the approximate partial derivatives exist. The slide should make students see why measurability and Fubini-style slicing enter before any tangent-plane picture becomes legitimate.
Core Payoff

Lipschitz Control Forces Differentiability Almost Everywhere

Rademacher principle
Theorem

If \(f:\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n\) is Lipschitz, then \(f\) is differentiable at \(\mathcal{L}^m\)-almost every point.

Why The Hypothesis Is Strong

The same constant \(M\) controls all small increments:

\(\|f(x)-f(y)\|\le M\|x-y\|.\)

That bound prevents the exceptional set from filling a positive-density cloud around typical points.

slices approximate differential density upgrade ordinary derivative
Rademacher's theorem is the first major payoff. A Lipschitz bound gives uniform control in every small ball, so one-dimensional differentiability along slices can be promoted to an approximate differential and then to an ordinary differential at almost every point. The proof strategy matters more here than the full technical proof.
From Pointwise Growth To Pieces

Finite Local Slope Organizes A Map Into Lipschitz Pieces

Stepanoff-type move
Local Condition

On a set \(A\), suppose the upper first-order growth is finite at the points being studied:

\(\displaystyle \limsup_{x\to a}\frac{\|f(x)-f(a)\|}{\|x-a\|}<\infty.\)
Conclusion Shape

The set can be covered by countably many measurable pieces on which \(f\) behaves with a concrete Lipschitz constant. Rademacher then applies piece by piece.

Common Trap

Finite slope at each point is not the same as one global Lipschitz constant; the theorem wins by sorting points into controlled layers.

This slide separates local finite slope from global Lipschitz behavior. Stepanoff-type reasoning says that if the pointwise upper slope is finite on a set, the set can be organized into countably many pieces where the map behaves Lipschitzly. That decomposition is a recurring move in rectifiability.
Beyond First Order

Higher Differentials Are Taylor Jets With Normed Remainders

symmetric multilinear data
Jet At A Point

The \(k\)-jet records the value and the multilinear pieces

\(\displaystyle J_a^k f = \big(f(a),Df(a),D^2f(a),\ldots,D^k f(a)\big).\)
Taylor Test

A jet is meaningful when the polynomial built from those pieces predicts \(f(x)\) with a remainder smaller than \(\|x-a\|^k\) at the prescribed order.

Why GMT Needs It

Extension, flattening, and generic factorization arguments need controlled smooth data even when the original information is specified on a closed or thin set.

Higher differentials should be presented as bookkeeping for Taylor jets, not as a new mystery. Each order records a symmetric multilinear piece, and the remainder condition says whether the jet genuinely approximates the function. This prepares Whitney extension without needing the audience to memorize the full compatibility formula.
Extension Theorem Orientation

Whitney Extension Glues Compatible Local Predictions

closed-set data
Problem

A closed set \(A\subset\mathbb{R}^m\) carries a polynomial \(P_a\) at each \(a\in A\). When do these local Taylor predictions come from one ambient \(C^k\) function?

Answer Shape

Compatibility demands that nearby jets predict each other to the right order. A smooth partition of unity then blends the local polynomials without destroying the estimates.

Geometric Role

Extension theorems let proofs leave a rough or closed set, perform smooth operations in the ambient space, and return with controlled derivatives.

Whitney extension is a repair theorem for data known only on a closed set. The compatibility tests prevent neighboring jets from contradicting each other, while a smooth partition of unity glues local polynomial predictions into one ambient function. Emphasize that the theorem creates ambient smoothness from local coherent data.
Blow-Up Geometry

Tangent Cones Record Directions That Survive Under Zoom

arbitrary subsets
Definition

A vector \(v\) lies in \(\operatorname{Tan}(S,a)\) if rescaled secants from \(a\) to points of \(S\) can approach \(v\).

Basic Properties
  • The tangent cone is closed.
  • It is stable under multiplication by nonnegative scalars.
  • For smooth submanifolds it is the usual tangent space.
Map Interaction

At generic points of a smooth map, tangent directions to level sets are kernels, while tangent directions to images are images of the derivative.

Tangent cones turn zooming into a definition that works for arbitrary subsets, not only smooth manifolds. The cone keeps every direction that can be approached by rescaled secants. For a smooth submanifold it collapses to the usual tangent space, which is why the definition is worth the abstraction.
Differentiating On A Set

Relative Differentiation Restricts Ambient Linear Data

domain is geometry
Relative Differential

For \(f:S\to Y\) and \(a\in \operatorname{Clos}S\), choose a local ambient extension \(g\) differentiable at \(a\). Then

\(\displaystyle D_S f(a)=Dg(a)\big|_{\operatorname{Tan}(S,a)}.\)
Submanifold Case

When \(S=B\) is a \(C^1\) submanifold, relative differentiation matches the coordinate-chart derivative and can also be expressed through a local retraction onto \(B\).

Key Point

Only tangent directions to the domain are tested. Ambient directions that never occur in \(S\) do not belong to the derivative's domain.

Relative differentiation answers the practical question of how to differentiate a function whose domain is itself a set. One extends locally to the ambient space, differentiates there, and then restricts the linear map to the tangent cone. On a submanifold this agrees with the coordinate-chart picture students already know.
