Alternating \(k\)-Linear Maps

Orientation as sign
Alternating form
A \(k\)-form is a map \(\alpha:V^k\to \mathbb R\) that is linear in each slot and changes sign when two slots are exchanged.

Immediate Tests

$$\alpha(\ldots,v_i,\ldots,v_j,\ldots)=-\alpha(\ldots,v_j,\ldots,v_i,\ldots).$$
$$\alpha(v_1,\ldots,v_k)=0\quad\text{if two inputs agree.}$$

The sign convention is the algebraic source of oriented cancellation in boundaries and currents.

Inspection target: linearity in each slot plus sign reversal under swaps Failure mode: repeated direction has positive area
Slow down on alternation because it is the first place where orientation becomes algebraic. Multilinearity says each slot responds linearly, while alternation says swapping two directions reverses sign and repeating a direction kills volume. The parallelogram picture is a concrete way to see both facts before moving to abstract k slots.

Exterior Algebra Encodes Oriented Volume

Decomposable versus summed
Simple \(k\)-vector
$$\xi=v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k\in \Lambda_k V.$$

Coordinates

$$v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k=\sum_I \det(V_I)\,e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_k}.$$

Each determinant is a signed projected \(k\)-volume. A general \(k\)-vector can sum several projected pieces, while a simple one comes from a single oriented \(k\)-plane.

Simple pieces model tangent planes; sums model algebraic superposition Coordinates are signed minors
Emphasize that a decomposable k-vector is an oriented k-plane with magnitude, while a general k-vector may be a sum of such pieces. The determinant coefficients are not bookkeeping trivia; they are the coordinates of oriented projected volumes. This is the algebraic seed of approximate tangent planes and rectifiable pieces later in the course.

Pullback Reads Minors

Jacobian information by degree
Degree \(k\) pullback
$$A^*(dy_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_{i_k}) =\sum_J \det(A_{I,J})\,dx_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{j_k}.$$

Interpretation

The \(k\)-minors of \(A\) are exactly the signed changes in \(k\)-dimensional projected volume.

Parametrized surfaces and rectifiable charts use this operation to evaluate ambient forms on local tangent data.

Inspection target: minors, signs, and degree Allowed perturbation: change the matrix, then recompute all selected minors
Present pullback as the operation that lets measurements travel backward through parametrizations. The matrix of a map produces minors, and those minors are exactly the signed area or k-volume changes seen by exterior forms. This is where ordinary Jacobians widen into the family of all oriented coordinate projections.

Mass And Comass Are Dual Gauges

Size control
Comass of a \(k\)-covector
$$\|\alpha\|_*=\sup\{|\alpha(\xi)|:\xi \text{ simple},\ |\xi|=1\}.$$
Mass of a \(k\)-vector
$$\|\xi\|=\sup\{|\alpha(\xi)|:\|\alpha\|_*\le 1\}.$$

The supremum is over simple unit inputs. That restriction is what keeps the gauge tied to geometric tangent planes rather than arbitrary coordinates.

Mass bounds currents; comass bounds test forms Duality: size is read through the pairing
Contrast mass and comass as two ways to read the same pairing. Comass asks how large a covector can be on every unit simple input, while mass is the dual gauge on multivectors. This prepares students for currents, where algebraic size must be controlled even when the underlying geometric set is irregular.

Orientation And Boundary Signs

Alternating convention in action
Simplex boundary
$$\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_k]=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^i[v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_i,\ldots,v_k].$$

Why It Matters

When two oriented cells share a face, opposite induced orientations cancel. This is the combinatorial ancestor of boundary cancellation for currents.

A sign error creates a false boundary. The diagnostic question is whether shared interior pieces disappear under the chosen orientations.

Inspection target: shared faces cancel Failure mode: an interior face survives
Boundary signs should be treated as an orientation test rather than a formula to memorize. Walking around the triangle shows why the middle edge receives the opposite sign. Later, when currents have boundaries, this same alternating convention is what makes shared faces cancel instead of being counted twice.

Failure Cases Students Should Name

Hypothesis audit

Not Linear

A slot responds quadratically, so splitting a vector no longer splits the measurement.

Not Alternating

Repeated directions create nonzero volume, so the object no longer measures \(k\)-dimensional spread.

Not Finite

The diagnostic loses boundedness, which signals that mass or comass control has failed.

Wrong Sign

Shared faces reinforce instead of canceling, creating a boundary where none should remain.

Instructor prompt

For each failure, ask which hypothesis broke, what visible symptom appears, and which later GMT construction would become unreliable.

Inspection target: named hypotheses, not vague mistakes Use failures to motivate definitions
Use the four failure cards as a short oral quiz. Each one breaks a different part of the contract: linear response, orientation sensitivity, finiteness, or boundary consistency. Students should be able to name the failing hypothesis and predict what visible or numerical symptom would appear in the diagram or diagnostic.

Instructor Exercise: One More Sample

Lab extension
Exercise

Choose two independent vectors \(v,w\in\mathbb R^2\), apply a \(2\times2\) matrix \(A\), and compare \(A v\wedge A w\) with \(\det(A)(v\wedge w)\). Then orient a triangle using the transformed edge data and compute its boundary signs.

Expected Submission

  • Named object: the simple \(2\)-vector \(v\wedge w\).
  • Protected invariant: determinant-scaled oriented area.
  • Hypothesis: \(A\) is the allowed linear perturbation.
  • Boundary check: interior edge cancellation remains signed.
interactive-lab.html Prompt: object, invariant, hypothesis, boundary case
Frame the exercise as a studio task, not a long problem set. The students choose a concrete two-vector input, apply a small matrix, compute the determinant change, and then check a boundary sign. The important assessment is whether their written diagnostic names the protected invariant and the hypothesis it used.
