Definition

A Current Is A Test-Form Functional

The notation is deliberately dual: \(T\) acts on \(k\)-forms, while the geometry of \(T\) may be a curve, surface, chain, or rough rectifiable set.

Definition 5.1

Let \(U\subset \mathbb{R}^n\). A \(k\)-current \(T\) on \(U\) is a continuous linear map from compactly supported smooth \(k\)-forms to real numbers:

\(T:\mathcal{D}^k(U)\to \mathbb{R},\qquad \omega\mapsto T(\omega).\)

Continuity is taken with respect to the usual test-form topology: supports stay in a compact set and all derivatives converge uniformly.

Reading The Notation
  • \(\omega\) is the probe: a smooth \(k\)-form with compact support.
  • \(T(\omega)\) is the measured response.
  • Dimension \(k\) is the degree of the forms being tested.
  • Support records where tests can produce nonzero response.
Functional languageCurrents generalize chains and submanifolds
Pause on the reversal of perspective. Instead of starting with a set and asking which points it contains, we start with a rule that answers every smooth compactly supported form. That rule can come from a smooth manifold, a polygonal chain, or a rectifiable set with multiplicity, but the definition itself lives in the dual space.

Mass

The Size Of A Current Is Tested By Comass

Mass is the current analogue of total variation. It measures the largest response to bounded test forms and localizes to a mass measure when finite.

Comass Bound

For a smooth \(k\)-form \(\omega\), the comass norm controls the largest value on unit simple \(k\)-vectors.

\(\|\omega\|_\ast=\sup\{|\langle\omega,\xi\rangle|:\xi\text{ simple}, |\xi|\le 1\}\)

A finite-mass current obeys \(|T(\omega)|\le M(T)\|\omega\|_\ast\) for compactly supported probes.

Mass Measure

Finite mass gives a Radon measure \(\|T\|\) with local estimates on open sets \(V\subset U\).

\(M_V(T)=\|T\|(V)\)
  • For a smooth oriented curve, mass is length with multiplicity.
  • For a surface, mass is area with density.
  • For a chain, mass adds absolute weights of pieces.
Estimate before compactnessMass bounds are the lecture's main quantitative invariant
Emphasize that mass is not found by choosing one favorite form. It is a supremum over all forms whose comass is controlled, so it captures the largest possible signed response. For rectifiable examples this collapses to familiar length, area, or weighted volume, but the definition is robust enough for weak limits.
Boundary

The Boundary Is Defined By Moving \(d\) To The Test Form

The boundary of a current is another current. It is built to make Stokes' theorem true by definition, even when the geometric object is rough.

Definition 5.2

For a \(k\)-current \(T\), its boundary is the \((k-1)\)-current

\((\partial T)(\eta)=T(d\eta)\)

Here \(\eta\) is a compactly supported smooth \((k-1)\)-form. Since \(d^2=0\), boundaries have no boundary: \(\partial(\partial T)=0\).

Algebraic Stokes principleBoundary checks detect missing orientation data
Treat the formula as the exact current-level version of Stokes' theorem. The exterior derivative belongs to the test form, while the boundary belongs to the current. In examples, endpoint signs and edge cancellations are not cosmetic bookkeeping; they are the data that make partial of partial equal zero.
Rectifiable Structure

Integral Currents Add Integer Multiplicity

Rectifiability is the bridge from arbitrary functionals back to geometric objects with approximate tangent planes almost everywhere.

Carrier

A countably \(k\)-rectifiable set \(M\) is covered, up to \(\mathcal{H}^k\)-null error, by countably many Lipschitz images.

\(M\approx \bigcup_i f_i(A_i)\)
Orientation

An approximate unit simple \(k\)-vector \(\xi(x)\) fixes the sign of the pairing at almost every point of \(M\).

\(\langle\omega(x),\xi(x)\rangle\)
Multiplicity

An integer density \(\theta(x)\) counts sheets with sign and weight. Integral currents require integer rectifiability and finite boundary mass.

\(\theta(x)\in\mathbb{Z}\)

Canonical local model: \(T(\omega)=\int_M \theta(x)\langle\omega(x),\xi(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^k(x)\). This is the original geometric picture that the functional definition protects under limiting operations.

Rectifiable currentsCarrier plus orientation plus multiplicity
Make the three pieces explicit because students often remember only the support. The support tells where the current lives, the orientation tells how forms are signed, and the multiplicity tells how many sheets pass through. Integral currents impose an arithmetic condition, which is why they are suited to compactness and minimization problems.
Maps

Pushforward Transports Currents By Pulling Back Tests

Currents move naturally under Lipschitz maps. The map acts on the current by composing test forms with the pullback.

Definition 5.3

For a proper Lipschitz map \(f:U\to V\), the pushforward current is defined by

\((f_\#T)(\omega)=T(f^\#\omega).\)
  • Dimension is preserved when the differential has full rank on the active tangent planes.
  • Mass is controlled by the Lipschitz constant to the \(k\)-th power.
  • Boundary commutes with pushforward: \(\partial(f_\#T)=f_\#(\partial T)\).
Functorial behaviorMove geometry by testing against pulled-back forms
The pushforward formula is a useful antidote to vague language about moving a rough surface. We do not need to track every visual point first; we define the transported current by how it responds to a test form in the target. The pullback places that form back on the original current, where the old rule already applies.
Limits

Weak Convergence Tests Every Form Separately

Currents are designed for compactness: a sequence may not converge as sets, but its responses to every test form can converge.

Weak Convergence

A sequence \(T_j\) converges weakly to \(T\) when

\(T_j(\omega)\to T(\omega)\quad\text{for every }\omega\in\mathcal{D}^k(U).\)

Oscillations may vanish if all compactly supported smooth tests average them out.

Compactness Template

Uniform local bounds on \(M(T_j)\) and \(M(\partial T_j)\), together with tight support control, produce subsequential weak convergence.

\(M(T_j)+M(\partial T_j)\le C\)

The limit remains in the intended class when rectifiability and integrality are protected by the compactness theorem.

Stress the operational meaning of weak convergence. To verify it, the instructor fixes one smooth compactly supported form at a time and watches the numbers converge. The topology is intentionally less rigid than Hausdorff convergence, which is why compactness becomes possible, but the mass and boundary estimates are needed to prevent meaningless escape.
Flat Norm

Close Currents May Differ By A Small Error And A Small Filling

The flat norm measures whether a difference can be decomposed into a small residual current plus the boundary of a small higher-dimensional filling.

Flat Decomposition

For two \(k\)-currents \(T\) and \(S\), search for currents \(R\) and \(Q\) satisfying

\(T-S=R+\partial Q\)
\(\mathcal{F}(T-S)=\inf\{M(R)+M(Q)\}\)

The filling \(Q\) explains geometric displacement; the residual \(R\) explains leftover unmatched mass.

Flat distanceGeometry plus homological cancellation
Flat distance is best introduced as an accounting scheme. If two curves are slightly displaced, their difference may be the boundary of a narrow strip, so the relevant cost is the strip area rather than the full length twice. This prepares students for variational arguments where minimizing sequences move while retaining controlled filling cost.

Warnings

Currents Are Not Merely Sets With Fancy Notation

The same visible subset can define different currents. Orientation, density, boundary, and test-form topology all matter.

Same Set, Different Sign

Reversing orientation changes \(T(\omega)\) to \(-T(\omega)\) for many probes, while the support remains unchanged.

Cancellation Is Real

Two sheets with opposite orientation can cancel as currents. A drawing that counts both sheets without signs misses the analysis.

Mass Is Not Weakly Continuous

Mass is lower semicontinuous under good hypotheses, but oscillation and cancellation can make naive equalities false.

Quick Check

If a proposed argument mentions only the support of \(T\), ask which test forms are being paired, what orientation is used, what multiplicity is present, and whether boundary mass is controlled.

Common mistakesThe functional data is part of the geometry
This is the slide to slow down and prevent several future errors. A current is not determined by a picture of its support unless the orientation and multiplicity are also understood. Likewise, weak convergence is not the same thing as convergence of masses. Keep asking what each test form would measure.
