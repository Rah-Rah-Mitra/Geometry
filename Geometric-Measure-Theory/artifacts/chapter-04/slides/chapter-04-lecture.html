Three tests for a geometric set

Covering test

Can \(E \subset \mathbb{R}^n\) be covered up to \(\mathcal{H}^m\)-null error by countably many Lipschitz images of \(\mathbb{R}^m\)?

Microscope test

At almost every point, do density ratios settle and do rescaled views look increasingly like an \(m\)-plane?

Boundary test

When the pieces are oriented and weighted, do shared faces cancel so the remaining boundary is the intended one?

Definition scaffold

Rectifiable means countably charted, not smooth

Terminology orientation: Lipschitz maps, Hausdorff measure, equality of measures on rectifiable pieces.
Working definition

A set \(E\) is \(m\)-rectifiable when, modulo \(\mathcal{H}^m\)-null error, it lies in a countable union of Lipschitz images \(f_i(A_i)\), with \(A_i \subset \mathbb{R}^m\).

\( \mathcal{H}^m\!\left(E \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} f_i(A_i)\right)=0 \)

Projection to remember

Rectifiability is a measure statement with charts. Smooth manifolds are examples, but finite unions of rough Lipschitz graphs are already inside the theory.

Countable covering tolerates negligible residue; it does not demand a single global parameterization.

Lipschitz chartsHausdorff measure is the bookkeeping measure
Emphasize that the word countable does real work. A rectifiable set may have many pieces, seams, corners, and a negligible remainder. What it cannot have, in positive m-dimensional measure, is geometry that refuses all Lipschitz chart descriptions at the scale being measured.

Treat density as the first microscope before discussing tangent planes. Students often try to recognize a smooth curve by eye, but the measure-theoretic test asks how mass grows inside shrinking balls. Stable normalized growth is the quantitative hint that an m-dimensional object is present.
Blow-up principle

Approximate tangents are limiting flat pictures

Source orientation: tangential properties of Suslin sets; rectifiability and density.

The final picture is flat in measure, not necessarily pointwise on every sample.

Approximate tangent

A plane \(T_xE\) is detected when most of \(E\cap B(x,r)\), after rescaling by \(1/r\), concentrates near that plane as \(r\) shrinks.

Rectifiable direction

For an \(m\)-rectifiable set of locally finite \(\mathcal{H}^m\)-measure, the approximate tangent plane exists for \(\mathcal{H}^m\)-almost every point.

Do not overread

A tangent plane is an almost-everywhere statement. A corner or a seam may exist, but a positive-measure cloud of incoherent tangents breaks the claim.

Approximate tangent planesBlow-up view of local flatness
Connect this to the density slide. Density says the right amount of mass is present; the tangent test asks whether that mass has a coherent direction. The qualifier approximate matters because isolated bad samples and negligible seams do not control the almost-everywhere geometry of the set.
Area and coarea lens

Lipschitz images carry mass through Jacobians

Source orientation: area and coarea of Lipschitz maps; projections of rectifiable sets.
Area formula memory

For a Lipschitz parameterization \(f:A\to\mathbb{R}^n\), the Jacobian measures how \(m\)-dimensional volume is distorted, while multiplicity records how many preimages land at the same point.

\( \int_A J_m f(x)\,dx \;\leadsto\; \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} N(f,A,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^m(y) \)

Why this belongs here

Rectifiability lets measure on a rough set be computed chart by chart, then reconciled across overlaps by multiplicity and null-set bookkeeping.

The same image point may be counted more than once when a chart folds over itself.

Area formula orientationCharts make rough mass computable
Present this as the computational bridge rather than a theorem to memorize. If the set is built from Lipschitz charts, then m-dimensional mass should be computable by changing variables on those charts. The Jacobian measures stretching, and the multiplicity term prevents folded sheets from being undercounted.
Projection criteria

Good shadows can certify visible geometry

Source orientation: rectifiability and projections; examples of unrectifiable sets.

Projection tests convert invisible local direction into visible shadows.

Rectifiable behavior

For most relevant directions, a rectifiable set has projections whose measured shadows match the tangent information and area formula expectations.

Failure mode

A purely unrectifiable component can carry mass while projecting too thinly in many directions. It has measure without coherent chart geometry.

Instructor checkpoint

Ask: if I only saw a family of shadows, could I infer a countable collection of tangent directions, or only scattered mass?

Projection diagnosticsRectifiable versus purely unrectifiable behavior
Use projections to give students a second kind of evidence. A rectifiable set is not merely large; it casts shadows in ways compatible with tangent planes and area. The contrasting dust picture is deliberately schematic, showing why positive measure alone does not force rectifiability.
From sets to currents

Orientation turns a rectifiable set into a tester

Notebook vocabulary: oriented pieces, rectifiability, currents, and boundary checks.
Rectifiable current model

A current packages an oriented rectifiable set \(E\), an integer multiplicity \(\theta\), and an integration rule against smooth compactly supported forms.

\( T(\omega) = \int_E \langle \omega(x), \tau(x) \rangle\, \theta(x)\, d\mathcal{H}^m(x) \)

Why orientation matters

The same geometric support can represent different algebraic objects. Direction decides whether adjacent pieces reinforce or cancel under boundary.

A current is not just a set; it is a rule for measuring forms on that set.

Rectifiable currentsOrientation + multiplicity + integration
This is the conceptual handoff from rectifiable sets to homological integration language. The set supplies the support, but the current is an operator: it eats a test form and returns a number. Orientation and multiplicity are the extra data that make boundary algebra possible.
Boundary check

Cancellation is the algebra behind gluing

Notebook prompt: name the invariant, vary the parameter, and describe a boundary case where a hypothesis matters.

Interior faces cancel when adjacent orientations are compatible.

\( \partial(T_1+T_2)=\partial T_1+\partial T_2 \)

Invariant

Gluing pieces should not invent an interior boundary. Compatible orientations make interior edges appear twice with opposite signs.

Boundary failure

If a piece is missing, oriented incorrectly, or weighted differently, the interior edge survives as a defect current.

Lab question

Which hypothesis protects cancellation: local finiteness, orientation agreement, integer multiplicity, or finite mass?

Boundary operatorCurrent algebra checks the geometry
Slow down on this slide because it makes the current language tangible. The boundary operator is not an extra decoration; it records whether the oriented geometry was assembled consistently. If an internal edge remains after gluing, the algebra has detected a real geometric defect.
