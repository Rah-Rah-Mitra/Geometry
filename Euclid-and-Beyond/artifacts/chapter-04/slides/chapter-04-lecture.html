Segment Arithmetic

How addition, multiplication, units, and coordinates can be built from ruler geometry and similar triangles.

Notebook: Euclid-and-Beyond/chapter-04-segment-arithmetic/04-segment-arithmetic.ipynb Source: Euclid-and-Beyond/Euclid and Beyond.pdf
Open by making the chapter feel like a construction project rather than a list of formulas. We already know how to talk about congruent segments, order, and triangles; now those tools are asked to manufacture arithmetic. The key promise is that coordinates will not be imported from algebra but recovered from geometry by choosing a unit and proving the operations behave like a field.
Chapter Question

When does a line of segments become a number system?

The chapter treats marked segments as the raw material for arithmetic, not as already-numbered lengths.

The target structure

  • Segment addition comes from laying one representative after another.
  • Multiplication is encoded by a similar-triangle machine.
  • A chosen unit segment sets the scale of every coordinate.
  • The coordinate theorem checks that incidence and betweenness survive the translation.
Segment arithmetic begins as geometry on a ray.Slide 2 / 18
The guiding question should be heard literally: before coordinates are available, the only legitimate inputs are points, segments, congruence, betweenness, and constructions. Addition is intuitive because one can place segments end to end. Multiplication is the deeper move, because it uses similarity to turn a two-dimensional construction into a one-dimensional product.

Construction 1

Addition Is Concatenation With A Memory Of Order

Definition By Laying Off

Place a representative of a from the origin, then place a representative of b starting at its endpoint. The new endpoint represents a + b.

The operation is well-defined because congruent replacements give congruent total segments.

Addition is the operation least hidden from the picture.Slide 4 / 18
This construction is deliberately simple, because it establishes the pattern for the rest of the chapter. A segment is represented by a placement on a chosen ray, but the arithmetic object is the congruence class rather than the drawing. The proof obligation is to show that replacing either summand by a congruent copy changes only the representative, not the resulting sum.
Order

Betweenness Supplies The Ordered Part Of Arithmetic

Segment comparison

  • a < b means a copy of a fits strictly inside a copy of b from the same origin.
  • a = b means the endpoints coincide after laying off congruent representatives.
  • Positivity is not an algebraic axiom yet; it is visible position on the chosen ray.
Pause here to connect order with the earlier axioms of betweenness. The statement that one segment is smaller than another has a geometric witness: after placing both from the same origin, one endpoint lies between the origin and the other endpoint. This matters later because an ordered field is not just a field with labels; its positive cone must agree with the geometry of the ray.
Construction 2

The Similar-Triangle Product Machine

Product as a ratio transfer

Put the unit and a on one ray, put b on a second ray, and use a line parallel to the unit-to-b connector.

The intercepted segment represents a · b because similar triangles preserve the ratio 1 : b = a : a b.

This is the central visual of the lecture, so do not rush it. The point is not that the drawing literally computes a decimal product, but that similarity transfers the ratio determined by the unit triangle to a larger triangle whose base is the segment a. The formula becomes meaningful only after the unit has been chosen and after we know the parallel construction is independent of incidental drawing choices.
Inverse Operation

Division Reverses The Same Similarity

Solving b x = a

Build the triangle that would multiply an unknown segment by b.
Force the output endpoint to land on the given segment a.
Read the corresponding base segment as a / b, with b nonzero.
Division is best presented as the same machine run backward. If multiplication sends a base length through a fixed ratio determined by b, division asks which base length would produce the desired output a. This also clarifies why the zero segment is excluded: the similarity setup collapses when the controlling segment cannot define a nondegenerate ratio.
Proof Obligations

The Constructions Must Behave Like A Field

Additive Laws

Commutativity, associativity, zero, and additive inverses are checked by moving copies along a line and comparing endpoints.

Multiplicative Laws

Products inherit associativity and commutativity from repeated ratio transfers and symmetry of the similar-triangle constructions.

Distributivity

The hard law says that one product machine applied to a concatenated segment equals the concatenation of two product outputs.

This slide identifies the proof work that arithmetic hides. Addition laws mostly follow from the one-dimensional picture, while multiplication laws require repeated use of similarity and parallel transfer. Distributivity is the conceptual bridge: it says that scaling a whole concatenated segment agrees with scaling its pieces and then concatenating the outputs.
Scale

The Unit Segment Is A Coordinate Gauge

What changes, what does not

Changing the unit rescales coordinates, but it does not change incidence, betweenness, or congruence relations in the plane.

The arithmetic field is presented through a gauge: numbers depend on the unit, while geometric statements must survive a unit change.

Treat the unit as a gauge choice, because that language helps students distinguish coordinate labels from geometric facts. A longer unit makes the same physical segment receive a smaller coordinate, but the segment itself and its incidence relations have not changed. The later coordinate theorem is credible only if the construction respects that separation between scale-dependent names and scale-independent geometry.
Coordinates On A Line

A Point Receives A Coordinate By Its Oriented Segment

Line coordinate recipe

  • Choose an origin O and a positive unit point U.
  • For a point P on the line, compare the directed segment OP with repeated unit segments.
  • Points on opposite sides of the origin receive opposite signs.
This is the first moment where the construction begins to look like ordinary analytic geometry. Emphasize that the coordinate is not attached to a point in isolation; it is attached to a point after choosing an origin, an orientation, and a unit. The sign convention packages the two rays through the origin into one ordered arithmetic line.
Coordinates In A Plane

Parallel Projection Turns Points Into Ordered Pairs

Coordinate pair

Given two axes through the origin, project P parallel to each axis to read its two line coordinates.

The construction converts a geometric point into (x(P), y(P)) using only incidence, parallels, and the arithmetic already built on each axis.

The coordinate plane construction is a translation device. Parallel projection lets one ask two one-dimensional questions about a point: where does it land on the first axis, and where does it land on the second axis. The important invariant is that lines should become linear equations over the constructed field, so incidence in the plane becomes algebraic incidence in coordinates.
Incidence Becomes Algebra

Lines Are The First Test Of The Coordinate Translation

Expected dictionary

geometric line ↔ ax + by + c = 0

  • Collinearity becomes a linear dependence condition.
  • Parallel lines share a direction ratio.
  • Betweenness is reflected by ordered coordinates along the line.
This slide states the first serious validation target for coordinates. If the construction is faithful, a straight geometric line should not turn into an arbitrary set of pairs; it should be described by one linear equation over the segment field. This gives students a concrete reason for all the earlier arithmetic checks: without distributivity and order compatibility, the line dictionary would fail.
Coordinate Theorem Scaffold

What The Isomorphism Proof Has To Preserve

The proof is not one statement but a chain of preservation claims: arithmetic gives coordinates, coordinates turn lines into equations, and the order on the segment field records betweenness.

Use this as a proof map rather than a theorem to memorize. Each arrow marks a preservation claim: operations must be well-defined, line coordinates must respect signed order, plane coordinates must reflect parallel projection, and linear equations must capture geometric lines. The final coordinate model is convincing only because incidence and betweenness have been carried through the entire chain.
Why Similarity Is Doing The Heavy Lifting

One Triangle Pattern Powers Several Algebraic Facts

Product

Transfer a ratio from the unit triangle to a triangle with base a.

Quotient

Fix the output and read the base that would produce it through the same ratio.

Coordinates

Use parallels to compare projections and preserve ratios along axes.

This slide gives students a unifying mental model. Similar triangles are not a separate topic inserted into arithmetic; they are the mechanism that transports ratios without already having numerical multiplication. When the same pattern appears in product, quotient, and coordinate projection, students can see why the chapter sits after similarity rather than before it.
Common Mistakes

Three Confusions To Prevent Early

Measuring too soon

Do not assume real numbers are already attached to every segment. The chapter is constructing a number system from the geometry.

Forgetting the unit

The product machine depends on a chosen unit. Changing that unit rescales coordinates and must be accounted for.

Drawing dependence

A construction is not finished until different representatives and parallel choices give the same segment class.

geometry first → arithmetic second → coordinates third
These warnings are meant to stop three very natural shortcuts. Students who already know analytic geometry may silently import real-number length, but that would erase the chapter's purpose. The other two mistakes are more subtle: treating the unit as invisible, and accepting one clean drawing as a proof of well-definedness.
Worked Example

Read A Product From A Concrete Unit Triangle

Inspection task

Suppose the base ray marks 1 and 3, while the vertical ray marks 2. The line through the point over 3 parallel to the unit connector reaches height 6.

3 · 2 = 6

The example intentionally uses familiar integer marks, but phrase them as repeated unit segments rather than pre-existing numbers. The unit triangle has base one and height two; scaling the base to three forces the height to scale to six by similarity. This gives the audience a quick sanity check for the more abstract construction without replacing the proof.
In-Class Exercise

Diagnose A Failed Product Construction

Question

A student draws the unit-to-b connector correctly, then draws a second connector through a that is close to parallel but not actually parallel. Which algebraic law becomes unreliable first, and why?

Hint: compare what happens when the same product is redrawn at a larger scale.

Let students identify that the construction has lost similarity, so ratios are no longer being transferred faithfully. The first casualty is well-defined multiplication, and distributivity will also become suspect because different drawings of what should be the same product can produce different outputs. The exercise is useful because it makes the parallel condition feel like a mathematical necessity rather than a drafting preference.
Recap

What Segment Arithmetic Gives Us

A constructed field

Segments, once organized by congruence and a unit, support addition, multiplication, order, and inverses.

A geometric product

Similar triangles supply multiplication without assuming numerical length in advance.

A scale-aware coordinate system

Coordinates depend on origin, orientation, and unit, while incidence statements must remain geometric.

A bridge to analytic geometry

Lines become linear equations, betweenness becomes ordered parameters, and geometric planes become coordinate planes over the constructed field.
