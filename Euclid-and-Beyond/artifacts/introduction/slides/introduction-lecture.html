Construction

A Construction Is Executable Only After Existence Is Visible

Exact Check

AB = AC = BC = 1

  • Data: a starting segment AB.
  • Operations: draw two circles with radius AB.
  • Assumption: the circles meet at a usable point C.
  • Conclusion: triangle ABC is equilateral.
The equilateral construction is small enough to hold in memory, which makes it useful for exposing a hidden premise. Drawing two circles is an operation, but completing the triangle requires an intersection assertion in the plane. Later axiom systems make this kind of existence claim explicit, so this slide is the first bridge from Euclid to foundations.

Axioms

Modern Foundations Keep Proof Roles Separate

Instructor Focus

  • Euclid's diagrams carry implicit existence and order information.
  • Hilbert-style foundations later force those assumptions into the open.
  • A computational graph is a useful proxy for auditing dependency claims.
  • The graph does not prove the theorem; it organizes what a proof must justify.
Read the dependency graph as a proof-state visual rather than as a decorative flowchart. Data, permitted moves, and inferred objects are different kinds of nodes, and a good proof keeps those roles visible. This is also where students can see why modern foundations care about ordering assumptions before conclusions.

Coordinates

Coordinates Are a Translation Layer, Not a Replacement

Translation Principle

Introduce coordinates when they expose a checkable invariant: distance, incidence, intersection, orientation, or field closure.

geometry → algebra → diagnostic → geometry

The Introduction does not treat coordinates as the death of synthetic geometry. Coordinates enter when a geometric problem benefits from arithmetic diagnostics, especially distance, incidence, and intersection tests. The key teaching point is that numerical checks can support a claim without becoming the geometric idea itself.

Models

The Parallel Postulate Opens a Model-Building Door

What Changes?

  • The drawing surface is no longer merely paper intuition.
  • A model supplies a precise meaning for lines and distance.
  • The postulate becomes a testable structural choice.
  • Non-Euclidean geometry is coherent when the model rules cohere.
The parallel postulate matters here because it creates the first major fork in the course's model-building story. In Euclidean geometry, one expects a rigid uniqueness behavior for parallels through a point. The Introduction previews the fact that changing this assumption leads to internally coherent geometries rather than merely bad drawings.

Method

The Course Runs on Two Complementary Habits

Synthetic Habit
  • Name the permitted objects.
  • State the existence assumptions.
  • Track which conclusions depend on which rules.
  • Keep diagrams subordinate to proof.
Computational Habit
  • Choose an encoding for the object.
  • Compute diagnostics with known invariants.
  • Record residuals and finite checks.
  • Keep numbers subordinate to geometry.
This slide makes the lecture's methodology explicit. Synthetic reasoning tracks allowed objects and proof obligations, while computational reasoning tracks encodings, residuals, and reproducible checks. The course works best when students use both habits and remain aware of the translation boundary between them.

Cautions

Three Misreadings to Avoid Before the Course Starts

Misreading 1

Modern means anti-Euclid

Modern geometry keeps Euclid's questions alive while making the rules and models more explicit.

Misreading 2

Coordinates settle everything

Coordinates provide diagnostics, but the target is still a geometric claim with assumptions and invariants.

Misreading 3

Models make truth arbitrary

A model changes the interpretation of terms, not the demand for coherent rules and checkable consequences.

The productive reading is: Euclid supplies durable problems; modern geometry supplies sharper languages for asking which versions of those problems are true.

Use this as a pause before the recap to clear away tempting simplifications. Modernization does not mean abandoning Euclid, and models do not mean anything goes. The productive view is that Euclid supplies durable questions, while later chapters sharpen the rules under which those questions can be answered.
