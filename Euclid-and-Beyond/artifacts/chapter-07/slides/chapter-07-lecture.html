Neutral geometry

The Parallel Postulate Is a Branch Point, Not a Drawing Convention

Before the Branch

Incidence, order, congruence, and many triangle arguments remain available without selecting unique parallels.

After the Branch

Euclidean geometry has one parallel through an exterior point; hyperbolic geometry has limiting directions and a positive triangle defect.

neutral statements are hypothesis-sensitivetriangle defect enters after the branch
The parallel postulate should be presented as a branch decision rather than a drawing habit. Many facts about incidence, congruence, order, and triangles can be discussed before the branch is chosen. Once the branch is chosen, Euclidean triangles keep zero defect while hyperbolic triangles acquire a positive defect that will later become area.

Circle inversion

Inversion Supplies the Euclidean Machinery Behind Disk Geodesics

Inversion Rule

P maps to O + r2(P - O) / ||P - O||2

The operation is its own inverse, so reciprocal points on a ray swap positions predictably.

Geometric Payoff

Circles orthogonal to the boundary inversion circle are preserved as sets; these become the visible carriers of disk geodesics.

inversion involution error: 0product error: 0
Circle inversion is the bridge because it is a Euclidean operation with non-Euclidean consequences in the disk model. The two finite checks, involution and reciprocal radius product, are the computational anchors. Once those are accepted, orthogonal circles become natural candidates for disk geodesics.
Poincare disk

Disk Lines Are Euclidean Circles Orthogonal to the Ideal Boundary

Geodesic Predicate

||C||2 - R2 = 1

A disk geodesic is either a diameter or an arc of a circle whose support meets the unit boundary at right angles.

Visual Inspection

The arc may bow strongly in the Euclidean picture, but its boundary orthogonality is the invariant to check.

artifact: poincare-and-inversion-labs.pngorthogonality residual below 1e-13
This slide explains why Poincare disk lines look circular on the screen. The ideal boundary is not crossed by hyperbolic points, and a geodesic must meet it orthogonally in the Euclidean drawing. Ask students to inspect the supporting circle condition: center norm squared minus radius squared equals one.
Model equivalence

The Cayley transform gives a controlled change of model rather than a new theory. Shapes change: disk arcs become half-plane semicircles or vertical rays. Distances and geodesic predicates agree, which is why the notebook checks round trips and distance equality between the two views.
Ends and boundary arithmetic

Ideal Endpoints Turn Half-Plane Motions Into Boundary Arithmetic

Boundary Data

A half-plane geodesic is determined by either a pair of finite endpoints or a finite endpoint plus infinity.

Distance Calibration

distance from i to edi equals d

The notebook check records vertical distance log error near 2.22 × 10-16.

endpoint pairs: 3dilations sampled: 3
Ends are ideal boundary data, so the half-plane model turns some geometry into endpoint arithmetic. Translations and dilations move boundary coordinates in simple ways, while the vertical ray from i to exp(d)i has hyperbolic length d. This is a good place to emphasize that infinity is organized, not vague.
Limiting parallels

Bolyai's Angle of Parallelism Measures the Width of the Parallel Cone

Distance to a Line

If a point is hyperbolic distance d from a line, the limiting parallel angle satisfies:

tan(Π(d) / 2) = e-d

Reading the Formula

Near the line the cone is wide; far from the line the limiting directions compress toward the perpendicular.

symbolic tangent identity: 0angle parallelism error below 1e-15
Bolyai's angle of parallelism makes limiting parallels measurable from a distance. The formula says that a farther line creates a narrower cone of limiting directions from the point. Students should read the curve monotonically: the angle approaches a right angle near the line and shrinks toward zero at large distance.
Hyperbolic distance

The Disk Is Visually Euclidean but Metrically Hyperbolic

Distance Formula

cosh d(u,v) = 1 + 2||u-v||2 / ((1-||u||2)(1-||v||2))

The denominator explains why approaching the ideal boundary costs infinite hyperbolic distance.

Area and angle defect

For Curvature Minus One, Triangle Area Is the Missing Angle

Defect Formula

Area = π - (A + B + C)

Angles are measured conformally in the disk, while side lengths use the hyperbolic metric.

Sample Trend

Compact, wide, and near-ideal triangles have decreasing angle sums and increasing positive defects.

minimum lab defect: 0.4825near-ideal sample defect: 2.3050
Conformality is the reason Euclidean-looking tangent angles can be trusted inside the disk, even though distances are not Euclidean distances. For curvature minus one, area equals pi minus the angle sum for a hyperbolic triangle. The three sample triangles make the trend inspectable: as triangles reach farther toward the boundary, angle sum falls and defect grows.
Hyperbolic trigonometry

The Euclidean Pythagorean Pattern Becomes a Cosh Identity

Right-Triangle Law

cosh(c) = cosh(a) cosh(b)

The formula is the hyperbolic replacement for the Euclidean square-sum identity.

For the sampled right triangle, the pythagorean residual is 4.44 × 10-16, and the sine-rule residual is zero.

The Euclidean formula a squared plus b squared equals c squared becomes a cosh identity. The point is not to memorize one more equation, but to see that hyperbolic trigonometry has precise internal checks.
Common misconception

Do Not Move Statements Across Layers Without Their Hypotheses

Neutral Layer

Uses incidence, betweenness, congruence, and order without selecting a unique-parallel rule.

Euclidean Branch

Unique parallels and zero triangle defect become available only after the Euclidean postulate is selected.

Hyperbolic Branch

Limiting parallels, positive defect, orthogonal-circle geodesics, and hyperbolic trig replace Euclidean expectations.

hypotheses are part of the theorempictures alone do not decide geometry
This comparison slide asks students to keep three layers separate. The neutral layer states what does not use the parallel postulate, the Euclidean branch has zero defect, and the hyperbolic branch has positive defect and multiple limiting parallel directions. Many errors in this chapter come from moving a statement from one layer to another without checking its hypotheses.
