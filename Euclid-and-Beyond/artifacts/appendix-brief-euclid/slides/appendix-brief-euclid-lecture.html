Lecture Aim

From Reference List To Working Map

Translation Principle

A Euclid tag is treated as a named checkpoint: an assumption, construction, ratio, identity, or finite classification that later chapters can invoke and test.

  • Definitions become predicates.
  • Postulates become allowed moves.
  • Propositions become reusable invariants.

Foundational Layer

Definitions, Postulates, Common Notions

Definitions

Turn nouns into typed predicates: point, line, circle, angle, boundary, equality of segments.

Postulates

Turn permitted acts into constructors: join two points, extend a segment, draw a circle from center and radius.

Common Notions

Turn equality rules into transformations: substitution, addition, subtraction, and comparison of wholes and parts.

That is why the appendix matters even after the course leaves straightedge-and-compass style exposition.

Misconceptions

What The Appendix Is Not Doing

Not A Short Proof Archive

Tags point to reusable structures. They do not carry enough detail to replace a full proof or construction.

Not A Historical Translation

Modern predicates, residues, and graphs clarify course use; they are not claims about Euclid's original notation.

Not A Decorative Index

The map is useful only when each reference can answer what assumption, witness, or invariant is being imported.

Use tags as checkpoints, not ornaments15 / 16
This slide guards against three common overreadings. The appendix is not a substitute for Euclid, and the deck's modern language is a teaching translation rather than an historical reconstruction. The responsible use is to ask what a tag contributes to the course: an assumption, a witness object, a ratio, an obstruction, or a finite classification.
