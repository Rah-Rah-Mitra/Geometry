Foundations

Undefined Terms, Defined Behavior

Hilbert begins with primitives

  • Points and lines are not reduced to coordinates.
  • Relations such as incidence, betweenness, and congruence carry the content.
  • A model is acceptable only when all required relations obey the axioms.
Stress the difference between a drawing and a model. A drawing suggests relations, while the axioms specify which relations are legal and how they interact. This is the first shift from Euclidean practice to Hilbert's formal method: proofs become portable because they no longer depend on visual assumptions that were never stated.
Incidence

Two Points Should Select One Line

Incidence Predicate

For distinct points P and Q, there exists exactly one line incident with both.

The finite affine plane is small but useful: it lets students audit all pairs and see uniqueness as a table property.

This slide uses a finite affine model as a microscope for incidence, not as a full Hilbert plane. The highlighted row line is only one of the carriers; the important fact is that all unordered pairs behave the same way. Mention that the notebook checks this by counting line memberships rather than trusting the visual grid.
Incidence Failure

Existence Is Not Enough: Uniqueness Can Fail

A tempting bad structure

  • Several subsets can be called lines.
  • Two named lines may share the same two points.
  • Then the phrase the line through P,Q is ambiguous.

Incidence axioms protect proof language from accidental multi-valued choices.

Now make the failure concrete. If two different carriers both pass through the same pair of points, a later argument cannot refer to the unique line determined by those points. The drawing is intentionally simple because the mathematical warning is about language: without uniqueness, many familiar proof steps stop being functions.
Order

Betweenness Turns Lines Into Ordered Places

What order gives us

  • Segments become finite pieces of lines.
  • Rays become half-lines with a chosen start.
  • Interiors and sides can be discussed without a coordinate grid.
Betweenness is the first place where the picture of a line gains an internal order. The relation is not just a convenience for drawing segments; it is the device that makes rays, interiors, and crossing arguments meaningful. Ask students to notice how much ordinary Euclidean language quietly assumes this order has already been installed.
Separation

Pasch Is the Warning Light for Hidden Order

Pasch-style reasoning

A line meeting one side of a triangle, while missing the vertices, must meet exactly one of the other two sides under the appropriate hypotheses.

The axiom prevents a line from silently slipping through the interior without an accountable crossing.

Pasch is easy to underestimate because the real-plane drawing feels self-evident. In Hilbert's framework the point is exactly that such separation behavior deserves an axiom. Connect this to the notebook's sign-change test: the computation gives a modern way to see what the order axiom is protecting.
Congruence I

Segment Congruence Is a Transport Tool

Not a measurement claim

Congruence says a segment can be copied to a specified ray in a controlled way. Measurement may later assign numbers, but the axiom group already supplies geometric copying.

Keep the word transport in the foreground. Segment congruence is not merely saying two lengths compare; it gives a construction-like permission to place a copy on a chosen ray. This is the bridge from Euclid's hands-on operations to Hilbert's relational language.
Congruence II

Angle Copying Plus SAS Powers Triangle Proofs

The triangle engine

If two sides and the included angle are transported correctly, the remaining side and the remaining angles are forced.

SAS turns local congruence data into a global triangle conclusion.

Present SAS as an axiom with computational personality: it is a constraint propagation rule. Once the included angle and adjacent sides are matched, the rest of the triangle data cannot drift independently. The notebook's residual check models this idea by applying a rigid motion and verifying that the forced quantities agree up to numerical precision.
Hilbert Plane

A Neutral Plane Is Already Rich

Incidence

Lines are determined and contain enough points for geometry to begin.

Betweenness

Order supports segments, rays, sides, interiors, and crossing arguments.

Congruence

Lengths, angles, and triangles can be transported and compared.

A Hilbert plane has these groups without yet deciding Playfair's parallel axiom or the circle-circle intersection property.

This is the pause point where students often realize that neutral geometry is not empty geometry. Incidence, order, and congruence already support a substantial body of arguments. The important separation is that parallel behavior and certain intersection existence results are not smuggled into the definition of a Hilbert plane.

Obstructions

The Rational Plane Looks Euclidean Until It Does Not

Two useful failures

  • The line y = 0 meeting x² = 2 asks for irrational coordinates.
  • Two unit circles one unit apart meet at irrational heights.
  • Thus plausible coordinate rules do not guarantee all Euclidean construction points.
The rational plane is a good stress test because it preserves many algebraic-looking habits while failing at existence. The circles in the visual would intersect in the real plane, but their intersection coordinates leave the rational field. This is why the chapter isolates intersection behavior instead of assuming every constructed point automatically belongs to the model.
Intersection Axioms

Line-Circle Behavior Needs Witnesses

Existence pattern

A line with one point inside a circle and another outside must meet the circle. The geometric content is not the round shape; it is the guaranteed crossing under inside-outside evidence.

Separate the visual classification from the axiom's logical role. Secant, tangent, and outside cases are familiar, but the formal issue is when inside and outside witnesses force an actual point of intersection. This lets the class see why continuity-like assumptions enter construction arguments.
Circle-Circle E

The Extra Intersection Switch

Why add E?

  • Hilbert-plane axioms give a strong neutral toolkit.
  • They do not by themselves force every circle-circle crossing used in constructions.
  • E says the expected intersection point belongs to the plane under the stated inside-outside conditions.
This is a good moment to connect the formal axiom to compass-and-straightedge practice. Constructions often ask for a point where two circles meet, but a weak model may not contain that point. The E axiom adds the relevant existence behavior without collapsing all neutral geometry into Euclidean geometry by itself.
Parallel Postulate

Playfair's Axiom Is a Separate Choice

Neutral versus Euclidean

Many incidence, order, and congruence results do not need a parallel axiom. Euclidean plane structure appears when the neutral Hilbert toolkit is supplemented by Playfair's choice and the intersection existence axiom.

Avoid presenting Playfair as merely another technical clause. It changes the global parallel theory while leaving much of the neutral toolkit intact. The dashed alternatives in the visual are not claiming a specific hyperbolic model; they remind students that uniqueness is an additional decision, not a consequence of the earlier groups.
Proof State

Read Proofs as Dependency Paths

Treat this graph as a reading strategy for the whole chapter. When a proof invokes a construction or comparison, ask which earlier permission is being spent. This also prepares students for later chapters, where changing the model changes which dependency paths remain available.
Applied Lab

A Model-Auditing Protocol

Six witness questions

  • Can two points choose more than one line?
  • Can side signs predict a segment crossing?
  • Can a segment be copied to a specified ray?
  • Can an angle be copied to a chosen side?
  • Do circle intersections stay inside the model?
  • How many parallels pass through an external point?
End the body of the lecture by turning the chapter into a usable protocol. The point is not to certify an infinite model by sampling, but to learn the type of witness each axiom group demands. This keeps formal axioms connected to concrete mathematical behavior without pretending that pictures are proofs.
Recap

What Hilbert Adds to Euclid

  • Undefined objects become rigorous through relations.
  • Incidence controls uniqueness of line choice.
  • Betweenness controls order, sides, and separation.
  • Congruence controls transport of segments, angles, and triangles.
  • Intersection and parallel axioms are explicit extra switches.

A Euclidean plane is best viewed here as a Hilbert plane plus the additional existence and parallel behavior needed for the classical Euclidean toolkit.

Close by returning to the initial motivation: Hilbert makes Euclidean reasoning inspectable. The students should now be able to describe a proof step by naming its permission, and they should be suspicious of models that look familiar but fail one switch. This sets up later chapters where coordinate fields and alternative geometries deliberately modify the underlying axiom bundle.
