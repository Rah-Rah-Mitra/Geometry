Graph Notation

A Diagram Is A Combinatorial Object

vertices + edges

Vocabulary That Carries Over

  • Vertex set: the objects being connected.
  • Edges: allowed one-step moves.
  • Labels/colors: which move created the edge.
  • Paths: finite strings of adjacent moves.
  • Distance: shortest path length in the graph.

Cayley graphs add group multiplication to this ordinary graph vocabulary.

Notation first, geometry second3 / 15
Begin with ordinary graph language before adding group structure. A vertex is an object, an edge records adjacency, orientation records direction when needed, and a label records the move type. The important habit is to read a diagram as data, not as decoration.
Cayley Construction

Repeat One Local Multiplication Rule Everywhere

Cay(G, S)

Definition Lens

Given a group G and generating set S, the Cayley graph has one vertex for each element of G.

\(g \xrightarrow{\;s\;} gs \qquad (g \in G,\; s \in S)\)

Every generator supplies the same colored move from every vertex, so local multiplication becomes global geometry.

Edges encode right multiplication by generators4 / 15
Present the Cayley construction as a repeated local rule. From every group element g and every generator s, draw the same colored move from g to gs. This makes the graph homogeneous: the view from any vertex is a translated copy of the view from the identity.
Generator-Colored Edges

The Grid Remembers Commuting Moves

Z^2 with a,b

Reading The Colored Square

  • Blue edges apply one generator.
  • Teal edges apply the other generator.
  • Two paths from e reach the same vertex.
  • The closed square records a commuting relation.
\(e \to a \to ab \quad\text{and}\quad e \to b \to ba\)
Labels make algebra inspectable5 / 15
Pause on the generator colors in the grid because they encode the chosen generating set. A blue horizontal step and a teal vertical step commute, so the square loop is not an accident of the drawing. It is the visible trace of the relation ab equals ba.
Word Metric

Distance Is Shortest Generator Length

balls + spheres

Metric From A Graph

The word length of g is the distance from the identity in the Cayley graph.

\(|g|_S = d_{\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)}(e,g)\)

The radius n ball contains elements reachable in at most n steps; the sphere contains exactly the new frontier.

Word length is graph distance6 / 15
Introduce the word metric as graph distance from the identity. The ball of radius n is the part reachable by at most n generator steps, and the sphere is the new frontier at exactly n steps. Stress that the metric depends on the selected generators.

Free Groups

Reduced Words Prevent Immediate Backtracking

normal forms

Reduction Rule

Adjacent inverse letters cancel. A reduced word has no neighboring pair of the form \(ss^{-1}\) or \(s^{-1}s\).

a b b^-1 a^-1 b

After cancellation, the remaining word names the same group element with a shorter path.

unique reduced word \( \longleftrightarrow \) unique geodesic from the identity
Free reduction is a graph-building rule8 / 15
Reduced words are the syntax that keeps the free group tree from immediately folding back on itself. After writing a letter, the inverse letter is forbidden as the next outward step. That simple local rule creates unique geodesics back to the identity.
Free Group Cayley Graph

The Ball Looks Like A Rooted Tree

F(a,b)

Local Degree Check

  • At the root: four generator choices.
  • Away from the root: one edge points back.
  • Outward choices: three non-cancelling letters.
  • No closed path survives reduction.
\(|S_0|=1,\quad |S_1|=4,\quad |S_2|=12,\quad |S_3|=36\)
Notebook check: free_group_ball_is_tree = true9 / 15
Read the rooted tree from the identity outward. The root has four choices for a, a inverse, b, and b inverse; every later vertex has one edge back toward a shorter word and three outward choices. This is the visual reason the free-group ball is a tree.

Finite Graph Windows

Do Not Mistake The Cut Boundary For The Group

model caution

What Survives Enlargement?

  • Generator labels and local degree near the identity.
  • Cycles forced by relations inside the window.
  • Branching rules away from immediate cancellation.
  • Sphere counts that stabilize as the radius grows.

Boundary effects are useful reminders that a finite rendering is a controlled sample of an infinite graph.

Finite models are evidence, not exhaustion13 / 15
Warn students about finite windows. A rendered ball has an artificial boundary where the drawing stops, so edge vertices look incomplete. The durable information is the pattern that remains stable as the radius grows: local labels, forced cycles, branching rules, and distance counts.

Recap

What A Cayley Graph Remembers

takeaways

Remembers

  • Generator steps as colored edges.
  • Word length as graph distance.
  • Relations as closed labeled paths.
  • Branching and cycle patterns visible in balls.
  • Coarse numerical data such as sphere counts.

Forgets Or Depends On

  • The spelling of a word after it represents a group element.
  • The chosen generating set is part of the model.
  • A finite drawing never exhausts an infinite graph.
  • Attractive layout geometry is not itself an invariant.
