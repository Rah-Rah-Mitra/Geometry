Analytic Face

An Invariant Mean Is A Translation-Blind Average

Definition Skeleton

A mean on bounded functions is a functional \(m\) satisfying:

  • \(m(1)=1\) and \(m(f)\ge 0\) when \(f\ge 0\).
  • \(m(f+g)=m(f)+m(g)\) and \(m(cf)=c\,m(f)\).
  • \(m(g\cdot f)=m(f)\) for every group element \(g\).
\(m(f)=m(f\circ L_g)\)
Mean: exact invariance in an infinite setting1 / analytic model
Pause on the mean because it is intentionally abstract. Students should hear positivity, normalization, and translation invariance as the three permissions that make an average behave globally. The orbit diagram reminds them that moving every input by a group element should not change the reported value.
Finite-Combinatorial Face

Følner Sets Are Almost Unmoved By Generators

Working Translation

For a finite generating set \(S\), a sequence \(F_n\) should satisfy

$$\frac{|sF_n\,\triangle\,F_n|}{|F_n|}\to 0\quad(s\in S).$$

The set is allowed to move, but the moved part must become negligible.

Inspection habit. Watch the rim, not the center. If the rim loses relative weight as the region grows, the finite window is behaving like a good averaging witness.

Approximate invariance replaces exact infinite averagingFølner criterion
Use this slide as the conceptual bridge from functional analysis to finite geometry. A Folner set is not exactly invariant; it is mostly unchanged after each generator move. The overlap picture lets students see approximate invariance before the boundary formula appears.
Cayley Graph Inspection

Boundary-To-Volume Ratio Is The Visual Test

Generator Boundary

For a finite set \(F\) in a Cayley graph, count the edges labelled by \(S\) that leave \(F\).

$$\rho_S(F)=\frac{|\partial_S F|}{|F|}$$

Amenable behavior means there are finite \(F_n\) with \(\rho_S(F_n)\to 0\).

Boundary is measured relative to a generating setCayley graph ratio
Define the boundary operationally in a Cayley graph: generator steps that leave the chosen finite set. The ratio is the inspection target because it separates temporary edge effects from asymptotic behavior. A good sequence makes the escaping part negligible compared with the whole region.
Amenable Model Family

Large Lattice Boxes Become Almost Invariant

Example: \(\mathbb{Z}^d\)

Boxes \(F_n=[-n,n]^d\cap\mathbb{Z}^d\) are standard witnesses.

$$|\partial F_n|\asymp n^{d-1},\qquad |F_n|\asymp n^d$$

Thus \(|\partial F_n|/|F_n|\) has order \(1/n\).

Changing the shape can change early ratios. The invariant question asks whether some growing family can drive the ratio down.

Følner sequence: finite regions with vanishing relative rimlattice intuition
Make the lattice example feel almost physical. Large intervals or squares have a surface layer that grows slower than the interior, so small translations disturb only a thin rim. This gives students the first durable mental model for why abelian groups are amenable.

Obstruction Face

Paradoxical Decomposition Is The Anti-Average Signal

Schematic Logic

If a group can be partitioned into pieces that, after translations, reassemble into two full copies, a normalized invariant mean would imply

\(1 = 2\)

So paradoxical decompositions rule out amenability.

Tarski viewpoint: amenability forbids paradoxical doublingobstruction
Present paradoxical decomposition as the opposite of a stable average. If finitely many pieces can be shifted into two full-looking copies, then a translation-invariant normalized mean would have to assign incompatible sizes. Equivalent Faces

The Same Property Wears Several Useful Masks

Invariant Mean

An exact averaging functional exists on bounded functions and ignores left translation.

Følner Witnesses

Finite sets become almost invariant under each generator as their size grows.

No Paradox

The group cannot be cut into finitely many translated pieces that reassemble into two copies.

Coarse Stability

For finitely generated groups, amenability survives quasi-isometry.

Calibration

Examples And Nonexamples Keep The Test Honest

Amenable Families

  • Finite groups: average over all elements.
  • \(\mathbb{Z}^d\): boxes give Følner sequences.
  • Groups of subexponential growth: balls cannot expand too fast forever.
  • Solvable groups: amenability is stable through extensions.

Nonamenability Signals

  • Free groups \(F_k\) for \(k\ge 2\): branching tree frontier persists.
  • Any group containing such a free subgroup is nonamenable.
  • Paradoxical decompositions provide a certificate against invariant means.
  • Absence of an obvious free subgroup is not a proof of amenability.
Examples are tests for intuition, not a replacement for criteriaamenable / nonamenable
Read the table as a calibration device. Finite, abelian, and solvable groups support averaging behavior, while a free subgroup of rank two is a strong nonamenability witness. Also mention that the converse is delicate, so the table is a guide rather than a classification theorem.
Algebraic Toolkit

Closure Moves Let Amenability Travel Through Constructions

Useful Stability Rules
  • Subgroups of amenable groups are amenable.
  • Quotients of amenable groups are amenable.
  • Extensions with amenable kernel and quotient are amenable.
  • Directed unions of amenable groups remain amenable.
Closure rules convert local examples into familiesalgebraic stability
Amenability becomes usable because it survives common algebraic constructions. Quotients, subgroups, extensions, and directed unions can carry the property in controlled ways. The point of the diagram is strategic: once a group is built from known amenable pieces, a new boundary experiment is often unnecessary.
Coarse Geometry Payoff

Amenability Is A Quasi-Isometry Invariant

Theorem Template

If finitely generated groups \(G\) and \(H\) are quasi-isometric, then \(G\) is amenable exactly when \(H\) is amenable.

The proof uses bounded geometry: images of finite sets are thickened so that holes and bounded distortions do not overwhelm volume.

Coarse equivalence cannot create persistent frontier from vanishing boundaryQI invariant
Frame quasi-isometry invariance as the chapter's coarse-geometry payoff. For finitely generated groups, a rough equivalence distorts distances by bounded multiplicative and additive errors, but it does not turn vanishing boundary ratios into persistent frontiers. The thickened-image diagram shows why finite noise can be absorbed.

Recap

Takeaways

  • A mean is the exact invariant-average ideal.
  • Følner sequences are finite, inspectable witnesses.
  • Boundary-to-volume ratios separate lattices from free trees.
  • Paradoxical decompositions are certificates of nonamenability.
  • For finitely generated groups, the property is quasi-isometry invariant.
