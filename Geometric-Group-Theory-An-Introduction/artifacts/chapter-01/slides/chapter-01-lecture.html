First Construction

Cayley Graph Rule

03 / 12
Definition Sketch Given a group \(G\) and generating set \(S\), the Cayley graph has vertex set \(G\). For every \(g \in G\) and \(s \in S\), draw an edge \(g \to gs\).
  • Vertices are algebraic objects, not points chosen from Euclidean space.
  • Edges record multiplication by chosen generators.
  • Changing \(S\) changes the drawing, but not the group.
Define the Cayley graph operationally. For each group element g and generator s, draw an edge from g to gs. When the generating set is symmetric the drawing can be treated as undirected, but the multiplication rule is still the engine. The local star around g is the whole construction repeated everywhere.
Metric From Generators

Word Metric

04 / 12
Distance Formula
\(d_S(g,h)=|g^{-1}h|_S\)
\(|x|_S=\) shortest length of a word in \(S\) representing \(x\)

Interpretation

A word is a route in the graph. Relations let routes shorten, fold, or close. The metric asks for the shortest route after those algebraic identifications have been respected.

Present word length as the distance from the identity in the Cayley graph. A word is a route, and the metric asks for the shortest route after using all relations. This is the first moment where algebraic simplification and graph distance become the same kind of evidence.

From Words To Identifications

Relations Fold Geometry

06 / 12
Warning The same visual graph is not obtained before and after adding relations. Relations shorten words and create cycles because formerly distinct endpoints are forced to agree.

First Fold To Remember

The relation \(ab=ba\) says the route "a then b" and the route "b then a" arrive at the same element. In a Cayley graph, that equality is a square.

The free group begins as a tree because reduced words do not close up. Adding a relation identifies different words, and those identifications fold the tree into cycles or grids. The commutation relation ab equals ba is the cleanest first fold because it turns two routes into the same endpoint.

Small Computations

Worked Metric Checks

09 / 12
Example 1 In \(\mathbb{Z}^2\) with standard generators, \(d((0,0),(3,2))=|3|+|2|=5\).
Example 2 In \(F(a,b)\), the word \(abb^{-1}a^{-1}b\) freely reduces to \(b\), so its word length is \(1\).
Make the metric concrete with two quick checks. In the standard grid, the shortest route from the identity to (3,2) has length five. In a free group, adjacent inverse letters cancel before length is read. These examples show why the graph metric remembers relations without storing a multiplication table on the slide.
Reading Computed Balls

Finite Windows Are Evidence

10 / 12
Reading Rule A finite graph ball is not the infinite group. Treat it as evidence for a pattern, then ask what survives when the radius grows.
  • Boundary degree drops are usually artifacts of the finite window.
  • Repeated branching, cycles, and distance envelopes are structural signals.
  • Every visual claim should come with a check or invariant.
Finite Cayley graph balls are microscope views of an infinite object. They are useful only when the inspection target is stable as the window grows. Boundary vertices are artifacts of the cutoff, while branching, cycles, approximate growth, and recurring distance envelopes are meaningful signals.
Applied Lab

Frame the lab as a prediction exercise before computation. Start with the free tree, add a single relation, and say what should happen geometrically. A torsion relation should close a cycle; a commutation relation should create squares; a longer relation should fold branches along a repeating path.
