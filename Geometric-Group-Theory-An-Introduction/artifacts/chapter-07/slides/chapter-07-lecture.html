From manifolds to graphs

Curvature Becomes A Metric Test

Classical instinct

Curvature usually suggests infinitesimal comparison: tangent planes, Jacobi fields, or local angle excess.

Graph replacement

A Cayley graph only gives vertices, edges, and shortest-path distance. The usable question becomes: how do geodesic triangles behave?

graph curvature signal = uniform triangle thinness
Metric triangle behavior replaces smooth curvature formulas.02 / 16
Use this slide to change the students' measurement habit. We are not differentiating a metric tensor, and there is no sectional curvature formula on a Cayley graph. Instead, every claim must be read from distances, geodesic segments, and how triangles sit near their sides.

Core definition

Delta-Thin Triangles

Definition

A geodesic triangle is delta-thin if each side lies inside the delta-neighborhood of the union of the other two sides.

\(\Delta(x,y,z)\) is \(\delta\)-thin

Hyperbolic space. One constant \(\delta\) works for all geodesic triangles in the metric space.

The constant is global; it is not chosen separately for each triangle.04 / 16
Build the definition slowly from one chosen side. For each point on that side, ask whether it is close to one of the other two sides. Hyperbolicity is the existence of one uniform delta that works for every geodesic triangle, not just for the triangle currently drawn.

Groups become metric spaces

Cayley Graph Hyperbolicity

Cayley graph recipe

  • Vertices are group elements.
  • Edges multiply by generators.
  • Word length becomes graph distance from the identity.

Free group model. Reduced words branch without creating cycles, so the Cayley graph is a tree and every geodesic triangle is a tripod.

Cayley graphs translate algebra into coarse metric geometry.07 / 16
Now move from spaces to groups. Once a finite generating set is chosen, group elements become vertices and generator multiplication becomes edges. The free group drawing should be read simultaneously as a graph of reduced words and as the geometric object whose triangles certify hyperbolicity.
Definition for groups

The Group Definition

Hyperbolic group

A finitely generated group is hyperbolic when one, equivalently every, Cayley graph for a finite generating set is a hyperbolic metric space.

finite generating sets \(\Rightarrow\) quasi-isometric Cayley graphs

Reason this matters. The property belongs to the group, not to a decorative choice of generators or layout.

Quasi-isometry invariance makes the definition generator-independent.08 / 16
Emphasize that the definition is stable under changing finite generators because Cayley graphs for finite generating sets are quasi-isometric. That is the reason hyperbolic group is a group property rather than a property of one accidental drawing or one preferred word metric.
Stability mechanism

Quasi-Geodesics Stay Near Geodesics

Stability principle

In a hyperbolic space, a quasi-geodesic segment with fixed constants remains within a bounded distance of the genuine geodesic between its endpoints.

Teaching use

Thin triangles prevent efficient paths from drifting through large flat regions, because those regions would create many widely separated near-geodesics.

Fellow travelling is the bridge from geometry to algorithms.09 / 16
Use the fellow-travelling picture to motivate why hyperbolic spaces are algorithmically rigid. A path that is efficient up to fixed multiplicative and additive error cannot wander through a broad flat region, because no such broad region exists. Thinness pulls efficient paths back toward geodesics.
Algorithmic consequence

Word Problem Intuition

Loop-shortening picture

A word equal to the identity is a loop in the Cayley graph. Hyperbolicity gives bounded local evidence when the loop is not geodesic.

Dehn-style reduction

With a suitable finite presentation, a long boundary arc of a relator can be replaced by a shorter complementary arc until the word vanishes or no rule applies.

Hyperbolicity turns global triviality into bounded local evidence.10 / 16
Present the word problem as a loop-shortening process. In a hyperbolic Cayley graph, a non-geodesic loop has bounded local evidence of inefficiency. With a suitable finite presentation, that evidence becomes a Dehn-style reduction rule: repeatedly replace a longer side of a relation by a shorter complement.
Algebraic repetition becomes geometry

Infinite-Order Elements

Coarse axis

If \(g\) has infinite order, the sequence \(\ldots,g^{-1},e,g,g^2,\ldots\) gives a quasi-geodesic line in the Cayley graph.

Boundary preview

The two directions of this axis become attracting and repelling ideal endpoints in the boundary picture.

Infinite-order elements are undistorted in hyperbolic groups.11 / 16
Connect algebraic repetition to geometry. Powers of an infinite-order element form a coarse axis in the Cayley graph: moving by the element translates points along that axis by a positive amount up to bounded error. In hyperbolic groups this produces two limiting directions at infinity.
Structural payoff

Narrow Centralizers

Commuting with a translation

If \(h g = g h\), then \(h\) coarsely sends the \(g\)-axis to itself. Hyperbolicity leaves little room to shear away from that axis.

Contrast with flats

In \(\mathbb{Z}^2\), many independent translations commute because there is a whole plane of parallel axes. Hyperbolicity collapses that freedom to a narrow corridor.

For infinite-order elements, centralizers are forced into one coarse direction.12 / 16
Use this as a structural payoff rather than a full proof. In a hyperbolic group, anything commuting with an infinite-order element must coarsely preserve its axis. That forces the centralizer into a narrow corridor and leads to the virtually cyclic behavior that distinguishes hyperbolic groups from groups with flats.
Common obstruction

Flat Products Break Thinness

Warning pattern

Products of infinite groups often contain quasi-isometric grid regions. Those grids support triangles whose thickness grows with scale.

\(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\) behaves like a flat, not like a tree

The obstruction is geometric: a large rectangle gives many nearly efficient routes that stay far apart.

Grid-like flats are the standard visual obstruction to hyperbolicity.13 / 16
Pause on the obstruction slide because it prevents overgeneralization. Products often contain grid-like regions, and grid-like regions support thick triangles. This is why the chapter treats free groups as the clean model while warning that direct products behave more like flat geometry than negative curvature.
Looking ahead

Boundary Preview

Equivalence of rays

Two geodesic rays define the same boundary point when they remain within bounded distance of one another.

Two mental pictures

  • Free group boundary: infinite reduced words.
  • Hyperbolic plane boundary: a circle of ideal directions.
Chapter 08 will develop ends and boundaries more systematically.14 / 16
Preview the next chapter without making boundary theory do too much work yet. Rays that remain within bounded distance represent the same ideal endpoint. In a tree these endpoints form a Cantor-like collection of infinite reduced words, while a hyperbolic plane model suggests a continuous circle of directions.

Lecture synthesis

Takeaways

Definitions

  • Hyperbolic spaces have uniformly thin geodesic triangles.
  • Hyperbolic groups are finitely generated groups whose Cayley graphs are hyperbolic.

Consequences

  • Quasi-geodesics fellow travel geodesics.
  • Word problem algorithms exploit local shortening.
  • Infinite-order elements define coarse axes and boundary directions.
