Groups And Homomorphisms: What Survives A Map?

Structure Preservation
\(\phi(xy)=\phi(x)\phi(y)\)
  • The kernel records elements that become invisible.
  • The image records the part of the target actually reached.
  • Normal kernels make quotient groups compatible with multiplication.
kernels explain quotientsimages explain represented structure
Frame a homomorphism as a structure-preserving lens. The kernel records what becomes invisible, the image records what survives in the target, and the quotient principle explains why normal subgroups matter. This prepares students to see presentations as quotients of free groups rather than as mysterious notation.
Generating Sets

Generators As An Alphabet For Movement

Working Definition

A subset \(S\) generates \(G\) when every element of \(G\) is expressible as a finite word in letters from \(S\) and their inverses.

  • Finite generation means a finite alphabet reaches the whole group.
  • Word length begins once the alphabet is fixed.
  • Different generating sets can give different local graphs.
alphabet firstmetric intuition second
Slow down on the language metaphor. A generating set is not yet geometry by itself, but it lets every element be reached by words. Once a finite generating set is chosen, word length becomes a first distance model, and changing the alphabet changes the local shape of balls.
Free Groups

Before Relations, Reduced Words Form A Tree

No Extra Equations

The free group on \(S\) keeps exactly the cancellations forced by \(s s^{-1}=e\) and no additional identifications.

reduce: a b b^-1 a^-1 b -> b

Tree branches are words. Missing cycles mean no relator has yet closed a path.

baseline: no relationscycles enter later
The free group slide should feel like the no-identifications baseline. Reduced words cancel adjacent inverse letters and otherwise remain distinct. The rooted tree is useful because every edge appends one generator choice, so cycles are absent until relations are deliberately imposed.
Universal Property

A Map Out Of A Free Group Is Chosen On Letters

Routing Rule

Any function \(S \rightarrow G\) extends uniquely to a homomorphism \(F(S) \rightarrow G\).

  • The source has no extra equations to check.
  • Images of words are computed by multiplying images of letters.
  • Generated groups are quotients of these universal sources.
choose lettersthe group map follows
Present the universal property as a routing rule rather than as a slogan. To define a homomorphism from a free group, choose the images of the free generators, then extend by reading words. This is why free groups are the raw material for constructing all generated groups.
Relations

Relations Create Identifications

Relator Consequences

A relator \(w\) forces \(w=e\), but the quotient also kills conjugates and products of relators.

  • One declared loop creates a whole normal closure of invisible words.
  • Graphically, distinct paths can now end at the same vertex.
  • The quotient Cayley graph remembers the imposed cycles.
relations are quotient instructionsnormal closure supplies all consequences
Point to the two words before and after the relation is imposed. A relator does not merely add an equation to a list; it forces every conjugate and product consequence to vanish in the quotient. The picture makes the algebraic closure operation feel like glued paths in a graph.
Presentations

Presentations As Machines For Quotients

Presentation
\(\langle S \mid R\rangle = F(S)/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\)
  • Generators choose allowed moves.
  • Relators choose loops that must close.
  • The quotient is the group whose Cayley graph reflects those instructions.
presentation notation has an internal pipelinefree group then quotient
Use this slide to connect notation with computation. The presentation first builds the free group on the alphabet, then divides by the normal closure of the chosen relators. In a Cayley graph, short relators become short loops, while missing relators leave branches free to keep expanding.

Cayley Preview

Cayley Graph Regularity Is A Generator Audit

Uniform Move Rule

For a fixed generating set, every vertex has the same menu of right-multiplication moves.

  • Graph degree checks whether the construction treated all group elements equally.
  • Loops and duplicate inverse moves can change the displayed degree count.
  • The chapter artifact records a singleton degree set: \(\{3\}\).
artifact: dihedral-degree-regularity.pngrecorded degree set: {3}
Stress that regularity is not an accident of drawing style. Every group element has the same available generator moves, so the same degree appears at every vertex when the generating set is fixed. The degree histogram is a compact audit that the graph was built uniformly.
Finiteness Conditions

Finite Generation Is Not Finite Presentation

Finitely Generated

A finite alphabet reaches every group element.

\(|S| < \infty\)

Finitely Presented

A finite alphabet plus a finite list of relator rules describes the group.

\(|S|, |R| < \infty\)
finite alphabet is weakerfinite rulebook is stronger
This is the caution slide. Finite generation gives a finite alphabet, while finite presentation also gives a finite rulebook. The gap matters because a short alphabet can still hide infinitely many necessary relations, and algorithmic questions about whether words collapse can become genuinely hard.
New Groups From Old

Direct Products: Independent Coordinates

Direct Product
\((g,h)(g',h')=(gg',hh')\)
  • Factor moves commute because they affect different coordinates.
  • Cayley previews often resemble grids or layered boxes.
  • Product geometry is a first test case for assembling large graphs.
independent coordinatescommuting generator families
Explain direct products as independent motion in two coordinates. A generator from the first factor moves horizontally, and a generator from the second factor moves vertically. The grid preview is not the whole story for every product, but it shows why product constructions often create layered metric geometry.
Extensions

Extensions: Fibers Over A Quotient

Extension Pattern
\(1 \rightarrow N \rightarrow G \rightarrow Q \rightarrow 1\)
  • \(N\) is the kernel, so it sits inside each fiber.
  • \(Q\) records the coarse base motion.
  • A semidirect product adds an action of \(Q\) on \(N\).
kernel fibersquotient base with possible twist
Use the fiber picture to separate kernel and quotient information. An extension places a normal subgroup over each quotient element, while a semidirect product remembers how the quotient acts on the kernel. The twist arrows warn students that assembling groups is not always a rectangular product.
Free Products

Alternating Syllables, Then Optional Glue

Free Product

Words alternate nontrivial syllables from the factors, with no cross-factor relation unless it is explicitly imposed.

Amalgamation

Specified subgroups are identified first, so the resulting normal forms carry a controlled glue.

free product: alternating normal formamalgamation: selected subgroup identification
Free products should look like alternating syllables with no forced cancellation between factors. Amalgamation adds a controlled glue: selected subgroups are identified before the alternating word structure expands. This slide previews why Bass-Serre pictures later turn algebraic splittings into trees.
HNN Extensions

A Stable Letter Adds A Corridor

Corridor Relation
\(t a t^{-1} = \vartheta(a)\)
  • Subgroups \(A\) and \(B\) of a base group are identified by an isomorphism.
  • The new generator \(t\) transports one side to the other.
  • This is a presentation-level preview of actions on trees.
presentation adds a new directiontree actions are waiting nearby
Treat the stable letter as a corridor generator. It moves from one embedded subgroup to another according to a specified isomorphism, so the group gains a new direction while preserving a prescribed identification. This construction belongs in the deck because it is a standard bridge from presentations to group actions on trees.
