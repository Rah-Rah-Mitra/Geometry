Points and Planes: Duality in $\mathbb{P}^3$

Homogeneous point

Finite $\mathbf{X}=(x,y,z,1)^\top$; ideal point has last coordinate zero.

Homogeneous plane

$\boldsymbol\pi=(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3,\pi_4)^\top$, equality up to scale.

Duality

Swap the roles of point and plane; every incidence statement still holds.

Incidence

$$\boldsymbol\pi^\top \mathbf{X} = 0$$

Plane from three points

$$\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{X}_1^\top\\\mathbf{X}_2^\top\\\mathbf{X}_3^\top\end{bmatrix}\boldsymbol\pi = \mathbf{0}$$

Projective Transport: How $H$ Acts

Points

A $4\times4$ collineation sends $\mathbf{X}\mapsto H\mathbf{X}$.

Planes

Planes transform by the inverse transpose, preserving incidence.

The invariant

Incidence stays at numerical zero; the notebook audits residuals at machine precision with $\det H \neq 0$.

Transport rules

$$\mathbf{X}' = H\mathbf{X}, \qquad \boldsymbol\pi' = H^{-\top}\boldsymbol\pi$$

Incidence preserved

$$\boldsymbol\pi'^\top \mathbf{X}' = \boldsymbol\pi^\top H^{-1} H \mathbf{X} = \boldsymbol\pi^\top \mathbf{X}$$

Lines: Plücker Coordinates and the Klein Quadric

One cross product fails

A 3D line needs six numbers, not three.

Plücker matrix

Skew $4\times4$ built from two points $\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}$.

Two representations

Point-built $L$ and plane-built dual $L^{*}$ describe the same line, linked by duality.

Plücker matrix

$$L = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^\top - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^\top$$

Klein constraint

$$l_{12}l_{34}+l_{13}l_{42}+l_{14}l_{23}=0$$

Quadrics and Dual Quadrics

Point quadric

Symmetric $4\times4$ matrix $Q$; the locus is $\mathbf{X}^\top Q\mathbf{X}=0$.

Dual quadric

Equation on tangent planes via $Q^{*}\sim Q^{-1}$.

Stays symmetric

Any collineation preserves symmetry of $Q$ and $Q^{*}$.

Point and dual loci

$$\mathbf{X}^\top Q\,\mathbf{X}=0, \qquad \boldsymbol\pi^\top Q^{*}\boldsymbol\pi=0$$

Transformation

$$Q' = H^{-\top} Q\, H^{-1}, \qquad Q^{*\prime} = H Q^{*} H^\top$$

The Plane at Infinity $\pi_\infty$

Affine upgrade

$\pi_\infty=(0,0,0,1)^\top$ in an affine frame.

Fixed by affinities

An affine map fixes $\pi_\infty$ as a set; a general projective map moves it.

Parallelism

Parallel planes meet in a line that lies entirely inside $\pi_\infty$.

Affine invariance

$$H_A^{-\top}\,\pi_\infty \sim \pi_\infty$$

Projective frame

$$H_P^{-\top}\,\pi_\infty \not\sim \pi_\infty$$

The Absolute Conic $\Omega_\infty$

Metric upgrade

$\Omega_\infty$ lives on $\pi_\infty$ and encodes angles.

No real points

It is a conic of complex directions; teach it through polarity.

Absolute dual quadric

$Q^{*}_\infty$ packs $\pi_\infty$ and $\Omega_\infty$ into one $4\times4$ object.

Angle from $Q^{*}_\infty$

$$\cos\theta = \dfrac{\boldsymbol\pi_1^\top Q^{*}_\infty \boldsymbol\pi_2}{\sqrt{(\boldsymbol\pi_1^\top Q^{*}_\infty\boldsymbol\pi_1)(\boldsymbol\pi_2^\top Q^{*}_\infty\boldsymbol\pi_2)}}$$

Null direction

$$Q^{*}_\infty\,\pi_\infty = \mathbf{0}$$

Printed pp. 65-86The Absolute Conic
Place the absolute conic Omega-infinity on the plane at infinity as the metric upgrade object. It has no real point locus, so teach it through polarity and conjugacy: orthogonal directions are conjugate with respect to it. The absolute dual quadric packages pi-infinity and the conic into one four by four object.
