Residual Error Is Not Estimation Error

Residual error

Estimate $\hat X$ versus the noisy measurement $X$. What the fit leaves behind.

Estimation error

Estimate $\hat X$ versus the noise-free truth $\bar X$. What we actually care about.

The trap

Four points interpolate exactly: zero residual, yet noisy transfer.

Two distances

$e_{\text{res}}=\lVert X-\hat X\rVert,\quad e_{\text{est}}=\lVert \bar X-\hat X\rVert$

More data, opposite trends

Extra correspondences raise the residual but constrain the truth, so estimation error falls.

Printed pp. 132-150Residual versus Estimation Error
Stress that these two errors answer different questions. Residual compares the estimate against noisy measurements; estimation error compares it against the noise-free truth. Four correspondences interpolate perfectly, so residual is zero yet the transfer is noisy. Adding points raises the residual but constrains the truth, lowering estimation error.

Measurement Space and the Model Manifold

Measurement vector

$X$ stacks all observed image coordinates into one high-dimensional point.

Model manifold

$\mathcal S_M$ is every $X$ reachable by some parameter vector $P$.

Maximum likelihood

Gaussian noise makes ML the nearest point of $\mathcal S_M$ to $X$.

Printed pp. 132-150Measurement and Model Manifold
Set up the geometric picture. Measurements live in a high-dimensional space; the model manifold is every measurement reachable by some parameter vector. Maximum likelihood under Gaussian noise finds the nearest manifold point. This nearest-point view is what turns estimation into orthogonal projection on the following slide.

The Tangent-Plane Projection Identity

Linearize

Replace the curved manifold by its tangent plane at the estimate.

Orthogonality

The residual leg is normal to the tangent; the estimation leg lies in it.

Pythagoras

$\lVert X-\bar X\rVert^2=\lVert X-\hat X\rVert^2+\lVert \hat X-\bar X\rVert^2$

Printed pp. 132-150Tangent-Plane Projection Identity
Explain the linearization. Replacing the curved manifold by its tangent plane makes maximum likelihood an orthogonal projection, so the measured-to-true vector splits into estimated-to-true plus measured-to-estimated legs that are perpendicular. Pythagoras then gives the squared-error identity the notebook verifies to machine precision, the backbone of the bounds.

Lower Bounds for Residual and Estimation Error

General bound

$e_{\text{res}}\!\approx\!\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac{N-d}{N}},\quad e_{\text{est}}\!\approx\!\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac{d}{N}}$

One-image noise

$N=2n$ measurements, $d=8$ homography parameters.

Two-image noise

$N=4n$ and $d=2n+8$: point positions are estimated too.

Printed pp. 132-150Performance Bounds
Walk through the bound formulas. With N measurements and d essential parameters, residual scales with the parameters projected away and estimation with the parameters kept. For one-image homography noise, N equals two n and d equals eight; the two-image case also estimates point positions, so d grows. Residual rises while estimation falls.

Forward Propagation: the Jacobian Sandwich

Linearize $f$

Near the operating point, $f(X+\delta)\approx f(X)+J\delta$ with $J=\partial f/\partial X$.

Propagation law

$\Sigma_{f}=J\,\Sigma_{X}\,J^{\!\top}$

Workhorse

Maps any input covariance to the covariance of a derived quantity.

Printed pp. 132-150Covariance by Forward Propagation
Introduce first-order propagation. A function f carries an input covariance to an output covariance through the Jacobian sandwich J Sigma J transpose. This forward rule is the workhorse: feed it the covariance of measurements or of homography entries and it returns the covariance of any derived quantity, such as a transferred point.

Backward Propagation to Estimated Parameters

Information matrix

$\Sigma_{P}=\big(J^{\!\top}\Sigma_{X}^{-1}J\big)^{+}$

Scale null space

A homography is homogeneous, so one direction is unconstrained.

Pseudo-inverse

$J^{\!\top}\Sigma^{-1}J$ is rank deficient; use $(\cdot)^{+}$, not a plain inverse.

Notebook check

The $9\times 9$ information matrix has rank exactly $8$ on four correspondences.

Forward vs backward

Forward sends $X\!\to\!f$; backward recovers parameter covariance from data covariance.

Printed pp. 132-150Backward Propagation to Parameters
Contrast backward propagation. To get the covariance of estimated parameters we invert the information matrix J transpose Sigma inverse J. Because a homography is homogeneous, it has a scale null direction, so the information matrix is rank deficient and we take a pseudo-inverse. The notebook confirms the information rank is eight.

Confidence Ellipses and Mahalanobis Distance

Mahalanobis distance

$\delta^2=(x-\mu)^{\!\top}\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)\sim\chi^2_k$

Axes from eigenvectors

Eigenvectors of $\Sigma$ orient the ellipse; eigenvalues set axis lengths.

Level from chi-square

Radius $\sqrt{\chi^2_k(\alpha)}$ fixes the confidence probability.

Printed pp. 132-150Confidence Ellipses
Make covariance visual. The eigenvectors of a two-by-two covariance give the ellipse axes and its eigenvalues set their lengths; scaling by the chi-square quantile fixes the confidence level. Mahalanobis distance is the natural metric, and its square follows a chi-square law, so a chosen probability picks the ellipse radius directly.

Point-Transfer Covariance Under a Mapping

Transfer covariance

$\Sigma_{x'}=J_h\,\Sigma_h\,J_h^{\!\top}$

Inside the support

On the unit-circle correspondences the ellipses stay tight.

Extrapolation risk

RMS uncertainty grows like $\sqrt{1+r^4}$ away from the center.

Printed pp. 132-150Transfer Covariance Under a Homography
Connect propagation to point transfer. The transfer Jacobian maps homography-entry covariance to a point covariance, J h Sigma h J h transpose. On the unit-circle correspondences the ellipses stay tight inside the support but balloon outside it; the radial uncertainty grows like root one plus r to the fourth, exposing extrapolation risk.

Monte Carlo: Covariance from a Cloud

Perturb and re-estimate

Add noise to the data, run the estimator, repeat many times.

Fit the spread

The sample covariance of the output cloud, fitted as an ellipse.

Empirical covariance

$\hat\Sigma=\tfrac{1}{M-1}\sum_{i}(x_i-\bar x)(x_i-\bar x)^{\!\top}$

Printed pp. 132-150Monte Carlo Covariance
Present Monte Carlo as the empirical audit. We perturb the data many times, re-estimate, and collect a cloud of outputs whose sample covariance we fit with an ellipse. It needs no Jacobian and so validates the linear theory, exposing bias, variance, and degeneracy that small-noise formulas might silently miss.

A Small Residual Can Hide Degeneracy

Well-spread layout

Grid correspondences give a tight, near-isotropic transfer cloud.

Nearly collinear layout

Almost the same training residual, vastly larger transfer covariance.

Notebook result

$\dfrac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\text{collinear}}}{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\text{spread}}}>10^{4}$

Printed pp. 132-150Configuration Hides Degeneracy
Drive home the warning from the Monte Carlo run. Well-spread and nearly collinear layouts gave almost identical training residuals, yet the collinear probe-transfer covariance was tens of thousands of times larger. A small reprojection residual does not certify a reliable transformation; the point configuration governs covariance far more than the residual.

An Evaluation Workflow You Can Reuse

Report both errors

Residual and estimation RMS; a low residual alone is not enough.

Propagate to the target

Carry covariance to the transferred point, line, or triangulation you will use.

Compare to the bound

Benchmark the estimator against the tangent-plane lower bound.

Audit with Monte Carlo

Sweep noise levels and at least two configurations.

Three distinct rulers

$e_{\text{res}},\quad \Sigma_P=(J^{\!\top}\Sigma^{-1}J)^{+},\quad \Sigma_{x'}=J_h\Sigma_h J_h^{\!\top}$

The core lesson

Evaluation is geometric: residual, parameter covariance, and extrapolation each measure something different.

Printed pp. 132-150Evaluation Workflow Recap
Close with the workflow. Always report residual and estimation error, propagate covariance to the quantity you will actually use, compare against the lower bound, and confirm with Monte Carlo across noise levels and configurations. Evaluation is geometric: residual, parameter covariance, and extrapolation each measure something different.
