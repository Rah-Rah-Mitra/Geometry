Back-Projection: Camera Rows are Planes

Line to plane

An image line $\ell$ back-projects to the plane $\pi = P^{\!\top}\ell$ through the camera center.

Point to ray

An image point back-projects to a ray, the meet of two such planes.

Incidence

Planes that share a world point $X$ give $\ell^{\!\top}(PX)=0$.

Printed pp. 411-433Back-Projection: Rows are Planes
Every row of a camera matrix is a plane in homogeneous three-space, and an image line back-projects to the plane formed from those rows. A point back-projects to a ray, the meet of two planes. This reading is the foundation: incidence in the image becomes a statement about planes sharing a world point in three dimensions.

The Stacked Camera Matrix

Joint constraint

$$M = \begin{bmatrix} P_1 & x_1 & 0 & 0\\ P_2 & 0 & x_2 & 0\\ \vdots & & & \ddots \end{bmatrix}$$

Common point

$X$ exists $\iff$ $M$ is rank deficient.

Minors vanish

Rank deficiency forces every maximal $4\times4$ minor of the stacked rows to be zero.

The engine

Expanding those $4\times4$ minors generates every multi-view tensor in the chapter.

Up to scale

All homogeneous equalities hold up to a nonzero scale factor.

Printed pp. 411-433The Stacked Camera Matrix
Collect the rows of all the cameras that view a point into one tall matrix and append the image measurements. A common world point exists exactly when this matrix is rank deficient, so every maximal minor must vanish. Expanding those four-by-four minors is the engine that produces every multi-view tensor in the chapter.

One $4\times4$ Determinant, Three Tensors

$F$

Two rows from each of two cameras.

$\mathcal{T}$

Two rows from one camera, one each from two others.

$\mathcal{Q}$

One row from each of four cameras.

Printed pp. 411-433Choosing Rows: One Determinant, Three Tensors
The tensors differ only in how the four rows of a four-by-four determinant are drawn from the cameras. Two rows from each of two cameras give the fundamental matrix; two from one camera and one each from two others give a trifocal entry; one row from each of four cameras gives a quadrifocal entry. Same recipe, different view counts.

The Bifocal Tensor: Fundamental Matrix

Construction

Delete one row from each camera; the remaining four rows give a determinant bilinear in $x_1,x_2$.

Bilinear (epipolar) relation

$$x_2^{\!\top} F\, x_1 = 0$$

Rank two

$F$ has rank $2$; its left and right null vectors are the epipoles.

One relation

A point match yields a single bilinear epipolar equation per pair.

Printed pp. 411-433The Bifocal Tensor: Fundamental Matrix
With two views, deleting one row from each camera leaves four rows whose determinant is a bilinear form in the two image points. Collecting these gives the fundamental matrix, the two-view tensor. The epipolar relation states that corresponding points satisfy this single bilinear equation, and the matrix has rank two.

The Trifocal Tensor

Shape

A $3\times3\times3$ tensor $\mathcal{T}_i^{\,jk}$.

Rows

Two rows from one camera, one row from each of the other two views.

Trilinear relation

$$\ell'_j\,\ell''_k\,\mathcal{T}_i^{\,jk} = \ell_i$$

Printed pp. 411-433The Trifocal Tensor
Three views give a three-by-three-by-three tensor. Each entry is a determinant using two rows from one distinguished camera and one row from each of the other two. The resulting trilinear relations tie a point to lines through its images, and a point-line-line incidence produces residuals that vanish on consistent data.

The Quadrifocal Tensor

Shape

A $3\times3\times3\times3$ tensor $\mathcal{Q}^{ijkl}$ with $3^4=81$ entries.

Rows

Exactly one row from each of the four cameras.

Quadrilinear relation

$$\ell_i\,\ell'_j\,\ell''_k\,\ell'''_l\,\mathcal{Q}^{ijkl} = 0$$

Printed pp. 411-433The Quadrifocal Tensor
Four views give a three-by-three-by-three-by-three tensor with eighty-one entries, each a determinant taking exactly one row from each of the four cameras. The quadrilinear relations contract this tensor against four image lines through corresponding points. This is the four-view tensor, and the chapter argues it is the last genuinely new one.

Why Four Views Is the Maximum

Only four rows

A $4\times4$ determinant has four rows, so it samples at most four distinct cameras.

No fifth tensor

Any five-view constraint factors through $F$, $\mathcal{T}$, and $\mathcal{Q}$.

Hierarchy closes

$\mathcal{Q}$ is the last genuinely new multi-view tensor.

Printed pp. 411-433Why Four Views Is the Maximum
A four-by-four determinant has only four rows, so it can sample at most four distinct cameras. Any constraint among five or more views factors into the bifocal, trifocal, and quadrifocal tensors already in hand. There is no genuinely new five-view tensor; the quadrifocal tensor closes the hierarchy at four views.

Counting Independent Equations

Array size vs rank

A four-view point track writes $81$ rows, one per free triple of indices.

Per-view skew rank

$$S^{\!\top}S = \lVert x\rVert^2 I - x x^{\!\top},\quad \operatorname{rank} S = 2$$

Kronecker cap

Four rank-$2$ factors give rank $2^4=16$ per track.

Printed pp. 411-433Counting Independent Equations
A four-view point track writes eighty-one rows because each free index runs over three values, yet each view contributes only a rank-two skew matrix. The Kronecker structure caps the rank at two to the fourth, sixteen, independent equations per track. The pattern sixteen, then trilinear, then bilinear mirrors the descending view counts.

Tracks, Dependencies, and the Null Direction

Independent-equation curve

$$r(n) = 16n - \tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$$

Not additive

Pairwise dependencies subtract rank, so tracks do not simply add $16$ apiece.

Six tracks

The formula reaches $81$, but the true tensor sits in the nullspace.

Printed pp. 411-433Tracks, Dependencies, and the Null Direction
Stacking several point tracks does not simply add sixteen equations apiece; pairwise dependencies subtract rank along the curve sixteen-n minus n-choose-two. Six camera-consistent tracks would reach eighty-one, but the true tensor sits in the homogeneous nullspace, so the rank caps at eighty and the tensor is recovered up to scale.

Correspondence Counting Regimes

Projective

$11m - 15$ camera degrees of freedom.

Affine

$8m - 12$; the plane at infinity is fixed.

Plane homographies

$3m - 4$; only the last camera columns remain free.

Printed pp. 411-433Correspondence Counting Regimes
The remaining camera freedom decides how many correspondences are needed. General projective cameras carry eleven-m minus fifteen degrees of freedom; affine cameras drop to eight-m minus twelve; and known plane homographies leave only three-m minus four. Coplanar reference points therefore simplify the four-view problem far more than a small perturbation would suggest.

Synthesis: One Determinant Pattern

Joint image

Every constraint is a rank or minor condition on the stacked cameras.

One pattern, three reads

$F$, $\mathcal{T}$, $\mathcal{Q}$ are the same determinant at $2$, $3$, $4$ views.

Stable equations

Orthonormal lines through each point recover the $16$ independent rows cleanly.

Recap of view-counts

$$\underbrace{F}_{2}\;\to\;\underbrace{\mathcal{T}}_{3}\;\to\;\underbrace{\mathcal{Q}}_{4}\;\to\;\varnothing$$

Bottom line

The quadrifocal tensor is the last new tensor; everything else reduces to it.

Printed pp. 411-433Synthesis: One Determinant Pattern
All multi-view constraints reduce to rank and minor conditions on the joint image of the stacked cameras. The fundamental, trifocal, and quadrifocal tensors are one determinant pattern read at two, three, and four views. Choosing orthonormal lines through each point recovers exactly the independent equations with a controlled numerical scale.
