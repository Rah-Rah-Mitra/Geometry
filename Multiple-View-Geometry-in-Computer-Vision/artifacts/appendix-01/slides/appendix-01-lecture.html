Two Kinds of Index

Contravariant (upper)

Coordinates of a point $x^i$. They scale inversely to the basis.

Covariant (lower)

Components of a line or plane $l_i$. They scale with the basis.

Why it matters

The opposite behaviour is exactly what keeps $l_i x^i$ meaningful.

Pairing

$l_i\, x^i \;=\; \text{scalar, frame-independent}$

Reading slots

An upper slot expects a vector argument; a lower slot expects a covector. The position of the index tells you how the entity reacts to a re-coordinatisation.

Printed pp. 562-564Two Kinds of Index
Distinguish upper and lower slots from the start. A point carries an upper, contravariant index because its coordinates scale inversely with the basis, while a line or plane carries a lower, covariant index because its components scale with the basis. That bookkeeping keeps proportionalities consistent through any change of frame.

Transformation Under a Basis Change

Point: inverse

New coordinates solve $A\,\tilde x = x$, so $x^i$ transforms by $A^{-1}$.

Line: transpose

Components follow $\tilde l = A^{\top} l$, the dual rule.

Invariant

The contraction $l_i x^i$ is unchanged: the lab confirms it to machine precision.

Printed pp. 562-564Transformation Under a Basis Change
Apply a change of basis given by a matrix and watch the two index types move oppositely. Contravariant coordinates transform by the inverse, covariant components by the transpose. The notebook solves and multiplies both, then confirms that a single scalar contraction returns the same number in either basis.

Einstein Summation Convention

The rule

An index repeated once up and once down in a term is summed over its range.

Dummy vs free

Repeated indices are dummy and renamable; unrepeated indices are free and name the output.

With and without the sign

$\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{3} l_i x^i \;\equiv\; l_i x^i$

Matrix product

$(AB)^i_{\;k} = A^i_{\;j}\,B^j_{\;k}$, the dummy $j$ summed away, the free $i,k$ surviving.

Printed pp. 562-564Einstein Summation Convention
The convention is purely a labour saver: a symbol that appears once up and once down in a term is implicitly summed over its range, so we drop the summation sign. A repeated index is dummy and can be renamed; a free index survives and names the resulting object.

Contraction as the Core Operation

Definition

Setting one upper index equal to one lower index and summing.

Effect

Order drops by two; two tensors are joined into one.

Use

Incidence and correspondence tests are contractions that should vanish.

Printed pp. 562-564Contraction as the Core Operation
Contraction is the engine of the whole notation. Summing a shared upper-lower pair glues two tensors and lowers the total order by two. Every incidence test in the book, a point on a line, a correspondence on the fundamental matrix, is just a contraction whose value should vanish.

Raising and Lowering Indices

The idea

A rank-three object trades an upper index for a lower one and back.

No metric here

Projective space has no privileged metric, so epsilon does the converting.

Vector to antisymmetric matrix

$[a]_{\times}^{\,jk} \;=\; \varepsilon^{ijk} a_i$

Same act, two forms

The cross-product matrix $[a]_\times$ and the vector $a$ are two encodings of one geometric operation, linked by $\varepsilon$.

Printed pp. 562-564Raising and Lowering Indices
A metric or any chosen rank-three object lets us trade an upper index for a lower one and back. In projective vision there is no privileged metric, so raising and lowering is mostly done by the epsilon tensor, which converts between a vector and the antisymmetric object that represents the same geometric act.

The Levi-Civita Epsilon Tensor

Totally antisymmetric

$+1$ on even permutations, $-1$ on odd, $0$ when an index repeats.

Cross product

$(a\times b)_i = \varepsilon_{ijk}\,a^j b^k$.

Determinant

$\det M = \varepsilon_{ijk}\,M^i_{\;1}M^j_{\;2}M^k_{\;3}$.

Printed pp. 562-564The Levi-Civita Epsilon Tensor
Epsilon is totally antisymmetric: plus one on even permutations, minus one on odd, zero when an index repeats. Contracting it with two vectors reproduces their cross product, and contracting it with three reproduces a determinant. The notebook checks both equalities to machine precision against NumPy.

Join of Two Points into a Line

The join

The line through points $x$ and $y$ is their cross product.

Index check

Two upper indices summed away leave one lower index, the covariant slot a line must have.

Duality

The meet of two lines is the same formula with roles swapped.

Line through two points

$l_i \;=\; \varepsilon_{ijk}\, x^j y^k$

Printed pp. 562-564Join of Two Points into a Line
The flagship example: the line through two image points is their cross product, written with epsilon as l_i equals epsilon_ijk x^j y^k. The summed pair of upper indices produces a single lower index, exactly the covariant slot a line should carry. Duality gives the meet of two lines the same way.

The Fundamental Matrix in Index Form

Two lower indices

Write the fundamental matrix as $F_{ij}$, a covariant rank-two object.

Epipolar constraint

Corresponding points contract $F$ to zero.

Epipolar line

$F_{ij}x^j$ is itself a line in the second image.

Correspondence

$x'^{\,i}\, F_{ij}\, x^j \;=\; 0$

Same machinery

The epipole and the line built from points share the antisymmetric $\varepsilon$ structure: $F$ maps a point in one view to its epipolar line in the other.

Printed pp. 562-564The Fundamental Matrix in Index Form
With indices the epipolar constraint is one contraction. Writing F with two lower indices, the relation x-prime to the i F_ij x to the j equals zero says corresponding points contract F to zero. The notebook ties this to the same antisymmetric machinery that built lines from points.

Trifocal and Quadrifocal Tensors

Trifocal

$T_i^{\;jk}$: one lower, two upper indices, relating three views.

Transfer

Contract with a point and two lines to constrain entities across images.

Quadrifocal

$Q^{ijkl}$ extends the pattern to four views.

Trilinear incidence

$x^i\, l'_j\, l''_k\, T_i^{\;jk} \;=\; 0$

Printed pp. 562-564Trifocal and Quadrifocal Tensors
Three and four views need genuine higher-order tensors. The trifocal tensor carries one lower and two upper indices, T_i jk, and contracting it with a point and two lines transfers and constrains entities across three images. The quadrifocal tensor extends the same pattern with four indices to four views.
