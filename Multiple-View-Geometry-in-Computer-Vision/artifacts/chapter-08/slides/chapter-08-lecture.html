Camera Center: Homography vs Parallax

Fixed center

Pure rotation gives a homography; rays keep their identity.

Moved center

Near and far points split: that split is motion parallax.

Conjugacy

$$H = K\,R\,K^{-1}$$

Printed pp. 195-236Camera Center: Homography vs Parallax
Stress that a fixed camera center makes every change a pure rotation, so points along one ray stay coincident and the induced map is the conjugacy K R K-inverse. The moment the center translates, near and far points split into parallax, which is the first depth signal in any image sequence we encounter.

The Image of the Absolute Conic

Pixels to rays

Calibration sends an image point to a direction $d = K^{-1}x$.

IAC and its dual

$$\omega = K^{-T}K^{-1}, \quad \omega^{*} = KK^{T}$$

Recover $K$

Cholesky of $\omega^{-1}$ returns the intrinsics directly.

Printed pp. 195-236The Image of the Absolute Conic
Introduce omega as the calibration written as a conic, equal to K-inverse-transpose times K-inverse. It depends only on the intrinsics, never on rotation, so it is the bridge between pixel algebra and ray angles. Cholesky of its inverse returns K, which is why omega is the calibration target.

Angles from Image Points

Angle formula

$\omega$ turns ray dot products into image-coordinate algebra.

Cosine via $\omega$

$$\cos\theta = \frac{v_i^{T}\omega\,v_j}{\sqrt{v_i^{T}\omega v_i}\,\sqrt{v_j^{T}\omega v_j}}$$

Orthogonality

Perpendicular directions give $v_i^{T}\omega\,v_j = 0$.

Printed pp. 195-236Angles from Image Points
Show that omega turns dot products of rays into a measurable form, so the cosine of the angle between two image points uses omega in numerator and denominator. The key corollary: orthogonal directions force v-i transpose omega v-j equal to zero, a single linear constraint per orthogonal pair.

Calibration from Orthogonal Vanishing Points

Three orthogonal directions

A Manhattan scene gives three mutually perpendicular vanishing points.

Linear system

$$v_x^{T}\omega v_y = v_y^{T}\omega v_z = v_z^{T}\omega v_x = 0$$

Walk through the calibration pipeline: each orthogonal vanishing-point pair gives one linear equation in the entries of omega. With square pixels three mutually orthogonal vanishing points suffice; stack the constraints, solve the nullspace by SVD, then Cholesky-factor to read focal length and principal point.

Rotation Invariance of $\omega$

Pose cancels

Rotating the camera changes the homography but not $\omega$.

Symbolic check

$$(KR)^{-T}(KR)^{-1} = K^{-T}K^{-1} = \omega$$

Why it matters

Calibration needs no known orientation of the camera.

Printed pp. 195-236Rotation Invariance of Omega
Reinforce the symbolic check from the notebook: replacing K by K R inside the IAC formula leaves omega unchanged because the rotation cancels with its inverse transpose. Calibration therefore does not require knowing camera orientation, which is what makes vanishing-point calibration practical from a single uncontrolled photo.
