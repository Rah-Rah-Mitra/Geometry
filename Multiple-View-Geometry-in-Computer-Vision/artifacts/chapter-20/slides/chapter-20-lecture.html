Duality of Points and Cameras

Carlsson-Weinshall duality: swap the two roles

Primal reading

$N$ cameras observing $M$ scene points.

Dual reading

$M$ cameras observing $N$ scene points.

The bridge

Fix a projective basis of reference points; centres and points become interchangeable.

Printed pp. 502-514Duality of Points and Cameras
This chapter runs across printed pages 502 to 514 and develops Carlsson-Weinshall duality. The headline is simple and surprising: once we fix a projective basis of reference points, scene points and camera centres become interchangeable objects, so any theorem about cameras viewing points has a free dual about points viewing cameras.

The Reduced Camera

Fix a basis

Four world basis points $E_1,\dots,E_4$ project to the image basis $e_1,\dots,e_4$.

Four parameters

A general camera collapses to the diagonal data $a,b,c,d$.

Reduced camera matrix

$$P(a,b,c,d)=\begin{bmatrix} a & 0 & 0 & d\\ 0 & b & 0 & d\\ 0 & 0 & c & d\end{bmatrix}$$

Why diagonal

The fixed basis removes all but four degrees of freedom.

Printed pp. 502-514The Reduced Camera
Everything rests on the reduced camera. Choosing four world basis points that map to the four image basis points strips a general camera down to four free parameters a, b, c, d on the diagonal. In this frame the projection equation becomes a symmetric template that we can read in two directions.

The Symmetric Projection Product

One product, two readings

$$P(a,b,c,d)\,X=\begin{bmatrix} aX+dT\\ bY+dT\\ cZ+dT\end{bmatrix}=P^\star(X)\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\\c\\d\end{bmatrix}$$

The swap

Camera data $(a,b,c,d)$ and point data $(X,Y,Z,T)$ exchange roles.

Printed pp. 502-514The Symmetric Projection Product
Walk through the central identity. The reduced projection of a point has entries aX+dT, bY+dT, cZ+dT. The same three numbers arise when a reduced camera assembled from the point coordinates acts on the parameter vector a,b,c,d. That coordinate-level symmetry is the engine of the whole duality.

Interchanging Points and Cameras

Map of the swap

Each camera centre becomes a point; each point becomes a camera centre.

Measurements kept

The image incidences are preserved exactly under the exchange.

Solvability transfers

A solvable primal gives a solvable dual of swapped size.

The interchange

$$\{C_i\}_{i=1}^{N},\,\{X_j\}_{j=1}^{M}\;\longmapsto\;\{X_j\}_{j=1}^{M}\text{ as cams},\,\{C_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\text{ as pts}$$

Projective, not metric

The swap lives in the fixed projective frame, not in Euclidean structure.

Printed pp. 502-514Interchanging Points and Cameras
Now state the interchange cleanly. A configuration of N cameras and M points maps to a dual configuration of M cameras and N points, with centres and points swapping seats. The image measurements are preserved as incidences, so a solvable primal problem guarantees a solvable dual problem of swapped dimensions.

Incidence Is the Invariant

Back-projection identity

$$\mathbf{l}^\top P\,X = (P^\top \mathbf{l})^\top X = 0$$

Read both ways

An image line through $x=PX$ back-projects to a plane $P^\top\mathbf{l}$ through $X$.

Printed pp. 502-514Incidence Is the Invariant
Stress what actually survives the swap. The scalar l-transpose P X is symmetric in point and camera data, and equals the back-projected plane acting on the point. Image lines through a measured point back-project to planes meeting along the camera ray. Incidence, not metric structure, is what duality preserves.

Canonical Image Frames

Pick four anchors

In each view, choose four image points to play the basis role.

Send to the basis

A homography maps them to $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and the centroid $e_4=(1,1,1)$.

Legality

Valid only when no three anchors are collinear.

Printed pp. 502-514Canonical Image Frames
Explain the first algorithmic step. In every view we pick four image points and apply a projective transformation sending them to the canonical image basis e1, e2, e3 and the centroid e4. This is legal precisely when no three anchors are collinear, and it makes the reduced template apply view by view.

The Dual Algorithm: Transpose and Extend

1. Canonicalize

Send the four anchors to the image basis in every view.

2. Transpose

Drop the special rows; transpose the free $m\times n$ block.

3. Extend and solve

Append four synthetic $e_k$ rows, solve the dual, map back.

Describe the bookkeeping. After canonicalizing, drop the four special rows, transpose the remaining m-by-n measurement block, and append four synthetic rows carrying e1 through e4. Those synthetic rows force the dual solution to use reduced cameras. Solve the dual reconstruction, then map the answer back to the original problem.

One algorithm

Under the swap one algorithm services both, and counting arguments for one problem immediately yield the minimal-data count for its dual.

The Reduced Fundamental Matrix

Synthetic correspondences

The four $e_k\leftrightarrow e_k$ matches are already absorbed.

Vanishing structure

Zero diagonal and zero total sum leave five free parameters.

Six-point algorithm

Each extra dual correspondence adds one linear equation.

Reduced $F$ constraints

$$F_{kk}=0,\qquad \sum_{i,j}F_{ij}=0,\qquad \det F = 0$$

Rank two

The determinant condition keeps $F$ a valid fundamental matrix.

Printed pp. 502-514The Reduced Fundamental Matrix
Specialize to two views. A reduced fundamental matrix has already absorbed the four synthetic correspondences e_k to e_k, so its diagonal and its total sum vanish, leaving five free parameters. Each extra same-image dual correspondence adds one linear equation, which is what makes the six-point dual algorithm work.

The Reduced Trifocal Tensor

Three views, seven points

The dual route uses a reduced trifocal tensor.

No tidy template

A small explicit parametrization is not available here.

Constrained least squares

$$\min_{t}\;\|A\,t\|^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad C\,t = 0$$

Exact then data

Synthetic constraints $C t=0$ are exact; real correspondences minimize inside the nullspace.

Seven-point result

This delivers the dual seven-point reconstruction.

Printed pp. 502-514The Reduced Trifocal Tensor
Extend to three views and seven points. A neat small parametrization is not available, so the safe pattern is constrained least squares: synthetic correspondences give exact linear constraints C t equals zero, and real correspondences give a data term minimized inside that nullspace. This delivers the seven-point dual result.

Conditioning Caveat and Takeaways

Recap

Fix the basis, exploit the symmetric template, swap roles, reuse one result as two.

Failure mode

Legal geometry can still be a poor place to measure error.

Invariant

Incidence survives every relabeling step.

Printed pp. 502-514Conditioning Caveat and Takeaways
Close on the practical caveat and the summary. Canonicalizing with nearly collinear anchors is geometrically legal but numerically dangerous, inflating the homography condition number and distorting noise. Recap: fix the basis, exploit the symmetric template, swap points and cameras, and reuse one reconstruction result as two.
