Computation of the Fundamental Matrix F

From noisy point correspondences to a rank-two epipolar geometry

The data

A set of image correspondences $x_i \leftrightarrow x_i'$ between two views, some of them mismatched.

The goal

A consistent $3\times3$ fundamental matrix $F$ of rank two encoding the epipolar geometry.

The challenge

Noise, conditioning, the singularity constraint, and outliers must all be handled.

The Epipolar Constraint

Defining equation

$$x'^{\top} F\, x = 0$$

Linear in the entries

The bilinear form is linear in the nine unknown entries of $F$, so one correspondence gives one equation.

Scale and rank

$F$ is defined up to scale and must satisfy $\det F = 0$: eight degrees of freedom.

One row per match

$$\big[x'x,\,x'y,\,x',\,y'x,\,y'y,\,y',\,x,\,y,\,1\big]\,\mathbf{f}=0$$

Reading it

$\mathbf{f}$ stacks the entries of $F$ row-wise; the coefficients come from the two image points $(x,y)$ and $(x',y')$.

Printed pp. 279-309The Epipolar Constraint
Open with the single algebraic fact the whole chapter rests on: every correspondence satisfies x-prime transpose F x equals zero. Show that this bilinear identity is linear in the nine entries of F, so each match supplies exactly one homogeneous equation. That observation is what makes a direct linear solution possible.

The Linear (8-Point) Algorithm

Homogeneous system

$$A\,\mathbf{f}=0,\qquad A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 9}$$

Solve by SVD

$\mathbf{f}$ is the right singular vector of the smallest singular value of $A$.

Why "eight"

Eight matches fix the scale-free nine-vector; more points give a least-squares fit.

Printed pp. 279-309The Linear Eight-Point Algorithm
Stack the per-correspondence rows into a design matrix A and solve A f equals zero by SVD, taking the singular vector of the smallest singular value. Emphasize that eight points fix the scale-free nine-vector, but the raw solution ignores the singularity of F and is sensitive to coordinate scaling.

Why Normalization Matters

Raw pixels misbehave

Coordinates in the hundreds make columns of $A$ differ by orders of magnitude; the SVD spends precision on units, not geometry.

Hartley normalization

$$\hat{x}=T x,\quad \tfrac{1}{n}\sum\lVert\hat{x}_i\rVert^2=2$$

Recipe

Translate centroid to origin, scale RMS radius to $\sqrt{2}$, independently in each image.

Printed pp. 279-309Why Normalization Matters
Raw pixel coordinates run into the hundreds, so columns of A differ by orders of magnitude and the SVD wastes precision on units. Hartley normalization translates each image's centroid to the origin and scales the RMS radius to root two, dramatically lowering the condition number before the null vector is trusted.

The Normalized 8-Point Pipeline

Denormalization

$$F = T'^{\top}\,\hat{F}\,T$$

Each stage matters

Skip normalization and the estimate degrades; skip rank enforcement and the epipole is ill-defined.

Output

A rank-two $F$ in the original pixel frame, ready for residual scoring.

Printed pp. 279-309The Normalized Eight-Point Pipeline
Walk the four-stage pipeline: normalize each image by a similarity T, solve the linear system by SVD, enforce rank two, then denormalize with T-prime transpose F T. The notebook measures the conditioning improvement factor at over one hundred thousand, which is the whole reason normalization is mandatory, not optional.

Enforcing the Rank-2 Constraint

Why singular

$\det F = 0$ guarantees all epipolar lines pass through a common epipole $e'$.

Closest singular matrix

$$F=U\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,0)\,V^{\top}$$

Procedure

Take the SVD of the linear estimate, zero the smallest singular value $\sigma_3$, reconstruct.

Printed pp. 279-309Enforcing the Rank-2 Constraint
The linear estimate is generally full rank, but a true fundamental matrix is singular so that all epipolar lines meet at the epipole. Take the SVD, set the smallest singular value to zero, and reconstruct. This Frobenius-closest singular matrix is what restores a consistent epipole and pencil of epipolar lines.

The 7-Point Algorithm

Seven matches

The system $A\mathbf{f}=0$ now has a two-dimensional null space spanned by $F_1, F_2$.

Pencil of solutions

$$F=\alpha F_1+(1-\alpha)F_2$$

Impose singularity

Use $\det F=0$ to pin down $\alpha$ instead of discarding it.

Cubic in α

$$\det\!\big(\alpha F_1+(1-\alpha)F_2\big)=0$$

One or three roots

Real roots give candidate $F$ matrices, each already rank two by construction.

Use

Minimal sample size of seven is ideal as the RANSAC hypothesis generator.

Printed pp. 279-309The 7-Point Algorithm
With only seven correspondences the linear system has a two-dimensional null space spanning F1 and F2. Impose the singularity constraint det of alpha F1 plus one minus alpha F2 equals zero, a cubic in alpha. The real roots give one or three candidate fundamental matrices that are already rank two by construction.

Algebraic vs Geometric Error

Algebraic error

$\sum (x_i'^{\top}Fx_i)^2$ is what the linear method minimizes; convenient but not in pixels.

Sampson distance

$$\frac{(x'^{\top}Fx)^2}{(Fx)_1^2+(Fx)_2^2+(F^{\top}x')_1^2+(F^{\top}x')_2^2}$$

Practical residual

Sampson is the first-order reprojection error and the score used to judge inliers.

Printed pp. 279-309Algebraic vs Geometric Error
Contrast the algebraic residual minimized by the linear method with geometrically meaningful error measured in pixels. The reprojection error is the gold standard; the Sampson distance is its first-order approximation, cheap to evaluate and the practical residual used to judge whether a point lies near its epipolar line.

The Gold-Standard Estimate

Reprojection cost

$$\min_{\hat{x}_i,\hat{x}_i'}\sum_i d(x_i,\hat{x}_i)^2+d(x_i',\hat{x}_i')^2$$

Subject to

$\hat{x}_i'^{\top}F\hat{x}_i=0$ for a consistent camera pair $P=[I\,|\,0],\,P'$.

MLE under Gaussian noise

Minimizing reprojection error is maximum likelihood when noise is isotropic Gaussian.

Parameterize through cameras

Estimate $P'$ and 3D points so $F$ stays rank two automatically during optimization.

How to solve

Initialize from the normalized 8-point result, refine with Levenberg-Marquardt; Sampson error is a fast surrogate objective.

Printed pp. 279-309The Gold-Standard MLE
Under Gaussian noise the maximum-likelihood estimate minimizes total reprojection error over a consistent camera pair and a set of estimated 3D points. Parameterize through cameras so F stays rank two automatically, initialize from the normalized eight-point result, and refine with Levenberg-Marquardt. Sampson error gives a fast surrogate objective.

Robust Estimation with RANSAC

The problem

A few mismatches wreck least squares; we need to reject outliers, not just down-weight them.

The loop

Sample a minimal subset, fit $F$, score all matches by Sampson distance, count inliers below a pixel threshold, keep the best.

Result

Inlier median residual far below the rejected outliers in the notebook.

Printed pp. 279-309Robust Estimation with RANSAC
Real match sets contain mismatches that break least squares. RANSAC samples minimal seven or eight point subsets, fits F, scores correspondences by Sampson distance against a pixel threshold, and keeps the hypothesis with the most inliers. The notebook recovers a clean inlier set with median residuals far below the rejected outliers.

Degeneracies and Takeaways

Planar degeneracy

Coplanar points are explained by a homography and leave a larger design-matrix null space; many $F$ fit almost equally well.

The recipe

normalize $\to$ solve $\to$ rank 2 $\to$ score $\to$ refine

Remember

Rank enforcement restores the epipole; Sampson scores inliers; RANSAC rejects mismatches.

Caution

Rectification magnifies a poorly identified epipole, so diagnose $F$ first.

Printed pp. 279-309Degeneracies and Takeaways
Close on the planar degeneracy: coplanar points are explained by a homography and leave a larger design-matrix null space, so many fundamental matrices fit almost equally well. Recap the recipe of normalize, solve, enforce rank two, score robustly, and refine, and warn that rectification magnifies a poorly identified epipole.
