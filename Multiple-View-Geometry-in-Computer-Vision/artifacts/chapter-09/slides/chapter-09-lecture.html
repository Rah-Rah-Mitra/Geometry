Epipolar Geometry and the Fundamental Matrix

The intrinsic geometry of two views, encoded in one matrix $F$

The picture

Two camera centres, a baseline, and a shared 3D point fix a plane that governs all matching.

The algebra

$F$ is the rank-two map that turns that geometry into the relation $x'^{\top} F x = 0$.

The payoff

Correspondence search collapses from a whole image to a single epipolar line.

Printed pp. 239-261Epipolar Geometry and the Fundamental Matrix
This chapter, printed pages 239 to 261, opens the two-view story. Two cameras looking at one scene share a rigid geometry that is independent of the scene structure. Our goal is to read the fundamental matrix as the algebraic machine that encodes that geometry, turning correspondence search into a search along a single line.

The Two-View Setup

Three ingredients

Centres $C$, $C'$ and a scene point $X$ define a triangle in space.

Image rays

$X$ projects to $x$ in view one and $x'$ in view two.

Scene independence

The relation between $x$ and $x'$ depends on cameras, not on $X$.

Printed pp. 239-261The Two-View Setup
Set the stage carefully: two camera centers, the baseline joining them, and a 3D point seen by both. The three points define an epipolar plane, and the matter of correspondence is entirely controlled by this triangle. Everything algebraic that follows is a coordinate shadow of this one picture, so spend time on it.

Epipoles, Epipolar Planes, and Lines

Epipole $e$, $e'$

Image of the other camera centre; where the baseline meets the image plane.

Epipolar plane

Any plane containing the baseline; one per scene point.

Pencil of lines

Every epipolar line passes through the epipole.

Printed pp. 239-261Epipoles, Planes, and Lines
Name the actors. The epipole in each image is where the baseline pierces that image plane, equivalently the projection of the other camera centre. Every epipolar plane contains the baseline, so every epipolar line passes through the epipole. The pencil of planes about the baseline maps to a pencil of lines through the epipole.

The Fundamental Matrix Defined

Defining relation

$$x'^{\top} F x = 0$$

What it is

A $3\times 3$ homogeneous matrix, the algebraic encoding of the epipolar geometry.

Scale

Defined up to scale: only ratios of entries carry meaning.

Holds for every match

The relation is satisfied by all corresponding pairs $(x, x')$, regardless of the scene point that produced them.

Printed pp. 239-261The Fundamental Matrix Defined
Now the algebra. The fundamental matrix is the unique three by three homogeneous matrix satisfying the incidence relation for every corresponding pair. It is the algebraic shadow of the epipolar plane. Stress that it is homogeneous, so only its ratios matter, and that it is defined purely by the two cameras, not the scene.

A Point Maps to Its Epipolar Line

Point-to-line map

$$l' = F x, \qquad x'^{\top} l' = 0$$

Correlation, not collineation

$F$ sends a point in image one to a line in image two.

Symmetric twin

$l = F^{\top} x'$ gives the epipolar line in image one.

Printed pp. 239-261Point Maps to Epipolar Line
This is the workhorse identity. Multiplying a point in the first image by F yields the coefficients of a line in the second image. The corresponding point must lie on that line. Emphasise that F is a correlation, mapping points to lines rather than points to points, which is why correspondence reduces to a line search.

Rank Two and Seven Degrees of Freedom

Singular by design

$$\det F = 0, \qquad \operatorname{rank} F = 2$$

Counting

$9$ entries, minus $1$ for scale, minus $1$ for $\det F = 0$, leaves $7$ degrees of freedom.

Why it matters

A full-rank estimate is not a valid $F$; the rank-two constraint must be enforced.

Printed pp. 239-261Rank Two and Seven Degrees of Freedom
Two structural facts pin F down. It has rank two, so its determinant vanishes and it has a one-dimensional null space. Counting nine entries, minus overall scale, minus the rank-two constraint, leaves seven degrees of freedom. Both facts are essential when estimating F from correspondences, since a full-rank estimate is wrong.

Epipoles Are the Null Vectors of $F$

Null space

$$F e = 0, \qquad F^{\top} e' = 0$$

Right null vector

$e$ is the epipole in image one.

Left null vector

$e'$ is the epipole in image two.

Consequence

Every line $F x$ contains $e'$, so the pencil meets at the epipole.

Printed pp. 239-261Epipoles as Null Vectors
The epipoles re-enter algebraically. The right epipole is the right null vector of F, the left epipole the left null vector. This is exactly why epipolar lines all meet at the epipole: every line F x contains e prime. The notebook verified these null residuals down to machine precision, confirming the geometry.

$F$ from the Camera Matrices

Closed form

$$F = [e']_{\times}\, P' P^{+}$$

Pieces

$P^{+}$ is the pseudo-inverse of the first camera; $[e']_{\times}$ is the skew form of the second epipole.

Epipole term

$e' = P' C$ is the image of the first centre in the second view.

Reading the formula

$P^{+}$ back-projects $x$ to a ray; $P'$ images that ray; the cross product with $e'$ forms the line. The left factor $[e']_{\times}$ explains directly why $e'$ is a left null vector of $F$.

Printed pp. 239-261F from the Camera Matrices
Connect F back to the cameras. Given the two projection matrices, F is the cross-product matrix of the second epipole times the second camera times the pseudo-inverse of the first. This formula shows F is computable directly from calibration, and it makes transparent why the second epipole appears as a left null vector.

The Essential Matrix

Calibrated relation

$$E = K'^{\top} F K = [t]_{\times} R$$

Normalised coordinates

$E$ acts on calibrated rays $\hat x = K^{-1} x$.

Signature

Two equal nonzero singular values and one zero: $(\sigma, \sigma, 0)$.

Why stricter than $F$

$E$ carries metric motion $R$ and $t$, so its singular spectrum is constrained beyond mere rank two. The notebook recovered the equal-singular-value pattern from the true motion, the calibrated upgrade, and an OpenCV estimate.

Printed pp. 239-261The Essential Matrix
Calibration sharpens the story. Pre-multiplying by the two intrinsic matrices converts F into the essential matrix, which acts on normalised image coordinates. E equals the translation cross-product times the rotation, so it carries the metric motion. Its signature is two equal nonzero singular values and one zero, stricter than a generic rank-two matrix.

Special Motions and the Epipole

General motion

Finite epipole; lines form a visible pencil meeting at a point.

Lateral translation

Epipole goes to infinity ($w = 0$); epipolar lines become parallel.

Invariant

$\operatorname{rank} F = 2$ survives whenever $C \neq C'$.

Printed pp. 239-261Special Motions
Special motions test understanding. Pure lateral translation sends the epipole to infinity, so the epipolar lines become parallel rather than meeting at a finite point. Forward motion places the epipole near the image centre. Crucially, the rank-two contract survives as long as the two camera centres remain distinct.

Correspondence Reduced to a Line

Naive search

Without geometry, a match for $x$ could be anywhere in image two: a 2D scan.

Epipolar search

$F$ predicts the line $l' = F x$; the match lies on it: a 1D scan.

Foundation of stereo

Rectification makes these lines horizontal scanlines.

Printed pp. 239-261Correspondence Along a Line
Here is the payoff for stereo. Instead of scanning a whole image for a match, you scan one epipolar line predicted by F. This collapses a two-dimensional search to one dimension, the foundation of rectified stereo and the practical reason epipolar geometry matters in reconstruction pipelines.
