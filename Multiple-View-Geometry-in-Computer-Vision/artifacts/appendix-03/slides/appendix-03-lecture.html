The Measurement Model

Signal plus noise

Each observation is a deterministic prediction corrupted by random error.

Noise model matters

Choosing the error distribution turns fitting into a well-posed problem.

Model

$\mathbf{y} = f(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol\theta) + \boldsymbol\varepsilon, \quad \boldsymbol\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma)$

In vision

$f$ is projection; $\boldsymbol\theta$ holds camera, point, or tensor parameters.

Printed pp. 568-577The Measurement Model
Everything starts with a model: measurements equal a deterministic function of the parameters plus random noise. Fixing the noise distribution is what converts a vague fitting problem into a well-posed statistical one, and it dictates which cost function we should actually minimize.

Maximum Likelihood

Definition

Pick the parameters that make the observed data most probable.

Gaussian case

Log-likelihood becomes a negative sum of squared residuals.

Consequence

ML estimate equals the least-squares geometric-error minimizer.

Estimator

$\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}_{\mathrm{ML}} = \arg\max_{\boldsymbol\theta} \prod_i p(\mathbf{y}_i\mid\boldsymbol\theta)$

Reduces to

$\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\theta} \sum_i \lVert \mathbf{y}_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i;\boldsymbol\theta)\rVert^2$

Printed pp. 568-577Maximum Likelihood
Maximum likelihood selects the parameters that make the observed data most probable. For independent isotropic Gaussian noise the log-likelihood reduces to a negative sum of squared residuals, so the maximum likelihood estimate is exactly the least-squares minimizer of geometric error.

Likelihood Surface and the MLE

The peak

The MLE sits at the top of the likelihood surface.

Curvature

Sharp peak means precise; flat ridge means weakly constrained.

Printed pp. 568-577Likelihood Surface and the MLE
The likelihood is a surface over parameter space whose peak is the maximum likelihood estimate. Curvature at that peak controls how sharply the data pin the parameters; a broad flat ridge signals a poorly conditioned or near-degenerate estimation problem.

MAP and Bayesian Estimation

Posterior

Prior times likelihood, normalized, gives the posterior over parameters.

MAP estimate

The posterior mode shifts from the MLE toward the prior.

Full Bayesian

Keep the whole posterior, not just a single point estimate.

Bayes rule

$p(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbf{y}) \propto p(\mathbf{y}\mid\boldsymbol\theta)\, p(\boldsymbol\theta)$

Printed pp. 568-577MAP and Bayesian Estimation
A prior multiplies the likelihood to give a posterior, and its mode is the maximum a posteriori estimate. The prior pulls the solution away from the raw maximum likelihood point toward prior belief, while full Bayesian estimation keeps the whole posterior instead of one number.

Bias and Variance

Bias

Systematic gap between the estimator's average and the truth.

Variance

Scatter of the estimate under repeated noisy samples.

Trade-off

A little bias can lower total error if it cuts variance.

MSE decomposition

$\mathrm{MSE}(\hat\theta) = \mathrm{Bias}(\hat\theta)^2 + \mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)$

Printed pp. 568-577Bias and Variance
We judge an estimator by two quantities: bias measures systematic offset of its average from the truth, variance measures its scatter under resampling. Mean squared error decomposes into both, so a slightly biased estimator can still beat an unbiased but high-variance competitor.

Fisher Information

Definition

Expected curvature of the log-likelihood at the true parameter.

Score variance

Equivalently, the variance of the log-likelihood gradient.

Reading

More information means a sharper peak and more informative data.

Fisher matrix

$\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\dfrac{\partial^2 \log p}{\partial\boldsymbol\theta\,\partial\boldsymbol\theta^\top}\right]$

Geometry link

It is the metric that turns parameter shifts into measurable change.

Printed pp. 568-577Fisher Information
The Fisher information matrix is the expected curvature of the log-likelihood, equivalently the variance of the score. Large Fisher information means the likelihood is sharply peaked and the data are highly informative about the parameters, which is the quantity the Cramer-Rao bound depends on.

The Cramer-Rao Lower Bound

The bound

No unbiased estimator beats the inverse Fisher information.

Efficiency

An estimator meeting the bound is called efficient.

ML is asymptotically efficient

Maximum likelihood attains it as the sample count grows.

CRLB

$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}) \;\succeq\; \mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol\theta)^{-1}$

Printed pp. 568-577The Cramer-Rao Lower Bound
The Cramer-Rao bound states that any unbiased estimator has covariance no smaller than the inverse Fisher information. It sets a hard floor on achievable precision; an estimator that meets it is efficient, and maximum likelihood attains it asymptotically as samples grow.

Algebraic vs Geometric Error

Algebraic error

Cheap linear residual, but no clear statistical meaning.

Geometric error

True image-plane reprojection distance; the ML cost under Gaussian noise.

Why it matters

$\min \sum_i d\big(\mathbf{y}_i,\, f(\mathbf{x}_i;\boldsymbol\theta)\big)^2$

Printed pp. 568-577Algebraic vs Geometric Error
Linear algorithms minimize an algebraic residual that is fast but lacks statistical meaning and weights points unevenly. Geometric or reprojection error measures true image-plane distance, so its minimizer is the maximum likelihood estimate under Gaussian image noise and is the gold standard.

Nuisance Parameters

Definition

Parameters we must estimate but do not ultimately care about.

Example

Unknown scene points in triangulation and bundle adjustment.

Handling

Profile or marginalize them for a correct parameter covariance.

Split

$\boldsymbol\theta = (\boldsymbol\theta_{\text{wanted}},\; \boldsymbol\theta_{\text{nuisance}})$

Cost

They enlarge the problem yet are essential to a consistent fit.

Printed pp. 568-577Nuisance Parameters
Triangulation and reconstruction carry nuisance parameters such as the unknown scene points that we must estimate but do not ultimately care about. They inflate the problem dimension yet are essential; profiling or marginalizing them yields a correct covariance for the parameters we want.

Why the Gold Standard

Model

Gaussian image noise sets the likelihood.

Estimator

ML equals least-squares geometric error.

Certificate

Cramer-Rao bounds its achievable precision.

Initialization

A linear algebraic estimate seeds the iteration.

Gold-standard recipe

$\hat{\boldsymbol\theta} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol\theta} \sum_i \lVert \mathbf{y}_i - f(\mathbf{x}_i;\boldsymbol\theta)\rVert^2$

Takeaway

Reprojection-error minimization is statistics made geometric.

Printed pp. 568-577Why the Gold Standard
Putting the pieces together: the Gaussian measurement model makes maximum likelihood equal to least-squares geometric error, the Cramer-Rao bound certifies its optimal precision, and a linear estimate supplies the starting point. This is exactly why reprojection-error minimization is the book's gold standard.
