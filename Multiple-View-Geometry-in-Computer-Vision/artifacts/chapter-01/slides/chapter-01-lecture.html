Why Projective Geometry Is the Right Language

Shape is not invariant

A square becomes a quadrilateral; a circle becomes an ellipse.

Incidence is invariant

Straight lines stay straight and meetings are preserved.

A camera is a projective map

So projective geometry models scene and image alike.

Projective map

$\mathbf{x}' \sim H\,\mathbf{x},\qquad H\in PGL(3)$

Printed pp. 1-22Why Projective Geometry
Perspective projection turns a square into a quadrilateral and a circle into an ellipse, yet straight lines stay straight. The correct invariant is projective incidence, not Euclidean shape. Because a camera is itself a projective map, projective geometry is the natural language for both scene points and image measurements throughout the course.

Homogeneous Coordinates and Incidence

Points as scale classes

$(x,y)\mapsto(x,y,1)$; any nonzero multiple names the same point.

Lines are 3-vectors

A line $\mathbf{l}$ and the join of two points share the same algebra.

Equality after normalizing

In code, compare directions, not raw arrays.

Incidence and join

$\mathbf{l}^{\!\top}\mathbf{x}=0,\quad \mathbf{l}=\mathbf{x}_1\times\mathbf{x}_2$

Printed pp. 1-22Homogeneous Coordinates
Homogeneous coordinates append a scale entry so that a point is a whole ray of nonzero vectors. Equality means agreement after normalization. A line is also a three vector, and a point lies on it exactly when their dot product vanishes, giving a clean symmetric incidence algebra for the plane.

Points and Lines at Infinity

Ideal points

Last coordinate zero: $(d_1,d_2,0)$ is a direction where parallels meet.

Line at infinity

All ideal points lie on $\mathbf{l}_\infty=(0,0,1)$.

Affine from projective

Choosing $\mathbf{l}_\infty$ is what fixes parallelism.

Printed pp. 1-22Points and Lines at Infinity
Setting the last coordinate to zero gives ideal points where parallel lines meet. They all sit on one line at infinity. Choosing that special line is what separates affine from projective structure, and its image under a homography is the visible horizon or vanishing line that the notebook constructs.

The Pinhole Camera and the 3×4 Matrix

Central projection

Every point on a ray through the center shares one image point.

Depth is lost

Incidence survives; range along the ray does not.

Camera matrix

$\mathbf{x}\sim P\,\mathbf{X},\quad P=K\,[\,R\mid \mathbf{t}\,]$

Printed pp. 1-22The Pinhole Camera
A pinhole camera maps every point on a ray through the center to the same image coordinate, so depth is lost but incidence survives. The three by four camera matrix is a projective map of rank three from world points to image points, decomposing into intrinsic calibration, rotation, and translation.

Single View: Calibration and Vanishing Points

Vanishing points

Images of directions — the ideal points of parallel families.

Calibration object

The IAC $\omega=K^{-\top}K^{-1}$ encodes the intrinsics.

One view yields angles

Orthogonal directions constrain $K$ from a single image.

Angle from rays

$\cos\theta=\dfrac{\mathbf{v}_1^{\!\top}\omega\,\mathbf{v}_2}{\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_1^{\!\top}\omega\,\mathbf{v}_1}\,\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_2^{\!\top}\omega\,\mathbf{v}_2}}$

Printed pp. 1-22Single View: Calibration
From one image we can still recover structure. Vanishing points are images of directions, the points at infinity of parallel families. Their geometry constrains the calibration matrix through the image of the absolute conic, letting a single view yield angles, orientations, and partial metric information about the scene.

Two Views and the Fundamental Matrix

A point gives a line

In a second view, $\mathbf{x}$ determines an epipolar line, not a point.

Rank-2 incidence

$F$ packages the two-view constraint with $\det F=0$.

Scene-independent

$F$ depends only on the cameras, not the structure.

Epipolar constraint

$\mathbf{x}'^{\!\top}F\,\mathbf{x}=0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}F=2$

Epipolar line and epipoles

$\mathbf{l}'=F\mathbf{x},\quad F\mathbf{e}=0,\ F^{\!\top}\mathbf{e}'=0$

Printed pp. 1-22Two Views: Fundamental Matrix
With two separated centers a point in one image no longer fixes a point in the other; it fixes an epipolar line. The fundamental matrix packages this incidence: every correspondence satisfies x prime transpose F x equals zero, and F has rank two, encoding the entire two view relation independent of scene.

Epipolar Geometry, Visualized

The pencil of lines

Every epipolar line passes through the epipole.

Search collapses

A match in view two lies on one line, not the whole image.

Baseline geometry

Epipoles are the images of the opposite camera centers.

Printed pp. 1-22Epipolar Geometry
Each epipolar line is the image of the ray back projected from the other view, and all such lines pass through the epipole, the image of the opposing camera center. This pencil of lines is the visual fingerprint of the fundamental matrix and reduces correspondence search from a plane to one line.

The Reconstruction Ambiguity

Projective

Uncalibrated images fix structure only up to a 4×4 transform.

Affine

Locating the plane at infinity restores parallelism.

Metric

Fixing the absolute conic restores angles and length ratios.

Image-invariant gauge

$P\mathbf{X}=(PH^{-1})(H\mathbf{X})$

Printed pp. 1-22Reconstruction Ambiguity
From uncalibrated images reconstruction is only projective. Replacing every point by H X and every camera by P H inverse leaves all image measurements unchanged, so a point cloud can be image perfect yet metrically distorted. Extra information narrows the ambiguity through the projective, affine, and metric strata.

The Euclidean Upgrade

Absolute conic

The conic on the plane at infinity carries metric structure.

Its image

$\omega=K^{-\top}K^{-1}$ is what a calibrated image measures.

Two upgrades

$\mathbf{l}_\infty$ removes affine slack; $\omega$ removes metric slack.

Metric from calibration

$\omega=K^{-\top}K^{-1},\quad \cos\theta=\dfrac{\mathbf{d}_1^{\!\top}\omega\,\mathbf{d}_2}{|\mathbf{d}_1|_\omega\,|\mathbf{d}_2|_\omega}$

Printed pp. 1-22The Euclidean Upgrade
Calibration supplies the missing metric object. For intrinsics K the image of the absolute conic is omega equals K inverse transpose K inverse, and it lets images measure angles between back projected rays. Locating the plane at infinity removes affine ambiguity; fixing the absolute conic removes the remaining metric ambiguity.

Three- and N-View Geometry

Trifocal tensor

Relates points and lines across three views; enables transfer.

Transfer

Two views triangulate a 3D point; the third is a projection.

Bundle adjustment

Jointly refine all cameras and points by reprojection error.

Bundle adjustment objective

$\min_{P_j,\mathbf{X}_i}\ \sum_{i,j}\,d\!\big(\mathbf{x}_{ij},\,P_j\mathbf{X}_i\big)^2$

Printed pp. 1-22Three- and N-View Geometry
Three views add the trifocal tensor, a richer incidence object relating points and lines across the triple and enabling transfer to a predicted third view. Across many images bundle adjustment jointly refines all cameras and structure by minimizing total reprojection error, the gold standard refinement of the whole pipeline.
