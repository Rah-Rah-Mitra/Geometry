Estimation: 2D Projective Transformations

Recovering a homography $H$ from noisy point correspondences

The setup

Two images of a plane, linked by $x' = Hx$. We observe matches, not $H$.

The route

DLT → normalization → geometric cost → ML refinement → RANSAC.

This chapter, spanning printed pages 87 through 131, asks one question: given noisy point matches between two images of a plane, recover the homography that relates them. We preview the route from the linear DLT solver, through normalization and geometric cost, to maximum-likelihood refinement and robust RANSAC estimation.

The Estimation Problem

Nine entries, eight DoF

$H$ is defined only up to scale, so one entry is redundant.

Minimal data

Four general correspondences fix $H$ exactly.

Projective map

$$x' = H x, \qquad H \in \mathbb{P}^{8}$$

Over-determined

More matches than needed plus noise → minimise a cost.

Printed pp. 87-131The Estimation Problem
A planar projective map is a three by three matrix acting on homogeneous coordinates. It has nine entries but only eight degrees of freedom because overall scale is irrelevant. Four general correspondences fix it exactly; with more, noisy matches, we must minimise some cost and choose which one carefully.

DLT: The Cross-Product Constraint

Parallel vectors

$x'$ and $Hx$ are equal up to scale, hence collinear.

Two of three

The cross product gives three scalar rows; only two are independent.

Linear in $h$

$$x' \times H x = 0$$

Row block

$\begin{bmatrix} -x^\top & 0 & u\,x^\top \\ 0 & -x^\top & v\,x^\top \end{bmatrix} h = 0$

Printed pp. 87-131DLT: Cross-Product Constraint
Each correspondence forces the image point and the mapped point to be parallel as homogeneous vectors, so their cross product vanishes. Expanding that cross product gives three scalar equations linear in the entries of H, of which only two are independent. This is the algebraic heart of the Direct Linear Transformation.

Stack the Rows, Solve by SVD

Design matrix

Two rows per match stack into $A$; seek $A h = 0$.

Null vector

Exact data → rank $8$, a one-dimensional nullspace.

Solution

$\hat h = $ last right singular vector of $A$

Printed pp. 87-131Stacking Rows and SVD
Two independent rows per match stack into a design matrix A, and we seek the vector h with A h equal to zero. With exact data the matrix has rank eight and a one-dimensional nullspace. The singular value decomposition returns that null vector as the right singular vector of smallest singular value.

Data Normalization Is Essential

The pathology

Raw pixels mix entries near $1$ with products near $10^6$.

Hartley recipe

centroid $\to 0$,   mean dist $\to \sqrt{2}$

Coordinates, not geometry

Similarity $T$; denormalize with $\tilde H = T'^{-1} H T$.

Printed pp. 87-131Data Normalization
Raw pixel DLT mixes entries near one with products near a million, wrecking conditioning. Hartley normalization translates each point cloud to its centroid and scales it so the mean distance from the origin equals root two. This similarity changes coordinates, not geometry, yet dramatically improves the numerical nullspace problem.

Algebraic vs Geometric Error

Algebraic

$\|A h\|$ — what DLT minimises. Units are not pixels.

Geometric

Reprojection distance in the image plane, in pixels.

Caution

small $\|Ah\|$ $\;\not\Rightarrow\;$ small pixel error

Printed pp. 87-131Algebraic vs Geometric Error
DLT minimises the algebraic residual, the norm of A h, whose units are not pixels. The geometric error instead measures reprojection distance in the image plane, where the points actually live. The two disagree, and a small algebraic residual is no guarantee of small pixel error before normalization.

Reprojection and Sampson Error

Symmetric transfer

Sum residuals in both images at once.

Reprojection

Estimate corrected $\hat x, \hat x'$ that lie exactly on $H$.

Symmetric cost

$$\textstyle\sum_i d(x_i, H^{-1}x_i')^2 + d(x_i', Hx_i)^2$$

Sampson

first-order approx of geometric error

Printed pp. 87-131Reprojection and Sampson Error
Symmetric transfer error sums squared distances in both images. The reprojection error also estimates corrected points lying exactly on the map. The Sampson error is a first-order approximation of that geometric cost, cheap to compute yet far closer to the true geometric distance than the raw algebraic residual.

The Maximum Likelihood Estimate

Noise model

Independent isotropic Gaussian measurement error.

Gold standard

Normalized DLT init, then Levenberg–Marquardt refinement.

ML = geometric error

$$\hat H = \arg\min_H \sum_i d(x_i', Hx_i)^2$$

Why iterate

The cost is nonlinear; DLT seeds a good basin.

Printed pp. 87-131Maximum Likelihood Estimate
Under independent isotropic Gaussian measurement noise, the negative log likelihood is exactly the sum of squared geometric reprojection errors. So minimising geometric error is the maximum-likelihood estimate. The gold-standard algorithm initialises with normalized DLT, then iteratively refines using Levenberg-Marquardt over the homography and corrected points.

Robust Estimation with RANSAC

The problem

Mismatches break every least-squares cost.

The idea

Sample $4$ matches, fit $H$, count inliers within a band.

Winner

Largest support set; then refine on its inliers.

Printed pp. 87-131Robust Estimation: RANSAC
Real correspondences contain mismatches that no least-squares cost can survive. RANSAC repeatedly draws a minimal sample of four matches, fits a homography, and counts inliers agreeing within a pixel threshold. The model with the largest support wins, and the homography is then refined on its inlier set.

Threshold and Iteration Budget

Threshold

Too small rejects good matches; too large admits outliers.

Match the noise

Set the band to expected localization error.

Adaptive trials

$$N = \frac{\log(1-p)}{\log\!\big(1-(1-\epsilon)^{4}\big)}$$

Reading it

$\epsilon$ outlier rate, $p$ success target; $N$ grows with $\epsilon$.

Printed pp. 87-131Choosing the RANSAC Threshold
The inlier threshold must match the expected localization noise. Set it too small and good matches are rejected; too large and outliers are admitted. The required sample count grows with the outlier fraction, so the probability of drawing one all-inlier minimal set drives the adaptive iteration budget.

Degenerate Configurations

Collinearity

No three of the four points may lie on a line.

Clustering

Tightly bunched points give an ill-conditioned design.

Bad samples

A degenerate RANSAC draw fits a spurious map.

Printed pp. 87-131Degenerate Configurations
Four points define a homography only if no three are collinear; nearly collinear or tightly clustered points give an ill-conditioned design even when algebraically solvable. RANSAC samples can also be degenerate, fitting a spurious map. Detecting and rejecting these configurations protects both the linear solver and the robust estimator.

The Full Estimation Workflow

1. Normalized DLT

Conditioned linear initialization.

2. RANSAC

Isolate a coherent inlier set under contamination.

3. ML refine

Minimise pixel reprojection error from that seed.

Golden rule

compare $H$ by action on points, not entries

Scale-free

$H$ and $\lambda H$ are the same map; normalize before comparing.

Printed pp. 87-131The Estimation Workflow
Putting it together: normalized DLT gives a conditioned linear initialization, RANSAC isolates a coherent inlier set when matches are contaminated, and maximum-likelihood refinement minimises pixel reprojection error from that start. Always compare homographies by their action on points, never by raw matrix entries, since the map is scale-free.
