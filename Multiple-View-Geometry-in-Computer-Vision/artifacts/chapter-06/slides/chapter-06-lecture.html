The Basic Pinhole Camera

Central projection

Center $C$, world point $X$, and image $x$ are collinear.

Similar triangles

Depth $Z$ scales the projection; focal length $f$ sets the rate.

Pinhole map

$(X,Y,Z)^\top \mapsto (fX/Z,\; fY/Z)^\top$

Printed pp. 153-177The Basic Pinhole Camera
Start with the central projection idea. A world point, the camera center, and the image point are collinear, so similar triangles give the focal-length scaling. Writing this in homogeneous coordinates linearizes the nonlinear division by depth, which is the whole reason projective coordinates earn their keep here.

The Camera Matrix $P = K[R \mid t]$

Finite camera

$x = PX,\qquad P = K\,[\,R \mid t\,],\quad t = -RC$

11 degrees of freedom

Five in $K$, three in $R$, three in $C$ — $P$ is defined up to scale.

Rank three

The left $3\times 3$ block $M = KR$ is invertible for a finite camera.

Printed pp. 153-177The Camera Matrix
Here is the master equation. The full finite camera factors into a calibration matrix times a rigid motion. K holds everything internal to the sensor, while R and t encode where the camera sits and how it points. Every later camera model is a constrained special case of this factorization.

Intrinsics: The Calibration Matrix $K$

Upper triangular

$K = \begin{bmatrix} f_x & s & x_0 \\ 0 & f_y & y_0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

Five parameters

Focal lengths $f_x,f_y$; principal point $(x_0,y_0)$; skew $s$.

Aspect & skew

Aspect ratio $=f_y/f_x$; skew $s\approx 0$ for real CCD sensors.

Printed pp. 153-177The Calibration Matrix
K is upper triangular and carries five degrees of freedom: two focal lengths in pixel units, the principal point offset, and skew. Skew is nearly zero for real CCD sensors. Aspect ratio is the ratio of the two focal entries. These parameters live entirely in the image plane and ignore world pose.

Extrinsics: Pose $R$ and $t$

World to camera

$X_{\text{cam}} = R\,(X - C),\qquad t = -RC$

Rotation $R$

Orthonormal: world axes expressed in camera coordinates.

Watch out

$t$ is not the position; recover $C = -R^\top t$.

Printed pp. 153-177Extrinsics
The extrinsics place the camera in the world. R rotates world axes into the camera frame, and the translation satisfies t equals minus R times C. Emphasize that t is not the camera position; you must invert the rotation to recover the center C, a common source of sign and frame confusion.

The Camera Center

Null vector

$P\,C = 0,\qquad C = \text{right null space of } P$

Unique

$\operatorname{rank} P = 3$, so the null space is one-dimensional.

Check

Numerically $\lVert P\,C\rVert \approx 10^{-15}$ in the notebook lab.

Printed pp. 153-177The Camera Center
The camera center is the unique point the camera cannot see, and algebraically it is the right null vector of P. Because P has rank three, its null space is one-dimensional, giving exactly one center up to scale. We verify this numerically: P times the homogeneous center returns the zero vector.

Principal Plane, Axis, and Point

Principal plane

Row $P^3$ is the plane through $C$ parallel to the image plane.

Principal axis

Direction $m^3$ (left part of $P^3$) points along the optical axis.

Principal point

$x_0 = M\,m^3$ — where the axis meets the image plane.

Printed pp. 153-177Principal Geometry
Read the rows of P geometrically. The third row is the principal plane through the center, parallel to the image plane. Its left three entries give the principal axis direction. The principal axis pierces the image plane at the principal point, which is also where the optical axis meets the sensor.

Columns and Rows of $P$

Columns

$P = [\,p_1\;\; p_2\;\; p_3\;\; p_4\,]$

Columns 1–3

Vanishing points of the world $X,Y,Z$ axes.

Column 4

$p_4$ is the image of the world origin.

Rows

Three world planes; $P^3$ is the principal plane.

Printed pp. 153-177Columns and Rows
The columns are vanishing points of the world coordinate axes, and the fourth column is the image of the world origin. The rows are world planes: two through image axes and one principal plane. Knowing this lets you sketch a camera's behavior without ever multiplying it out fully.

Forward Projection $x = PX$

One multiply, one divide

$\tilde{x} = PX,\qquad (u,v) = (\tilde{x}_1/\tilde{x}_3,\; \tilde{x}_2/\tilde{x}_3)$

Scale invariant

$P(\lambda X)$ and $(\mu P)X$ give the same image point.

Check

Projecting $X$ and $7.5\,X$ yields the identical pixel.

Printed pp. 153-177Forward Projection
Forward projection is one matrix multiply followed by a homogeneous divide. Scaling the world point or the matrix does not change the image point, because both live in projective space. We confirm scale invariance numerically: projecting X and seven point five times X yields the identical pixel.

Back-Projection to a Ray

Image point to ray

$X(\lambda) = P^{+}x + \lambda\,C$

Not a point

One pixel fixes a direction, not depth — depth is free.

Through $C$

The ray joins the center $C$ and the pseudo-inverse point $P^{+}x$.

Why two views

A second camera intersects rays to triangulate $X$.

Printed pp. 153-177Back-Projection
A single image point does not fix depth, so back-projection returns a ray, not a point. The ray passes through the camera center and the back-projected point given by the pseudo-inverse of P. This one-to-many inverse is exactly why a second view is needed to triangulate structure.

Cameras at Infinity: Affine Models

Affine camera

$P_\infty = \begin{bmatrix} m_{11} & m_{12} & m_{13} & t_1 \\ m_{21} & m_{22} & m_{23} & t_2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

Center at infinity

Perspective division vanishes; projection becomes linear.

The family

Orthographic, weak perspective, para-perspective.

Printed pp. 153-177Affine Cameras
Push the camera center to infinity and perspective division disappears. The result is the affine camera family: orthographic, weak perspective, and para-perspective. Their bottom row is fixed at zero zero zero one, so parallel world lines stay parallel in the image. They are linear approximations valid for shallow, distant scenes.

Synthesis and Checks

Null space

$PC=0$: center recovered with residual $\approx 10^{-15}$.

Factorization

$K[R\mid -RC]$ reconstructs $P$ up to scale; RQ recovers $K,R$.

Scale

Projection is invariant to homogeneous scale.

Parallelism

Affine keeps parallel directions; perspective does not.

Printed pp. 153-177Synthesis and Checks
Close by tying the algebra to runnable invariants from the notebook. The center sits in the null space, the block product reconstructs P up to scale, projection is scale invariant, and affine projection preserves parallel directions while perspective does not. Each claim is carried by a residual, not just by prose.
