Measurements and the Observation Graph

Data

Image point $x_{ij}$: camera $i$ sees point $j$, often incompletely.

Bipartite edges

$E=\{(i,j): X_j \text{ visible in } P_i\}$

Missing data

Holes are normal; visibility is a mask, not a full grid.

Printed pp. 434-457Measurements and the Observation Graph
Define the raw data carefully. Image points indexed by camera and point form a bipartite observation graph whose edges are the visible measurements. Missing data is the rule, not the exception. Bundle adjustment tolerates holes because residuals are local, while direct SVD factorization needs complete tracks, a distinction worth repeating.

Bundle Adjustment: The Cost

Total reprojection error

$$\min_{P_i,\,X_j}\;\sum_{(i,j)\in E} d\!\big(x_{ij},\,P_i X_j\big)^2$$

Maximum likelihood

Optimal under Gaussian image-noise; the geometric gold standard.

Non-convex

Needs a good initialization; it polishes, it does not bootstrap.

Parameters

All cameras, all points, optionally calibration, jointly refined.

Residual

Each term measures one point reprojected into one image.

Printed pp. 434-457Bundle Adjustment: The Cost
Introduce the gold standard. Bundle adjustment jointly optimizes all cameras and all points to minimize total squared reprojection error under an assumed noise model. It is maximum likelihood when noise is Gaussian. Emphasize that it needs a good initialization because the cost is non-convex, so it is the last step, not the first.

Sparse Structure and the Schur Complement

Block arrowhead

Each residual touches one camera block and one point block.

Reduced camera system

$$\big(U - W V^{-1} W^{\top}\big)\,\delta_c = e_c$$

Sparse LM

Levenberg-Marquardt exploits the pattern; never form a dense $J^\top J$.

Printed pp. 434-457Sparse Structure and Schur Complement
This is the computational heart of the chapter. Each residual touches exactly one camera and one point, so the normal matrix is block-arrowhead sparse. Levenberg-Marquardt with the Schur complement eliminates the large point block first, leaving a small reduced camera system. Walk the partition U, W, V slowly on the diagram.

Affine Factorization (Tomasi-Kanade)

Center

Affine cameras map centroids to centroids; subtract each image centroid.

Rank-3 factorization

$$\hat W_{2m\times n} = M_{2m\times 3}\,S_{3\times n}$$

Truncate

Closest noisy solution is the rank-3 SVD; the 4th singular value is noise.

Printed pp. 434-457Affine Factorization
Present Tomasi and Kanade. Affine cameras map centroids to centroids, so subtracting each image centroid removes translation and leaves a measurement matrix that factors as motion times structure. The matrix is rank three in the noiseless case, so the closest reconstruction is the rank-three SVD truncation. This is the cleanest result in the chapter.

The Factorization $W = M\,S$ via SVD

Truncated SVD

$$W \approx U_3 \Sigma_3 V_3^{\top},\quad M = U_3\Sigma_3^{1/2},\; S=\Sigma_3^{1/2}V_3^{\top}$$

Motion $M$

Stacked camera rows: the $2m\times 3$ factor.

Structure $S$

3D point coordinates: the $3\times n$ factor.

Printed pp. 434-457The Factorization W = M S
Make the SVD mechanics concrete. Take the centered two-m by n matrix, compute its SVD, keep the top three singular values, and split the truncated factors into a motion matrix and a structure matrix. The rank-three plateau in the spectrum is the auditable invariant. The remaining linear ambiguity is fixed by metric constraints.

Non-Rigid Factorization: Larger Subspace

Shape basis

A deforming object is a blend of $l$ basis shapes.

Rank bound

$\operatorname{rank} W \le 3l \quad(l=2 \Rightarrow 6)$

Caveat

Motion blocks repeat; the recovery ambiguity is harder than rigid.

Printed pp. 434-457Non-Rigid Factorization
Extend the subspace idea to deforming scenes. With l basis shapes each trajectory lives in a 3l-dimensional subspace, so the singular value drop moves from after three to after 3l, here six for two shapes. The motion matrix keeps repeated camera blocks, and the factorization ambiguity is harder, which is the honest caveat to state.

Projective Factorization: Rank 4 Needs Depths

Depth-weighted measurement

$$\lambda_{ij}\,x_{ij} = P_i X_j,\qquad \operatorname{rank} W = 4$$

Iterate depths

Unknown $\lambda_{ij}$ are estimated, then rank-4 truncation is taken.

Gauge

Row-column rescaling is an exact freedom; renormalize each pass.

Printed pp. 434-457Projective Factorization with Depths
Now the projective case. Image points are scale classes, so the equation only becomes a rank-four factorization once the correct projective depths weight each measurement. Unit depths generally do not give rank four. Depths are estimated iteratively, and the row-column scaling freedom is an exact gauge that should be normalized between iterations.

Conditioning and Normalization

Algebraic ≠ geometric

A small SVD residual is not yet a small reprojection error.

Isotropic normalization

Recenter and rescale image coordinates before the homogeneous SVD.

Effect

The same rank-4 object recovers with a far smaller condition number.

Printed pp. 434-457Conditioning and Normalization
Drive home the warning that the algebraic matrix is not the geometric error. Isotropic image normalization sharply lowers the effective condition number of the homogeneous system before the SVD, so the same rank-four object is recovered far more reliably. Pair this with the residual descent curve from the bundle solve as a diagnostic habit.

Plane-Based and Sequence Initialization

Plane homographies

A reference plane fixes the left $3\times 3$ block $M_i$; solve for columns $t_i$.

Scale ambiguity

$\dim \ker = 1 \;(\text{common projective scale})$

Sequences

Merge pair and triplet windows into one global start; bundle adjust.

Printed pp. 434-457Plane-Based and Sequence Methods
Cover two initialization devices. A known reference-plane homography fixes the left three-by-three camera block, leaving a linear homogeneous system for the final columns whose null space is the one-dimensional common scale. For sequences, pairwise and triplet windows are merged into a global start. Both are initializers, not substitutes for final bundle adjustment.

Synthesis and Takeaways

Bundle adjustment

Sparse nonlinear least squares on the observation graph; the gold standard.

Factorization

Affine is a rank-3 SVD; non-rigid lifts to $3l$.

Projective

Depths and normalization decide whether rank-4 means anything geometric.

Always

State the residual checks and the remaining projective, affine, or metric ambiguity.

Printed pp. 434-457Synthesis and Takeaways
Close by tying the routes together. Bundle adjustment is sparse nonlinear least squares on the observation graph; affine factorization is a rank-three approximation; projective factorization needs depths and normalization to mean anything geometric; plane and sequence methods initialize. Always state the remaining projective, affine, or metric ambiguity before claiming success.
