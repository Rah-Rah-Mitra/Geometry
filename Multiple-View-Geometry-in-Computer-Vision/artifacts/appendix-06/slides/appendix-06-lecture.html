The Nonlinear Least-Squares Problem

Residual vector

$\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf P)$ stacks per-point reprojection errors in pixels.

Cost

Half the squared norm of the residual; nonlinear and non-convex in $\mathbf P$.

Objective

$$C(\mathbf P)=\tfrac12\,\lVert \boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf P)\rVert^2=\tfrac12\sum_i \lVert \hat{\mathbf x}_i(\mathbf P)-\mathbf x_i\rVert^2$$

No closed form

Projection is nonlinear, so we iterate from a starting estimate $\mathbf P_0$.

Printed pp. 597-627The Nonlinear Least-Squares Problem
State the target precisely: find parameters that minimize the squared norm of a vector of residuals measuring geometric error. The cost surface is nonlinear and non-convex, so we cannot solve in one shot. Stress that geometric error in image units is the quantity we actually care about.

Newton's Method

Full Hessian

Uses gradient $\mathbf g$ and the true second-derivative matrix $\mathbf H$.

Cost & risk

Quadratic convergence near the optimum, but $\mathbf H$ is expensive and may be indefinite far away.

Newton step

$$\mathbf H\,\boldsymbol\delta=-\mathbf g,\qquad \mathbf H=\sum_i \nabla\epsilon_i\,\nabla\epsilon_i^{\!\top}+\sum_i \epsilon_i\,\nabla^2\epsilon_i$$

The wrinkle

The second term needs $\nabla^2\epsilon_i$; dropping it gives Gauss-Newton.

Printed pp. 597-627Newton's Method
Newton uses the full Hessian, so it needs second derivatives of every residual. Near a minimum it converges quadratically, but the Hessian is expensive and can be indefinite far from the solution, sending steps uphill. This motivates the cheaper Gauss-Newton approximation that follows.

Gauss-Newton

Approximate Hessian

Replace $\mathbf H$ by $\mathbf J^{\!\top}\mathbf J$, using only first derivatives.

Normal equations

One linear solve per iteration from the Jacobian alone.

Gauss-Newton step

$$(\mathbf J^{\!\top}\mathbf J)\,\boldsymbol\delta=-\mathbf J^{\!\top}\boldsymbol\epsilon,\qquad \mathbf J=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\epsilon}{\partial\mathbf P}$$

Caveat

Fast where residuals are small; no safeguard when the linear model is poor.

Printed pp. 597-627Gauss-Newton
Gauss-Newton drops the second-derivative term and approximates the Hessian by J-transpose-J. The step solves the normal equations built from the Jacobian alone. It is fast and accurate near the solution where residuals are small, but it offers no safeguard when the linear model is a poor fit far away.

Gradient Descent and the Two Regimes

Gradient descent

Step along $-\mathbf g$. Robust but slow; crawls down narrow valleys.

Gauss-Newton

Fast curvature-aware jumps, but reckless when far from the basin.

The blend

Levenberg-Marquardt chooses between them automatically.

Printed pp. 597-627Gradient Descent and the Two Regimes
Gradient descent moves along the negative gradient and is robust but slow, crawling through narrow valleys. Gauss-Newton is fast but reckless. Levenberg-Marquardt blends the two so the solver can be cautious when far out and aggressive when close, choosing automatically rather than by hand.

Levenberg-Marquardt

Damped normal equations

Add $\lambda$ to the diagonal of $\mathbf J^{\!\top}\mathbf J$.

Interpolation

$\lambda\!\to\!0$ gives Gauss-Newton; $\lambda$ large gives a short descent step.

LM step

$$(\mathbf J^{\!\top}\mathbf J+\lambda\,\mathbf D)\,\boldsymbol\delta=-\mathbf J^{\!\top}\boldsymbol\epsilon$$

Adapt $\lambda$

Accept or reject each trial; tune $\lambda$ from the trust ratio.

Printed pp. 597-627Levenberg-Marquardt
Levenberg-Marquardt augments the diagonal of the normal matrix with a damping term lambda. Small lambda recovers Gauss-Newton; large lambda yields a short gradient-descent step. After each trial the step is accepted or rejected and lambda is adapted from the trust ratio, the agreement between predicted and actual reduction.

Convergence Diagnostics

Four traces

Accepted objective, damping $\lambda$, trust ratio, pixel residuals.

Trust ratio

Actual reduction over predicted; near $1$ means the model is reliable.

Acceptance test

$$\rho=\frac{C(\mathbf P)-C(\mathbf P+\boldsymbol\delta)}{\text{predicted reduction}}$$

Result

Objective $2.4\!\times\!10^5\!\to\!15$; median error below one pixel.

Printed pp. 597-627Convergence Diagnostics
A final parameter vector is not evidence. We audit four traces together: accepted objective, damping lambda, the trust ratio comparing actual to predicted reduction, and pixel residual statistics. Accepted objectives are monotone non-increasing, and the median pixel error falls below one pixel by the end.

Parametrization and the Manifold

Over-parametrization

Redundant coordinates (e.g. a $4$-vector for a rotation) carry a constraint.

Minimal local chart

Use the right number of free directions in the tangent space.

Update on manifold

Apply the increment so each step respects the geometry.

Printed pp. 597-627Parametrization and the Manifold
Many vision quantities live on manifolds, not flat vector spaces. Over-parametrization with a constraint or a redundant rotation representation distorts the update. We prefer a minimal local parametrization and apply the increment on the manifold so each step respects the geometry of the unknown.

Robust Cost and Influence

Attenuated residual

Rewrite a robust cost as a weighted residual norm.

Reweighting

Iterate the weighted solve; outliers earn small weights.

Weight from cost

$$w=\frac{\sqrt{C(\delta)}}{\delta},\qquad C(\mathbf P)=\tfrac12\sum_i w_i^2\,\epsilon_i^2$$

Notebook result

Cauchy attenuation cut clean-data error $6.0\!\to\!1.3$ px; outliers stay visible.

Printed pp. 597-627Robust Cost and Influence
Outliers wreck a pure least-squares fit. A robust cost is rewritten as a weighted residual norm with an attenuation factor, downweighting large residuals through iteratively reweighted solves. The notebook shows Cauchy attenuation cutting outlier influence while keeping the bad points visible in the diagnostic, not deleted.

Sparse Bundle Adjustment

Block split

Unknowns separate into camera blocks and point blocks.

Locality

One observation touches exactly one camera and one point.

Arrowhead

Block-diagonal $\mathbf B$, $\mathbf C$ with a coupling band $\mathbf E$.

Printed pp. 597-627Sparse Bundle Adjustment
In bundle adjustment the unknowns split into camera blocks and point blocks, and one observation touches exactly one camera and one point. The normal matrix gains a block arrowhead structure. Exploiting this sparsity is what makes adjusting thousands of cameras and points computationally tractable at all.

The Schur Complement Reduction

Eliminate points

Fold point information into the camera block first.

Reduced solve

Solve the small camera system, then back-substitute for points.

Schur complement

$$(\mathbf B-\mathbf E\,\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf E^{\!\top})\,\boldsymbol\delta_c=-\mathbf g_c+\mathbf E\,\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf g_p$$

Verified

Schur camera and point steps match the dense solve to $\sim\!10^{-14}$.

Printed pp. 597-627The Schur Complement Reduction
Eliminate the point blocks first. The Schur complement folds point information into a much smaller reduced camera system, which we solve, then back-substitute for the points. The notebook verifies the Schur camera and point steps match the dense solve to machine precision: same step, cheaper route.
