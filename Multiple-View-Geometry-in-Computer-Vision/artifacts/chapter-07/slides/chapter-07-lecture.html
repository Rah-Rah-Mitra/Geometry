The Resectioning Problem

Given

World points $\mathbf{X}_i$ on a calibration object and noisy image points $\mathbf{x}_i$.

Sought

The homogeneous matrix with $\mathbf{x}_i = P\,\mathbf{X}_i$.

Degrees of freedom

$P$ is defined up to scale: 12 entries, 11 free.

Projection

$$\mathbf{x}_i \simeq P\,\mathbf{X}_i, \quad P \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times4}$$

Homogeneous scale

$$P \;\sim\; \lambda P, \quad \lambda \neq 0$$

Why depth matters

A single plane underconstrains a full projective camera; use depth relief.

Printed pp. 178-194The Resectioning Problem
Resectioning assumes a calibration object with accurately known world coordinates imaged to noisy pixel measurements. Each correspondence is one equation source. The goal is the homogeneous three by four matrix mapping world points to image points, defined only up to scale, so eleven degrees of freedom must be pinned down from the data.

The DLT Linear Equations

Cross-product trick

$\mathbf{x}_i \times P\mathbf{X}_i = \mathbf{0}$ because the vectors are parallel.

Two rows each

Each correspondence yields two independent linear equations in $\mathbf{p}$.

Solve

$\mathbf{p}$ is the null space of $A$, found by SVD.

Per-point equations

$$\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0}^\top & -w_i\mathbf{X}_i^\top & y_i\mathbf{X}_i^\top \\ w_i\mathbf{X}_i^\top & \mathbf{0}^\top & -x_i\mathbf{X}_i^\top \end{bmatrix}\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}$$

Stacked system

$$A\,\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}, \quad \mathbf{p}=\operatorname{vec}(P)$$

Printed pp. 178-194DLT Linear Equations
The Direct Linear Transformation turns each correspondence into two independent rows by exploiting that the image point and its projection are parallel, so their cross product vanishes. Stacking these rows builds the design matrix A, and the camera vector p lives in its null space, recovered by singular value decomposition.

Minimal and Over-determined Solution

Minimal case

11 unknowns need $5\tfrac12$ points, so 6 points in general position give an exact fit.

Over-determined

More points $\Rightarrow$ least squares via the smallest singular vector.

Caveat

This minimizes algebraic error, to be judged later in pixels.

Least squares

$$\min_{\|\mathbf{p}\|=1}\|A\mathbf{p}\| \Rightarrow \mathbf{p}=\mathbf{v}_{\min}$$

Printed pp. 178-194Minimal and Over-determined Solution
Eleven unknowns need at least five and a half correspondences, so six points in general position give an exact minimal solution. More points over-determine the system; the singular vector for the smallest singular value gives the algebraic least squares answer, which we then must judge in pixels rather than trust blindly.

Data Normalization in 2D and 3D

Translate

Shift each point cloud to its centroid.

Scale

Mean distance to $\sqrt2$ (image) and $\sqrt3$ (world).

Denormalize

Recover $P$ as $T^{-1}\tilde P\,U$.

Conditioning transforms

$$\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=T\mathbf{x}, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{X}}=U\mathbf{X}$$

Denormalized camera

$$P = T^{-1}\,\tilde{P}\,U$$

Why

Raw pixel and metric scales differ by orders of magnitude, wrecking the null vector.

Printed pp. 178-194Data Normalization
Raw pixel and world coordinates span wildly different magnitudes, wrecking the conditioning of A. We translate each cloud to its centroid and scale so the mean distance is root two in two dimensions and root three in three dimensions, run DLT on normalized data, then denormalize the matrix. This step is essential, not cosmetic.

Algebraic versus Geometric Error

Algebraic

The residual $\|A\mathbf{p}\|$; cheap to minimize, no metric meaning.

Geometric

Pixel distance between $\mathbf{x}_i$ and its reprojection $P\mathbf{X}_i$.

Inspect direction

Random residuals mean noise; structured residuals mean model error.

Reprojection cost

$$\sum_i d\big(\mathbf{x}_i,\,P\mathbf{X}_i\big)^2$$

Printed pp. 178-194Algebraic versus Geometric Error
The DLT minimizes the algebraic residual A p, which has no direct geometric meaning. What we actually care about is the geometric image error: the pixel distance between each measured point and its reprojection. Inspecting residual vectors, not just the residual norm, reveals whether errors are random noise or structured model failure.

The Gold Standard Estimate

Objective

Minimize total squared reprojection error over all 11 parameters.

Method

Iterative nonlinear least squares, e.g. Levenberg-Marquardt.

Seed

The normalized DLT estimate initializes the descent.

Maximum likelihood (Gaussian noise)

$$\hat P=\arg\min_{P}\sum_i \big\| \mathbf{x}_i - \widehat{P\mathbf{X}_i}\big\|^2$$

Interpretation

Under isotropic Gaussian image noise this is the MLE camera.

Printed pp. 178-194Gold Standard Refinement
The gold standard estimate refines the DLT seed by minimizing total squared reprojection error over all eleven parameters using iterative nonlinear least squares such as Levenberg Marquardt. Under Gaussian image noise this is the maximum likelihood estimate, and the algebraic DLT solution merely provides the starting point for that descent.

Extracting $K$, $R$, $t$ by RQ Decomposition

Factor the front block

$M = K R$ with $K$ upper triangular, $R$ a rotation.

RQ split

RQ decomposition of $M$ yields intrinsics $K$ and pose $R$.

Center

$C$ is the right null vector of $P$; then $t=-RC$.

Calibrated form

$$P = K\,[\,R \mid \mathbf{t}\,], \quad \mathbf{t}=-R\mathbf{C}$$

Printed pp. 178-194Recovering K, R, t by RQ
A finite projective camera factors as K times R bracket minus R C. Its leading three by three block is an upper triangular calibration matrix times a rotation, so an RQ decomposition splits it into intrinsics K and orientation R. The camera center C follows as the right null vector of P, fixing the translation.

Restricted Camera Estimation

Constraints

Zero skew, known aspect ratio, or fixed principal point.

Fewer parameters

Fit only the free physical unknowns, not a general $P$.

Honest fit

Exposes wrong intrinsics a full $P$ would silently absorb.

General intrinsics

$$K=\begin{bmatrix} \alpha_x & s & x_0 \\ 0 & \alpha_y & y_0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Typical restriction

$$s=0, \quad \alpha_x=\alpha_y$$

In the lab

Known-$K$ pose via solvePnP matches full DLT at sub-pixel error.

Printed pp. 178-194Restricted Camera Estimation
When skew is zero, pixels are square, or aspect ratio is known, the general projective camera over-parametrizes the problem. Restricted estimation fits only the free physical parameters, typically by minimizing geometric error with those constraints enforced. The result is less flexible but more honest, exposing wrong intrinsics that a full P would silently absorb.

Radial (Lens) Distortion Correction

Symptom

Straight lines bow; residuals grow radially toward the image edge.

Model

Radial displacement $L(r)$ about the principal point.

Fix first

Correct distortion before reading residuals as noise.

Radial model

$$\mathbf{x}_d = L(r)\,(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0)+\mathbf{x}_0$$

Printed pp. 178-194Radial Distortion Correction
Real lenses bend straight lines, displacing points radially from the principal point by an amount growing toward the periphery. Forcing a pinhole model onto distorted data leaves a structured, radially patterned residual field rather than random scatter. We fit or correct the distortion coefficients first, then judge the remaining residual as genuine measurement noise.

Recap and Calibration Checklist

Estimate

Stack DLT equations, normalize, solve the null space by SVD.

Validate

Refine to the gold standard; inspect pixel residual fields.

Decompose

RQ-factor $P$ into $K$, $R$, $t$; center is the null vector.

Constrain and correct

Restrict known intrinsics; remove radial distortion before judging error.

Workflow

$$\text{DLT}\;\to\;\text{gold standard}\;\to\;K,R,t$$

Key invariant

The camera center $\mathbf{C}$ satisfies $P\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{0}$ for any valid $P$.

Printed pp. 178-194Recap and Checklist
Resectioning turns correspondences into a homogeneous system solved by SVD, but every estimate must be validated geometrically. Normalize before DLT, inspect pixel residuals, factor P by RQ to read intrinsics and pose, restrict parameters when physics is known, and remove radial distortion before declaring the remaining error to be noise.
