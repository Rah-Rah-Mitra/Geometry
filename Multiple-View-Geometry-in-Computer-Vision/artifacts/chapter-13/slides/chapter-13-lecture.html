A Plane Induces a Homography

Construction

Back-project $x$ to the ray, intersect the chosen plane, then project that point into the second view.

Why it is projective

Each step is a projective map of the plane, so the composition is one fixed homography $H$.

Single matrix

The same $H$ transfers every point of that plane between the images.

Plane transfer

$$x' = H\,x \quad\text{for all } x \text{ imaging the plane } \pi$$

Plane model (camera 1)

$$n^{\top} X + d = 0$$

$H$ is $3\times 3$, nonsingular for a generic plane and view pair.

Printed pp. 325-343A Plane Induces a Homography
Trace a ray through an image point until it meets the chosen plane, project that intersection into the second view, and the composition is a projective map. Because the plane is fixed, the same two-by-two-by-three matrix sends every planar point from one image to the other, giving the plane-induced homography H.

The Calibrated Formula

Plane-induced homography

$$H = K'\Big(R - \tfrac{t\,n^{\top}}{d}\Big)K^{-1}$$

Read the terms

$R$ rotates the ray; $-t\,n^{\top}/d$ is a rank-one translation term scaled by inverse depth.

Sign convention

With $n^{\top}X+d=0$ and $P'=K'[R\,|\,t]$, flipping either convention flips the rank-one term.

Printed pp. 325-343The Calibrated Formula
For calibrated cameras with second camera rotation R and translation t, a plane with unit normal n at depth d induces H equal to K-prime times R minus t n transpose over d, all times K inverse. The rank-one term encodes translation scaled by inverse depth; rotation supplies the rest of the warp.

Homography From a Plane, Plane From $H$

Plane → H

Substitute $n,d,R,t$ into the formula and read off the transfer matrix directly.

H → plane

Factor $K'^{-1}HK$ into a rotation plus a rank-one part to recover $n$ and $d$.

Decomposition

$$K'^{-1}H K = R - \tfrac{t}{d}\,n^{\top}$$

Ambiguity

The factorization generically yields two physically plausible $(R,t,n,d)$ solutions; extra points disambiguate.

Check

A DLT homography from planar matches must match the closed-form $H$.

Printed pp. 325-343Homography From a Plane, Plane From H
The map runs both directions. Given the plane and motion we read off H by the formula; given H and the camera pair we factor it back into a rotation part and a rank-one translation part, recovering the plane normal and depth up to the usual ambiguities. Decomposition generically yields two physically plausible solutions.

Compatibility With $F$

Factorization of F

$$F = [e']_{\times}\,H$$

Symmetry constraint

$$H^{\top}F + F^{\top}H = 0$$

Meaning

$Hx$ always lands on the epipolar line $Fx$ of $x$ in the second view.

Printed pp. 325-343Compatibility With F
A plane homography and the fundamental matrix are not independent. Any valid plane homography satisfies F equal to the skew of the second epipole times H, and equivalently H transpose F plus F transpose H equals zero. This is the algebraic statement that the transferred point Hx lies on the epipolar line of x.

Plane Plus Parallax

Off-plane points

$Hx$ predicts where the ray hits the plane, not the true 3D point's image.

Parallax

The gap $x' - Hx$ is the plane-induced parallax, a usable signal, not an error.

Direction

Each parallax vector points along the epipolar line toward the epipole $e'$.

Printed pp. 325-343Plane Plus Parallax
Apply H to an off-plane point and you predict the image of where its ray pierces the plane, not the true correspondence. The residual from Hx to the real x-prime is parallax: it points along the epipolar line toward the epipole, and its length grows with the point's relative depth off the plane.

Parallax Scales With Relative Depth

Parallax relation

$$x' = Hx + \rho\, e', \qquad \rho \propto \frac{\text{distance off plane}}{\text{depth}}$$

On the plane

Distance off the plane is zero, so $\rho = 0$ and $x' = Hx$ exactly.

Off the plane

The further from the plane, the longer the displacement from $Hx$ toward $e'$.

Printed pp. 325-343Parallax Scales With Relative Depth
The parallax magnitude is governed by the ratio of the point's distance off the plane to its depth, multiplied by the camera baseline. Points on the plane have zero parallax; the further a point sits from the plane, the longer its displacement from Hx along the epipolar line toward the epipole.

Recovering $F$ From a Plane Homography

Setup

Know a plane homography $H$ plus the parallax of a few off-plane points.

Find the epipole

Parallax join lines $Hx \times x'$ all pass through $e'$; intersect two of them.

Assemble F

With $e'$ in hand, set $F = [e']_{\times}H$.

Printed pp. 325-343Recovering F From a Plane Homography
Plane plus parallax inverts into an estimation recipe. With a plane homography and the parallax of a few off-plane points, the epipole sits at the common intersection of the parallax join lines, and F equals the skew of that epipole times H. Two off-plane points generically pin the epipole down.

The Infinite Homography $H_\infty$

Plane at infinity

$$H_\infty = K'RK^{-1} = \lim_{d\to\infty} H$$

Transfers directions

It maps vanishing points and directions between the two views.

Rotation only

The translation term vanishes, isolating the camera rotation $R$.

Pure rotation

If $t=0$, $H_\infty$ transfers all depths, parallax-free.

Printed pp. 325-343The Infinite Homography
Let the plane recede to infinity, or equivalently drop the translation term, and H reduces to K-prime R K inverse, the infinite homography. It transfers vanishing points and directions between the views, isolates the rotation-only part of the motion, and is the backbone of camera calibration and rotation estimation.

Homologies and Plane Transfer

Planar homology

A homography with a fixed point (vertex) and a fixed line (axis) of fixed points.

Where it appears

Symmetry and shadow relations between two coplanar figures in one image.

Chaining transfer

$H_\infty$ composes successive transfers to relay matches across a sequence.

Printed pp. 325-343Homologies and Plane Transfer
When a plane homography fixes a point and a line of points, it is a planar homology, the structure behind symmetry and shadow relations between two coplanar configurations in one image. The infinite homography composes successive transfers, letting a known plane relay correspondences across a whole image sequence.

Image mosaicing

Takeaways

Plane → H

A nondegenerate world plane induces a full-rank image homography $H$.

Formula

$H = K'(R - t n^{\top}/d)K^{-1}$ exposes rotation, translation, normal, and depth.

Compatible with F

$F=[e']_\times H$ and $H^{\top}F+F^{\top}H=0$; $Hx$ lies on $Fx$.

Parallax is signal

Off-plane points displace from $Hx$ along the epipolar line toward $e'$, scaled by relative depth.

Rotation component

$$H_\infty = K'RK^{-1}$$

Transfer, rectification, and mosaicing all rest on these relations.

Printed pp. 325-343Takeaways
A nondegenerate world plane induces a full-rank image homography H equal to K-prime times R minus t n transpose over d times K inverse. It is compatible with F, off-plane points reveal themselves through epipolar parallax, and the infinite homography isolates rotation. Together these power transfer, rectification, and mosaicing.
