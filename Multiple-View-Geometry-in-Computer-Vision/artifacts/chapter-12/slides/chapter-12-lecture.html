Structure Computation

Triangulating a 3D point from two known cameras and a noisy match

The question

Cameras $P,P'$ known; given $x \leftrightarrow x'$, where is $X$?

The catch

Measured rays usually miss, so triangulation is an estimator.

The goal

Choose the objective that matches trustworthy geometry.

Open by stating the two-view structure problem: cameras are known, a noisy match is given, find the 3D point. Stress that because measured rays rarely meet, triangulation is an estimator and our job is to choose the estimator whose objective matches the geometry we actually trust.

Image Noise Makes the Rays Skew

Ideal case

Exact $x,x'$ give coplanar rays that intersect at $X$.

Noisy case

Measured rays pass each other: a skew pair, no intersection.

Midpoint estimate

Midpoint of the common perpendicular is Euclidean, not invariant.

Printed pp. 310-324Noisy Rays Are Skew
Walk through the picture of two back-projected rays. With perfect measurements they intersect at the world point; with pixel noise they pass each other and become skew. The common-perpendicular gap is a useful Euclidean diagnostic but not a projective objective worth minimizing.

Linear (DLT) Triangulation

Constraint

$x \times PX = 0, \quad x' \times P'X = 0$

Stacked system

$A X = 0,\quad A=\begin{bmatrix} u\,p^{3\top}-p^{1\top}\\ v\,p^{3\top}-p^{2\top}\\ u'p'^{3\top}-p'^{1\top}\\ v'p'^{3\top}-p'^{2\top}\end{bmatrix}$

Solve

$X$ is the unit null vector: smallest singular vector of $A$.

Two rows per view

Each image gives two independent linear equations in $X$.

Fast, but algebraic

SVD minimizes $\lVert AX\rVert$, an algebraic residual with no metric meaning.

Role

Excellent initializer for the geometric optimization to follow.

Printed pp. 310-324Linear DLT Triangulation
Derive the four homogeneous equations from x cross PX equals zero. Stack them and take the SVD null vector. Emphasize this is fast and a great initializer, but it minimizes an algebraic residual with no direct geometric meaning, so it is only a starting point.

Why Linear Methods Are Not Projective-Invariant

Invariance test

$\widehat{X}(PH^{-1},P'H^{-1}) \stackrel{?}{=} H\,\widehat{X}(P,P')$

DLT fails

The norm $\lVert AX\rVert$ is not preserved by a projective $H$.

Midpoint fails

Euclidean distance has no meaning in a projective frame.

Image correction passes

It alters only measured $x,x'$ before triangulating, so it commutes with $H$.

Printed pp. 310-324Not Projective Invariant
Explain that a good triangulator should commute with projective change of the world frame. DLT and the midpoint method do not, because their residual norm is not invariant. Image-space correction does commute, because it touches only the measured points before triangulating.

The Geometric (Reprojection) Error

Cost function

$\mathcal{C}(\widehat{x},\widehat{x}') = d(x,\widehat{x})^2 + d(x',\widehat{x}')^2$

Subject to

$\widehat{x}'^{\top} F\, \widehat{x} = 0$

Meaning

Maximum likelihood under isotropic Gaussian image noise.

Measured vs reprojected

Distance between the observed point and the projection of $X$.

Versus algebraic

This metric cost replaces the meaningless DLT residual.

Two unknowns

The constraint leaves one free parameter on the epipolar pencil.

Printed pp. 310-324Geometric Reprojection Error
Define the geometric cost as the sum of squared distances between measured and reprojected points. This is the maximum likelihood objective under isotropic Gaussian image noise, and it is the quantity the optimal method actually minimizes, unlike the algebraic DLT residual.

Optimal Triangulation: The Hartley-Sturm Idea

Reduce the problem

The optimal $\widehat{x}$ lies on an epipolar line $l(t)$ through epipole $e$.

One parameter

Pencil of epipolar lines is parametrized by a single scalar $t$.

Reduced cost

$s(t)=\dfrac{t^2}{1+f^2t^2}+\dfrac{(ct+d)^2}{(at+b)^2+f'^2(ct+d)^2}$

Printed pp. 310-324Optimal Triangulation
State the optimal-triangulation idea: the corrected pair must satisfy the epipolar constraint exactly while staying closest to the measurements. Hartley and Sturm parametrize the epipolar pencil by one scalar and reduce the search to the real roots of a degree-six polynomial.

Finding the Minimum: A Degree-6 Polynomial

Stationarity

$s'(t)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; g(t)=0,\quad \deg g = 6$

Pick the global root

Evaluate $s(t)$ at every real root and at $t=\infty$; keep the smallest.

Local minima exist

Never trust the first descent: the cost can have several wells.

Printed pp. 310-324The Degree-Six Polynomial
Show how the cost becomes a one-parameter rational function of the pencil parameter. Setting its derivative to zero clears to a degree-six polynomial; evaluate the cost at each real root and the asymptotic value, then keep the global minimum rather than the first local one.

Correcting Points to Corresponding Epipolar Lines

Optimal correction

$\widehat{x},\widehat{x}'$ are the closest points on a matched epipolar pair.

Then triangulate

Corrected rays meet exactly, so any method returns the optimum.

Sampson step

$\delta = -\dfrac{x'^{\top}Fx}{\lVert F_{1,2}x\rVert^2+\lVert F^{\top}_{1,2}x'\rVert^2}\,(\cdot)$

Printed pp. 310-324Correcting To Epipolar Lines
Interpret the solution geometrically: the corrected points are the closest points on a matched pair of corresponding epipolar lines. After correction the two rays meet exactly, so any triangulator, even plain DLT, returns the optimal point. Contrast with the cheap first-order Sampson step.

Uncertainty of the Reconstructed Point

Covariance propagation

$\Sigma_X = \sigma^2 \,(J^{\top}J)^{-1},\quad J=\partial(\hat x,\hat x')/\partial X$

Anisotropy

Largest eigenvector of $\Sigma_X$ stretches along the viewing rays.

Lesson

Small residual $\neq$ well-localized point.

Printed pp. 310-324Covariance Of The Point
Propagate image covariance through the projection Jacobian: the point covariance is sigma squared times the inverse normal matrix. The largest uncertainty axis stretches along the viewing rays, so a low residual does not guarantee a well-localized reconstruction.

Degenerate Configurations: Narrow Ray Angle

Wide angle

Rays cross sharply; depth is well constrained, $J^{\top}J$ well conditioned.

Narrow angle

Rays nearly parallel; $J^{\top}J$ ill-conditioned, depth variance explodes.

Diagnostic

Watch the ray angle, not just baseline length.

Printed pp. 310-324Small Baseline Degeneracy
Connect uncertainty to geometry: as the angle between rays shrinks the normal matrix becomes ill-conditioned and depth variance blows up. Teach students to monitor ray angle, not baseline length alone, and to flag distant structure seen under a narrow angle.

Cheirality and Reconstructing a 3D Line

Cheirality

$(P_{3}X)>0 \;\wedge\; (P'_{3}X)>0$

Line as plane meet

Image lines $l,l'$ back-project to planes $P^{\top}l$, $P'^{\top}l'$.

Degeneracy

If the line lies in an epipolar plane, the two planes collapse.

Printed pp. 310-324Cheirality And Line Reconstruction
Add two practical checks. Cheirality requires positive depth in both cameras, ruling out reflected solutions a small residual cannot detect. A 3D line is recovered as the intersection of two back-projected planes, which degenerates when the line lies in an epipolar plane.

Recommended Workflow and Takeaways

1. Initialize

Linear DLT gives a finite, robust starting point.

2. Optimize

Optimal image correction or nonlinear reprojection refinement.

3. Validate

Check cheirality and ray angle before trusting the point.

Core message

The objective, not just the residual, defines a sound triangulation method.

Printed pp. 310-324Workflow And Takeaways
Summarize the recommended pipeline: DLT to initialize, optimal image correction or nonlinear refinement to reach the geometric optimum, then cheirality and ray-angle diagnostics before trusting the point. Recap that the objective, not just the residual, defines a sound triangulation method.
