Rank and Null Space

Rank

Number of independent columns: the dimension of the image of the map.

Null space

All directions sent to zero. A homogeneous solution lives here.

Rank-nullity

$\operatorname{rank}(A) + \dim\ker(A) = n$

Vision cue

A constraint matrix that should be rank deficient flags estimable structure when its smallest singular value collapses.

Printed pp. 578-587Rank and Null Space
Rank counts the independent columns, while the null space collects every direction the matrix annihilates. In vision these are not abstractions: a homogeneous solution is a null vector, and a constraint matrix that should be rank deficient announces estimable structure exactly when its smallest singular value drops toward zero.

The Singular Value Decomposition

Factorization

$A = U D V^{\mathsf T}$

Read it geometrically

$V^{\mathsf T}$ rotates, $D$ scales along axes by the singular values $\sigma_i \ge 0$, $U$ rotates again.

Printed pp. 578-587The Singular Value Decomposition
Every real matrix factors as a rotation, an axis-aligned scaling, and another rotation. The singular values are the scale factors along the principal axes. This rotate-scale-rotate picture is the single most useful mental model in the appendix, because it reduces an opaque matrix to three geometric moves.

Null Space from the SVD

Homogeneous least squares

$\min_{\lVert x\rVert=1}\lVert Ax\rVert \;\Rightarrow\; x = v_n$

The answer is one column

$v_n$ is the last column of $V$, the right singular vector with the smallest $\sigma$.

Used everywhere

DLT homography, the fundamental matrix, conic fitting — all end on this single line.

Printed pp. 578-587Null Space from the SVD
When we minimize the norm of A x subject to unit x, the answer is the last column of V, the right singular vector with the smallest singular value. Almost every linear estimator in the course, from the DLT homography to the fundamental matrix, ends with exactly this one line.

Low-Rank Approximation

Eckart–Young

$A_r = \sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i u_i v_i^{\mathsf T}$

Closest rank-$r$ matrix

Zero the smallest singular values: $A_r$ minimizes $\lVert A - B\rVert$ over all rank-$r$ $B$.

Printed pp. 578-587Low-Rank Approximation
Zeroing the smallest singular values gives the closest rank r matrix in Frobenius and spectral norm, the Eckart and Young theorem. This is how we force the fundamental matrix to rank two and how noisy measurement or track matrices are cleaned by discarding the singular values that merely encode noise.

Eigen-decomposition and Symmetry

Symmetric matrix

$S = Q \Lambda Q^{\mathsf T}, \quad Q^{\mathsf T}Q = I$

Spectral theorem

Real eigenvalues, orthonormal eigenvectors — $S$ diagonalizes by a pure rotation.

Positive definite

All $\lambda_i > 0$: valid covariances and genuine ellipse conics.

Printed pp. 578-587Eigen-decomposition and Symmetry
A symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and an orthonormal eigenbasis, so it diagonalizes by a rotation. Positive definiteness means every eigenvalue is strictly positive, which is what makes a covariance matrix valid and a conic a genuine ellipse rather than a degenerate or imaginary curve.

QR and RQ Decomposition

Two orderings

$A = QR \qquad A = RQ$

QR

Orthogonal $Q$ times upper-triangular $R$. Gram–Schmidt in matrix form.

RQ

Upper-triangular $R$ times orthogonal $Q$ — the camera-friendly order.

Printed pp. 578-587QR and RQ Decomposition
QR writes a matrix as an orthogonal factor times upper triangular; RQ reverses the order to upper triangular times orthogonal. RQ is the one we want for cameras, because the calibration matrix is upper triangular and the orientation is a rotation, matching the physical structure of a finite projective camera.

Factoring the Camera P

Finite camera

$P = K\,[\,R \mid t\,], \quad M = KR$

RQ on the left block

Apply RQ to $M = P_{1:3}$: upper-triangular $K$, rotation $R$.

Sign fixing

Force positive focal terms ($k_{ii}>0$) and $\det R = +1$; centre from $-M^{-1}p_4$.

Printed pp. 578-587Factoring the Camera P
The left three by three block of a finite camera is calibration times rotation. RQ recovers both: K is the upper-triangular intrinsics and R is the rotation. We fix signs so the focal lengths are positive and the determinant of R equals one, then read the centre from the remaining column.

Cholesky and the Absolute Conic

Cholesky of a PD matrix

$S = L L^{\mathsf T}, \quad L \text{ lower-}\triangle$

From $\omega$ to $K$

The IAC obeys $\omega = K^{-\mathsf T}K^{-1}$. Cholesky-factor $\omega$, then invert to read off $K$.

Printed pp. 578-587Cholesky and the Absolute Conic
A positive definite matrix factors as a lower triangular times its transpose. Applied to the image of the absolute conic, Cholesky extracts the calibration directly: omega equals K inverse transpose times K inverse, so factoring omega and inverting gives K without ever decomposing a full camera matrix.

Pseudo-inverse and Conditioning

Moore–Penrose

$A^{+} = V D^{+} U^{\mathsf T}$

Solves least squares

$D^{+}$ inverts the nonzero $\sigma_i$. Back-projecting an image point uses $P^{+}$.

Condition number

$\kappa = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ predicts how noise is amplified — normalize data.

Printed pp. 578-587Pseudo-inverse and Conditioning
The pseudo-inverse inverts the nonzero singular values and solves least squares; the back-projection of an image point through a camera uses it directly. The condition number, the ratio of largest to smallest singular value, predicts how measurement noise is amplified, which is why data normalization matters so much in practice.

Skew-Symmetric Matrices

Cross product as a matrix

$a \times b = [a]_\times\, b$

Rank two, null vector $a$

$[a]_\times$ is $3\times3$, rank $2$; its one-dimensional null space is $a$ itself.

Where it appears

Epipolar lines, the essential matrix $E=[t]_\times R$, triangulation constraints.

Printed pp. 578-587Skew-Symmetric Matrices
The cross product a cross b equals the skew matrix of a times b. That skew matrix is rank two, and its one-dimensional null space is the vector a itself. This is the algebra behind epipolar lines, the essential matrix, and triangulation constraints written as vanishing cross products.

Toolbox Recap

SVD

Rotate–scale–rotate; null space is $v_n$; rank-$r$ truncation is the closest low-rank matrix.

RQ

Factor $M=KR$ into upper-triangular calibration and rotation.

Cholesky

$\omega = K^{-\mathsf T}K^{-1}$ gives $K$ from the absolute conic.

Diagnostics

Pseudo-inverse for least squares; condition number for noise sensitivity; skew matrices for cross products.

Printed pp. 578-587Toolbox Recap
Hold on to the rotate-scale-rotate picture of the SVD, the last column of V as a null space, rank-r truncation as the closest low-rank matrix, RQ as the camera factorization, and Cholesky as the route from omega to K. Each decomposition turns a matrix back into geometry you can reason about.
