Signed Projective Depth

Three ingredients

Third row of $P$, sign of $\det M$, and the homogeneous scale of $X$.

Not just $(PX)_3$

The raw third coordinate alone has no invariant sign.

Depth scalar

$$x = w\,PX,\qquad \operatorname{depth} = \frac{\operatorname{sign}(\det M)\,(PX)_3}{X_4\,\lVert m^3\rVert}$$

Invariance

Rescaling $P\mapsto\lambda P$ or $X\mapsto\mu X$ leaves the sign fixed.

Printed pp. 515-532Signed Projective Depth
Define depth as a signed quantity built from three pieces: the third row of the projection, the determinant sign of the left block, and the homogeneous representative. Emphasize that the raw third coordinate of P X is not enough; without the determinant and scale factors the sign is meaningless and not invariant.

Projective Freedom Loses Cheirality

The gauge

Reconstruction is fixed only up to $X\mapsto HX$, $P\mapsto PH^{-1}$.

The danger

Some $H$ flip a point across $\pi_\infty$, reversing its depth sign.

Same images, flipped depth

$$x_{ij}=P_jX_i=(P_jH^{-1})(HX_i),\quad \operatorname{sign}(w)\text{ can change}$$

Resolution

Restrict $H$ to transforms that respect the front/back labels.

Printed pp. 515-532Projective Ambiguity Loses Cheirality
Explain that an arbitrary projective transform can flip a point across the plane at infinity, swapping its depth sign while preserving all image measurements. This is why reconstruction from correspondences alone leaves orientation undetermined and motivates restricting the transformation to those that respect cheirality.

The Cheiral Inequalities

Points

Each reconstructed point must lie on the chosen side: $X_i^\top v > 0$.

Centres

Each camera centre: $\delta\,C_j^\top v > 0$.

Meaning

$v$ is the candidate $\pi_\infty$; $\delta$ is the transform's orientation sign.

Half-space system

$$X_i^\top v > 0,\qquad \delta\,C_j^\top v > 0,\quad \delta=\operatorname{sign}(\det H)$$

Prerequisite

Representative signs fixed so every $w_{ij}$ is positive.

Printed pp. 515-532The Cheiral Inequalities
State the inequalities precisely after representative signs are fixed: points must satisfy X transpose v positive, centres must satisfy delta times C transpose v positive. The vector v encodes the candidate plane at infinity and delta is the orientation sign of the transform. These are linear half-space constraints.

Feasibility as a Linear Program

Strict to slack

Replace each $a^\top v > 0$ with $a^\top v \ge d$.

Maximize margin

Solve for the largest common $d$ over bounded $v$.

LP certificate

$$\max_{v,\,d}\ d \quad\text{s.t.}\quad a_k^\top v \ge d,\ \ \lVert v\rVert_\infty \le 1$$

Separating Plane and Orientation

Separable

A plane splits all centres from all points: both $\delta$ cells feasible.

Interleaved

A camera sits among the points: the $\delta=-1$ cell vanishes.

Consequence

The opposite-orientation realization survives only when separable.

Printed pp. 515-532Separating Plane and Orientation
Connect the two delta cells to geometry. When a plane separates all camera centres from all visible points, both orientations stay feasible and an opposite-orientation realization survives. Interleave a camera among the points and the delta equals minus one cell vanishes, removing that ambiguity.
