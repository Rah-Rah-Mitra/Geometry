Canonical Cameras and Definition

Canonical frame

A projective change of coordinates puts the first camera at the identity.

Cameras

$P=[\,I\mid 0\,],\ P'=[\,A\mid a_4\,],\ P''=[\,B\mid b_4\,]$

Tensor slices

$T_i = a_i\, b_4^\top - a_4\, b_i^\top$

Index convention

Slice index belongs to view 1; rows to view 2, columns to view 3.

Printed pp. 365-390Canonical Cameras and Definition
Work through the canonical frame where the first camera is the identity projection. Show that the tensor slices are differences of outer products formed from the columns of the second and third camera matrices. Make clear the index convention: the slice index belongs to view one, rows to view two, columns to view three.

Line-Triplet Incidence Creates the Tensor

Back-projection

Each image line back-projects to a plane through its camera centre.

Point-line-line relation

$l_i = l'_j\, l''_k\, T_i{}^{jk}$

Printed pp. 365-390Line-Triplet Incidence
Explain that each image line back-projects to a plane through its camera centre, and three corresponding lines must share one space line. Derive the point-line-line relation: contracting the tensor with the two image lines reproduces the line in the first view up to scale. This incidence is what creates the tensor.

Tensor Slices and Rank

Rank two

Each slice is a difference of two outer products, so $\operatorname{rank} T_i \le 2$.

Null-vectors

Left null-vector is an epipolar line in view 2; right null-vector in view 3.

From the SVD

$u_i^\top T_i = 0,\qquad T_i\, v_i = 0$

Numerical check

Compute slice ranks and confirm null-vectors directly from the decomposition.

Printed pp. 365-390Tensor Slices and Rank
Each slice is a rank-two matrix because it is a difference of two outer products. Its left null-vector is an epipolar line in view two and its right null-vector an epipolar line in view three. Have students compute slice ranks numerically and confirm the null-vectors directly from the singular value decomposition.

Epipoles and Fundamental Matrices

Epipoles

The slice null-lines meet at the epipoles $e'$ and $e''$ of the first camera.

Recovered fundamentals

$F_{21}=[e']_\times[T_1,T_2,T_3]\,e''$

$F_{31}=[e'']_\times[T_1^\top,T_2^\top,T_3^\top]\,e'$

Shadows

Pairwise fundamentals are projections of the fuller three-view geometry.

Printed pp. 365-390Epipoles and Fundamental Matrices
The null-lines of the three slices meet at the epipoles of the first camera seen in views two and three. Once those epipoles are recovered, the tensor yields the two fundamental matrices relating the first view to the others. Stress that pairwise fundamentals are shadows of the fuller three-view geometry.

Plane-Induced Homographies

Line selects a plane

A line $l'$ in view 2 back-projects to a plane through the second camera centre.

Induced map

That plane induces a homography from view 1 to view 3.

Contraction

$H_{13}(l') = [\,T_1^\top l',\ T_2^\top l',\ T_3^\top l'\,]$

Transfer engine

This contraction is the mechanism behind tensor-based point transfer.

Printed pp. 365-390Plane-Induced Homographies
Contracting the tensor with a line in the second view produces a homography from view one to view three. Geometrically the line selects a plane through the second camera centre, and that plane induces the point-to-point map. This is the engine behind tensor-based point transfer, so make the construction explicit.

Point Transfer to the Third View

Given

A correspondence $x \leftrightarrow x'$ between views 1 and 2.

$x'' = H_{13}(l')\, x,\quad l' \ni x'$

Contrast this with intersecting two epipolar lines, which is the pairwise alternative students already know from earlier chapters.

Why Tensor Transfer Is More Stable

Epipolar transfer fails

Intersecting $F_{31}x$ and $F_{32}x'$ degenerates when the lines are near-parallel.

Trifocal plane

The collapse occurs near the plane through the three camera centres.

Printed pp. 365-390Transfer Stability
Pairwise epipolar transfer intersects two lines in the third image and degenerates when those lines are nearly parallel, which happens near the trifocal plane through the three centres. Tensor transfer avoids that collapse. Walk through the error-versus-offset curve so students see the conditioning advantage quantitatively, not just conceptually.

Choosing the Transfer Line

Free choice

For an exact pair, any non-epipolar line through $x'$ gives the same $x''$.

Forbidden choice

The epipolar line sends the transfer vector toward zero.

Degeneracy

$l' = l'_{\text{epi}} \ \Rightarrow\ H_{13}(l')\,x \to 0$

Practical rule

Pick a line well away from the epipolar direction so the point stays well defined.

Printed pp. 365-390Choosing the Transfer Line
Warn that the line through the second-view point must not be the epipolar line. For an exact correspondence any other line gives the same transferred point, but the epipolar choice sends the transfer vector to zero and leaves the homogeneous point undefined. This is the practical caveat behind tensor point transfer.

Trilinear Constraints as Incidence Tests

Point-point-point

An executable test built from the two skew-symmetric point matrices.

Relation

$[x']_\times \Big(\textstyle\sum_i x_i T_i\Big) [x'']_\times = 0$

Consistent triple

All nine entries vanish to floating-point roundoff.

Perturbed view

Disturbing one image leaves a structured residual signature.

Printed pp. 365-390Trilinear Constraints
Present the point-point-point relation as an executable incidence test built from the two skew-symmetric point matrices and the contracted tensor. For a consistent triple all nine entries vanish to roundoff; perturbing one view leaves a structured residual signature. These trilinearities are the algebraic heart of three-view matching.

Internal Constraints and Takeaways

18 of 27

The tensor has twenty-seven entries but only eighteen degrees of freedom.

Valid tensor

Internal constraints must hold; not every $3\times3\times3$ array is a trifocal tensor.

Projectively invariant

Homogeneous; the 3D frame choice and scale must not be mistaken for geometry.

Unifies transfer

Encodes line and point transfer and exposes epipoles and fundamentals of the triple.

Printed pp. 365-390Constraints and Takeaways
Close by noting the tensor has twenty-seven entries but only eighteen degrees of freedom, so a valid tensor must satisfy internal constraints. Recap that it is homogeneous and projectively invariant, unifies line and point transfer, and exposes the epipoles and fundamental matrices of the triple.
