Length-Minimizing Geodesics

A Geodesic Must Win a Global Contest

Definition Target

A minimizing geodesic is not merely stationary for length; its length equals the intrinsic distance between endpoints.

Common Failure

A geodesic can keep solving the geodesic equation after it stops minimizing, especially after a cut point or conjugate point.

local equation: \(\nabla_{\gamma'}\gamma'=0\)
global test: \(L(\gamma)=d(p,q)\)
Present a minimizing geodesic as a local certificate that has survived a global competition. The straight-looking curve is not trusted because it looks straight; it is trusted because nearby routes are longer within the relevant domain. Ask students to watch where minimization can fail before geodesic extension fails.
Complete Surfaces

Completeness Is About Never Running Out of Geodesic Time

Geodesic Completeness

Every geodesic with initial data \((p,v)\) extends to all real parameter values.

Metric Consequence

Completeness turns the distance function into a reliable global object rather than a local approximation.

Do Not Confuse

A curve can be infinitely long without every geodesic being extendable; the promise is uniform over initial data.

Completeness should be introduced as a promise about every unit-speed geodesic, not merely as a statement about drawing longer parameter intervals. The diagram separates three ideas that beginners often merge: local existence, indefinite extension, and distance-realizing behavior. Completeness guarantees the middle one, while minimization requires extra control.
Completeness Bridge

Completeness Turns Local Equations Into Global Access

The Teaching Version

Completeness is the condition that lets distance geometry, compactness behavior, and geodesic existence speak to each other.

Classroom Use

When a proof needs a shortest path, first ask whether the hypotheses make a minimizing geodesic available.

complete + connected \(\leadsto\) minimizing segments between points
Use the Hopf-Rinow-style slide as the chapter's main bridge between analysis and geometry. On a complete connected surface, metric compactness of closed bounded sets, geodesic extension, and the existence of minimizing segments reinforce one another. The exact theorem has hypotheses, but the teaching point is that completeness turns local equations into global access.
Jacobi Fields

Neighboring Geodesics Measure Curvature By Separation

Inspection Target

Track the length of the separation field \(J\) along a radial geodesic.

Model Equation

The scalar comparison in constant curvature behaves like \(j''+Kj=0\), with \(j(0)=0\) and \(j'(0)=1\).

area growth in polar coordinates is controlled by \(j(r)\)
Introduce Jacobi fields as a measuring device for a family of geodesics. The important number is the separation length between neighboring rays, because its zeros or shrinkage warn that the exponential map is folding. Students should read the blue, gray, and amber curves as three curvature regimes rather than as unrelated formulas.
Conjugate Points

Conjugacy Means the Exponential Map Has Folded

Definition Signal

A point \(\gamma(t_0)\) is conjugate to \(\gamma(0)\) when a nonzero Jacobi field vanishes at both ends.

Geometric Translation

There is an infinitesimal family of geodesics that begins together and meets again to first order.

Minimizing Warning

Past the first conjugate point, the same geodesic is no longer a safe shortest-path certificate.

Define conjugate points through loss of rank in the exponential map, then translate that into a visible collapse of geodesic polar coordinates. A conjugate point does not mean the geodesic stops; it means a variation has found another first-order way to arrive. This distinction is crucial before discussing minimization past the first conjugate point.
Covering Surfaces

Lift the Path, Preserve the Local Metric, Reveal the Topology

Local Statement

A covering map can be locally one-to-one and metric-preserving on small neighborhoods.

Global Statement

The same projection can identify distant lifted points and turn straight lifted paths into closed curves downstairs.

\(\text{length}(\widetilde\gamma)=\text{length}(\pi\circ\widetilde\gamma)\) locally
Frame covering surfaces as a way to separate local metric geometry from global topology. The lift keeps angles and lengths locally, while the projection may identify points far apart upstairs. This is why flat cylinders and tori can share local Euclidean behavior while carrying different closed geodesic patterns.
Inner-Product Preserving Maps

Local Isometry Is a Metric Test, Not a Topology Test

\(\langle dF_p u,dF_p v\rangle_{F(p)}=\langle u,v\rangle_p\)

Preserved

Lengths of sufficiently small curves, angles between tangent vectors, and geodesic equations under the map.

Not Forced

One-to-one global behavior, absence of closed curves, or equality of fundamental groups.

Use the isometry slide to distinguish local inner-product preservation from global sameness. A map can preserve the first fundamental form on small neighborhoods while still wrapping, covering, or identifying distant points. The inspection target is whether lengths of lifted paths agree before and after projection.
Constant Curvature

Three Model Geometries, Three Separation Laws

Positive

Geodesics tend to refocus; conjugate points appear in the model.

Zero

Separation grows linearly; the plane is the neutral comparison.

Negative

Nearby geodesics spread; polar coordinates remain well behaved longer.

The constant-curvature comparison slide gives students three reference geometries for the rest of the chapter. Positive curvature focuses geodesics, zero curvature preserves linear separation, and negative curvature spreads geodesics apart. These are not slogans: the Jacobi separation functions make the comparison measurable.
Bonnet-Type Compactness

Positive Curvature Forces Geodesics to Refocus

Curvature Hypothesis

A positive lower bound on Gaussian curvature keeps geodesics from separating indefinitely.

Global Consequence

For a complete connected surface, bounded diameter leads toward compactness in the surface setting.

Memory Hook

Positive curvature behaves like a lens: it focuses geodesic families and limits how far shortest paths can remain shortest.

Present Bonnet as the positive-curvature compactness mechanism. Uniform positive lower curvature prevents geodesics from remaining globally minimizing beyond a controlled distance, so the whole complete surface has bounded diameter. The slide should make the conclusion feel like focusing, not like a mysterious compactness trick.
Hadamard-Type Global Coordinates

Nonpositive Curvature Removes the Focusing Obstruction

Hypotheses to Notice

Completeness gives geodesic extension; simple connectedness removes deck transformations; \(K\le 0\) prevents conjugate focusing.

Conclusion Shape

The exponential map at a point behaves like a global coordinate system for the whole surface.

\(\exp_p:T_pM\to M\) becomes globally trustworthy
Present Hadamard as the opposite global story. On a complete simply connected surface with nonpositive curvature, the exponential map has no conjugate-point obstruction and behaves like a global coordinate chart. Point out that simple connectedness removes covering ambiguity while curvature removes focusing.
Topology Meets the Metric

Shortening a Loop Can Produce a Closed Geodesic

Variational Idea

Choose a nontrivial free homotopy class and seek the shortest loop inside that class.

Geodesic Outcome

If the infimum is achieved by a regular curve, first variation pushes the representative toward geodesic behavior.

Hypothesis Watch

Compactness or completeness assumptions matter because a minimizing sequence may otherwise escape.

Use the closed-geodesic slide to connect topology back to the metric. A nontrivial loop class on a compact surface can often be tightened until it becomes geodesic, provided the class cannot disappear by contraction. The diagram is a workflow: choose a loop class, shorten within the class, and inspect the limiting representative.

Recap and Instructor Prompt

Global Surface Geometry: A Four-Step Reading Habit

Extend

Check geodesic completeness before relying on long paths.

Minimize

Separate solving the geodesic equation from winning the distance problem.

Focus

Use Jacobi fields and conjugate points to detect where polar coordinates fail.

Lift

Move to a covering surface when topology hides a simple local metric story.

Prompt

Given a complete surface with a known curvature sign and a visible closed loop, decide first which theorem or lift should be tried before doing any calculation.
