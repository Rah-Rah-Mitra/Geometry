Moving Frame

Adapted Frames Split the Data

Decomposition Habit

D(direction field) = tangent contribution + normal contribution

Why Frames Matter

  • Tangent derivatives produce connection coefficients.
  • Normal derivatives produce the shape operator.
  • Compatibility of these derivatives becomes the structural equations.
Adapted frame = tangent basis plus chosen unit normalVisual target: separate components of change
Introduce an adapted frame as the bookkeeping device that keeps tangent and normal information from mixing accidentally. The tangent vectors span the visible patch direction, while the unit normal records how the patch is sitting in ambient three-space. The useful habit is to ask, for every derivative, which part remains tangent and which part points normal.
Metric Data

First Fundamental Form

Definition

I = E du2 + 2F du dv + G dv2

E = xu · xu, F = xu · xv, G = xv · xv

What It Measures

  • Length of tangent vectors and curves.
  • Angles between coordinate directions.
  • Area density through sqrt(EG - F2).
Intrinsic object: determined by tangent inner productsArea density: sqrt(EG - F^2)
Present the first fundamental form as the surface's intrinsic measuring instrument. The coefficients E, F, and G come from dot products of patch derivatives, so they determine lengths, angles, and area inside the patch. Emphasize that an observer living on the surface can recover this data without seeing the ambient normal.
Extrinsic Data

Second Fundamental Form and Shape

Shape Operator

S(T) = - dN(T)

II(T, U) = I(S(T), U)

Interpretation

  • First-order change of the normal becomes a tangent-plane operator.
  • Second-order position data becomes a symmetric bilinear form.
  • Eigenvalues of S are the principal curvatures.
Extrinsic: it uses the chosen normal fieldOperator viewpoint turns bending into linear algebra
Now add the extrinsic part of the story. The second fundamental form measures the normal component of second-order change, while the shape operator packages the same information as a tangent-plane linear map. The diagram should make clear that normal motion is detected by differentiating directions that already lie in the tangent plane.
Curvature Axes

Principal Directions

Diagonal Reading

S(e1) = k1 e1,   S(e2) = k2 e2

Use This Lens

  • Normal curvature varies by tangent direction.
  • Principal directions are stationary directions of that variation.
  • Principal curvatures are the two extreme readings.
Principal directions diagonalize the shape operatorNormal curvature is directional
Explain principal directions as the tangent directions where the shape operator stops mixing directions. In those directions normal curvature takes its extreme values, so the surface has a clean pair of bending readings at the point. This is a linear algebra moment inside differential geometry: diagonalizing the operator produces geometric axes.
Curvature Summary

Mean and Gaussian Curvature

Elliptic Point

K = k1k2 > 0

Both principal bends have the same sign.

Hyperbolic Point

K = k1k2 < 0

The two principal bends oppose each other.

Parabolic Point

K = 0

One principal direction carries no bend.

H = (k1 + k2) / 2,    K = k1k2

Mean curvature averages bendingGaussian curvature multiplies bending
Use the three panels to distinguish signed curvature patterns. Positive Gaussian curvature means both principal curvatures have the same sign, negative Gaussian curvature means they have opposite signs, and zero means one direction is locally flat or the product vanishes. Mean curvature is the averaged bending and can vanish even when the surface is not flat.
Intrinsic Differentiation

Covariant Derivatives Stay Tangent

Connection View

DTY = ∇TY + II(T,Y)N

Reading the Formula

  • The tangent piece is the covariant derivative.
  • The normal coefficient is controlled by the second fundamental form.
  • Christoffel symbols are the coordinate bookkeeping for the tangent piece.
Intrinsic derivative = tangent component of ambient changeNormal component remembers bending
Motivate the covariant derivative as the tangent part of an ordinary ambient derivative. Differentiating a tangent vector field in three-space may create a normal component, but intrinsic calculus keeps only the part that another surface observer can measure. This prepares the Christoffel symbols as metric-controlled correction terms rather than arbitrary coefficients.
The Hinge

Gauss Equation: Curvature Becomes Intrinsic

The Surprise

K is computable from the first fundamental form.

Why This Matters

  • K begins life as the determinant of S.
  • The Gauss equation rewrites it using metric derivatives.
  • Local isometries preserve K because they preserve the metric.
Core invariant: Gaussian curvatureTheorema egregium as a metric statement
This is the conceptual hinge of the lecture. The Gauss equation starts from adapted-frame derivatives but ends by showing that Gaussian curvature is determined by the first fundamental form and its derivatives. Stress the surprise: K was introduced using normals, yet local isometries preserve it because it is encoded in the metric.
Preservation

Local Isometry as Metric Agreement

Operational Test

Isurface A(T,U) = Isurface B(dφT,dφU)

Preserved Locally

  • Lengths of small curves.
  • Angles between tangent directions.
  • Gaussian curvature and intrinsic geodesic calculus.
Local isometry permits bending without stretchingCongruence is stricter
Define local isometry operationally: corresponding tangent vectors have the same inner products after mapping. The cylinder and plane are the clean mental model because their embeddings look different while the local metric can agree. The learner should separate bending in space from stretching on the surface.
Coordinate Control

Orthogonal Coordinates

Simplification

F = 0, so I = E du2 + G dv2

What Remains

  • The coordinate curves meet at right angles.
  • Scale factors E and G still vary across the patch.
  • Orthogonality simplifies formulas but does not flatten the surface.
Orthogonal does not mean unit speedScale factors remain in E and G
Orthogonal coordinates simplify calculations by making F equal zero, so the coordinate grid meets at right angles. The formulas are still surface formulas, not plane formulas; scale factors remain and carry the local stretching. Encourage students to inspect whether a coordinate simplification is geometric or merely notational.
Signed Normals

Orientation and the Gauss Map

Gauss Map

N: surface patch → unit sphere

Orientation Does

  • Chooses one continuous side of the surface.
  • Makes signed normal-dependent quantities coherent.
  • Turns normal movement into a map that can be measured.
Reverse orientation: N changes signNormal sweep carries curvature information
Orientation chooses a continuous side of the surface, and the Gauss map sends each point to that chosen unit normal. Changing orientation reverses the normal field, but it does not erase the curvature information carried by how the normals move. The key inspection target is the movement of normals, not the surface picture alone.
Accumulation

Total Curvature

Area-Weighted Curvature

Total curvature over R = integral of K dA over R

What to Inspect

  • Positive and negative regions may cancel.
  • The Gauss map records swept normal directions.
  • Global statements require care about region, orientation, and boundary.
Total curvature is a signed accumulated quantityNotebook diagnostic: torus window cancellation near zero
Move from pointwise curvature to accumulated curvature. Total curvature integrates signed Gaussian curvature over a region, while the Gauss map gives a complementary way to picture how much normal direction is swept. Use this slide to remind students that local signs and global cancellation can both matter.
Rigidity Question

Congruence Needs First and Second Data

Distinction

Same I does not force same embedding in R3.

Congruence Needs

  • First fundamental form for intrinsic measurement.
  • Second fundamental form for bending in space.
  • Compatibility equations so the data actually integrates to a surface.
Plane and cylinder: locally isometric, not congruentFundamental theorem viewpoint
Contrast local isometry with congruence. The first fundamental form controls intrinsic measurements, but congruence in three-space also needs compatible second fundamental information and the structural equations. This is why a plane and cylinder can be locally isometric without being related by a rigid motion.
