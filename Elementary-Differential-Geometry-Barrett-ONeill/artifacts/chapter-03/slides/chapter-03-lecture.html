What Euclidean Structure Adds

Definition target

Euclidean space is affine point data together with a vector inner product. The inner product supplies length, angle, and orthogonality.

  • Points move by affine formulas.
  • Vectors measure displacement between points.
  • The dot product decides metric claims.
  • Orientation requires an ordered volume test.
The key point is that Euclidean space carries more than a list of coordinates. Coordinates let us calculate, but the metric, inner product, and orientation are the objects a rigid motion must respect. Emphasize that the origin is not sacred: translations move it, while the vector-level inner product remains the measuring device.

Rigid Motions Have One Linear Part

F(p) = a + C pwithCTC = I
Isometry model

If F preserves Euclidean distance, its displacement behavior is encoded by one orthogonal matrix C and one translation vector a.

1Translation changes position, not displacement.
2Orthogonality preserves dot products.
3Distances follow from |C(q-p)| = |q-p|.
This is the algebraic model for the whole chapter. A Euclidean isometry is represented as a translation followed by an orthogonal linear map, so the distance calculation collapses to the identity C transpose C equals I. The displayed tetrahedron is deliberately asymmetric so that students can see the object move without confusing the motion with a symmetry of the object.

A Distance Witness, Not Just a Drawing

For two points p and q, the moved distance is checked by subtracting first.

F(q) - F(p) = C(q - p)
|F(q)-F(p)|2 = (q-p)TCTC(q-p)
= |q-p|2

The translation vector vanishes from the edge calculation; the orthogonal matrix does the measuring work.

Walk through the two-segment measurement in the diagram. The original edge and moved edge have different locations and directions in the drawing, but their lengths agree because the orthogonal part preserves dot products. This is the first example of a visual paired with a numerical witness.

Tangent Maps of Rigid Motions

Local rule

At every base point p, the differential sends an attached tangent vector v to C v.

dFp(v) = C v

The subscript p matters conceptually, but for rigid motions the matrix does not vary with p.

For a general smooth map, the derivative can depend on the base point. For a rigid motion F(p)=a+Cp, the tangent map is unusually clean: every tangent vector is carried by the same matrix C, regardless of where it is attached. This is why rigid motions preserve not just distances between points, but the entire first-order measuring apparatus.

Frames Are Moving Instruments

An ordered orthonormal frame records three mutually perpendicular unit directions attached to a point.

Frame check

Lengths and pairwise dot products certify orthonormality. Determinant sign certifies handedness.

  • ei · ej = 0 for distinct directions.
  • |ei| = 1 for each direction.
  • det[e1 e2 e3] = +1 or -1.
A frame is a portable measuring instrument: three unit directions, mutually perpendicular, attached to a point. Under an orientation-preserving rigid motion, the frame remains right handed; under a reflection, it becomes left handed. The diagram turns the determinant sign into a visible handedness test.

Orientation Is a Signed Volume Test

det[u\ v\ w] = u · (v × w)
+

Frame agrees with the chosen orientation.

-

Frame reverses the chosen orientation.

Rigid motions with determinant +1 preserve the sign; determinant -1 flips it.

This slide turns orientation into a computation. The determinant of the matrix with columns u, v, w equals the triple scalar product, so its sign records which side of the plane the third vector occupies. A rigid motion with determinant plus one preserves this sign, while determinant minus one reverses it.

The Linear Part Splits Into Two Components

SO(3)

CTC = I, det C = +1

Rotations and orientation-preserving rigid motions.

O(3) outside SO(3)

CTC = I, det C = -1

Reflections or reflection-like motions that reverse orientation.

Pause here to classify the linear part before returning to geometry. Orthogonal matrices split into two components according to determinant sign, and the translation vector then places the motion in space. This separation keeps the algebra small while retaining the geometric consequences.

Curvature and torsion describe local shape up to rigid motion.

Lecture principle

A coordinate formula is useful only after we can name the quantity it preserves or the sign convention it changes.

This slide connects the chapter back to the broader course. Euclidean geometry studies the properties that remain unchanged under rigid motions: length, angle, orthogonality, curvature of curves, and torsion up to the appropriate orientation convention. Coordinates are useful only to the extent that they expose those invariants cleanly.

Curves Under a Rigid Motion

γ̃(t) = a + Cγ(t)
γ̃'(t) = Cγ'(t)
|γ̃'(t)| = |γ'(t)|

Translation drops out after differentiation. Orthogonality keeps the velocity norm and angle data intact.

A curve moved by a rigid motion has velocity, speed, and curvature transformed in predictable ways. Translation disappears under differentiation, and the orthogonal matrix preserves vector norms. The result is that the shape data of a curve is unchanged, even when the curve is relocated and rotated in space.

The helix is a compact test case because both curvature and torsion are visible but not trivial. The deck uses this as the curve-level analogue of determinant sign.

Congruence Strategy for Curves

1

Match speed scale

Compare curves using compatible parameters, preferably arc length.

2

Match invariants

Check curvature and torsion functions on the interval.

3

Align one frame

Choose a rigid motion carrying the initial Frenet frame to the other.

4

Propagate

The same differential equations force the frames and curves to agree.

A congruence theorem becomes a reproducible certificate when each step has a witness.
Present congruence as an evidence chain. If two curves have matching curvature and torsion data on corresponding parameter intervals, one tries to build the rigid motion that aligns an initial frame and then propagates the Frenet equations. The theorem is not being quoted here; it is being turned into an operational proof plan.
