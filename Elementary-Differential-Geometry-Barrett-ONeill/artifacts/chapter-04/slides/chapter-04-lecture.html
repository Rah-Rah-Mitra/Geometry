Regular Patches Are Local Instruments

Definition Target

A patch x(u,v) is useful when its partial velocity vectors are independent. Then the coordinate grid gives a real tangent plane instead of collapsing to a curve or point.

Rank Test

x_u × x_v != 0

Independence is the local permission to do surface calculus.

Stress that a patch is not just a way to draw a surface; it is an instrument that reports coordinates and tangent directions. The cross product test is a compact way to detect whether the instrument is working at a point. If the two partial velocities become dependent, the apparent coordinate grid loses a dimension, and the calculus built on it no longer describes a surface there.

The Tangent Plane Is The Local Workspace

Tangent Vectors

v = a x_u + b x_v at p = x(u,v)

Normal Direction

N = (x_u × x_v) / |x_u × x_v|

Misread To Avoid

The ambient arrow is only meaningful after its base point is fixed. Tangent vectors on a surface carry both a point and a direction.

Describe the tangent plane as the place where surface calculus happens. The ambient three-dimensional picture is helpful, but the vectors that matter are based at a point and spanned by the two coordinate velocities. The normal vector is useful because it separates tangential motion from extrinsic bending, but the chapter repeatedly returns to measurements made inside the tangent plane.

Directional Derivatives Depend On Tangent Data

Function On A Surface

For a scalar function f:S -> R, a tangent vector differentiates by first reading f along a curve whose velocity is the vector.

v[f] = d/dt f(α(t)) at t = 0

α(0) = p and α'(0) = v.

The curve definition prevents a common mistake: a derivative on a surface is not a free-floating arrow in space. A tangent vector acts on functions by asking how the function changes along any surface curve with that initial velocity. The formula is independent of the particular curve once the base point and velocity are fixed, which is why tangent vectors can be treated as derivations.

Vector Fields Are Smooth Choices Of Tangent Vectors

Local Expression

X = A(u,v) x_u + B(u,v) x_v

Smoothness means the coefficient functions are smooth in every valid patch, with compatible changes on overlaps.

Coordinate Trap

The components A,B change when the patch changes; the geometric field is the patch-independent object.

Vector fields are best introduced as an organized family of tangent vectors, one at each point under discussion. In a patch, the field has two coefficient functions because the patch provides a moving basis. On overlaps those coefficients transform, so students should not mistake the coordinate pair for the field itself. The visual reinforces that the base point changes with every arrow.

The Metric Records Tangent Measurements

Metric Coefficients

E = x_u dot x_u,   F = x_u dot x_v,   G = x_v dot x_v

Length Of A Tangent Vector

|a x_u + b x_v|^2 = E a^2 + 2F ab + G b^2

Meaning

Intrinsic lengths and angles can be computed from patch data even when the surface is curved in space.

Frame the first fundamental form as the measurement ledger attached to a patch. The three coefficients are dot products of the moving basis vectors, so they remember how the coordinate grid is stretched and sheared. This is a key bridge in the chapter: it lets students compute lengths, angles, and areas from local data while understanding that the numbers describe tangent geometry, not just a drawing.

Differential Forms Measure Oriented Inputs

One-Form

ω = P du + Q dv

A one-form eats one tangent vector and returns a signed scalar measurement.

Two-Form

η = H du ∧ dv

A two-form eats an ordered tangent parallelogram and returns signed area density.

Introduce forms as measurement rules rather than as mysterious notation. A one-form measures a tangent direction, so it is naturally integrated along a curve. A two-form measures an ordered pair of tangent directions, so it is naturally integrated over a surface region. The orientation language matters because reversing the order of the inputs reverses the sign of the measurement.

Surface Maps Move Calculus By Pushforward And Pullback

Differential

dF_p : T_pS -> T_{F(p)}S'

Pullback Of Forms

(F*ω)_p(v) = ω_{F(p)}(dF_p v)

Push tangent vectors forward; pull measurement rules back so they can be evaluated before moving.

Maps between surfaces are where the calculus becomes functorial. The differential sends tangent vectors from the source surface to the target surface, while pullback sends forms in the opposite direction. The opposite directions are not a trick: a measurement on the target can be evaluated on a source vector only after the vector has been pushed forward by the differential.

Surface Integrals Need Density And Orientation

Patch Area Element

dA = |x_u × x_v| du dv

Scalar Integral

integral_R f(x(u,v)) |x_u × x_v| du dv

Changing coordinates is allowed only when the Jacobian factor and orientation convention are accounted for.

This slide turns the cross product from a regularity test into an area density. The parameter rectangle does not usually preserve area when it is bent and stretched onto the surface, so the factor measuring the parallelogram spanned by the partial velocities must be included. Orientation enters when the integrand is a two-form rather than a scalar density, because reversing the patch order changes the sign.

Boundary Integrals Test The Interior

Stokes Pattern

integral_boundaryR ω = integral_R dω

Inspection Target

The boundary circulation should match the accumulated infinitesimal rotation inside the region, with signs controlled by orientation.

If the boundary direction and normal convention are mismatched, the formula usually fails by a sign before it fails by substance.

Present the boundary theorem as a unifying pattern rather than as a memorized equation. The boundary integral measures a one-dimensional accumulation around the edge, while the exterior derivative measures the corresponding two-dimensional density inside. Orientation is the bookkeeping that makes the two sides agree. Many computational mistakes in this chapter are sign mistakes, so the normal and induced boundary direction should be named explicitly.

Overlap Checks Keep Local Calculus Honest

On An Overlap

Two patches may describe the same surface points. A correct calculation must transform through the transition map without changing the geometric measurement.

x(u,v) = y(r(u,v), s(u,v))

[x_u x_v] = [y_r y_s] J

This slide is the checkpoint that prevents the chapter from becoming patch-dependent algebra. Students should see two grids on the same surface and ask what has changed and what has not. Components, Jacobians, and coordinate expressions change; tangent vectors, form evaluations, lengths, and integrals are the geometric targets that must be preserved under the correct transformation rules.

Orientability Is A Global Consistency Question

Local Fact

Every regular patch has a local normal from x_u × x_v.

Global Question

Can these local normals be chosen continuously and compatibly over the whole surface?

Orientability is invisible in one small patch; it appears when the patch data must close up around global paths.

Orientability is a good example of why the chapter cannot stop at local patches. Every regular patch provides a normal direction locally, but a surface may still refuse a continuous global choice. The lecturer should connect this directly to integration of two-forms: global signed area requires a coherent orientation, while local formulas alone cannot guarantee that coherence.

Topology Records What Local Calculus Cannot Erase

Global Data
  • Closed curves can carry period information for one-forms.
  • Holes and handles affect whether local measurements combine globally.
  • Patch atlases must cover the surface and agree on overlaps.

Inspection prompt: find a loop, choose a form, and ask whether the measured total is forced to vanish by local exactness.

Keep the topology discussion tied to calculus rather than treating it as a separate epilogue. Locally, a small patch can look like a disk and every formula may behave exactly as expected. Globally, a loop around a handle can measure information that no single coordinate chart sees. This is the reason periods, orientation, and atlas compatibility belong in the same chapter as derivatives and integrals.

A Local Calculation Ledger

Patchx(u,v)
Basisx_u, x_v
NormalN = (x_u × x_v)/|x_u × x_v|
MetricE, F, G
Area|x_u × x_v| du dv
Use The Ledger To Audit Work
  • Does the rank test hold on the region being used?
  • Are vectors and forms based at the correct point?
  • Did the coordinate change include the Jacobian?
  • Is the orientation convention visible in the sign?

A polished computation states the geometric object, the patch used to compute it, and the invariant that should survive if the patch is changed.

This slide is meant to slow students down before examples or problem work. Most errors in surface calculus happen because one line of the ledger is skipped: the patch is not regular, the tangent basis is not tracked, the area factor is omitted, or the sign convention is implicit. Encourage students to annotate calculations with the geometric object being computed and with the check that would reveal a coordinate mistake.

Exit Problems For The Chapter

Patch Check

Given a proposed parametrization, find the region where x_u × x_v is nonzero and explain what fails outside it.

Form Check

Compute a one-form on two tangent vectors written in different patch coordinates and verify the same scalar result.

Integral Check

Choose a boundary orientation and predict whether reversing the normal changes the sign of the surface integral.

Close with problems that ask for explanations, not just computations. The patch check tests whether students understand regularity as an assumption that can fail on a subset. The form check tests coordinate independence in a small setting. The integral check forces the orientation convention into the open, which is exactly where it should be before students proceed to later curvature and global geometry.

What Chapter 04 Adds To The Toolkit

  • Regular patches supply local coordinates and tangent bases.
  • Tangent vectors differentiate functions through surface curves.
  • The metric converts patch data into lengths, angles, and area density.
  • Differential forms express oriented measurements and integrate naturally.
  • Maps, overlaps, and topology test whether local formulas assemble globally.
End by naming the durable habits students should carry forward. When a surface problem appears, they should first choose a reliable patch or explain why several patches are needed. Then they should separate tangent data, metric data, form data, and orientation data before integrating or changing coordinates. The next chapters can build curvature and deeper geometry on this foundation because the measurement language is now stable.
