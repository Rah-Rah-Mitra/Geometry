Intrinsic Measurement on Surfaces

Lecture Route

From Tangent Measurements to Global Invariants

1. Metric Surface

Attach an inner product gₚ to each tangent plane. Lengths, angles, and areas become intrinsic data.

2. Connection

Differentiate moving tangent fields, then keep the tangent component. This is the intrinsic derivative.

3. Curvature

Measure how the connection rotates frames around small loops. The result is Gaussian curvature.

4. Topology

Integrate curvature and boundary turning. Gauss-Bonnet turns local bending into Euler characteristic.

metric → connection → geodesics and curvature → Gauss-Bonnet
Inspection Rule

The recurring question is whether an object is geometric or merely a coordinate record.
Definition

Geometric Surfaces Carry Tangent Inner Products

Geometric Surface

A smooth surface M becomes Riemannian when each tangent space TₚM has a smoothly varying inner product gₚ(u, v).

ds² = E du² + 2F du dv + G dv²
  • E, F, G are coordinate coefficients, not the geometry itself.
  • Lengths of curves come from integrating the speed measured by g.
  • Angles and areas are recovered inside tangent planes.
Metric coefficientsCoordinate data versus invariant measurement
Introduce the definition with the warning that coefficients are how the metric is written, not what the metric is. The visual separates the parameter domain from the surface and then places the metric ellipse directly in a tangent plane. Ask students which items would change under a different chart and which measurement statement would remain true.
Computation

The First Fundamental Form Is a Measuring Device

Coordinate Recipe

For a patch x(u, v), tangent vectors are xᵤ and xᵥ. The metric coefficients record the ambient dot products:

E = xᵤ · xᵤ,   F = xᵤ · xᵥ,   G = xᵥ · xᵥ

The same formula gives curve speed: if α(t) = x(u(t), v(t)), then

|α′|² = E u′² + 2F u′v′ + G v′²
First fundamental formLengths, angles, and areas from one matrix
This slide turns the first fundamental form into an operational object. The matrix is useful because it converts coordinate velocities into intrinsic speeds, and the ellipse gives students a picture of positive definiteness. Stress that a curve can wiggle in parameter space while its length is computed by the metric, not by the drawing of the parameter grid.
Moving Instruments

Orthonormal Frames Make the Metric Look Standard

Frame View

An orthonormal frame (e₁, e₂) replaces arbitrary coordinates with tangent unit vectors. At each point, g(eᵢ, eⱼ) = δᵢⱼ.

Connection Form

As the frame moves, its tangent rotation is encoded by a 1-form ω₁₂. It answers: how much does e₁ turn toward e₂ along this direction?

Frames trade coordinate coefficients for rotation bookkeeping. This is often the cleanest path to curvature in dimension two.

Orthonormal framesMetric simplicity, connection complexity
The frame is an instrument rather than an extra geometric object. At one point the metric looks like the Euclidean dot product, but the price is that the frame changes from point to point. This is where connection forms enter: they measure the rotation needed to compare nearby instruments.
Intrinsic Differentiation

Covariant Derivative Keeps the Tangent Part

Definition

For a tangent vector field Y along a surface curve with velocity X, the covariant derivative ∇ₓY is the tangent component of the ordinary derivative of Y.

ordinary change = tangent change + normal change
  • The tangent part belongs to the surface geometry.
  • The normal part depends on how the surface sits in space.
  • Parallel fields satisfy ∇ₓY = 0.
Covariant derivativeTangent projection is the intrinsic operation
Make the projection idea very explicit. In Euclidean space we can differentiate a vector field normally, but the result may point partly away from the tangent plane. The covariant derivative is the surface's answer to differentiation because it discards the normal component and keeps the change that a surface-bound observer can detect.
Transport

Parallel Transport Preserves Length but Can Rotate Direction

Parallel Field Along a Curve

A vector field Y is parallel along γ when ∇γ′Y = 0. Its length is preserved because the connection is compatible with the metric.

d(g(Y, Y))/dt = 2g(∇γ′Y, Y)

On a curved surface, parallel transport around a loop can return a rotated vector. That rotation is not a bookkeeping error; it is curvature being measured by transport.

Parallel transportPreserved length, path-dependent direction
Parallel transport is the cleanest way to let students feel curvature without a formula first. The vector never changes according to the surface connection, yet a loop can bring it back rotated. This apparent paradox is productive: length preservation and direction preservation around closed paths are different claims on a curved surface.
Straightness

Geodesics Have Zero Intrinsic Acceleration

Geodesic Equation

A surface curve γ is a geodesic when its velocity is parallel along itself:

∇γ′γ′ = 0

Extrinsically, a geodesic may bend in space. Intrinsically, its acceleration has no tangent component, so a surface-bound observer feels no sideways steering.

Common Pitfall

Shortest curves are geodesics locally under suitable hypotheses. Not every long geodesic segment remains globally minimizing.

GeodesicsIntrinsic straightness is not visual straightness
Use the contrast between the two curves to separate visual intuition from the definition. A geodesic on a drawn surface can look curved because the drawing is extrinsic, while the intrinsic acceleration condition is about tangent projection. The warning prevents the common overstatement that geodesics are always shortest over arbitrary distances.
Curvature

Curvature Is Frame Rotation Per Unit Area

Structure Equation

For an oriented orthonormal coframe (θ₁, θ₂), the curvature 2-form has the form

Ω₁₂ = K θ₁ ∧ θ₂

In dimension two, the single scalar K is Gaussian curvature. It is intrinsic: different embeddings with the same metric share the same K.

Gaussian curvatureLocal holonomy density
This slide connects the transport picture to the curvature formula. The small loop is not meant to prove the structure equation, but it gives the right mental model: curvature is the density of rotational mismatch produced by parallel transport. Point out that in two dimensions the curvature form collapses to one scalar times the area form.
Models

Three Constant-Curvature Behaviors

Positive

Geodesics tend to reconverge. Spherical triangles have angle excess.

Zero

Parallel lines remain parallel. Euclidean triangle angles sum to π.

Negative

Geodesics spread apart. Triangle angles show a deficit from π.

Curvature signsConvergence, neutrality, divergence
The three panels should be read as behavior diagrams, not as literal exhaustive models. Positive curvature encourages geodesics to meet, zero curvature preserves the Euclidean comparison, and negative curvature pushes initially nearby geodesics apart. This prepares the audience for Gauss-Bonnet, where the sign of curvature influences angle accounting.
Symmetry

Clairaut Coordinates Reveal a Conserved Quantity

Clairaut Relation

On a surface of revolution, a unit-speed geodesic satisfies a conserved product involving radius and angle with the parallel direction.

r cos φ = constant

When the geodesic moves toward a narrower parallel, the angle must adjust. The formula is a diagnostic for whether a plotted curve is behaving like a geodesic.

Clairaut parametrizationsSymmetry becomes a conserved quantity
Present Clairaut's relation as a conservation law rather than as an isolated trick. Rotational symmetry gives a direction that the metric does not distinguish, and that symmetry produces a constant along geodesics. Have students inspect the surface of revolution: as the radius changes, the angle term must compensate if the curve remains geodesic.
Curvature Accounting

Gauss-Bonnet Balances Curvature and Turning

Local Form

For a compact region with piecewise smooth boundary, the total Gaussian curvature, boundary geodesic curvature, and corner angles satisfy a topological balance.

∫∫ K dA + ∫ k_g ds + Σ exterior angles = 2πχ

On a geodesic polygon the boundary geodesic-curvature term disappears along each side, leaving curvature and corner turning to determine the angle relation.

Gauss-BonnetLocal formula for regions
This is the bridge from curvature as a local density to curvature as a global accounting term. The formula has three visible ingredients: interior curvature, boundary turning, and corner turning. Point out that no single term is automatically topological, but the sum is forced to match the Euler characteristic of the region.
Global Theorem

Total Curvature Knows the Euler Characteristic

Closed Surface Form

For a compact oriented surface without boundary, Gauss-Bonnet reduces to one striking identity.

∫∫M K dA = 2πχ(M)

The left side is analytic and metric-dependent pointwise. The right side is topological. The theorem says the integral forgets many metric details.

Global Gauss-BonnetCurvature integral equals topology
The closed-surface formula deserves a pause because it is one of the chapter's conceptual peaks. The integrand can vary dramatically across a chosen metric, yet the total is locked to the Euler characteristic. Use the three icons to emphasize that adding handles changes the allowable total curvature by a fixed topological amount.
Applications

Vector-Field Index Counts Forced Singularities

Poincare-Hopf Flavor

A vector field with isolated zeros has local indices. On compact surfaces, the sum of these indices is controlled by the Euler characteristic.

The theorem is a companion to Gauss-Bonnet: local rotation data, accumulated correctly, becomes a global topological number.

Interpretation

An index is not the size of a vector. It is how the direction field winds when one loops around a singularity.

Vector fieldsLocal winding, global obstruction
Connect vector-field index to the same accounting habit as Gauss-Bonnet. Around a zero, the field directions rotate, and the winding number records that local behavior. The striking point is global: on some surfaces zeros cannot be eliminated because the total index must match the Euler characteristic.
Recap

What Chapter 07 Adds to the Geometry Toolkit

  • A Riemannian metric turns tangent spaces into measurable planes.
  • The covariant derivative is ordinary change projected back to the surface.
  • Geodesics are curves with zero intrinsic acceleration.
  • Curvature measures how parallel transport fails to close up around loops.
  • Gauss-Bonnet converts curvature and turning into Euler characteristic.
  • Vector-field index is another local-to-global counting principle.
