Location and Spread on Metric Spaces

Averaging by minimizing distance, not adding coordinates

Setting

A metric space $(M,\rho)$ and a random point $X$. No vector structure assumed.

Goal

Define a location (mean) and a spread (variance) using only the distance $\rho$.

Why

Curved shape spaces have no meaningful coordinate average.

Printed pp. 21-35Location and Spread on Metric Spaces
This chapter, printed pages 21 through 35, replaces the Euclidean mean with a notion that lives on any metric space. We minimize expected squared distance instead of adding coordinates, so the same definition will later serve curved shape spaces where vector averages make no sense at all.

The Frechet Function

Definition

For each $p\in M$, average the squared distance to $X$.

Reading

A scalar landscape over the whole space, built only from $\rho$.

Euclidean check

On $\mathbb{R}^d$ it recovers the usual mean and variance.

Frechet function

$F(p) = \mathbb{E}\big[\rho(p, X)^2\big]$

Printed pp. 21-35The Frechet Function
The Frechet function measures, for each candidate location, the average squared distance to the random point. It is the metric-space stand-in for the variance functional, defined entirely through the distance rho without referring to charts, tangent vectors, or any linear structure on the underlying space.

Frechet Mean and Total Variance

Mean set

The set of minimizers of $F$, not necessarily a single point.

Total variance

The minimum value $V = \min_p F(p)$ measures spread.

Vocabulary

“Mean” may denote a set; “variance” is one number.

Mean set

$M_F = \arg\min_{p\in M} F(p)$

Total Frechet variance

$V = \min_{p\in M} \mathbb{E}\big[\rho(p,X)^2\big]$

Printed pp. 21-35Frechet Mean and Total Variance
The Frechet mean is the set of points where the Frechet function attains its minimum, and the minimum value is the total Frechet variance. Because the minimizer may not be unique, we speak of a mean set rather than a single mean point throughout this chapter.

Empirical Frechet Mean

Plug-in

Replace $\mathbb{E}$ by the average over the sample $X_1,\dots,X_n$.

Estimator

The minimizers of $F_n$ form the sample Frechet mean set.

Computation

Minimize one scalar built from squared distances.

Empirical Frechet function

$F_n(p) = \dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \rho(p, X_i)^2$

Sample mean set

$\widehat{M}_n = \arg\min_{p\in M} F_n(p)$

Printed pp. 21-35Empirical Frechet Mean
Replacing the population expectation with an average over observed sample points gives the empirical Frechet function. Its minimizers form the sample Frechet mean set, the estimator we actually compute. Everything reduces to minimizing one scalar objective built from pairwise squared distances among data and candidates.

Existence on Metric Spaces

Hypotheses

$(M,\rho)$ complete; $F(p)<\infty$ for some $p$; bounded support helps.

Mechanism

$F$ is lower semicontinuous and proper, so its infimum is attained.

Conclusion

The mean set is nonempty and compact.

Existence (theorem)

$F$ l.s.c. + proper $\Rightarrow M_F \neq \varnothing$, compact

Caveat

Existence is easy; uniqueness is where geometry enters.

Printed pp. 21-35Existence on Metric Spaces
On a complete and suitably bounded metric space the Frechet function is lower semicontinuous and proper, so its infimum is attained and the mean set is nonempty and compact. Existence is therefore cheap; it is uniqueness that demands the geometric work in the next slides.

When the Mean Is a Set

Symmetry

Symmetry against curvature can tie many minimizers.

Circle example

Uniform law on the circle: every point minimizes $F$ equally.

Lesson

A single “mean point” can be meaningless; report the set.

Non-uniqueness

$|M_F| > 1$ when $F$ is flat at its minimum

Printed pp. 21-35When the Mean Is a Set
Symmetry of the distribution against the curvature of the space can leave several equally good minimizers. The uniform law on a circle is the canonical warning: every point ties for minimum Frechet value, so the mean set is the whole circle rather than any single representative location.

Karcher Means: Local Minimizers

Relaxation

A Karcher mean is any local minimizer of $F$.

Practicality

Reachable by gradient descent; often locally unique.

Warning

A Karcher mean need not be a (global) Frechet mean.

Inclusion

$M_F \subseteq \{\text{Karcher means}\}$

Printed pp. 21-35Karcher Means: Local Minimizers
Karcher means relax the global minimum to any local minimizer of the Frechet function. They are easier to find by gradient descent and often unique nearby, but a Karcher mean need not be a Frechet mean, so the distinction matters when reporting a location estimate.

Strong Consistency

Ziezold

Sample mean set converges into the population mean set a.s.

Bhattacharya-Patrangenaru

Refined consistency, including set-valued limits.

Set convergence

Measured by how far sample minimizers can stray.

Strong consistency

$\limsup_{n}\widehat{M}_n \subseteq M_F \quad \text{a.s.}$

If unique

When $M_F=\{\mu\}$, $\widehat{\mu}_n \to \mu$ almost surely.

Printed pp. 21-35Strong Consistency
The Ziezold and Bhattacharya-Patrangenaru theorems guarantee that the sample Frechet mean set shrinks into the population mean set almost surely as the sample size grows. Consistency holds even when the limit is a set, with set-valued convergence measured by how far sample minimizers can stray.

A Central Limit Theorem

Hypotheses

$M_F=\{\mu\}$ interior and unique; $F$ smooth near $\mu$.

Statement

The recentered sample mean is asymptotically Gaussian.

Sandwich

Hessian of $F$ and gradient covariance combine.

Central limit theorem

$\sqrt{n}\,(\widehat{\mu}_n - \mu) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\,H^{-1}\Sigma\,H^{-1}\big)$

Ingredients

$H = \nabla^2 F(\mu)$; $\Sigma = \mathrm{Cov}\,\nabla_p\rho(\mu,X)^2$.

Printed pp. 21-35A Central Limit Theorem
When the population Frechet mean is a unique interior point and the function is smooth there, the recentered sample mean is asymptotically normal. The Hessian of the Frechet function and a distance-gradient covariance combine in a sandwich form that controls the limiting Gaussian fluctuations.

Sample Mean Convergence

Setup

Draw from a law concentrated away from symmetry.

Behavior

The estimate $\widehat{\mu}_n$ tightens toward $\mu$ as $n$ grows.

Meaning

Consistency made concrete: spread of estimates shrinks like $1/\sqrt{n}$.

Rate

$\|\widehat{\mu}_n - \mu\| = O_p(n^{-1/2})$

Printed pp. 21-35Sample Mean Convergence
As we draw more points from a distribution concentrated away from symmetry, the empirical Frechet mean settles toward the single population minimizer. The visual shows the estimate tightening with growing n, illustrating the consistency statement that the earlier slide proved abstractly for general spaces.

Computing and Checking

Minimize

Numerically minimize $F_n$ over a grid or by descent.

Bootstrap

Resample to put bands on the estimated location.

Diagnostic

Wide circular bands flag near-symmetry and unreliable means.

Confidence set

$C_\alpha = \{p : F_n(p) \le \widehat{q}_{1-\alpha}\}$

Printed pp. 21-35Computing and Checking
In practice we minimize the empirical Frechet function numerically and audit it with bootstrap resampling. Bootstrap bands on the circle reveal whether the estimated mean is stable or whether near-symmetry has spread the location across a wide arc, flagging an unreliable single-point summary.
