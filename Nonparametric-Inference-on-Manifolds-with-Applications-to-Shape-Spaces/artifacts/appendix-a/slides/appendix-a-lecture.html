Topological Manifolds

Locally Euclidean

Every point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open set of $\mathbb{R}^m$.

Mild global rules

Hausdorff and second countable keep analysis well behaved.

Defining property

$\forall p \in M,\ \exists\, U \ni p,\ \ U \cong \mathbb{R}^m$

Why we care

Second countability gives partitions of unity for global gluing.

Printed pp. 209-213Topological Manifolds
A topological manifold looks locally like Euclidean space of fixed dimension, while staying Hausdorff and second countable so analysis behaves. These mild global conditions rule out pathologies and guarantee partitions of unity, which we need to glue local statistics into a single global estimator on the manifold later in the text.

Charts and the Atlas

Chart

A homeomorphism $\varphi:U \to \varphi(U)\subset\mathbb{R}^m$ giving local coordinates.

Atlas

A family of charts whose domains cover all of $M$.

Coordinate chart

$\varphi(p) = \big(x^1(p),\dots,x^m(p)\big)$

Working tool

Charts are the coordinates in which means and densities are computed.

Printed pp. 209-213Charts and the Atlas
A chart is a homeomorphism from an open set of the manifold to an open set of Euclidean space, giving local coordinates. An atlas is a collection of charts covering the manifold. Charts are the working coordinates in which we actually compute means, projections, and densities throughout the inference chapters.

Transition Maps and Smooth Structure

Smooth compatibility

$\psi\circ\varphi^{-1} \in C^{\infty}\big(\varphi(U\cap V)\big)$

Smooth structure

All transition maps $C^{\infty}$ defines the differentiable structure.

Payoff

Coordinate-free quantities become genuinely well defined.

Printed pp. 209-213Transition Maps and Smooth Structure
Where two charts overlap, the composition of one inverse with the other is a transition map between Euclidean open sets. Requiring every transition map to be infinitely differentiable defines the smooth, or C-infinity, structure. This compatibility is exactly what lets us call a coordinate-free quantity well defined rather than chart dependent.

Smooth Maps and Diffeomorphisms

Smooth map

$f:M\to N$ is smooth if its chart representatives are $C^{\infty}$.

Diffeomorphism

A smooth bijection with smooth inverse: the right equivalence.

Local reading

$\chi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1} \in C^{\infty}$

Invariance

Intrinsic estimators are diffeomorphism invariant, not coordinate bound.

Printed pp. 209-213Smooth Maps and Diffeomorphisms
A map between manifolds is smooth when it is smooth read through any compatible charts. A diffeomorphism is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse, the right notion of equivalence for smooth manifolds. Diffeomorphism invariance is why estimators built intrinsically do not depend on an arbitrary choice of coordinates.

Immersions and Embeddings

Immersion

Injective differential at every point: $\mathrm{rank}\, df_p = \dim M$.

Embedding

An immersion that is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Whitney embedding

$M^m \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{2m}$ smoothly

Why it matters

Every manifold sits in $\mathbb{R}^N$, justifying extrinsic shape analysis.

Printed pp. 209-213Immersions and Embeddings
An immersion has an injective differential at every point; an embedding is an injective immersion that is also a homeomorphism onto its image. The Whitney embedding theorem says every smooth manifold embeds in some Euclidean space, which justifies the extrinsic, ambient-space analysis the book often uses for shapes.

The Tangent Space

Via curves

$T_pM = \{\gamma'(0) : \gamma(0)=p\}$

Via derivations

$v(fg) = v(f)\,g(p) + f(p)\,v(g)$

Dimension

$\dim T_pM = \dim M$; the linear stage for tangent statistics.

Printed pp. 209-213The Tangent Space
The tangent space at a point collects velocity vectors of smooth curves through that point, equivalently the derivations of germs of smooth functions. It is a vector space of the same dimension as the manifold and is the linear stage where tangent-space statistics, covariances, and confidence regions are computed in the main text.

The Differential and Pushforward

Pushforward

A linear map $df_p:T_pM\to T_{f(p)}N$ on tangent vectors.

On velocities

It sends $\gamma'(0)$ to the velocity of the image curve $f\circ\gamma$.

Differential

$df_p(\gamma'(0)) = (f\circ\gamma)'(0)$

Statistical use

Pushforwards transport tangent covariance: the manifold delta method.

Printed pp. 209-213The Differential and Pushforward
A smooth map induces a linear differential, the pushforward, sending tangent vectors to tangent vectors by acting on curve velocities. It is the coordinate-free derivative whose rank classifies immersions and submersions. Pushforwards transport tangent-space uncertainty along maps, the precise mechanism behind the delta method on manifolds.

Submanifolds and Regular Values

Regular value

$c$ is regular if $df_p$ is surjective at every $p\in f^{-1}(c)$.

Regular value theorem

Then $f^{-1}(c)$ is a smooth submanifold.

Level set

$\dim f^{-1}(c) = \dim M - \dim N$

Examples

The sphere $S^{n}$ and matrix groups arise this way.

Printed pp. 209-213Submanifolds and Regular Values
A regular value of a smooth map is one whose every preimage point has surjective differential. The regular value theorem then makes the preimage a smooth submanifold of predictable dimension. This is the standard machine for certifying that constraint sets, like spheres and matrix groups, are genuine smooth manifolds we can do statistics on.

Orientation

Consistent bases

A smoothly varying choice of ordered basis across charts.

Jacobian test

Orientable iff all transition Jacobians have positive determinant.

Compatibility

$\det D(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}) > 0$

Why we need it

Integrating top-forms defines signed volume and density measures.

Printed pp. 209-213Orientation
An orientation is a consistent, smoothly varying choice of basis ordering across charts, possible only when transition Jacobians keep positive determinant. Orientability lets us integrate top-degree forms and define signed volume, which underlies volume measures and densities used when defining intrinsic means and nonparametric estimators on the manifold.

Quotient Manifolds and Group Actions

Free

No nonidentity element fixes a point; orbits do not self-intersect.

Proper

The action map is proper, keeping orbits cleanly separated.

Smooth quotient

Then $M/G$ is a smooth manifold with the projection a submersion.

Quotient theorem

$G \curvearrowright M \ \text{free, proper} \Rightarrow M/G \in C^{\infty}$

Shape spaces

Quotient configurations by similarity to get the shape manifold.

Printed pp. 209-213Quotient Manifolds and Group Actions
When a Lie group acts smoothly, freely, and properly on a manifold, the orbit space inherits a unique smooth quotient structure. Free and proper together prevent collapsing and tangled orbits. This is the exact mechanism producing shape spaces: quotient configurations by similarity or rotation to obtain the smooth manifold on which we infer.
