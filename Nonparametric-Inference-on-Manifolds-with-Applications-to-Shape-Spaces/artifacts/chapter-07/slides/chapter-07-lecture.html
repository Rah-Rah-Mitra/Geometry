Removing Translation

Center it

Subtract the centroid from every landmark.

Helmert subspace

Multiply by the Helmert submatrix $H$.

Cost

Removes exactly $m$ degrees of freedom.

Centered span

$XH \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times(k-1)},\quad XH\mathbf{1}=0$

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Removing Translation
Translation is removed by centering each configuration at its centroid, equivalently multiplying by the Helmert submatrix. The result lives in an m by k minus one dimensional subspace where the column sums vanish. This is the first quotient and it discards exactly m degrees of freedom.

Removing Scale: The Pre-shape Sphere

Centroid size

The Frobenius norm of the centered shape.

Normalize

Divide by size to fix unit scale.

Pre-shape

Centered, unit-size configurations.

Pre-shape

$z = \dfrac{XH}{\lVert XH\rVert_F},\qquad \lVert z\rVert_F = 1$

Pre-shape sphere

$z\in S^{\,m(k-1)-1}$

Not yet a shape

Rotation $SO(m)$ still acts freely on $z$.

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Pre-shape Sphere
After centering we divide by the Frobenius norm, the centroid size, fixing unit size. Centered unit-size configurations form the pre-shape sphere, a sphere of dimension m times k minus one, minus one. Pre-shapes still carry an arbitrary rotation, so they are not yet shapes.

Rotation Orbits on the Sphere

Group action

$SO(m)$ rotates each pre-shape.

Orbit

The curve $\{Rz : R\in SO(m)\}$.

Same shape

One orbit = one physical shape.

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Rotation Orbits
The rotation group SO(m) acts on the pre-shape sphere, sweeping each pre-shape around an orbit. All pre-shapes in one orbit represent the same physical shape. The shape is the orbit itself, so the shape space is the set of these rotation orbits on the sphere.

The Quotient Shape Space

Definition

Kendall's shape space is the orbit space.

Projection

$\pi$ sends $z$ to its orbit $[z]$.

Quotient point

One point per similarity shape.

Shape space

$\Sigma_k^m = S^{\,m(k-1)-1} \big/ SO(m)$

Projection map

$\pi : z \;\longmapsto\; [z] = \{Rz : R\in SO(m)\}$

Reading

A shape is a rotation orbit.

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Quotient Shape Space
Kendall's shape space is the pre-shape sphere quotiented by SO(m). Its dimension is the sphere dimension minus the dimension of the rotation group. We collapse each orbit to a single point, and that quotient point is the similarity shape of the landmark configuration.

Counting the Dimension

Start

$mk$ raw coordinates.

Subtract

$-m$ translation, $-1$ scale.

Subtract

$-\binom{m}{2}$ for rotations.

Dimension

$\dim\Sigma_k^m = m(k-1)-1-\tbinom{m}{2}$

Planar case $m=2$

$\dim\Sigma_k^2 = 2k-4 = \dim \mathbb{CP}^{k-2}$

Kendall's $\mathbb{CP}^{k-2}$

Planar shapes form a complex projective space.

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Dimension Count
Counting carefully: mk coordinates, minus m for translation, minus one for scale, minus the rotation group dimension m choose two. For planar shapes this gives two k minus two, the complex projective space familiar from Kendall's original construction of planar shapes.

A Riemannian Submersion

Vertical

Tangent to the orbit; shape unchanged.

Horizontal

Orthogonal to the orbit; moves the shape.

Metric

Shape metric read from horizontal vectors.

Split

$T_zS = \mathcal{V}_z \oplus \mathcal{H}_z,\quad \mathcal{H}_z = \mathcal{V}_z^{\perp}$

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Riemannian Submersion
The quotient map is a Riemannian submersion. Tangent vectors split into vertical directions, tangent to the orbit, and horizontal directions, orthogonal to it. Only horizontal motion changes the shape, so the shape metric is read off from horizontal vectors on the pre-shape sphere.

Procrustes Registration

Optimal rotation

Rotate one pre-shape onto another.

SVD solution

From the cross-product $z_1 z_2^\top$.

Residual

Mismatch left after best alignment.

Best rotation

$\hat R = \arg\max_{R\in SO(m)} \operatorname{tr}(R\, z_2 z_1^\top)$

Via SVD

$z_1 z_2^\top = U\Sigma V^\top,\quad \hat R = U V^\top$

Alignment correlation

$\rho = \sum_i \sigma_i$, sum of singular values.

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Procrustes Registration
To compare two pre-shapes we optimally rotate one onto the other, the Procrustes registration solved by the singular value decomposition of their cross-product. The optimal rotation aligns them as closely as possible, and the residual angle after alignment is what the shape distance measures.

Procrustes Geodesic Distance

Two pre-shapes

$z_1, z_2$ on the sphere $S$.

Best-align

Rotate $z_2$ to maximize correlation.

Distance

Arc-cosine of the aligned correlation.

Shape distance

$d_\Sigma([z_1],[z_2]) = \arccos\!\Big(\max_{R} \langle z_1, Rz_2\rangle\Big)$

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Procrustes Geodesic Distance
The shape distance is the geodesic distance on the quotient, equal to the arc-cosine of the maximal alignment correlation. It equals the great-circle angle between orbits, achieved at the Procrustes-optimal rotation. This invariant distance underlies every Frechet mean and test built later in the course.

Singularities and Degeneracy

Generic

Full-rank configurations, free orbits.

Degenerate

Collinear or rank-deficient landmarks.

Stabilizer

Larger stabilizer shrinks the orbit.

Singular stratum

$\operatorname{rank}(XH) < m \;\Rightarrow\; \text{smaller orbit}$

Smooth part

Away from singular strata, $\Sigma_k^m$ is a smooth manifold.

Why it matters

Nonparametric inference behaves well on the smooth part.

Printed pp. 82-86 · PDF 97-101Singularities and Degeneracy
Orbits have constant dimension only where the configuration is non-degenerate. Collinear or otherwise rank-deficient configurations have smaller stabilizers shrinking their orbits, creating singular strata. Away from these singularities the shape space is a smooth manifold, which is where nonparametric inference is well behaved.
