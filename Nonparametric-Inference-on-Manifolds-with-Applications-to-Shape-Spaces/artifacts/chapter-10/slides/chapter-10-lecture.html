Stiefel manifolds $V_{k,m}$

Orthonormal frames as manifold-valued data

The object

Ordered orthonormal $k$-frames in $\mathbb{R}^m$.

Why it matters

A curved sample space for frame, shape and directional statistics.

Printed pp. 130-134Stiefel manifolds
This short chapter, printed pages 130 to 134, introduces the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal frames. We frame it as a curved sample space sitting inside Euclidean matrix space, which is exactly the setup the book exploits for extrinsic statistical inference on frame and shape data.

Definition of $V_{k,m}$

As matrices

$m\times k$ matrices $X$ whose columns are orthonormal.

One equation

All the structure lives in the constraint $X^\top X = I_k$.

Definition

$V_{k,m} = \{\, X \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times k} : X^\top X = I_k \,\}$

Reading it

Each column has unit norm; distinct columns are perpendicular.

Printed pp. 130-134Definition of V_k,m
The Stiefel manifold is the set of m-by-k matrices whose columns form an orthonormal k-frame, captured by the single matrix equation X-transpose-X equals the identity. Each constraint removes degrees of freedom, and counting them gives the manifold dimension we derive next.

Counting the dimension

Free entries

$X$ has $km$ real entries before constraints.

Constraints

$X^\top X = I_k$ is symmetric: $k$ norms $+\;\binom{k}{2}$ orthogonalities.

Dimension

$\dim V_{k,m} = km - \dfrac{k(k+1)}{2}$

Check

The $\tfrac{k(k+1)}{2}$ equals the count of independent entries of a symmetric $k\times k$ matrix.

Printed pp. 130-134Dimension count
We count constraints in the symmetric equation X-transpose-X equals identity. The k diagonal unit-norm conditions plus the k-choose-two off-diagonal orthogonality conditions subtract from the km free entries, yielding dimension km minus k times k plus one over two.

Two special cases

One frame vector

$V_{1,m} = S^{m-1}$, the unit sphere — a single unit vector.

Full frame

$V_{m,m} = O(m)$, the orthogonal group — a complete orthonormal basis.

Endpoints

$\dim S^{m-1} = m-1, \quad \dim O(m) = \tfrac{m(m-1)}{2}$

In between

General $V_{k,m}$ interpolates between a sphere and a group.

Printed pp. 130-134Special cases
Two special cases anchor intuition. A single orthonormal vector is just a point on the unit sphere, so V one m equals S to the m minus one. A full frame of m vectors is an orthogonal matrix, so V m m equals the orthogonal group O of m.

A point of $V_{2,3}$

Dimension

$\dim V_{2,3} = 2\cdot 3 - 3 = 3$.

Frame vs. plane

The frame remembers orientation and ordering; the plane (a Grassmannian point) forgets them.

Printed pp. 130-134Orthonormal 2-frame in R^3
Here we picture a point of V two three: two perpendicular unit vectors in three-space. Their span is a plane, but the frame remembers the actual ordered pair of axes, not just the plane. This distinction is what makes the frame, not the subspace, the data object.

Equivariant Euclidean embedding

Ambient space

$V_{k,m}\subset \mathbb{R}^{m\times k}$ with the Frobenius inner product.

Symmetry

$X\mapsto QX$ for $Q\in O(m)$ preserves $X^\top X = I_k$ and distances.

Frobenius metric

$\langle A,B\rangle = \mathrm{tr}(A^\top B), \quad \|QX\| = \|X\|$

Payoff

Equivariance is exactly what extrinsic inference exploits.

Printed pp. 130-134Equivariant embedding
The Stiefel manifold inherits the Euclidean inner product of matrix space, the Frobenius inner product. Left multiplication by an orthogonal matrix preserves the constraint and the metric, so the embedding is equivariant, which is precisely the property extrinsic analysis needs.

The extrinsic mean

Step 1

Average the frames in the ambient space: $\bar X = \tfrac1n\sum_i X_i$.

The catch

$\bar X$ is generally not orthonormal — it leaves the manifold.

Extrinsic mean

$\mu_E = P(\bar X), \quad P:\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}\to V_{k,m}$

Need

A projection $P$ onto the nearest orthonormal frame.

Printed pp. 130-134Extrinsic mean idea
For extrinsic analysis we average frames in the ambient matrix space, then project the Euclidean average back onto the manifold. The average of orthonormal frames is generally not orthonormal, so a well-defined projection map to the nearest frame is the heart of the construction.

Projection via polar / SVD

Polar projection

$\bar X = U\Sigma V^\top \;\Longrightarrow\; P(\bar X) = UV^\top$

Optimality

$UV^\top$ minimizes $\|\bar X - X\|_F$ over $X\in V_{k,m}$ when $\bar X$ has full rank.

Printed pp. 130-134Polar projection via SVD
The nearest orthonormal frame to a generic matrix is found by polar decomposition. Writing the average as U-Sigma-V-transpose, the projection keeps the rotational factor U-V-transpose and discards the singular values, giving the unique closest point in Frobenius distance when the matrix has full rank.

Diagnostics: residuals on the frame

Orthogonality residual

Measure $\|X^\top X - I_k\|$ to confirm the result lands on $V_{k,m}$.

Concentration

Singular values $\sigma_i$ of $\bar X$ report how tight the frame sample is.

Sanity check

$r = \|P(\bar X)^\top P(\bar X) - I_k\|_F \approx 0$

Why it matters

A nonzero residual means the estimator drifted off the manifold.

Printed pp. 130-134Residual diagnostics
After projecting we check the residual: how far X-transpose-X sits from the identity tells us the orthogonality error, and the singular values of the average measure how concentrated the frame sample is. These diagnostics certify that the estimator stayed on the manifold.

Bridge to shape spaces

Pre-shapes

Centered, scaled configurations live as scaled orthonormal frames.

Reflection shape

The Gram matrix $X^\top X$ gives reflection-invariant coordinates.

Gram construction

$G = X^\top X, \quad \text{shape} = X / O(k)\text{ or }O(m)$

Directional link

$V_{1,m}=S^{m-1}$ ties frames to directional statistics.

Printed pp. 130-134Link to shape spaces
Stiefel manifolds connect to shape statistics through pre-shapes and the reflection-shape Gram construction. Scaled orthonormal frames represent centered configurations, and quotienting by rotation yields shape; the Gram matrix X-transpose-X gives reflection-invariant shape coordinates used later in the book.

Summary

Object

$V_{k,m}=\{X:X^\top X=I_k\}$, dimension $km-\tfrac{k(k+1)}{2}$.

Special cases

$V_{1,m}=S^{m-1}$ and $V_{m,m}=O(m)$.

Extrinsic mean

Average, then polar/SVD project: $\mu_E = UV^\top$.

Forward

Feeds pre-shape, reflection-shape, and directional inference.

Printed pp. 130-134Summary
To summarize: the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal frames has dimension km minus k k plus one over two, specializes to spheres and orthogonal groups, embeds equivariantly in matrix space, and supports an extrinsic mean computed by polar projection. These tools feed directly into shape and directional inference.
