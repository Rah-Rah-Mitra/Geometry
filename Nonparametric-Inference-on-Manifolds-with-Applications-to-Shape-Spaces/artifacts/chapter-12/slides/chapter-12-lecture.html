$\mathbb{RP}^d$: Lines Through the Origin

Definition

A point is a one-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$.

No sign, no scale

$x$ and $\lambda x$ name the same direction for $\lambda\neq 0$.

Axial data

Crystal axes, undirected fibres, geological strikes.

Quotient definition

$\mathbb{RP}^d = \big(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{0\}\big)\big/\!\sim,\quad x\sim\lambda x$

Printed pp. 147-155Lines Through the Origin
Real projective space collects one-dimensional subspaces of Euclidean space, so a datum is a direction without a chosen sign or scale. We stress that any nonzero representative names the same projective point, which is exactly why axial measurements like crystal orientations belong naturally in this quotient.

The Antipodal Quotient $S^d/\{\pm 1\}$

Sphere model

Pick the unit representative, then glue $x$ to $-x$.

Double cover

$S^d \to \mathbb{RP}^d$ is two-to-one and smooth.

Manifold

Compact, $d$-dimensional, locally Euclidean; no global sign.

Quotient map

$\pi:S^d\to\mathbb{RP}^d,\quad \pi(x)=\pi(-x)=[x]$

Printed pp. 147-155The Antipodal Quotient
Identifying each unit vector with its negative collapses the sphere into real projective space, a smooth compact manifold of the same dimension. The double cover viewpoint explains why local charts work yet no global sign convention exists, which the notebook visual makes concrete on the two-sphere.

Distance Between Unsigned Directions

Acute angle

Compare lines by the angle in $[0,\pi/2]$.

Sign blind

$|\langle x,y\rangle|$ ignores which representative we chose.

Chordal surrogate

Built from outer products; motivates the embedding.

Projective distance

$\rho([x],[y]) = \arccos\,|\langle x,y\rangle|,\quad \|x\|=\|y\|=1$

Chordal / embedding form

$d^2 = \tfrac12\,\|xx^{\top}-yy^{\top}\|_F^2 = 1-\langle x,y\rangle^2$

Printed pp. 147-155Projective Distance
Because antipodes coincide, the natural distance compares unsigned directions through the acute angle between representative lines. We show the chordal surrogate built from the outer product, motivating the embedding of the next slide and explaining why naive spherical distance double counts antipodal pairs in this geometry.

The Veronese-Type Embedding $x\mapsto xx^{\top}$

Sign erased

$(-x)(-x)^{\top}=xx^{\top}$, so the map is well defined on $\mathbb{RP}^d$.

Equivariant

Rotations act compatibly: $j(Rx)=R\,j(x)\,R^{\top}$.

Target

Rank-one projections inside symmetric matrices $S(d{+}1)$.

Embedding

$j([x]) = xx^{\top},\quad \operatorname{tr}(xx^{\top})=1,\ (xx^{\top})^2=xx^{\top}$

Printed pp. 147-155The Veronese Embedding
Mapping a unit representative to its outer product sends real projective space equivariantly into symmetric matrices, erasing sign because the outer product of a vector and its negative agree. This embedding is the workhorse that converts intrinsic projective questions into ordinary linear algebra on symmetric matrices.

Extrinsic Mean = Leading Eigenvector

Average then project

Mean the matrices $\bar A=\frac1n\sum x_i x_i^{\top}$.

Spectral projection

Nearest rank-one projection is the top eigenvector.

Uniqueness

Needs a gap $\lambda_1>\lambda_2$ of $\bar A$.

Extrinsic mean

$\mu_E = [u_1],\quad \bar A\,u_1=\lambda_1 u_1,\ \lambda_1=\lambda_{\max}(\bar A)$

Printed pp. 147-155Extrinsic Mean as Leading Eigenvector
The extrinsic mean is found by averaging the embedded outer products and projecting back, which Rayleigh's principle identifies with the top eigenvector of the average matrix. Existence and uniqueness reduce to a simple spectral gap condition between the first and second eigenvalues of that average.

Asymptotics: CLT and Tests

Tangent CLT

Embedded means are asymptotically normal in the tangent space.

Confidence regions

Bootstrap ellipsoids around $\mu_E$.

Two samples

Chi-squared statistic on mean differences.

Central limit theorem

$\sqrt{n}\,(\widehat{\mu}_E-\mu_E) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$

Printed pp. 147-155CLT and Two-Sample Tests
A central limit theorem in the tangent space of the embedded mean yields asymptotic normality, supporting bootstrap confidence regions and chi-squared style two-sample tests. We emphasize that all inference happens after the equivariant embedding, where Euclidean asymptotics legitimately apply to the projected statistics.

Why Vision Needs Projective Shape

Quotient by the full projective group, not just rotations.

Printed pp. 147-155Pinhole Imaging and Projective Transforms
A pinhole camera maps a scene to the image plane up to an unknown projective transformation, so two photographs of one rigid scene differ by a homography. This is why recognition must factor out the whole projective group rather than only the similarity transformations used in Kendall shape.

The Cross-Ratio: a Projective Invariant

Four collinear points

Cross-ratio is unchanged by any perspective map.

Invariant coordinates

Cross-ratios register a configuration intrinsically.

Or use a frame

A projective frame fixes coordinates without sign or scale.

Cross-ratio

$(A,B;C,D)=\dfrac{AC\cdot BD}{BC\cdot AD}$ is projectively invariant

Printed pp. 147-155Projective Invariants: the Cross-Ratio
The cross-ratio of four collinear points is the fundamental projective invariant, unchanged by any perspective map. We compute it explicitly and note that such invariants, or a registered projective frame, are what let us define a shape that survives arbitrary camera viewpoints and lighting changes.

Projective Shape Space

Register a frame

Fix $d{+}2$ points as a projective frame.

Remaining points

Become invariant projective coordinates.

Product manifold

A projective shape is a point in a product of $\mathbb{RP}^d$.

Projective shape

$\sigma(C) \in \big(\mathbb{RP}^{d}\big)^{k-d-2} = \mathbb{RP}^d\times\cdots\times\mathbb{RP}^d$

Printed pp. 147-155Projective Shape Space
Fixing a projective frame on a configuration registers the remaining points into projective coordinates, so a projective shape becomes a point in a product of real projective spaces. This product manifold inherits the extrinsic means, embeddings, and limit theory developed earlier in this chapter.
