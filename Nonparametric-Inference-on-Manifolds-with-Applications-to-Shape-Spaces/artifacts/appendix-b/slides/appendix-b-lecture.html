The Riemannian Metric

Inner product per point

Each tangent space $T_pM$ carries its own inner product $g_p$.

Smoothly varying

$g$ depends smoothly on the base point $p$.

Why it matters

No metric means no distance and no intrinsic mean.

Definition

$g_p(u,v) = \langle u, v\rangle_p,\quad u,v \in T_pM$

Norm of a tangent vector

$\lVert v\rVert_p = \sqrt{g_p(v,v)}$

Printed pp. 214-217The Riemannian Metric
The Riemannian metric assigns a smoothly varying inner product to each tangent space, letting us measure lengths and angles of tangent vectors. Everything downstream depends on it: without a metric there is no notion of distance, no geodesic, and no intrinsic mean on a curved sample space such as a shape space.

Length and Energy of Curves

Length

Integrate the speed of the curve.

Energy

Integrate the squared speed; favors constant-speed parametrization.

Geodesic distance

$d(p,q)$ is the infimum of lengths over joining curves.

Length functional

$L(\gamma) = \int_0^1 \lVert \dot\gamma(t)\rVert\,dt$

Energy functional

$E(\gamma) = \tfrac12\int_0^1 \lVert \dot\gamma(t)\rVert^2\,dt$

Distance

$d(p,q) = \inf_{\gamma:\,p\to q} L(\gamma)$

Printed pp. 214-217Length and Energy of Curves
Integrating the norm of a velocity vector gives a curve its length, and integrating the squared norm gives its energy. The metric makes both well defined. Minimizing length over curves joining two points produces the geodesic distance, the quantity that the book's intrinsic means are built to minimize on average.

The Levi-Civita Connection

Covariant derivative

$\nabla_X Y$ differentiates a vector field $Y$ along $X$.

Metric compatible

Parallel transport preserves inner products.

Torsion-free

$\nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X = [X,Y]$.

Fundamental theorem

$Xg(Y,Z) = g(\nabla_X Y, Z) + g(Y, \nabla_X Z)$

Printed pp. 214-217Levi-Civita Connection
The Levi-Civita connection is the unique covariant derivative that is compatible with the metric and torsion-free. It tells us how to differentiate vector fields along curves and how to parallel transport tangent vectors, supplying the acceleration term whose vanishing defines a geodesic on the manifold.

Geodesics and Geodesic Distance

Zero acceleration

$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = 0$: the curve never turns relative to the connection.

Locally minimizing

Short geodesic arcs realize the distance between their endpoints.

Use in the book

$d(p,q)$ is the ruler inside every Frechet function.

Geodesic equation

$\ddot\gamma^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\dot\gamma^i\dot\gamma^j = 0$

Frechet function

$F(p) = \int_M d(p,x)^2\, Q(dx)$

Printed pp. 214-217Geodesics and Geodesic Distance
Geodesics are curves of zero covariant acceleration; locally they minimize length and realize the geodesic distance between nearby points. This distance is the intrinsic ruler the book uses to define Frechet functions, so the entire theory of intrinsic location estimation rests on the geodesic structure described here.

Exponential and Logarithm Maps

Exponential map

$\exp_p(v) = \gamma_v(1)$

Logarithm map

$\log_p(q) = \exp_p^{-1}(q)$

Why it matters

$\log_p$ lifts data into $T_pM$ so Euclidean averaging is legitimate.

Printed pp. 214-217Exponential and Logarithm Maps
The exponential map sends a tangent vector at a point to the geodesic endpoint reached at unit time, linearizing the manifold near that point. Its local inverse, the logarithm map, lifts data back into the tangent space, where Euclidean computations such as averaging residuals become legitimate again.

Injectivity Radius and Normal Coordinates

Injectivity radius

The largest $r$ with $\exp_p$ a diffeomorphism on the $r$-ball of $T_pM$.

Normal coordinates

Geodesics through $p$ become straight coordinate lines.

Consequence

Inside $r$, $\log_p$ is single valued and statistics are unambiguous.

Local chart

$\exp_p : B(0,r)\subset T_pM \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; B(p,r)\subset M$

Printed pp. 214-217Injectivity Radius and Normal Coordinates
Within the injectivity radius the exponential map is a diffeomorphism, giving normal coordinates centered at a point where geodesics through the center are coordinate lines. Staying inside this radius is what guarantees that the logarithm map is single valued and that tangent-space statistics are unambiguous.

Sectional Curvature

Two-plane curvature

$K(\sigma)$ measures spreading of geodesics in a tangent plane $\sigma$.

Sign matters

$K>0$ focuses geodesics; $K<0$ spreads them apart.

Comparison theorems

Curvature bounds control distance growth and convexity.

Sectional curvature

$K(u,v) = \dfrac{g(R(u,v)v,\,u)}{g(u,u)g(v,v)-g(u,v)^2}$

Printed pp. 214-217Sectional Curvature
Sectional curvature measures how geodesics emanating from a point spread apart or reconverge along a two-plane in the tangent space. Positive curvature focuses geodesics, negative curvature spreads them, and this sign controls comparison theorems that bound how the squared-distance function behaves on the manifold.

Curvature and the Karcher Mean

Regular ball

Small enough that $p\mapsto d(p,x)^2$ is geodesically convex: unique mean.

Large ball

On positively curved spaces convexity fails: minimizers can split.

Convexity radius

$r < \tfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\Delta}},\quad \Delta = \sup K$

Printed pp. 214-217Curvature and the Karcher Mean
In a regular geodesic ball, curvature bounds make the squared-distance function geodesically convex, so the Frechet or Karcher mean is unique. Outside such a ball, on positively curved spaces, multiple minimizers can appear, which is exactly why the book imposes support conditions for its uniqueness theorems.

Riemannian Submersions and Shape Quotients

Vertical / horizontal

$T_pM = \mathcal{V}_p \oplus \mathcal{H}_p$; the fibers are the group orbits.

Geodesics descend

Horizontal geodesics project to geodesics on the quotient.

Shape spaces

Kendall shape space is a quotient of the preshape sphere.

Submersion

$\pi : S \to S/G,\quad d_\pi^* g \big|_{\mathcal H}\ \text{isometric}$

Printed pp. 214-217Riemannian Submersions and Shape Quotients
A Riemannian submersion splits tangent vectors into vertical and horizontal parts and makes horizontal geodesics descend to geodesics below. Kendall shape spaces arise as such quotients of preshape spheres, so submersion geometry is how distances and means transfer from the sphere down to shape space.

Synthesis: From Geometry to Inference

Metric → distance

Defines $d(p,q)$, the argument of every Frechet function.

exp / log → tangent stats

Move data in and out of $T_pM$ for estimation.

Curvature → uniqueness

Regular-ball convexity guarantees one Karcher mean.

Submersion → shape space

Builds the quotient where shape inference lives.

The intrinsic mean

$\mu = \arg\min_{p\in M}\ \int_M d(p,x)^2\,Q(dx)$

Take-home

Each geometric tool licenses one step of nonparametric manifold inference: nothing here is decorative.

Printed pp. 214-217Synthesis: Geometry to Inference
Each tool maps to an inference step: the metric defines distance, geodesics define the Frechet function, exp and log move data to and from tangent space, curvature certifies uniqueness inside a regular ball, and submersions build shape space. Together they make nonparametric manifold inference rigorous rather than heuristic.
