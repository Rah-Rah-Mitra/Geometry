Extrinsic Mean via Top Eigenstructure

Average

Mean the embedded Gram matrices in the ambient symmetric space.

Project

Map the average onto the rank-$m$ PSD set.

Read the spectrum

Keep the top $m$ eigenvalues and eigenvectors.

Extrinsic mean

$\mu_E = P\big(\bar G\big), \quad \bar G = \tfrac1n\sum_{i} G_i$

Projection

$P(\bar G) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \lambda_j\, u_j u_j^\top$

Printed pp. 110-129Extrinsic Mean via Top Eigenstructure
The extrinsic mean is obtained by averaging the embedded Gram matrices in the ambient symmetric space and projecting that Euclidean average onto the rank-m PSD set. The projection keeps the leading m eigenvalues and eigenvectors, so the population mean shape is read directly from the dominant spectrum.

Existence and Uniqueness

Spectral gap

Projection is single valued when $\lambda_m > \lambda_{m+1}$.

Nonfocal

A gapped distribution has a unique extrinsic mean.

Failure mode

A tie at the cutoff makes the mean ambiguous.

Printed pp. 110-129Existence and Uniqueness
The projection onto rank-m positive semidefinite matrices is single valued exactly when the m-th and the next eigenvalue of the average Gram matrix are distinct. Under this spectral gap the extrinsic mean exists and is unique, and the distribution is then called nonfocal, guaranteeing a well defined estimator.

Two-Sample Tests

Embed both

Compute sample extrinsic means for each population.

Compare in tangent

Difference of projected Gram matrices in the tangent chart.

Wald statistic

Normality gives a chi-squared test for equal mean shapes.

Hypothesis

Test statistic

$T_n = n\,\hat\delta^\top \hat\Sigma^{-1} \hat\delta \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{q}$

To compare two populations we embed both, form their sample extrinsic means, and study the difference of projected Gram matrices in the tangent chart. The asymptotic normality from the central limit theorem yields a chi-squared Wald statistic, giving a nonparametric two-sample test for equality of reflection mean shapes.
