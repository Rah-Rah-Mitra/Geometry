Product Kernels on Mixed Sample Spaces

Factor by coordinate

One kernel per manifold factor.

Stays a density

A product of densities is a density on $X\times Y$.

Adaptive

Bandwidths tune per space.

Product kernel

$K\big((x,y);\theta\big) = K_X(x;\theta_X)\,K_Y(y;\theta_Y)$

Printed pp. 182-208 · PDF 197-223Product Kernels on Mixed Spaces
Because predictor and response may live on different manifolds, the mixing kernel factors as a product of one kernel per coordinate. Each factor is a proper density on its own space, so the product remains a valid density on the product manifold while letting bandwidths and concentrations adapt independently across the mixed sample space.

Dirichlet Process Mixture Prior

Random mixing

$P\sim DP(\alpha, P_0)$ is a.s. discrete.

Stick-breaking

Weights $\pi_k$ decay geometrically.

Concentration

$\alpha$ tunes the cluster count.

Mixture density

$f(x,y) = \displaystyle\sum_{k\ge 1} \pi_k\, K\big((x,y);\theta_k\big)$

Printed pp. 182-208 · PDF 197-223Dirichlet Process Mixture Prior
A Dirichlet process supplies the random mixing distribution, giving an almost surely discrete measure whose atoms become cluster locations. Its stick-breaking weights decay geometrically, the concentration parameter controls how many effective clusters appear, and the base measure seeds kernel parameters, producing an infinite, data-adaptive mixture on the product manifold.

The Bayes Classifier from Posterior Probabilities

Class probabilities

Discrete $Y$ gives $p_j(x)=P(Y=j\mid x)$.

Bayes rule

Pick the label maximizing $p_j(x)$.

Optimal classifier

$\hat{c}(x) = \arg\max_{j}\; p_j(x) = \arg\max_{j}\, \dfrac{w_j f_j(x)}{\sum_\ell w_\ell f_\ell(x)}$

On shapes

Assign a configuration to its most plausible group mixture.

Printed pp. 182-208 · PDF 197-223The Bayes Classifier
When the response is a discrete label, the conditional density collapses to posterior class probabilities at each predictor location. The Bayes rule predicts the group maximizing that probability, minimizing misclassification risk. On shape spaces this means assigning a configuration to the group whose mixture explains its landmark coordinates most plausibly.

Nonparametric Two-Sample Testing on $M$

Hypotheses

$H_0: f_1 = f_2$ vs $H_1: f_1 \ne f_2$.

Priors

A pooled density vs two separate densities.

Model comparison

$\text{BF}_{10} = \dfrac{p(\text{data}\mid H_1)}{p(\text{data}\mid H_0)}$

Reading

Posterior on the mixture discrepancy answers "same or different?"

Printed pp. 182-208 · PDF 197-223Two-Sample Testing of Distributions
To ask whether two groups share a distribution on the manifold, we place a nonparametric prior on each group's density and on a shared pooled density. Comparing the marginal likelihoods, or equivalently the posterior on a discrepancy between mixtures, yields a Bayes factor that weighs equality against difference without distributional assumptions.

Posterior predictive checks and simulation calibrate the factor's behavior under the null, guarding against overconfident rejections when sample sizes are small or kernels are misspecified.
