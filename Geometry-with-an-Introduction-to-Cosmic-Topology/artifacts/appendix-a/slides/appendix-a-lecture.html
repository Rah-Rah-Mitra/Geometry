From Reference List To Course Map

Every Symbol Has A Layer

Model Layer

Where The Object Lives

Examples include \(\mathbb{C}\), \(D\), \(\mathbb{P}^2\), \(H^3\), \(S^3\), and \(T^3\). The notation fixes coordinates, boundary conventions, and what counts as a visible point.

Transformation Layer

What Can Move

Pairs such as \((\mathbb{C},T)\), \((\mathbb{C},E)\), \((\mathbb{C}^+,M)\), \((D,H)\), and \((X_k,G_k)\) pair a space with its allowed symmetries.

Measurement Layer

What Can Be Checked

Modulus, argument, angle, curvature \(k\), Euler characteristic \(\chi(S)\), and density terms \(\Omega_m,\Omega_\Lambda,\Omega_k\) are prompts for computation.

Read: identify the ambient model before interpreting a picture.

Compute: ask which invariant should survive the transformation.

Diagnose: separate coordinate appearance from intrinsic geometry.

Use this slide to reset the students' reading habit. A glossary entry should not be memorized in isolation; it should be sorted into model, transformation, or measurement information. Once that sorting is done, many later formulas become easier to parse because the notation already tells the learner which kind of question is being asked.
Complex-Plane Symbols

The First Computational Package

Coordinate Vocabulary

\(\mathbb{C}\): complex plane

\(S^1=\{z: |z|=1\}\): unit circle

\(\overline{z}\), \(|z|\), \(\arg z\): reflection, length, direction

\[ z = r e^{i\theta},\qquad \overline{z}=r e^{-i\theta},\qquad z\overline{z}=|z|^2 \]

Inspection target: multiplication rotates and scales, conjugation reverses orientation, and the unit circle records pure direction.

The complex plane is the course's first reusable laboratory. Ask students to point to the same number in three forms: a dot, a vector, and a formula. Then connect the symbol list to quick checks: conjugation should preserve modulus, multiplication by a unit complex number should preserve distance from the origin, and argument should record the rotational part.
Transformation Symbols

Geometry Means Space Plus Allowed Motions

Erlangen Reading

Each ordered pair \((X,G)\) says: study the space \(X\) through the features unchanged by the group \(G\).

NotationWorking meaning
\((\mathbb{C},T)\)translations; vector differences matter
\((\mathbb{C},E)\)Euclidean motions; distances and angles matter
\((\mathbb{C}^+,M)\)Mobius maps on the extended plane; clines and cross-ratio structure matter
\((D,H)\)hyperbolic motions of the disk; boundary-orthogonal geodesics matter
This is the place to say explicitly that a geometry is not just a shape drawn on the board. The notation couples a space with the transformations we agree to ignore. When students ask whether two configurations are the same, the answer depends on which group is active. Have them compare translations, Euclidean motions, Mobius maps, and disk isometries.
Model Symbols Across The Course

A Ladder From Coordinates To Curvature

Unifying Family

The symbols \((X_k,G_k)\) package the three constant-curvature worlds into one notation. The sign of \(k\) tells which geometry is being modeled.

\[ k>0 \Rightarrow \text{elliptic},\quad k=0 \Rightarrow \text{Euclidean},\quad k<0 \Rightarrow \text{hyperbolic} \]

The diagram is a derived course map: it emphasizes how later model names reuse earlier coordinate machinery.

The model ladder is a useful bridge from the early chapters to the later surface and universe chapters. Walk left to right across the top row, then drop to the disk and projective models. Students should notice that a familiar coordinate drawing can carry a different metric once the transformation group changes. The parameter k then records the common curvature language.

Here the appendix becomes a classification checklist. The important teaching move is to pair each symbol with the operation it remembers: connected sum, edge identification, handle addition, cross-cap addition, or Euler characteristic. Ask students to explain how two drawings that look different can represent the same surface once the quotient rule and invariant are named.
Cosmic Topology Symbols

From Geometry To Observable Universe Models

Three-Dimensional Models

\(H^3\): hyperbolic 3-space

\(S^3\): 3-sphere

\(T^3\): 3-torus

\[ \Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda+\Omega_k=1,\qquad \Omega_k \text{ records curvature contribution} \]

Local curvature evidence and global topology evidence answer different questions: shape can be hard to infer from a small observable region.

End the symbol list by reconnecting to the course title. H three, S three, and T three are not isolated names; they are candidate local or quotient models for space. The Omega terms enter as observational bookkeeping. Stress the distinction between curvature information and topology information, since a universe can be locally measured while its global identifications remain subtle.
Teaching With The Appendix

A Review Protocol For Any Symbol

  1. Name the model: locate the symbol in \(\mathbb{C}\), \(D\), \(\mathbb{P}^2\), \(X_k\), a surface, or a 3-manifold.
  2. Name the action: identify the transformations or identifications being allowed.
  3. Name the invariant: choose the measurement that should stay stable.
  4. Run a check: draw, compute a residual, and explain the visible evidence.
Mini Lab Prompt

Pick one symbol family and build a two-line test: one construction, one invariant check, one sentence about what would fail if the notation were misread.

\[ \text{symbol} \longrightarrow \text{model} \longrightarrow \text{motion} \longrightarrow \text{check} \]
