Open the lecture by positioning elliptic geometry as the positive-curvature partner to the hyperbolic chapter. Antipodal Pairing

The Quotient Starts With Opposite Points

Stereographic projection turns diametrically opposed sphere points into reciprocal plane points.

Definition

For \(z \in \mathbb C^+\), the antipodal partner is

\(z^a = -1/\overline z\), for \(z \ne 0,\infty\)
\(0^a=\infty,\qquad \infty^a=0\)

Equivalently, finite partners satisfy \(z\overline{w}=-1\). They lie on the same Euclidean line through the origin, on opposite sides.

Spend time on the conjugate in the formula, because it is the part that makes the partner stay on the same Euclidean line through the origin. A quick check is to write z as rho times a unit complex number; the antipode has radius one over rho and direction reversed.
Elliptic Lines

Great Circles Become The Straight Lines

A cline is a line or circle in the extended plane. It becomes an elliptic line exactly when it is closed under antipodes.

Recognition Test

A cline \(C\) is a great circle when

\(z\in C \Longrightarrow z^a\in C\)
  • Lines through the origin pass the test.
  • The unit circle passes the test.
  • A circle through one antipodal pair contains every partner of every point on it.
This is the place to connect the plane picture to spherical intuition. On the sphere, great circles are geodesics; after projection they appear as clines. The closure under antipodes is the operational test students can use without having to redraw the full sphere each time.
Symmetry

Reflection On The Sphere Looks Like Inversion

Inversions are not imported by analogy; they are what spherical reflections become after stereographic projection.

Equator Case

Reflecting \((a,b,c)\) across the equator gives \((a,b,-c)\). Their projected points stay on one ray and satisfy

\(|\phi(P)|\,|\phi(P^*)| = 1\)

So the equatorial reflection corresponds to inversion in the unit circle. General great-circle reflections correspond to inversions in great clines.

Frame this as a bridge between Chapter 3 and Chapter 6. Inversion is familiar from clines and Mobius geometry, but now it has a spherical meaning. Students should see why reflections about great circles are legitimate elliptic motions rather than arbitrary plane tricks.
Elliptic Motions

The Allowed Maps Preserve Antipodal Pairing

The transformation group is the set of Mobius maps that send antipodal pairs to antipodal pairs.

Group S

A transformation \(T\) belongs to \(S\) when

\(w=z^a \Longrightarrow T(w)=T(z)^a\)

A convenient normal form is

\(T(z)=e^{i\theta}\dfrac{z-z_0}{1+\overline{z_0}z}\)
Why This Matters
  • Rotations about the origin are elliptic motions.
  • Any point can be moved to the origin.
  • A second point can be placed on the real axis.
  • Distance and angle formulas may be proved in convenient position, then transported.
Do not dwell on every algebraic step unless the class needs it. The instructional payoff is that the group is transitive enough to normalize configurations. Once students accept that the maps preserve the quotient structure and angles, many later formulas can be derived in a friendly coordinate position.
Model Space

The Projective Disk Is Not A Disk With A Wall

The closed unit disk is a coordinate chart for \(P^2\): each boundary point is identified with its opposite boundary point.

Common Misread

The boundary circle is not an edge of the world. Crossing at \(e^{i\theta}\) reenters at \(-e^{i\theta}\).

\(e^{i\theta}\sim e^{i(\theta+\pi)}\)

This is why two elliptic lines always meet even when a Euclidean drawing tempts the word parallel.

Invite students to imagine walking straight through the disk boundary. The reentry point is not a new rule for motion; it is how the chosen model records the antipodal quotient. This slide is essential for preventing the later line and circle pictures from being interpreted as Euclidean disk geometry.
Measurement

Elliptic Distance Is A Spherical Angle In Disguise

Distances are shortest great-circle separations after antipodal identification, expressed in projective disk coordinates.

Distance Formula

For finite coordinates, a useful expression is

\(d_S(p,q)=2\arctan\left|\dfrac{q-p}{1+\overline{p}q}\right|\)

Because antipodal points are identified in \(P^2\), geometric distance is read as the shorter representative separation.

Compare this formula with the hyperbolic disk formula from the previous chapter. Both are written using complex coordinates and Mobius invariance, but the sign in the denominator and the positive-curvature interpretation change the behavior of large circles. Emphasize that small-radius measurements still look nearly Euclidean.
Elliptic Circles

Large Circles Reveal The Quotient

Circles centered at the origin look familiar for small radius, then fold through antipodal identification as radius grows.

Small Radius

Euclidean intuition is reliable to first order.

\(C(r)\approx 2\pi r\)
Positive Curvature

For \(r<\pi/2\), circumference is smaller than Euclidean growth predicts.

\(C(r)=2\pi\sin r\)
Large Radius

The circle may be represented by pieces of paired clines inside the disk.

boundary points are glued
Inspection target: which parts of a drawn circle are coordinate artifacts, and which parts record intrinsic distance from the center?
This slide should slow down the phrase circle centered at p. In the projective disk, a circle is defined by constant elliptic distance, not by Euclidean shape. The circumference formula is a clean positive-curvature signature, while the large-radius picture reminds students that the model is doing quotient bookkeeping.
Area

Lunes Are The Counting Device For Triangle Area

A lune is bounded by two elliptic lines; antipodal boundary identification makes the shaded region connected.

Lune Formula

Move the lune vertex to the origin and integrate the projective disk area density.

\(dA=\dfrac{4r}{(1+r^2)^2}\,dr\,d\theta\)
\(A_{\text{lune}}=2\alpha\)
The main idea is not the integral technique alone; it is the normalization move. Because elliptic motions preserve area and angle, the lune can be moved to an easy position. Then the polar integral becomes a transparent computation of why the area is proportional to the angle.
Triangle Area

Positive Curvature Makes Excess Visible

Three lunes cover the projective plane once, and the triangle two extra times.

Theorem

For an elliptic triangle with angles \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\),

\(A(\Delta)=\alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi\)

Therefore every nondegenerate elliptic triangle has angle sum greater than \(\pi\).

Contrast this directly with hyperbolic defect. In negative curvature, the missing angle measures area; here the extra angle measures area. The counting argument with lunes is memorable because it avoids treating the formula as a mysterious curvature slogan and instead explains where each copy of the triangle goes.
Elliptic Trigonometry

Spherical Formulas Become Disk Computations

Normalize one vertex to the origin and place another on the real axis; the group \(S\) carries the result back to every triangle.

Two Laws
\(\cos c=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b\cos\gamma\)
\(\dfrac{\sin a}{\sin\alpha}=\dfrac{\sin b}{\sin\beta}=\dfrac{\sin c}{\sin\gamma}\)

Right triangle corollary: if \(\gamma=\pi/2\), then \(\cos c=\cos a\cos b\).

Treat the trigonometry as a proof of consistency between algebraic coordinates and intrinsic geometry. The formulas look spherical because the model comes from the sphere, but the lecture should stress that they are valid in the projective disk after antipodal identification and normalization by elliptic transformations.
Revisiting Euclid

The First Four Postulates Survive; The Fifth Changes

Euclidean, hyperbolic, and elliptic geometry agree locally but disagree globally about parallels.

Euclidean

Exactly One Parallel

Through a point off a line, exactly one line avoids the given line.

Hyperbolic

Many Avoiding Lines

The disk model admits multiple geodesics through the point that do not meet the given line.

Elliptic

No Parallel Lines

Any two elliptic lines meet, because great circles meet on the sphere and antipodal points are identified.

local Euclidean behavior does not determine global topology
Use this comparison to close the mathematical arc and point toward cosmic topology. A small observer may measure nearly Euclidean circles and triangles, yet the global space can still have positive, zero, or negative curvature. The parallel postulate is where those global alternatives become logically visible.
Recap And Board Questions

What Students Should Be Able To Explain

The lesson is successful when learners can move between quotient language, disk pictures, and formulas without conflating them.

Core Takeaways
  • Antipodal points become one point in elliptic geometry.
  • Great circles are the elliptic lines.
  • Group \(S\) preserves antipodes, angle, and the measurement structure.
  • Area records triangle angle excess.
Quick Checks
  • Compute the antipode of \(z=2+i\).
  • Explain why two elliptic lines cannot be parallel.
  • Sketch a circle of radius slightly larger than \(\pi/2\).
  • Find triangle area from angles \(92^\circ,62^\circ,27^\circ\).
End with questions that force model switching. The first is algebraic, the second is topological, the third is visual, and the fourth is metric. If students can answer all four while naming the model they are using, they are ready for the next chapter on geometry on surfaces.
