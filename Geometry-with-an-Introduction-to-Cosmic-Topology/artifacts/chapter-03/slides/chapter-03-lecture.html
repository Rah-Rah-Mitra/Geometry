Transformations As Geometric Tests

Working Definition

A transformation is a rule on a model space whose geometry is read from the features it preserves and the features it deliberately changes.

Layer
Question
Example
Model

Complex plane, extended plane, disk

Good lecture habit: picture first, formula second, invariant third.

A transformation is useful when a visible before-and-after comparison can be paired with a computable invariant.

The important pedagogical move is to separate three questions that students often blend together. Coordinates tell us how to record points, transformations tell us which changes are legal, and invariants tell us what the geometry actually notices after the coordinates have moved.

Basic Complex Maps On A Test Grid

Inspection Task

Track the same square grid under four simple maps. The grid is not the theorem; it is a measuring device for distortion.

  • z \mapsto z+b: displacement is uniform.
  • z \mapsto az: angles survive when a is complex multiplication.
  • Affine shear preserves straightness but not angle.
  • Composition records a motion as an ordered recipe.

Derived from the chapter notebook artifact: basic-transform-grid.png.

Ask students to compare horizontal and vertical grid lines before naming each formula. Translation moves every point by the same vector, multiplication rotates and scales about the origin, and affine shear is the useful counterexample because it keeps lines straight while visibly changing angles.

Reflections Compose Into Familiar Motions

Parallel mirrors produce a translation; intersecting mirrors produce a rotation.

Composition Principle

Two reflections restore orientation. Their product is a translation when the mirror lines are parallel and a rotation when the lines meet.

reflect, then reflect again = an orientation-preserving motion
Why It Matters Later

Transformation groups are not lists of isolated formulas. They are generated by simple moves whose products organize the geometry of a model.

This slide is a bridge from elementary Euclidean motion to the group language used later. Do not rush the phrase generated by. Let students see that two simple orientation-reversing moves can produce a translation or a rotation depending only on the relative placement of the mirrors.

Inversion Turns Radius Into Reciprocal Radius

Unit-Circle Inversion

For a nonzero point z, inversion sends it along the same ray to a point whose distance from the origin is reciprocal.

I(z)=\frac{1}{\overline{z}}, \qquad |z|\,|I(z)|=1
Common Mistake

Inversion preserves angles in magnitude, but it does not preserve distances and it reverses the inside/outside relation of the unit circle.

The radial product check is the visual invariant for this slide.

Introduce inversion as a distance rule before discussing clines. Students should be able to point at one ray, choose a point inside the unit circle, find its image outside, and say why the two radii multiply to one. Then note the angle-preserving surprise.

Angle Preservation: A Proof Picture

Conformality Claim

A circle inversion preserves the magnitude of the angle between two smooth curves at an intersection point away from the center.

1Use tangent lines as local first-order data.
2Invert the two tangent directions through the same point.
3Observe the radial component flips consistently.
4The unoriented angle magnitude is unchanged.
Instructor Note

The slide gives a proof roadmap, not a full algebraic proof. The algebraic derivative calculation can be assigned after the visual argument lands.

Frame this as a proof picture rather than a theorem dump. The local geometry of a smooth curve is its tangent direction, so the angle question can be reduced to what inversion does to two tangent directions meeting at the same noncentral point.

Clines Remove A False Distinction

Definition

A cline is either a line or a circle in the extended complex plane. This single word is the right object language for inversion and Mobius maps.

  • Lines through the inversion center stay lines.
  • Lines missing the center become circles through the center.
  • Circles through the center become lines.
  • Circles missing the center remain circles.

Once infinity is admitted, line and circle behavior becomes one stable family.

Students often try to memorize separate line cases and circle cases. Use this slide to replace that memory burden with a better category. The word cline says that the transformation is behaving cleanly, while our Euclidean vocabulary was artificially split.

The Point At Infinity Is A Geometric Bookkeeper

Stereographic projection turns the extended complex plane into a sphere model.

Extended Plane

The complex plane plus one point, denoted \infty, lets transformations send lines to circles and still remain functions on one compact model.

\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
Interpretation

Infinity is not a distant endpoint on a ruler. It is the single point that closes the plane so clines and rational maps have clean global behavior.

The point at infinity can sound like a formal trick, so make it concrete with the sphere picture. A line in the plane becomes a circle on the sphere through the north pole, which explains why lines and circles belong together in this chapter.

Mobius Maps Are Generated By Simple Moves

Definition

A Mobius transformation is a fractional linear map with nonzero determinant, acting on the extended complex plane.

M(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}, \qquad ad-bc\ne 0
Generator View

Translations, rotations, dilations, and inversion generate the family. This makes the formula less mysterious and the geometry more inspectable.

Present the fractional linear formula after the generator story. The determinant condition prevents collapse to a constant map, while the decomposition into simple moves explains why Mobius transformations preserve clines and angles instead of making that preservation feel miraculous.

The Cross Ratio Is The Four-Point Check

Invariant

For four distinct points, the cross ratio is unchanged by every Mobius transformation for which the expressions are defined.

[z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4]=\frac{(z_3-z_1)(z_4-z_2)}{(z_3-z_2)(z_4-z_1)}
Lab Prompt

Choose four points, apply a sampled Mobius map, and compare the two complex numbers. The residual should be near numerical roundoff.

Treat the cross ratio as the chapter's most compact computational invariant. It is not just a formula to simplify; it is a four-point test that survives Mobius movement, giving students a concrete way to verify that a complicated-looking map is still in the correct transformation family.

Fixed Points Organize Mobius Dynamics

Classification Lens

Instead of plotting every orbit, first solve M(z)=z. The number and type of fixed points predict the qualitative motion.

  • Elliptic behavior: rotation around fixed structure.
  • Hyperbolic behavior: attraction and repulsion along clines.
  • Parabolic behavior: one repeated fixed point.
  • Loxodromic behavior: spiral plus expansion or contraction.

Normal forms make orbit geometry visible before classification vocabulary is memorized.

Use this slide to shift from static preservation to dynamics. Once a map is iterated, fixed points become the anchors of the picture. Students should describe whether nearby points rotate, drift, expand, contract, or spiral before attaching the classification names.

Why This Chapter Prepares Hyperbolic Geometry

Bridge Principle

The Poincare disk model uses clines orthogonal to the boundary circle as hyperbolic lines. Mobius transformations preserving the disk preserve that incidence condition.

Course Connection

Chapter 3 builds the transformation vocabulary; later chapters use it to define non-Euclidean straightness and distance without abandoning complex coordinates.

Euclidean picture + transformation group = non-Euclidean model
Make the future payoff explicit. The disk on the slide is drawn in Euclidean coordinates, but the clines orthogonal to the boundary will become hyperbolic lines. The chapter's transformation tools explain why this model has coherent geometry rather than just attractive drawings.

Three Pitfalls To Name Out Loud

Angle Is Not Distance

Conformal maps preserve angle magnitude locally, but lengths and areas can change dramatically.

Lines Are Not Safer

A circle through the inversion center may become a line, so separate line/circle casework hides the stable object.

Infinity Is Active

The point at infinity is not decorative notation; it makes rational maps and clines behave globally.

Projection Warning

A correct coordinate picture can still mislead about intrinsic measurement. Always say which model is being drawn and which invariant is being checked.

This is the slide to slow down and prevent durable misconceptions. The most common error is to hear angle-preserving and infer distance-preserving. A second error is to treat infinity as algebraic housekeeping rather than a geometric point needed for global transformation behavior.

Takeaways

Conceptual Summary
  • Transformations are studied by their invariants.
  • Inversion swaps radial scale while preserving angle magnitude.
  • Clines are the stable line-circle family.
  • Mobius maps preserve clines, angles, and cross ratios.
