Definition

Lie Group = Smooth Manifold + Group Law

Lie Group
A Lie group is a smooth manifold G with a group structure such that multiplication and inversion are smooth maps.
m : G × G → G,   (g,h) ↦ gh
i : G → G,   g ↦ g-1
Examples to keep in view: S1, Rn under addition, GL(n,R), O(n), and isometry groups.
This slide should slow down the two compatibility requirements. Multiplication and inversion are algebraic operations, but the appendix asks them to be smooth maps. That compatibility is exactly why matrix groups, circles, and isometry groups can be studied with differential geometry.
Infinitesimal Data

Tangent Space At The Identity

Lie Algebra
The Lie algebra g is TeG with a bracket coming from left-invariant vector fields.
[X,Y] = XY - YX
for matrix groups, after identifying invariant fields
  • The identity element is the anchor point.
  • Left translation carries tangent vectors across G.
  • The bracket measures infinitesimal noncommutativity.
Introduce the Lie algebra as the infinitesimal shadow of the group. The tangent space at the identity carries more than linear structure once left-invariant vector fields are identified with tangent vectors. The bracket records the first noncommutative behavior.
Bridge Map

Exponential Map: Local Motion From Algebra

exp : g → G,   X ↦ gammaX(1)
A vector X at the identity defines a one-parameter subgroup gammaX(t). The exponential map records where that flow reaches at time one.
Matrix picture: exp(tX) solves A'(t)=XA(t), A(0)=I. The geometric idea is flow first, formula second.
Explain the exponential map as a bridge, not a magic formula. A tangent vector determines a one-parameter subgroup, and evaluating it at time one returns a nearby group element. For matrices this resembles the usual matrix exponential, but the geometric meaning is broader.
Conjugation

Adjoint Action And Bracket

Two Differentiations
Conjugation Cg(h)=ghg-1 fixes the identity, so its derivative acts on TeG.
Adg = d(Cg)e,    adX(Y) = [X,Y]
  • Ad is a representation of G on its algebra.
  • ad is the infinitesimal version.
  • The Jacobi identity is the consistency law for this bracket.
Here the key message is that conjugation differentiates twice: first it gives the adjoint representation on the Lie algebra, and then its derivative gives the bracket. This makes the bracket visible as the infinitesimal change caused by moving one symmetry through another.
Geometry From Symmetry

Group Actions On Manifolds

Left Action
A smooth action is a smooth map G × M → M, written (g,p) ↦ g.p, satisfying identity and compatibility laws.
e.p = p,    (gh).p = g.(h.p)
Orbit: G.p. Stabilizer: Gp = {g : g.p=p}. Fundamental vector fields differentiate the action.
Group actions are the appendix's return route to Riemannian manifolds. An action assigns a diffeomorphism to each group element, so algebraic composition becomes geometric motion. Orbits, stabilizers, and infinitesimal generators organize how much symmetry a point sees.

Warning

Common Failure Modes

Algebra Only

A group without a compatible smooth manifold structure is not yet a Lie group.

Smoothness Gap

A set-theoretic action does not automatically give smooth maps M → M.

Exponential Trap

The exponential map is a local bridge; globally it may fail to be one-to-one or onto.
Do not replace geometric hypotheses by visual similarity. The invariant check must specify what is preserved: metric, orientation, connection, volume, or another structure.
This warning slide prevents a common overgeneralization. A group is not automatically a Lie group, a smooth action is stronger than a pointwise permutation, and the exponential map need not be globally onto or one-to-one. These distinctions matter in later model-space examples.
