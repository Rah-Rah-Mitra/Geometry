Lie Groups

Definition

A smooth manifold that is also a group, with smooth product and inverse.

Left translation

$L_g(h)=gh$ is a diffeomorphism for each fixed $g$.

Smoothness axioms

$m:G\times G\to G,\quad i:G\to G$ smooth

Examples

$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$, $\mathrm{O}(n)$, $\mathrm{SU}(n)$, the circle $S^1$.

Printed pp. 407-427Lie Groups
A Lie group fuses algebra and geometry: the multiplication and inversion maps are smooth, so the group operations are differentiable. Left translation is a diffeomorphism for every fixed element, which lets us transport tangent data from the identity across the whole manifold without distortion.

Left-Invariant Fields and the Lie Algebra

Left-invariant

$(L_g)_* X = X$ for all $g$: the field is pinned by its value at $e$.

Bracket closes

$[X,Y]$ of left-invariant fields is again left-invariant.

Lie algebra

$\mathrm{Lie}(G)\cong T_eG,\quad [\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]$

Why it matters

Finite-dimensional linear data encodes the whole group near $e$.

Printed pp. 407-427Left-Invariant Fields
A vector field is left-invariant when each left translation pushes it to itself. Evaluation at the identity gives a vector-space isomorphism between left-invariant fields and the tangent space there, and the bracket of two such fields is again left-invariant, making the tangent space a Lie algebra.

One-Parameter Subgroups

Definition

A smooth homomorphism $\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G$ with $\gamma(s+t)=\gamma(s)\gamma(t)$.

Generator

$\gamma'(0)=X\in\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ determines $\gamma$ uniquely.

Printed pp. 407-427One-Parameter Subgroups
A one-parameter subgroup is a smooth homomorphism from the additive real line into the group. Each is the integral curve through the identity of a unique left-invariant field, so its velocity at zero recovers the generating Lie algebra element. The flow is complete, defined for all real times.

The Exponential Map

Construction

$\exp(X)=\gamma_X(1)$, the time-one point of $X$'s subgroup.

Local chart

$d(\exp)_0=\mathrm{id}$, so $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism near $0$.

Matrix case

$\exp(X)=\displaystyle\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{X^k}{k!}$

Caution

$\exp$ need not be surjective or globally injective.

Printed pp. 407-427The Exponential Map
The exponential sends a Lie algebra vector to the time-one value of its one-parameter subgroup. It is a smooth map whose differential at the origin is the identity, hence a diffeomorphism near zero. For matrix groups it coincides with the familiar power-series matrix exponential.

Lie Group Homomorphisms

Smooth and multiplicative

$F:G\to H$ smooth with $F(gh)=F(g)F(h)$.

Induced map

$F_{*e}:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathrm{Lie}(H)$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Intertwining

$F\circ\exp_G=\exp_H\circ\,F_{*e}$

Consequence

Curved group maps reduce to linear maps of algebras.

Printed pp. 407-427Lie Group Homomorphisms
A Lie group homomorphism is a smooth map respecting multiplication. Its differential at the identity is a Lie algebra homomorphism, and exponentials intertwine the two maps. This induced-map principle reduces questions about curved group maps to linear algebra on the tangent spaces at the identities.

Group Actions on Manifolds

Smooth action

$\theta:G\times M\to M$ smooth, $e\cdot p=p$, $(gh)\cdot p=g\cdot(h\cdot p)$.

Transitive

A single orbit fills all of $M$.

Free

$g\cdot p=p$ forces $g=e$: no nontrivial fixed points.

Proper

$(g,p)\mapsto(g\cdot p,p)$ is a proper map; preimages of compacts are compact.

Printed pp. 407-427Group Actions
A smooth action lets a group move the points of a manifold. We track four properties: transitive when one orbit fills the space, free when only the identity fixes any point, and proper when the action-with-projection map is proper. These conditions control the quotient geometry.

Orbits and Isotropy Subgroups

Orbit

$G\cdot p=\{g\cdot p:g\in G\}$, the reach of a point.

Isotropy

$G_p=\{g:g\cdot p=p\}$, the stabilizer subgroup of $p$.

Printed pp. 407-427Orbits and Isotropy
The orbit of a point is everywhere it can be sent; its isotropy subgroup is everything fixing it. The orbit is the image of the group modulo the isotropy subgroup. Conjugate points have conjugate isotropy groups, so orbits of a transitive action all look the same.

Homogeneous Spaces $G/H$

Quotient theorem

Smooth, transitive, proper action $\Rightarrow$ $G/G_p$ is a manifold.

Diffeomorphism

$G/G_p\cong M$ via $gG_p\mapsto g\cdot p$.

Model geometries

$S^n=\mathrm{O}(n{+}1)/\mathrm{O}(n)$

Examples

Spheres, projective spaces, hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^n$.

Printed pp. 407-427Homogeneous Spaces
When a Lie group acts smoothly, transitively, and properly, the quotient by an isotropy subgroup is a smooth manifold and the action map is a diffeomorphism onto it. Such homogeneous spaces are the model geometries: spheres, projective spaces, and hyperbolic space all arise this way.

The Adjoint Representation

Conjugation

$C_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$ fixes $e$, so it differentiates at the identity.

Adjoint

$\mathrm{Ad}(g)=(C_g)_{*e}:\mathrm{Lie}(G)\to\mathrm{Lie}(G)$.

Bracket appears

$\mathrm{ad}(X)Y=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_0\mathrm{Ad}(\exp tX)Y=[X,Y]$

Meaning

The group acts on its own infinitesimal symmetries.

Printed pp. 407-427The Adjoint Representation
Conjugation by a group element fixes the identity, so its differential acts on the Lie algebra; this is the adjoint representation. Differentiating the adjoint at the identity yields the little-adjoint, which is exactly the Lie bracket. These representations encode how the group acts on its own infinitesimal symmetries.

Bi-Invariant Metrics

Definition

A metric invariant under all left and all right translations.

Existence

Every compact Lie group admits one (average over the group).

Ad-invariance

$\langle\mathrm{Ad}(g)X,\mathrm{Ad}(g)Y\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle$

Geodesics

Through $e$ they are exactly the one-parameter subgroups $\exp(tX)$.

Printed pp. 407-427Bi-Invariant Metrics
A bi-invariant metric is invariant under both left and right translations. It exists on every compact Lie group and makes the adjoint maps isometries of the Lie algebra. Its geodesics through the identity are exactly the one-parameter subgroups, tying curvature directly to the bracket structure.
