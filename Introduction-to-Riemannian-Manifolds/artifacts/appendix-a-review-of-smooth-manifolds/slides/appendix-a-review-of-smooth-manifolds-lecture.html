Topological Manifolds

Locally Euclidean

Every point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open set of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Separation hypotheses

Hausdorff and second countable rule out pathologies and supply partitions of unity.

Definition

$M^n$ locally Euclidean, Hausdorff, second countable

Dimension

The integer $n$ is well defined by invariance of domain.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Topological Manifolds
A topological manifold is locally like Euclidean space yet globally curved. We require Hausdorff and second countable so that pathologies like the line with two origins are excluded and partitions of unity exist, which later makes global Riemannian metrics possible by patching local data together.

Charts, Atlases, and Transition Maps

Chart

A homeomorphism $\varphi:U\to\widehat U\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ giving local coordinates.

Transition map

On overlaps, $\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}$ compares the two coordinate systems.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Charts and Atlases
A chart gives coordinates near a point, and an atlas covers the manifold with such charts. Where two charts overlap, the transition map records how the coordinate systems disagree. Compatibility of these transitions is exactly what upgrades a bare topological manifold into a smooth one.

Smooth Structures

Smooth atlas

All transition maps are $C^\infty$ on the overlaps where they are defined.

Maximality

A smooth structure is a maximal smooth atlas, independent of chart choice.

Compatibility

$\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\in C^\infty\big(\varphi(U\cap V)\big)$

Why it matters

Smoothness becomes intrinsic: properties no longer depend on a chosen coordinate system.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Smooth Structures
A smooth atlas has all transition maps infinitely differentiable, and a maximal such atlas is a smooth structure. Smoothness is intrinsic: it never depends on a particular chart, only on whether overlaps are compatible. This frees every later construction from the accident of chosen coordinates.

Smooth Maps and Diffeomorphisms

Smooth map

$F:M\to N$ is smooth if its coordinate representations are $C^\infty$.

Diffeomorphism

A smooth bijection with smooth inverse — the equivalence of smooth manifolds.

Coordinate form

$\widehat F = \psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}\in C^\infty$

Classification

Smooth manifolds are studied up to diffeomorphism, not up to homeomorphism.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Smooth Maps and Diffeomorphisms
A map between manifolds is smooth when it looks smooth in every coordinate representation. A diffeomorphism is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse, the natural notion of equivalence here. Two diffeomorphic manifolds are indistinguishable as smooth objects, so we classify manifolds up to diffeomorphism.

Tangent Vectors as Derivations

Derivation

A linear $v:C^\infty(M)\to\mathbb{R}$ obeying the Leibniz product rule at $p$.

Coordinate basis

The partials $\partial/\partial x^i\big|_p$ span the tangent space $T_pM$.

Leibniz rule

$v(fg)=f(p)\,v(g)+g(p)\,v(f)$

No ambient space

This intrinsic definition needs no embedding of $M$ in $\mathbb{R}^N$.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Tangent Vectors
A tangent vector at a point is a derivation: a linear map on smooth germs obeying the product rule. The coordinate partials form a basis, so the tangent space is a vector space of the manifold's dimension. This algebraic definition needs no ambient space to host the manifold.

The Tangent Space and Velocities

Velocity

$\gamma'(0)f=\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0}(f\circ\gamma)$

Tangent bundle

$TM=\bigsqcup_p T_pM$ collects all tangent spaces into one smooth manifold.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390The Tangent Space
Each point carries its own tangent space, a flat linear approximation of the manifold there. Curves through the point give velocity vectors, and every tangent vector arises this way. Assembling all tangent spaces produces the tangent bundle, the natural arena for vector fields and dynamics.

The Differential and Pushforward

Differential

$dF_p:T_pM\to T_{F(p)}N$ is the best linear approximation of $F$ at $p$.

Pushed curves

It sends the velocity of $\gamma$ to the velocity of $F\circ\gamma$.

Action and matrix

$(dF_p v)(g)=v(g\circ F),\quad [dF_p]=\Big(\dfrac{\partial F^j}{\partial x^i}\Big)$

Chain rule

$d(G\circ F)=dG\circ dF$ — the chain rule made geometric.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390The Differential and Pushforward
A smooth map's differential linearly sends tangent vectors forward, turning the velocity of a curve into the velocity of its image. In coordinates the differential is the Jacobian matrix. This pushforward is the chain rule made geometric, and it underlies immersions, submersions, and rank conditions.

Vector Fields

Section

A smooth choice of $X_p\in T_pM$ for each $p$, i.e. a section of $TM$.

As an operator

Equivalently a derivation $X:C^\infty(M)\to C^\infty(M)$.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Vector Fields
A vector field smoothly assigns a tangent vector to each point, a section of the tangent bundle. Equivalently it is a derivation of the algebra of smooth functions. In a chart it becomes a first-order differential operator whose coefficient functions vary smoothly across the manifold.
Introduction to Riemannian ManifoldsAppendix A

The Lie Bracket

Commutator

$[X,Y]$ measures the failure of $X$ and $Y$ to commute as operators.

Lie algebra

Antisymmetric and satisfying the Jacobi identity on vector fields.

Definition

$[X,Y]f = X(Yf) - Y(Xf)$

Geometry

$[X,Y]=0$ exactly when the two flows commute and coordinates straighten both.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390The Lie Bracket
The Lie bracket of two vector fields measures their failure to commute as flows. It is again a vector field, antisymmetric and satisfying the Jacobi identity, making vector fields a Lie algebra. Vanishing bracket means the flows commute and local coordinates can straighten both fields simultaneously.

Submanifolds and the Rank Theorem

Immersion

$dF_p$ injective everywhere; an embedding also is a topological embedding.

Constant rank

Maps of constant rank look like linear projections in adapted charts.

Rank theorem normal form

$\widehat F(x^1,\dots,x^m)=(x^1,\dots,x^r,0,\dots,0)$

Consequence

Regular level sets of submersions are embedded submanifolds.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Submanifolds and the Rank Theorem
Immersions have injective differential; embeddings are immersions that are also topological embeddings, yielding embedded submanifolds. The constant rank theorem normalizes any constant-rank map to a linear projection in suitable coordinates, explaining why level sets of submersions are smooth submanifolds of predictable dimension.

Flows and Integral Curves

Integral curve

A curve $\gamma$ whose velocity equals the field: $\gamma'(t)=X_{\gamma(t)}$.

Flow

The one-parameter group $\theta_t$ of diffeomorphisms generated by $X$.

Flow equations

$\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta_t(p)=X_{\theta_t(p)},\quad \theta_0=\mathrm{id}$

Lie derivative

Differentiating along the flow defines $\mathcal{L}_X$, with $\mathcal{L}_X Y=[X,Y]$.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Flows of Vector Fields
Integrating a vector field produces its flow, a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms whose orbits are the field's integral curves. The fundamental theorem guarantees existence and uniqueness locally. Flows convert infinitesimal data into finite motion and define the Lie derivative measuring change along the field.

Synthesis: The Tower of Structure

Bottom to top

Topology → charts → smoothness → tangent vectors → differentials → fields, brackets, flows.

Next step

Add an inner product on each $T_pM$ to obtain a Riemannian metric.

Key invariants

Dimension, smooth structure, rank of maps, and the Lie-algebra structure of fields.

Takeaway

Every Riemannian construction is built atop these chart-independent smooth notions.

Lee, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds — printed pp. 371-390Synthesis
The appendix builds one tower: topology supports charts, charts support smoothness, smoothness supports tangent vectors and differentials, and these support fields, brackets, submanifolds, and flows. With this scaffolding fixed, the main text can add a metric and develop genuinely Riemannian geometry on solid ground.
