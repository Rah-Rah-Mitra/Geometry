Topological Preliminaries: What Makes a Manifold

Topological n-Manifold

A second-countable Hausdorff space that is locally homeomorphic to \mathbb{R}^n.

Why Each Axiom

  • Hausdorff separates distinct points.
  • Second countability supplies partitions of unity.
  • Local Euclidean shape gives charts.

Failure Mode

The line with two origins is locally Euclidean but not Hausdorff: limits become ambiguous.

Charts are the bridge to calculusSlide 3 / 10
Three conditions show up in every definition of a manifold and each one earns its place. Hausdorff lets us separate points and trust limits, second countability buys partitions of unity which we will need to glue local objects, and local homeomorphism to Euclidean space gives the charts that turn topology into calculus. The line with two origins is the standard cautionary example; mention it so students remember why the Hausdorff hypothesis is non-decorative.

Smooth Structure: Compatible Charts and Atlases

Smooth Compatibility

Charts (U,\varphi) and (V,\psi) are smoothly compatible if the transition map \psi\circ\varphi^{-1} is C^\infty on its domain.

Smooth Atlas

A collection of pairwise smoothly compatible charts whose domains cover M.

Maximal Atlas

A smooth structure is the unique maximal atlas containing a given one; this is what makes “smooth manifold” well-defined.

Smoothness lives in the transition mapsSlide 4 / 10
The smooth structure does not live on the manifold; it lives in the transitions between charts. Two charts are compatible exactly when their composition through the overlap is smooth as a map between open sets of Euclidean space. Stress that we take the maximal atlas so that two different starting atlases that agree on overlaps define the same smooth manifold; this removes a layer of arbitrary choice that students often worry about.

Smooth Maps and Diffeomorphisms

F: M \to N \text{ is smooth iff } \psi \circ F \circ \varphi^{-1} \text{ is } C^\infty \text{ for all charts}

Smooth Map

Test smoothness in any chart; the maximal atlas guarantees the answer does not depend on which chart you pick.

Diffeomorphism

A smooth bijection with smooth inverse. Diffeomorphic manifolds are interchangeable as smooth objects.

Local Diffeomorphism

Smooth and locally invertible. Charts themselves are local diffeomorphisms.

Smoothness is checkable chart by chartSlide 5 / 10
The definition of a smooth map is engineered so the check reduces to ordinary multivariable calculus inside Euclidean space. Once chart independence is justified, smoothness becomes a local computation. Highlight that diffeomorphism is the right equivalence relation for smooth manifolds, just as homeomorphism is for topological spaces; later, the Riemannian setting will refine this further with isometries.

Tangent Vectors: Derivations at a Point

Derivation

v: C^\infty(M) \to \mathbb{R}, \; v(fg)=v(f)g(p)+f(p)v(g)

Tangent Space

T_pM is an n-dimensional real vector space; a chart gives the basis \partial/\partial x^1,\dots,\partial/\partial x^n.

Velocity

If \gamma:I\to M is smooth with \gamma(0)=p, then \gamma'(0)(f)=(f\circ\gamma)'(0) is a tangent vector at p.

Three pictures, one vector spaceSlide 6 / 10
Tangent vectors can be introduced as equivalence classes of curves, as derivations on smooth functions, or as columns of numbers in a chart. The derivations definition is the cleanest because it lives intrinsically on the manifold, but students should be comfortable converting between the three pictures fluently. The basis induced by a chart is the workhorse for computations and connects every later Riemannian formula back to ordinary partial derivatives.

Regular Value Theorem

If c is a regular value of F:M\to N, then F^{-1}(c) is an embedded submanifold of dimension \dim M-\dim N.

Worked Example

F(x)=|x|^2 on \mathbb{R}^{n+1} has 1 as a regular value, so the unit sphere S^n is an embedded submanifold.

Rank of dF decides the typeSlide 7 / 10
All three submanifold notions are captured by the rank of the differential, which is the single piece of data students should learn to read first. Immersions allow tangent injection but tolerate global pathology, embeddings add the right topology, and submersions are responsible for the cut-out picture of level sets. The sphere example anchors the regular value theorem in a familiar object so the abstract statement does not float free of geometry.

Vector Bundles: Smoothly Varying Fibers

Vector Bundle

A smooth surjection \pi:E\to M whose fibers E_p=\pi^{-1}(p) are k-dim vector spaces, locally trivialized as U\times\mathbb{R}^k.

Tangent Bundle

TM=\bigsqcup_{p\in M} T_pM is the prototype vector bundle and carries its own smooth structure.

Section

A smooth map \sigma:M\to E with \pi\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}_M; chooses one vector in each fiber.

Fibers + smooth trivializations = bundleSlide 8 / 10
A vector bundle packages a family of vector spaces over a manifold so that fibers vary smoothly. The local triviality condition is what allows local computations to behave like ordinary linear algebra, while the absence of a global trivialization is what produces nontrivial geometry. The tangent bundle is the canonical example to keep in mind because every Riemannian construction we meet later is either a tensor bundle built from TM or a section of one.

Vector Fields, Flows, and Covering Maps

Vector Field

A smooth section X:M\to TM. In a chart, X=X^i\,\partial/\partial x^i.

Flow Theorem

Each X integrates to a unique maximal local flow \theta_t with \partial_t\theta_t(p)=X(\theta_t(p)).

Lie Bracket

[X,Y]f = X(Yf)-Y(Xf) measures non-commutativity of flows.

Covering Map

A surjection \pi:\tilde M\to M such that every p\in M has an evenly covered neighborhood: \pi^{-1}(U) is a disjoint union of copies of U.

Why It Matters

Lifts geodesics and Riemannian metrics; the universal cover trivializes \pi_1(M).

Vector fields move; covers unfoldSlide 9 / 10
Two constructions close out the appendix: vector fields with their flows, and covering maps. The flow theorem promotes a vector field into a one-parameter family of partial diffeomorphisms, and the Lie bracket then measures whether two flows commute. Covering maps come back when we discuss completeness and universal covers in Riemannian geometry; the evenly covered neighborhood condition is the precise reason geodesics lift uniquely once a starting point is chosen.
