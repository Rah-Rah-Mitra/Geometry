Frame the whole chapter with one sentence: bound the curvature, and the geometry is trapped between the same bound applied to a constant-curvature model space. Everything downstream is a way of making that slogan rigorous. The technical engine is the second variation of arc length, repackaged first as Jacobi fields, then as a Riccati equation for the shape operator of geodesic spheres. Tell students that the model spaces are the sphere of curvature kappa, Euclidean space, and hyperbolic space, and that the constant-curvature Jacobi fields involve the functions s-kappa and c-kappa they should keep at their fingertips.

The Comparison Principle

The Slogan

A bound on curvature forces a bound on geometry. Compare the unknown manifold against a constant-curvature model where everything is computable.

Model Spaces

M^n_\kappa:\ S^n,\ \mathbb{R}^n,\ \mathbb{H}^n

Curvature exactly \kappa. Jacobi solutions are s_\kappa,\ c_\kappa.

Two Directions

Sectional bounds control geodesic spreading (Rauch); Ricci bounds control volume (Bishop-Gromov).

Bound curvature, bound geometrySlide 2 / 10
This is the conceptual spine of the chapter. Stress that comparison theorems never compute the geometry of the unknown manifold directly; they sandwich it. The model spaces are the simply connected space forms of constant curvature kappa, and their Jacobi fields are explicit: the curvature-kappa sine s-kappa and cosine c-kappa. Point out the asymmetry that students always confuse: a curvature upper bound gives a lower bound on how fast geodesics spread and on conjugate distance, while a curvature lower bound gives the reverse. Foreshadow that for volume only a Ricci lower bound is needed, not a full sectional bound.

The Hessian of the Distance Function

Radial Field

Let r(x)=d(p,x). Then \nabla r is the unit radial field, with |\nabla r|=1 and \nabla_{\nabla r}\nabla r=0.

Hessian = Shape Operator

$$\nabla^2 r(X,Y)=\langle S X,Y\rangle$$

The Hessian of r restricted to a geodesic sphere is its second fundamental form / shape operator S.

Eigenvalues

Eigenvalues of \nabla^2 r are the principal curvatures of the geodesic sphere; their sum is \Delta r.

Distance Hessian encodes sphere curvatureSlide 3 / 10
The distance function is the right object because its level sets are geodesic spheres and its gradient is the unit radial field generating the geodesics from p. Make explicit that the Hessian of r, evaluated on directions tangent to a geodesic sphere, is exactly the shape operator of that sphere viewed as a hypersurface. So controlling the distance Hessian is the same as controlling how geodesic spheres bend. The trace of the Hessian is the Laplacian of r, which is the mean curvature of the sphere and will reappear in volume and Laplacian comparison. Note the singular set: r is smooth away from p and the cut locus.

The Riccati Equation for the Shape Operator

$$S' + S^2 + R = 0,\qquad R(\cdot)=R(\cdot,\partial_r)\partial_r$$

Derivation

Differentiate the shape operator S=\nabla^2 r along the radial geodesic. The connection identity gives a matrix Riccati ODE.

Scalar Trace

$$(\Delta r)' + |S|^2 + \operatorname{Ric}(\partial_r,\partial_r)=0$$

Tracing yields a scalar equation in the mean curvature \Delta r.

Constant Curvature

If K\equiv\kappa then S=\dfrac{s_\kappa'}{s_\kappa}\,\mathrm{Id}=\dfrac{c_\kappa}{s_\kappa}\,\mathrm{Id}.

Curvature enters as the Riccati forcing termSlide 4 / 10
This is the technical heart. The shape operator S of the geodesic spheres satisfies the matrix Riccati equation S-prime plus S-squared plus R equals zero along the radial geodesic, where R is the tangential curvature operator. The curvature is literally the forcing term. Emphasize the structure: if you increase R you push S down, meaning spheres bend less and grow slower; decrease R and S grows. Tracing gives the scalar equation in the Laplacian of r whose forcing is Ricci, which is precisely why Ricci alone suffices for the Laplacian and volume statements. In the constant-curvature model, S is a scalar multiple of the identity equal to c-kappa over s-kappa, the explicit comparison value.

Jacobi Fields and the Rauch Comparison

Jacobi Equation

$$J'' + R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma = 0$$

A Jacobi field measures the infinitesimal spreading of a geodesic variation.

Rauch Comparison

If K_M\le K_{\tilde M} then Jacobi fields in M grow at least as fast: |J|\ge|\tilde J| before the first conjugate point.

Link to Riccati

The log-derivative J'/J solves the same Riccati equation as S.

Lower curvature spreads geodesics moreSlide 5 / 10
Jacobi fields are the linearization of the geodesic flow and solve the second-order Jacobi equation with the curvature operator as coefficient. Make the connection back to the previous slide explicit: a Jacobi field that vanishes at p is essentially S applied to itself, so the logarithmic derivative of its norm satisfies the same Riccati equation as the shape operator. Rauch is then a Sturm comparison statement for that ODE: smaller curvature means the field is less restrained and grows faster, so geodesics spread more and conjugate points come later. Warn that Rauch is sharp only up to the first conjugate point, where the comparison can break.

Sectional Curvature Comparison

Upper Bound K\le\kappa

Geodesics spread at least as fast as in M_\kappa. No conjugate points before the model's. Distances inside small triangles are larger (CAT-type, Toponogov flavor).

  • Conjugate radius bounded below
  • Exponential map a local diffeomorphism farther out

Lower Bound K\ge\kappa

Geodesics spread at most as fast. Conjugate points appear no later than the model. Triangles are fatter than in M_\kappa.

  • Diameter controlled (toward Bonnet-Myers)
  • Toponogov angle comparison

The Asymmetry to Remember

An upper bound controls Jacobi field growth from below; a lower bound controls it from above. The inequality flips because curvature is the negative forcing term in the Jacobi equation.

Rauch in both directionsSlide 6 / 10
This slide collects the two halves of Rauch into a usable dictionary. Walk through each column slowly because the direction-flipping is where students lose the thread. With a curvature upper bound, geodesics spread at least as fast as in the model, conjugate points are pushed out, and the exponential map stays nonsingular longer, which is the route to Cartan-Hadamard for nonpositive curvature. With a lower bound, spreading is suppressed, conjugate points arrive early, triangles fatten, and diameters shrink, which feeds Bonnet-Myers. The warning box states the sign mechanism: curvature is the negative coefficient in the Jacobi equation, so raising it damps growth.

Ricci Lower Bound and Bishop-Gromov Volume Comparison

Hypothesis

$$\operatorname{Ric}\ge (n-1)\kappa\, g$$

Only a Ricci lower bound is needed, because volume traces the Riccati equation.

Bishop-Gromov

The ratio $$\dfrac{\operatorname{vol}\big(B_r(p)\big)}{\operatorname{vol}\big(B^\kappa_r\big)}$$ is nonincreasing in r and \le 1.

Mechanism

The volume density \sqrt{\det g} has log-derivative \Delta r, bounded above by the model via the Riccati trace.

Ricci lower bound caps volume growthSlide 7 / 10
Bishop-Gromov is the volume incarnation of the Riccati trace. Stress the economy: only a Ricci lower bound is required, not a sectional bound, because the volume element in geodesic polar coordinates has logarithmic derivative equal to the Laplacian of r, whose forcing term is exactly Ricci of the radial direction. The Riccati comparison then bounds that mean curvature above by the model value, so the volume density grows no faster than in the space form. Integrating gives the absolute Bishop bound vol leq model, and the stronger Gromov statement is monotonicity of the ratio of ball volumes, which is the workhorse for precompactness and rigidity results.

Diameter Bound: Bonnet-Myers as Comparison

Bonnet-Myers Theorem

If \operatorname{Ric}\ge (n-1)\kappa\,g with \kappa>0, then $$\operatorname{diam}(M)\le \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa}}$$ and M is compact with finite \pi_1.

Comparison Proof Idea

Along a geodesic of length L>\pi/\sqrt\kappa the index form goes negative: a conjugate point must occur, so the geodesic is no longer minimizing.

Matches the Model

The bound is exactly the diameter \pi/\sqrt\kappa of the round sphere S^n_\kappa; equality forces the sphere.

Ricci positivity bounds diameterSlide 8 / 10
Present Bonnet-Myers as the diameter comparison theorem so it sits in the same family. The proof is pure second-variation: feed the index form a sinusoidal variation field along a geodesic, and a positive Ricci lower bound forces the index form negative once the length exceeds pi over root kappa. A negative index form means the geodesic is not minimizing, so no two points can be farther apart than that. Hence the manifold is bounded, and completeness plus Hopf-Rinow gives compactness; the universal cover argument gives finite fundamental group. The bound is sharp: it equals the round sphere's diameter, and equality is a rigidity statement returning the sphere.

Laplacian Comparison

$$\operatorname{Ric}\ge (n-1)\kappa\,g\ \Longrightarrow\ \Delta r \le (n-1)\,\frac{c_\kappa(r)}{s_\kappa(r)} = \Delta^\kappa r$$

What It Says

The mean curvature of geodesic spheres is bounded above by the constant-curvature value, in the barrier / distributional sense across the cut locus.

Why It Holds

It is the scalar Riccati trace: (\Delta r)'+\tfrac{(\Delta r)^2}{n-1}+\operatorname{Ric}\le 0 by Cauchy-Schwarz, then Sturm comparison.

What It Powers

Bishop-Gromov volume growth, splitting theorems, heat-kernel and gradient estimates, spectral bounds.

Across the Cut Locus

The inequality holds globally as a barrier (viscosity) inequality even though r is not smooth on the cut locus, where it can only jump downward.

Ricci controls the Laplacian of distanceSlide 9 / 10
Laplacian comparison is the analytic distillation of everything before it. The Laplacian of the distance function is the mean curvature of geodesic spheres and equals the trace of the shape operator, so its evolution is the scalar Riccati equation whose nonlinear term, after Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the squared shape operator below by mean-curvature-squared over n minus one, is controlled and whose forcing is Ricci. Sturm comparison then bounds Delta r above by the model value. Be careful about regularity: r fails to be smooth on the cut locus, so the inequality is stated in the barrier or distributional sense, and crucially the singularities only help because Delta r can jump down there. This single estimate is the gateway to most modern Ricci-lower-bound geometry.

What Comparison Theory Delivers

1

One Engine

The shape operator of geodesic spheres obeys the Riccati equation S'+S^2+R=0. Curvature is the forcing term.

2

Two Inputs

Sectional bounds drive Rauch (geodesic spreading); Ricci lower bounds drive Bishop-Gromov and the Laplacian estimate.

3

Three Outputs

Bounds on conjugate points, volumes, and diameters — Rauch, Bishop-Gromov, Bonnet-Myers.

Diagnostic Question

When you meet a comparison theorem, ask: which curvature is bounded and in which direction, which ODE does the relevant geometric quantity satisfy, and which model space provides the sharp constant?

The engine is the Riccati equation for the shape operator with curvature as forcing; the matrix version governs sectional comparison and Rauch, the scalar trace governs Ricci comparison, Bishop-Gromov, and the Laplacian estimate. The two inputs are sectional bounds and Ricci lower bounds; remind students which theorems need only the weaker Ricci hypothesis. The three outputs are conjugate-point, volume, and diameter control.
