Chapter 6Geodesics and Distance
Set the agenda by stating that this chapter promotes geodesics from solutions of an ODE to the central tool that controls distance on a Riemannian manifold. Highlight the four pillars: a variational characterization, local minimization in normal neighborhoods, the induced distance function, and a global completeness theorem. Tell students that everything we do today rests on the first variation formula and on a single careful use of the inverse function theorem.

Two Functionals on Admissible Curves

Length Functional

L(\gamma) = \int_a^b |\dot\gamma(t)|_g\,dt

Reparametrization-invariant; vanishes only on constant curves.

Energy Functional

E(\gamma) = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_a^b |\dot\gamma(t)|_g^2\,dt

Depends on parametrization; smoother to differentiate.

Cauchy-Schwarz Bridge

L(\gamma)^2 \le 2(b-a)\,E(\gamma),

with equality exactly when |\dot\gamma| is constant. A minimizer of E on a fixed interval therefore has constant speed and minimizes L too.

Length vs energy: same minimizers, different easeSlide 2 / 10
Stress that length is what we ultimately care about because it is a geometric invariant of the image of the curve. Energy is the convenience: it is convex in the velocity and produces an ODE without square roots. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality reconciles them and forces constant-speed parametrization on energy minimizers. This is the moment to remind students that the variational problem is the same one Euler studied for shortest paths.

The First Variation Yields the Geodesic Equation

Variation Identity

\tfrac{d}{ds}\Big|_{0} E(\gamma_s) = -\!\int_a^b \langle V, D_t\dot\gamma\rangle\,dt

after using metric compatibility and integration by parts with V(a)=V(b)=0.

Geodesic Equation

D_t\dot\gamma \equiv 0

Stationarity for every admissible V forces the covariant acceleration to vanish.

Stationary energy = vanishing covariant accelerationSlide 3 / 10
Walk through the derivation in three moves: differentiate energy under the integral, swap the order of differentiation using the symmetry lemma, then integrate by parts using metric compatibility. The boundary terms vanish because we fixed endpoints, leaving an integral that must vanish against every admissible variation field. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations then forces the covariant acceleration to be zero pointwise. Underscore that this is the same ODE we previously took as a definition; here we earn it from a variational principle.

Geodesics Locally Minimize Length

Theorem

For every point p\in M there exists a neighborhood U such that for all q\in U the radial geodesic from p to q is the unique length-minimizing curve in M from p to q, up to reparametrization.

Tool

The exponential map \exp_p:T_pM\supset B_r(0)\to M is a diffeomorphism for some r>0; the image is a geodesic ball at p.

Local vs Global

Local minimization is automatic from the variation principle plus the Gauss lemma. Global minimization is not: a great circle on the sphere stops minimizing once it passes the antipode.

Local minimization is structural, not globalSlide 4 / 10
Frame the slide around the slogan: geodesics are local, distance is global. The inverse function theorem applied to the exponential map at the origin gives a radius beyond which the ODE solutions sweep out a diffeomorphic image of a Euclidean ball. Inside that ball every other admissible curve is strictly longer than the radial geodesic. Remind students that minimization can fail beyond the injectivity radius even when the geodesic continues to exist.

Uniformly Normal Neighborhoods

Definition

W\subset M is uniformly normal if there exists \delta>0 such that for every p\in W the geodesic ball B_\delta(p) is normal and contains W.

Consequence

Any two points in W are joined by a unique minimizing geodesic that depends smoothly on the endpoints.

Use

Provides the local building block for piecewise-geodesic approximations of arbitrary curves.

One uniform radius governs every nearby pointSlide 5 / 10
Pinpoint the upgrade from normal to uniformly normal. A normal neighborhood lives at a single basepoint; a uniformly normal neighborhood works for every basepoint inside it with one shared radius. This is the technical refinement that lets us patch local minimizers and is the device behind the proof that the distance function is continuous. Mention that compactness of a closed geodesic ball plus continuous dependence of the exponential map are what make the construction possible.

The Gauss Lemma: Radial Orthogonality

Statement

\langle d(\exp_p)_v(v),\, d(\exp_p)_v(w)\rangle = \langle v,w\rangle

whenever w\perp v in T_pM.

Geometric Reading

Geodesic spheres are level sets of the radial distance; the radial geodesic crosses them perpendicularly.

Payoff

Any competing curve picks up a strictly positive transverse contribution, so the radial geodesic minimizes length.

The orthogonality that turns the variational argument into a strict inequalitySlide 6 / 10
The Gauss lemma is the geometric statement behind the inequality that makes radial geodesics shortest. Once we know that the radial vector is orthogonal to the geodesic sphere, we can decompose any competing curve into a radial part and a tangential part. The Pythagorean estimate gives a strictly positive transverse cost unless the curve stays radial. Encourage students to read the lemma as a regularity statement about the exponential map: angles at the base point persist outward, at least along the radial direction.

The Riemannian Distance Generates the Topology

Definition

d_g(p,q) = \inf\{L(\gamma): \gamma\text{ piecewise smooth from }p\text{ to }q\}

The infimum is taken over admissible curves.

Theorem

d_g is a metric and induces the same topology as the manifold's smooth atlas.

Underline that the distance function is not a new structure layered on top of the manifold; it is the metric that the smooth and Riemannian data already determine. Each metric axiom reduces to a concrete local fact: the Gauss lemma supplies the separation property and the uniformly normal neighborhoods supply the basis comparison. The students should leave the slide convinced that geodesic balls really are the basic open sets for the original topology.

Hopf-Rinow: Equivalent Completeness Statements

Theorem (Hopf-Rinow)

For a connected Riemannian manifold (M,g), the following are equivalent:

  • (M,d_g) is a complete metric space.
  • Closed and bounded subsets of M are compact.
  • Every maximal geodesic is defined for all time.
  • \exp_p is defined on all of T_pM for some (equivalently, every) p.

Corollary

If any one of the equivalents holds, then any two points of M are joined by a minimizing geodesic.

Hypothesis Watch

The punctured Euclidean plane \mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\} is connected but not geodesically complete; the infimum from (1,0) to (-1,0) is not attained.

Strategy

The proof grows a maximal minimizer one uniformly normal ball at a time and uses sequential compactness to extend it.

Four very different statements with one shared proofSlide 8 / 10
Hopf-Rinow is the central global theorem of the chapter. State the four equivalents carefully so students see that two are metric, one is dynamical, and one is analytic. The proof proceeds by extension: starting at the supremum of times for which a minimizer exists, use the uniformly normal neighborhood at the limit point to extend it. The counter-example with a puncture shows that completeness is not automatic and that bounded sets can fail to be compact without it.

Semigeodesic Coordinates as a Distance Tool

Construction

Around a hypersurface \Sigma shoot unit-speed geodesics in the normal direction. Use arc length r and coordinates on \Sigma as transverse charts.

Block Metric

g = dr^2 + h_{ij}(r,y)\,dy^i dy^j

The off-diagonal terms vanish by the Gauss lemma.

Distance Application

r equals signed distance to \Sigma in the tube; level sets of r are parallel hypersurfaces.

Fermi coordinates: the chart that diagonalizes radial distanceSlide 9 / 10
Position semigeodesic coordinates, also called Fermi coordinates, as the natural successor to normal coordinates. Where normal coordinates radiate from a point, semigeodesic coordinates radiate from a hypersurface. The metric block decomposes because the radial direction is orthogonal to the transverse one by the Gauss lemma applied along the hypersurface. The payoff is operational: many distance and curvature computations near a hypersurface reduce to ODEs in the radial variable.
