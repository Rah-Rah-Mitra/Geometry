Geodesics as Zero Acceleration

Straightest curve

Velocity is parallel-transported along itself.

Chart-free

The condition uses only $\nabla$, no coordinates.

Definition

$D_t \dot\gamma(t) = \nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = 0$

Consequence

Speed $|\dot\gamma|$ is constant along any geodesic.

Printed pp. 151-192Geodesics as Zero Acceleration
A geodesic is the connection's notion of a straight line: its velocity is parallel along itself, so covariant acceleration vanishes. Because the Levi-Civita connection is built from the metric, this single condition encodes everything the metric knows about going straight, independent of any chart.

The Geodesic Equation

Local form

Covariant straightness becomes a second-order ODE system.

Existence

One maximal geodesic per point and direction.

In coordinates

$\ddot{x}^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\dot{x}^j = 0$

Initial data

Specify $\gamma(0)=p$ and $\dot\gamma(0)=v$.

Printed pp. 151-192The Geodesic Equation
In coordinates the covariant condition becomes a coupled system of second-order ordinary differential equations driven by the Christoffel symbols. Existence and uniqueness for such systems guarantees exactly one maximal geodesic through each point in each direction, the fact that underwrites every later construction.

The Exponential Map

Definition

Flow the geodesic with velocity $v$ for unit time.

Spray

All geodesics through $p$ in one smooth map.

Exponential map

$\exp_p(v) = \gamma_v(1), \quad \gamma_v(t)=\exp_p(tv)$

Differential

$d(\exp_p)_0 = \mathrm{id}$ on $T_pM$.

Printed pp. 151-192The Exponential Map
Following the unique geodesic with a given initial velocity for unit time defines the exponential map from the tangent space into the manifold. It packages all geodesics through one point into a single smooth map and is the workhorse for transferring linear structure to the curved space.

Normal Neighborhoods

Local diffeo

$\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism on a star-shaped ball.

Unique radial

Each point joined to $p$ by one radial geodesic.

Printed pp. 151-192Normal Neighborhoods
Because the exponential map has invertible differential at the origin, it is a diffeomorphism on a small star-shaped ball. Its image is a normal neighborhood in which every point is joined to the center by a unique radial geodesic, giving us privileged coordinates centered at that point.

Normal and Geodesic Coordinates

Straight rays

Geodesics through $p$ are straight lines in the chart.

Metric at $p$

$g_{ij}(p)=\delta_{ij}$ and $\Gamma^k_{ij}(p)=0$.

Normal coordinates

$x \;\longmapsto\; \exp_p\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_i x^i E_i\Big)$

Payoff

Curvature computations simplify at the center.

Printed pp. 151-192Normal and Geodesic Coordinates
Pulling back through the exponential map yields normal coordinates in which geodesics through the origin are straight lines, the metric is the identity there, and Christoffel symbols vanish at the center. These coordinates dramatically simplify local computations and proofs about curvature.

The Gauss Lemma

Orthogonality

Radial geodesics meet geodesic spheres at right angles.

Why it matters

The key step that makes radial paths efficient.

Gauss lemma

$\big\langle\, d(\exp_p)_v(v),\, d(\exp_p)_v(w)\,\big\rangle = \langle v, w\rangle$

Reading

The radial direction stays orthogonal to spheres.

Printed pp. 151-192The Gauss Lemma
The Gauss lemma states that radial geodesics from the center meet the geodesic spheres around it orthogonally. This orthogonality is the geometric heart of why radial geodesics are efficient, and it converts metric questions near a point into clean statements about radius and angle.

Riemannian Distance

Definition

Infimum of lengths over admissible curves $p\to q$.

Metric space

$(M,d)$ satisfies the metric axioms.

Distance

$d(p,q) = \inf_{\gamma}\; L(\gamma),\quad L(\gamma)=\!\int |\dot\gamma|\,dt$

Topology

The metric topology equals the manifold topology.

Printed pp. 151-192Riemannian Distance
The Riemannian distance between two points is the infimum of lengths of admissible curves joining them. This turns a connected Riemannian manifold into a genuine metric space whose topology agrees with the manifold topology, linking smooth geometry to the language of analysis.

Geodesics Locally Minimize

Local claim

In a normal ball the radial geodesic is shortest.

Global caveat

Far away a geodesic can overshoot the optimum.

Local minimization

$L(\gamma_{\text{radial}}) = d(p,q) \le L(\sigma)$

Example

A great-circle arc past the antipode is no longer minimal.

Printed pp. 151-192Geodesics Locally Minimize
Using the Gauss lemma we show that inside a normal ball the radial geodesic is strictly shorter than any other curve to the same point. So geodesics are locally length-minimizing, although globally a geodesic may overshoot the true shortest route, as on a sphere.

Minimizing Geodesic vs Competitor

Velocity split

$|\dot\sigma|^2 = \dot r^2 + |\dot\sigma^{\perp}|^2 \ge \dot r^2$

Conclusion

Only the radial part lowers length; detours strictly cost.

Printed pp. 151-192Minimizing vs Competitor
We compare the radial geodesic against a longer competitor curve between the same endpoints. Decomposing the competitor's velocity into radial and spherical parts and applying the Gauss lemma shows only the radial part contributes the minimum length, so any detour strictly costs extra arc length.

Geodesic Completeness

Definition

Every maximal geodesic is defined for all time.

Failure

A puncture lets a geodesic escape in finite time.

Complete iff

$\exp_p \text{ defined on all of } T_pM,\ \forall p$

Examples

$\mathbb{R}^n$ and compact manifolds are complete.

Printed pp. 151-192Geodesic Completeness
A manifold is geodesically complete when every maximal geodesic extends to all real time, so the exponential map is defined on the whole tangent space. Completeness is exactly the condition preventing geodesics from running off an edge or into a puncture in finite time.

The Hopf-Rinow Theorem

Equivalences

Geodesic, metric, and Heine-Borel completeness agree.

Payoff

Any two points joined by a minimizing geodesic.

Theorem

$M \text{ complete} \Rightarrow \exists\, \gamma:\ L(\gamma)=d(p,q)$

Closure

Connection and distance are finally reconciled.

Printed pp. 151-192The Hopf-Rinow Theorem
Hopf and Rinow prove that geodesic completeness, metric completeness, and the Heine-Borel property all coincide for a connected Riemannian manifold. Crucially, any one of them guarantees that every pair of points is joined by a globally minimizing geodesic, closing the loop between connection and distance.
