Multilinear Maps

Linear in each slot

Freeze every argument but one; what remains is a linear map.

Bilinear case

Two vector inputs, one scalar output, additive and homogeneous in each.

Definition

$T(\alpha v + v', w) = \alpha\,T(v,w) + T(v',w)$

Why it matters

Separate linearity, not joint linearity, defines a tensor.

Printed pp. 391-406Multilinear Maps
A multilinear map is linear in each slot when the others are frozen. The bilinear case is the workhorse: feeding two vectors returns a scalar that respects scaling and addition separately in each argument. This separate-linearity is the structural fingerprint that distinguishes tensors from ordinary linear maps on a product space.

The Tensor Product

Linearize multilinearity

Replace a multilinear map by a single linear map on $V \otimes W$.

Universal property

Every bilinear map factors uniquely through $\otimes$.

Decomposable elements

$(v \otimes w)(\xi,\eta) = \xi(v)\,\eta(w)$

Basis

$\{e_i \otimes e_j\}$ spans $V \otimes W$.

Printed pp. 391-406The Tensor Product
The tensor product packages a multilinear map as a single linear object on a larger space. Its universal property says every multilinear map factors uniquely through the canonical map into the tensor product, so building tensors and proving identities reduces to checking statements on simple decomposable elements.

Covariant and Contravariant Tensors

Covariant slots

Eat vectors; lower indices. The metric $g_{ij}$ is the model.

Contravariant slots

Eat covectors; upper indices. The inverse metric $g^{ij}$.

Type (k, l) tensor

$T : \underbrace{V^* \!\times\! \cdots}_{k}\times \underbrace{V \!\times\! \cdots}_{l} \to \mathbb{R}$

Mixed type

An endomorphism is a $(1,1)$-tensor $T^i{}_j$.

Printed pp. 391-406Covariant and Contravariant Tensors
A tensor of type two index slots takes copies of the tangent space and copies of its dual. Covariant slots eat vectors, contravariant slots eat covectors. The metric is the canonical covariant two-tensor, while the inverse metric is its contravariant partner, and mixed types interpolate between these two extremes.

The Tensor Bundle

Fiberwise tensors

Glue the tensor space $T^{(k,l)}_p M$ over each point $p$.

Smooth bundle

Local frames and coframes give component functions.

Printed pp. 391-406The Tensor Bundle
Assembling all tensors of a fixed type over every point of the manifold yields the tensor bundle, a smooth vector bundle whose fiber is the tensor space at that point. Local frames and coframes produce coordinate components, and transition functions act by the appropriate tensor power of the Jacobian.

Tensor Fields

Smooth section

A tensor chosen at each point, varying smoothly with $p$.

Components

A smoothly varying array $T^{i\cdots}_{j\cdots}(x)$ in coordinates.

Local expansion

$T = T_{ij}\,dx^i \otimes dx^j$

Smoothness test

Feed smooth fields; outputs are smooth functions.

Printed pp. 391-406Tensor Fields
A tensor field is a smooth section of a tensor bundle: a tensor chosen at each point that varies smoothly. In coordinates it is a smoothly varying array of component functions. Smoothness can be tested either by component functions or by feeding the field smooth vector and covector arguments.

Symmetric and Alternating Tensors

Symmetric

Unchanged by swapping arguments; carries quadratic forms like $g$.

Alternating

Flips sign under a transposition; vanishes on repeats.

Defining identities

$S(v,w)=S(w,v), \quad A(v,w)=-A(w,v)$

Consequence

$A(v,v)=0$ measures oriented volume.

Printed pp. 391-406Symmetric and Alternating Tensors
Covariant tensors split by their behavior under permuting arguments. Symmetric tensors are unchanged by swaps and carry quadratic forms like the metric. Alternating tensors flip sign under a transposition and vanish on repeated arguments, which is exactly the structure that measures oriented volume of parallelepipeds.

The Wedge Product

Antisymmetrize

The wedge $\alpha \wedge \beta$ is the alternating part of $\alpha \otimes \beta$.

Oriented area

$\alpha \wedge \beta$ measures the signed area of the spanned parallelogram.

Printed pp. 391-406The Wedge Product
The wedge product antisymmetrizes the tensor product, producing alternating tensors that are graded-commutative. Two covectors wedged together build the oriented area element of the parallelogram they span, and the determinant appears naturally as the coefficient relating wedge products of basis covectors.

Differential Forms

Alternating fields

A $k$-form is an alternating covariant $k$-tensor field.

Exterior algebra

Purpose

Forms are the objects you integrate over oriented submanifolds.

Printed pp. 391-406Differential Forms
A differential k-form is an alternating covariant k-tensor field. The forms of all degrees assemble into the exterior algebra bundle, and a one-form pairs with vectors to return functions. Forms are precisely the objects that can be integrated over oriented submanifolds, which motivates the entire calculus that follows.

The Exterior Derivative

Raises degree

$d$ sends $k$-forms to $(k{+}1)$-forms and extends $df$.

Nilpotent

$d \circ d = 0$ encodes commuting mixed partials.

Antiderivation

$d(\alpha \wedge \beta) = d\alpha \wedge \beta + (-1)^{p}\,\alpha \wedge d\beta$

Natural

Commutes with pullback; basis of de Rham cohomology.

Printed pp. 391-406The Exterior Derivative
The exterior derivative raises form degree by one, generalizes the differential of a function, and squares to zero. It is the unique antiderivation extending the gradient that is natural under pullback. The identity d composed with d equals zero encodes that mixed partials commute and underlies de Rham cohomology.

Tensor Contraction and Index Notation

Pair and sum

Contract one upper with one lower slot, lowering type by $(1,1)$.

Einstein convention

A repeated upper/lower index is implicitly summed.

Contraction

$\operatorname{tr} T = T^i{}_i, \qquad (\operatorname{div} X) = \nabla_i X^i$

Examples

Trace of an endomorphism; divergence of a field.

Printed pp. 391-406Tensor Contraction and Index Notation
Contraction pairs one contravariant slot with one covariant slot and sums, lowering type by one in each. In index notation a repeated upper and lower index is summed by the Einstein convention. The trace of an endomorphism and the divergence of a tensor field are the most familiar contractions in practice.
