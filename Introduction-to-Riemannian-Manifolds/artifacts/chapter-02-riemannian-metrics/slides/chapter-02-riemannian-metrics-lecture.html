Riemannian Metrics

A smoothly varying inner product: where measurement begins

The object

A metric $g$ assigns an inner product $g_p$ to every tangent space $T_pM$.

What it buys us

Norms of vectors, angles, lengths of curves, and a distance function.

Why it matters

It is the structure that promotes a smooth manifold to a geometric one.

Printed pp. 9-54Riemannian Metrics
This chapter, printed pages nine through fifty-four, installs the single piece of structure that turns a bare smooth manifold into a place where we measure. A Riemannian metric is a smoothly varying inner product, and from it lengths, angles, and distance all follow naturally.

Definition of a Metric

Symmetric 2-tensor

$g$ is a smooth field of bilinear forms on tangent spaces.

Symmetric

$g_p(v,w)=g_p(w,v)$ for all $v,w\in T_pM$.

Positive definite

$g_p(v,v)>0$ whenever $v\neq 0$.

Definition

$g\in\Gamma\big(S^2 T^*M\big),\quad g_p:T_pM\times T_pM\to\mathbb{R}$

Smoothness

For smooth $X,Y$, the function $p\mapsto g_p(X_p,Y_p)$ is smooth on $M$.

Printed pp. 9-54Definition of a Metric
A Riemannian metric is a smooth symmetric two-tensor field that is positive definite at every point. Symmetry and positive definiteness make each fiber a genuine inner product, while smoothness ties neighboring inner products together so that everything we build varies differentiably across the manifold.

Components in Coordinates

Component matrix

$g_{ij}=g\!\left(\partial_i,\partial_j\right)$ is symmetric and positive definite.

Local form

$g = g_{ij}\,dx^i\otimes dx^j$ in any chart.

Euclidean model

$\bar g = \delta_{ij}\,dx^i\,dx^j = (dx^1)^2+\cdots+(dx^n)^2$

Change of chart

$g_{kl}' = \dfrac{\partial x^i}{\partial y^k}\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial y^l}\,g_{ij}$ keeps it a tensor.

Printed pp. 9-54Components in Coordinates
In any chart the metric is recorded by the symmetric matrix of components g sub i j, the inner products of coordinate vector fields. Positive definiteness of this matrix at each point is exactly the local condition we verify, and the matrix changes by the usual tensor law between charts.

Reading the Metric: Inner-Product Ellipses

Unit ball

$B_p=\{\,v\in T_pM : g_p(v,v)=1\,\}$

Intuition

A long ellipse means short vectors are "cheap" in that direction; a small ellipse means the metric stretches distances there.

Smooth field

The ellipses deform continuously, encoding how geometry bends across the manifold.

Printed pp. 9-54Inner-Product Ellipses
Visualize the metric as a field of unit balls, one ellipse per point describing which tangent vectors have length one. Where the ellipse is small the metric is large and the manifold feels stretched; the smooth deformation of these ellipses is the geometry the metric encodes.

Norm and Length of Curves

Induced norm

$|v|_g=\sqrt{g_p(v,v)}$ measures the size of a tangent vector.

Length functional

Integrate the speed of $\gamma$ along the parameter interval.

Reparametrization

Length depends only on the image, not the speed of traversal.

Length of a curve

$L_g(\gamma)=\displaystyle\int_a^b \big|\gamma'(t)\big|_g\,dt$

Printed pp. 9-54Norm and Length of Curves
The metric gives each tangent vector a norm, and integrating the speed of a curve against that norm produces its length. This length is independent of how the curve is parametrized, so it is a genuine geometric quantity attached to the image of the path, not to its clock.

Musical Isomorphisms: Sharp and Flat

Lowering (flat)

$v^\flat = g(v,\cdot)$ sends a vector to a covector.

Raising (sharp)

$\omega^\sharp$ is the vector with $g(\omega^\sharp,\cdot)=\omega$.

Gradient

$\operatorname{grad} f = (df)^\sharp$, the metric dual of the differential.

In components

$(v^\flat)_i = g_{ij}v^j,\qquad (\omega^\sharp)^i = g^{ij}\omega_j$

Printed pp. 9-54Musical Isomorphisms
Positive definiteness lets the metric convert vectors into covectors and back. Lowering an index with flat sends a vector field to a one-form; raising with sharp inverts it. These musical isomorphisms identify the tangent and cotangent bundles and define the gradient of a function.

Pullback Metrics

Construction

Pull a target metric back through the differential of a smooth map.

Immersions

If $F$ is an immersion, $F^*g$ stays positive definite.

Submanifolds

The induced metric on a submanifold is the pullback of the inclusion.

Definition

$(F^*g)_p(v,w) = g_{F(p)}\big(dF_p v,\, dF_p w\big)$

Example

A surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ inherits the first fundamental form from the round dot product of the ambient space.

Printed pp. 9-54Pullback Metrics
A smooth map with injective differential pulls a metric back to its domain by feeding pushed-forward vectors into the target inner product. When the map is an immersion the pullback stays positive definite, which is exactly how submanifolds inherit their induced Riemannian metric from an ambient space.

Isometries and Local Isometries

Isometry

A diffeomorphism $\varphi$ with $\varphi^*\tilde g = g$.

Preserves geometry

Lengths, angles, and distances are all carried across unchanged.

Local isometry

A map that is an isometry onto a neighborhood of each point.

Equivalence

$(M,g)\cong(\tilde M,\tilde g)\iff \exists\,\varphi:\varphi^*\tilde g=g$

Isometry group

The isometries of $(M,g)$ form a group $\operatorname{Iso}(M,g)$; classifying it is a recurring goal.

Printed pp. 9-54Isometries
An isometry is a diffeomorphism whose pullback returns the original metric, so it preserves all lengths and angles. Local isometries relax this to neighborhoods. Isometries form a group, and deciding when two Riemannian manifolds are isometric is a central problem the rest of the book attacks.

Existence: Every Manifold Has a Metric

Local metrics

On each chart, transport the Euclidean metric back to $M$.

Glue smoothly

Combine the local metrics with a subordinate partition of unity.

Stays definite

A convex sum of positive-definite forms is positive definite.

Patched metric

$g = \displaystyle\sum_\alpha \psi_\alpha\, g_\alpha,\qquad \sum_\alpha \psi_\alpha = 1$

Printed pp. 9-54Existence by Partitions of Unity
Every smooth manifold admits a Riemannian metric. Choose Euclidean metrics in coordinate patches, then glue them with a partition of unity subordinate to the cover. The convex combination of positive-definite forms stays positive definite, so the patched object is a global metric.

The Riemannian Distance Function

Definition

Infimum of lengths over all admissible curves joining $p$ to $q$.

Metric space axioms

Symmetry, the triangle inequality, and $d(p,q)=0\Leftrightarrow p=q$.

Distance

$d_g(p,q)=\inf\big\{\,L_g(\gamma):\gamma\text{ joins }p,q\,\big\}$

Printed pp. 9-54Riemannian Distance
Define the distance between two points as the infimum of lengths of admissible curves joining them. This distance is symmetric, satisfies the triangle inequality, and vanishes only on the diagonal, making a connected Riemannian manifold into a genuine metric space.

The Metric Topology Theorem

Two topologies

The manifold topology and the topology of metric balls $B_r(p)$.

They agree

The distance $d_g$ induces exactly the original topology of $M$.

Theorem

$\tau_{d_g} = \tau_M$ on a connected manifold

Consequence

Analytic distance arguments and smooth-structure arguments see the same open sets, so limits and continuity transfer freely.

Printed pp. 9-54The Metric Topology
A key theorem says the topology induced by the Riemannian distance agrees with the original manifold topology. So no new open sets appear and none are lost: the analytic distance and the smooth structure see the same neighborhoods, which keeps later limit arguments honest and reliable.

Constructions: Products and Warped Products

Product metric

$g = g_1 \oplus g_2$ on $M_1\times M_2$ splits along factors.

Warped product

Scale the second factor by a positive function $f$ on the first.

Models

Surfaces of revolution and cosmological spacetimes.

Warped product

$g = g_1 \oplus f^2\, g_2,\qquad f\in C^\infty(M_1),\ f>0$

Printed pp. 9-54Product and Warped Metrics
New metrics arise from old ones. A product manifold carries the direct sum of factor metrics; a warped product scales one factor by a positive function on the other. Warped products model surfaces of revolution and cosmological spacetimes with a single flexible construction.
