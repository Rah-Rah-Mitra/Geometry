Chapter 2Riemannian Metrics
Frame the chapter as the moment a smooth manifold becomes a measurable space. Until a metric is chosen we have no lengths, no angles, no distances, and no notion of orthogonality. The plan today is to define the metric tensor cleanly, build a small library of standard examples, and then look at the structural operations: pullback, conformal change, products, and the indefinite analogue. By the end students should know exactly which geometric quantities the metric controls.

The Riemannian Metric: A Pointwise Inner Product

Definition

A Riemannian metric on a smooth manifold M is a smooth symmetric positive-definite (0,2)-tensor field g: at each point p, g_p is an inner product on T_pM.

Local Coordinates

g = g_{ij}\, dx^i \otimes dx^j, \qquad g_{ij} = g(\partial_i, \partial_j)

The matrix (g_{ij}) is symmetric and positive-definite at every point.

What Smoothness Buys

The functions g_{ij} are C^\infty; this is what lets us differentiate the metric and define curvature later.

One inner product per point, varying smoothlySlide 2 / 10
Stress the parallel with linear algebra: at a single point the metric is just an inner product, and all the familiar consequences apply. The novelty is the word smoothly. The matrix entries depend on the point, and they vary as smooth functions in any chart. Positive definiteness must hold at every point, which is the property that fails for pseudo-Riemannian metrics later in the chapter. Mention that pure symmetry without positivity gives a much weaker structure.

Three Foundational Examples

Euclidean

\bar g = \delta_{ij}\, dx^i\, dx^j

The flat metric on \mathbb{R}^n. All g_{ij} are constant; the components do not depend on the point.

Round Sphere

\mathring g\;\text{on}\;S^n(R)

Inherited from \mathbb{R}^{n+1}. In spherical coordinates on S^2(R): R^2(d\varphi^2 + \sin^2\!\varphi\, d\theta^2).

Induced Submanifold

g = \iota^* \bar g

If \iota: N \hookrightarrow (M,\bar g) is an immersion, restricting \bar g to tangent vectors of N gives a Riemannian metric on N.

Flat, round, induced: the working laboratorySlide 3 / 10
Anchor everything to these three families. The Euclidean metric is the constant case, where curvature computations vanish identically and everything reduces to linear algebra. The round sphere is the cleanest case of nontrivial constant curvature. The induced metric construction is the most important practically, because it produces almost every example we ever draw, surfaces, curves, level sets, and submanifolds of model spaces. Make sure students see that an inner product on the ambient space gives an inner product on every tangent subspace.

Pullback Metrics and Isometries

Pullback

For F: N \to M smooth and g a metric on M,

(F^*g)_p(u,v) = g_{F(p)}\bigl(dF_p u,\, dF_p v\bigr).

Positive-definite precisely when dF_p is injective at every p (immersion).

Isometry

A diffeomorphism F: (N,h) \to (M,g) with F^*g = h. Local version: F is a local isometry when this holds on a neighborhood of every point.

Why It Matters

Isometries are the symmetries of Riemannian geometry. Anything intrinsic, length, angle, curvature, geodesic, must agree under pullback by an isometry.

Pullback transports a metric backward along a mapSlide 4 / 10
The pullback construction is the engine behind almost every metric we will encounter outside of model spaces. The differential transports tangent vectors forward; the metric is then measured downstream and copied back. The positivity condition is exactly the immersion condition, which explains why local diffeomorphisms automatically pull metrics back to metrics. Isometries are the next abstraction: they are the morphisms that respect everything Riemannian geometry can see.

Length of Curves and the Distance Function

L_g(\gamma) = \int_a^b \sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(\dot\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t)\bigr)}\; dt

Riemannian Distance

d_g(p,q) = \inf\bigl\{\, L_g(\gamma) : \gamma\;\text{piecewise smooth from}\;p\;\text{to}\;q\,\bigr\}

Metric Space Theorem

For a connected Riemannian manifold, d_g is a metric on M in the standard topological sense, and the induced topology coincides with the manifold topology.

The metric integrates to a distanceSlide 5 / 10
This is the bridge from infinitesimal geometry to global geometry. The length functional is well defined because positivity of the metric ensures the integrand is real. The distance is then an infimum over all admissible curves, and the surprise is that this infimum recovers a genuine metric space structure compatible with the smooth topology. Caution students: the infimum may not be attained without a completeness hypothesis, which is the content of the Hopf-Rinow theorem later.

Conformal Change: Rescaling Without Rotating

Conformal Metric

\tilde g = e^{2f}\, g, \qquad f \in C^\infty(M)

Two metrics are conformal when one is a positive smooth multiple of the other.

What Is Preserved

Angles between tangent vectors are unchanged. Ratios of lengths in different directions are unchanged at each point.

What Is Not Preserved

Lengths, distances, geodesics, and curvature generally change. The Levi-Civita connection is genuinely different.

Conformal: angle-preserving rescalingSlide 6 / 10
Conformal changes are the simplest deformation of a metric that still does something nontrivial. The pointwise scalar factor stretches all directions equally, so it preserves the angle measurement but distorts distance. This is also how the hyperbolic disk model is built from the Euclidean disk; the formula uses a conformal factor that blows up at the boundary. Set up the expectation that curvature will transform by a second-order PDE involving the scalar factor.

Products and Warped Products

Riemannian Product

(M_1 \times M_2,\; g_1 \oplus g_2)

Tangent spaces split as direct sums; the cross terms in the metric are zero. Examples: cylinders S^1 \times \mathbb{R}, flat tori \mathbb{R}^n / \Lambda.

Warped Product

M_1 \times_\varphi M_2: \quad g = g_1 \oplus \varphi^2 g_2

The fiber metric g_2 is rescaled by a positive smooth function \varphi: M_1 \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Building new metrics from oldSlide 7 / 10
Products and warped products are the workhorses of constructing nontrivial examples. The direct sum metric is the obvious one and corresponds to placing the two factors at right angles. Warped products allow one factor to shrink or expand as you move in the other, and they capture surfaces of revolution and the standard models of constant curvature in geodesic polar coordinates. Encourage students to recognize the warping factor when reading a coordinate-form metric in the wild.

Pseudo-Riemannian Metrics: Dropping Positivity

Definition

A pseudo-Riemannian metric is a smooth symmetric nondegenerate (0,2)-tensor field. The signature (p,q) is constant on a connected manifold.

Minkowski Model

\eta = -dt^2 + dx_1^2 + \cdots + dx_n^2

Signature (1,n): timelike vectors satisfy \eta(v,v)<0, spacelike >0, null =0.

What Breaks

No canonical distance function. Length of timelike curves measures proper time, not metric distance. Causal cones replace the round unit sphere.

Indefinite signature opens spacetime geometrySlide 8 / 10
Replace positive definiteness with mere nondegeneracy and the geometry changes character. Vectors are now classified by causal type, and the unit “sphere” of the metric is a pair of hyperboloids cut out by a sign condition. The Minkowski model is the prototype, and almost every definition and theorem in Riemannian geometry has a pseudo-Riemannian analogue if one is careful about sign conventions. Note that distance no longer exists in the usual sense; one integrates proper time along timelike curves instead.

Generalizations Beyond the Riemannian Setting

Sub-Riemannian

An inner product is given only on a smooth distribution \mathcal{D} \subset TM. Length and distance still make sense, but only along horizontal curves.

Finsler

Replace the inner product norm by a smooth fiberwise norm F: TM \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}. Asymmetric and direction-dependent length functionals are allowed.

Pseudo / Lorentzian

Drop positivity, keep nondegeneracy. Specialized for general relativity when signature is (1,n).

Where the Riemannian Hypothesis Pays Off

The Riemannian assumption supplies a clean distance, a complete metric space topology, a unique torsion-free metric connection, and a robust comparison theory. Each generalization above relaxes one of these payoffs and replaces it with a more delicate substitute.

One axiom relaxed, one structure lostSlide 9 / 10
End the technical content by placing the Riemannian setting inside a larger landscape. Sub-Riemannian geometry restricts the directions in which the metric is even defined and is the natural framework for control theory and the Heisenberg group. Finsler geometry replaces inner products with fiberwise norms and allows direction-dependent speed. The pseudo-Riemannian world is essential for physics. Each of these generalizations sacrifices one of the clean Riemannian payoffs, which makes those payoffs easier to appreciate.

Diagnostic Question

When a new metric appears, ask: is it positive-definite, what construction produced it (pullback, conformal, warped, induced), and which symmetries (isometries) preserve it?

Chapter 2 recapSlide 10 / 10
Close by emphasizing that the metric tensor is the smallest data set that turns a smooth manifold into a geometry. Connections, geodesics, and curvature in later chapters all take the metric as input. The operations introduced today, induced, pullback, conformal, product, warped, will recur constantly. The diagnostic question at the bottom is the habit students should carry forward: identify the construction, identify the signature, identify the isometry group, and only then start computing.
