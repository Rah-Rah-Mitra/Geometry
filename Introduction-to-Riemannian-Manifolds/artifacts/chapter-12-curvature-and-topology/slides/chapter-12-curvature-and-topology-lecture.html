Cartan-Hadamard Theorem

Theorem

If $M$ is complete, simply connected, and $K \le 0$, then $\exp_p:T_pM \to M$ is a diffeomorphism.

Consequence

$M \approx \mathbb{R}^n$; no conjugate points.

Global chart

$M \cong T_pM \cong \mathbb{R}^n$

Uniqueness

One minimizing geodesic between any two points.

Printed pp. 345-370Cartan-Hadamard Theorem
For a complete, simply connected manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature, the exponential map at any point is a diffeomorphism onto the whole manifold. Such a Hadamard manifold is therefore diffeomorphic to Euclidean space, has no conjugate points, and every two points are joined by a unique minimizing geodesic.

Why the Exponential Map Wins

Jacobi estimate

$K \le 0 \;\Rightarrow\; |J(t)| \ge |J'(0)|\,t$

Lift

Local diffeo + completeness $\Rightarrow$ covering map.

Printed pp. 345-370Why the Exponential Map Wins
Nonpositive curvature makes Jacobi fields grow at least linearly, so they never vanish away from the origin and conjugate points cannot occur. The exponential map is then a local diffeomorphism everywhere; completeness and simple connectivity upgrade this to a global covering, hence a diffeomorphism onto the manifold.

Topology of Nonpositive Curvature

Aspherical

$\pi_k(M) = 0$ for all $k \ge 2$.

Classifying space

$M$ is a $K(\pi_1, 1)$; homotopy type is $\pi_1$.

Torsion-free

$\pi_1(M)\ \text{has no nontrivial finite subgroup}$

Obstruction

Spheres admit no complete $K \le 0$ metric.

Printed pp. 345-370Topology of Nonpositive Curvature
Because a complete nonpositively curved manifold has Euclidean universal cover, it is aspherical: all higher homotopy groups vanish. Its fundamental group carries the entire homotopy type, the manifold is a classifying space, and torsion in that group is forbidden, sharply restricting which spaces admit such metrics.

Constant Curvature and Killing-Hopf

Cartan local-to-global

Complete, simply connected manifolds with the same constant $K$ are isometric.

Killing-Hopf

Every space form $= \tilde{M}/\Gamma$, $\Gamma$ discrete, free.

Classification

$M = \mathbb{S}^n/\Gamma,\ \mathbb{R}^n/\Gamma,\ \text{or}\ \mathbb{H}^n/\Gamma$

Meaning

Curvature constant $\Rightarrow$ cover is a model space.

Printed pp. 345-370Constant Curvature and Killing-Hopf
Cartan's local-to-global theorem shows that complete simply connected manifolds of the same constant curvature are isometric. The Killing-Hopf theorem then classifies every complete constant-curvature manifold as a quotient of the sphere, Euclidean space, or hyperbolic space by a discrete group of isometries acting freely.

Bonnet-Myers Theorem

Theorem

If $\mathrm{Ric} \ge (n-1)\kappa\,g$ with $\kappa>0$, then $M$ is compact and $\pi_1(M)$ is finite.

Diameter bound

Conjugate points appear within a fixed length.

Diameter estimate

$\operatorname{diam}(M) \le \dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa}}$

Cover

Universal cover is also compact $\Rightarrow$ finite $\pi_1$.

Printed pp. 345-370Bonnet-Myers Theorem
If the Ricci curvature is bounded below by a positive constant, Myers theorem bounds the diameter and forces the manifold to be compact with finite fundamental group. Positive Ricci curvature makes geodesics develop conjugate points within a fixed length, so no geodesic can minimize beyond the diameter bound.

Synge and Orientability

Even dimension

Orientable, compact, $K>0$ $\Rightarrow$ simply connected.

Odd dimension

Compact, $K>0$ $\Rightarrow$ orientable.

Tool

$I''(0) = -\!\int \langle R(\dot\gamma,J)\dot\gamma, J\rangle\,dt < 0$

Idea

A parallel field shortens a closed geodesic: contradiction.

Printed pp. 345-370Synge and Orientability
Synge's theorem refines the positive-curvature story using the second variation along closed geodesics. An even-dimensional, orientable, compact manifold of positive sectional curvature is simply connected, while in odd dimensions positive curvature forces orientability, illustrating how parity of dimension enters global conclusions.

The Sphere Theorem

Quarter pinching

$\tfrac14 < K \le 1 \;\Rightarrow\; M \approx_{\text{homeo}} \mathbb{S}^n$

Sharpness

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ has $K\in[\tfrac14,1]$: the bound is optimal.

Printed pp. 345-370The Sphere Theorem
The quarter-pinched sphere theorem states that a complete simply connected manifold whose sectional curvature lies in the half-open interval one-quarter to one is homeomorphic to a sphere. The pinching constant one-quarter is sharp: complex projective space with curvature in the closed interval shows the bound cannot be relaxed.

Curvature Obstructions and Recap

Torus

No positive Ricci metric (Bonnet-Myers).

Sphere

No nonpositive curvature metric (Cartan-Hadamard).

Space forms

Constant $K$ rigidly fixes the cover.

The dictionary

$K \le 0:\ \mathbb{R}^n\ \text{cover}\quad K>0:\ \text{compact}$

Takeaway

Infinitesimal bending becomes global classification.

Printed pp. 345-370Curvature Obstructions and Recap
We close by reading the theorems as obstructions: a torus admits no positive Ricci metric by Bonnet-Myers, and a sphere admits no nonpositive curvature metric by Cartan-Hadamard. Curvature sign therefore obstructs topology, and these capstone results convert infinitesimal bending into rigid global classification.
