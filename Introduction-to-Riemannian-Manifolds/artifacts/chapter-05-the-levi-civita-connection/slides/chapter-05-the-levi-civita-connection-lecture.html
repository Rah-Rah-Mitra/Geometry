Connections: A Reminder

Tensorial slot

$\nabla_{fX}Y = f\,\nabla_X Y$ is linear over functions in $X$.

Leibniz slot

$\nabla_X(fY)=(Xf)Y + f\,\nabla_X Y$ in $Y$.

Connection

$\nabla:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)$

Non-uniqueness

The space of connections is an affine space; many exist before we impose conditions.

Printed pp. 115-150Connections: A Reminder
A connection lets us differentiate one vector field along another while staying inside the tangent bundle. Recall its tensoriality in the first slot and Leibniz behaviour in the second, and note that without extra conditions there are infinitely many connections on a single smooth manifold.

Condition I: Metric Compatibility

Product rule

$X\langle Y,Z\rangle = \langle\nabla_X Y,Z\rangle + \langle Y,\nabla_X Z\rangle$

Equivalent form

Parallel transport along any curve is a linear isometry of tangent spaces.

Printed pp. 115-150Condition I: Metric Compatibility
Metric compatibility says the connection commutes with the inner product through a product rule. Equivalently parallel transport along any curve is a linear isometry, so transported frames stay orthonormal and angles and lengths are preserved exactly as the vectors are carried along.

Condition II: Torsion-Freeness

Torsion tensor

$\tau(X,Y)=\nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X - [X,Y]$

Symmetric connection

$\tau\equiv 0$ means $\nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X = [X,Y]$.

Coordinate view

In a chart, symmetry says $\Gamma^k_{ij}=\Gamma^k_{ji}$: the lower indices commute.

Why it matters

Second covariant derivatives of functions stay symmetric, mirroring ordinary calculus.

Printed pp. 115-150Condition II: Torsion-Freeness
The torsion tensor measures the failure of the connection to be symmetric in its two arguments. A symmetric, or torsion-free, connection satisfies the bracket identity, so mixed covariant derivatives differ only by the Lie bracket and second derivatives of functions stay symmetric.

Fundamental Theorem of Riemannian Geometry

Theorem

On $(M,g)$ there is a unique connection $\nabla$ that is metric compatible and torsion-free.

Name

This $\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita connection of $g$.

Printed pp. 115-150Fundamental Theorem of Riemannian Geometry
The fundamental theorem of Riemannian geometry asserts that on every Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian manifold there exists one and only one connection that is simultaneously metric compatible and torsion-free. This unique object is named the Levi-Civita connection and underlies all later curvature theory.

The Koszul Formula

Koszul formula

$2\langle\nabla_X Y,Z\rangle = X\langle Y,Z\rangle + Y\langle Z,X\rangle - Z\langle X,Y\rangle$

$+\,\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle - \langle[Y,Z],X\rangle + \langle[Z,X],Y\rangle$

Uniqueness

The right side never mentions $\nabla$, so the inner products fix $\nabla_X Y$ completely.

Existence

Define $\nabla$ by this formula; one checks it is a connection that is compatible and symmetric.

Printed pp. 115-150The Koszul Formula
The Koszul formula expresses the inner product of the covariant derivative entirely through the metric and Lie brackets. Because its right side never mentions the connection, it both forces uniqueness and constructs existence, since the right side defines a genuine connection satisfying both required conditions.

Christoffel Symbols from the Metric

Coordinate formula

$\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{k\ell}\big(\partial_i g_{j\ell} + \partial_j g_{i\ell} - \partial_\ell g_{ij}\big)$

Not a tensor

$\Gamma^k_{ij}$ transforms with an extra inhomogeneous term under coordinate changes.

Built from first derivatives

Only $\partial g$ and the inverse metric $g^{k\ell}$ enter the formula.

Covariant derivative

$\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j = \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\partial_k$ recovers $\nabla$ in the chart.

Printed pp. 115-150Christoffel Symbols from the Metric
In a coordinate chart the Koszul formula collapses to the classical Christoffel symbol formula built from first derivatives of the metric components and the inverse metric. These symbols are not tensors, yet they encode the connection completely and let us compute covariant derivatives explicitly.

The Tangential Connection

Projection

$\nabla^\top_X Y = \pi^\top\big(\overline{\nabla}_X Y\big)$

By uniqueness

$\nabla^\top$ is compatible and symmetric, so it equals the intrinsic Levi-Civita connection.

Printed pp. 115-150The Tangential Connection
For a submanifold of Euclidean space the tangential connection projects the ambient flat derivative back onto the tangent space. This projected operation is metric compatible and symmetric, so by uniqueness it must equal the intrinsic Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric.

Naturality under Isometries

Intertwining

$\varphi_*\big(\nabla_X Y\big) = \tilde\nabla_{\varphi_* X}\,\varphi_* Y$

Consequence

Every invariant built from $\nabla$ is automatically an isometry invariant.

Metric determines $\nabla$

Koszul depends only on $g$, so isometries preserve the connection.

Local isometries too

The intertwining holds for any local isometry between Riemannian manifolds.

Printed pp. 115-150Naturality under Isometries
The Levi-Civita connection is determined by the metric alone, so any isometry carries one chapter's connection to another. Pushforward through an isometry intertwines the covariant derivatives, which is why every metric invariant we build from this connection is automatically an isometry invariant.

Common Pitfalls

Symbols are not tensors

You cannot conclude $\Gamma=0$ in one chart implies $\Gamma=0$ everywhere.

Two indices, two roles

Symmetry is in the lower pair $ij$; compatibility is about the metric $g$.

Both conditions needed

Either condition alone leaves many connections; together they pin down one.

Sign and ordering

Watch the cyclic sign pattern in Koszul; a flipped term breaks compatibility.

Printed pp. 115-150Common Pitfalls
Students often forget that Christoffel symbols are not tensors and that torsion-freeness is a choice, not an automatic property. We stress that symmetry refers to the lower indices, that compatibility is about the metric, and that both are needed for the connection to be Levi-Civita.
