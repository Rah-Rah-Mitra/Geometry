Chapter 5The Levi-Civita Connection
Open the chapter by reminding students that they already know what a connection is in the abstract, but until now they had no preferred connection on a Riemannian manifold. The point of this chapter is to single out exactly one connection from the Riemannian metric and then to use it as the engine that drives geodesics, the exponential map, normal neighborhoods, and Fermi coordinates. Frame the whole arc as: metric chooses connection, connection chooses geodesics, geodesics chart the neighborhood.

The Tangential Connection on Submanifolds

Ambient Derivative

For a vector field Y on the submanifold, extend, take the Euclidean directional derivative D_X Y, then project.

Tangential Connection

(∇^⊤_X Y)_p = π^⊤_p (D_X Y)_p

The tangential part is independent of the chosen extension and defines a connection on M.

Why It Matters

This concrete construction motivates the abstract notion and predicts the answers we will demand on any Riemannian manifold.

Submanifold geometry as the model caseSlide 2 / 10
Walk through the construction explicitly. We sit inside a Euclidean ambient space, where directional differentiation is unambiguous. The trouble is that the derivative of a tangent vector field is no longer tangent to the submanifold, so we project orthogonally back to the tangent space. Emphasize two facts before moving on: the projection is metric-compatible because we are using the ambient inner product, and the result is symmetric because Euclidean differentiation is symmetric on coordinate vector fields. These two properties will reappear as the defining axioms of the Levi-Civita connection in the next slide.

Fundamental Theorem of Riemannian Geometry

Statement

On every Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian manifold (M,g) there exists a unique connection ∇ that is

  • compatible with the metric: ∇ g = 0
  • torsion-free: ∇_X Y − ∇_Y X = [X,Y]

Metric Compatibility

X⟨Y,Z⟩ = ⟨∇_X Y, Z⟩ + ⟨Y, ∇_X Z⟩

Inner products are preserved under parallel transport.

Symmetry vs Torsion-Free

On a coordinate frame these read ∇_{∂_i} ∂_j = ∇_{∂_j} ∂_i, hence Γ^k_{ij} = Γ^k_{ji}.

Two axioms pin down exactly one connectionSlide 3 / 10
This is the central theorem of the chapter. Present it as a uniqueness-and-existence package: uniqueness comes first because it is forced by an algebraic manipulation, and existence comes second because we can read the Koszul formula as a definition. Stress the geometric reading of metric compatibility, namely that inner products do not drift under parallel transport, and the geometric reading of being torsion-free, namely that the connection does not twist coordinate frames. Both axioms are independent and together they leave no freedom.

The Koszul Formula Forces Uniqueness

2⟨∇_X Y, Z⟩ = X⟨Y,Z⟩ + Y⟨Z,X⟩ − Z⟨X,Y⟩ + ⟨[X,Y],Z⟩ − ⟨[Y,Z],X⟩ + ⟨[X,Z],Y⟩

Step 1

Expand X⟨Y,Z⟩, Y⟨Z,X⟩, and Z⟨X,Y⟩ with metric compatibility.

Step 2

Use torsion-freeness to replace each ∇_A B − ∇_B A by the bracket [A,B].

Step 3

Add the first two, subtract the third; isolate the unknown ⟨∇_X Y, Z⟩.

Reading the Identity

The right side uses only the metric and the bracket. Since Z is arbitrary, the inner product determines ∇_X Y pointwise. Existence follows by reversing the argument and checking the two axioms hold.

Koszul: three metric derivatives plus three bracketsSlide 4 / 10
The Koszul formula is the workhorse of the existence-uniqueness proof. Walk students through the cyclic structure: the three permutations of X, Y, Z appear once each in the metric derivatives, and the three pairwise brackets appear once each among the structure terms. Once a student sees that the right-hand side depends only on the metric and the Lie bracket, they understand why the connection is forced. Mention that this is also a recipe: in any concrete chart, plug in coordinate frames and the formula becomes the Christoffel formula in the next slide.

Christoffel Symbols in Terms of the Metric

Γ^k_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2} g^{kl}\!\left( ∂_i g_{jl} + ∂_j g_{il} − ∂_l g_{ij} \right)

How It Is Derived

Take X=∂_i, Y=∂_j, Z=∂_l in the Koszul formula. Coordinate brackets vanish, leaving only metric derivatives.

Bookkeeping Reminders

  • Symmetric in lower indices: Γ^k_{ij} = Γ^k_{ji}.
  • Not the components of any tensor.
  • Vanish at the origin of any normal chart.
Coordinate face of the Levi-Civita connectionSlide 5 / 10
This is the computational face of the theorem. The Christoffel symbols are not tensorial, but they are the only thing one has to differentiate vector fields in coordinates. Make sure students understand that the symmetry of the lower indices is equivalent to the torsion-free axiom, not an extra assumption. Mention that the same expression will reappear in geodesic equations, in the formula for the exponential map, and in normal coordinate computations later in the chapter.

The Exponential Map at a Point

Definition

exp_p(v) = γ_v(1)

where γ_v is the unique geodesic with γ_v(0)=p and γ'_v(0)=v.

Local Behavior

Smooth on its open domain E ⊂ TM; the differential at the origin is the identity, so exp_p is a local diffeomorphism near 0 ∈ T_pM.

Rescaling Law

exp_p(tv) = γ_v(t)

One geodesic equation, one family of evaluations.

Tangent vectors become geodesic endpointsSlide 6 / 10
The exponential map is the geometric upgrade of the geodesic ODE. Every tangent vector at p selects one geodesic and the map records its position one unit of time later. Highlight the local diffeomorphism statement: at the origin the derivative is the identity, so a neighborhood of zero in the tangent space is identified with a neighborhood of p on the manifold. This is the doorway to normal coordinates and the linearization techniques used throughout Riemannian geometry.

Normal Neighborhoods and Normal Coordinates

Normal Neighborhood

An open U ∋ p on which exp_p is a diffeomorphism from a star-shaped V ⊂ T_pM. Such U always exists.

Normal Coordinates

Choose an orthonormal basis of T_pM. Pulling back through exp_p^{-1} gives a chart in which

  • g_{ij}(p) = δ_{ij}
  • Γ^k_{ij}(p) = 0
  • radial lines through 0 are geodesics

Inspection Output

The chapter notebook ships a normal-neighborhood lab whose stored sanity checks confirm geodesic-radial behavior on the model surface.

Caution

The vanishing of Christoffel symbols holds only at p itself; their derivatives there encode curvature.

Existing artifact: normal neighborhood inspectionSlide 7 / 10
Normal coordinates are the most useful coordinate system in Riemannian geometry because they let students compute as if working in flat Euclidean space at the basepoint while still tracking curvature in higher derivatives of the metric. Make sure they remember the trade-off: simplification holds only at p, not in a neighborhood, and the second derivatives of g at p store the curvature data. Connect this to the notebook lab, which numerically demonstrates that radial paths from the origin really are unit-speed geodesics.

Fermi Coordinates Along a Submanifold

Setup

Let S ⊂ M be an embedded submanifold. The normal exponential map sends (p, w) ∈ NS with w ⊥ T_pS to exp_p(w).

Fermi Chart

Choose tangential coordinates on S and orthonormal normal coordinates. The resulting chart makes S the zero set of the normal coordinates and the normal directions are geodesic.

Why It Helps

Metric and connection simplify along S: the metric is block-diagonal and Christoffel symbols with two normal indices vanish on S.

Generalize normal coordinates from a point to a submanifoldSlide 8 / 10
Fermi coordinates are the submanifold version of normal coordinates. Replace the basepoint p by an embedded submanifold S, and replace radial geodesics by geodesics that leave S orthogonally. Highlight the structural reward: the metric is block-diagonal along S, so the geometry of S inside M decouples cleanly into intrinsic and extrinsic parts. This is the toolkit students will use for tubular neighborhoods and for computing second fundamental forms in later chapters.

Geodesics in the Three Model Spaces

Euclidean R^n

γ(t) = p + tv

Straight affine lines; Christoffels vanish identically.

Round Sphere S^n

γ(t) = cos(t) p + sin(t) v

Great circles; constant speed; antipodal conjugate at t=π.

Hyperbolic H^n

Vertical lines and half-circles meeting the boundary at right angles (Poincaré model).

γ(t) = cosh(t) p + sinh(t) v

Common Pattern

All three families arise from a single ODE ∇_{γ'} γ' = 0 with the model-specific connection. The constants of integration parametrize choice of basepoint and direction.

One geodesic equation, three flavors of solutionsSlide 9 / 10
Use this slide as a comparative anchor. The same geodesic equation produces three qualitatively different families of curves once the metric is fixed. Stress that the formulas on the sphere and hyperbolic space involve circular and hyperbolic functions exactly because the curvatures are plus and minus one, while the Euclidean case has no curvature and so produces purely linear motion. Mention that these closed-form solutions are why the model spaces serve as comparison benchmarks throughout the rest of the book.
