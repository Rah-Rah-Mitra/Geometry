The Riemann Curvature Endomorphism

Definition

R(X,Y)Z = ∇X∇YZ − ∇Y∇XZ − ∇[X,Y]Z

A type (1,3) tensor field, tensorial in all three arguments.

Reading

Compares parallel transport along two routes around a small loop spanned by X and Y, applied to the test vector Z.

Curvature measures noncommutativity of covariant differentiationSlide 2 / 10
Lead with the operational definition. The two double-derivatives would commute in Euclidean space, and the bracket correction is exactly what makes the expression tensorial rather than merely a second-order differential operator. Check tensoriality slot by slot: the result depends only on the pointwise values of X, Y, and Z, not on extensions. The picture of a small parallelogram with output vector R(X,Y)Z is the holonomy intuition you will return to throughout the chapter.

Curvature as the Obstruction to Flatness

Local Flatness Theorem

A Riemannian manifold (M, g) is locally isometric to Euclidean space near every point if and only if its curvature tensor vanishes identically.

Proof Sketch

  • R = 0 lets a local parallel frame be integrated.
  • The frame yields coordinates in which g becomes the identity matrix.
  • Conversely, the Euclidean connection has zero curvature, and isometries pull this back.

Caveat

Flat does not mean globally Euclidean: a flat torus is locally Euclidean but not diffeomorphic to Rn.

Vanishing R as the precise local-Euclidean criterionSlide 3 / 10
This is the headline theorem of the chapter. Tie the two directions together: locally Euclidean immediately gives zero curvature because Christoffel symbols vanish in the standard chart, while zero curvature lets you integrate an orthonormal parallel frame and use it to construct flat coordinates. Mention the subtle point that local flatness is genuinely a pointwise tensorial condition, and emphasize that flat tori show why global structure is not determined by local geometry.

Algebraic Symmetries of R

Skew in the First Pair

Rabcd = − Rbacd

Built into the commutator definition.

Skew in the Second Pair

Rabcd = − Rabdc

Comes from metric compatibility of the connection.

Pair Interchange

Rabcd = Rcdab

Derived from the first three identities together.

First Bianchi Identity

Rabcd + Racdb + Radbc = 0

Cyclic sum on the last three indices vanishes.

Counting

On an n-dimensional manifold the symmetries cut the independent components of R from n4 down to n2(n2 − 1)/12. In dimension 2 there is exactly one independent component; in dimension 4 there are twenty.

Four identities organize every curvature computationSlide 4 / 10
Treat the four identities as a working checklist. The first two are direct, the pair interchange is the bookkeeping consequence, and the first Bianchi identity reflects torsion freeness. Spend a moment on dimension counting because students often suspect curvature has many more independent components than it does. Dimension two with one component motivates Gaussian curvature; dimension four with twenty components prepares the later Weyl-Ricci split.

Sectional Curvature K(σ)

Definition

K(σ) = R(v,w,w,v) / (|v|2|w|2 − ⟨v,w⟩2)

Independent of the basis chosen for σ.

Why It Helps

K is a function on the Grassmann bundle of 2-planes that determines R completely.

Special Values

Constant K equal to 0, +1, −1 recovers the Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic model geometries.

One scalar per 2-plane recovers the full curvature tensorSlide 5 / 10
Frame sectional curvature as the geometric reading of R. Independence of basis is worth verifying once, then trust it. The key fact is that knowing K on every 2-plane recovers R, so any theorem about curvature can in principle be tested by checking sectional curvatures. Connect the constant curvature values back to the model manifolds students have already met: Euclidean space, the round sphere, and hyperbolic space.

Second Bianchi and the Ricci Identities

Second Bianchi Identity

∇eRabcd + ∇cRabde + ∇dRabec = 0

A first-order differential identity, cyclic in the last three indices.

Ricci Identity

(∇2X,Y − ∇2Y,X) Z = R(X,Y) Z

The commutator of second covariant derivatives equals the curvature endomorphism.

Generalization to Tensors

For an arbitrary tensor field T, the commutator (∇2X,Y − ∇2Y,X) T acts on each slot of T by R(X,Y), with appropriate signs for covariant and contravariant indices. This is how curvature enters every integration by parts on a Riemannian manifold.

Differential identities propagate curvature through covariant calculusSlide 6 / 10
Second Bianchi is the differential analogue of the algebraic Bianchi identity and is the engine behind divergence formulas like the contracted Bianchi identity used to derive the Einstein tensor. The Ricci identity is the same statement read as a calculational rule: whenever you commute two covariant derivatives on any tensor, R appears. Note that proofs in later chapters often start with this single move.

Contractions: Ricci and Scalar Curvature

Ricci Tensor

Ric(Y, Z) = tr( X ↦ R(X, Y) Z )

The unique nontrivial trace of R, symmetric thanks to pair interchange.

Scalar Curvature

S = trg Ric

A single number per point; the average sectional curvature, up to a dimension factor.

Contracted Bianchi

div(Ric) = ½ dS

Two traces compress R into a symmetric tensor and a scalarSlide 7 / 10
Walk through the two contraction steps as a visible compression of information. The Ricci tensor is symmetric because of the pair interchange identity, and it carries the average sectional curvature in directions tangent to a given vector. Scalar curvature is the further metric trace. The contracted Bianchi identity is the technically important consequence: it forces the Einstein tensor Ric - (1/2) S g to be divergence free, which is why general relativity uses it on the left-hand side of Einstein's equation.

The Weyl Tensor: Conformally Invariant Curvature

Definition (n ≥ 4)

W = R − (Schouten part built from Ric and S)

Subtracting all traces from R leaves the trace-free part W.

Decomposition

R splits as scalar piece + trace-free Ricci piece + Weyl piece. Each summand is a tensor with the algebraic symmetries of R and is preserved by isometries.

Conformal Invariance

Under g → e2φ g, the (1,3) Weyl tensor Wabcd is unchanged. It detects whether a metric is locally conformal to the flat one.

R = scalar piece + trace-free Ricci piece + WeylSlide 8 / 10
Present the Weyl tensor as what remains of R after all traces are removed. In dimensions four and higher, vanishing Weyl is the precise local condition for the metric to be conformally flat, while in dimension three Weyl vanishes automatically and a different tensor takes over. Stress orthogonality of the three pieces under the natural inner product on curvature-type tensors: this is what justifies calling W the trace-free part.

How Curvature Transforms Under Conformal Change

g̃ = e2φ g  ⇒  R̃ = e2φ ( R − (∇2φ − dφ ⊗ dφ + ½ |∇φ|2 g) ⊙ g )

Rescaling Rule

The new Riemann tensor adds a Kulkarni-Nomizu correction built only from φ and its first two covariant derivatives.

Ricci Shift

Ric̃ = Ric − (n − 2)(∇2φ − dφ ⊗ dφ) − (Δφ + (n − 2)|∇φ|2) g

Linear in second derivatives of the conformal factor.

Invariance Anchor

W is unchanged: the entire correction lies in the scalar and trace-free Ricci parts of R.

Useful Consequences

  • Yamabe problem rephrases scalar curvature as a PDE in φ.
  • Conformal flatness reduces to W = 0 plus a Cotton tensor condition in dimension three.
  • Spheres and Euclidean space are conformally equivalent via stereographic projection.
Conformal change shifts traces of R but fixes the Weyl pieceSlide 9 / 10
This is the calculational climax of the chapter. Emphasize the pattern: only second-order derivatives of the conformal factor appear, and they enter as a Kulkarni-Nomizu product with g. The transformation formula for Ricci is the working tool for the Yamabe problem and prescribed scalar curvature problems. Always reconcile with the invariance of W: every term added under conformal change must live in the scalar plus trace-free Ricci subspace.
