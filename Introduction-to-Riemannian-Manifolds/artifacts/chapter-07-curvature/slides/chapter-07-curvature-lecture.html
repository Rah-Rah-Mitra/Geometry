Second Covariant Derivatives Do Not Commute

Euclidean baseline

For ordinary derivatives, $\partial_i\partial_j = \partial_j\partial_i$.

General connection

$\nabla_X\nabla_Y$ and $\nabla_Y\nabla_X$ differ; the gap is curvature.

Curvature endomorphism

$R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$

Bracket correction

The $\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ term removes the dependence on how $X,Y$ vary.

Printed pp. 193-224Second Covariant Derivatives Do Not Commute
On Euclidean space mixed partials commute, but a general connection breaks this symmetry. The curvature endomorphism measures the defect when the order of covariant differentiation is swapped along two directions, including a correction for the Lie bracket of the vector fields involved.

The Riemann Curvature Tensor

Tensoriality

$R$ is $C^\infty$-linear in $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ separately.

Pointwise

Its value depends only on the vectors at a point, not on extensions.

Covariant form

$Rm(X,Y,Z,W) = \langle R(X,Y)Z, W\rangle$

Components

$R\indices{^l_{ijk}}$ encode the tensor in any chart via the Christoffel symbols.

Printed pp. 193-224The Riemann Curvature Tensor
We package the commutator defect into a multilinear map that is tensorial in all three arguments. Tensoriality means curvature depends only on pointwise vector values, not on extensions, which is why it qualifies as a genuine tensor field rather than a differential operator.

Flat Manifolds

Definition

A manifold is flat when $R \equiv 0$ everywhere.

Characterization

Flat $\iff$ locally isometric to Euclidean space.

Local flatness theorem

$R = 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \exists\ \text{chart with } g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$

Reading

Curvature is exactly the obstruction to flat coordinates.

Printed pp. 193-224Flat Manifolds
A manifold is flat when its curvature tensor vanishes identically. The central theorem states that vanishing curvature is equivalent to being locally isometric to Euclidean space, so the Riemann tensor is exactly the obstruction to finding flat coordinate charts around each point.

Parallel Transport and Holonomy

Holonomy angle

$\Delta\theta \approx K \cdot \text{Area}(\text{loop})$

Flat case

If $R=0$, transport is path independent and every loop returns the vector unchanged.

Printed pp. 193-224Parallel Transport and Holonomy
Curvature manifests geometrically as holonomy: transporting a vector around a small closed loop returns it rotated by an angle proportional to the enclosed area times curvature. On a flat manifold transport is path independent, so every loop returns the vector unchanged.

Symmetries of the Curvature Tensor

Antisymmetry

$Rm(X,Y,Z,W) = -Rm(Y,X,Z,W)$ and $= -Rm(X,Y,W,Z)$.

Pair symmetry

$Rm(X,Y,Z,W) = Rm(Z,W,X,Y)$.

Component identities

$R_{ijkl} = -R_{jikl} = -R_{ijlk} = R_{klij}$

Consequence

Independent components drop to $\tfrac{1}{12}n^2(n^2-1)$.

Printed pp. 193-224Symmetries of the Curvature Tensor
The covariant four-tensor obeys antisymmetry in the first pair, antisymmetry in the second pair, and symmetry under exchange of the two pairs. These algebraic identities drastically cut the number of independent components and underpin every later contraction and decomposition we perform.

The Bianchi Identities

First (algebraic)

Cyclic sum over the last three slots vanishes.

Second (differential)

Cyclic sum of covariant derivatives vanishes.

Identities

$R_{ijkl}+R_{iklj}+R_{iljk}=0$

$\nabla_m R_{ijkl}+\nabla_k R_{ijlm}+\nabla_l R_{ijmk}=0$

Corollary

Contraction forces $\operatorname{div}\big(\operatorname{Ric}-\tfrac12 S g\big)=0$.

Printed pp. 193-224The Bianchi Identities
The first, algebraic Bianchi identity cyclically permutes three arguments to zero. The second, differential Bianchi identity constrains the covariant derivative of curvature and, after contraction, forces the Einstein tensor to be divergence free, a fact central to general relativity.

Sectional Curvature

Per two-plane

Each tangent plane $\sigma = \operatorname{span}(u,v)$ gets a number $K(\sigma)$.

Gaussian link

It equals the Gaussian curvature of the geodesic surface tangent to $\sigma$.

Definition

$K(\sigma)=\dfrac{\langle R(u,v)v,u\rangle}{|u|^2|v|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2}$

Completeness

All sectional curvatures determine the full Riemann tensor.

Printed pp. 193-224Sectional Curvature
Sectional curvature assigns a number to each tangent two-plane, recovering the Gaussian curvature of the surface swept by geodesics tangent to that plane. The full set of sectional curvatures determines the entire Riemann tensor, making this scalar function a complete curvature invariant.

The Sign of Sectional Curvature

Geodesic spreading

$\ddot J + K\,J = 0 \quad (\text{Jacobi equation})$

Zero curvature

Geodesic spacing grows linearly: parallel lines stay parallel.

Printed pp. 193-224The Sign of Sectional Curvature
Positive sectional curvature makes nearby geodesics converge, as on a sphere where meridians meet at the poles. Negative curvature makes them diverge exponentially, as on a saddle. Zero curvature keeps geodesic spacing linear, exactly the Euclidean behavior of parallel lines.

Ricci Curvature

A trace

Contract the Riemann tensor over one index to get a symmetric two-tensor.

Meaning

Average sectional curvature through a chosen direction.

Definition

$\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}\big(Z\mapsto R(Z,X)Y\big)=R\indices{^k_{ikj}}$

Volume role

Ricci governs how geodesic-ball volume departs from Euclidean.

Printed pp. 193-224Ricci Curvature
The Ricci tensor is the trace of the curvature endomorphism over one slot, a symmetric two-tensor measuring average sectional curvature through a direction. It controls how the volume of small geodesic balls deviates from the Euclidean value and drives comparison theorems later in the book.

Scalar Curvature

Full trace

Contract Ricci against the metric to get one scalar function.

Physics

It is the integrand of the Einstein-Hilbert action.

Definition

$S = \operatorname{tr}_g \operatorname{Ric} = g^{ij}R_{ij}$

Geometry

$S$ captures the leading volume distortion of small balls.

Printed pp. 193-224Scalar Curvature
Scalar curvature contracts the Ricci tensor fully against the metric, producing a single function on the manifold. It captures the leading volume distortion of small geodesic balls relative to Euclidean balls and serves as the integrand of the Einstein-Hilbert action in physics.
