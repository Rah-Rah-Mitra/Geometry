Three Surfaces, Three Curvatures

Informal Question

If two surfaces look identical at a single point under a microscope, is there an intrinsic number distinguishing them?

Three Witnesses

  • The plane is the zero of the theory.
  • The sphere bends consistently toward itself.
  • The saddle bends in opposite senses in two directions.

Reading Caution

Bending in an ambient space is extrinsic. Chapter 1 asks for an intrinsic measure that an inhabitant of the surface could detect.

Plane, sphere, saddle: the canonical trioSlide 2 / 10
Use these three pictures as the textbook does on the opening pages. The plane is the calibrating example with zero curvature, the sphere shows uniform positive bending, and the saddle shows mixed bending whose product is negative. Insist that all three look locally like a piece of paper at first glance, so the curvature we are after must come from comparing nearby behaviour, not from a single tangent plane.

Gaussian Curvature: An Intrinsic Number at a Point

K(p) = \kappa_1(p)\,\kappa_2(p)

Two Principal Curvatures

At each point of a surface, the maximum and minimum normal curvatures across tangent directions are \kappa_1 and \kappa_2. Their product is Gaussian curvature.

Theorema Egregium (preview)

Gauss showed that K is intrinsic: it depends only on lengths measured inside the surface, not on how the surface is embedded.

K = product of principal curvaturesSlide 3 / 10
Walk students through the construction in two stages. First, choose a normal direction and compare how the surface deviates from its tangent plane along each tangent direction; the extreme values are the principal curvatures. Second, take their product. The deep surprise that the chapter previews is the Theorema Egregium: this number can be recovered without ever leaving the surface, which is exactly why it deserves to be called intrinsic.

How an Inhabitant Detects Curvature

Angle Excess

\sum \theta_i - \pi = \int_T K\,dA

The deviation from \pi is the integral of curvature, by Gauss-Bonnet on a geodesic triangle.

Operational Reading

An inhabitant who can measure angles and never leaves the surface still detects K.

Intrinsic geometry through trianglesSlide 4 / 10
This is the bridge from the abstract definition to something a student can imagine performing. Hand them a tiny triangle whose sides are geodesics and tell them to measure its three angles. On a sphere the sum is greater than pi; on a saddle it is less. This is the same data Gauss-Bonnet packages, and the chapter uses it to argue that curvature is not just a number but a measurable feature of intrinsic geometry.

From Surfaces to Higher Dimensions: Sectional Curvature

K(\sigma) = \langle R(e_1,e_2)e_2, e_1\rangle

Choose a 2-Plane

At each point p and each 2-plane \sigma \subset T_pM, the sectional curvature is the Gaussian curvature of the surface swept out by geodesics tangent to \sigma.

Why It Works

Sectional curvature reduces higher-dimensional curvature to the 2-dimensional case one plane at a time, then encodes all those numbers in the Riemann tensor R.

One number per 2-plane in the tangent spaceSlide 5 / 10
Frame sectional curvature as the most economical generalization of Gaussian curvature. We pick a 2-plane inside the tangent space, run geodesics tangent to it, and read the Gaussian curvature of that surface. The Riemann tensor is just the bookkeeper that stores all of these numbers simultaneously. This is the abstraction the rest of the book will lean on.

Classification: Constant Curvature Model Spaces

CurvatureDimension 2Higher DimensionDefining Feature
K = 0Euclidean plane \mathbb{R}^2Euclidean space \mathbb{R}^nParallel lines stay parallel.
K = +1Unit sphere S^2Round sphere S^nGeodesics close up; positive triangle excess.
K = -1Hyperbolic plane \mathbb{H}^2Hyperbolic space \mathbb{H}^nExponential volume growth; negative excess.

Classification Theorem (2D)

A complete simply connected surface of constant curvature is isometric to \mathbb{R}^2, S^2, or \mathbb{H}^2 (rescaled).

Classification Theorem (nD)

A complete simply connected n-manifold of constant sectional curvature is isometric to \mathbb{R}^n, S^n, or \mathbb{H}^n.

Three model spaces in every dimensionSlide 6 / 10
The classification results are the first big payoff of the curvature concept. Once you ask for constant sectional curvature and complete simply connected geometry, there are only three answers in each dimension. Use this as motivation: the rest of the book is in part the story of how local curvature data forces global shape, with these three models as the rigid endpoints.

Local-to-Global Teasers

Cartan-Hadamard

If a complete manifold has sectional curvature K \le 0 everywhere, its universal cover is diffeomorphic to \mathbb{R}^n via the exponential map.

Nonpositive curvature spreads geodesics apart, so no conjugate points appear.

Bonnet-Myers

If a complete manifold has \mathrm{Ric} \ge (n-1)k > 0 everywhere, then it is compact with diameter at most \pi/\sqrt{k}.

Positive curvature forces geodesics to focus, capping the size of the manifold.

The Pattern

Both theorems read a pointwise inequality and conclude something about the entire manifold. This is the local-to-global mechanism the book will formalize after building the connection and the Jacobi equation.

From a sign at each point to the shape of the whole spaceSlide 7 / 10
Preview Cartan-Hadamard and Bonnet-Myers as the two faces of the local-to-global story. Negative curvature lets geodesics spread, so the universal cover unfolds into Euclidean space. Positive Ricci curvature focuses geodesics, so the manifold cannot be larger than a sphere of comparable curvature. Students should leave this slide expecting the rest of the course to make these inequalities precise.

Sectional

Per 2-plane curvature. Recovers Gaussian curvature on a surface.

Ricci

Average of sectional curvatures in directions orthogonal to a given vector.

Metric Compatibility

The Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free connection with \nabla g = 0.

Sanity Lab
