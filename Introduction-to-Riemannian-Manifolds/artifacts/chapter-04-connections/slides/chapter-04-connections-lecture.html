Connections

How do you differentiate a vector field on a manifold?

The obstruction

Tangent vectors at different points live in different spaces $T_pM \neq T_qM$. There is no canonical way to subtract them, so the difference quotient defining a derivative is meaningless.

The fix

A connection adds the missing structure: a coordinate-free rule for differentiating one vector field along another.

Printed pp. 85-114The Problem of Differentiation
This chapter, spanning printed pages 85 to 114, opens with a basic obstruction: ordinary directional derivatives compare vectors in different tangent spaces, which a manifold does not let us do canonically. A connection is the extra structure that repairs this, supplying a coordinate-independent rule for differentiating vector fields.

Connections in a Vector Bundle

Definition

$\nabla : \mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)$

written $(X,s)\mapsto \nabla_X s$.

Three axioms

  • $C^\infty$-linear in $X$ (tensorial)
  • $\mathbb{R}$-linear in $s$
  • Leibniz: $\nabla_X(fs)=(Xf)\,s+f\,\nabla_X s$

Tensorial vs. derivation

Tensoriality in $X$ means $\nabla_X s|_p$ depends only on $X_p$. The Leibniz rule makes the section slot a genuine derivation, not a tensor.

Why a bundle?

Differentiating sections of any vector bundle $E\to M$ requires this structure; the tangent bundle is the leading case.

Printed pp. 85-114Connections in a Vector Bundle
We define a connection abstractly on any vector bundle as an operator that is linear and tensorial in the direction slot but obeys a Leibniz product rule in the section slot. This axiomatic packaging is what makes the covariant derivative behave like differentiation while remaining intrinsic to the bundle.

Connection in the Tangent Bundle

Linear connection

Take $E=TM$. Then $\nabla_X Y$ is again a vector field: a rule for differentiating $Y$ in the direction $X$.

Same axioms

$\nabla_{fX_1+X_2}Y = f\nabla_{X_1}Y+\nabla_{X_2}Y$

$\nabla_X(fY)=(Xf)Y+f\nabla_X Y$

Printed pp. 85-114Connection in the Tangent Bundle
Specializing the bundle to the tangent bundle gives a linear or affine connection. Here both arguments are vector fields, and the covariant derivative of one field along another measures how the field changes as you move, after subtracting the artifact introduced by the coordinate frame itself.

Christoffel Symbols

Definition

$\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j = \Gamma^{k}_{ij}\,\partial_k$

The $n^3$ functions $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ encode the connection in a chart.

Not a tensor

The $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ transform with an extra inhomogeneous term. They record how the coordinate frame twists, not an intrinsic quantity.

Printed pp. 85-114Christoffel Symbols
In a chart the connection is fully encoded by its action on coordinate frames, producing the Christoffel symbols. These functions are not tensor components; they record how the frame twists. The covariant derivative formula adds them as a correction term to the ordinary partial derivative of the components.

The Covariant Derivative Formula

In coordinates

$\nabla_X Y = \big(X^i\partial_i Y^k + X^i Y^j \Gamma^{k}_{ij}\big)\,\partial_k$

Two pieces

  • Naive term $X^i\partial_i Y^k$ — chart-dependent
  • Correction $X^iY^j\Gamma^k_{ij}$ — restores tensoriality

Why the correction

Neither term is a tensor by itself. The inhomogeneous transformation of $\partial_i Y^k$ is exactly cancelled by that of $\Gamma^k_{ij}$, so the sum is well defined.

Reading it

Differentiate the components as usual, then add a term that accounts for the changing basis frame.

Printed pp. 85-114The Covariant Derivative Formula
Writing both fields in coordinates yields the master formula: the covariant derivative equals the naive component derivative plus a Christoffel correction. The first term depends on the chart and is not tensorial alone; the connection coefficients precisely cancel that chart dependence so the sum transforms correctly.

Covariant Derivative Along a Curve

Induced operator

$D_t : \Gamma(\gamma^*TM)\to\Gamma(\gamma^*TM)$

For a field $V(t)$ along $\gamma$:

$D_t V = \big(\dot V^k + \dot\gamma^i V^j \Gamma^{k}_{ij}\big)\partial_k$

Properties

  • $\mathbb{R}$-linear, Leibniz in scalars
  • Agrees with $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\tilde V$ for extensions $\tilde V$
Printed pp. 85-114Covariant Derivative Along a Curve
Every connection induces a unique operator differentiating vector fields defined only along a curve. It satisfies linearity, a Leibniz rule, and consistency with restrictions of ambient fields. This is the device that lets us speak of acceleration and of a vector staying constant as it travels along the curve.

Parallel Transport

Parallel field

$D_t V \equiv 0$

$V$ is "constant" along $\gamma$ in the sense of the connection.

Transport equation

$\dot V^k(t) + \dot\gamma^i(t)\,V^j(t)\,\Gamma^{k}_{ij} = 0$

Existence & uniqueness

A linear first-order ODE: each $V(t_0)$ extends to a unique parallel field.

Printed pp. 85-114Parallel Transport
A vector field along a curve is parallel when its covariant derivative along the curve vanishes identically. In coordinates this is a linear first-order system in the components, so by existence and uniqueness for ODEs each initial vector extends to exactly one parallel field along the whole curve.

The Parallel Transport Map

Linear isomorphism

$P^{\gamma}_{t_0 t_1} : T_{\gamma(t_0)}M \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } T_{\gamma(t_1)}M$

Sends each initial vector to its parallel-transported value.

Path dependence

$P^\gamma$ depends on the path, not only its endpoints. Around a closed loop on a curved space it returns vectors rotated — this holonomy foreshadows curvature.

Printed pp. 85-114The Parallel Transport Map
Solving the parallel transport equation defines a linear isomorphism between tangent spaces at the curve's endpoints. This map depends on the path, not just the endpoints, and on a curved surface transporting a vector around a loop returns it rotated. That path dependence is the seed of curvature.

Geodesics and Acceleration

Acceleration

$a(t) = D_t\dot\gamma(t)$

Covariant derivative of velocity along the curve.

Geodesic equation

$\ddot\gamma^{\,k} + \dot\gamma^i\dot\gamma^j\,\Gamma^{k}_{ij} = 0$

Straightest curves

A geodesic has zero acceleration — the connection's notion of a straight line.

Self-parallel

Along a geodesic the velocity field is parallel: $D_t\dot\gamma = 0$. The curve transports its own tangent vector.

Determined by initial data

A point $p$ and a velocity $v\in T_pM$ pin down a unique geodesic, by existence and uniqueness for the second-order ODE.

Printed pp. 85-114Geodesics and Acceleration
With covariant differentiation along a curve we define the acceleration as the covariant derivative of the velocity. A geodesic is a curve of zero acceleration, the connection's notion of a straight line. In coordinates this is the second-order geodesic equation built from the Christoffel symbols.

The Total Covariant Derivative

Extending to tensors

A connection on $TM$ extends uniquely to all tensor bundles by two rules: it commutes with contractions, and it satisfies a tensor product Leibniz rule.

Total derivative

$\nabla : \Gamma(T^{(k,l)}M)\to\Gamma(T^{(k,l+1)}M)$

$(\nabla F)(\dots,X)=\nabla_X F(\dots)$ raises covariant rank by one.

On functions

$\nabla f = df$: the total covariant derivative recovers the differential.

Leibniz across factors

$\nabla_X(F\otimes G)=(\nabla_X F)\otimes G + F\otimes(\nabla_X G)$, so the rule is forced once it is fixed on vectors and functions.

Printed pp. 85-114The Total Covariant Derivative
A connection extends from vector fields to all tensor fields by demanding it commute with contractions and obey a tensor Leibniz rule. The total covariant derivative raises the covariant rank of a tensor by one, packaging all directional derivatives into a single tensor field of one higher order.

Differentiating Tensors in Coordinates

Component rule

$\nabla_m F^{\,i}_{\,j} = \partial_m F^{\,i}_{\,j} + \Gamma^{i}_{mk}F^{\,k}_{\,j} - \Gamma^{k}_{mj}F^{\,i}_{\,k}$

Index bookkeeping

  • + Γ term per upper index
  • − Γ term per lower index

Consistency

Reduces to $\partial_m f$ on functions and to the earlier $\nabla_X Y$ formula on vector fields. The signs guarantee the output is a tensor.

Printed pp. 85-114Differentiating Tensors in Coordinates
The component formula for a tensor's covariant derivative adds a positive Christoffel term for each upper index and subtracts one for each lower index. This bookkeeping guarantees the result transforms as a tensor, and it reduces to the gradient on functions and to the earlier rule on vector fields.

Synthesis

The chain of ideas

  • Naive differentiation fails intrinsically
  • A connection $\nabla$ supplies the correction
  • $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ encode it in a chart
  • $D_t$ gives parallel transport & geodesics
  • Total $\nabla$ carries it to all tensors
