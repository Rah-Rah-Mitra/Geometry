Multilinear Maps

One slot at a time

A map is multilinear if it is linear in each argument when all others are held fixed.

Not jointly linear

Scaling every input at once scales the output by the product, not the sum. Joint linearity is a different, weaker idea.

Linearity in slot $j$

$T(\dots, a v + b v', \dots) = a\,T(\dots,v,\dots) + b\,T(\dots,v',\dots)$

Why it matters

Values on basis tuples determine the whole map: $n^k$ numbers fix a covariant $k$-tensor.

Printed pp. 304-326Multilinear Maps
Multilinearity is linearity in one slot at a time, never joint linearity in all inputs together. We fix every argument but one and demand the leftover dependence be linear. This separate-slot structure is exactly what lets components on basis vectors reconstruct the whole map by expansion.

The Abstract Tensor Product

A new space

$V \otimes W$ converts bilinear maps out of $V \times W$ into linear maps out of a single space.

Universal property

Every bilinear $B$ factors uniquely as a linear map through $\otimes$. This characterizes the construction.

Type $(k,l)$ tensors

$T^k_l(V) = \underbrace{V^* \otimes \cdots \otimes V^*}_{k} \otimes \underbrace{V \otimes \cdots \otimes V}_{l}$

Reading

A $(k,l)$-tensor is a multilinear functional on $k$ vectors and $l$ covectors.

Printed pp. 304-326The Tensor Product of Spaces
The abstract tensor product packages a bilinear map as a single linear map on a new space. Its universal property says every bilinear map factors uniquely through the tensor product. That is why tensors of type k,l are just multilinear functionals on copies of V and its dual.

Tensor Product of Tensors

Concatenate slots

$(\alpha \otimes \beta)(v,w) = \alpha(v)\,\beta(w)$. The first factor reads the first input, the second reads the rest.

A basis appears

The products $\varepsilon^{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon^{i_k}$ form a basis for covariant $k$-tensors.

Printed pp. 304-326Tensor Product of Tensors
The tensor product of two tensors concatenates their slots: the first factor reads the first block of arguments, the second factor reads the rest. Elementary products of dual basis covectors give a basis for covariant tensors, so every tensor is a coordinate combination of these building blocks.

Covariant, Contravariant, Mixed

Covariant

All slots eat vectors. A $(k,0)$-tensor lives in $V^*\otimes\cdots\otimes V^*$.

Contravariant

All slots eat covectors. A $(0,l)$-tensor lives in $V\otimes\cdots\otimes V$.

Mixed

A $(k,l)$-tensor has both kinds of slot at once.

Examples

$g \in T^2_0 \quad v \in T^0_1 \quad A \in T^1_1$

Naming preview

The labels foreshadow how each kind of index responds to a change of basis.

Printed pp. 304-326Covariant, Contravariant, Mixed
Variance tracks which slots eat vectors and which eat covectors. Covariant slots take vectors, contravariant slots take covectors, and a mixed k,l tensor has both. The names anticipate how components respond to a change of basis, which is the transformation law we examine next.

Components and the Transformation Law

Components are evaluations

$T_{i_1\dots i_k} = T(e_{i_1},\dots,e_{i_k})$. The tensor on basis vectors gives its coordinate array.

Object vs. array

The multilinear object is invariant; only its components shift under a new basis.

Change of basis

$\tilde T_{j_1\dots j_k} = T_{i_1\dots i_k}\,(B^{-1})^{i_1}_{j_1}\cdots(B^{-1})^{i_k}_{j_k}$

Index rule

Lower (covariant) slots get $B^{-1}$; upper (contravariant) slots get $B$.

Printed pp. 304-326Components and Transformation Law
Components are the tensor evaluated on basis vectors and dual covectors. Change the basis and the components change by inserting the change-of-basis matrix into each lower slot and its inverse into each upper slot. The multilinear object is invariant; only its coordinate array shifts.

A Bilinear Form Eats Two Vectors

The grid contracts inputs

A $(0,2)$-tensor $B$ takes $v$ on the first slot and $w$ on the second, returning $v^\top B\, w$.

Bilinear form

$B(v,w) = B_{ij}\,v^i w^j$

Printed pp. 304-326Eating Two Vectors
Here is the picture to hold onto: a covariant 2-tensor is a grid that swallows two vectors and returns a scalar. The first vector selects a row weighting, the second a column weighting, and the component matrix B contracts them. Bilinearity is reading each input through its own slot.

Symmetric and Alternating Tensors

Symmetrization $\mathrm{Sym}$

Average over all permutations of the arguments. The result is unchanged by any swap.

Alternation $\mathrm{Alt}$

Signed average: each permutation weighted by its sign. The result flips under a swap.

Projection operators

$\mathrm{Sym}\,T = \tfrac{1}{k!}\sum_\sigma {}^\sigma T, \quad \mathrm{Alt}\,T = \tfrac{1}{k!}\sum_\sigma (\mathrm{sgn}\,\sigma)\,{}^\sigma T$

Key fact

An alternating tensor vanishes whenever two arguments coincide. This is the seed of differential forms.

Printed pp. 304-326Symmetric and Alternating
Symmetrization and alternation are projection operators on tensors. The symmetric part averages over permutations of the arguments; the alternating part takes a signed average. Symmetric tensors ignore swaps, while alternating tensors flip sign and vanish whenever two arguments coincide, the seed of differential forms.

Splitting a 2-Tensor

Symmetric + skew

$B = \tfrac{1}{2}(B + B^\top) + \tfrac{1}{2}(B - B^\top)$

Two meanings

The symmetric half is a quadratic form; the skew half measures signed area and vanishes on repeated inputs.

Printed pp. 304-326Splitting a 2-Tensor
Any covariant 2-tensor splits cleanly into a symmetric and an alternating part: B equals half of B plus its transpose, plus half of B minus its transpose. The symmetric piece is a quadratic form, the skew piece a signed area, and together they reconstruct B exactly.

Tensor Fields on Manifolds

Smooth sections

A tensor field assigns one tensor to each tangent space, varying smoothly across the manifold.

Smoothness criterion

In a chart, smoothness of the field is exactly smoothness of its component functions.

A $(0,2)$-field in a chart

$A = A_{ij}(x)\,dx^i \otimes dx^j$

Section of a bundle

$A : M \to T^2_0 T^*M$ with $\pi \circ A = \mathrm{id}_M$.

Printed pp. 304-326Tensor Fields on Manifolds
A tensor field is a smooth section of a tensor bundle: one tensor attached to each tangent space, varying smoothly point to point. In a chart its components are smooth functions, and smoothness of the field is exactly smoothness of those coordinate component functions across the chart.

Operations on Tensor Fields

Tensor product

Multiply two fields pointwise to raise total type and combine slots.

Contraction

Pair one upper slot with one lower slot and sum, lowering type by $(1,1)$.

Contraction

$(\mathrm{tr}\,A)_{j} = A^{i}{}_{ij}$

Smoothness preserved

Both operations send smooth fields to smooth fields, building the full algebra of fields.

Printed pp. 304-326Operations on Tensor Fields
Tensor fields support tensor product and contraction pointwise. Contraction pairs one upper slot with one lower slot and sums, lowering type by one and one. These operations are smooth, so they send smooth tensor fields to smooth tensor fields, building the full algebra of fields used later.

Pullback of Covariant Tensors

Insert the differential

For $F:M\to N$, a covariant tensor on $N$ pulls back by feeding $dF$ into every slot.

No inverse needed

Pullback works for any smooth map. Pushing vectors forward, by contrast, needs a diffeomorphism.

Matrix form, 2-tensor

$(F^*A)_p = J_F^\top\, A(F(p))\, J_F$

Field statement

$(F^*A)(v,w) = A\big(dF(v),\,dF(w)\big)$, slot by slot.

Printed pp. 304-326Pullback of Covariant Tensors
Covariant tensors pull back along any smooth map by inserting its differential into every slot. For a 2-tensor this is the matrix sandwich J transpose times A composed with F times J. Crucially no inverse appears, so pullback needs no diffeomorphism, unlike pushing vectors forward.

The Tensor Characterization Lemma

The test

An operation on vector fields that is $C^\infty(M)$-linear in each slot is a tensor field.

Functions, not just constants

Linearity over smooth functions, not merely over scalars, is the decisive condition.

Pointwise dependence

$T(fX,\dots) = f\,T(X,\dots) \;\Rightarrow\; (TX)_p \text{ depends only on } X_p$

Why it works

Function-linearity forces locality: the value at $p$ uses only the inputs at $p$.

Printed pp. 304-326Tensor Characterization Lemma
The characterization lemma is the workhorse test. An operation on vector fields that is multilinear over smooth functions, not merely over constants, is automatically pointwise: it is a tensor field. Function-linearity in each slot is exactly the condition that the value at a point depends only on inputs at that point.
