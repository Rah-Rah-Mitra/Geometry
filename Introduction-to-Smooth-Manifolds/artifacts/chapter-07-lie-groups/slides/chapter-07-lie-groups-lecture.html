Definition of a Lie Group

Group structure

A set $G$ with associative product, identity $e$, and inverses.

Manifold structure

$G$ is a smooth manifold without boundary.

Compatibility

Both operations are smooth maps of manifolds.

Smoothness conditions

$$m:G\times G\to G,\quad (g,h)\mapsto gh$$ $$\iota:G\to G,\quad g\mapsto g^{-1}$$

Both $m$ and $\iota$ are required to be smooth.

Consequence

Left and right translations are diffeomorphisms.

Printed pp. 150-173Definition of a Lie Group
Define a Lie group as a smooth manifold carrying a group structure whose multiplication and inversion are smooth. Point out that smoothness of inversion is sometimes automatic, but here we just demand it. Emphasize the boundaryless requirement and remind students that the group identity sits at a distinguished point of the manifold.

The Standard Examples

General & special linear

$GL(n,\mathbb{R})=\{A:\det A\neq 0\}$ $$SL(n,\mathbb{R})=\{A:\det A=1\}$$

Orthogonal & unitary

$O(n):A^{\!T}A=I,\;\; U(n):A^{*}A=I$ $$SO(n)=O(n)\cap SL(n,\mathbb{R})$$

Open vs. level set

$GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ is open in matrix space; $SL$, $O$, $U$ are level sets cut by equations.

Compact abelian models

The circle $\mathbb{S}^1$ and the torus $\mathbb{T}^n=\mathbb{S}^1\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}^1$.

Trivial cases

Any countable group with the discrete topology is a $0$-dimensional Lie group.

Highlight that determinant and transpose conditions cut these groups out as level sets or open sets.

Left Translation $L_g$

Definition

$$L_g:G\to G,\qquad L_g(h)=gh$$

Inverse: $L_g^{-1}=L_{g^{-1}}$, smooth, so $L_g$ is a diffeomorphism.

Why it matters

$L_g$ sends the identity $e$ to $g$, carrying tangent data at $e$ to any point of $G$.

Right translation

$R_g(h)=hg$ is likewise a diffeomorphism.

Printed pp. 150-173Left Translation
Introduce left translation as multiplication on the left by a fixed element. Show that it is a diffeomorphism with smooth inverse given by the opposite element. This is the workhorse of the chapter: it carries the identity to any chosen group element and lets local data at the identity propagate across the whole manifold.

Left-Invariant Vector Fields

Invariance

$$(L_g)_*X = X \quad\text{for all }g\in G$$

Equivalently $X_{gh}=d(L_g)_h(X_h)$ at every $h$.

Determined at $e$

Translation carries the single vector $X_e$ to every point, so $X$ is fixed by its value at the identity.

Building one

Pick any $v\in T_eG$ and set $X_g=d(L_g)_e(v)$ — this is smooth and left-invariant.

Printed pp. 150-173Left-Invariant Vector Fields
A vector field is left-invariant when every left translation pushes it forward to itself. Explain that such a field is completely determined by its single value at the identity, because translation carries that one tangent vector to every other point. This rigid correspondence is the bridge to the Lie algebra constructed next.

The Lie Algebra of $G$

Definition

$$\mathrm{Lie}(G)=\{\text{left-invariant fields}\}$$

Closed under the Lie bracket: $[X,Y]$ is left-invariant when $X,Y$ are.

Evaluation isomorphism

$$\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;T_eG,\quad X\mapsto X_e$$

Dimension

$\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim T_eG=\dim G$.

Bracket transported

The bracket on $T_eG$ is read off by extending vectors to invariant fields, bracketing, then evaluating at $e$.

Example

$\mathrm{Lie}\big(GL(n,\mathbb{R})\big)=\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})$ with $[A,B]=AB-BA$.

Printed pp. 150-173The Lie Algebra
Define the Lie algebra as the space of left-invariant vector fields, closed under the Lie bracket. Stress the evaluation isomorphism with the tangent space at the identity, so the Lie algebra has the same dimension as the group. The bracket on identity vectors is inherited by transporting fields and bracketing them.

One-Parameter Subgroups

Definition

$$\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G,\quad \gamma(s+t)=\gamma(s)\,\gamma(t)$$

A smooth homomorphism from the additive line.

Integral curve

Each $\gamma$ is the integral curve through $e$ of the left-invariant field with $X_e=\gamma'(0)$.

Correspondence

Left-invariant fields $\leftrightarrow$ one-parameter subgroups, bijectively.

Printed pp. 150-173One-Parameter Subgroups
A one-parameter subgroup is a smooth homomorphism from the real line into the group. Explain that each one is the integral curve through the identity of a left-invariant field, and conversely every left-invariant field generates one. This is the seed of the exponential map developed in later chapters.

Homomorphisms & Covering

Lie group homomorphism

$$F:G\to H,\quad F(gh)=F(g)F(h)$$

Smooth; an isomorphism if bijective with smooth inverse.

Covering model

$$\varepsilon(t)=e^{2\pi i t}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{S}^1,\quad \ker\varepsilon=\mathbb{Z}$$

Printed pp. 150-173Homomorphisms and Covering
A Lie group homomorphism is a smooth group homomorphism; isomorphisms are smooth bijections with smooth inverse. Use the exponential wrapping of the line onto the circle as the model: it respects multiplication, has the integers as kernel, and locally looks like a diffeomorphism while globally covering the circle infinitely.

Lie Subgroups: Embedded vs. Immersed

Kernels embed

$\ker F$ is a properly embedded Lie subgroup, hence closed.

Closed $\Leftrightarrow$ embedded

For Lie subgroups, closedness and embeddedness coincide — far stronger than for general submanifolds.

Cautionary case

An injective homomorphism may give an immersed image that is not embedded.

Printed pp. 150-173Lie Subgroups
Distinguish embedded from immersed Lie subgroups. Kernels of homomorphisms are properly embedded, hence closed. The cautionary example is the irrational line on the torus: an injective homomorphism whose image is immersed but dense, so not embedded. Closedness and embeddedness coincide for Lie subgroups, which is far stronger than for general submanifolds.

Group Actions, Orbits, Stabilizers

Smooth action

$$\theta:G\times M\to M,\;\; \theta(gh,p)=\theta(g,\theta(h,p))$$

Each $g$ acts as a diffeomorphism of $M$.

Orbit

$G\cdot p=\{g\cdot p\}$, the image of the orbit map $g\mapsto g\cdot p$.

Stabilizer

$G_p=\{g:g\cdot p=p\}$, the fiber of that orbit map.

Printed pp. 150-173Group Actions
A smooth action assigns to each group element a diffeomorphism of a manifold, compatibly with multiplication. The orbit is the image of the orbit map and the stabilizer is its fiber. For the rotation action on the plane, nonzero points trace circles with trivial stabilizer while the origin is fixed by the whole group.

Equivariance & Constant Rank

Equivariant map

$$F(g\cdot p)=g\cdot F(p)$$

$F$ intertwines a $G$-action on $M$ with one on $N$.

Transitive domain

If $G$ acts transitively on $M$, it can move any tangent computation to any other point.

Theorem

An equivariant map with transitive domain action has constant rank.

Printed pp. 150-173Equivariance and Constant Rank
An equivariant map intertwines two actions. When the domain action is transitive, the group moves any tangent calculation to any other point, so the differential has the same rank everywhere. Present the tangent square: vertical arrows are linear isomorphisms induced by translations, forcing the two horizontal maps to share a rank.

The Circle & the Torus

Circle group

$$\mathbb{S}^1=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\},\quad e^{i\alpha}e^{i\beta}=e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}$$

Translation $L_z$ is a rigid rotation by the angle of $z$.

Torus

$$\mathbb{T}^n=\mathbb{R}^n/\mathbb{Z}^n=\mathbb{S}^1\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}^1$$

Compact & abelian

Both are compact, connected, commutative Lie groups.

Translations = flows

Each translation is the time-flow of a constant left-invariant field on the group.

Universal cover

$\mathbb{R}^n$ covers $\mathbb{T}^n$, with $\mathbb{Z}^n$ as the deck group.

Printed pp. 150-173The Circle and the Torus
Treat the circle as the cleanest compact abelian Lie group, with multiplication being angle addition and translations being rigid rotations. The torus is its higher-dimensional analogue, a product of circles covered by Euclidean space. These translations are exactly the flows of the constant left-invariant fields on the group.

Synthesis & What Comes Next

Translations are the engine

Actions realize groups
