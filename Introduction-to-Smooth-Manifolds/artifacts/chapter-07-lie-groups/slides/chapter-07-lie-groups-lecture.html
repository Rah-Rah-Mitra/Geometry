Homomorphisms

A Covering Map Makes Kernels Visible

\(\epsilon(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)\)

Kernel As A Fiber

\(\ker \epsilon = \mathbb{Z}\). The samples \(-2,-1,0,1,2\) all land at the identity within numerical precision.

Why It Matters

Homomorphisms have constant rank, and their kernels become the prototype for Lie subgroups.

Equivariant Maps

Commuting With Actions Transports Rank

Constant Rank Mechanism

If \(F(g\cdot p)=g\cdot F(p)\) and the domain action is transitive, every differential calculation is a translated copy of every other one.

\(\epsilon_2(x_1,x_2)=(e^{2\pi i x_1},e^{2\pi i x_2})\)
2, 2, 2rank samples for the covering map
EquivarianceSlide 7 / 10
Equivariance is the diagrammatic statement that the map commutes with the action. When the domain action is transitive, tangent calculations can be transported from one point to another, which is why the rank becomes constant. The covering map samples all have rank two, and equivariance residuals are tiny.
