Vector Fields as Smooth Sections

Definition

$X:M\to TM,\quad \pi\circ X=\mathrm{id}_M$

A section assigns $X_p\in T_pM$ and the projection sends it back to $p$.

Smoothness

$X$ is smooth iff its components are smooth in every chart.

Not just arrows

A section is a smooth right inverse of $\pi$, not a loose collection.

Printed pp. 174-204Smooth Sections of TM
Stress that a vector field is not a loose bag of arrows. It is a section: a smooth right inverse of the bundle projection that selects exactly one tangent vector over each point. The circle is the cleanest picture because tangency is visible, and the section appears as the horizontal graph v equals one in bundle coordinates.

The Space $\mathfrak{X}(M)$

Real vector space

Sums and real scalar multiples of smooth fields are smooth fields.

$C^\infty(M)$-module

Coefficients may be smooth functions: $(fX)_p=f(p)X_p$.

Module structure

$f,g\in C^\infty(M),\;X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)$

$f(X+Y)=fX+fY,\quad (fg)X=f(gX)$

Why it matters

Function coefficients are what let local frames express any field $X=X^iE_i$.

Printed pp. 174-204The Space X(M)
Here we collect all smooth vector fields into the single space written script X of M. Emphasize the two algebraic structures living on it simultaneously: a real vector space and a module over the ring of smooth functions, so coefficients are themselves functions, not just constants. This module view is what makes local frames meaningful later.

Local Frames and Coframes

Frame

$(E_1,\dots,E_n),\quad (E_i)_p \text{ a basis of } T_pM$

Coordinate frame

The fields $\partial/\partial x^i$ form the canonical local frame in a chart.

Obstruction

Not every manifold has a global frame; $S^2$ has no nonvanishing field (hairy ball).

Printed pp. 174-204Local Frames and Coframes
A frame is an ordered tuple of fields that is a basis of the tangent space at every point of its domain. Coordinate frames are the prototype, but not every manifold admits a global frame. Mention parallelizability and the hairy ball theorem so students see that the existence of nonvanishing fields is a real topological obstruction.

One Field, Two Coordinate Languages

Cartesian

$X=-y\,\partial_x+x\,\partial_y$

Polar

$X=\partial_\theta,\quad X(r)=0,\;X(\theta)=1$

Domain warning

Polar coordinates are not a chart at the origin; check smoothness chart by chart.

Printed pp. 174-204Coordinate Expressions
Drive home that one geometric field has many coordinate faces. The rotation field minus y d-x plus x d-y is exactly the angle field in polar coordinates. Changing coordinates changes the component functions, never the underlying assignment. Flag the domain warning: the polar expression is not a single chart at the origin, so smoothness must be checked chart by chart.

Vector Fields as Derivations of $C^\infty(M)$

Action on functions

$(Xf)(p)=X_p f=\textstyle\sum_i X^i(p)\,\partial_i f(p)$

Linear over $\mathbb{R}$

$X(af+bg)=aXf+bXg$ for real $a,b$.

Leibniz rule

$X(fg)=fXg+gXf$.

Theorem

$\mathrm{Der}\big(C^\infty(M)\big)\;\cong\;\mathfrak{X}(M)$

Every derivation of the smooth function algebra is a unique smooth vector field. This is the coordinate-free identity of a field.

Caution

$X$ is $\mathbb{R}$-linear but not $C^\infty(M)$-linear: it is first order, not tensorial.

Printed pp. 174-204Fields as Derivations
This is the conceptual hinge of the chapter. A smooth vector field acts on smooth functions by directional differentiation, and that action is linear over the reals and satisfies the Leibniz product rule. The theorem is that derivations of the smooth function algebra are exactly the smooth vector fields, which gives us a coordinate-free identity for a field.

Reading $Xf$ Against Level Curves

Skim a contour

Arrow tangent to a level curve of $f$ $\Rightarrow$ $Xf\approx 0$.

Cut across

Arrow crossing contours $\Rightarrow$ $|Xf|$ large.

Directional derivative

$Xf=\langle \nabla f,\;X\rangle$ in a chart

Printed pp. 174-204Xf and Level Curves
Use the contour picture to make the derivation tangible. Where an arrow skims along a level curve of f, the directional derivative X f is near zero; where the arrow cuts across contours, X f is large. This visual makes the abstract operator concrete and previews why brackets measure a genuine geometric failure to commute.

Smooth Maps and $F$-related Fields

$F$-relatedness

$dF_p(X_p)=Y_{F(p)}\quad\forall\,p\in M$

Pushforward

$F_*X$ exists as a field on $N$ only if it is single-valued over fibers.

Diffeomorphism

Unique preimages $\Rightarrow$ $F_*X$ always well defined.

Printed pp. 174-204F-related Fields
Introduce relatedness carefully: X and Y are F-related when the differential of F sends X at p to Y at F of p, for every p. Pushforward of a field needs single-valuedness over fibers. A diffeomorphism has unique preimages so its pushforward always exists; a general smooth map can fail, and we will see that failure explicitly.
Introduction to Smooth ManifoldsChapter 8

When Pushforward Fails: the Fold

Fold map

$F(t)=t^2,\quad F^{-1}(1)=\{-1,+1\}$

Conflict

Two preimages push the same source field to opposite target vectors.

Contrast

The circle parametrization is well behaved; relatedness holds cleanly.

Printed pp. 174-204Pushforward Failure
The fold map t to t squared is the cautionary example. The two preimages of a positive target point push the same source field to opposite target vectors, so no single target field can represent the pushforward there. Contrast that with the circle parametrization, where relatedness holds cleanly. The lesson: fibers with multiple points break naive pushforward.

The Lie Bracket $[X,Y]$

Definition

$[X,Y]f = X(Yf)-Y(Xf)$

Coordinate formula

$[X,Y]^k = X(Y^k)-Y(X^k)$

Second order cancels

$XY$ and $YX$ are second order, but the bracket is first order: a field again.

Printed pp. 174-204The Lie Bracket
Define the bracket as the commutator X Y minus Y X of the two derivations. The surprise is that although each composite is second order, the second-order parts cancel and the bracket is again a first-order operator, hence a genuine vector field. Present the coordinate formula and verify it on the partial-x, x partial-y example giving partial-y.

$\mathfrak{X}(M)$ as a Lie Algebra

Bilinear

$[\,\cdot,\cdot\,]$ is $\mathbb{R}$-linear in each slot.

Antisymmetric

$[X,Y]=-[Y,X]$.

Jacobi identity

$\big[X,[Y,Z]\big]+\big[Y,[Z,X]\big]+\big[Z,[X,Y]\big]=0$

Worked check

$X=\partial_x,\;Y=x\,\partial_y\;\Rightarrow\;[X,Y]=\partial_y$

Naturality

$X\sim_F X',\;Y\sim_F Y'\Rightarrow [X,Y]\sim_F[X',Y']$

Brackets survive pushforwards, restrictions, and homomorphisms.

Printed pp. 174-204X(M) as a Lie Algebra
Collect the bracket's algebraic properties: it is bilinear, antisymmetric, and satisfies the Jacobi identity, so the space of smooth fields is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra. Then state naturality: F-related fields have F-related brackets. This is the structural payoff, because it means brackets survive pushforwards, restrictions, and homomorphisms exactly when the hypotheses hold.

Lie Algebra of a Lie Group

Left-invariance

$(L_g)_*X=X\quad\Rightarrow\quad \mathrm{Lie}(G)\cong T_eG$

Affine group

$(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,\,ad+b),\;a>0$, identity $(1,0)$.

Basis fields

$E_1=a\,\partial_a,\quad E_2=a\,\partial_b,\quad [E_1,E_2]=E_2$

Printed pp. 174-204Left-Invariant Fields
Left-invariant fields are determined by their value at the identity, so the Lie algebra of a Lie group is finite-dimensional, isomorphic to the tangent space at the identity. Use the affine group of the line as the smallest visible nonabelian model: two basis fields a partial-a and a partial-b with the single nonzero bracket equal to E two.

Recap and Failure Modes

Section

A field is a smooth section of $TM$; coordinates give smooth components.

Derivation

Acting on $C^\infty(M)$ is the coordinate-free identity of a field.

Lie algebra

The bracket makes $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ a Lie algebra; left-invariance shrinks it to $T_eG$.

Failure modes to remember

  • Pushforward is fragile off diffeomorphisms (the fold).
  • A field need not restrict to a submanifold unless tangent along it.
  • $X$ is $\mathbb{R}$-linear, not $C^\infty$-linear.
  • Global frames may not exist ($S^2$).
Diagnostic family

$X=\partial_x,\;Y_k=x^k\partial_y\Rightarrow[X,Y_k]=k\,x^{k-1}\partial_y$

Printed pp. 174-204Recap and Failure Modes
Close by replaying the arc and naming the failure modes that keep students honest. A field is a smooth section; acting on functions makes it a derivation; relatedness controls pushforward and is fragile off diffeomorphisms; the bracket makes the field space a Lie algebra; and left-invariant fields shrink that algebra to finite dimension for a Lie group.
