Vector Fields As Smooth Sections

Definition

A vector field on \(M\) is a smooth map \(X:M\to TM\) such that the bundle projection returns the base point:

\(\pi\circ X=\operatorname{id}_M.\)

Circle Model

The field \(X=\partial_\theta\) assigns a unit tangent vector at every point of \(S^1\), and in coordinates it becomes the graph \(\theta\mapsto(\theta,1)\).

Checks

Radial dot product is 0; max unit-norm error is 1.1\cdot10^{-16}.

Artifact: vector-fields-section-as-graph.pngSlide 3 / 10
Here the definition is concrete: a section chooses one vector over each base point and the projection forgets the vector part. On the circle, every red arrow is tangent because the radial dot product check is zero. The right panel is the same field in bundle coordinates, where the selected section is just the horizontal graph v equals one above the base coordinate theta.

One Field, Two Coordinate Languages

Local Form

In a chart, a field has smooth component functions:

\(X=\sum_i X^i\,\partial_i.\)

Rotation Field

\(X=-y\,\partial_x+x\,\partial_y\)

On the punctured plane the same assignment is \(X=\partial_\theta\), so \(X(r)=0\) and \(X(\theta)=1\).

Domain Warning

The polar expression is not a chart at the origin. Coordinate simplicity can hide a domain restriction.

Artifact: coordinate-expression-rotation-field.pngSlide 4 / 10
This slide separates the field from its coordinate clothing. Cartesian coordinates describe the rotation field as minus y partial x plus x partial y; polar coordinates describe the same motion as pure angle speed. The checks say radius is constant and angle speed is one, but the red excluded point matters because polar coordinates do not cover the origin.

Acting On Functions: Fields As Derivations

First-Order Operator

A smooth field differentiates functions by

\(Xf=\sum_i X^i\,\partial_i f.\)

Derivation Rule

\(X(fg)=f\,Xg+g\,Xf.\)

This is real-linear and obeys the product rule, but it is not \(C^\infty(M)\)-linear in \(f\).

Notebook Model

\(X=(1+x^2)\partial_x+(x-y)\partial_y\)

The sampled \(Xf\) range is approximately \([-3.876,4.131]\).

Artifact: vector-field-acts-on-function.pngSlide 5 / 10
Transition from arrows to operators. The color field is Xf: where arrows cross level curves strongly, the directional derivative is large; where arrows run nearly along contours, it is near zero. The symbolic check verifies the product rule exactly, so the picture and the algebra agree that a vector field is a derivation of smooth functions.

Smooth Maps: Related Fields And Pushforward Failure

Related-Field Test

Fields \(X\) on \(M\) and \(Y\) on \(N\) are \(F\)-related when

\(dF_p(X_p)=Y_{F(p)}\quad\text{for all }p.\)

Fiber Obstruction

For \(F(t)=t^2\) and \(X=\partial_t\), the two points over \(u=1\) demand target values \(-2\) and 2.

Artifacts: pushforward-related-and-failure.png; pushforward-fold-failure.htmlSlide 6 / 10
Treat related fields as a fiber consistency test. A diffeomorphism has one preimage, so a pushforward field is automatic, but a general smooth map has to give one target vector no matter which preimage was used. The fold map fails at u equals one because t equals minus one pushes to minus two while t equals one pushes to plus two.

Lie Bracket: The Commutator Is First Order Again

Coordinate Formula

If \(X=X^i\partial_i\) and \(Y=Y^i\partial_i\), then

\([X,Y]^k=X(Y^k)-Y(X^k).\)

Model Computation

\(X=\partial_x,\quad Y=x\,\partial_y,\quad [X,Y]=\partial_y.\)

Checks

Coordinate fields commute; the Jacobi identity and product rule residuals are exactly zero in the symbolic tests.

Artifact: lie-bracket-coordinate-check.pngSlide 7 / 10
The commutator is the first big algebraic payoff of the derivation viewpoint. Two first-order operators composed in either order look second-order, but the second derivatives cancel in the difference. In the model, Y changes with x, so differentiating Y in the X direction leaves a vertical field: the bracket is partial y.

Applied Lab: When Does The Bracket Vanish?

Parameter Family

\(X=\partial_x,\qquad Y_k=x^k\partial_y.\)

The bracket is \(k x^{k-1}\partial_y\), so only \(k=0\) commutes everywhere.

Artifact: applied-lab-bracket-parameter.pngSlide 8 / 10
Use the k-family as a diagnostic lab for the whole chapter. The coordinate expression is easy, the derivation rule computes the bracket, and the resulting vector field shows exactly when the flows fail to commute. For k equals zero nothing changes with x; for positive k the vertical field changes as x changes, and the bracket records that variation.

Lie Algebra Of A Lie Group: Left-Invariant Fields

Affine Group Model

\((a,b)(c,d)=(ac,ad+b),\qquad a>0.\)

A left-invariant field is determined by its value at the identity \((1,0)\).

Basis Fields

\(E_1=a\partial_a,\qquad E_2=a\partial_b,\qquad [E_1,E_2]=E_2.\)

Matrix Check

The same nonzero bracket matches the matrix commutator model; the trace of a commutator is zero.

Artifact: affine-group-left-invariant-fields.pngSlide 9 / 10
Now move from arbitrary manifolds to Lie groups. Left translation propagates a tangent vector at the identity into a global field, so the Lie algebra is finite-dimensional even though vector fields on the group form an infinite-dimensional space. The affine group is the smallest visible nonabelian example in the notebook: the bracket of E1 and E2 returns E2.

Lecture Recap

1

Section

A vector field is a smooth section \(X:M\to TM\), so every vector is attached to the point where it lives.

2

Derivation

The operator \(f\mapsto Xf\) is the coordinate-free bridge to brackets and product rules.

3

Structure

Related fields, Lie brackets, and left-invariant fields explain which constructions survive maps and group multiplication.

Instructor Question

Given a new vector field, can we identify its section, choose useful coordinates, compute its action on functions, and decide what its bracket measures?
