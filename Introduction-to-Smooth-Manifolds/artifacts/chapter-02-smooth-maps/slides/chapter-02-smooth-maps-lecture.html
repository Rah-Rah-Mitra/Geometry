Smooth Functions: Coordinates Make the Test Ordinary

Definition

f:M\to\mathbb{R}^k is smooth at p if f\circ\varphi^{-1} is smooth in some chart \varphi around p.

Worked Example

f(x,y)=x^2+y^2. In polar coordinates this reads f=r^2, which is ordinary smooth on the chart domain.

Chart Independence

Smooth transition maps make the test independent of which legitimate chart is chosen.

Differentiate after translation, never on the bare setSlide 2 / 10
The definition is deliberately local: we pull a neighborhood into Euclidean coordinates and apply ordinary calculus there. Stress that polar coordinates are not special; the lesson is that the coordinate expression is an ordinary smooth function and that smooth transition maps guarantee the answer does not depend on the chart. The example f equals x squared plus y squared becoming r squared makes the translation concrete and checkable.

Smooth Maps: The Coordinate Square

Coordinate Representation

F:M\to N is smooth near p when \psi\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1} is ordinary smooth, with F(U)\subseteq V.

Circle Example

\varepsilon(t)=e^{2\pi i t}:\mathbb{R}\to S^1. An angle chart unwraps an arc, and the coordinate expression is linear in t.

Two charts, one ordinary map between Euclidean open setsSlide 3 / 10
A map between manifolds needs two translations, source and target, so the central object is the commuting square. The condition F of U lands inside V is part of the definition, not an afterthought; without it a discontinuous map can be disguised on fragments. The exponential winding onto the circle is the cleanest example: locally an angle chart unwraps a short arc and the coordinate map is just linear in t.

Diffeomorphisms: Smooth Sameness

Definition

A diffeomorphism is a bijective smooth map with smooth inverse: the precise form of “same smooth space.”

Ball to Plane

F(x)=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-|x|^2}},  G(y)=\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{1+|y|^2}}, with G\circ F=\mathrm{id}.

Invariants

Diffeomorphisms preserve dimension and boundary; the ball boundary is excluded because radii near 1 are pushed to infinity.

Bijective, smooth, smooth inverseSlide 4 / 10
A diffeomorphism is the smooth category's notion of isomorphism, so diffeomorphism invariance is what makes a property genuinely geometric rather than coordinate dependent. The radial map sending the open ball onto the whole plane is memorable: it shows why the boundary cannot belong to the domain, since points near radius one are flung to infinity. Have students verify both compositions return the identity, on samples and symbolically in the radial variable.

Partitions of Unity and Paracompactness

Existence Theorem

Every open cover of a smooth manifold admits a partition of unity subordinate to it.

Three Conditions

  • 0\le\psi_i\le 1 and \sum_i\psi_i=1;
  • \operatorname{supp}\psi_i\subseteq U_i;
  • the supports are locally finite.

Why It Exists

Manifolds are paracompact, so every cover has a locally finite refinement.

Local finiteness makes the infinite sum legalSlide 5 / 10
Introduce the partition of unity as a locally finite family of smooth weights summing to one, each weight trapped inside a prescribed open set. Paracompactness is the structural reason existence holds: every open cover refines to a locally finite one, so near any point only finitely many weights are nonzero and the sum is genuinely smooth. The picture of three bumps adding to the dashed line one is the finite shadow of the general construction.

Bump Functions and Smooth Extensions

The Analytic Trick

h(t)=e^{-1/t} for t>0, else 0: positive on one side, flat to all orders at 0.

From Flat to Bump

Combine shifted copies into a cutoff, then a radial bump \psi=1 on a ball, 0 outside a larger ball.

Extension

Multiply local data by a bump to extend it smoothly by zero across the whole manifold.

Smooth yet compactly supported — impossible for analytic functionsSlide 6 / 10
The engine behind every partition of unity is the smooth function that is flat to infinite order at a point yet positive nearby. Emphasize that this is genuinely a feature of the smooth category and impossible for analytic functions, which is why smooth manifolds are so flexible. Walk the three panels left to right: flat function, cutoff descending from one to zero, and a radial bump with visible compact support that we can multiply against local data to extend by zero.

Building Smooth Maps with Partitions of Unity

Recipe

  • Refine the cover by regular coordinate balls.
  • Put a bump inside each refined set.
  • Sum the bumps; local finiteness keeps it smooth.
  • Divide by the positive total.
  • Reindex back to the original cover.

Gluing

If local maps already agree on overlaps, they assemble into one global map — rigid and unique.

Blending

When agreement fails, take a partition-weighted average \sum_i\psi_i\,f_i — flexible, but the codomain must support weighted sums.

Gluing is exact agreement; blending is weighted averagingSlide 7 / 10
Give the five-step recipe as the operational form of the existence proof: refine by coordinate balls, drop a bump in each, sum, normalize by the positive total, reindex. Then contrast the two local-to-global strategies. Gluing is rigid and needs exact overlap agreement; blending tolerates disagreement but demands a codomain where weighted sums make sense, such as a vector space. The hard work in later chapters is proving the blended object keeps the intended geometric property.

The Smooth Structure Makes “Smooth Map” Well-Defined

Independence Lemma

Because transition maps are smooth, “smooth at p” does not depend on the chart used to test it.

Locality

Smoothness is checked on open neighborhoods; agreeing local definitions glue to a unique smooth map.

What Could Break

Without compatible charts, two observers could disagree on whether the same map is smooth.

Compatible charts ⇒ chart-independent smoothnessSlide 8 / 10
This slide answers the foundational worry: why is the definition not chart dependent? The answer is the smooth structure itself. Transition maps between overlapping charts are diffeomorphisms, so composing with them preserves ordinary smoothness, and any two legitimate charts give the same verdict at p. Pair this with locality: smoothness is an open-neighborhood condition, and local definitions that agree on overlaps glue to a unique global smooth map.

Reading the Traps

Both failures pass a careless “local” check. The step map ignores continuity and the target-chart landing condition; the cusp ignores that exact agreement at one point is not agreement of derivatives.

Locality without continuity and target-chart care is a trapSlide 9 / 10
Use the table as a diagnostic drill. The top three rows are the constructions we built: winding onto the circle, the radial diffeomorphism, and a bump extension. The bottom two are the standard traps. The step function looks locally constant on each side but violates continuity and the target-chart condition; the absolute value cusp shows that agreeing at a single point is not the same as derivatives agreeing. Both warn that locality must be applied honestly.
