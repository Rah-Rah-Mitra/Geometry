Open the chapter by separating two impulses students bring. The first is to picture a manifold as a curved surface sitting in some larger Euclidean space; the second is the actual definition, which never mentions an ambient space. Stress that the whole point of Chapter 1 is to specify exactly the local and global data that makes calculus well defined intrinsically.

Topological Manifolds: Three Hypotheses

Definition

An n-manifold M is a topological space that is:

  • locally Euclidean of dim n: every p has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of \(\mathbb{R}^n\);
  • Hausdorff: distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods;
  • second-countable: a countable basis for the topology.

Why the safeguards

The line with two origins fails Hausdorff; an uncountable discrete set fails second-countability. Both block partitions of unity later.

Local Euclidean data plus global topological hygieneSlide 2 / 10
Walk through each hypothesis and say what it buys. Locally Euclidean is the heart: near every point the space looks like an open piece of R-n, and the fixed dimension n is part of the definition. Hausdorff and second-countable feel technical, so motivate them by their failures.

Charts: Local Coordinate Systems

Coordinate chart

A pair \((U,\varphi)\) with U \subseteq M open and \(\varphi: U \to \widehat{U} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\) a homeomorphism onto an open set.

Coordinate functions

\(\varphi(p) = (x^1(p),\dots,x^n(p))\)

These are the coordinates in which later derivatives, tangent vectors, and forms are computed.

A chart is the local language for calculus, not a decorationSlide 3 / 10
Define a chart precisely as a homeomorphism from an open set of the manifold onto an open set of Euclidean space, and name its components the coordinate functions. The pedagogical message is that a chart is the local language we will compute in for the rest of the course.

Smooth Atlas: The Rule Lives on Overlaps

Transition map

\(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:\varphi(U\cap V)\to\psi(U\cap V)\)

A map between open subsets of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) — here ordinary multivariable calculus applies.

Smoothly compatible

Two charts are compatible if their transition map and its inverse are \(C^{\infty}\) (or U\cap V=\varnothing). A smooth atlas is a chart cover that is pairwise compatible.

Transition maps reduce smoothness to Euclidean calculusSlide 4 / 10
This is the conceptual hinge of the chapter. A manifold has no intrinsic notion of smooth function yet, but the transition map between two charts is a map between open subsets of R-n, where we already know what smooth means. So we transfer smoothness from Euclidean space to the manifold by demanding that every overlap map be infinitely differentiable with smooth inverse.

Smooth Structure: Pass to the Maximal Atlas

Maximal smooth atlas

A smooth atlas \(\mathcal{A}\) not properly contained in any larger smooth atlas. Equivalently, it contains every chart compatible with all of its charts.

Existence and uniqueness

Every smooth atlas is contained in a unique maximal one: adjoin all charts compatible with it. A smooth structure is such a maximal atlas; a smooth manifold is \((M,\mathcal{A})\).

Why maximal?

Maximality removes dependence on an arbitrary finite list of charts. Any compatible chart we later invent is already legal, so constructions never hinge on a chosen atlas.

In practice

To define a smooth manifold we exhibit only one convenient smooth atlas; the maximal structure it generates is then implicit and canonical.

\(\mathcal{A}\ \subseteq\ \overline{\mathcal{A}}\ =\ \{\text{all charts compatible with }\mathcal{A}\}\)
Specify one atlas; inherit the unique maximal structureSlide 5 / 10
Explain the maximality convention and why it matters. If we defined a smooth manifold as a space with a smooth atlas, then two different atlases that are mutually compatible would technically give different structures, which is awkward. The fix is to pass to the maximal atlas: include every chart compatible with the ones we have.

Examples: One Local Test, Many Constructions

A working gallery

  • \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and its open subsets — one global chart.
  • \(S^n\) via two stereographic charts; overlap map smooth.
  • Graphs of smooth f: domain projects back as a chart.
  • Projective space \(\mathbb{RP}^n\): identify antipodal directions; homogeneous-coordinate charts.
  • Products M\times N: product charts give dimension m+n.
  • \(GL(n,\mathbb{R})\): open in matrix space (det ≠ 0).
Spheres, graphs, quotients, products, matrix groups — same local logicSlide 6 / 10
Run the gallery quickly but make each construction earn its place by naming its charts. Euclidean space and open subsets are trivially manifolds with a single identity chart. The sphere is the flagship: two stereographic projections, one from the north pole and one from the south, cover it, and the overlap map is smooth, an inversion.

Manifolds with Boundary: Model on a Half-Space

Half-space model

\(\mathbb{H}^n=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n: x^n\ge 0\}\)

Charts now map onto open subsets of \(\mathbb{H}^n\) rather than \(\mathbb{R}^n\).

Boundary is intrinsic

A point is a boundary point if some chart sends it to \(x^n=0\). Smooth transitions preserve this: boundary maps to boundary, interior to interior. \(\partial M\) is itself a smooth (n-1)-manifold.

Boundary points are coordinate-independent, not artifacts of a pictureSlide 7 / 10
Introduce the boundary version by changing only the local model: open subsets of the closed upper half-space replace open subsets of Euclidean space. A point is a boundary point if a chart sends it to the hyperplane where the last coordinate vanishes. The crucial theorem, which depends on invariance of the boundary, is that this notion is intrinsic: a smooth coordinate change cannot turn an interior point into a boundary point.

Topological Properties: Toward Partitions of Unity

Consequences of the definition

A topological manifold is locally compact, locally connected, and — thanks to second-countability and Hausdorff — paracompact: every open cover has a locally finite refinement.

Partition of unity (teaser)

Subordinate to any open cover \(\{U_\alpha\}\) there exist smooth \(\psi_\alpha\ge 0\) with \(\sum_\alpha \psi_\alpha \equiv 1\) and \(\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_\alpha\subseteq U_\alpha\).

Why we care now

Partitions of unity are the tool that glues local chart constructions into global objects: metrics, vector fields, integrals.

Paracompactness makes smooth gluing possibleSlide 8 / 10
Connect the topological hypotheses to their main downstream consequence. Because a manifold is Hausdorff and second-countable, it is paracompact, meaning every open cover admits a locally finite refinement. Paracompactness, together with smooth bump functions, yields partitions of unity subordinate to any open cover: a family of nonnegative smooth functions summing to one, each supported in a cover element.

The cube obstruction

On \(\mathbb{R}\), u=x^3 is smooth but the reverse \(x=u^{1/3}\) has unbounded derivative at 0. The two charts each define a smooth structure, but they are not mutually compatible.

Recap: The Layered Object We Built

1

Topology

Locally Euclidean with fixed n, Hausdorff, second-countable — the substrate for analysis.

2

Charts & atlas

Local coordinates whose transition maps are \(C^\infty\); compatibility is the real test.

3

Structure & boundary

Maximal atlas makes it canonical; \(\mathbb{H}^n\) supplies an intrinsic boundary.

Diagnostic question for every later object

When tangent vectors, vector fields, forms, and integrals appear: in which chart is it defined, does it survive every smooth transition map, and is its value independent of the chart chosen? Chapter 1 guarantees that this question always has a coherent answer.

Close by reassembling the layers and pointing forward. The topology supplies a well-behaved substrate; charts supply local coordinates; the compatibility rule on transition maps supplies smoothness; the maximal atlas makes the smooth structure canonical; and the half-space model extends everything to spaces with boundary. Leave students with the diagnostic question they should ask of every object in the rest of the book: which chart defines it, does it transform correctly under every transition map, and is it independent of the chart chosen.
