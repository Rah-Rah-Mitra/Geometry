The Topological Manifold

Definition

$M$ is a topological $n$-manifold if it is locally Euclidean of dimension $n$, Hausdorff, and second-countable.

Locally Euclidean

Every point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Hausdorff

Distinct points lie in disjoint open sets.

Second-countable

The topology has a countable basis.

Fixed dimension

The same $n$ works everywhere; mixed-dimension spaces are excluded.

Printed pp. 1-31The Topological Manifold
A topological manifold rests on three pillars: locally Euclidean of fixed dimension, Hausdorff, and second-countable. Stress that locally Euclidean alone is too weak. The two global hypotheses rule out pathologies like the line with two origins and guarantee partitions of unity later, so do not treat them as decoration.

Why the Global Hypotheses Matter

Line with two origins

Locally Euclidean and second-countable, but not Hausdorff: the two origins have no disjoint neighborhoods, so sequence limits are not unique.

Second-countability buys

Paracompactness and existence of partitions of unity, the tool that globalizes local constructions.

Printed pp. 1-31Why the Global Hypotheses Matter
Walk students through the two safeguards concretely. The line with two origins is locally Euclidean and second-countable yet fails Hausdorff, so limits are not unique. Second-countability bounds the number of charts and yields paracompactness. Both conditions are exactly what later integration and partition-of-unity arguments will require.

Coordinate Charts

Definition

A chart is a pair $(U,\varphi)$ with $U\subseteq M$ open and $\varphi:U\to\widehat U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ a homeomorphism.

Local coordinates

The components $\varphi=(x^1,\dots,x^n)$ are the coordinate functions on $U$.

What they are for

Every future derivative, tangent vector, and differential form is computed in these coordinates.

Printed pp. 1-31Coordinate Charts
A chart is a homeomorphism from an open set of the manifold onto an open set of Euclidean space. Emphasize that the component functions are the local coordinates in which every future derivative, tangent vector, and form will be computed. A chart is the working coordinate system, not a mere label on a neighborhood.

Transition Maps on Overlaps

Definition

$\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:\varphi(U\cap V)\to\psi(U\cap V)$ is the transition map between charts $(U,\varphi)$ and $(V,\psi)$.

An honest map

It goes between open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so we can ask whether it is differentiable.

The habit

Never just check each chart; check what happens on the overlap.

Printed pp. 1-31Transition Maps on Overlaps
When two charts overlap, composing one with the inverse of the other gives a transition map between open subsets of Euclidean space. This is an honest map you can differentiate. The entire smoothness question reduces to the regularity of these overlap maps, so train students to always inspect the overlap, never just each chart alone.

Smooth Atlas

Definition

An atlas $\mathcal{A}=\{(U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha)\}$ is smooth if every transition $\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ is $C^\infty$.

Smoothly compatible

Two charts are compatible when they overlap in a way whose transition is smooth (vacuously so if they are disjoint).

Covers $M$

The chart domains $U_\alpha$ together fill the whole manifold.

Why it matters

The atlas is exactly the data needed to declare a map between manifolds smooth, independent of which chart you pick.

Printed pp. 1-31Smooth Atlas
A smooth atlas is a collection of charts covering the manifold whose every pairwise transition map is infinitely differentiable. Charts whose overlap transition is smooth are called smoothly compatible. The atlas is the package of data that finally lets us say a function or map between manifolds is itself smooth.

The Smooth Structure

Maximal atlas

A smooth structure is a maximal smooth atlas: every chart compatible with $\mathcal{A}$ is already in it.

Why maximal

Two compatible atlases generate the same calculus, so we collapse them into one canonical object.

Existence and uniqueness

Every smooth atlas extends to a unique maximal one, obtained by adding all compatible charts.

Smooth manifold

A topological manifold together with a chosen smooth structure.

Printed pp. 1-31The Smooth Structure
Two smooth atlases that are compatible determine the same calculus, so we pass to the maximal atlas, the union of all compatible charts. This maximal atlas is the smooth structure. Maximality removes any dependence on an arbitrary finite list of charts and makes the smooth manifold a well-defined object.

Compatibility Is a Real Condition

Two charts on $\mathbb{R}$

$x\ \longmapsto\ x$ versus $u=x^3$, with reverse change $x=u^{1/3}$.

The forward direction is fine

$u=x^3$ is smooth everywhere.

The reverse direction fails

$\tfrac{d}{du}u^{1/3}$ blows up at $u=0$, so the two atlases are smooth yet incompatible.

Printed pp. 1-31Compatibility Is a Real Condition
Compatibility is a genuine condition, not automatic. On the real line the standard coordinate and the cube coordinate u equals x cubed each give a smooth one-chart atlas, but the reverse change x equals u to the one-third has unbounded derivative at zero. So these two atlases are smooth yet mutually incompatible.

The Sphere via Stereographic Charts

Two charts suffice

Projection from the north pole charts $S^n\setminus\{N\}$; projection from the south pole charts $S^n\setminus\{S\}$.

Smooth overlap

On $S^n\setminus\{N,S\}$ the transition is inversion $y=\dfrac{x}{|x|^2}$, smooth for $x\neq 0$.

Printed pp. 1-31The Sphere via Stereographic Charts
The sphere is the model example. Stereographic projection from the north pole charts everything but that pole, and projection from the south pole charts everything but the other. Their overlap transition is inversion, which is smooth away from the origin. Two charts, smoothly glued, give the sphere its standard smooth structure.

A Gallery of Smooth Manifolds

Euclidean and graphs

$\mathbb{R}^n$ with one identity chart; graphs of smooth functions project to their domain.

Spheres

$S^n$ as a regular level set of $|x|^2$, or via stereographic charts.

Products

$M\times N$ is a smooth $(m+n)$-manifold with product charts.

Matrix groups

$\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$ is open in $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ since $\det\neq 0$ is an open condition.

Open subset rule

Any open subset of a smooth manifold inherits a smooth structure by restricting charts.

Quotients

$\mathbb{RP}^n$, $\mathbb{CP}^n$, and Grassmannians arise as well-behaved quotients.

Printed pp. 1-31A Gallery of Smooth Manifolds
Survey the constructions that recur all term: Euclidean space itself, graphs of smooth functions, spheres as regular level sets, real and complex projective spaces as quotients, products of manifolds, open matrix groups like the general linear group, and Grassmannians. Each one is verified by the same local-coordinate plus smooth-transition logic.

Projective Spaces and Grassmannians

Projective space

$\mathbb{RP}^n=(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\})/\!\sim$, where $x\sim\lambda x$ for $\lambda\neq 0$.

Affine charts

$U_i=\{[x]:x^i\neq 0\}$ with coordinates $x^j/x^i$; overlaps are smooth rational maps.

Complex case

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ is built the same way over $\mathbb{C}$, a smooth $2n$-manifold.

Printed pp. 1-31Projective Spaces and Grassmannians
Projective space identifies nonzero vectors that differ by a scalar; its standard charts are the affine patches where one homogeneous coordinate is nonzero. Grassmannians generalize this to k-dimensional subspaces, charted by graphs over a fixed complement. Both inherit Hausdorff, second-countable smooth structures from these explicit overlapping charts.

Manifolds with Boundary

New local model

$\mathbb{H}^n=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n: x^n\ge 0\}$, the closed upper half-space.

Two kinds of point

Interior points map to $x^n>0$; boundary points map to $x^n=0$.

Intrinsic

Boundary status is invariant under smooth charts; $\partial M$ is a manifold of dimension $n-1$.

Printed pp. 1-31Manifolds with Boundary
To allow edges we change the local model from open subsets of Euclidean space to open subsets of the closed half-space. Points hitting the bounding hyperplane are boundary points; the rest are interior. Boundary status is intrinsic, invariant under smooth charts, and the boundary is itself a manifold of one lower dimension.

Diagnostic Checklist and Recap

A space passes when

It is locally Euclidean of fixed dimension, Hausdorff, second-countable, and carries mutually smooth charts.

It fails when

Dimensions mix, points cannot be separated, or chosen charts are incompatible — like $\mathbb{R}$ with standard and cube charts at once.

The layered model

Topology first, smooth compatibility second; examples and boundary reuse the same overlap logic.

What we gained

A stable local coordinate language for tangent spaces, vector fields, integration, and forms to come.

Printed pp. 1-31Diagnostic Checklist and Recap
We close with a diagnostic checklist applied to candidate spaces. A space fails when it is not locally Euclidean, mixes dimensions, separates points poorly, or carries incompatible charts. Recap the layered model: topology first, smooth compatibility second, examples and boundary built from the same overlap logic that drives all later calculus.
