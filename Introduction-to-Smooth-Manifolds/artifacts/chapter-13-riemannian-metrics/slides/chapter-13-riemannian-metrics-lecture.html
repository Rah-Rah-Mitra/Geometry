A Riemannian Metric Is Local Linear Algebra That Varies Smoothly

Definition

A Riemannian metric on a smooth manifold M is a smooth symmetric covariant 2-tensor field g whose value gp is positive definite on each tangent space TpM.

g = gij dxi dxj,   (gij(p)) > 0
The word metric is overloaded. In this chapter, the tensor g comes first; the metric-space distance dg is constructed from curve length later.
v = vi ∂i,   w = wj ∂j
gp(v,w) = gij(p)viwj
1
Norm
Length is |v|g = √g(v,v).
2
Angle
Angles come from the same bilinear form.
3
Frame
Local frames can be orthonormalized smoothly.
4
Density
In coordinates, √det(G) rescales volume.
Frame the definition as a familiar inner product moved onto every tangent space. The smoothness condition is what lets the measurements change coherently as the base point moves, while positive definiteness is what makes length and angle behave as students expect.

Why Partitions Of Unity Preserve Positivity

Existence Roadmap
  • Choose coordinate charts and put a Euclidean metric in each chart.
  • Use a smooth partition of unity to weight the local metrics.
  • At each point, at least one weight is positive and all weights are nonnegative.
  • A positive weighted average of positive definite forms stays positive definite.
Numerical Witness

The notebook patch has nonnegative weights summing to one, minimum eigenvalue 0.647155, and minimum determinant 0.940313.

Present this as the proof's one real idea. Smooth partitions of unity handle global assembly, but the reason the result is still Riemannian is purely convex: positive definite matrices form a cone closed under positive weighted averaging.

Pullback Metrics Are Gram Matrices Of Pushed-Forward Tangents

Interactive artifact: the helicoid F(u,v) = (u cos v, u sin v, v) colored by induced area density.
Pullback Criterion

If F: M → N is smooth and g is Riemannian on N, then F*g is Riemannian exactly when F is an immersion.

Helicoid Check
JTJ = diag(1, 1 + u2)

The symbolic residual is zero and the sampled minimum eigenvalue is 1.0. The entry 1 + u2 measures the growing cost of moving in the helical direction.

For a submanifold inclusion, this same pullback is the induced metric: restrict the ambient metric to tangent vectors of the submanifold.
Connect the formula to the students' linear algebra memory: columns of the Jacobian are tangent vectors in the target, and J transpose J records all their pairwise inner products. The immersion condition is exactly what prevents the Gram matrix from losing rank.

Orthonormal Frames Are Easy; Flat Coordinates Are Special

Flatness Test

The chapter's flatness criterion says that a metric is locally Euclidean exactly when one can find local coordinates whose coordinate frame is orthonormal.

Common Mistake

An orthonormal frame need not be a coordinate frame. Curvature begins to appear when the best local measuring sticks cannot be integrated into Euclidean coordinates.

Use this slide to separate two facts that students often blend together. A metric always gives local orthonormal frames, but flatness asks for something stricter: coordinates whose coordinate vector fields are orthonormal, which requires an integrability condition.

Distance Is The Infimum Of Metric Lengths

Curve Length
Lg(γ) = ∫ab |γ'(t)|g dt

The distance between two points is the infimum of these lengths over piecewise smooth curves joining them.

Conformal Experiment

The straight sample has length 4.854; the grid path has length 4.741 because it avoids the high-density region.

Metric-Space Checks

The sampled densities are positive, distances are positive, and the triangle slack in the check is 5.503.

Stress that the distance function is not an extra axiom. It is built from the Riemannian norm of velocities, then minimized over curves. The figure is a computational analogy for the infimum: paths bend away from regions where the metric makes motion expensive.

The Metric Identifies Vectors And Covectors

Flat And Sharp
Xflat = gijXi dxj,   ωsharp = gijωj∂i

Lowering an index multiplies by the metric matrix. Raising an index multiplies by the inverse metric.

Gradient

The gradient is grad f = (df)sharp. In polar coordinates for the Euclidean metric, G = diag(1,r2), so the angular component receives the factor 1/r2.

Notebook checks: sharp(flat(X)) = X and <grad f,Y>g = df(Y) both have zero symbolic residual.
Make the point that the tangent-cotangent identification is not natural without a metric. The same covector df becomes a vector field only after the inverse metric is applied, which is why the polar-coordinate gradient differs from the naive coordinate derivative.

What Breaks When Positivity Is Removed?

Pseudo-Riemannian Boundary

A pseudo-Riemannian metric is symmetric and nondegenerate with constant signature, but it is not required to be positive definite.

  • Nonzero null vectors can have squared length zero.
  • There is no Riemannian norm or angle theory built from positivity.
  • The partition-of-unity proof does not transfer: positive averages of nondegenerate forms can become degenerate.
Checks

The Lorentz matrix determinant is -1, the sampled null vector has squared length 0, and the averaged Lorentz forms are degenerate at the midpoint.

Treat this as a boundary marker for the chapter. The tensor language survives, but the geometry changes sharply: null directions show that nondegenerate does not mean positive, and the clean existence argument for Riemannian metrics no longer applies.
