Stokes For Chains

Integration Respects Boundary And Coboundary

\(\int_\sigma d\omega=\int_{\partial\sigma}\omega\)

Standard Triangle Check

For \(\omega=x\,dy\), \(d\omega=dx\wedge dy\). The area integral and boundary integral are both \(1/2\).

trueStokes identity holds
Stokes compatibilitySlide 4 / 10
This is the local compatibility engine. On the standard triangle, the form x dy has exterior derivative dx wedge dy. The area integral is one half, and only the slanted edge contributes one half to the boundary integral, so Stokes holds at the chain level.

Applied Lab

What Breaks The Bridge?

Wrong Signs

Change alternating boundary signs and \(\partial^2=0\) can fail.

Nonsmooth Simplex

Pullback of a differential form is not justified at the nonsmooth point.

No Stokes

Exact forms would no longer automatically define zero singular cochains.

Instructor Prompt

For each proof step, name the exact compatibility it needs: boundary signs, smoothing, Stokes, naturality, Mayer-Vietoris, or the five lemma.

Proof diagnosticsSlide 9 / 10
The applied lab is a sanity test for the theorem's dependencies. If boundary signs are changed, boundary squared will fail; if a nonsmooth simplex is used without smoothing, pulling back forms is not justified; if Stokes compatibility is dropped, exact forms no longer automatically define zero singular cochains.

Chains

Singular chains encode topological cycles with signed boundaries.

Smoothing

Smooth singular chains have the same homology on smooth manifolds.

Stokes

Integration respects boundary and coboundary, so it descends to cohomology.

Pairing

Isomorphism
