Singular Simplices and Chains

Singular simplex

A continuous map $\sigma:\Delta^p\to M$ from the standard $p$-simplex into the space.

Singular chain

A finite formal sum $c=\sum_i a_i\,\sigma_i$ of simplices with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{R}$.

Standard simplex

$\Delta^p=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1}: t_i\ge 0,\ \textstyle\sum_i t_i=1\}$

Printed pp. 467-489Singular Simplices and Chains
Start concretely. A singular p-simplex is just a continuous map from the standard simplex into the space, and a chain is a finite formal sum of these with integer or real coefficients. Everything that follows is bookkeeping on these maps, so make sure students see the standard simplex clearly.

The Boundary Operator

Alternating faces

$\partial\sigma=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^i\,\sigma\!\circ\! F_{i,p}$

Signs are essential

The alternating signs are exactly what forces $\partial\circ\partial=0$, the identity that defines homology.

Cancellation

On a triangle the three oriented edges cancel at every vertex, giving zero boundary of boundary.

Printed pp. 467-489The Boundary Operator
The boundary is the alternating sum of restrictions to faces. Stress that the alternating signs are not decoration; they are exactly what makes boundary of a boundary vanish. Show the triangle whose three oriented edges cancel at every vertex, and tie it to the matrix identity in the notebook.

Singular Homology and Cohomology

Homology

$H_p(M)=\dfrac{\ker\partial_p}{\operatorname{im}\partial_{p+1}}$: cycles modulo boundaries.

Cohomology

$H^p(M;\mathbb{R})$ is the dual: real functionals on chains that vanish on boundaries.

Real coefficients

$H^p(M;\mathbb{R})\cong \operatorname{Hom}\!\big(H_p(M),\mathbb{R}\big)$

Why real?

Integration of forms produces real numbers, so we pair against real singular cohomology.

Printed pp. 467-489Singular Homology and Cohomology
Cycles are chains with zero boundary; boundaries are images of the boundary map; homology is their quotient. Real singular cohomology is the dual, the real functionals on chains that vanish on boundaries. Emphasize that we use real coefficients here precisely so integration of forms can land in the picture.

Smooth chains

Restrict to smooth simplices $\sigma:\Delta^p\to M$ so forms can be pulled back.

Integrating Forms over Chains

Definition

$\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega=\int_{\Delta^p}\sigma^{*}\omega$

Extend linearly

For a chain $c=\sum a_i\sigma_i$, set $\int_c\omega=\sum a_i\int_{\sigma_i}\omega$.

Periods

A closed $p$-form fed to a $p$-cycle returns one real number, its period.

Printed pp. 467-489Integrating Forms over Chains
Define integration of a p-form over a smooth p-chain by pulling back and integrating over the standard simplex, then extending linearly. This is the raw operation behind everything. Make sure students see that a closed form fed to a cycle returns a single real number, the period.

Stokes Makes It Well Defined

Stokes for chains

$\displaystyle\int_{c}d\eta=\int_{\partial c}\eta$

Exact over cycles

If $\partial c=0$ then $\int_c d\eta=\int_{\partial c}\eta=0$: exact forms integrate to zero on cycles.

Closed over boundaries

If $\omega$ is closed and $c=\partial b$, then $\int_{\partial b}\omega=\int_b d\omega=0$.

Consequence

Both vanishings together say the pairing $(\,[\omega],[c]\,)\mapsto\int_c\omega$ depends only on the cohomology and homology classes.

Descends to classes

$[\omega]\in H^p_{dR},\quad [c]\in H_p \;\Rightarrow\; \int_c\omega \text{ well defined}$

Printed pp. 467-489Stokes Makes It Well Defined
Stokes for chains says the integral of d-eta over a chain equals the integral of eta over its boundary. Two consequences: exact forms integrate to zero over cycles, and closed forms integrate to zero over boundaries. Those two vanishings are exactly what is needed for the map on cohomology to be well defined.

Stokes Check on the Triangle

Take

$\eta=x\,dy,\qquad d\eta=dx\wedge dy$

Over the triangle

$\int_{\Delta}d\eta$ is the area of the standard triangle, equal to $\tfrac12$.

Over the boundary

Only the slanted edge contributes; that line integral also equals $\tfrac12$. Stokes confirmed.

Ground the abstraction with the notebook's exact computation. Take eta equal to x dy on the standard triangle. Its exterior derivative is the area form, so the integral over the triangle is one half. Only the slanted boundary edge contributes, and that line integral also gives one half. Stokes confirmed.

The de Rham Homomorphism

The map

$\mathcal{I}:H^p_{dR}(M)\to H^p(M;\mathbb{R})$

On classes

$\mathcal{I}[\omega]$ is the cohomology class whose value on $[c]$ is $\int_c\omega$.

Well defined

Stokes guarantees the value is independent of the chosen representatives on both sides.

Linear and natural

$\mathcal{I}$ is linear in $[\omega]$ and commutes with maps induced by smooth maps $F:M\to N$.

Naturality

$\mathcal{I}\circ F^{*}=F^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}$

Printed pp. 467-489The de Rham Homomorphism
Assemble the pieces. The de Rham map sends a de Rham class to the singular cohomology class that integrates it over smooth cycles. Stokes guarantees this descends to cohomology on both sides. State it cleanly as a natural homomorphism from de Rham to real singular cohomology in each degree.

Periods and the Pairing

Pairing

$\langle [\omega],[c]\rangle=\int_c\omega\in\mathbb{R}$

Circle example

The normalized angular form $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\,d\theta$ on $S^1$ returns the winding number of a cycle.

Linear in cycles

Positive, reversed, constant, and double loops give $1,\,-1,\,0,\,2$.

Printed pp. 467-489Periods and the Pairing
Concretely the map is a pairing: a cohomology class and a homology class produce a number, the period. On the circle the normalized angular form returns the winding number of a cycle. Walk through positive, reversed, constant, and double loops so students feel the pairing is linear and honest.

The de Rham Theorem

Theorem

$\mathcal{I}:H^p_{dR}(M)\;\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\;H^p(M;\mathbb{R})$

Statement

For every smooth manifold $M$ and every degree $p$, the de Rham homomorphism is an isomorphism.

Meaning

De Rham cohomology computes the real singular cohomology. An analytic invariant equals a topological one.

Payoff

Forms and $d$ on one side, cycles and $\partial$ on the other, carry exactly the same information.

Printed pp. 467-489The de Rham Theorem
State the theorem. The de Rham homomorphism is an isomorphism, so de Rham cohomology computes real singular cohomology of any smooth manifold. This is the payoff: an analytic invariant built from forms and the exterior derivative equals a purely topological one built from cycles and boundaries.

Proof: Mayer-Vietoris and the Five Lemma

Commuting ladder

$\mathcal{I}$ commutes with pullbacks and with Mayer-Vietoris connecting maps.

Two sequences

The de Rham and singular long exact sequences sit in one commutative diagram.

Five lemma

If the neighboring vertical maps are isomorphisms, the middle one is too.

Sketch the architecture. Integration commutes with pullbacks and with Mayer-Vietoris connecting maps, so the de Rham and singular long exact sequences sit in one commutative ladder. The five lemma then forces the middle map to be an isomorphism whenever its neighbors are. Naturality is the engine.

Proof: Local to Global

Base case

Convex open sets are de Rham: both cohomologies vanish above degree zero and are one-dimensional below.

Finite covers

Mayer-Vietoris and the five lemma upgrade a two-set de Rham cover, then induct on the number of sets.

General manifold

A countable, locally finite arrangement of such pieces reaches any smooth manifold.

Three load-bearing ideas

  • Stokes makes integration a cochain map.
  • Smoothing shows smooth chains suffice.
  • Naturality drives the five-lemma induction.
Conclusion

$H^p_{dR}(M)\cong H^p(M;\mathbb{R})$ for all $M,p$

Printed pp. 467-489Proof: Local to Global
Finish with the induction. Convex sets are de Rham because both cohomologies are trivial above degree zero. Mayer-Vietoris plus the five lemma upgrades finite de Rham covers, and a countable locally finite arrangement reaches any manifold. Recap that Stokes, smoothing, and naturality are the three load-bearing ideas.
