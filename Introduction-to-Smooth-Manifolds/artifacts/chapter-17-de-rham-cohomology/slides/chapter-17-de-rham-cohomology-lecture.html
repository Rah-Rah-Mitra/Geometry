Closed forms modulo exact forms — measuring the holes of a manifold

The driving question

When can we solve $d\eta = \omega$ globally? A necessary condition is $d\omega = 0$. When is it sufficient?

The obstruction

On a manifold with holes, a closed form can fail to be exact. That failure is an invariant we can compute.

The de Rham Complex

A cochain complex

The exterior derivative chains the spaces of forms together, degree by degree.

The key identity

$d \circ d = 0$. Every exact form is closed, so each image sits inside the next kernel.

Closed and exact

$Z^p = \ker(d_p), \quad B^p = \operatorname{im}(d_{p-1})$

$d^2 = 0 \implies B^p \subseteq Z^p$.

Printed pp. 440-466The de Rham Complex
Everything rests on d squared equals zero, which guarantees images sit inside kernels. I want students to see the cochain complex as a chain of vector spaces and maps, with closed forms as kernels and exact forms as images stacked one stage earlier in the sequence.

The Cohomology Groups

Definition

$$H^p_{dR}(M) = \dfrac{\ker d_p}{\operatorname{im} d_{p-1}} = \dfrac{Z^p(M)}{B^p(M)}$$

What a class is

A cohomology class $[\omega]$ collects all closed forms differing from $\omega$ by an exact form.

Why it matters

$[\omega] \neq 0$ means $\omega$ is closed but not exact — a genuine global obstruction to solving $d\eta = \omega$.

A real vector space

$H^p_{dR}(M)$ is a quotient vector space; its dimension is the $p$-th Betti number $b_p$.

Printed pp. 440-466Cohomology Groups
Here we define the p-th de Rham cohomology as closed modulo exact. Stress that this is a quotient vector space, that its elements are equivalence classes of closed forms differing by exact forms, and that a nonzero class is a genuine global obstruction to integration.

Degree Zero Counts Components

Closed 0-forms

$df = 0$ means $f$ is locally constant. There are no exact 0-forms, so $H^0 = Z^0$.

Result

$\dim H^0_{dR}(M) = \#\{\text{connected components}\}$

Three blobs

Here $\dim H^0 = 3$: the data of $f$ is one constant per piece.

Printed pp. 440-466H-zero Counts Components
The degree zero group is the friendliest case. A closed zero-form is a locally constant function, exactness is empty in degree zero, so the dimension of H-zero equals the number of connected components. This grounds cohomology in something students already picture clearly.

The Angle Form on the Punctured Plane

The form

$$\omega = \dfrac{-y\,dx + x\,dy}{x^2 + y^2}$$

Closed

A direct computation gives $d\omega = 0$ on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$.

Not exact

Its integral around the unit circle is $2\pi \neq 0$, so no global potential exists.

Printed pp. 440-466The Angle Form
This is the chapter's signature example. The angle form on the punctured plane is closed everywhere yet has loop integral two pi around the origin, so it cannot be globally exact. Walk the room through why local antiderivatives exist but cannot be glued consistently.

Pullback Descends to Cohomology

Naturality of d

$F^*(d\omega) = d(F^*\omega)$

Induced map

$F^*$ sends closed to closed and exact to exact, so $F^* : H^p(N) \to H^p(M)$ is well defined.

Functor laws

$(\mathrm{id})^* = \mathrm{id}$ and $(G \circ F)^* = F^* \circ G^*$ — contravariant in the map.

Diffeomorphism invariance

A diffeomorphism induces an isomorphism on cohomology, so the groups are smooth invariants.

Printed pp. 440-466Induced Maps
Because pullback commutes with the exterior derivative, a smooth map descends to a linear map on cohomology. Emphasize the functor laws: identity goes to identity and composition reverses order, so diffeomorphic manifolds automatically have isomorphic cohomology.

Homotopy Invariance

Theorem

If $F \simeq G$ are homotopic, then $F^* = G^*$ on cohomology.

Homotopy operator

$d h + h d = i_1^* - i_0^*$

Consequence

Homotopy-equivalent spaces have isomorphic de Rham cohomology.

Printed pp. 440-466Homotopy Invariance
The deep theorem: homotopic maps induce equal cohomology maps. The engine is the homotopy operator h satisfying d h plus h d equals the difference of the two endpoint pullbacks. I show the cylinder picture so students see integration along the interval direction producing an exact term.

The Poincaré Lemma

Lemma

On a contractible (or star-shaped) region, every closed $p$-form with $p \geq 1$ is exact.

Cohomology of $\mathbb{R}^n$

$H^p_{dR}(\mathbb{R}^n) = \begin{cases}\mathbb{R}, & p=0\\ 0, & p>0\end{cases}$

Local triviality

Cohomology is purely a global measurement; locally forms are always exact.

Printed pp. 440-466Poincare Lemma
Homotopy invariance pays off immediately. A contractible or star-shaped region is homotopy equivalent to a point, so all higher cohomology vanishes. Every closed p-form on such a region is exact. This is the local triviality that makes cohomology a purely global measurement.

Computing the Circle

Result

$H^0_{dR}(S^1) = \mathbb{R}, \qquad H^1_{dR}(S^1) = \mathbb{R}$

The generator

The angle form $d\theta$ generates $H^1(S^1)$; integrating it over the circle gives $2\pi$.

Same hole

$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ retracts onto $S^1$, so it shares this cohomology exactly.

Printed pp. 440-466Computing the Circle
Now we compute. The circle has H-zero and H-one both equal to R; the angle form generates the one-dimensional first cohomology. Connect this back to the punctured plane, which deformation retracts to the circle and therefore shares its cohomology exactly.

The Mayer–Vietoris Sequence

The setup

Cover $M = U \cup V$ by two open sets. Restriction and difference maps weave the pieces together.

Long exact sequence

$\cdots \to H^p(M) \to H^p(U)\!\oplus\!H^p(V) \to H^p(U\!\cap\!V) \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} H^{p+1}(M) \to \cdots$

The connecting map $\delta$

This is where new global classes appear: a mismatch on the overlap becomes a class in higher degree.

Why it computes

Choose $U, V$ contractible so their cohomology is trivial; then exactness pins down $H^*(M)$.

Printed pp. 440-466Mayer-Vietoris
This is the main computational machine. An open cover by U and V produces a long exact sequence linking the cohomology of the union, the pieces, and the intersection. The connecting homomorphism is where new global classes are born from mismatches on overlaps.

Mayer–Vietoris on the Circle

Degree 0–1 segment

$0 \to H^0(S^1) \to H^0(U)\!\oplus\!H^0(V) \to H^0(U\!\cap\!V) \to H^1(S^1) \to 0$

The bookkeeping

Ranks $1, 2, 2$ force a one-dimensional cokernel: $\delta$ produces exactly the circle generator, so $H^1 = \mathbb{R}$.

Printed pp. 440-466MV on the Circle
I run the circle through Mayer-Vietoris concretely. Two contractible arcs whose intersection has two components force a one-dimensional cokernel, and the connecting map produces exactly the generator of H-one. This rank bookkeeping is the heart of the notebook computation.

Spheres and Top-Degree Cohomology

Spheres, by induction

$H^p_{dR}(S^n) = \begin{cases}\mathbb{R}, & p=0 \text{ or } p=n\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$

Top degree

A compact connected oriented $n$-manifold has $H^n_{dR}(M) = \mathbb{R}$, detected by integration.

The detector

$$[\omega] \;\longmapsto\; \int_M \omega$$

A top form is exact iff its total integral vanishes.

Hypotheses matter

Compactness, orientability, and choice of support all change the answer — do not skip them.

Printed pp. 440-466Spheres and Top Degree
The sphere computation falls out by induction with Mayer-Vietoris: the n-sphere has R in degrees zero and n and zero between. Top-degree cohomology of a compact connected oriented manifold is R, detected by integrating an orientation form over the whole space.

Degree Theory

By integration

For $F: M \to N$ between compact oriented $n$-manifolds, $\int_M F^*\omega = (\deg F)\int_N \omega$.

By signed preimages

$\deg F = \sum_{p \in F^{-1}(q)} \operatorname{sign}\det dF_p$ at a regular value $q$.

Circle example

$z \mapsto z^k \implies \deg = k$

Printed pp. 440-466Degree of a Map
The payoff is degree theory. A smooth map between compact oriented manifolds of equal dimension carries an integer degree, computable either by comparing integrals of a pulled-back top form or by counting signed preimages of a regular value. For the circle map z to z to the k, both give k.

Recap: Holes Become Numbers

Definition

$H^p_{dR} = $ closed modulo exact — the obstruction to solving $d\eta = \omega$.

Local vs global

Poincaré lemma kills cohomology locally; nontriviality is purely topological.

Invariance

Homotopy-equivalent spaces share cohomology; it is a topological invariant.

Computation

Mayer–Vietoris assembles cohomology from simple covers; $H^0$ counts components.

Sample computations

$H^1(S^1) = \mathbb{R}, \quad H^p(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0\ (p>0)$

Payoff

Top-degree cohomology gives every map a homotopy-invariant integer degree.

Printed pp. 440-466Recap
We close by tying the thread together. Cohomology measures the obstruction to global exactness, the Poincare lemma kills it locally, homotopy invariance makes it topological, and Mayer-Vietoris computes it. Holes become numbers, and those numbers feed degree theory and the rest of differential topology.
