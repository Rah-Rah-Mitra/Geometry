From Arrows to Operators

The Euclidean clue

An arrow $v$ at $a\in\mathbb{R}^n$ already acts on functions as a directional derivative.

What survives

The operator $D_v$ needs no ambient space, so it transplants onto a manifold.

Base point matters

The same components at a different point give a different operator.

Directional derivative

$$D_v f(a) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} v^i\, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x^i}(a)$$

Reading

$v$ does not move $a$; it measures how $f$ changes at $a$.

Printed pp. 50-76From Arrows to Operators
In Euclidean space an arrow at a point is also a directional-derivative operator. That operator is what survives onto a manifold. Stress that the base point is part of the data: the same components at a different point give a different operator, because differentiation happens at the attachment point, not in the air.

Derivations at a Point

Definition

$v:\,C^\infty(M)\to\mathbb{R}$ is linear and

$$v(fg) = f(p)\,v(g) + g(p)\,v(f)$$

Consequence

Linearity plus Leibniz forces $v(c)=0$ on every constant $c$.

Why this is the right axiom

The product rule is the only fact about differentiation that the definition keeps, and everything else follows from it.

The space

Such derivations form a vector space $T_pM$, the tangent space at $p$.

Printed pp. 50-76Derivations at a Point
Define a tangent vector as a linear map on smooth germs satisfying the Leibniz rule at p. Show that linearity plus Leibniz forces it to annihilate constants. The product rule is the load-bearing axiom; everything coordinate-free about tangent vectors descends from these two lines, so spend real time here.

The Coordinate Basis

Coordinate vectors

$$\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p f = \frac{\partial \hat f}{\partial x^i}(\hat p)$$

Basis & dimension

These $n$ derivations form a basis, so $\dim T_pM = \dim M = n$.

Printed pp. 50-76The Coordinate Basis
A chart turns the abstract derivation space into components. The coordinate vectors are the pullbacks of the standard partials under the chart. Emphasize dimension: the tangent space has the same dimension as the manifold, and these n vectors form a basis. The sphere picture audits the formula, it is not the definition.

Coordinate Components

Expansion

$$v = \sum_i v^i \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p, \qquad v^i = v(x^i)$$

Bridge

Acting on the coordinate functions reads off the components.

Not intrinsic

The tuple $(v^1,\dots,v^n)$ belongs to a chart. Change the chart and the tuple changes, even though $v$ is the same vector.

Computable

This is the step that lets us run symbolic checks on tangent vectors.

Printed pp. 50-76Coordinate Components
Every tangent vector expands in the coordinate basis with components given by its action on the coordinate functions. This is the bridge between the abstract derivation and a computable n-tuple. Warn students that the n-tuple is not intrinsic: it belongs to a chart, and a different chart gives a different tuple for the same vector.

The Differential (Pushforward)

Definition

$dF_p: T_pM \to T_{F(p)}N$

$$\big(dF_p(v)\big)(h) = v(h\circ F)$$

Coordinate-free

Pulling back $h$ along $F$ is what makes this well defined.

Printed pp. 50-76The Differential
The differential, or pushforward, of a smooth map sends tangent vectors at p to tangent vectors at F(p). The defining formula is dF_p(v) acting on h equals v acting on h composed with F. It is coordinate-free by construction. Make sure students see that the pullback of the test function is what makes this well defined.

The Jacobian Is the Matrix of $dF_p$

Coordinate matrix

$$dF_p\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right) = \sum_j \frac{\partial F^j}{\partial x^i}(p)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y^j}$$

Check

Velocity of the image curve equals $J_F(p)\,\gamma'(0)$.

Printed pp. 50-76The Jacobian Matrix
Once charts are fixed on both sides, the differential becomes a matrix: the Jacobian of the coordinate representative. Walk through the curve picture. The velocity of the image curve equals the Jacobian times the domain velocity. This is the concrete payoff of the abstract definition, and it is exactly what the notebook checks symbolically.

The Chain Rule as Functoriality

Composition

$$d(G\circ F)_p = dG_{F(p)} \circ dF_p$$

Identity

$$d(\mathrm{Id}_M)_p = \mathrm{Id}_{T_pM}$$

In coordinates

The Jacobians multiply: $J_{G\circ F} = J_G\, J_F$.

Big picture

One law reorganizes the entire multivariable chain rule into a statement about tangent spaces.

Printed pp. 50-76The Chain Rule
Functoriality is the chain rule in categorical clothing. The differential of a composite is the composite of differentials, and the identity map pushes forward to the identity. In coordinates this is just matrix multiplication of Jacobians. Tell students this single law reorganizes a whole semester of multivariable calculus into one clean statement.

Components Obey the Transition Jacobian

Transformation law

$$\tilde v^j = \sum_i \frac{\partial \tilde x^j}{\partial x^i}\, v^i$$

Polar warning

The angular basis vector has length $r$; the chart degenerates at $r=0$.

Printed pp. 50-76Change of Coordinates
Components transform by the transition Jacobian, never by copying the same tuple. Polar coordinates make this vivid: the angular basis vector has length r and the chart dies at the origin. The applied lab sweeps r and shows the naive copy law accumulating error while the correct law has zero residual.

Velocity Vectors of Curves

Curve velocity

$$\gamma'(0)\,f = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0} (f\circ\gamma)(t)$$

Realization

Every tangent vector is some curve's velocity, so derivation and velocity pictures agree.

Boundary care

Two-sided curves inside a half-plane force zero normal velocity at a boundary point.

Endpoint curves

Allowing one-sided derivatives realizes inward and outward normal vectors too.

Printed pp. 50-76Velocity of a Curve
A curve produces a tangent vector by differentiating test functions along it. Conversely every tangent vector is some curve's velocity, so the derivation and velocity pictures coincide. At a boundary point the curve domain matters: two-sided curves force zero normal velocity, while one-sided endpoint curves realize inward and outward vectors.

The Tangent Bundle $TM$

Definition

$$TM = \coprod_{p\in M} T_pM, \qquad \pi(p,v) = p$$

Smooth $2n$-manifold

Charts pair base coordinates with velocity coordinates, so $TM$ is locally a product.

Printed pp. 50-76The Tangent Bundle
Collect all tangent spaces into one set, the tangent bundle, a smooth 2n-manifold. Its charts pair base coordinates with velocity coordinates, so locally it is a product. The projection forgets the velocity and returns the base point. Stress that local product structure does not force a global product; that gap is the seed of bundle theory.

Alternative Definitions, Same Object

Derivations

Clean for proofs and intrinsically coordinate-free; abstract at first contact.

Curve velocities

Most intuitive; needs an equivalence relation to become a vector space.

Component tuples

Fast to compute, but chart-bound and carrying a transition law on its back.

Strategy

Switch viewpoints fluidly: each definition buys ease somewhere and costs it elsewhere.

Printed pp. 50-76Alternative Definitions
The chapter compares the derivation, velocity, and equivalence-class definitions. They all describe the same object but at different costs: derivations are clean for proofs, velocities are intuitive, and physicist tuples are fast to compute but chart-bound. Encourage students to switch viewpoints fluidly rather than committing to one.

Recap: The Tangent Toolkit

Vector = derivation

Attached to $p$, it differentiates functions there.

Differential

$dF_p(v)(h)=v(h\circ F)$ pushes vectors forward.

Jacobian

The matrix of $dF_p$ once charts are chosen.

Transition law

Components transform by the transition Jacobian.

Tangent bundle

$TM$ is a smooth $2n$-manifold; $T$ is functorial.

One line to remember

$$d(G\circ F)_p = dG_{F(p)}\circ dF_p$$

Printed pp. 50-76Recap
Recap the through-line: a tangent vector is attached to a point and differentiates functions there. The differential is its pushforward, the Jacobian is its matrix once charts are chosen, components obey a transition law, and the tangent bundle packages everything functorially. These invariants are exactly the executable checks in the notebook.
