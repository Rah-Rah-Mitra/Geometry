Integral Curves: Velocity Equals Field Value

Definition

$\gamma'(t) = V_{\gamma(t)} \quad\text{for all } t$

Two models

For $V=\partial_x$ the curves are horizontal lines at unit speed. For $W=-y\,\partial_x+x\,\partial_y$ the nonzero curves are circles.

Chart view

In coordinates this is the autonomous system $\dot{\gamma}^i = V^i(\gamma)$.

Printed pp. 205-248Integral Curves
Start from the defining equation rather than any picture. An integral curve is a curve whose velocity at every instant equals the field evaluated at the point it currently occupies. In a chart this is a first-order autonomous ordinary differential equation, so the geometric notion becomes the analytic one we already know how to solve locally.

Existence and Uniqueness

Theorem (ODE)

$\dot{\gamma}^i = V^i(\gamma),\quad \gamma(0)=p$

has a unique solution on an open interval about $0$.

Existence

Smoothness of $V$ gives an integral curve through each point on some short interval.

Uniqueness

Two integral curves with the same initial point agree wherever both are defined.

Smooth dependence

Solutions depend smoothly on the starting point $p$ — the key to building flows.

Printed pp. 205-248Existence and Uniqueness
The fundamental existence and uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations does all the analytic heavy lifting. Smoothness of the field gives a unique integral curve through each starting point on some open time interval, and curves sharing initial data agree where both are defined. Solutions also depend smoothly on the initial point, which we will need immediately for flows.

Flows: A One-Parameter Group

Group law

$\theta_t \circ \theta_s = \theta_{t+s},\quad \theta_0 = \mathrm{id}$

Two readings

Fix $p$: trace its integral curve $t\mapsto\theta(t,p)$. Fix $t$: a diffeomorphism $\theta_t$ of the manifold.

Inverse

$\theta_t^{-1}=\theta_{-t}$ — sliding backward undoes sliding forward.

Printed pp. 205-248Flows
Solving every initial value problem at once assembles the integral curves into a single map theta. Fixing the point traces its curve; fixing the time gives a diffeomorphism that slides the whole manifold along the curves. The group law theta_t after theta_s equals theta_{t+s} makes this a one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms, the algebraic heart of the chapter.

The Fundamental Theorem on Flows

Theorem

Every smooth $V$ generates a unique maximal flow $\theta$ on an open domain $\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times M$ with $\{0\}\times M\subseteq\mathcal{D}$.

Generator

$\left.\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\theta(t,p) = V_p$

Open slices

Each $M_t=\{p:(t,p)\in\mathcal{D}\}$ is open, and $\theta_t:M_t\to M_{-t}$ is a diffeomorphism.

Local group law

$\theta_t\circ\theta_s=\theta_{t+s}$ holds whenever both sides are defined.

Printed pp. 205-248Fundamental Theorem on Flows
Every smooth vector field generates exactly one maximal flow. Its domain is an open subset of the real line times the manifold containing the zero slice, each time slice is open, and on it theta_t is a diffeomorphism onto another slice with inverse theta_{-t}. The vector field is recovered as the infinitesimal generator, the t-derivative of the flow at time zero.

Flow Domains and Maximality

Blow-up

$V=x^2\partial_x:\ \ \theta(t,x_0)=\dfrac{x_0}{1-tx_0}$

The maximal interval stops at the blow-up time $t=1/x_0$.

Missing points

On the punctured plane, plain translation escapes by running into the removed origin.

Printed pp. 205-248Flow Domains and Maximality
A local solution is not a global action; the domain is part of the object. The field x squared d-dx on the line blows up in finite time, while plain translation on the punctured plane escapes by running into the missing origin. Maximality records these genuine obstructions: blow-up, missing points, and escape from every compact set.

Complete Vector Fields

Definition

$V$ is complete if $\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{R}\times M$: the flow runs for all time, a global $\mathbb{R}$-action.

Escape Lemma

If a maximal integral curve is defined on a finite interval, it eventually leaves every compact set.

Compact support $\Rightarrow$ complete

A field that vanishes outside a compact set has a global flow.

Corollary

Every smooth vector field on a compact manifold is complete.

Printed pp. 205-248Complete Vector Fields
A vector field is complete when its flow is defined for all real time, giving a genuine one-parameter group acting on the whole manifold. Compactly supported fields are complete, and as a corollary so is every smooth field on a compact manifold, since a curve leaving every compact set is the only way maximal time can stay finite.

Flowout and Canonical Coordinates

Flowout map

$\Psi(t,s) = \theta_t\big(X(s)\big)$

$X$ parametrizes a hypersurface $S$ transverse to $V$.

Canonical form

Where $V\neq0$, there are coordinates with $V=\partial_t$: every nonvanishing field is locally straight, parallel flow.

Printed pp. 205-248Flowout and Canonical Form
Flow a hypersurface that the field never touches tangentially, and the flowout map sending pair t and s to theta_t of the point becomes a local diffeomorphism near the surface. In the resulting coordinates the vector field is simply the coordinate field d-dt. This canonical-form theorem says any nonvanishing field looks locally like straight, parallel, unit-speed flow lines.

The Lie Derivative of a Vector Field

Definition

$(\mathcal{L}_V W)_p = \left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\big(d\theta_{-t}\big)_{\theta_t(p)} W_{\theta_t(p)}$

Why pull back?

Field values at nearby points live in different tangent spaces; the flow carries them to one common space.

Printed pp. 205-248The Lie Derivative
Comparing one vector field to another is subtle because nearby field values live in different tangent spaces. The flow of V supplies the missing identification: pull W back along the inverse flow into one tangent space, then differentiate at time zero. That derivative is the Lie derivative of W with respect to V, the infinitesimal change of W as the flow drags it.
Introduction to Smooth ManifoldsChapter 9

$\mathcal{L}_V W = [V,W]$

Theorem

$\mathcal{L}_V W = [V,W]$

The flow-derivative is exactly the Lie bracket of operators.

Worked model

$V=\partial_x,\ W=x\,\partial_y$. Pulling back changes $W$ by $t\,\partial_y$, so the derivative is $\partial_y$.

Check

$[\partial_x,\, x\,\partial_y] = \partial_y$

Payoff

An analytic limit becomes pure commutator algebra — computable in any chart.

Printed pp. 205-248Lie Derivative Equals the Bracket
The central computation identifies the Lie derivative with the Lie bracket: L_V W equals bracket V W. For V the x-translation and W equal to x times d-dy, pulling back changes the field by t times d-dy, so the derivative is d-dy, exactly the bracket. This turns an analytic limit into pure commutator algebra of differential operators.

Flows Commute Iff the Bracket Vanishes

Criterion

$\theta^V_t\circ\theta^W_s = \theta^W_s\circ\theta^V_t \iff [V,W]=0$

Open square

If $[V,W]\neq0$, the two routes around the square differ by a second-order gap governed by the bracket.

Coordinates

Commuting independent fields can be completed to a coordinate grid.

Printed pp. 205-248Commuting Flows
Two fields commute as operators exactly when their flows commute as maps. Move time t along one then time s along the other; if the bracket vanishes the two routes close on the same point, and the fields can be completed to a coordinate grid. When the bracket is nonzero the flow square opens by a second-order gap measured precisely by that bracket.

Time-Dependent Vector Fields

Two-time flow

$\Phi(t,t_0,p)$

Needs current time $t$ and start time $t_0$, not just elapsed time.

Model

$V(t,x)=x/t$ on $t>0$ has flow $\Phi(t,t_0,x_0)=\dfrac{t}{t_0}\,x_0$.

Cocycle law

$\Phi(t_2,t_1,\Phi(t_1,t_0,x_0)) = \Phi(t_2,t_0,x_0)$

Reading

Composition pivots on the middle time $t_1$; the autonomous group law is the special case $\Phi$ depends only on $t-t_0$.

Printed pp. 205-248Time-Dependent Fields
Allowing the field to depend on time forces the flow to carry two time arguments, the current time and the starting time, not merely elapsed time. The model x over t on positive t has flow t over t-naught times x-naught. Composition uses the middle time through a cocycle law rather than simple addition of times.

First-Order PDE Characteristics

Linear Cauchy problem

$A u = f,\quad u|_{S}=u_0$

$A=-y\,\partial_x+x\,\partial_y$ supplies characteristic curves; $u$ solves an ODE along each.

Quasilinear

The unknown becomes a coordinate; the solution graph is a flowout in $(x,y,z)$ space.

Noncharacteristic

Transversality of $S$ to the characteristics gives a local graph solution.

Printed pp. 205-248First-Order PDE Characteristics
The flowout viewpoint becomes the method of characteristics. A first-order Cauchy problem reduces to ordinary differential equations along characteristic curves traced by an associated field. For the quasilinear case the unknown becomes a coordinate and the solution graph is built as a flowout in one higher dimension. Noncharacteristic initial data is the transversality that makes a local graph solution exist.

Recap: Field to ODE to Geometry

Curves and flow

Integral curves solve $\gamma'=V$; the unique maximal flow packages them as a one-parameter group.

Completeness

Compact support or a compact manifold forces a global flow.

Straightening

Flowout coordinates turn any nonvanishing field into $\partial_t$.

Lie derivative

$\mathcal{L}_V W=[V,W]$; flows commute iff the bracket vanishes.

One idea

Flowing along a field converts geometry and first-order PDEs into ODEs we can solve.

Printed pp. 205-248Recap
We turned one smooth vector field into integral curves, a unique maximal flow, completeness criteria, straightening coordinates, the Lie derivative equal to the bracket, a commuting-flows test, time-dependent cocycles, and the method of characteristics. The recurring idea is that flowing along a field converts geometry and partial differential equations into ordinary differential equations we can solve.
