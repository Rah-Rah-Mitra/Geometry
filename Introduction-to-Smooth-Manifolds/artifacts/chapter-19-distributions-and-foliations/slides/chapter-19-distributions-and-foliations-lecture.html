Distributions and Foliations

When does a field of tangent planes fit into integral surfaces?

The object

A smoothly varying field of tangent $k$-planes, a rank-$k$ subbundle $D\subset TM$.

The obstruction

Planes can twist so much that no surface stays tangent. The Lie bracket measures it.

The resolution

Frobenius: involutive $\Leftrightarrow$ integrable, with a flat coordinate chart.

Printed pp. 490-514Distributions and Foliations
This chapter, printed pages 490 through 514, asks when a smoothly chosen field of tangent k-planes actually fits together into integral surfaces. The whole drama is governed by the Lie bracket: planes may twist so badly that no surface can stay tangent, and the Frobenius theorem pins down exactly when they cannot.

Distributions as Subbundles

Definition

A rank-$k$ distribution assigns to each $p$ a $k$-dimensional subspace $D_p\subset T_pM$, varying smoothly.

Local frame

Smoothness $=$ near each point, $k$ smooth fields span $D$: a local frame for the subbundle.

Two pictures

Geometric plane field $\leftrightarrow$ algebraic span of vector fields.

Subbundle of $TM$

$D=\bigsqcup_{p\in M} D_p\subset TM,\quad \operatorname{rank} D=k$

Local spanning frame

$D=\operatorname{span}(X_1,\dots,X_k)$ on $U$

Printed pp. 490-514Distributions as Subbundles
A distribution is a rank-k subbundle of the tangent bundle, equivalently a smoothly varying choice of k-dimensional subspace at every point. Smoothness means each point has a neighborhood with k smooth vector fields spanning the planes. Keep both pictures in mind: a geometric plane field and an algebraic span of local frames.

Integral Manifolds and Integrability

Integral manifold

An immersed submanifold $N$ with $T_pN=D_p$ at every $p\in N$.

Integrable

An integral manifold passes through each point of $M$.

Immersed, not embedded

Leaves may wind densely; embeddedness is too strong to demand.

Defining property

$T_pN=D_p\quad\forall\,p\in N$

Printed pp. 490-514Integral Manifolds
An integral manifold of a distribution is an immersed submanifold whose tangent space at every point equals the assigned plane. The distribution is integrable when one such manifold passes through every point. Stress that integral manifolds need not be embedded, which is why immersed leaves appear later in foliations and Lie subgroups.

The Bracket Obstruction

A plane field in $\mathbb{R}^3$

$X=\partial_x+y\,\partial_z,\qquad Y=\partial_y$.

The bracket escapes

$[X,Y]=-\partial_z$ points straight out of every plane.

Verdict

No surface absorbs the vertical drift: no integral surface exists.

Printed pp. 490-514The Bracket Obstruction
Here is the obstruction in concrete coordinates. The fields X equals partial-x plus y partial-z and Y equals partial-y span a plane field in three-space, yet their bracket is minus partial-z, pointing straight out of every plane. No surface can absorb that vertical drift, so this distribution has no integral surface through the origin.

Involutivity

Definition

$D$ is involutive if $X,Y\in\Gamma(D)\Rightarrow[X,Y]\in\Gamma(D)$: closed under the bracket.

Necessary

Integrable $\Rightarrow$ involutive: brackets of fields tangent to a leaf stay tangent.

The question

Is involutivity also sufficient? Frobenius says yes, locally.

Closure under bracket

$X,Y\in\Gamma(D)\ \Longrightarrow\ [X,Y]\in\Gamma(D)$

Implication chain

$\text{integrable}\Rightarrow\text{involutive}$

Check by frame

It suffices to test brackets $[X_i,X_j]$ of a local spanning frame.

Printed pp. 490-514Involutivity
Abstract the example: a distribution is involutive when the bracket of any two tangent vector fields stays tangent. Integrability forces involutivity, since brackets of fields tangent to an integral manifold are again tangent to it. The deep content of Frobenius is that this easy necessary condition is also sufficient locally.

The 1-Form Criterion

Annihilator

A corank-1 distribution is $D=\ker\alpha$ for a local 1-form $\alpha$.

Differential ideal

Involutive $\Leftrightarrow\ \alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$.

The bad example

$\alpha=dz-y\,dx$, so $d\alpha=dx\wedge dy$ and $d\alpha(X,Y)=1\neq0$.

Form-language criterion

$D=\ker\alpha\ \text{involutive}\iff\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$

Escaping bracket as a number

$d\alpha(X,Y)=1$

Same obstruction

The nonzero $d\alpha(X,Y)$ is exactly the bracket $[X,Y]$ leaving $D$.

Printed pp. 490-514The 1-Form Criterion
Dually, a corank-one distribution is the kernel of a one-form alpha. Involutivity becomes alpha wedge d-alpha equals zero, the differential-ideal condition. For our bad example alpha is dz minus y dx, so d-alpha is dx wedge dy and d-alpha on X and Y equals one. That single nonzero number is the escaping bracket in form language.

The Frobenius Theorem

Theorem (Frobenius)

A smooth distribution is integrable if and only if it is involutive.

Flat normal form

Near each point there is a chart in which $D$ is the span of the first $k$ partials.

Leaves are slices

Integral manifolds become $\{x^{k+1},\dots,x^n=\text{const}\}$.

Canonical / flat chart

$D=\operatorname{span}\!\Big(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^1},\dots,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^k}\Big)$

Printed pp. 490-514The Frobenius Theorem
This is the central theorem. Every involutive distribution is integrable, and more: around each point there is a flat chart in which the distribution is the span of the first k coordinate partials. Integral manifolds become coordinate slices with the last n minus k coordinates held constant. Involutivity is exactly the integrability condition.

Frobenius in a Flat Chart

An integrable field

$V=\partial_x+y\,\partial_z,\quad W=\partial_y+x\,\partial_z$.

Bracket vanishes

$[V,W]=0$, so $D$ is involutive, hence integrable.

The leaves

$V,W$ are tangent to $w=z-xy=\text{const}$.

Flattening change of coordinates

$(u,v,w)=(x,\,y,\,z-xy),\quad \det J=1$

Printed pp. 490-514Flat Chart Example
Watch Frobenius in action. The fields partial-x plus y partial-z and partial-y plus x partial-z have zero bracket and are tangent to the level surfaces of w equals z minus xy. The coordinate change to (x, y, z minus xy) has Jacobian determinant one and flattens the distribution into the span of the first two partials.

Foliations

Definition

A foliation partitions $M$ into immersed integral-manifold leaves.

Foliation charts

The flat charts of Frobenius: leaves appear as parallel slices.

Plaques

A single chart can meet a leaf in countably many slices, the plaques.

Printed pp. 490-514Foliations
Globally, an involutive distribution partitions the manifold into immersed integral-manifold leaves: a foliation. Foliation charts are the flat charts, in which the leaves appear as parallel coordinate slices called plaques. Each leaf may meet a single chart in countably many plaques, which is the source of the immersed, non-embedded behavior.

Gluing Plaques into Leaves

Maximal leaf

The maximal connected integral manifold through $p$.

Overlap relation

Plaques that overlap in adjacent charts lie on the same leaf.

Connected components

Leaves $=$ connected components of the plaque-overlap graph.

Printed pp. 490-514Gluing Plaques into Leaves
The maximal connected integral manifold through a point is built by gluing plaques across overlapping flat charts. Two plaques that overlap lie on the same leaf, so leaves are connected components of the plaque overlap relation. The notebook models this with a graph whose connected components recover exactly the global leaves.

Lie Subgroups from Subalgebras

Construction

A Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}$ gives a left-invariant involutive distribution.

The leaf at $e$

Its leaf through the identity is the connected Lie subgroup with algebra $\mathfrak{h}$.

On the torus

Rational slope $\to$ closed circle; irrational slope $\to$ dense immersed winding.

Printed pp. 490-514Lie Subgroups from Subalgebras
Frobenius pays off in Lie theory. A Lie subalgebra determines a left-invariant involutive distribution, and the leaf through the identity is the connected Lie subgroup with that algebra. On the torus, a rational slope gives a closed circle subgroup while an irrational slope winds densely as an immersed, non-embedded subgroup.

Overdetermined PDEs as Frobenius Problems

The system

$u_x=\alpha(x,y,u),\quad u_y=\beta(x,y,u)$ seeks a graph tangent to $D$.

The distribution

$X=\partial_x+\alpha\,\partial_z,\quad Y=\partial_y+\beta\,\partial_z$.

Solvability

Arbitrary initial data $\Leftrightarrow [X,Y]\in\operatorname{span}(X,Y)$: a compatibility condition.

Cross-derivative compatibility

$\beta_x+\alpha\beta_z-\alpha_y-\beta\alpha_z=0$

Compatible

$u_x=y,\ u_y=x\ \Rightarrow\ u=xy+c$

Incompatible

$u_x=0,\ u_y=x\ \Rightarrow\ \text{obstruction}\neq0$

Printed pp. 490-514Overdetermined PDE Systems
An overdetermined system u-x equals alpha, u-y equals beta seeks a graph tangent to the distribution spanned by partial-x plus alpha partial-z and partial-y plus beta partial-z. Local solutions with arbitrary initial value exist exactly when the bracket stays in the span, a cross-derivative compatibility condition. Compatible systems integrate; incompatible ones cannot.
