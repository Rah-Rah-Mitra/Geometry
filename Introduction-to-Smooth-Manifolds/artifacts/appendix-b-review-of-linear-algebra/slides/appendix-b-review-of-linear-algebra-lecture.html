Appendix BReview of Linear Algebra
Frame this appendix as the algebraic substrate of the whole book. Every tangent space is a finite-dimensional real vector space, every cotangent space is its dual, and every tensor at a point is a multilinear gadget on those two spaces. The goal of the lecture is not to reprove linear algebra but to recall exactly the pieces that recur pointwise on a manifold: basis-induced coordinates, rank, duality, multilinearity, and orientation. Keep returning to the slogan that the manifold theory is this algebra done smoothly over every point.

Vector Spaces, Bases, and Dimension

Basis

An independent spanning list. Every \(v\) has unique coordinates relative to it.

Dimension

All bases of \(V\) have the same length \(n=\dim V\); a choice of basis is an isomorphism \(V\cong\mathbb{R}^n\).

On a manifold

Each tangent space \(T_pM\) is such a \(V\); a chart supplies a basis.

Coordinates come from a basis, not from the spaceSlide 2 / 10
Stress the distinction between a vector space and a coordinatization of it. The space exists abstractly; a basis is an external choice that turns abstract vectors into tuples in R to the n. Invariance of dimension is the first theorem that makes the word dimension meaningful, and it is exactly why tangent spaces of a fixed manifold all share one dimension. The notebook represents a basis as a full-rank column matrix and checks unique coordinates; make the point that uniqueness of coordinates is the defining computational symptom of a basis. Affine cosets through a non-origin point are not subspaces, a trap worth naming early.

Linear Maps, Matrices, and Rank-Nullity

Matrix of a map

After ordered bases, \(T\) becomes a matrix; image is the column span, kernel is the solution space.

Rank-Nullity

\(\dim V = \operatorname{rank} T + \dim\ker T\)

Canonical rank form

Independent basis changes reduce \(T\) to an identity block of size \(r\) with zeros elsewhere.

Image and kernel split the domainSlide 3 / 10
A linear map between fixed-basis spaces is a matrix, but the invariant content is rank, kernel, and image, not the array of entries. Rank-nullity is the bookkeeping identity that the kernel exactly accounts for the dimensions the map fails to use. The notebook builds matrices of prescribed rank and verifies domain dimension equals rank plus nullity, and it also illustrates a singular map collapsing a kernel line onto a one-dimensional image. The canonical rank theorem, where independent domain and codomain basis changes produce an identity block, is the coordinate face of the constant-rank theorem students will meet for smooth maps. Emphasize that rank is the single number that survives every basis change.

Dual Spaces and Covectors

Dual space

\(V^* = \{\,\omega:V\to\mathbb{R}\ \text{linear}\,\}\)

Dual basis

\(\varepsilon^i(e_j)=\delta^i_j\), so \(\dim V^*=\dim V\).

On a manifold

\(T_p^*M\) is dual to \(T_pM\); differentials \(dx^i\) are the dual basis to \(\partial/\partial x^i\).

Covectors measure vectors; the dual basis is reciprocalSlide 4 / 10
Duality is the conceptual heart of why manifolds carry both tangent and cotangent objects. A covector is a linear gauge that reads off a number from each vector, visualized as evenly spaced level sets that the vector pierces. The dual basis is defined by the reciprocity relation epsilon i of e j equals the Kronecker delta, which makes components of vectors upper-indexed and components of covectors lower-indexed. On a manifold the coordinate differentials dx i are precisely the dual basis to the coordinate vector fields, and this is why a one-form is integrated while a vector field is flowed. Note that for finite dimensions V is naturally isomorphic to its double dual, but V to V star is basis-dependent.

Bilinear Forms and Inner Products

Bilinear form

\(B(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T} G\, v,\qquad G_{ij}=B(e_i,e_j)\)

Linear in each slot; recorded by the Gram matrix \(G\).

Inner product

Symmetric and positive definite: \(G=G^{\mathsf T}\), \(v^{\mathsf T}Gv>0\) for \(v\neq0\). Supplies lengths and angles.

Gram-Schmidt

Produces an orthonormal basis with \(Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I\), preserving each prefix span.

The Gram matrix encodes a bilinear form in a basisSlide 5 / 10
A bilinear form is the type (0,2) object that a Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian metric will become pointwise. In a basis it is exactly its Gram matrix, and symmetry plus positive definiteness of that matrix is what upgrades a form to an inner product carrying genuine geometry. Gram-Schmidt is the algorithm that converts any basis into an orthonormal frame, and the notebook verifies the strong statement that Q transpose Q equals the identity, that prefix spans are preserved at each step, and that the resulting isomorphism is a linear isometry. Tie this directly to orthonormal frames on manifolds, which are the moving frames used throughout Riemannian computations.

Determinants and Orientation of a Vector Space

Alternating volume

The unique alternating multilinear top form normalized at the identity; its sign records orientation.

Identities

\(\det(AB)=\det A\,\det B,\ \det A^{\mathsf T}=\det A\)

Similarity-invariant, so \(\det T\) is defined for an endomorphism.

Orientation

Bases split into two classes by the sign of the transition determinant.

Sign records orientation; magnitude records volumeSlide 6 / 10
The determinant is the prototype alternating top-degree multilinear form, and it is worth presenting it that way rather than as a Leibniz sum, because the alternating viewpoint connects directly to the wedge algebra two slides later. The two sign behaviors matter: magnitude is signed area or volume, and sign distinguishes a basis from its mirror image even when the volumes agree. The notebook checks the appendix identities exactly: transpose invariance, multiplicativity, triangular products, and similarity invariance, which is what lets a determinant of an endomorphism be defined without reference to a basis. Orientation of a vector space is exactly the choice of one of the two equivalence classes of ordered bases, the seed of orientability of manifolds.

Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, and Diagonalization

Eigenpair

\(Tv=\lambda v,\quad v\neq 0\)

Eigenvalues are the roots of \(\det(T-\lambda I)=0\).

Diagonalization

A basis of eigenvectors gives \(P^{-1}TP=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)\).

Spectral theorem

A symmetric \(G\) has an orthonormal eigenbasis: \(G=Q\Lambda Q^{\mathsf T}\).

Eigenvectors are the invariant directions of a mapSlide 7 / 10
Eigenvalues capture the directions an endomorphism merely scales, and diagonalizability is the statement that an eigenbasis exists so the map becomes a pure rescaling along axes. Connect the characteristic polynomial to the determinant from the previous slide. The case that recurs most in geometry is the spectral theorem for symmetric matrices, since metric Gram matrices and shape operators and Hessians are all symmetric, giving real eigenvalues and an orthonormal eigenbasis. This is the algebra behind principal curvatures, principal axes, and the diagonalization of a quadratic form. Note diagonalizability can fail in general, foreshadowing Jordan structure, but symmetry guarantees it.

Tensor Products and Multilinear Algebra

Tensor product

The universal space turning bilinear maps on \(V\times W\) into linear maps on \(V\otimes W\).

Type (r,s)

\(T^r_s(V)=V^{\otimes r}\otimes (V^*)^{\otimes s}\)

Dimension \(n^{r+s}\); basis of tensor monomials.

Bridge

Tensor fields are sections of \(T^r_s(TM)\).

Multilinearity becomes linearity on the tensor productSlide 8 / 10
Introduce the tensor product through its universal property: it is the space that converts multilinear maps into ordinary linear maps, which is exactly why it linearizes all the messy multi-slot objects of geometry. Build the type (r,s) space as r copies of V tensored with s copies of V star, with dimension n to the r plus s and a basis of tensor monomials of basis vectors and dual basis covectors. This is the pointwise model of the tensor bundle: a tensor field is a smooth choice of one such tensor over every point. Emphasize that the slot count r and s is the type, the same bookkeeping the companion Riemannian appendix calls slots, and that contraction lowers the type by pairing one V slot with one V star slot.

The Alternating Algebra Λ⁷V*

Alternating tensors

\(\alpha\wedge\beta=-\,\beta\wedge\alpha\)

A repeated input makes the form vanish.

Dimensions

\(\dim \Lambda^k V^*=\binom{n}{k}\)

Top degree \(k=n\) is one-dimensional: the determinant line.

Bridge

Differential \(k\)-forms are sections of \(\Lambda^k T^*M\).

Antisymmetry encodes oriented k-volumeSlide 9 / 10
The alternating subalgebra of the tensor algebra is where determinants, orientation, and integration all live. Define alternating tensors by sign change under transposition of arguments and note the immediate consequence that a repeated input forces vanishing. The dimension count n choose k is worth deriving from the antisymmetric basis indexed by increasing multi-indices, and the top degree being one-dimensional is precisely why the determinant is unique and why a top form gives a volume element. The wedge product assembles low-degree forms into oriented k-volume measurements. This slide is the direct algebraic ancestor of differential forms and of the de Rham complex, so signal that everything about forms and Stokes theorem starts here.

From Linear Algebra to the Bundles of a Manifold

1

Tangent

\(T_pM\) is a vector space; a chart gives its basis \(\partial/\partial x^i\).

2

Cotangent

\(T_p^*M=(T_pM)^*\); the dual basis is the differentials \(dx^i\).

3

Tensor & forms

\(T^r_s(T_pM)\) and \(\Lambda^k T_p^*M\) give tensor and form bundles.

Diagnostic Question

For any object built on a manifold, ask: what vector space is it built on, what is its tensor type, how do its components transform, and which invariant, rank, determinant, or orientation, does it carry?

Appendix B recapSlide 10 / 10
Close the loop by mapping each algebraic construction onto a bundle. The tangent space is the vector space, the cotangent space is its dual with the differentials as dual basis, the type (r,s) tensor spaces give tensor bundles, and the alternating powers of the cotangent space give the bundles of differential forms. Determinants reappear as orientation and as volume, eigenstructure reappears in curvature operators, and Gram-Schmidt reappears as orthonormal frames. Leave students with the diagnostic habit: name the underlying vector space, name the tensor type, track how components transform under a change of chart, and identify which invariant the object measures. That habit is what the rest of the smooth manifolds course will exercise repeatedly.
