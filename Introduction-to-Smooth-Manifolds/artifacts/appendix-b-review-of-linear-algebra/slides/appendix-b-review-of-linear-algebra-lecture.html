Vector Spaces, Subspaces, Bases

Span

All linear combinations of a list; the columns of a matrix generate it.

Independence

No nontrivial combination is zero, so coordinates are unique.

Dimension

Every basis has the same size; that common number is $\dim V$.

Basis property

$v = \sum_{i=1}^{n} c^i\, e_i, \qquad (c^1,\dots,c^n)\ \text{unique}$

Caution

A subspace contains $0$; an affine coset $v_0+W$ generally does not.

Printed pp. 617-641Vector Spaces, Subspaces, Bases
Start concrete: a list of vectors is the columns of a matrix, span is the set of products, and a basis is the case where coordinates are recovered uniquely. Stress that subspaces pass through the origin while affine cosets need not, and that dimension is a well-defined invariant of the space.

Linear Maps and Their Matrices

From map to matrix

Fix ordered bases of $V$ and $W$; the columns record where basis vectors go.

Composition

Composing maps multiplies their matrices in the matching bases.

Change of basis

An endomorphism's matrix changes by similarity $B = P^{-1}AP$.

Matrix of a map

$T(e_j) = \sum_i A^i{}_j\, f_i$

Invariants

Rank and determinant do not depend on the basis chosen.

Printed pp. 617-641Linear Maps and Their Matrices
Once ordered bases are fixed, a linear map becomes a matrix whose columns are the images of the basis vectors. Emphasize that the same abstract map yields different matrices in different bases, related by similarity for endomorphisms, while rank and determinant survive the change.

Rank-Nullity: Kernel and Image

Rank-Nullity

$\dim V = \dim(\ker T) + \dim(\operatorname{im} T)$

Kernel

Vectors sent to $0$; its dimension is the nullity.

Image

Span of the columns; its dimension is the rank.

Printed pp. 617-641Rank, Nullity, Kernel, Image
This is the central bookkeeping identity. The kernel measures collapse, the image measures spread, and their dimensions always sum to the domain dimension. Walk through the diagram: the kernel line is crushed to the origin while everything else lands on the image line, so two plus one equals three here.

Isomorphisms and Canonical Rank Form

Isomorphism test

A square map is invertible iff $\ker T = 0$ and $\operatorname{im} T = W$.

Two-sided basis change

Independent bases in domain and codomain reveal pure rank data.

Foreshadow

This is the linear core of the constant-rank theorem for manifolds.

Canonical form

$P A Q = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad r = \operatorname{rank} A$

Reading it

$r$ directions are preserved; the remaining $n-r$ form the kernel.

Printed pp. 617-641Isomorphisms and Canonical Rank Form
A square map is an isomorphism exactly when its kernel is trivial and its image is full. Independent basis changes in domain and codomain reduce any map to an identity block of size r padded with zeros. This canonical form is the linear shadow of the constant-rank theorem used later for submanifolds.

The Dual Space $V^{*}$

Functionals

$V^{*}$ is the space of linear maps $\omega : V \to \mathbb{R}$.

Dual basis

From $(e_i)$ build $(\varepsilon^i)$ reading off the $i$-th coordinate.

Covectors ahead

Differentials $df$ and one-forms live in dual spaces.

Kronecker relation

$\varepsilon^i(e_j) = \delta^i{}_j, \qquad \dim V^{*} = \dim V$

Action

$\omega(v) = \sum_i \omega_i\, v^i$ pairs a covector with a vector.

Printed pp. 617-641The Dual Space
The dual space collects all linear functionals on V. Each basis induces a dual basis of coordinate functionals satisfying the Kronecker relation. Stress that this is the algebraic source of covectors and one-forms: tangent vectors live in V, while differentials of functions live in V star.

Direct Sums and Quotient Spaces

Direct sum

$V = U \oplus W$ splits each vector uniquely into pieces.

Universal arrows

Maps into a product, maps out of a sum, glue from the factors.

Quotient

$V/W$ collapses $W$ to zero, leaving cosets $v + W$.

First isomorphism

$V/\ker T \;\cong\; \operatorname{im} T$

Dimensions

$\dim(V/W) = \dim V - \dim W$, matching rank-nullity.

Printed pp. 617-641Direct Sums and Quotients
Direct sums split a space into complementary pieces with projection and injection arrows, while quotients collapse a subspace to zero. Highlight the universal properties: maps into a product, maps out of a sum, and the first isomorphism theorem relating image to a quotient by the kernel.

Determinant as Signed Volume

Characterization

$\det:\ \text{multilinear},\ \text{alternating},\ \det I = 1$

Identities

$\det(AB)=\det A\,\det B$, $\det A^{\mathsf T}=\det A$.

Orientation

The sign distinguishes a frame from its mirror image.

Printed pp. 617-641Determinants as Signed Volume
Define the determinant by its multilinear, alternating, normalized character, then read it geometrically as the signed volume-scaling factor of a map on the unit cube. The sign records orientation. List the identities we will reuse: product, transpose, triangular, and invariance under similarity.

Eigenvalues and Diagonalization

Eigenpair

$Tv = \lambda v$ with $v \neq 0$: a direction merely scaled.

Characteristic polynomial

Eigenvalues are roots of $\det(T - \lambda I) = 0$.

Symmetric case

Symmetric operators have an orthonormal eigenbasis.

Diagonal form

$P^{-1} T P = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$

When

Diagonalizable iff the eigenvectors form a basis of $V$.

Printed pp. 617-641Eigenvalues and Diagonalization
Eigenvectors are directions the map merely scales, and eigenvalues are the scale factors, the roots of the characteristic polynomial. A map is diagonalizable when eigenvectors form a basis. Mention that symmetric operators are always orthogonally diagonalizable, a fact used for metrics and second derivatives.

Tensor and Exterior Algebras

Tensor product

$V \otimes W$ is universal for bilinear maps out of $V \times W$.

Tensor algebra

Stack all powers $V^{\otimes k}$ into one graded algebra.

Exterior algebra

Quotient by $v \wedge v = 0$ to get alternating products.

Wedge and determinant

$\dim \Lambda^k V = \binom{n}{k}, \qquad \Lambda^n V \cong \mathbb{R}\,\text{ via }\det$

Forms ahead

$\Lambda^k V^{*}$ is the algebra of differential $k$-forms.

Printed pp. 617-641Tensor and Exterior Algebras
Tensor products multiply vector spaces by the universal property for bilinear maps; the tensor algebra stacks all the powers. The exterior algebra quotients by the alternating relation, giving wedge products whose top degree recovers the determinant. This is the algebra underneath multilinear forms and the differential forms of the book.
