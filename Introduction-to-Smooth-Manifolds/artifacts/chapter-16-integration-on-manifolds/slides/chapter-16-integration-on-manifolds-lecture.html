Integration on Manifolds

Differential forms as the intrinsic integrands, and Stokes's theorem as the unifier

The integrand

An $n$-form, not a function, integrates over an oriented $n$-manifold.

The engine

Partitions of unity glue local chart integrals into a global one.

The theorem

$\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega$ generalizes every classical integral law.

This chapter, printed pages 400 to 439, builds the intrinsic theory of integration. Why Forms, Not Functions

Functions fail

A scalar $f$ has no chart-independent integral: changing coordinates drags in an unwanted Jacobian factor.

Forms succeed

A top form already transforms by the Jacobian determinant, so its integral is intrinsic.

The obstruction

$\int f\,dV$ depends on the chart; $\int \omega$ does not.

Resolution

Build the theory around $n$-forms; the determinant is exactly the bookkeeping we need.

Printed pp. 400-439Why Forms, Not Functions
Open with the failure of naive integration. A scalar function has no coordinate-free integral on a manifold because the change-of-variables Jacobian appears uninvited. An n-form already carries that Jacobian determinant in its transformation law, so its integral is well defined. This is the motivating insight for the entire chapter.

The Signed Volume Meter

Determinant

$dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n(v_1,\dots,v_n)=\det[v_1\,\cdots\,v_n]$.

Antisymmetry

Swapping two vectors flips the sign; dependent vectors give zero.

Printed pp. 400-439The Signed Volume Meter
Stress that an alternating top tensor measures signed volume of a tuple of tangent vectors as a determinant. Swapping two vectors flips the sign, and linearly dependent vectors give zero. This antisymmetry is exactly why the determinant, and hence orientation, enters the change-of-variables formula later.

Integrating Forms on Euclidean Space

Erase the wedge

For $\omega=f\,dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n$, integrate the coefficient $f$.

Local prototype

Every manifold integral reduces to this Euclidean case via a chart.

Definition

$\displaystyle\int_U \omega \;=\; \int_U f(x)\,dx^1\cdots dx^n$

Compact support

Restrict to compactly supported forms so the Lebesgue integral converges.

Printed pp. 400-439Integrating Forms on Euclidean Space
Define the integral of a compactly supported n-form on an open subset of R-n by erasing the wedge and integrating its single coefficient as an ordinary Lebesgue integral. This is the local building block; everything on a manifold is reduced to this Euclidean prototype through charts.

Change of Variables and Orientation

Pullback law

$G^*\omega$ multiplies the substituted coefficient by $\det DG$.

The sign

It is $\det DG$, not $|\det DG|$ — orientation is built in.

Invariance

$\displaystyle\int_V G^*\omega=\pm\int_U\omega,\quad \pm=\operatorname{sgn}\det DG$

Consequence

Orientation-preserving maps keep the value; reversing maps flip its sign.

Printed pp. 400-439Change of Variables and Orientation
Here is the linchpin. The pullback of a top form multiplies the substituted coefficient by the Jacobian determinant, not its absolute value. An orientation-preserving diffeomorphism leaves the integral unchanged, while an orientation-reversing one flips its sign. This is why integration of forms requires an orientation to be unambiguous.

Defining the Manifold Integral

Step 1 — Charts

Cover $\operatorname{supp}\omega$ by oriented charts $\varphi_\alpha$.

Step 2 — Partition

Pick $\{\rho_\alpha\}$ subordinate to the cover.

Step 3 — Sum

Integrate each piece in coordinates, then add.

The definition

$\displaystyle\int_M\omega=\sum_\alpha\int_{\varphi_\alpha(U_\alpha)}\!\big(\varphi_\alpha^{-1}\big)^*(\rho_\alpha\,\omega)$

Well-defined

Independence of choices follows from the oriented change of variables.

Printed pp. 400-439Defining the Manifold Integral
Assemble the global integral. Choose oriented charts, subordinate a partition of unity to the cover, integrate each chart-supported piece in its coordinate representation, and sum. Independence of the chart and partition choices follows from the orientation-respecting change-of-variables formula, so the manifold integral is genuinely well defined.

Partition of Unity Assembly

Unity

$\sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha\equiv 1$ on $\operatorname{supp}\omega$, with each $\rho_\alpha\ge 0$.

Localization

Each $\rho_\alpha\,\omega$ sits inside a single chart, ready to integrate.

Printed pp. 400-439Partition of Unity Assembly
Make the bookkeeping concrete. The partition functions are nonnegative, sum to one on the support of the form, and each rho-times-omega lives inside a single chart. The integral over the manifold is the sum of these local integrals, and the unity condition guarantees nothing is double counted or lost.

Manifolds with Boundary

Boundary $\partial M$

An $(n-1)$-manifold sitting inside the $n$-manifold $M$.

Induced orientation

"Outward normal first": orient $\partial M$ so the outward vector precedes a boundary frame.

Printed pp. 400-439Manifolds with Boundary
Introduce the induced orientation on the boundary. Using the outward-pointing normal first convention, an oriented manifold with boundary hands its boundary a canonical orientation. Getting this sign convention right is what makes the boundary integral in Stokes's theorem unambiguous and consistent with the classical results.

Stokes's Theorem

Theorem

$\displaystyle\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega$

Hypotheses

$M$ oriented $n$-manifold with boundary, $\omega$ a smooth compactly supported $(n-1)$-form.

Proof roadmap

Use a partition of unity to reduce to a single chart.

Half-space core

On $\mathbb{H}^n$ the claim is the fundamental theorem of calculus applied coordinate by coordinate.

Reassemble

Sum the local identities; interior boundary terms cancel.

Printed pp. 400-439Stokes's Theorem
State the centerpiece. For a smooth compactly supported n-minus-one form on an oriented n-manifold with boundary, the integral of d-omega over the manifold equals the integral of omega over the boundary with its induced orientation. Sketch the proof: reduce by partition of unity to a half-space and apply the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Stokes on a Planar Region

Two dimensions

$\int_M d\omega$ equals the circulation of $\omega$ around $\partial M$.

Cancellation

Subdivide $M$: shared interior edges cancel, leaving only the outer loop.

Printed pp. 400-439Stokes on a Region
Visualize the two-dimensional case. Over a planar region the integral of d-omega equals the circulation of omega around the boundary curve. This is Green's theorem in disguise, and the picture of interior cancellation explains why only the outer boundary survives in the sum.

Classical Corollaries

FTC ($n=1$)

$\int_a^b f'\,dt=f(b)-f(a)$

Green ($n=2$)

$\oint_{\partial D}P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)$

Kelvin–Stokes

$\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S}$

Divergence

$\iiint_M(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F})\,dV=\oint_{\partial M}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$

Printed pp. 400-439Classical Corollaries
Show that the classics are special cases. The fundamental theorem of calculus is Stokes in dimension one, Green's theorem and the Kelvin-Stokes circulation theorem are the two-dimensional faces, and the divergence theorem is the flux form. One identity, d-omega over M equals omega over the boundary, contains them all.

Riemannian Integration

Volume form

An oriented Riemannian manifold has a canonical $dV_g$.

Coefficient

In coordinates $dV_g=\sqrt{\det g}\,dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n$.

Integral of a function

$\displaystyle\int_M f\,dV_g=\int_M f\,\sqrt{\det g}\;dx$

Payoff

Recovers length, area, and volume; gives the divergence theorem its flux meaning.

Printed pp. 400-439Riemannian Integration
Add a metric. An oriented Riemannian manifold carries a canonical volume form whose coordinate coefficient is the square root of the determinant of the metric. Integrating functions against this volume form recovers familiar surface area and volume, and the divergence theorem gains its metric flux interpretation.

Densities & Recap

Densities

Transform by $|\det DG|$, so they integrate even without orientation — e.g. on the Möbius band.

Volume vs. density

$dV_g$ needs orientation; the density $|dV_g|$ does not.

The arc

  • Forms integrate intrinsically via the determinant.
  • Partitions of unity assemble local pieces.
  • Stokes unifies $d$ with the boundary $\partial$.
  • Metrics and densities extend to measurement and nonorientable spaces.
Printed pp. 400-439Densities and Recap
Close with densities, the absolute-value cousin of the volume form, which integrate even when orientation fails, as on the Mobius band. Recap the arc: forms integrate intrinsically, partitions assemble them, Stokes unifies the boundary, and metrics and densities extend the theory to measurement and to nonorientable spaces.
