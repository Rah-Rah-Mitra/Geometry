One-Parameter Subgroups

Definition

A smooth homomorphism $\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G$ with $\gamma(s+t)=\gamma(s)\gamma(t)$.

From a vector

Each $X\in\mathfrak{g}=T_eG$ generates a left-invariant field $X^L$; its integral curve through $e$ is a one-parameter subgroup.

Why global

Left-invariant fields are complete, so the curve exists for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$.

Correspondence

$X \in \mathfrak{g} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \gamma_X:\mathbb{R}\to G$

Bijection between $\mathfrak{g}$ and one-parameter subgroups.

Defining ODE

$\gamma'(t)=(dL_{\gamma(t)})_e X,\quad \gamma(0)=e$

Printed pp. 515-539One-Parameter Subgroups
A one-parameter subgroup is a homomorphism from the additive reals into the group. The key claim is that each tangent vector at the identity gives exactly one such curve, realized as the integral curve through the identity of the left-invariant field it generates. Completeness of those fields is what makes the curve global.

Defining the Exponential Map

Definition

$\exp(X) = \gamma_X(1)$

The time-one value of the subgroup generated by $X$.

Homogeneity

$\exp(tX)=\gamma_X(t)$: scaling the generator just reparametrizes time.

Identity

$\exp(0)=e$ and $\gamma_{tX}(s)=\gamma_X(ts)$.

Printed pp. 515-539Defining the Exponential Map
Here is the definition that organizes the whole chapter. Take the one-parameter subgroup generated by X, evaluate it at time one, and call the result exp of X. Rescaling the generator just reparametrizes time, so exp of tX traces the whole subgroup. That homogeneity identity is used constantly.

The Matrix Exponential

Model for matrix groups

$\exp(A)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{A^k}{k!}$

Flow solver

$\exp(tA)$ solves the left-invariant ODE $\gamma'(t)=\gamma(t)A$ with $\gamma(0)=I$.

Three generators

Rotation, shear, and scaling produce visibly different group motions; each ODE residual is zero.

Printed pp. 515-539The Matrix Exponential
For matrix Lie groups the abstract exponential is the familiar power series sum of A to the k over k factorial. I stress that exp solves the left-invariant ODE gamma prime equals gamma A from the identity. The notebook checks three generators producing rotation, shear, and scaling, each with zero ODE residual.

So(2) and So(3): Skew Generators Become Rotations

so(2) generator

$A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \exp(tA)=R(t)$

Group law

$\exp\big((s{+}t)A\big)=\exp(sA)\exp(tA)$: addition of angles becomes composition of rotations.

so(3)

A skew-symmetric generator exponentiates to a rotation about its axis in $SO(3)$.

Printed pp. 515-539Lines Become Subgroups
This is the picture to carry away. A straight line through the origin of the Lie algebra is mapped by exp to a one-parameter subgroup curve in the group. For skew generators in so(2) the line of angles becomes the rotation circle, so additive structure becomes multiplicative rotation.

Naturality Under Homomorphisms

Naturality

$F(\exp_G X) = \exp_H\big((dF)_e X\big)$

One square explains a lot

A Lie group homomorphism $F:G\to H$ intertwines the two exponentials through its differential $dF_e$.

Consequences

Representations exponentiate; the matrix exponential models every matrix group's $\exp$.

Printed pp. 515-539Naturality Under Homomorphisms
The exponential map is natural. Any Lie group homomorphism intertwines the two exponentials through its differential at the identity. I emphasize that this single commuting square explains why representations exponentiate Lie algebra representations and why the matrix exponential models every matrix group's exponential.

Local Diffeomorphism and Normal Coordinates

Key differential

$d(\exp)_0 = \mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}$

Inverse function theorem

$\exp$ is a diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of $0\in\mathfrak{g}$ onto a neighborhood of $e\in G$.

Two kinds

First kind: $\exp(\sum x^iX_i)$. Second kind: $\exp(x^1X_1)\cdots\exp(x^nX_n)$.

Canonical coordinates

Exponential coordinates centered at $e$ are the normal coordinates of the first kind; a basis of $\mathfrak{g}$ gives them.

Chart

$(x^1,\dots,x^n)\mapsto \exp\!\Big(\sum_i x^i X_i\Big)$

A smooth chart on a neighborhood of the identity.

Printed pp. 515-539Local Diffeomorphism and Normal Coordinates
The differential of exp at the origin is the identity map of the Lie algebra. By the inverse function theorem exp is therefore a diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of zero onto a neighborhood of the identity. These exponential coordinates are the normal coordinates of the first kind, and a basis gives coordinates of the second kind.

Local Coordinate, Global Wrapping

Local

$d(\exp)_0$ is invertible, so $\exp$ is a coordinate near $0$.

Global

$\exp$ need not be injective or surjective; it can wrap and glue.

Model

$\theta\mapsto e^{i\theta}:\ \mathbb{R}\to S^1$

$\theta=0$ and $\theta=2\pi$ share an image.

Printed pp. 515-539Local versus Global
Locally exp is a perfect coordinate, but globally it can wrap, miss points, or glue distinct algebra elements together. The cleanest model is the reals onto the circle: the derivative at zero never vanishes, yet zero and two pi share an image. I use this to caution against treating exp as a bijection.

The Closed Subgroup Theorem

Theorem

A topologically closed subgroup $H\subseteq G$ is an embedded Lie subgroup.

Recover the algebra

$\mathfrak{h}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:\exp(tX)\in H\ \forall t\}$ is a Lie subalgebra.

Slice via exp

Split $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{b}$; then $\exp(U\cap\mathfrak{h})$ is exactly $H$ in a small exp chart.

Printed pp. 515-539The Closed Subgroup Theorem
This is the chapter's flagship theorem. A merely topologically closed subgroup is automatically an embedded Lie subgroup. The proof recovers the candidate subalgebra as the set of vectors whose whole one-parameter subgroup lies in H, then uses exponential coordinates to build a slice chart. Closedness is the only hypothesis.

Infinitesimal Generators of Group Actions

Differentiate the action

A smooth action $G\times M\to M$ differentiates to a Lie algebra action by vector fields.

Rotation action

$X(x,y)=(-y,\;x)$

Generator of $A$ for $SO(2)$ acting on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Flow is the action

$X$ is tangent to the circular orbits and its flow is rotation; velocity matches $X$ to numerical precision.

Printed pp. 515-539Infinitesimal Generators
A smooth group action differentiates to a Lie algebra action by vector fields called infinitesimal generators. For the rotation action on the plane the generator of the skew matrix is the field sending a point to minus y, x, tangent to the orbits. The notebook confirms the velocity matches this field to numerical precision.

The Adjoint Representation: Ad and ad

Ad

$\mathrm{Ad}(g)=d(C_g)_e:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}$

$C_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$ fixes $e$; its differential is a representation.

ad and the bracket

$\mathrm{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]$

Bridge identity

$\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)=\exp(\mathrm{ad}\,X)$ ties the group and algebra adjoints.

Printed pp. 515-539The Adjoint Representation
Conjugation fixes the identity, so its differential gives Ad, a representation of the group on its Lie algebra. Differentiating Ad again gives ad, and ad of X applied to Y is exactly the Lie bracket. The bridge identity Ad of exp X equals exp of ad X ties the group and algebra adjoints together.

Ideals and Normal Subgroups

Correspondence

For connected $G$, connected normal subgroups correspond to ideals of $\mathfrak{g}$.

Example bracket

$[H,E]=2E,\quad H=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1),\ E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$

Preserved line

$\mathrm{Ad}(\exp tH)\,E=e^{2t}E$, so $\mathrm{span}(E)$ is an ideal stable under conjugation.

Printed pp. 515-539Ideals and Normal Subgroups
For connected groups, connected normal subgroups correspond to ideals of the Lie algebra. The proof rides on the adjoint identity: a subspace is an ideal exactly when conjugation by exponentials preserves it. The notebook's example has bracket H with E equal to two E, so span of E is an ideal scaled by exp of two t.

The Lie Correspondence and Recap

Integration

Each finite-dimensional Lie algebra integrates to a unique simply connected Lie group.

Quotients

Other connected groups with the same algebra are quotients by discrete central subgroups.

Model

$\mathbb{R}$ integrates to $\mathbb{R}$; the circle is $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$.

Printed pp. 515-539The Lie Correspondence
We close with the correspondence. Each finite-dimensional Lie algebra integrates to a unique simply connected group, and every other connected group with that algebra is a quotient by a discrete central subgroup. The reals integrating to the reals, with the circle as a quotient, is the model. Exp is the tool that makes all of this work.
