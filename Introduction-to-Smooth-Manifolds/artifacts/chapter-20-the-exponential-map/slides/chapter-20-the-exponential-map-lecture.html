Definition And Local StructureTime-one flow

The Exponential Map Is The Time-One Evaluation Of A Lie Algebra Flow

Definition

\(\exp_G(X)=\gamma_X(1)\), where \(\gamma_X\) is the one-parameter subgroup with \(\gamma'_X(0)=X\).

Local Diffeomorphism

The differential \(d(\exp_G)_0\) is the identity on \(\mathfrak g\). The inverse function theorem then gives an exponential coordinate chart around \(e\).

What This Does Not Say

Local coordinates do not guarantee global injectivity or global surjectivity. The next slide makes that failure visible.

\(\exp_G:\mathfrak g\to G\)The bridge is canonical near the identity
Define the exponential map by starting with X in the Lie algebra, following the unique one-parameter subgroup generated by X, and evaluating at time one. Emphasize the local theorem: the derivative at zero is the identity, so exponential coordinates give a genuine chart near the identity even though the group may be curved.
Local Diffeomorphism, Global Wrapping\(\mathbb R\to S^1\)

Exponential Coordinates Can Wrap Around The Group

Local Test

\(\theta\mapsto \exp(i\theta)\), with derivative at \(0\) equal to \((0,1)\).

The derivative is nonzero, so the map is a coordinate near \(0\).

Global Failure

\(\exp(i\cdot 0)=\exp(i\cdot 2\pi)\)

The notebook records repeated image error \(3.46\times 10^{-16}\), so different algebra elements land at the same group point.

Teaching Warning

Do not read an exponential chart as a global coordinate system unless a separate theorem gives that conclusion.

A Topologically Closed Subgroup Has A Lie Algebra Slice

Algebra Recovered From The Subgroup

\(\mathfrak h=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\exp(tX)\in H\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb R\}\)

Slice Setup

Choose a complement \(\mathfrak b\) so \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak b\). In a small exponential chart, the subgroup directions are exactly the \(\mathfrak h\)-slice.

Local Model Check

The generated model has \(\dim\mathfrak g=2\), \(\dim\mathfrak h=1\), \(\dim\mathfrak b=1\), and slice codimension \(1\).

Proof image: \(\exp(U\cap\mathfrak h)=\exp(U)\cap H\)Closedness turns topology into embedded smooth structure
Present the closed subgroup theorem through the slice picture. The Lie algebra h consists of directions whose whole one-parameter subgroup remains in H. After choosing a complement b, exponential coordinates separate the subgroup directions from transverse directions near the identity.

Lie CorrespondenceSimply connected integration and quotients

Simply Connected Groups Classify Lie Algebras; Quotients Reuse Them

Existence And Uniqueness Up To Isomorphism

Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\) is the Lie algebra of a simply connected Lie group \(G\), unique up to Lie group isomorphism.

Same Algebra, Different Global Group

Connected Lie groups with algebra \(\mathfrak g\) arise from the simply connected \(G\) by quotienting by a discrete normal subgroup \(\Gamma\).

Example Anchor

\(\mathbb R\quad\leadsto\quad \mathbb R/\mathbb Z \cong S^1\)

The local algebra is the same line; the global topology remembers the quotient.

Adjoint RepresentationIdeals detect connected normal subgroups

Normality Becomes A Linear Stability Condition In The Lie Algebra

Dictionary Entry

For connected Lie groups, connected normal subgroups correspond to Lie algebra ideals.

Adjoint Translator

\(Ad(\exp X)=\exp(ad_X)\)

Conjugation in the group differentiates to the bracket operation in the algebra.

Two-Dimensional Check

\(H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad [H,E]=2E\)

The line \(\operatorname{span}(E)\) is stable, and \(Ad(\exp(tH))E=e^{2t}E\).
