Definition Pattern

A Vector Bundle Is Local Product Data

03 / 10
Definition
Rank \(k\) vector bundle over \(M\)
A smooth surjection \(\pi:E\to M\) whose fibers \(E_p\) are \(k\)-dimensional vector spaces, locally identified by \[ \Phi_U:\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times\mathbb{R}^k. \]
The local chart remembers the base point and records a vector coordinate.
Smoothness is tested by coordinate changes on \(U\cap V\).
\( \Phi_V\circ\Phi_U^{-1}(p,v)=(p,g_{VU}(p)v) \)
Start from one base point p and ask what data must be present near p. The projection remembers the base point, the fiber is a vector space, and a local trivialization gives coordinates without claiming a global product. Emphasize that smoothness is encoded by how these local product charts change coordinates on overlaps.

The Product Line Bundle And The Mobius Line Bundle

Example
Same local model, different return behavior.
Over a small arc \(U\subset S^1\), both examples look like \(U\times\mathbb{R}\). Around the full circle, the product preserves the fiber direction while the Mobius bundle reverses it.
Warning
A convincing local picture does not prove global triviality. The obstruction can live entirely in the gluing data.
Have students inspect the two surfaces with the same local question: over a short arc, can we straighten the fibers into U times R? Yes in both cases. The difference appears only after one circuit around the circle: the product returns with dot product plus one, while the Mobius example returns with dot product minus one.
Overlap Data

Transition Functions Are Where Twisting Lives

05 / 10
Chart Lemma Pattern
On overlaps, compare fiber coordinates.
\[ (p,v)\mapsto(p,g_{UV}(p)v),\qquad g_{UV}(p)\in GL(k,\mathbb{R}). \]
Upper overlap: \(g=+1\)
Lower overlap: \(g=-1\)
The key move is to stop looking at a single trivialization and compare two of them on an overlap. For a line bundle, the transition value lies in GL(1,R), so it is a nonzero scalar. In this model, one overlap component has sign plus one and the other has sign minus one, and the inverse products still check out.
Sections And Frames

A Nowhere-Zero Rank-One Section Is A Frame

06 / 10
Section
A smooth map \(s:M\to E\) with \(\pi\circ s=\operatorname{id}_M\). It chooses one vector \(s(p)\in E_p\) in every fiber.
Rank-One Test
For a line bundle, a nowhere-zero global section is a global frame, hence a global trivialization.
Product min
\(0.55\)
Forced zero
\(\theta=\pi\)
Endpoint rule
\(s(2\pi)=-s(0)\)
Define a section as a smooth choice of one vector in every fiber, then specialize to rank one. A nowhere-zero section is already a global frame, so it trivializes the line bundle. The Mobius endpoint rule reverses sign, forcing a zero by continuity; the figure makes the obstruction visible at theta equals pi.
Canonical Examples

Coordinate Charts Give Local Frames

07 / 10
Tangent Bundle
In a coordinate patch \(x(u,v)\), the vectors \(\partial x/\partial u\) and \(\partial x/\partial v\) form a local frame where they are independent.
\(du(\partial_u)=1,\quad du(\partial_v)=0,\quad dv(\partial_u)=0,\quad dv(\partial_v)=1\)
The check reports the dual pairing matrix as the identity, with numerical error \(2.8\times 10^{-17}\).
Connect the abstract local-frame language back to the tangent bundle students already know. A coordinate chart supplies two tangent vectors on a sphere patch, and a dual coframe tests them. The numerical check that the pairing matrix is the identity is not the proof, but it shows exactly what the proof is formalizing.

Bundle Homomorphisms And Constant Rank

08 / 10
Local Form
A bundle map over \(M\) is fiberwise linear. In a local frame, it is a smoothly varying matrix \(A(p):\mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\).
Section-level operator\(C^\infty(M)\)-linearity residual
Multiplication by a smooth function\(0\)
Differentiation\(2x(x+2)\)
Emphasize that a bundle homomorphism is fiberwise linear and smoothly dependent on the base point. In a trivialization this becomes a smoothly varying matrix. The constant-rank example keeps kernel and image dimensions stable, while the sine example drops rank and therefore cannot support a smooth subbundle candidate everywhere.
Subbundles

Constant Dimension Must Be Smoothly Certified

09 / 10
Subbundle Criterion
A subset \(D\subset E\) is a subbundle when each \(D_p\subset E_p\) is a vector subspace and local smooth frames span \(D_p\) with constant dimension.
The tangent and normal line fields along \(S^1\) have smooth rank-one frames.
The radial span \(\operatorname{span}(x,y)\) drops rank at the origin because the generator vanishes.
\(\ker F,\operatorname{im}F\) behave as bundles only when rank is constant.
Now shift from maps to subsets. A subbundle is not merely a chosen subspace in each fiber; those subspaces must vary smoothly and keep constant dimension. The circle tangent and normal lines pass the local-frame test, while the radial span over the plane fails at the origin because its generator becomes zero.
Generalization And Recap

Fiber Bundles Keep Local Products, Drop Linear Fibers

10 / 10
ObjectLocal modelWhy it matters
Vector bundle\(U\times\mathbb{R}^k\)Linear fiber coordinates and \(GL(k,\mathbb{R})\) transitions.
Two-sheet covering\(U\times\{1,2\}\)Constant discrete fiber, no vector-space fiber.
Mobius interval bundle\(U\times[-1,1]\)Locally product, but the model fiber is not closed under vector addition.
Exit Diagnostic
Given a new example, ask: what are the local products, what are the transition maps, do global frames exist, and where can rank or fiber dimension jump?
Close by widening the lens. Every vector bundle is a fiber bundle, but not every fiber bundle has vector-space fibers or linear transitions. The two-sheet covering has a discrete two-point fiber, and the interval-fiber Mobius model is locally a product without being a vector bundle. End with the diagnostic checklist: local product, transition behavior, sections, homomorphisms, and constant-rank tests.
