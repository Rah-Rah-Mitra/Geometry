Definition of a Vector Bundle

Data

$\pi : E \to M, \qquad E_p = \pi^{-1}(p)$

Total space and projection

$E$ is a smooth manifold; $\pi$ is a surjective submersion onto the base $M$.

Linear fibers

Each fiber $E_p$ is a $k$-dimensional real vector space; $k$ is the rank.

Local triviality

Every $p$ has a neighborhood $U$ and a diffeomorphism $\Phi : \pi^{-1}(U) \to U \times \mathbb{R}^k$.

Fiberwise linear

$\Phi$ restricts to a linear isomorphism $E_q \to \{q\}\times\mathbb{R}^k$ on each fiber.

Printed pp. 249-271Definition of a Vector Bundle
A vector bundle bundles a vector space onto each base point and demands local product structure. Stress the three pieces students forget: the projection is surjective and submersive, fibers carry a fixed dimension, and every local trivialization is fiberwise a linear isomorphism onto the model space.

Local Trivializations

Coordinates for fibers

A trivialization $\Phi$ assigns to each point of $E$ a base point and a vector of components.

Form

$\Phi(v) = \big(\pi(v),\, v^1,\dots,v^k\big)$

Always local

Triviality is free over a patch; the question is whether it extends globally.

Printed pp. 249-271Local Trivializations
A local trivialization is a coordinate system for fibers. Over a neighborhood the bundle looks exactly like a product, with the trivialization recording each vector's components. Emphasize that triviality is always available locally; the whole subject is about what survives when you try to extend it globally.

Transition Functions & the Cocycle

Compare two charts

On $U \cap V$ the two trivializations differ by a fiberwise linear map.

Transition map

$\Phi \circ \Psi^{-1}(q,v) = \big(q,\, \tau_{UV}(q)\, v\big)$

$\tau_{UV} : U \cap V \to GL(k,\mathbb{R})$ smooth.

Cocycle condition

Triple overlaps must be consistent.

Cocycle identity

$\tau_{UV}\,\tau_{VW} = \tau_{UW}$ on $U\cap V\cap W$

$\tau_{UU} = \mathrm{Id}, \quad \tau_{VU} = \tau_{UV}^{-1}$

Where twisting lives

A single chart hides twisting; the overlap is where it becomes visible.

Printed pp. 249-271Transition Functions and the Cocycle
Transition functions compare two trivializations on an overlap and take values in the general linear group. Walk through the cocycle identity slowly: it is the consistency condition guaranteeing that pairwise gluings agree on triple overlaps, and it is exactly the data needed to rebuild a bundle from charts.

The Bundle Construction Lemma

Converse engine

Transition data alone is enough to build a bundle.

Given

An open cover $\{U_\alpha\}$ of $M$ and smooth maps $\tau_{\alpha\beta} : U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \to GL(k,\mathbb{R})$ obeying the cocycle condition.

Then

There is a unique smooth rank-$k$ bundle $E \to M$ with those transition functions, formed by gluing $U_\alpha \times \mathbb{R}^k$.

Printed pp. 249-271The Bundle Construction Lemma
This lemma is the converse engine of the chapter. Given an open cover and smooth maps into the general linear group satisfying the cocycle condition, you can assemble a genuine smooth vector bundle. Point out that almost every bundle in later chapters is built precisely this way, from transition data.

The Tangent Bundle

Flagship example

$TM = \bigsqcup_{p} T_pM$ is a rank-$n$ bundle over $M^n$.

Coordinate frame

$\Big(\partial/\partial x^1, \dots, \partial/\partial x^n\Big)$

A chart gives a local frame, hence a trivialization.

Transition

$\tau_{UV} = \left[\dfrac{\partial \tilde x^i}{\partial x^j}\right]$ (Jacobian)

Printed pp. 249-271The Tangent Bundle
The tangent bundle is the chapter's flagship example. A coordinate chart hands you a local frame of coordinate vector fields, the frame gives the trivialization, and the change-of-coordinates Jacobian is the transition function. Make sure students see that smoothness of the bundle follows directly from smoothness of chart overlaps.

The Cotangent Bundle

Dualize each fiber

$T^*M = \bigsqcup_p (T_pM)^*$: a covector is a linear test on tangent vectors.

Dual coframe

$\big\langle\, dx^i,\ \partial/\partial x^j \,\big\rangle = \delta^i_j$

The pairing matrix is the identity.

Contravariant law

$\tau^*_{UV} = \big(\tau_{UV}^{-1}\big)^{\!\top}$

Reversed viewpoint

Tangent vectors span fibers; covectors are the dual measurements on them.

Why it matters

The dual-bundle pattern previews differential forms in the next chapter.

Printed pp. 249-271The Cotangent Bundle
The cotangent bundle dualizes every fiber. A covector is a linear test applied to tangent vectors, and the dual coframe is verified by a pairing matrix that must come out as the identity. Highlight the contravariant transition law: cotangent transitions use the inverse transpose Jacobian, reversing the tangent rule.

Trivial Bundle vs Möbius Band

Returns to itself

One trip around the circle leaves the fiber coordinate unchanged: sign $+1$.

The twist

Identical over any short arc, but the global return reverses orientation.

Printed pp. 249-271Trivial Bundle vs Mobius Band
Here is the heart of the chapter. The product line bundle over the circle closes up into a cylinder, but the Mobius band returns its fiber direction with a sign flip. Stress that both look identical over any short arc; the twist lives only in how the global return is recorded.

Sections & the Module of Sections

Section

$\sigma : M \to E, \qquad \pi \circ \sigma = \mathrm{id}_M$

One vector chosen smoothly in each fiber.

Module structure

Smooth sections $\Gamma(E)$ form a module over the ring $C^\infty(M)$.

Pointwise scaling

$(f\sigma)(p) = f(p)\,\sigma(p)$

Printed pp. 249-271Sections and the Module of Sections
A section picks one vector in each fiber, smoothly. The set of smooth sections forms a module over the ring of smooth functions, since you can scale a section pointwise by any smooth function. This module viewpoint is the algebraic backbone for everything that follows in the course.

Local & Global Frames

Local frame

$(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k),\quad \{\sigma_i(p)\}\ \text{basis of}\ E_p$

Trivialization in section language

A local frame and a local trivialization are the same data written two ways.

Global frame

A global frame is exactly a global trivialization.

Rank-one case

A nowhere-zero global section is a global frame, hence a trivialization.

Mobius obstruction

A section on the cover with $s(2\pi) = -s(0)$ must vanish somewhere by the intermediate value theorem. No global frame exists.

Printed pp. 249-271Local and Global Frames
A local frame is a trivialization rewritten in section language: independent sections forming a basis in every fiber. A global frame is equivalent to a global trivialization. For a line bundle, a nowhere-zero global section already is a global frame, which is why the Mobius forced zero obstructs triviality.

Bundle Homomorphisms

Fiberwise linear map

$F : E \to E', \quad F|_{E_p} : E_p \to E'_p\ \text{linear}$

In a trivialization, a smoothly varying matrix.

Characterization

Bundle maps over $M$ = exactly the $C^\infty(M)$-linear operations on sections.

Passes the test

Multiplication by a smooth function $f$: $\;f(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)=f\sigma_1+f\sigma_2$, pointwise.

Fails the test

Differentiation of sections is not $C^\infty(M)$-linear: the product rule injects $df$.

Why it matters

This cleanly separates tensorial maps from genuine differential operators.

Printed pp. 249-271Bundle Homomorphisms
A bundle homomorphism acts fiber by fiber as a smoothly varying matrix. The key lemma characterizes such maps as exactly the smooth-function-linear operations on sections. Multiplication by a smooth function passes this test; differentiation fails it, which is the cleanest way to separate tensorial maps from differential operators.

Subbundles

Constant-rank subspaces

A subbundle $D \subset E$ chooses a subspace $D_p \subset E_p$ of fixed dimension, smoothly.

Local frame criterion

Near each $p$, find smooth sections spanning $D_q$ for every nearby $q$.

Passes

Tangent and normal lines along the unit circle: orthonormal smooth frames, constant rank.

Fails

The radial field $(x,y)$ drops to the zero subspace at the origin: rank jump.

Printed pp. 249-271Subbundles
A subbundle is a constant-rank family of subspaces that assembles smoothly. The practical test is the local frame criterion: near each point you must find smooth sections spanning the proposed subspaces. The radial line field vanishing at the origin is the canonical failure, where rank drops and the subbundle condition collapses.

Fiber Bundles & Recap

Drop the linearity

A fiber bundle keeps the local product diagram but the model fiber $F$ need not be a vector space.

Examples

A two-sheet cover (fiber two points); the Möbius band with interval fiber $[-1,1]$ — not closed under addition.

Inclusion

Every vector bundle is a fiber bundle with $F=\mathbb{R}^k$; the converse fails.

Throughline

  • Local product, global gluing via $GL(k)$ transitions.
  • Frame and trivialization are one datum, two languages.
  • Rank-one: a nonzero global section = global frame.
  • Bundle maps are the $C^\infty(M)$-linear ones.
  • Subbundles need constant rank; rank jumps fail.
Printed pp. 249-271Fiber Bundles and Recap
Fiber bundles keep the local product diagram but drop the requirement that fibers be vector spaces with linear transitions. A two-sheet cover and the interval Mobius band illustrate non-linear model fibers. Close by recapping the throughline: local product, global gluing, and sections as the visible test of triviality.
