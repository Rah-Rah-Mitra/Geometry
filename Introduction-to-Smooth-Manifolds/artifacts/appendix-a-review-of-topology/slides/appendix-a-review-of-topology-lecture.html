Appendix AReview of Topology
Frame this appendix as the grammar that makes “smooth manifold” a precise object rather than a picture. We are not proving the theory of topology here; we are isolating exactly the hypotheses that later let manifold arguments behave geometrically. Tell students to read every definition as a test they could run on a finite example. Keep returning to one question: which property removes which pathology?

Topological Spaces: Open Sets Are the Primitive Data

Axioms of a Topology

∅, X ∈ τ

The collection τ of open sets is closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.

Derived Operators

  • Closure, interior, boundary, exterior all follow.
  • A finite non-Hausdorff space makes each one checkable by hand.
Open sets first; everything else is derivedSlide 2 / 10
Stress that a topology is just a chosen family of “open” subsets obeying three closure rules; there is no metric required. Once those sets are fixed, closure, interior, boundary, and exterior are all definable from them, not extra assumptions. The notebook builds a deliberately non-Hausdorff finite space precisely so these operators can be computed exactly. Have students notice that boundary is interior of the complement removed from the closure, a purely set-theoretic identity.

Continuity and Homeomorphism: Backward Tests on Open Sets

Continuity

f: X → Y is continuous iff f⁻¹(V) is open for every open V.

Homeomorphism

A continuous bijection whose inverse is continuous. It identifies two spaces topologically.

Caution

A continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism unless it is also open or closed.

Continuity is preimage-of-open, not image-of-openSlide 3 / 10
The single most important habit here is that continuity is a backward condition: preimages of open sets are open. This is why all the construction topologies later are described by which maps they must keep continuous. A homeomorphism is the topological notion of “the same space,” and we will eventually upgrade it to diffeomorphism. Warn students explicitly that a continuous bijection can fail to be a homeomorphism; the missing ingredient is an open or closed map condition.

Constructions are defined by what they keep continuousSlide 4 / 10
Present these four constructions as variations on one theme: pick the maps that must stay continuous, and the topology is forced. The subspace and product topologies are “initial” (test maps into them by composing outward), while the quotient and disjoint union are “final” (test maps out of them by precomposing). A basis is just an economical way to specify open sets without listing all of them. Each of these will reappear when we glue charts into a manifold.

Quotient Maps: Open Is Not Enough Unless Saturated

Quotient Topology

U ⊆ X/∼ is open iff q⁻¹(U) is open.

Saturation

An open arc around the seam pulls back to neighborhoods of both endpoints. A one-sided open interval is open in [0,1] but is not saturated.

Diagnostic

Read quotient maps through saturated sets, not arbitrary open sets.

Endpoint quotient q: [0,1] → S¹Slide 5 / 10
This slide isolates the single subtlety students most often get wrong about quotients. The endpoints 0 and 1 form one fiber, so an open arc around the seam must pull back to neighborhoods of both endpoints simultaneously. A one-sided interval near 0 is genuinely open in the interval, yet it is not saturated, so it does not descend to an open set in the circle. The takeaway: always intersect with full fibers before asking whether a set is open downstairs.

Connected, Path-Connected, and the Local Bridge

Connected

No splitting X = U ∪ V into disjoint nonempty open sets.

Path-Connected

Every pair of points is joined by a continuous path. Implies connected, but not conversely.

Local Path-Connectedness

The bridge: here components are open and path components equal components.

Local hypotheses reconcile the two notionsSlide 6 / 10
Distinguish the two global notions first: connected forbids a clean open splitting, path-connected demands actual paths. They are not the same in general, and the topologist's sine curve is the canonical counterexample. The appendix emphasizes that local path-connectedness is exactly the hypothesis that makes them agree and makes components open. Point out that manifolds are locally Euclidean and therefore locally path-connected, so on manifolds the distinction quietly disappears.

Compactness: Finite Certificates from Open Covers

Compact

Every open cover has a finite subcover.

Heine-Borel

In Rⁿ, compact ⇔ closed and bounded.

Proper Maps

Compact sets pull back to compact sets: compact control transported along a map.

Compactness replaces infinite cover data with finite dataSlide 7 / 10
Lead with the open-cover definition because it is the one that works in full generality, not just in metric spaces. Compactness is best sold as a finiteness principle: it converts an arbitrary, possibly infinite cover into a finite certificate you can argue with. Heine-Borel is the concrete face of this in Euclidean space, and it is what students will reach for first. Then introduce proper maps as the map-level analogue, since with second-countability and local compactness they yield the compact exhaustions used throughout manifold theory.

Hausdorff, Second-Countability, Paracompactness

Hausdorff (T₂)

Distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods, so limits are unique.

Second-Countable

A countable basis. Combined with Hausdorff + locally Euclidean, this is the standard definition of a manifold.

Paracompactness

Every open cover admits a locally finite refinement, which yields partitions of unity — the tool for globalizing local constructions.

Why It Matters

These remove pathologies: non-unique limits, non-metrizability, and the failure of bump-function gluing.

The hypotheses that make a manifold tameSlide 8 / 10
This is the slide where the appendix earns its place in a manifolds course. Hausdorff guarantees unique limits and lets us separate points; without it, even convergent sequences misbehave. Second-countability plus Hausdorff plus locally Euclidean is precisely the definition of a manifold we will use, and second-countability is what delivers paracompactness. Emphasize that paracompactness is not bureaucratic: it is exactly what produces partitions of unity, the device that stitches local charts and bump functions into global objects.

Fundamental Group and Covering Spaces

Fundamental Group

π₁(X,x₀): homotopy classes of loops at x₀ under concatenation.

Circle

π₁(S¹) ≅ Z, detected by winding number via the cover R → S¹.

Lifting Criterion

For z ↦ zⁿ, a loop of winding k lifts to a closed loop iff k ∈ nZ.

Loops become a computable invariantSlide 9 / 10
Introduce the fundamental group as the first genuinely algebraic topological invariant: homotopy classes of based loops form a group under concatenation. The circle is the model computation, and the universal cover R to S-one makes it concrete because lifting a loop turns its homotopy class into an integer endpoint displacement. The finite cover z to z-to-the-n encodes the lifting criterion as the subgroup nZ inside Z, which students can check numerically. Flag that covering spaces and lifts will reappear when we discuss orientability and universal covers of manifolds.

What This Appendix Supplies to Manifold Theory

1

Local Tests

Open sets are primitive; continuity is a backward test, so charts and gluing are checked locally.

2

Tame Hypotheses

Hausdorff, second-countable, locally compact: these turn wild topology into manifold-friendly spaces.

3

Invariants

Compactness, connectedness, and π₁ survive homeomorphism and constrain global structure.

Diagnostic Question

When a manifold construction appears, ask: which open sets define it, which separation and countability hypotheses are in force, and which topological invariant must be preserved?

Appendix A recapSlide 10 / 10
Close by collapsing the appendix into three reusable habits. First, open sets are primitive and continuity is a preimage test, which is why charts and transition maps are verified locally. Second, the tame hypotheses, Hausdorff, second-countable, and locally compact, are deliberate choices that exclude pathologies and enable partitions of unity. Third, compactness, connectedness, and the fundamental group are invariants that constrain what a manifold can be. Leave students with the diagnostic question on screen as their reading checklist for the rest of the course.
