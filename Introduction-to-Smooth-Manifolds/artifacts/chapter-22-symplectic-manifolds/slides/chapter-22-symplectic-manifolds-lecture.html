What Is a Symplectic Form

Closed

$d\omega = 0$. This is what later powers Darboux and conservation.

Nondegenerate

If $\iota_v\omega = 0$ then $v = 0$: the pairing is invertible.

Not a metric

$\omega$ is alternating, so it measures oriented area, not length.

Definition

$\omega \in \Omega^2(M), \quad d\omega = 0, \quad \tilde\omega : T_pM \xrightarrow{\sim} T_p^*M$

Both axioms matter

Closedness and nondegeneracy are independent and each indispensable.

Printed pp. 564-595What Is a Symplectic Form
Spell out the two defining conditions carefully. Nondegeneracy is an invertibility statement about the pairing, not positivity, so unlike a metric the form measures oriented area. Closedness is what later powers Darboux and the conservation laws. Emphasize that both axioms are essential and independent.

Nondegeneracy Forces Even Dimension

Skew matrix

In a basis $\omega$ becomes a skew-symmetric matrix $J$ with $J^\top = -J$.

Odd dimension fails

An odd skew matrix has $\det J = 0$, so it can never be invertible.

Canonical basis

A symplectic basis pairs each $A_i$ with its conjugate $B_i$.

Standard form

$\omega = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dp_i \wedge dq_i, \qquad \dim M = 2n$

Printed pp. 564-595Forced Even Dimension
Walk through why nondegeneracy forces even dimension: a skew matrix in odd dimension is always singular, so it cannot give an invertible pairing. The canonical basis pairs each position vector with its conjugate momentum vector. This is the algebraic heart students must internalize before everything else.

The Standard Symplectic Plane

The prototype

On $\mathbb{R}^2$ the symplectic form is exactly the area form.

Area form

$\omega = dp \wedge dq$

Symplectomorphisms

Maps preserving $\omega$ are exactly the area- and orientation-preserving ones.

The Cotangent Bundle as Phase Space

Tautological 1-form

$\theta$ is defined coordinate-free yet equals $\sum p_i\,dx^i$ in any chart.

Canonical structure

$\theta = \textstyle\sum_i p_i\,dx^i, \quad \omega = -d\theta = \textstyle\sum_i dx^i \wedge dp_i$

Always symplectic

Every $T^*M$ carries this form, modeling phase space.

Printed pp. 564-595The Cotangent Bundle
Introduce the tautological one-form, the single most clever construction in the chapter. It is defined coordinate-free yet becomes p dx in any chart. Then the canonical symplectic form is minus its exterior derivative. Emphasize that every cotangent bundle is automatically symplectic, modeling phase space.

Darboux's Theorem: No Local Invariants

Theorem (Darboux)

Near each point there are coordinates with $\omega = \sum_i dp_i \wedge dq_i$.

Contrast

Unlike Riemannian curvature, $\omega$ has no local invariant to detect.

Moser's method

Connect $\omega_0$ to $\omega_1$ by a path $\omega_t$; choose $V_t$ so the flow keeps the pullback constant.

Deformation identity

$d(\iota_{V_t}\omega_t) + \tfrac{d}{dt}\omega_t = 0$

Why it works

Closedness lets the residual be written as an exact form to cancel.

Printed pp. 564-595Darboux's Theorem
State Darboux as the punchline of local theory: every symplectic form looks standard near each point. Contrast sharply with Riemannian geometry, where curvature is a genuine local invariant. The Moser deformation argument connects the given form to the standard one through a clever time dependent vector field.

Hamiltonian Vector Fields

Definition

Given $H \in C^\infty(M)$, the field $X_H$ solves the interior-product equation.

Defining equation

$\iota_{X_H}\,\omega = dH$

Why well defined

Nondegeneracy inverts $\omega$, so $X_H$ exists and is unique.

In canonical coordinates

$X_H = \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial q^i} - \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial p_i}$

Geometric picture

$X_H$ is tangent to the level sets of $H$; it rotates around them.

Printed pp. 564-595Hamiltonian Vector Fields
Define the Hamiltonian vector field through the interior product equation. Nondegeneracy is exactly what makes this well defined, since it lets us invert the form. In the plane the field rotates around level curves of H. This is where geometry begins to drive dynamics in earnest.

The Flow Preserves $\omega$ and $H$

Cartan's formula

$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d(\iota_{X_H}\omega) + \iota_{X_H}d\omega = d(dH) = 0$

Symplectic flow

So the flow of $X_H$ preserves $\omega$ — the geometric Liouville theorem.

Energy conservation

$X_H H = dH(X_H) = \omega(X_H, X_H) = 0$

Conservation of energy

$H$ is constant along its own flow, so trajectories stay on level sets.

Printed pp. 564-595Flows Preserve Omega and Energy
Prove the two conservation facts using Cartan's magic formula. The Lie derivative of omega along the Hamiltonian field vanishes because omega is closed, so the flow is symplectic. Energy is conserved because H is constant along its own field. These are the geometric forms of Liouville and energy conservation.

The Poisson Bracket

Definition

$\{f,g\} = \omega(X_f, X_g)$

Lie algebra

$C^\infty(M)$ becomes a Poisson/Lie algebra: bilinear, antisymmetric, Jacobi.

From closedness

The Jacobi identity is a consequence of $d\omega = 0$.

In coordinates

$\{f,g\} = \textstyle\sum_i \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial q^i}\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial p_i} - \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial q^i}$

Conserved quantities

$\{f,H\}=0$ means $f$ is conserved along the flow of $H$ — a symmetry.

Printed pp. 564-595The Poisson Bracket
Introduce the Poisson bracket as the antisymmetric pairing of functions through their Hamiltonian fields. It makes smooth functions a Lie algebra and satisfies the Jacobi identity, which follows from closedness. Two functions commute exactly when each is conserved along the other's flow, giving symmetries and conserved quantities.

Hamilton's Equations and the Oscillator

Classical mechanics

$\dot q^i = \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}, \qquad \dot p_i = -\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}$

The dictionary

The Hamiltonian flow is Hamilton's equations of motion.

Oscillator

$H = \tfrac12(p^2+q^2)$ gives circular trajectories with conserved energy.

Printed pp. 564-595Hamilton's Equations
Connect everything to classical mechanics. In canonical coordinates the Hamiltonian flow is precisely Hamilton's equations, the time evolution of position and momentum. The oscillator gives concentric circular trajectories with conserved energy. Stress that this dictionary is why symplectic geometry is the natural language of mechanics.

Symplectic and Lagrangian Submanifolds

Symplectic

$\omega$ restricts to a symplectic form on the submanifold.

Isotropic

$\omega$ vanishes on the submanifold: $T S \subseteq (TS)^\omega$.

Lagrangian

Maximal isotropic at half dimension $n$ in a $2n$-manifold.

Prototype Lagrangian

$\Gamma_\alpha = \{(x,\alpha_x)\} \subset T^*M$ is Lagrangian $\iff d\alpha = 0$

Key fact

Graphs of closed 1-forms are exactly the Lagrangian graphs.

Printed pp. 564-595Symplectic and Lagrangian Submanifolds
Classify submanifolds by how omega restricts to them. Symplectic submanifolds inherit a symplectic structure; isotropic ones kill the form. Lagrangian submanifolds are the maximal isotropic case at half dimension and are central everywhere. Graphs of closed one-forms in the cotangent bundle are the prototypical Lagrangians.

A Glimpse of Contact Geometry

The odd cousin

Contact geometry is the odd-dimensional partner of symplectic geometry.

Contact condition

$\eta \wedge (d\eta)^n \neq 0 \quad\text{on } M^{2n+1}$

Jet bundle

On $J^1\mathbb{R}^n$, $\eta = dz - \sum p_i\,dx^i$ detects Legendrian sections.

First-order PDEs

Legendrian sections package first-order PDEs; flowouts build local solutions.

Legendrian test

A 1-jet section is Legendrian $\iff$ $\eta$ pulls back to $0$.

Where it points

Hamilton-Jacobi theory and the geometry of characteristics.

Printed pp. 564-595Contact Geometry
Close the theory with the odd dimensional cousin. A contact form is a maximally nondegenerate one-form whose kernel hyperplane field twists as much as possible. On a one jet bundle the canonical contact form detects Legendrian sections, packaging first order partial differential equations geometrically. This points forward to further study.

Recap and Takeaways

Algebra

A symplectic form is an invertible alternating pairing forcing even dimension.

Examples

$T^*M$ carries the canonical $\omega = -d\theta$, the model of phase space.

Local rigidity

Darboux removes all local invariants; everything is locally standard.

Dynamics

$X_H$ and the Poisson bracket generate energy-preserving Hamiltonian flow.

One line to remember

$\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH, \quad \{f,g\}=\omega(X_f,X_g)$

Beyond

Lagrangian submanifolds and contact geometry extend the picture.

Printed pp. 564-595Recap and Takeaways
Summarize the journey: a symplectic form is an invertible alternating area pairing forcing even dimension; cotangent bundles supply the canonical example; Darboux removes all local invariants; Hamiltonian fields and Poisson brackets generate energy preserving dynamics; Lagrangian submanifolds and contact geometry extend the picture.
