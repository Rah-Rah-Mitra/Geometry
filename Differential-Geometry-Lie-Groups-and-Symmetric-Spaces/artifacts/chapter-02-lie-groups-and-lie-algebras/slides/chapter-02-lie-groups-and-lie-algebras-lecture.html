At The Identity
Analytic group law

Analytic Groups Have Tangent Algebra

Definition Lens

A Lie group is a smooth manifold whose multiplication and inverse maps are analytic or smooth in local coordinates.

$$L_g(h)=gh,\qquad X^L_g = (dL_g)_e X$$

The bracket is the commutator of left-invariant vector fields.

Emphasize that the Lie algebra is not a separate object chosen after the fact. Left-invariant vector fields convert tangent vectors at the identity into global infinitesimal motions, and the commutator of those fields gives the bracket that remembers the group law to second order.
One-Parameter Subgroups
SE(2) model

Exponential Map As Motion

SE(2) Generator

For \(X=(\omega,v_x,v_y)\), the path \(t \mapsto \exp(tX)\) rotates and translates every point rigidly.

$$\exp((s+t)X)=\exp(sX)\exp(tX)$$

The notebook checks the subgroup law and the \(SO(2)\) rotation block.

The SE(2) model makes the exponential map concrete. A vector with angular and translational parts integrates to a rigid motion path. Near the identity this gives useful coordinates, while the one-parameter subgroup identity gives the algebraic reason the construction belongs to Lie theory.
Near The Identity
Local multiplication

Local Law And The Bracket

First Nonlinear Correction
$$\log(\exp X\,\exp Y)=X+Y+\frac12[X,Y]+\cdots$$

The bracket is the first visible obstruction to commutative addition in exponential coordinates.

The Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff picture should be presented as a local statement. The linear terms add tangent vectors, the first correction is the bracket, and higher terms encode iterated commutators. This is where noncommutativity first becomes visible in coordinates near the identity.
Associative Envelope
Heisenberg normal order

PBW Turns Brackets Into Normal Forms

Universal Enveloping Algebra

Build an associative algebra from symbols in \(\mathfrak g\) while enforcing

$$XY-YX=[X,Y].$$

For Heisenberg, \([X,Y]=Z\) and \(Z\) is central.

wordrewritenormal order
\(YX\)\(XY-Z\)\(X^1Y^1Z^0 - Z\)
\(YXX\)\(X^2Y-2XZ\)\(X^2Y + (-2)XZ\)
\(YYX\)\(XY^2-2YZ\)\(XY^2 + (-2)YZ\)

The slide uses a short original ledger matching the notebook's PBW check, not a copied exercise.

Introduce the universal enveloping algebra as an accounting device. It embeds Lie bracket information into an associative algebra by forcing XY minus YX to equal the bracket. The Heisenberg example is small enough that students can see a word being rewritten into PBW order.
Subgroups And Topology
Closedness warning

Subalgebras Integrate, But Images Need Not Close

Lie Subgroup Theorem Is Not Closedness

A subalgebra integrates to a connected immersed subgroup.

Its image in the ambient group can be dense rather than closed.

$$t\mapsto (e^{2\pi i t}, e^{2\pi i \alpha t}),\quad \alpha\notin \mathbb Q$$
This is the global warning after the local theory. A subalgebra integrates to a connected immersed subgroup, but the image can wind densely in a compact ambient group. The torus line with irrational slope is the cleanest way to separate algebraic integration from closed embedded subgroup structure.
Orbit-Stabilizer Geometry
Homogeneous spaces

Cosets Convert Symmetry Into Space

Model Example
$$SO(3)/SO(2) \cong S^2$$
  • \(SO(3)\) moves the north pole to any point on the sphere.
  • \(SO(2)\) is the stabilizer that rotates around the same pole.
  • A coset records the destination, not the chosen rotation.
Frame a homogeneous space as the record of where a base point can be moved. The stabilizer H captures the ambiguity in choosing a group element, so the quotient G/H becomes the geometric object. SO(3)/SO(2) equals the sphere because only the image of the north pole matters.
Actions
Infinitesimal motions on M

Transformation Groups Produce Fundamental Fields

Action Differentiation

If \(G\) acts on \(M\), each \(X\in\mathfrak g\) defines a field

$$X_M(p)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\exp(tX)\cdot p.$$

The kernel at a base point is the isotropy algebra.

Connect actions back to calculus on manifolds. Every Lie algebra vector differentiates an action curve through each point, producing a fundamental vector field. Isotropy kills the field at the base point, while directions transverse to isotropy become tangent directions on the orbit.
Conjugation
Adjoint representation

Adjoint Action Differentiates Conjugation

Adjoint Identity
$$\operatorname{Ad}(\exp X)=\exp(\operatorname{ad}X)$$

In \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), the \(H\) direction grades the \(E\) and \(F\) directions by opposite weights.

The adjoint representation measures how the group moves its own infinitesimal directions. In sl(2), conjugation by exp(tH) expands the E direction and contracts the F direction. The identity Ad(exp X) equals exp(ad X) is a precise bridge from group conjugation to the bracket.
Semisimplicity
Trace form

Killing Form Detects Semisimplicity

Diagnostic Form
$$B(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}X\,\operatorname{ad}Y)$$

For finite-dimensional Lie algebras over characteristic zero, nondegeneracy is the semisimple test.

real algebraKilling form signaturegeometric reading
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\)indefinitesemisimple, noncompact behavior
\(\mathfrak{su}_2\)negative definitecompact semisimple behavior

The notebook verifies the signatures from structure constants.

Present the Killing form as a trace measurement of how strongly the algebra acts on itself. Nondegeneracy is the semisimple diagnostic. Over the real numbers, the signature matters: sl(2,R) is semisimple but indefinite, while a compact semisimple real form has negative definite Killing form.
Invariant Differential Forms
Maurer-Cartan equations

Invariant Forms Store Structure Constants

Heisenberg Coframe
$$\omega_1=dx,\quad \omega_2=dy,\quad \omega_3=dz-x\,dy$$
$$d\omega_1=d\omega_2=0,\qquad d\omega_3=-\omega_1\wedge\omega_2$$

The exterior derivative remembers the bracket.

Bring the chapter back to differential forms. A left-invariant coframe turns bracket constants into exterior derivative equations. In the Heisenberg group, the central coordinate records swept area, and d omega3 equals minus omega1 wedge omega2, so the algebra is visible in the calculus of forms.
Structure And Geometry
Invariant data

Semisimple Groups And Invariant Metrics

Algebra

Semisimple means no nonzero solvable ideals, so the adjoint representation carries strong internal information.

Bilinear Data

The Killing form is canonical and invariant:

\(B([Z,X],Y)+B(X,[Z,Y])=0\)
Geometry

Left or bi-invariant tensors move algebraic data from \(T_eG\) across the manifold.

Use this synthesis slide to connect algebraic diagnostics to geometry. Semisimplicity removes solvable ideals, the Killing form supplies canonical bilinear data, and invariant forms or metrics move that data around the group. Compactness changes the signature, not the need for invariance.
Common Boundary Cases
Local versus global

Local-Global Failure Modes

Exponential Coordinates

Valid near the identity; global surjectivity can fail or become many-to-one.

Subgroup Integration

Subalgebras integrate to immersed connected subgroups; closed embedded images need extra hypotheses.

Coset Spaces

The quotient \(G/H\) is a smooth homogeneous space when \(H\) is an appropriate closed subgroup.

Invariant Forms

Maurer-Cartan equations are algebraic, but interpreting them globally depends on the group manifold.

Instructor Check

Ask students which claim is local, which is infinitesimal, and which requires a closed subgroup or compactness assumption.

Slow down on this slide because it prevents overgeneralization. Exponential coordinates, subgroup integration, and quotient construction are powerful, but each has a hypothesis boundary. The errors come from confusing local diffeomorphisms with global parametrizations, immersed subgroups with closed subgroups, or orbit sets with manifolds.
