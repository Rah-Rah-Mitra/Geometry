Reduction
geometry encoded by an involutive algebra

The Object Being Classified

Geometric input

An irreducible globally symmetric space has local symmetries and an invariant metric.

Algebraic carrier

An orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra records \( \mathfrak l=\mathfrak k+\mathfrak p \), an involution \(s\), and an invariant inner product.

Classification target

List the possible algebraic packages, then recover compact and noncompact symmetric spaces up to the expected global quotients.

Emphasize the change of language. A globally symmetric space carries an involution at each point, but the classification becomes workable after passing to an orthogonal symmetric Lie algebra. The metric data is not discarded; it is encoded by the compact or noncompact form and by the invariant inner product.
Type II
diagonal quotient model

Type II Is The Group Case

\((L_0 \times L_0)/\operatorname{diag}(L_0) \cong L_0\)
Symmetric algebra

The involution swaps two simple ideals, so the \(-1\) part is represented by anti-diagonal directions.

Metric signal

The quotient inherits a group law and a bi-invariant metric, placing compact simple groups in the classification.

Use the diagonal quotient model to demystify type II. When the algebra is a pair of isomorphic simple ideals exchanged by the involution, a point of the quotient can be represented by one group element. That is why compact simple groups with bi-invariant metrics sit naturally inside the symmetric-space list.
Type I
compact side first

Type I Splits Into Three Algebraic Questions

A. Compact simple algebras

Classify compact simple \( \mathfrak u \). This is where complexification and root systems eventually enter.

B. Involutions

Classify involutive automorphisms \(s\) of \( \mathfrak u \), up to conjugacy, and read \( \mathfrak k=\mathfrak u^s \).

C. Centers

Track finite central quotients when passing from local algebra to global symmetric spaces.

This is the main reduction slide for the compact type I case. Once type II has been separated, classification asks for compact simple algebras, involutions up to conjugacy, and centers or finite quotients. The point is that geometry has become algebra plus a small amount of global bookkeeping.
Real Forms
compact and noncompact mirrors

Real Forms And Compact Duals

\(\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak k+\mathfrak p_0,\qquad \mathfrak u=\mathfrak k+i\mathfrak p_0\)
Noncompact form

\(\mathfrak g_0\) supplies the tangent directions of a noncompact symmetric space.

Compact dual

\(\mathfrak u\) keeps the same fixed algebra while rotating tangent directions into a compact algebra.

Connect the compact and noncompact sides. A Cartan decomposition keeps the fixed algebra compact while multiplying the tangent part by an imaginary unit in the compact dual picture. The real form is not an afterthought; it tells which symmetric space is noncompact and which roots are restricted roots.

Fixed algebra

What remains unchanged by the involution.

Tangent block

The \(-1\) eigenspace that models the symmetric-space tangent directions.

Rank flag

The named families are best read as rows in a data table, not as disconnected labels. Each row identifies a compact algebra, a fixed algebra, a tangent block, a rank, and sometimes a Hermitian structure. Ask students to compare rows by how the block pattern changes.
AIII Example
one involution, two eigenspaces

AIII As A Block Split

\(s(X)=I_{p,q}XI_{p,q}\)
Fixed part

\(\mathfrak k=\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak u(p)+\mathfrak u(q))\), the block diagonal algebra.

Tangent part

\(\mathfrak p\) is carried by off diagonal complex blocks, the directions that move the \(p\)-plane.

Use AIII as the working example because the involution is visible on blocks. Conjugation by the signature matrix fixes block diagonal matrices and reverses off diagonal blocks. That one picture explains both the isotropy algebra and the tangent directions of the symmetric space.
Symmetric Pair Check
closure rules for the split

The Bracket Test

isotropy closes

\([\mathfrak k,\mathfrak k]\subseteq\mathfrak k\)

action on tangent

\([\mathfrak k,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak p\)

curvature signal

\([\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak k\)

Do not let the block picture remain informal. A decomposition is symmetric only when the bracket relations close in the correct pattern. The three relations show that the fixed part is a subalgebra, the tangent part is stable under isotropy, and two tangent directions bend back into isotropy.
Rank
maximal flat tangent directions

Rank Is Not Dimension

Dimension

Counts all tangent parameters in \( \mathfrak p \). For AIII, this grows like \(2pq\).

Rank

Counts the largest abelian subspace inside \( \mathfrak p \). For AIII, it is \( \min(p,q) \).

\(\operatorname{rank}(G/K)=\dim\mathfrak a,\quad \mathfrak a\subset\mathfrak p\)
This slide prevents a common misconception. Large tangent dimension does not imply large rank. Rank counts the largest abelian flat direction inside the tangent part, while dimension counts all tangent parameters. The classification table needs both numbers because they answer different geometric questions.
Root Systems
finite reflection data

Root Systems Begin The Finite Machine

Finite set

\(\Phi\subset E\setminus\{0\}\) spans a Euclidean space and contains only finitely many roots.

Reflection invariance

\(s_\alpha(\Phi)=\Phi\) for each root \( \alpha \).

Cartan integrality

\(2(\beta,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)\) is an integer for roots \( \alpha,\beta \).

Mark the moment when the chapter changes scale. Instead of carrying the full Lie algebra, the classification starts recording a finite set of roots with reflection symmetries and integral Cartan entries. The A2 hexagon is small enough for learners to inspect every reflection by eye.
Weyl Chambers
from roots to a basis

Weyl Chambers Choose Positivity

\(\Phi=\Phi^+\sqcup\Phi^-,\qquad \Delta=\{\hbox{simple roots}\}\)
Walls

Hyperplanes \(H_\alpha\) divide the Cartan space into chambers.

Choice

One chamber determines positivity and the simple-root basis.

Weyl chambers are not extra geometry placed on top of the root system. They are the regions left after the reflecting hyperplanes divide the Cartan space. Choosing one chamber selects positive roots and then a simple basis, which is the input for the Dynkin diagram.
Restricted Roots
reduced versus nonreduced

Root Strings And Nonreduced Behavior

Nonreduced warning

For restricted roots, both \( \alpha \) and \(2\alpha\) may occur.

Root string

The roots \( \beta+n\alpha \) occur in one consecutive string, with no gaps.

\(\beta-p\alpha,\ldots,\beta+q\alpha,\qquad p-q=\frac{2(\beta,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\)
Here the restricted-root warning matters. Ordinary complex root systems are reduced, but restricted roots for symmetric spaces can contain both alpha and two alpha. Root strings provide a checkable arithmetic rule, so the picture is not just a collection of arrows in the plane.
Dynkin Diagrams
Cartan matrix as a graph

Dynkin Diagrams Compress Cartan Integers

\(\langle \alpha_i,\alpha_j\rangle=2(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)/(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)\)
Nodes

Simple roots after a chamber has been chosen.

Edges

Products of off diagonal Cartan integers, with orientation for unequal root lengths.

Explain that a Dynkin diagram is a compressed encoding of the simple-root Cartan matrix. Missing edges, single edges, double edges, and triple edges record the products of off diagonal Cartan integers. Arrow direction matters only when the root lengths differ.
Handoff
from involutions to finite-order data

Classification Handoff

1. ReduceReplace symmetric spaces by orthogonal symmetric Lie algebras.
2. SplitSeparate type II group cases from type I involution cases.
3. ListOrganize classical families by compact algebra and fixed algebra.
4. EncodeUse roots, chambers, and Cartan integers to make finite data.
5. ExtendFinite-order automorphisms replace a two-piece split by eigenspace bookkeeping.
Close by naming the handoff. The span has reduced symmetric spaces to symmetric algebras, sorted type I and type II, organized the classical matrix families, and prepared root systems for Dynkin classification. Finite-order automorphisms generalize the involution idea by replacing a two-piece split with eigenspace bookkeeping.
