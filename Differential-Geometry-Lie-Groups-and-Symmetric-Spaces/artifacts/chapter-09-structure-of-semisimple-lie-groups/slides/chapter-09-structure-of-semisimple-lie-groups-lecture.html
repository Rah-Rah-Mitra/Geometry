Cartan Decomposition

Fold Every \(A_2\) Orbit Into One Closed Weyl Chamber

Refined Cartan Form

\(G = K\exp(\overline{\mathfrak a^+})K\)

The \(K\)-factors are not unique, but the point in \(\overline{\mathfrak a^+}\) is.

\(SL(3,\mathbb R)\) Model

Write \(\log A=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\) with \(h_1+h_2+h_3=0\). The Weyl group permutes the entries, and sorting picks the chamber representative.

Instructor Check

Ask which data changes under left and right \(K\)-multiplication. The chamber point survives; the angular factors do not.

Emphasize the uniqueness hidden inside the Cartan decomposition. The two K factors can move substantially, but once the A component is folded into the closed positive Weyl chamber, its logarithm is fixed. In the A2 picture, every Weyl orbit has one sorted representative, so the chamber point becomes the stable numerical invariant.
Iwasawa Coordinates

Separate Rotation, Scale, And Positive-Root Shear

Coordinate Form

\(g = k\,a\,n\)

\(k\in K\), \(a\in A\), and \(n\in N\). Positive restricted roots choose which triangular shears are allowed.

SL(3,R) model K orthogonal, det K = 1 A diag(a1,a2,a3), ai > 0 N upper unipotent det A = 1, diag N = (1,1,1)

QR factorization with positive diagonal is the computational analogy: the same matrix is repackaged into geometric roles.

Describe Iwasawa decomposition as a redistribution of matrix information, not as a loss of information. The compact factor holds angular data, the A factor records positive diagonal displacement, and the N factor stores positive-root shear. The slide uses an SL(3,R) model because QR factorization makes the same separation visible.
Bruhat Decomposition

Replace One Chart By Weyl-Indexed Cells

Cell Formula

\(G = \bigsqcup_{w\in W} B w B\)

For \(SL(3,\mathbb R)\), \(W\cong S_3\). Each permutation indexes one stratum.

Big Cell Habit

The longest element \(w_0\) gives the open dense cell. Other cells behave like controlled boundary pieces.

Teaching Contrast

Iwasawa supplies a global coordinate grammar; Bruhat supplies a finite incidence grammar.

Present Bruhat decomposition as a stratification rather than a single coordinate chart. The Weyl group labels the cells, inversion length gives the growth direction, and the longest element marks the open dense cell. This is a good moment to contrast the uniform global look of KAN coordinates with the finite cell language of B w B.
Rank-One Reduction

A Root String Turns A Large Group Into A Small Calculation

Microscope Principle

For an indivisible restricted root \(\alpha\), the subalgebra generated by \(\mathfrak g_\alpha\) and \(\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}\) has one noncompact direction in \(\mathfrak a\).

Two Possible Strings

If \(2\alpha\) is absent, the model is close to \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\). If \(2\alpha\) is present, formulas gain an extra nilpotent coordinate.

Why It Matters

Rank-one reductions are the local proof engine behind Bruhat boundary tests and the \(SU(2,1)\) component formulas.

Use the rank-one slide to explain why large semisimple groups can be studied through small root strings. The bracket degree rule is the visual invariant: degrees add when the corresponding root space exists and vanish when the sum leaves the string. This makes the alpha and two-alpha distinction concrete before the SU(2,1) formulas appear.
Rank-One Formula Model

\(SU(2,1)\): Two Nilpotent Coordinates, Three Component Tests

Normalized Parameters

Let \(r^2=c|X|^2\) for the \(\mathfrak g_\alpha\) direction and \(t^2=c|Y|^2\) for the \(\mathfrak g_{2\alpha}\) direction.

Component Expressions

\(e^H=(1+r^2)^2+4t^2\)
\(e^B=r^4+4t^2\)
\(2\cosh(2A)=2e^H-e^B\)
Treat SU(2,1) as the rank-one case where the two-alpha slot is genuinely active. The parameters r and t measure the alpha and two-alpha directions, and the formulas show which component detects displacement and which component detects the big-cell boundary. Stress that the exact constants are less important here than the dependency pattern.
Real Cartan Subalgebras

Maximal Abelian Is Not Enough

Nilpotent Line

\(\mathbb R E\)

Abelian and maximal in a small model, but \(ad(E)\) is nilpotent rather than semisimple. It fails the Cartan test.

Split Line

\(\mathbb R H\)

Eigenvalues are real after complexification. This is the split Cartan direction that feeds \(A\).

Compact Line

\(\mathbb R J\)

Eigenvalues are imaginary. This is compact-type Cartan data, visible in the angular \(K\)-world.

Separate maximal abelian from Cartan by using the sl(2,R) test. The nilpotent line is abelian but fails the semisimplicity requirement, while the split and compact lines are Cartan with different eigenvalue behavior. This slide helps prevent a common mistake: a real Cartan subalgebra is not just any largest commuting subalgebra.
Automorphisms

Root-System Symmetries Control Compact Real-Form Automorphisms

Two Symmetry Layers

The Weyl group is generated by root reflections. The full root-system automorphism group can be larger.

Quotient View

outer data \(\approx Aut(\Delta)/W\)

After chamber-folding symmetries are accounted for, diagram symmetries record the residual automorphism behavior.

Compact Real Form

Automorphisms preserve the root geometry, so the finite root diagram becomes a control panel for a continuous group.

Explain the automorphism slide as a quotient story. Weyl reflections already come from chamber folding, but the full root-system automorphism group can also include diagram symmetries. The A2 hexagon makes this visible: reflections and rotations preserve the roots, while the leftover symmetry after modding out by Weyl action records the outer diagram data.

Complete Multiplicative Jordan Decomposition

Elliptic, Hyperbolic, And Unipotent Pieces Commute

Decomposition

\(g = e\,h\,u = h\,u\,e = u\,e\,h\)

The pieces commute and are determined by the element in the adjoint semisimple setting.

Group-Theoretic Roles

  • elliptic: compact-like, visible near \(K\)
  • hyperbolic: positive semisimple, visible near \(A\)
  • unipotent: nilpotent exponential, visible near \(N\)
Use Jordan decomposition to reconnect the algebraic language to K, A, and N. Elliptic parts are compact-like, hyperbolic parts are A-like, and unipotent parts are N-like after appropriate conjugacy. The important condition is commutation: the complete multiplicative pieces combine cleanly only because each piece preserves the same decomposition of the element.
Proof Strategy

The Chapter Reuses One Invariant-Preserving Grammar

1. Choose A Slice

Pick \(\overline{\mathfrak a^+}\), \(A N\), or a Weyl representative so nonunique motion is isolated.

2. Reduce Rank

Move to root strings and rank-one subgroups where bracket and component formulas can be checked.

3. Track Finite Data

Record Weyl group elements, root automorphisms, parity, and conjugacy classes.

4. Reassemble

Return to \(G\) with a coordinate, cell, or Jordan piece that no longer depends on chosen factors.

Make the proof grammar explicit. Cartan and Iwasawa decompositions fix coordinates, Bruhat decomposition tracks which Weyl representative appears, rank-one reduction proves local formulas, and Cartan automorphism results classify the residual algebraic ambiguity. Students should see that the chapter is a network of invariants, not a sequence of isolated theorems.

Applied Lab

Move In The \(SU(2,1)\) Chart

Three Prompts

  1. Hold \(t=0\) and increase \(r\). Which component grows fastest?
  2. Hold \(r=0\) and increase \(|t|\). Which expression detects the big-cell boundary?
  3. Find the parameter pair where \(e^B=r^4+4t^2\) vanishes.

Expected Interpretation

The only zero of \(e^B\) in the model is \(r=t=0\). Away from that point, the big-cell coordinate is active.

Teaching Move

Ask for a prediction before revealing the formula. The grid should become evidence, not decoration.

Run the applied lab verbally as a parameter experiment. Increasing r turns on the alpha direction, increasing absolute t turns on the two-alpha direction, and the big-cell expression vanishes only at the identity parameter. Invite students to predict which formula grows fastest before reading the color scale or the algebraic expression.
