Almost Complex Manifolds

The First Test Is Algebraic

Definition

An almost complex structure is a smooth field of linear maps \(J_p:T_pM\to T_pM\) with \(J_p^2=-I\).

Algebra

\(J\) gives each tangent space the behavior of multiplication by \(i\).

Geometry

Integrability asks whether this formal complex tangent data comes from holomorphic coordinates.

In the chapter, the torsion obstruction is the checkpoint between almost complex and genuinely complex geometry.

Slow down on the difference between algebra and geometry. The equation J squared equals minus identity is checked independently at every tangent space, so it is local linear algebra. Integrability is stronger: it asks whether those tangent rotations can be coordinated by complex charts. That distinction matters because symmetric-space arguments first build an invariant J and then verify that it is compatible with the geometry.
Type Decomposition

Complexifying The Tangent Bundle Splits The Problem

Projection Operators

\(P^{1,0}=\frac{1}{2}(I-iJ),\qquad P^{0,1}=\frac{1}{2}(I+iJ)\)

  • \(P^{1,0}+P^{0,1}=I\)
  • \((P^{1,0})^2=P^{1,0}\) and \((P^{0,1})^2=P^{0,1}\)
  • \(P^{1,0}P^{0,1}=0\)
  • Integrability is visible as bracket closure of the \(T^{1,0}\) directions.
The type split is the chapter's first piece of reusable bookkeeping. Once the tangent bundle is complexified, J has two eigenbundles, and the projection formulas make that split calculable. Students should hear that the formulas are not cosmetic: they are how tensor statements later separate into same-type and mixed-type blocks.
Hermitian And Kahler Blocks

Compatibility Turns Tensor Calculations Into Block Patterns

Why Blocks Matter

A Hermitian metric pairs \(T^{1,0}\) with \(T^{0,1}\). Same-type metric blocks vanish, and Kahler parallelism keeps the type split stable under curvature.

  • Metric compatibility: \(g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y)\).
  • Kahler condition: \(\nabla J=0\).
  • Curvature respects type, so Ricci can be traced in complex coordinates.
Treat this slide as a visual substitute for a long index calculation. The Hermitian metric does not pair two holomorphic directions or two antiholomorphic directions; it pairs opposite types. The Kahler condition says the connection preserves J, so this type discipline survives differentiation and curvature operations instead of being a one-time coordinate trick.
Ricci Curvature

The Bergman Kernel Produces A Kahler Metric

Complex Coordinate Recipe

\(g_{i\bar j}=\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log K(z,z)\)

\(\mathrm{Ric}_{i\bar j}=-\partial_i\partial_{\bar j}\log\det(g)\)

For the unit disk, \(K(z,z)\) is proportional to \((1-|z|^2)^{-2}\), so the metric density blows up near the boundary and the Ricci form is a fixed negative multiple of the metric.

Connect this calculation to the later domain theorem. The Bergman kernel is not just an analytic object; differentiating its logarithm gives the metric that bounded domains naturally carry. In the disk model students can see two facts at once: the metric becomes large near the boundary, and the Ricci expression is locked to the metric by a simple symbolic identity.
Bounded Symmetric Domains

Disk Symmetries Model The General Definition

Rank-One Prototype

\(s_a(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\bar a z}\)

  • The point \(a\) is fixed.
  • The map reverses geodesics through \(a\).
  • The Bergman metric is preserved by holomorphic automorphisms.
  • The same pattern is abstracted in bounded symmetric domains.
Use the disk as a controlled laboratory for the definition. A bounded symmetric domain asks for a holomorphic involution centered at every point, with that point isolated as a fixed point. In the disk, the fractional linear formula makes each obligation visible, and the Bergman metric supplies the invariant geometry that will generalize.
Hermitian Symmetric Spaces

Symmetry Must Preserve The Complex Structure

Riemannian Symmetry

At each point \(p\), the geodesic symmetry reverses tangent vectors and preserves the metric.

Complex Compatibility

The same symmetries act holomorphically, so \(J\) is invariant under the transvection group.

Kahler Consequence

The invariant Hermitian metric is automatically Kahler in this symmetric setting.

This is the conceptual definition slide. A symmetric space already has geodesic symmetries, but the Hermitian adjective requires the complex structure to be part of the invariant geometry. The point for students is that J is not an arbitrary decoration on the manifold; it is respected by the same symmetry group that controls geodesics and curvature.
Recognition Test

The Center Of The Isotropy Group Supplies \(J\)

Infinitesimal Criterion

For an irreducible Hermitian symmetric space, the isotropy algebra has a central element \(H_0\) whose adjoint action on \(\mathfrak p\) is the complex structure.

Centrality

\([H_0,\mathfrak k]=0\)

Complex action

\((\operatorname{ad}H_0)^2=-I\) on \(\mathfrak p\)

Present the center criterion as the practical fingerprint of Hermitian type. Instead of searching for complex coordinates directly, one looks at the isotropy action. A central circle rotates every tangent complex line, and its derivative gives the operator J. Centrality is what makes the construction invariant rather than dependent on a chosen tangent vector.

Domains To Symmetric Spaces

A Bounded Symmetric Domain Carries Its Own Geometry

Key Mechanism

Holomorphic automorphisms transform the Bergman kernel by a Jacobian factor. After \(\partial\bar\partial\log\), the extra factor does not change the metric.

That is why the automorphism group acts by isometries and why point symmetries of the domain become Riemannian symmetries.

Frame this direction as the analytic-to-geometric theorem. A bounded symmetric domain begins as a complex domain with holomorphic point symmetries. The Bergman kernel manufactures a canonical Kahler metric, and the transformation law makes automorphisms isometries. From there the domain is not just an example; it becomes a Hermitian symmetric space of noncompact type.
Cartan-Harish-Chandra Representation

The Noncompact Space Enters A Bounded Vector Domain

Proof Skeleton

  • Complexify the Lie algebra and split the tangent part into \(\mathfrak p_-\oplus\mathfrak p_+\).
  • Use the open cell \(P_- K_{\mathbb C} P_+\) in the complexified group.
  • Project the \(G_0/K_0\) point to its \(P_-\) coordinate.
  • Show the image \(D\subset\mathfrak p_-\) is bounded and symmetric.
This is the algebraic heart of the final section. The theorem does not draw the bounded domain by guessing coordinates; it embeds the noncompact symmetric space into a complexified group picture and then reads off the P minus factor. The message is that boundedness comes from the Lie-theoretic factorization plus the Hermitian type decomposition.
Rank Coordinates

Strongly Orthogonal Directions Reveal Boundedness

The Visible Numerical Shadow

Along strongly orthogonal root directions, an unbounded geodesic parameter \(t\) is converted into a bounded coordinate through a hyperbolic tangent.

\(t\in\mathbb R \quad\longmapsto\quad \tanh(t)\in(-1,1)\)

Rank-two models display a bounded square; higher rank spaces show the same limiting behavior along compatible directions.

Use this slide to make the boundedness theorem feel inspectable. The full proof uses strongly orthogonal roots and the complexified group, but the coordinate behavior has a simple numerical fingerprint: real geodesic coordinates are sent through tanh. That is why unbounded motion in the symmetric space can land inside a bounded coordinate domain.

Closing Checklist

How To Recognize The Chapter In A New Example

Instructor Checklist

  • Find the tangent operator \(J\) and check \(J^2=-I\).
  • Split \(T_{\mathbb C}M\) into \(T^{1,0}\) and \(T^{0,1}\).
  • Use Hermitian blocks to control the metric and Ricci calculations.
  • Look for a central isotropy circle that acts as \(J\).
  • For noncompact type, track the image in \(\mathfrak p_-\) and test bounded coordinates.

One-Sentence Takeaway

Hermitian symmetric spaces are exactly where complex geometry, invariant curvature, and symmetric-space Lie theory cooperate strongly enough to produce bounded domains with canonical metrics.
