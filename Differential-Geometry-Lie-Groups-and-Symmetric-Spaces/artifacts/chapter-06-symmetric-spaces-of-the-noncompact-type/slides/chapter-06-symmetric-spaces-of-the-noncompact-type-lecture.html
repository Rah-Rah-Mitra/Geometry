Cartan Polar Coordinates
linear split becomes global geometry

The Noncompact Split Is More Than Linear Algebra

Symmetric Pair Data
\(\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak k_0\oplus\mathfrak p_0,\quad \theta|_{\mathfrak k_0}=1,\quad \theta|_{\mathfrak p_0}=-1.\)
Noncompact Polar Form
The map \((X,k)\mapsto \exp(X)k\) from \(\mathfrak p_0\times K\) to \(G\) is global, and \(\operatorname{Exp}:\mathfrak p_0\to G/K\) is a diffeomorphism.

Why It Matters

The tangent space at the base point no longer describes only a neighborhood; in noncompact type it parametrizes the whole symmetric space.

Cartan decompositionNotebook check: theta squared identity and positive upper-half-plane image
Present the Cartan decomposition as the first global-coordinate theorem, not as a bookkeeping split of the Lie algebra. In compact geometry an exponential chart can fold back on itself, but the noncompact model removes that obstruction. The proof thread uses the involution, the Killing-form sign pattern, and the convexity of geodesic distance to turn p0 into a complete coordinate plane for G/K.

Maximal Compact Subgroups
compact means fixed point

Maximal Compact Subgroups Are Isotropy Groups

Geometric Form
If \(C\subset G\) is compact, then \(C\) fixes a point of the noncompact symmetric space. After conjugating that point to the base point, \(C\subset K\).

Conjugacy Principle

All maximal compact subgroups are conjugate because any choice of fixed point can be moved to the base point.

Notebook Model

Disk automorphisms move sample fixed points \((0.42,0.22)\), \((-0.35,0.48)\), and \((0.18,-0.52)\) to the origin with zero residual.

Maximal compact subgroupsArtifact: maximal_compact_fixed_point_conjugacy.png
Frame this result as a geometric replacement for choosing K by hand. A compact subgroup acting on a noncompact symmetric space cannot drift forever; it has a fixed point. Once that point is moved to the base point, the subgroup becomes part of the isotropy group K. This is the bridge from global geometry to the conjugacy theorem for maximal compact subgroups.
Iwasawa Decomposition
K A N coordinates

\(G=KAN\) Refines Polar Coordinates

Factor Choice
\(\mathfrak a\subset\mathfrak p\) maximal abelian, \(\mathfrak n=\sum_{\alpha>0}\mathfrak g_\alpha,\quad A=\exp\mathfrak a,\quad N=\exp\mathfrak n.\)
Iwasawa Map
Multiplication \(K\times A\times N\to G\), \((k,a,n)\mapsto kan\), is a global analytic diffeomorphism.

Conceptual Payoff

Cartan coordinates use all of \(\mathfrak p\); Iwasawa coordinates separate rank directions \(A\) from nilpotent root directions \(N\).

Iwasawa decompositionThe notation \(KAN\) will drive the rest of the lecture
Introduce Iwasawa decomposition as the chapter's main coordinate engine. The K part remembers a compact frame, A carries the flat rank directions, and N carries the positive restricted-root directions. The important point is global uniqueness: this is not a local triangularization near the identity but an analytic product description of every group element.

where N comes from

Positive Restricted Roots Assemble The Nilpotent Algebra

Root-Space Sum
\(\mathfrak n=\mathfrak g_{\alpha_{12}}\oplus\mathfrak g_{\alpha_{23}}\oplus\mathfrak g_{\alpha_{13}}\) for split \(\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb R)\).

Triangular Reading

The positive root spaces are exactly the strictly upper-triangular elementary matrices in the notebook example.

Bracket Memory

The highest root direction is created by a bracket: the path from \(E_{12}\) to \(E_{23}\) produces \(E_{13}\).

\(\alpha_{12}\)\([H_{12},E]=2E\), \([H_{23},E]=-E\)
\(\alpha_{23}\)\([H_{12},E]=-E\), \([H_{23},E]=2E\)
\(\alpha_{13}\)\([H_{12},E]=E\), \([H_{23},E]=E\)
Restricted-root decompositionArtifact: sl3_restricted_roots_and_nilpotent_n.png
Explain that N is not an arbitrary unipotent subgroup bolted onto the decomposition. It is built from the positive restricted-root spaces determined by the maximal abelian subspace a. In the split sl(3,R) example, those spaces are the strictly upper-triangular entries, and the bracket relation from E12 and E23 to E13 shows why the sum is nilpotent but usually not abelian.
Nilpotent Structure
descending brackets stop

The Root Algebra \(\mathfrak n\) Is Nilpotent

Geometric Meaning
The nilpotent directions flow along horosphere-like coordinates transverse to the flat \(A\)-directions.

Why Nilpotence Is Useful

The exponential map on a nilpotent Lie algebra is algebraically tame: the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff series terminates.

Common Trap

Do not read \(N\) as a commutative translation group. The group is often nonabelian even when its coordinates look triangular.

\(\exp:\mathfrak n\to N\) supplies polynomial coordinates on the nilpotent factor.

Nilpotent algebraNotebook validation: root brackets and central descending series
Pause here because nilpotence is the reason N is computationally friendly. In root coordinates, repeated commutators eventually move beyond the available positive roots, so the descending bracket series stops. That is what lets exponential coordinates on N have polynomial multiplication laws. The warning is that polynomial does not mean commutative; bracket corrections still carry real geometric information.

Global Decomposition
AN parametrizes the space

The Symmetric Space Is The Solvable Model \(AN\)

Group-Level Statement
The multiplication map \(K\times A\times N\to G\) is one-to-one and onto; hence \(AN\to G/K\), \(an\mapsto anK\), is a global diffeomorphism.

Pedagogical Translation

Once the compact frame \(K\) is quotiented away, a point of the symmetric space is represented uniquely by a scale coordinate and nilpotent root coordinates.

Why This Is Strong

The theorem gives a global solvable-group chart, not merely a parametrization of a dense open cell.

Global KANSolvable model of noncompact type
This slide should make the quotient logic explicit. KAN is a coordinate system on the group, but the symmetric space is G modulo K. After quotienting, the compact frame disappears and the remaining AN factor gives a global solvable model. This is the structural reason noncompact symmetric spaces can be studied through solvable Lie groups without losing points.

The Decompositions Share One Proof Skeleton

Algebraic Input

The Cartan involution gives \(\mathfrak k+\mathfrak p\), and the maximal abelian subspace \(\mathfrak a\) organizes restricted roots.

Geometric Input

Compact groups fix points in the noncompact symmetric space, so maximal compact subgroups become isotropy groups.

Analytic Input

The exponential map behaves globally in the noncompact directions, while nilpotence makes the \(N\)-factor polynomial.

The final theorem is not a miracle product formula; it is the convergence of these three kinds of structure.

Proof roadmapUse as a board map before details or review
Use this dependency map either before proving the theorem or as a review after the examples. The algebraic side supplies the involution, the a-space, and the root spaces. The geometric side supplies the fixed-point interpretation of compact subgroups. The analytic side supplies global exponential behavior and nilpotent polynomial coordinates. Iwasawa decomposition sits at the meeting point of these ingredients.
Common Misreadings
keep the coordinates honest

Five Mistakes That Distort \(KAN\)

1. Treating \(K\) as disposable

\(K\) disappears in \(G/K\), but it is essential for the group-level decomposition and conjugacy of compact subgroups.

2. Treating \(A\) as all of \(\mathfrak p\)

\(A\) comes from a maximal abelian subspace; the remaining noncompact directions are organized through roots and \(N\).

3. Treating \(N\) as abelian

Nilpotent means brackets eventually stop, not that brackets vanish immediately.

4. Confusing local and global products

The theorem asserts a global analytic diffeomorphism, much stronger than a neighborhood coordinate chart.

5. Forgetting real dimensions in the complex case

Complex root spaces contribute twice their complex dimension when the algebra is viewed over the real numbers.

Correction Strategy

For each factor, ask which invariant certifies it: compact isotropy, positive diagonal, root bracket, nilpotent series, or reconstruction residual.

Misconception auditCoordinate claims need invariant checks
This slide is a useful checkpoint before assigning problems. Students often remember the letters K, A, and N but blur their roles. K is compact isotropy, A is a chosen flat direction, and N is built from positive restricted roots. The strongest correction is to tie each factor to an invariant or computation rather than to a visual mnemonic alone.
