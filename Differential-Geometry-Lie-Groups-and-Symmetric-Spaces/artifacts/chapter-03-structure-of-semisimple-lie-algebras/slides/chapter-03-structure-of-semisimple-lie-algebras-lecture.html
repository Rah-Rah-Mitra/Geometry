Lie and Engel Theorems

Triangular Form Turns Existence Into a Chain

Lie theorem heuristic

If a complex representation is solvable, there is a flag preserved by the whole algebra.

\(0 \subset V_1 \subset V_2 \subset \cdots \subset V_n\)
Engel theorem heuristic

If every adjoint operator is nilpotent, repeated brackets eventually stop, and a nonzero central direction appears.

Notebook witness: solvable_nilpotent_chain_dimensions.csvKey contrast: triangular versus strictly upper triangular.
Present Lie and Engel theorems through finite diagnostics rather than full proofs. Lie theorem pushes solvable representations toward upper triangular form, while Engel theorem identifies nilpotence by forcing repeated commutators to terminate. The chain dimensions give students a concrete clock.

The derived chain measures how quickly commutators erase nonabelian behavior, while the central descending chain measures how far brackets can move before all motion vanishes. The triangular five-by-five model is deliberately small, but the qualitative distinction survives in arbitrary finite-dimensional settings.
Cartan Subalgebras

Regular Elements Have Small Centralizers

In \( \mathfrak{sl}_3 \), a diagonal element measures off-diagonal directions by weight differences.

Definition lens

A Cartan subalgebra \( \mathfrak h \) is a nilpotent subalgebra that equals its normalizer; for semisimple complex algebras it acts as the simultaneous diagonal measuring subalgebra.

\(\mathfrak z_{\mathfrak{sl}_3}(H)\) has dimension \(2\) when \(H\) is regular.
Notebook table: regular centralizer 2; singular centralizer 4.Regular means no accidental repeated weight.
Move from nilpotent collapse to regular semisimple measurement. A Cartan subalgebra is not chosen merely for convenience; it is the probe whose adjoint eigenvalues split the algebra. In sl(3), regular diagonal elements have the expected two-dimensional centralizer, while singular ones gain extra symmetries.
Root Space Decomposition

The Cartan Subalgebra Sorts the Algebra by Weights

Root space
\(\mathfrak g_\alpha=\{X:[H,X]=\alpha(H)X\ \text{for all }H\in\mathfrak h\}\)

The zero weight is the Cartan part; nonzero weights are the roots.

\(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak g_\alpha\)
In sl(3), each off-diagonal E_ij is one root direction.The Cartan rank is 2.
This is the algebraic hinge of the chapter. Once a Cartan subalgebra h is fixed, every nonzero root alpha records a simultaneous eigenspace for ad h. The decomposition separates the silent Cartan directions from root directions that carry the bracket geometry.

Root Strings and Weyl Reflections

Strings Make the Diagram Integral

Cartan integer
\(\langle\beta,\alpha^\vee\rangle=\frac{2(\beta,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\in\mathbb Z\)

Reflection sends \( \beta \) to \( \beta-\langle\beta,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha \).

The notebook checks the \(A2\) Cartan matrix, uninterrupted root strings, and reflection preservation of the finite root set.

Reflections generate the Weyl group.Root strings convert angles into integers.
Root strings explain why root systems are not arbitrary point clouds. Moving along a root direction stops at integral endpoints, and reflection across the perpendicular hyperplane preserves the whole set. This is where Cartan integers begin controlling the geometry of the diagram.
Root Pattern and Brackets

The Bracket Knows Vector Addition

Rule
\([\mathfrak g_\alpha,\mathfrak g_\beta]\subseteq\mathfrak g_{\alpha+\beta}\)

If \( \alpha+\beta \) is not a root and not zero, the bracket vanishes in the semisimple root-space setting.

sl(3) witness
\([E_{12},E_{23}]=E_{13}\)

The root labels add: \( (e_1-e_2)+(e_2-e_3)=e_1-e_3 \).

Notebook table: sl3_root_commutator_table.csvRoot addition is a structural filter on brackets.
The bracket rule is the operational reason roots matter. If X_alpha and X_beta are root vectors, their bracket has weight alpha plus beta when that sum is a root, lands in h when beta is minus alpha, and otherwise vanishes. The sl(3) table verifies the rule explicitly.

Emphasize the structural payoff: after the Cartan subalgebra and roots are known, much of the semisimple algebra is constrained by a finite diagram and integer structure constants. This does not remove analysis or proof, but it turns a large algebra into inspected local interactions.
