Affine Locally Symmetric Spaces
Normal Coordinates

Local Symmetry Starts In Normal Coordinates

Local Model

At \(p\), normal coordinates identify nearby points with tangent vectors.

$$s_p(\operatorname{Exp}_p v)=\operatorname{Exp}_p(-v).$$

The space is locally symmetric when these local reflections preserve the affine or Riemannian data, not just the underlying set of nearby points.

Begin at one point and keep the picture deliberately local. In normal coordinates, the geodesic symmetry is just the operation that sends a tangent vector to its negative. The key question is not whether this reflection can be drawn, but whether it preserves the affine structure.
Connection-Level Consequences
Tensor Tests

Affine Symmetry Leaves A Curvature Fingerprint

Affine Reflection Test

If the local symmetry \(s_p\) is affine at each point, then the connection cannot have a preferred direction at \(p\).

$$T=0,\qquad \nabla R=0.$$

Vanishing torsion and parallel curvature are the portable signatures of local symmetry.

Use this slide to connect the reflection test with tensor conditions. If each local geodesic symmetry is affine, torsion disappears and curvature is parallel. That means a measurement made at one point can be transported consistently along nearby geodesics without changing its algebraic content.
Isometry Groups
From Point Symmetry To G/K

Global Symmetry Forces Homogeneity

Global Definition

A Riemannian symmetric space has an isometry \(s_p\) at every point \(p\) with \(p\) as an isolated fixed point.

Those symmetries generate enough isometries to move one point to another.

$$M \simeq G/K.$$

Shift from local data to global motion. A globally symmetric Riemannian space has point symmetries that extend to isometries, and those isometries can move the base point around the manifold. This is the conceptual reason the chapter arrives at a homogeneous model G over K.
Symmetric Pairs
Involution And Brackets

The Symmetric Pair Splits The Algebra

Involution

An automorphism \(\sigma^2=1\) decomposes the Lie algebra into eigenspaces.

Geometric Meaning

\(\mathfrak{k}\) fixes the base point, while \(\mathfrak{p}\simeq T_oM\).

Bracket Pattern

$$[\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}]\subset\mathfrak{k},\quad [\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p},\quad [\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{k}.$$

Present the involution as the algebraic shadow of a point reflection. The fixed part k is infinitesimal isotropy, while p represents tangent directions at the base point. The bracket containment rules are the structural checks that make geodesics and curvature computable.
Concrete Globally Symmetric Space
Rank-One Model

Sphere Model: \(SO(3)/SO(2)\)

Algebra Visible On The Sphere

At \(o=(0,0,1)\), rotations about the vertical axis form \(\mathfrak{k}\).

The two rotations that move \(o\) span \(\mathfrak{p}\).

$$[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{k}$$

is the bracket trace of spherical curvature.

Anchor the abstraction in the round sphere. Rotations about the vertical axis fix the north pole and form k. The rotations that tip the pole form p, and their one-parameter orbits trace great circles. This example should make the symbols G, K, k, and p visible.
Geodesic Formula
Exponentiating Tangent Directions

Geodesics Are Group Orbits

Base-Point Formula

For \(X\in\mathfrak{p}\), the geodesic through \(o=eK\) is

$$\gamma_X(t)=\exp(tX)\cdot o.$$

The homogeneous model turns a tangent vector into a one-parameter subgroup acting on the base point.

Emphasize that the formula exp(tX).o is not a decorative notation choice. It says that a tangent vector in p is promoted to a one-parameter subgroup, and the subgroup moves the base point along a geodesic of the symmetric space.
Exponential Differential
Rank-One Distortion Meter

The Exponential Differential Reads Curvature Sign

Three Signs, One Series

On rank-one examples, the transverse part of \(d\operatorname{Exp}\) is measured by:

$$\frac{\sin r}{r},\qquad 1,\qquad \frac{\sinh r}{r}.$$

The sign of curvature decides whether normal coordinates squeeze, stay flat, or stretch.

Use the three rank-one curves as a distortion meter for normal coordinates. Positive curvature squeezes transverse variation through sin(r) over r, flat geometry leaves it unchanged, and negative curvature stretches it through sinh(r) over r. The same formal series knows all three cases.
Curvature Formula
Nested Brackets

Curvature Is A Bracket Measurement

At The Base Point

For \(X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{p}\), the symmetric pair gives

$$R_o(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z].$$

The middle bracket moves into \(\mathfrak{k}\), then acts back on \(\mathfrak{p}\).

This slide is the algebraic center of the lecture. Once the symmetric pair is known, the curvature tensor at the base point is computed by a nested bracket. Students should read the sign comparison as evidence that geometry has been converted into Lie algebra bookkeeping.
Local To Global
Why One Point Can Be Enough

Parallel Curvature Makes Local Data Portable

Why It Matters

Parallel curvature changes local symmetry from a pointwise trick into reusable data.

$$\nabla R=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad R\ \text{is a transported template}.$$

This is the doorway to building a global model from curvature at a single base point.

Return to the local-to-global direction. Parallel curvature is what prevents the local model from changing as one moves. It lets the tangent-space curvature data act like a constant template, which is why the chapter can reconstruct a symmetric Lie algebra from one point.
Reconstruction
Curvature Builds A Lie Algebra

Reconstructing A Model From One Tangent Space

2

\(R_o(X,Y)\)

3

\(\mathfrak{k}\)

4

\([\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{k}\)

5

\(G/K\)

Explain the reconstruction as a disciplined build rather than magic. Start with p as the tangent space, add k as curvature-preserving endomorphisms, and define brackets so the curvature tensor becomes the p-p component. The Jacobi identity is the consistency test.
Compact Lie Groups
Bi-Invariant Metrics

Compact Lie Groups Are Symmetric

Group Case

For a compact connected Lie group with a bi-invariant metric, the symmetry at the identity is inversion.

$$s_e(g)=g^{-1}.$$

The homogeneous presentation is \((G\times G)/G^\ast\), where \(G^\ast\) is the diagonal subgroup.

Compact Lie groups with bi-invariant metrics are not side examples; they demonstrate the general construction in a familiar algebraic setting. The product group G times G acts by left and right translation, the diagonal subgroup fixes the identity, and inversion supplies the point symmetry.
Local And Global Claims
Completeness Matters

Locally Symmetric Versus Globally Symmetric

Local Symmetry

  • Defined in small normal neighborhoods.
  • Detected by \(T=0\) and \(\nabla R=0\).
  • Allows a curvature-based infinitesimal model.

Global Symmetry

  • Point reflections extend to global isometries.
  • The isometry group acts transitively.
  • The space is represented as \(G/K\).
Separate two claims that are easy to blur. Local symmetry is a differential condition visible in small neighborhoods, while global symmetry asks the point reflections to extend across the whole space. Completeness and simple connectedness are the bridge conditions that remove monodromy and boundary obstructions.
Totally Geodesic Pieces
Lie Triple Systems

Lie Triple Systems Stay Totally Geodesic

Closure Test

A subspace \(\mathfrak{s}\subset\mathfrak{p}\) is a Lie triple system when

$$[X,[Y,Z]]\in\mathfrak{s}$$

for all \(X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{s}\).

It is the tangent signal for a totally geodesic symmetric subspace.

Introduce Lie triple systems as the tangent-space signature of a totally geodesic submanifold. The closure condition says that repeated curvature-style brackets never leave the chosen subspace. Geometrically, geodesics starting tangent to that subspace keep living inside the smaller symmetric piece.
Applied Lab
Constant Curvature Bracket

Lab: Change The Curvature Sign

\(K=-1\)

\([P_1,P_2]=-K_0\)

Exp factor: \(\sinh(r)/r\). Curvature is negative and transverse directions spread.

\(K=0\)

\([P_1,P_2]=0\)

Exp factor: \(1\). The model is flat and normal coordinates have no transverse curvature distortion.

\(K=1\)

\([P_1,P_2]=K_0\)

Exp factor: \(\sin(r)/r\). Curvature is positive and transverse directions focus.

Treat the lab as a controlled experiment. Changing one sign in the constant-curvature bracket changes the p-p bracket, the exponential distortion factor, and the curvature sign together. The value of the notebook is that these consequences can be checked independently and then recognized as one structure.
