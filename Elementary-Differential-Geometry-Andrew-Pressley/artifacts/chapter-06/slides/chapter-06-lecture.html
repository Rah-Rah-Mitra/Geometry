Pressley Elementary Differential Geometry - Chapter 6

The First Fundamental Form

The metric coefficients \(E,F,G\) turn a surface patch into a local measuring instrument for length, angle, area, and map classification.

\(ds^2 = E\,du^2 + 2F\,du\,dv + G\,dv^2\)
Source span: printed pages 121-158; PDF pages 127-163.
Open the lecture by presenting the first fundamental form as the measuring rule that lives on a surface patch. The point is not to memorize three letters, but to see how dot products of tangent vectors become a compact local ledger. This chapter asks how far that ledger goes: it computes lengths, angles, areas, and decides what different surface maps preserve.
Chapter Question

What Can Be Read From One Quadratic Form?

Metric Ledger

\(M=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix}\), where \(E=\sigma_u\cdot\sigma_u\), \(F=\sigma_u\cdot\sigma_v\), and \(G=\sigma_v\cdot\sigma_v\).

Once \(M\) is known, first-order measurement can be done in coordinates without redrawing the surface in space.

Coordinate Step To Surface Step
This slide sets up the central translation: a parametrized patch gives two tangent directions, and their dot products assemble the Gram matrix. Emphasize that \(E,F,G\) are not arbitrary coefficients; they record the actual Euclidean lengths and mutual angle of \(\sigma_u\) and \(\sigma_v\). The class should read the diagram from left to right: a coordinate velocity becomes a physical tangent vector whose length is measured by \(M\).

Part I

Metric Coefficients As Local Rulers

The first fundamental form converts small coordinate displacements into actual surface lengths and angles.

This section divider is a conceptual pause: the class is leaving the visual patch and concentrating on the metric as a local ruler. Stress that the coordinate pair \((du,dv)\) by itself has no physical meaning until the surface tells us how the coordinate grid is stretched and sheared. The matrix is the compact object that performs that conversion.
Definition

The Gram Matrix Of A Patch

First Fundamental Form

\(I = d\sigma\cdot d\sigma = E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2\)

\(M=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_u\cdot\sigma_u&\sigma_u\cdot\sigma_v\\\sigma_v\cdot\sigma_u&\sigma_v\cdot\sigma_v\end{pmatrix}\)

Velocity Ellipse: \(x^T M x=1\)

If \(F\neq 0\), the coordinate grid is not orthogonal on the surface even when the \((u,v)\)-axes look perpendicular on paper.

Introduce \(M\) as a Gram matrix, which helps students connect this chapter to linear algebra they already know. The ellipse is useful because it shows the metric as an anisotropic measuring rule: different coordinate velocities can have the same physical speed, and the tilt records the cross term \(F\). The warning prepares students for a common mistake, which is to read orthogonality from the parameter domain instead of from the dot product on the surface.
Area Element

The Determinant Measures The Grid Cell

Parallelogram Spanned By \(\sigma_u,\sigma_v\)
Identity

\(\lVert \sigma_u\times\sigma_v\rVert^2 = EG-F^2=\det M\)

  • \(\sqrt{\det M}\,du\,dv\) is the surface area element.
  • \(\det M>0\) means the patch has two independent tangent directions.
  • Area preservation is a determinant test, not a full matrix test.
This slide isolates the determinant because it will become the decisive quantity in Archimedes' theorem and in equiareal maps. The parallelogram picture makes the algebraic expression \(EG-F^2\) feel less mysterious: it is simply the square of the area spanned by the two tangent vectors. Point out that preserving determinant is weaker than preserving the whole matrix, which is why area can survive while angles and lengths change.
Curve Length

Metric Speed Along A Coordinate Curve

Length Formula

\(L=\int_a^b \sqrt{E\dot u^2+2F\dot u\dot v+G\dot v^2}\,dt\)

Compute \(E,F,G\) at \((u(t),v(t))\), then feed in the coordinate velocity \((\dot u,\dot v)\).

A Curve In Parameters And On The Surface

Check against the embedding: \(\frac{d}{dt}\sigma(u(t),v(t))=\sigma_u\dot u+\sigma_v\dot v\), and its squared norm is exactly the integrand squared.

Here the first fundamental form becomes an operational formula for curve length. The important teaching move is to compare the coordinate curve with its image on the surface: the same parameter \(t\) is used, but the metric supplies the physical speed. The final check reassures students that the formula is not a new assumption; it is the chain rule plus the dot products already stored in \(M\).
Local Isometry

The Cylinder Keeps The Plane Metric

Wrap A Strip Without Stretching
Metric Comparison

\(\sigma(u,v)=(\cos u,\sin u,v)\)

\(\sigma_u=(-\sin u,\cos u,0),\quad \sigma_v=(0,0,1)\)

\(M=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\)

Same matrix as the flat strip, so local lengths and angles agree.

The cylinder example is the cleanest way to separate bending from stretching. Even though the strip and cylinder look different in three-dimensional space, the tangent dot products agree with those of the original coordinate plane. Use this to define local isometry concretely: the map preserves the entire first fundamental form, not merely the appearance of a grid.
Part II

Three Preservation Tests, One Matrix

A surface map can preserve all metric data, only angles, or only area; the distinctions are visible in \(M\).

=

Isometry

\(M_2=M_1\)

λ

Conformal

\(M_2=\lambda M_1\), \(\lambda>0\)

det

Equiareal

\(\det M_2=\det M_1\)

Pause here to make the classification logic explicit. All three tests compare metric matrices, but they demand different levels of agreement. This framing is especially helpful before stereographic projection and Archimedes' theorem, because those examples are often remembered visually while their actual metric behavior is more precise.

Use the table to keep students from conflating the three preservation words. Isometry is a full inner-product statement, conformality keeps angles because every tangent length is scaled by the same local factor, and equiareality only constrains the area density. The two boxes at the bottom explain why the implications run in one direction: full matrix equality implies determinant equality, but not conversely.
Conformal Example

Stereographic Projection Scales Evenly

Equal-Angle Coordinate Net, Changing Scale
Stereographic Metric

\(ds^2=\frac{4}{(1+u^2+v^2)^2}(du^2+dv^2)\)

  • \(F=0\), so the coordinate directions stay orthogonal.
  • \(E=G=\lambda(u,v)\), so all directions scale equally.
  • Area scales by \(\lambda^2\) for the two-dimensional element.
Stereographic projection is the chapter's model for conformality. The formula shows that the pulled-back metric is a scalar multiple of the flat metric, and the scalar depends on radius. Ask students to inspect the two crossing curves: the angle behavior is protected because every tangent direction at a point is multiplied by the same local factor, even though size changes from place to place.
Equiareal Example

Archimedes Swaps The Metric Coefficients

Sphere And Cylinder

\(M_{\text{sphere}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\cos^2\theta\end{pmatrix}\)

\(M_{\text{cyl}}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\)

\(\det M_{\text{sphere}}=\det M_{\text{cyl}}=\cos^2\theta\)

Horizontal Bands Keep Area

Equal determinants preserve area elements; different matrices warn that lengths and angles are not generally preserved.

Present Archimedes' theorem as a determinant computation rather than as a purely spatial surprise. The matrices differ by swapping where the \(\cos^2\theta\) factor appears, so tangent directions are distorted differently. Yet their determinants agree, which is exactly what the area element sees; this is the chapter's equiareal mechanism in a classical setting.
Part III

Spherical Geometry From The Same Metric Habit

On the unit sphere, distances and triangle identities arise by applying the first fundamental form to great-circle motion.

This section moves from local measurement to global-looking formulas on the unit sphere. The key message is that spherical distance is still metric length, but the minimizing curves are great-circle arcs. The dot-product formula for distance is convenient because it packages that metric length as a central angle between unit vectors.
Spherical Distance

Great-Circle Length Is A Central Angle

Unit Sphere Rule

\(d(p,q)=\arccos(p\cdot q)\)

For unit vectors, the dot product is the cosine of the angle at the center. That angle is the length of the shorter great-circle arc.

Chord Versus Surface Distance

The formula is intrinsic to the unit sphere: rotate the whole picture in space and the dot product, central angle, and great-circle length remain the same.

Students often confuse the Euclidean chord with the spherical distance, so explicitly contrast them. The chord is a straight line through space, while the metric distance on the sphere is measured along the surface. The dot product formula is powerful because it computes that surface length from ambient coordinates without choosing a latitude-longitude chart.
Spherical Triangles

Angles Live In Tangent Planes

Sides Are Arcs, Angles Are Tangent Angles
Triangle Data
  • Sides: central angles from vertex dot products.
  • Angles: compare tangent directions to the incident great circles.
  • Area on the unit sphere: spherical excess.

\(\operatorname{area}(\triangle)=\alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi\)

This slide translates a spherical triangle into computable metric data. The side lengths come from central angles, but the vertex angles are not angles between chords; they are angles between tangent directions to the great circles at the vertex. The spherical excess formula then turns that angle data into area, showing another way the first fundamental form controls measurement on the sphere.
Applied Lab

Classify A Map By Pulling Back Its Metric

Diagnostic Algorithm
  1. Compute \(M_1\) for the source patch.
  2. Compute the pulled-back \(M_2\) for the target description.
  3. Ask equality, scalar multiple, and determinant questions in that order.
Decision Flow
The applied lab turns the definitions into a repeatable procedure. Start with the strongest condition because equality of matrices immediately gives all the other first-order measurements, then relax to scalar multiple and determinant checks. This is a useful computational habit: a visual map may look convincing, but the pullback metric tells exactly what has been preserved.

This slide consolidates the examples into the diagnostic language students should carry forward. The cylinder, stereographic projection, and Archimedes map are deliberately placed side by side because they satisfy different matrix conditions. Encourage students to state both the classification and the evidence; the evidence is always a relation between metric matrices or their determinants.
Recap

The Chapter In One Metric Ledger

Takeaways
  • \(E,F,G\) are the dot products of the coordinate tangent vectors.
  • The quadratic form gives speed, length, angle, and area density.
  • Local isometry means equality of the whole matrix.
  • Conformality means scalar rescaling of the matrix.
  • Equiareality means equality of determinants.
  • Spherical distances and triangle areas are metric measurements on the unit sphere.
  • Equal determinants preserve area, not the full tangent shape.
