A Route Through Surface Curvature

1. Local Deviation
The tangent plane absorbs first-order motion. The remaining normal height is quadratic and becomes the second fundamental form.
2. Moving Normals
The Gauss map sends each point to its unit normal. Its derivative gives a tangent-plane operator, the Weingarten map.
3. Curves On Surfaces
A curve acceleration splits into normal bending and surface-visible turning, leading to normal and geodesic curvature.
4. Projected Derivatives
Ordinary differentiation in \(R^3\) is projected back to the tangent plane to define the covariant derivative.
5. Parallel Transport
A tangent vector field is parallel when its covariant derivative vanishes along the curve.
6. Checks
Symmetry, self-adjointness, curvature decomposition, and length preservation act as the chapter's sanity tests.
Give students a map before entering formulas. Each object in the chapter answers the same question from a different angle: what part of ambient change is genuinely surface geometry? The route is intentionally cumulative, so later slides keep referring back to the tangent plane, the normal field, and the projection operation.

From Tangent Plane Error To \(II\)

Definition
For a patch \(\sigma(u,v)\) with unit normal \(N\),
\(II = L\,du^2 + 2M\,du\,dv + N_c\,dv^2\)
where \(L=\sigma_{uu}\cdot N,\; M=\sigma_{uv}\cdot N,\; N_c=\sigma_{vv}\cdot N\).
  • The first fundamental form measures tangent lengths.
  • The second fundamental form measures second-order normal deviation.
  • The sign depends on the chosen orientation of \(N\).
The key intuition is that the tangent plane is already the best first-order approximation to the surface. Curvature appears in the next term, and only its normal component survives as an intrinsic bending measurement inside the chosen embedding. Emphasize that \(N_c\) is named this way here to avoid confusing the coefficient with the normal vector \(N\).

Two Quadratic Measurements On One Tangent Plane

Metric Data
\[ I(a,b)= \begin{bmatrix}a&b\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}E&F\\ F&G\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}a\\ b\end{bmatrix} \] Lengths and angles are read from \(E,F,G\).
Bending Data
\[ II(a,b)= \begin{bmatrix}a&b\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}L&M\\ M&N_c\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}a\\ b\end{bmatrix} \] Normal bending is read from \(L,M,N_c\).
This slide separates two matrices that live on the same coordinate tangent plane. The first form is the ruler; the second form is the bending detector. Many formulas later in the chapter are easier to remember as quotients or compatibility relations between these two quadratic measurements.

The Gauss Map Turns Normals Into Geometry

Definition
The Gauss map \(G:S\to S^2\) sends a point of an oriented surface to its chosen unit normal.
Interpretation
Rapid change in \(G\) means nearby tangent planes rotate rapidly. Curvature is encoded in this derivative of the normal field.

Inspection target: compare motion on the surface with motion of the normal tip on \(S^2\).

The Gauss map is conceptually simple but extremely powerful: it replaces a moving tangent plane by a moving point on the unit sphere. Students should watch the normal tip, not only the base point. This prepares the Weingarten map as the derivative of the Gauss map with a sign convention.

The Weingarten Map Is The Shape Operator

Operator Form
\[ W = I^{-1}II,\qquad II(v,w)=I(Wv,w). \] In geometric language, \(W=-dG\) after identifying the target tangent plane of \(S^2\) with \(T_pS\).
  • \(W\) is self-adjoint with respect to \(I\).
  • Its eigenvectors are principal directions.
  • Its eigenvalues become principal curvatures in Chapter 8.
Treat \(W\) as a machine that inputs a tangent direction and outputs another tangent vector describing how the normal changes in that direction. The relation \(II(v,w)=I(Wv,w)\) is the bridge between the bilinear form and the linear map. Self-adjointness is important because it tells us the bending operator has orthogonal eigendirections under the surface metric.

Normal Curvature Depends Only On Tangent Direction

Formula
For a nonzero tangent direction \(v\), \[ \kappa_n(v)=\frac{II(v,v)}{I(v,v)}. \]
Consequence
Any two surface curves passing through \(p\) with the same tangent direction have the same normal curvature at \(p\).
The quotient is a clean use of both fundamental forms. The denominator normalizes the tangent direction using the surface metric, while the numerator measures normal bending in that direction. Stress that normal curvature is not a property of the drawn curve alone; after fixing the tangent direction, the surface itself determines the normal component.

Reading Bending By Direction

Directional Experiment
At a fixed point, rotate a tangent direction \(v(\theta)\) and record \(II(v,v)/I(v,v)\). The result is a directional profile of bending.
  • Extremal directions are special because \(Wv\) is parallel to \(v\).
  • The next chapter names their values \(k_1,k_2\).
  • Here the important point is the transition from a form to a direction-dependent scalar.
This slide previews principal curvature without stealing the next chapter's thunder. A polar profile lets students see the Rayleigh quotient as a function of direction. The visual also justifies why self-adjointness matters: symmetric operators have well-behaved extremal directions.

A Curve Acceleration Splits Into Two Stories

Decomposition
For a unit-speed curve on the surface, \[ \gamma'' = \kappa_n N + \kappa_g\,Q \] where \(Q\) is tangent to the surface and perpendicular to \(\gamma'\).
Reading
\(\kappa_n\) is imposed by the surface normal. \(\kappa_g\) is the turning a surface-bound observer can feel inside the tangent plane.
Students often conflate curvature of a space curve with curvature of the surface. This split resolves the confusion by showing that ambient acceleration has a normal component and a tangent component. The tangent component is the one that detects whether the curve is trying to be straight from the surface's internal point of view.

Sphere Latitude: Normal And Geodesic Curvature

Worked Example
On the unit sphere, a latitude at angle \(\theta\) has \[ \kappa = \sec\theta,\qquad \kappa_n=-1,\qquad |\kappa_g|=|\tan\theta|. \]
Sanity Check
\[ \kappa^2=\kappa_n^2+\kappa_g^2 \] becomes \(\sec^2\theta=1+\tan^2\theta\).
The latitude is a compact example because everyone can picture it, and the identity reduces to a familiar trigonometric check. At the equator the geodesic curvature vanishes, so the equator is a geodesic. Away from the equator, the curve bends partly because the sphere bends and partly because the latitude turns within the surface.

Covariant Derivative: Differentiate, Then Project

Definition Along A Curve
If \(V(t)\) is a tangent vector field along \(\gamma(t)\), then \[ \nabla_{\gamma'}V = \bigl(V'(t)\bigr)^{\top}. \] Take the ordinary derivative in \(R^3\), then keep only the tangent component.
Common Mistake
\(V'(t)\) need not be tangent even when every \(V(t)\) is tangent. Projection is the essential operation.
This is one of the cleanest definitions in the embedded-surface setting. The ordinary derivative sees the surrounding \(R^3\), while the covariant derivative records what remains visible to a tangent observer. Make sure students hear that projection is not a cosmetic step; it is what turns ambient calculus into surface calculus.

Parallel Transport Means Zero Tangent Change

Definition
A tangent field \(V\) along \(\gamma\) is parallel when \[ \nabla_{\gamma'}V=0. \]
  • The vector may rotate in ambient space.
  • Its tangent-plane derivative is zero.
  • Lengths and inner products are preserved along the path.
Parallel transport is often surprising because the transported vector can visibly turn in space while still having zero covariant derivative. The point is that the surface's tangent planes are moving too. On a latitude of the sphere, the final vector need not match the initial tangent direction after one loop, which previews holonomy without needing the later theory.

Discussion Exercise: Diagnose A Surface Direction

Prompt
At a point of a surface, suppose a tangent direction \(v\) satisfies \(II(v,v)=0\) but \(I(v,v)>0\).

What does this say about normal curvature in that direction? Does it imply the surface is flat near the point?
Hint
Separate a statement about one direction at one point from a statement about all directions in a neighborhood.
Let students articulate the distinction before giving the answer. The quotient says \(\kappa_n(v)=0\), so that direction has no second-order normal bending at the point. It does not make the whole surface flat, because other directions may bend and the condition may fail at nearby points.

Takeaways

  • \(II\) is the normal quadratic term after the tangent plane has done its first-order job.
  • The Gauss map records the normal field; \(W=-dG\) converts its derivative into a tangent operator.
  • Normal curvature is \(II(v,v)/I(v,v)\), so it is determined by a tangent direction.
  • Curve acceleration splits into normal curvature and geodesic curvature.
  • The covariant derivative is ambient differentiation followed by tangent projection.
  • Parallel transport is zero covariant derivative and preserves the surface metric along the path.
Close by tying every formula back to the same projection principle. The chapter takes ambient data from \(R^3\) and repeatedly separates tangent information from normal information. That separation is the gateway to later intrinsic geometry, where the surface's own measurements become more important than the surrounding space.
