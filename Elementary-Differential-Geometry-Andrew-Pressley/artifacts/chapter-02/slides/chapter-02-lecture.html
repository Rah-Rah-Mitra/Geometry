How Much Does A Curve Curve?

Curvature, signed turning, osculating circles, torsion, and the Frenet frame as measurable geometry.

curvaturetorsionFrenet frame
This opening slide frames the chapter as a measurement problem rather than a vocabulary list. The point is to replace the visual impression of bending by quantities that survive changes of parameter. Emphasize that the moving tangent, normal, and binormal are not extra decoration; they are the instruments used to read curvature and torsion from a curve.

Lecture Route

from local bending to moving frames

1. Remove Speed

Curvature measures how the unit tangent changes with arc length, not with an arbitrary clock.

2. Add Orientation

Signed curvature records left-turning versus right-turning motion in the plane.

3. Fit A Circle

The osculating circle converts curvature into a radius and a center of curvature.

4. Leave The Plane

Torsion detects rotation of the osculating plane along a space curve.

5. Track The Frame

The Frenet-Serret equations organize the derivatives of \(T,N,B\).

6. Reverse The Process

Prescribed signed curvature can be integrated to design a plane curve.

This route mirrors the chapter notebook while keeping the lecture focused on the geometric questions students should ask. Start with the separation between shape and parametrization, because many later formulas are only meaningful after that separation is clear. The final reconstruction step is included to show that curvature is not merely a diagnostic; it can also be a recipe for making shape.

Arc Length Is The Right Clock

speed is parameter noise

Definition Pattern

For a unit-speed curve \(\gamma(s)\), the curvature is

\( \kappa(s)=\lVert T'(s)\rVert=\lVert\gamma''(s)\rVert \).

Arc length \(s\) makes the unit tangent \(T=d\gamma/ds\) a pure direction field. Curvature then asks how fast that direction changes per unit distance traveled.

The red and green tick marks show the essential nuisance: a parameter can slow down or speed up without changing the geometric trace. Curvature should not react to that nuisance, so the chapter first privileges arc length. Once the curve is parametrized by distance traveled, the derivative of the tangent has the correct units and interpretation.

Curvature Without Reparametrizing

the speed factors cancel

Parameter-Invariant Formula

If \(\gamma(t)\) is regular in space, then

\( \kappa(t)=\dfrac{\lVert \gamma'(t)\times\gamma''(t)\rVert}{\lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert^3} \).

The numerator sees acceleration perpendicular to velocity. The denominator removes the cubic speed inflation introduced by the chosen parameter.

This slide is the algebraic payoff of using arc length conceptually even when the data arrive with another parameter. The cross product kills the tangential acceleration, which only changes speed along the curve. The power of three in the denominator is a useful warning sign: curvature is sensitive to derivatives, but the formula is arranged so that the final value is geometric.

Curvature Is Second-Order Departure

tangent line first, bending second

Local Expansion

At unit speed,

\( \gamma(s+h)=\gamma(s)+hT(s)+\tfrac{h^2}{2}\kappa(s)N(s)+O(h^3). \)

The tangent line captures first-order motion. Curvature is the leading normal correction that the tangent line cannot explain.

This is a good place to slow down and connect formulas to a familiar approximation idea. A differentiable curve is first approximated by its tangent line, and curvature appears in the next visible term. The diagram makes the normal gap the object of attention, so students can see why curvature is a second-order invariant rather than a slope.

Signed Curvature Records Direction Of Turning

plane curves have an orientation test

Plane Formula

For \(\gamma(t)=(x(t),y(t))\),

\( \kappa_s(t)=\dfrac{x'y''-y'x''}{(x'^2+y'^2)^{3/2}}. \)

The magnitude is ordinary curvature. The sign distinguishes counterclockwise turning from clockwise turning.

Signed curvature is the same bending measurement with orientation retained. The determinant in the numerator is the two-dimensional cross product, so its sign says whether velocity rotates toward the left normal or the right normal. This sign matters later because integrating signed curvature reconstructs the tangent angle, while unsigned curvature would forget which way to turn.

Tangent Angle Differentiates To Signed Curvature

turning is curvature accumulated along distance

Angle Identity

If \(T=(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)\), then

\( \dfrac{d\varphi}{ds}=\kappa_s. \)

Total signed curvature is total tangent rotation. For many closed regular curves, this becomes an integer multiple of \(2\pi\).

The tangent angle turns the vector field along the curve into a scalar function. Its derivative with respect to arc length is signed curvature, which makes curvature feel like angular velocity measured per unit distance. This identity is also the conceptual bridge from local curvature to global turning behavior of closed plane curves.

The Osculating Circle Is The Best Local Circle

curvature becomes a radius

Center And Radius

When \(\kappa_s\ne0\),

\( R=\dfrac{1}{|\kappa_s|},\qquad C=\gamma+\dfrac{1}{\kappa_s}N_s. \)

The circle shares position, tangent direction, and curvature with the curve at the selected point.

The osculating circle turns the abstract number curvature into a concrete geometric object. Its radius is reciprocal to curvature, so high curvature means a small tight-fitting circle and low curvature means a large one. The sign determines which side of the tangent line the center lies on for an oriented plane curve.

The Evolute Tracks Centers Of Curvature

a curve behind the curve

Inspection Target

Move along the curve and watch the center \(C(s)\). Sharp bending pulls the center close; gentle bending sends it far away.

At inflection points, signed curvature passes through zero, so the center of curvature escapes to infinity.

The evolute is useful because it makes curvature variation visible as another curve. Rather than only asking how large curvature is at a point, students can watch where the osculating circle centers travel. Mention that near zero curvature the construction becomes unstable, which is why inflection points behave like singular events for the evolute.

Space Curves Need One More Measurement

curvature bends; torsion twists

Torsion

For a regular space curve with nonzero curvature, torsion measures the rotation of the osculating plane.

\( \tau=\dfrac{(\gamma'\times\gamma'')\cdot\gamma'''}{\lVert\gamma'\times\gamma''\rVert^2}. \)

A planar curve can have curvature but has zero torsion because its osculating plane does not twist.

Curvature alone cannot distinguish a flat circle from a helix with the same projected radius. Torsion supplies the missing measurement by asking how the curve's best-fitting plane rotates as the point moves. This is the first place where the binormal becomes essential rather than optional notation.

The Frenet Frame Carries Local Coordinates

three directions attached to one point

Frame Construction

\(T\) is the unit tangent. \(N\) points toward changing \(T\). \(B=T\times N\) completes the right-handed frame.

The frame turns a space curve into a moving coordinate system adapted to its bending and twisting.

The Frenet frame is often introduced as a collection of letters, but this slide should make it feel like a portable coordinate system. The tangent follows the curve, the normal follows the change in tangent, and the binormal records the orientation of the osculating plane. Stress that the frame is only well behaved where curvature is nonzero.

Frenet-Serret Equations Are Frame Bookkeeping

derivatives stay inside the frame

The Moving-Frame System

\( T'=\kappa N,\qquad N'=-\kappa T+\tau B,\qquad B'=-\tau N. \)

The coefficients \(\kappa\) and \(\tau\) are exactly the rates needed to keep the frame orthonormal and right-handed.

Prime means derivative with respect to arc length \(s\).
This slide is intentionally formula centered because the structure is the key visual object. Each derivative of a frame vector is expressed using the other frame vectors, and no component points along itself because unit vectors have perpendicular derivatives. The signs are worth discussing as orientation bookkeeping: curvature rotates \(T\) toward \(N\), while torsion rotates the normal-binormal plane.

Worked Example: A Circular Helix

constant curvature and torsion

Curve

\( \gamma(t)=(a\cos t,\;a\sin t,\;bt). \)

\( \kappa=\dfrac{a}{a^2+b^2},\qquad \tau=\dfrac{b}{a^2+b^2}. \)

The ratio \(b/a\) tilts the curve out of its circular shadow. Increasing pitch raises torsion while preserving the idea of circular bending.

The helix is a calibration example because its curvature and torsion are constant and easy to compare. When \(b=0\), the curve is a circle and torsion vanishes; when \(b\) grows, the curve twists more strongly through space. This example also helps students remember that curvature and torsion have the same denominator but measure different parts of the motion.

Designing A Plane Curve From Curvature

run the chapter backward

Reconstruction Recipe

\( \varphi(s)=\varphi_0+\int_0^s \kappa_s(u)\,du \)

\( \gamma(s)=\gamma_0+\int_0^s(\cos\varphi(u),\sin\varphi(u))\,du \)

The signed curvature function controls tangent direction first; position is obtained only after integrating the tangent.

This reconstruction slide is the chapter's most constructive moment. Given signed curvature, integrate once to obtain the tangent angle and then integrate the unit tangent to obtain a curve. This sequence is also a useful diagnostic for numerical work, because speed should remain close to one if the reconstruction is done in arc-length time.

Common Pitfalls

what to check before trusting a computation

Confusing Speed With Curvature

Large acceleration can come from changing speed. Curvature only uses the part of acceleration that changes direction.

Dropping Orientation Too Early

Unsigned curvature cannot reconstruct whether the curve turns left or right. Keep \(\kappa_s\) when orientation matters.

Using Frenet Frames At \(\kappa=0\)

The normal direction is not determined when the tangent is not changing. Inflection points require care.

Reading Torsion From A Projection

A planar drawing can hide out-of-plane twist. Torsion is a spatial measurement, not a shadow measurement.

This slide gathers the mistakes that most often create wrong answers even when the formulas look familiar. The recurring theme is to ask what geometric information a quantity is allowed to see. Speed, orientation, zero curvature, and projection all affect interpretation, so these checks make the formulas more reliable in practice.

Chapter 02 Synthesis

the curve as measured motion
Curvature

How quickly the unit tangent turns per unit arc length.

Signed Curvature

The oriented turning rate for plane curves, equal to \(d\varphi/ds\).

Osculating Circle

The circle that matches the curve through second-order bending data.

Torsion

The rate at which the osculating plane rotates along a space curve.

Frenet Frame

The moving orthonormal basis \(T,N,B\) adapted to the curve.

Reconstruction

Prescribed signed curvature can generate a unit-speed plane curve.

End by returning to the question in the chapter title: how much does a curve curve? The answer is layered, because plane and space curves require different amounts of information. Curvature measures bending, signed curvature measures oriented turning, torsion measures twisting, and the Frenet frame keeps all of these rates organized in a moving coordinate system.

Exit Problem

five-minute board check

Prompt

For \(\gamma(t)=(3\cos t,3\sin t,4t)\), compute \(\kappa\) and \(\tau\). Then explain geometrically what changes if the 4 is replaced by 0.

Expected conclusion: the curve becomes a planar circle, so torsion vanishes while curvature becomes \(1/3\).

This final prompt checks computation and interpretation together. Students can plug into the helix formulas, but the real target is the sentence explaining why torsion disappears in the circle limit. It also gives a clean closing contrast between bending around a circle and twisting through three-dimensional space.
