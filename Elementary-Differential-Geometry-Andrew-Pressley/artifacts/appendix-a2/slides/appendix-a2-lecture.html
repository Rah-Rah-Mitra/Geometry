Route Through The Appendix

Start compact. Work on the extended complex plane \(C_\infty = C \cup \{\infty\}\), visualized as the sphere.
Then act by matrices. A nonsingular \(2\times2\) complex matrix represents one fractional-linear transformation up to scalar multiple.
Then inspect invariants. Generalized Circles, cross-ratios, and angle data survive in specific ways.
Finally connect back. Stereographic charts and disk models use the same transformations as coordinate changes and isometries.
The sequence of topics is chosen to keep the geometry visible. Each new algebraic expression is paired with a geometric test: where infinity goes, whether a Circle stays a Circle, whether four-point data remains unchanged, and whether orientation is preserved. This gives students a route for checking their own calculations.

The Extended Plane Is The Natural Domain

Compactifying point. Large complex values approach one point on the sphere, not a direction-dependent horizon.
finite \(z\) maps to the sphere; \(|z|\to\infty\) maps to \(N\)
A fractional-linear map can send \(z=-d/c\) to \(\infty\), and can send \(\infty\) to \(a/c\) when \(c\ne0\).
The extended plane prevents a pole from being treated as a failure of the formula. Under stereographic projection, ordinary complex numbers occupy the sphere minus the north pole, and the missing north pole records infinity. Once this picture is in place, Mobius transformations become bijections of a compact surface.

Definition: Fractional-Linear Action

Mobius transformation. A nonsingular matrix \[ M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad ad-bc\ne0, \] acts by \(z\mapsto (az+b)/(cz+d)\) on \(C_\infty\).
Multiplying all four coefficients by the same nonzero scalar leaves the map unchanged.
The determinant condition is the line between a genuine transformation and a collapsed constant map.
The matrix viewpoint is more than a notation shortcut. It explains why scalar multiples represent the same transformation and why inverse matrices produce inverse maps. The determinant condition ensures that the two rows describe independent projective data, so the map does not crush the whole plane to one value.

Four Moves Generate The Intuition

Translation

\(z\mapsto z+b\): shifts the finite chart without changing scale or angle.

Dilation-Rotation

\(z\mapsto az\): one complex multiplication combines scaling and rotation.

Reciprocal

\(z\mapsto 1/z\): swaps near and far, and turns some lines into circles.

Full Fractional Map

The denominator introduces a finite pole and makes infinity move visibly.

The appendix can be read through a small set of elementary motions. Translations and complex multiplications feel familiar from Euclidean geometry, while the reciprocal map contributes the genuinely projective behavior. A general Mobius map is best understood by watching these actions combine rather than by staring only at four coefficients.

Generalized Circles: Lines And Circles Together

\(A|z|^2+\overline{B}z+B\overline{z}+C=0\)
One equation, two shapes. If \(A=0\), the object is a line; if \(A\ne0\), it is an ordinary circle.
This is the right family because reciprocal action can swap the roles of leading and constant terms.
The capitalized word Circle means the union of two familiar species: ordinary circles and straight lines. The real quadratic equation is the bookkeeping device that makes this union algebraically stable. Students should notice that the classification depends on a coefficient, not on drawing style.

Mobius Maps Preserve The Circle Class

Rule of thumb. A Circle through the finite pole of the map becomes a line; a Circle missing the pole generally remains an ordinary circle.

Conceptual reason: translations, complex multiplications, and reciprocals each preserve the generalized-Circle family, so their compositions do too.

Inversion Is Conjugate-Mobius Geometry

\(I(z)=a+\dfrac{r^2}{\overline{z}-\overline{a}}\)
Metric signature. \(|z-a|\,|I(z)-a|=r^2\).
Fixed setwise. A Circle orthogonal to the inversion circle is carried onto itself.
The conjugate in the denominator is the signal that orientation will reverse.
Inversion is a metric construction and a fractional-linear construction with conjugation inserted. The distance-product identity is the easiest way to test individual points, while orthogonal Circles give the global geometric invariant. This is where angle preservation separates from orientation preservation.

The Cross-Ratio Is The Four-Point Fingerprint

\([z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4]=\dfrac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}\)
Invariant. Applying the same Mobius transformation to all four points leaves this value unchanged, wherever the expression is defined in \(C_\infty\).
This explains why Mobius maps are projective coordinate changes: they preserve the projective relation among four marked points.
The cross-ratio is the algebraic invariant that makes the visual Circle-preservation story feel projective. It uses four points because three distinct points can be sent to three chosen targets by a Mobius transformation. The fourth point then records meaningful data that cannot be normalized away.
Projective invariant9 / 16

Three Points Determine The Coordinate Change

Projective flexibility. Three distinct points of \(C_\infty\) can be sent to three distinct target points by a unique Mobius transformation.
Cross-ratio method. Normalize the source triple to \(0,1,\infty\), normalize the target triple the same way, then compose.
Practical warning. Distinctness matters; if two marked points collide, the coordinate prescription degenerates.
The three-point control principle explains why Mobius transformations are rigid despite having a flexible formula. Once three distinct source points and three distinct targets are fixed, the transformation has no remaining freedom. The cross-ratio turns this principle into an actual construction.

Holomorphic Mobius Maps Preserve Oriented Angles

\(f'(z)=\dfrac{ad-bc}{(cz+d)^2}\)
Local action. Away from the pole, the derivative is multiplication by a nonzero complex number.
Complex multiplication scales and rotates tangent vectors, so signed angle data is preserved.
This is the conformal bridge to surface charts and stereographic projection.
At a finite point away from the pole, a holomorphic Mobius map has a nonzero complex derivative. Multiplication by that derivative rotates and scales every tangent vector by the same amount. Therefore the angle between two tangent directions, including its orientation, is preserved.

Conjugate-Mobius Maps Reverse Orientation

Insert reflection. A conjugate-Mobius map applies a Mobius transformation to \(\overline{z}\), or equivalently composes with reflection in the real axis.
Angle size survives. Reflection preserves the magnitude of angles.
Orientation changes. The signed area of a tiny oriented triangle flips sign.
Conjugation is the simplest way to see the difference between conformal and orientation-preserving. It keeps angle sizes intact because reflection is an isometry of the plane, but it reverses the order of a local frame. The notebook visual checks this with a tiny triangle whose signed area changes sign.

Decomposition: Reflections And Inversions

Mobius maps. Built from translations, rotations-dilations, and reciprocal moves; geometrically these are generated by inversions and reflections in suitable generalized Circles.
Conjugate-Mobius maps. Add one reflection, and the same Circle-preserving machinery remains available.
Teaching point. The formula \((az+b)/(cz+d)\) is compact, but the geometry is a composition of simple Circle operations.
The final proposition in the source span points toward a structural view: these transformations can be decomposed into elementary geometric operations. That statement is useful pedagogically because inversions and reflections are easier to see than an arbitrary coefficient quadruple. It also explains why Circle preservation and angle behavior survive composition.

Link To The Poincare Disk

\(\phi_a(z)=\dfrac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}z}\)
Disk automorphism. For \(|a|<1\), this Mobius map sends \(a\) to \(0\) and preserves the unit boundary.
It carries disk geodesics to disk geodesics and preserves the hyperbolic distance formula used earlier in the course.
This appendix is not isolated from the hyperbolic chapter. Disk-preserving Mobius transformations are precisely the coordinate changes that move points around the Poincare disk while preserving its geometry. The same algebra that sends Circles to Circles now sends boundary-orthogonal arcs to boundary-orthogonal arcs.

Summary

Mobius Maps Are The Coordinate Geometry Of \(C_\infty\)

They are projective matrix actions, preserve generalized Circles, keep cross-ratios fixed, preserve angle data in the holomorphic case, reverse orientation when conjugation is inserted, and power the conformal models used elsewhere in differential geometry.

Algebra to remember: \(z\mapsto (az+b)/(cz+d)\), scalar matrices identified, determinant nonzero.
Geometry to remember: pole behavior controls line-circle swaps, and four-point cross-ratio controls projective data.
Close by asking students to hold two views at once. Algebraically, Mobius transformations are matrix actions on homogeneous coordinates; geometrically, they are Circle-preserving conformal transformations of the extended plane. The appendix matters because it supplies a compact language for stereographic projection, inversions, and hyperbolic disk symmetries.
