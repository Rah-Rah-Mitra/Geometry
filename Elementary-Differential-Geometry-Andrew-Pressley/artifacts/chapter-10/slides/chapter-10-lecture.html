Gauss' Theorema Egregium

Gaussian curvature looks extrinsic, but the metric already knows it. This lecture follows the chain from first fundamental form to Christoffel symbols to intrinsic curvature.

Goal

Recover \(K\) from \(E,F,G\).

Tests

Compare bendings with one metric.

Open by separating the surprise from the slogan. Gaussian curvature is usually introduced by normal curvature and shape operators, so it appears to depend on how the surface sits in three-space. Intrinsic Data
First Fundamental Form

The Metric Matrix Is The Data Store

\(I = E\,du^2 + 2F\,du\,dv + G\,dv^2,\qquad g=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix}\)
What it determines
  • Lengths and angles of tangent vectors in the parameter domain.
  • The inverse matrix \(g^{-1}\), hence contraction of indices.
  • The Levi-Civita connection coefficients \(\Gamma^k_{ij}\).
What it does not determine

The surface normal and the individual second-form coefficients \(L,M,N\) may change under bendings that preserve \(I\).

Metric coefficientsSlide 3 / 16
Treat \(E,F,G\) as a coordinate expression for a measuring rule, not as three unrelated functions. Each small ellipse in the diagram represents the unit vectors according to that local rule. This framing helps students see why derivatives of the metric can describe how the coordinate basis changes.
Connection From The Metric
Levi-Civita

Christoffel Symbols Encode Intrinsic Basis Change

\( \Gamma^k_{ij}=\frac12 g^{k\ell}\left(\partial_i g_{j\ell}+\partial_j g_{i\ell}-\partial_\ell g_{ij}\right) \)
Read the formula

The right side uses only the metric matrix, its inverse, and first derivatives of the metric coefficients.

Geometric meaning

The coordinate tangent fields change as one moves; \(\Gamma\) records the tangential part of that change.

Christoffel symbols from \(g\)Slide 4 / 16
The formula is worth reading slowly because every symbol on the right is metric data. The diagram separates the visual motion of a basis from the intrinsic record of that motion. Once students accept that \(\Gamma\) is already determined by \(I\), the theorem has a plausible route.
The Egregium Mechanism
Metric to \(K\)

A Curvature Formula With No \(L,M,N\)

\( E K=(\Gamma^2_{11})_v-(\Gamma^2_{12})_u+\Gamma^1_{11}\Gamma^2_{12}+\Gamma^2_{11}\Gamma^2_{22}-\Gamma^1_{12}\Gamma^2_{11}-(\Gamma^2_{12})^2 \)
Input

First fundamental form \(I\), hence \(g\), \(g^{-1}\), and \(\Gamma^k_{ij}\).

\(E,F,G \longrightarrow \Gamma \longrightarrow K\)
Excluded from this side

The second fundamental form appears in the extrinsic formula \(K=(LN-M^2)/(EG-F^2)\), but not in the metric-only expression above.

The theorem says these two routes agree when the data comes from a genuine surface.

Theorema EgregiumSlide 5 / 16
This is the algebraic heart of the lecture, but it should not be presented as magic. The displayed equation is a compact bookkeeping result: it packages tangent-component compatibility into a curvature value. Emphasize that the absence of \(L,M,N\) is the remarkable feature.
Proof State
Gauss and Codazzi

Compatibility Is The Gatekeeper

Gauss side

Tangent components of mixed partial equality produce the intrinsic curvature formula.

Codazzi side

Normal-component bookkeeping constrains how \(L,M,N\) may vary with the same frame.

Careful statement

Compatibility does not mean the second form is determined by the first form.

Proof dependency graphSlide 6 / 16
Use this slide to prevent a common overstatement. The first form determines Gaussian curvature, not the entire second form or the whole embedding. The compatibility equations are the rules that allow the intrinsic and extrinsic computations to meet when the data actually comes from a surface.
Same Metric, Different Shape
Plane and Cylinder

Bending Without Stretching: \(du^2+dv^2\)

Plane: \(x(u,v)=(u,v,0)\)
Cylinder: \(y(u,v)=(\cos u,\sin u,v)\)
Shared first form

Both patches have \(E=1\), \(F=0\), and \(G=1\), so \(I=du^2+dv^2\).

Changed normal behavior

The cylinder bends in one direction, but its Gaussian curvature still vanishes.

Developable surface comparisonSlide 7 / 16
The plane-cylinder example is the fastest physical intuition for an isometry: rolling a sheet into a cylinder changes normals without changing distances measured on the sheet. This example also warns students not to confuse normal curvature with Gaussian curvature. One principal curvature changes, but the product remains zero.
Isometric Deformation
Catenoid and Helicoid

The Same Metric Can Look Very Different

\(I=\cosh^2u\,(du^2+dv^2)\)
Shared result

Both have \(K=-\operatorname{sech}^4u\) when computed from the shared metric.

Different embedding

The normal fields and second forms differ, so the visual bending is not the same.

Associated minimal surfacesSlide 8 / 16
This is the richer version of the plane-cylinder story. The catenoid and helicoid are visually distinct, yet their common conformal factor gives the same intrinsic curvature calculation. Encourage students to describe what changes in the picture and then name what remains fixed in the metric.
Verification Pattern
Two Routes To \(K\)

Extrinsic And Intrinsic Computations Must Agree

Extrinsic route
\(K=\dfrac{LN-M^2}{EG-F^2}\)

Uses the second fundamental form and therefore sees the chosen normal field.

Intrinsic route
\(E,F,G \rightarrow \Gamma^k_{ij} \rightarrow K\)

Uses only the first fundamental form and its derivatives.

Notebook check

For explicit patches, compute \(E,F,G,L,M,N\), compare both curvature routes, and verify that normal reversal changes \(L,M,N\) signs without changing \(K\).

Symbolic and numeric checksSlide 9 / 16
This slide turns the theorem into a laboratory procedure. The extrinsic route is still useful because it is often computationally direct for a known embedding. The theorem gives a second route and predicts equality, which is exactly the kind of identity that symbolic checks can test.
Normal Forms
Constant \(K\)

Geodesic Coordinates Reduce Curvature To An ODE

\(I=du^2+g(u)^2\,dv^2,\qquad K=-\dfrac{g''(u)}{g(u)}\)
Flat

\(g(u)=1\)

\(K=0\)

Spherical

\(g(u)=\cos u\)

\(K=1\)

Hyperbolic

\(g(u)=\cosh u\)

\(K=-1\)

Geodesic polar-style normal formsSlide 10 / 16
The ODE \(g''+Kg=0\) is a compact way to see the three basic local models. It turns curvature into the behavior of transverse spacing between geodesics. Flat spacing stays constant, positive curvature squeezes, and negative curvature expands.
Negative Curvature
Chebyshev Nets

Sine-Gordon Appears As Compatibility

\(I=du^2+2\cos\theta\,du\,dv+dv^2\)
\(II=2\sin\theta\,du\,dv,\qquad \theta_{uv}=\sin\theta\)
Interpretation

The angle between asymptotic coordinate curves becomes the unknown field.

Why it belongs here

The PDE is not imported from analysis; it is the compatibility condition for a special negative-curvature frame.

Sine-Gordon reductionSlide 11 / 16
Students may know sine-Gordon as a nonlinear PDE before seeing it in geometry. Here it arises from the same principle as the theorem: a moving frame has to be compatible when differentiated in two orders. The visual target is the crossing angle, because that angle is the field satisfying the equation.
Geodesic Mapping
Paths Versus Speed

Straight Lines Do Not Guarantee Preserved Length

\( \gamma(t)=(\cos t,0,\sin t)\)
Projection to \(z=-1\): \((- \cot t,0,-1)\)
Preserved

Unparameterized geodesic paths map to straight lines.

Not preserved

The speed becomes \(\csc^2 t\), so arclength parameter is lost.

Central projection modelSlide 12 / 16
This application distinguishes local isometries from maps that merely preserve geodesic traces. Central projection sends great circles to lines, so it behaves well at the level of unparameterized paths. The speed calculation shows exactly where it fails to be an isometry.
Concept Sorting
Avoiding Overclaims

What The Theorem Says And Does Not Say

Intrinsic consequences
  • Local isometries preserve \(K\).
  • Any formula for \(K\) can be rewritten using \(E,F,G\).
  • The metric carries second-derivative information about area distortion.
Not consequences
  • The embedding is not determined by the metric.
  • The full second fundamental form is not intrinsic.
  • A geodesic map need not preserve distances or speed.
Correct slogan: the first fundamental form determines Gaussian curvature.
Concept checkpointSlide 13 / 16
This is a useful midpoint checkpoint before practice. Many errors come from turning the theorem into a stronger rigidity claim than it actually is. Ask students to place examples from the previous slides into the two columns, especially the cylinder and helicoid examples.
Applied Lab
Change The Metric

Lab Workflow: Perturb The Data, Then Recompute

1. Choose metric data

Start with a diagonal metric such as \(du^2+g(u)^2dv^2\), or compare two embeddings with the same \(I\).

2. Compute both routes

Use Christoffel symbols for the intrinsic route and \(L,M,N\) when an explicit embedding is available.

3. Inspect invariants

Change parameters and check whether curvature agreement survives the deformation.

Notebook-centered practiceSlide 14 / 16
Frame the notebook work as an experiment in invariance rather than a symbolic exercise alone. Students should change a metric parameter and predict what must remain true before running the computation. The important habit is to compare routes and interpret disagreement as either a bug or incompatible data.
In-Class Exercise
Small Group Check

Exercise: Read Curvature From A Warped Metric

Prompt

For \(I=du^2+a(u)^2dv^2\), derive \(K=-a''/a\). Then evaluate \(a(u)=e^u\), \(a(u)=\cos u\), and \(a(u)=1+u^2\) on intervals where \(a>0\).

What to report
  • Sign of \(K\) for each choice.
  • Where the coordinate formula is valid.
  • One geometric interpretation of the spacing function \(a\).
Instructor cue

Ask which conclusions came from a drawing and which came entirely from the metric coefficients.

Formative assessmentSlide 15 / 16
This exercise gives students a manageable derivation using the same mechanism as the theorem. It also lets them practice domain awareness, because the metric coefficient must remain positive. The discussion question at the end brings the class back to the intrinsic message of the chapter.
Summary
Chapter 10

Takeaways

1

\(E,F,G\) determine the Levi-Civita connection and hence the intrinsic curvature formula.

2

Different embeddings can share a first fundamental form while changing normal data.

3

Gauss and Codazzi equations are compatibility tests, not decorative identities.

Final sentence

The metric carries more geometry than a surface drawing first suggests: it determines Gaussian curvature before any ambient normal is chosen.

\(I \longrightarrow \Gamma \longrightarrow K\)
End of lectureSlide 16 / 16
Close by returning to the opening chain. The theorem does not make extrinsic geometry irrelevant, but it identifies one major extrinsic-looking quantity as intrinsic. The examples and checks should leave students with a practical test: whenever a surface is bent without stretching, Gaussian curvature must stay fixed.
