Surface Patch

A Patch Is A Promise

A cylinder needs angle windows; the seam is coordinate bookkeeping.
Definition

A patch is a map \(\sigma: U \to \mathbb{R}^3\) from an open set \(U \subset \mathbb{R}^2\) that gives valid local coordinates on its image.

  • Domain matters: periodic formulas can stop being one-to-one.
  • The inverse coordinate view must be continuous on the image.
  • A patch describes a piece, not necessarily the whole surface.
Patch = formula + domain + local inverse behavior3 / 16
A surface patch is more than a parametrized drawing. It includes an open parameter domain, a one-to-one image on its surface piece, and enough inverse continuity to use the parameters as local coordinates. The cylinder visual is a good place to emphasize that a seam can be a coordinate decision rather than a geometric defect.
Atlas View

Surfaces Are Covered, Not Drawn Once

Working Object

A surface is understood through compatible local patches whose images cover it.

  • Sphere coordinates fail at poles unless another chart takes over.
  • Overlaps carry transition maps between parameter windows.
  • The atlas records where each coordinate description is trusted.
The overlap is where compatibility is checked.
Local data becomes surface data only after overlap checks4 / 16
Use the atlas slide to separate two ideas that students often merge: the surface is the object being covered, while charts are temporary coordinate windows. Overlaps are not an annoyance; they are the mechanism that lets local calculus agree from one window to another.
Regular Patch

Regularity Is Rank Two

\[\sigma_u(u,v) \times \sigma_v(u,v) \ne 0\]
Inspection Target

The two coordinate directions must not collapse into one line. Their cross product is a rank detector and a normal area vector at the same time.

Failure Mode

A cone vertex or a pole of a spherical formula may be a real singularity for that patch, even if nearby points are regular.

Rank two is the local plane condition5 / 16
The cross product test says that the two parameter directions must span a genuine plane in space. If the cross product vanishes, the formula may still draw a set, but it does not supply an allowable surface coordinate patch at that point.
Change Of Parameters

Reparametrization Preserves The Test

\[\tilde{\sigma} = \sigma \circ \Phi\]
\[\tilde{\sigma}_{\tilde{u}} \times \tilde{\sigma}_{\tilde{v}} = \det(D\Phi)\,(\sigma_u \times \sigma_v)\]
  • \(\det(D\Phi) \ne 0\) preserves regularity.
  • \(\det(D\Phi) > 0\) preserves the chosen normal direction.
  • \(\det(D\Phi) < 0\) reverses orientation.
Regularity is coordinate-invariant6 / 16
This is the first major invariance calculation of the chapter. A coordinate change multiplies the old normal area vector by the determinant of its Jacobian, so nonzero determinants preserve regularity. The sign of the determinant already foreshadows orientation.
Maps Between Surfaces

Smooth Maps Are Tested In Charts

Coordinate Test

A map \(f:S_1 \to S_2\) is smooth near \(p\) when \(\tau^{-1} \circ f \circ \sigma\) is a smooth map between open subsets of \(\mathbb{R}^2\).

  • Charts translate surface questions into multivariable calculus.
  • The test is local; choose charts around the point and its image.
  • Compatibility makes the answer independent of chart choice.
Smoothness lives in coordinate representatives7 / 16
A map between surfaces is smooth when its coordinate representative is smooth on compatible chart domains. The cylinder wrapping example is useful because it is locally well behaved but globally many-to-one, so smoothness and global injectivity should not be conflated.
Diffeomorphism Warning

Local Diffeomorphism Is Not Global Diffeomorphism

Common Mistake

Nonzero Jacobian in coordinates gives local invertibility. It does not automatically make the whole map one-to-one.

\[(0,y,z) \mapsto (\cos y,\sin y,z)\]
  • Small angle intervals unwrap smoothly.
  • Angles differing by \(2\pi\) land at the same point.
  • Global topology remains visible after local calculus succeeds.
Local coordinates do not erase global identification8 / 16
Pause on the distinction between local invertibility and global one-to-one behavior. A full turn in the angle coordinate returns to the same point on the cylinder, even though every small angular interval has a smooth inverse chart. This prepares students for later manifold examples where local information is powerful but incomplete.
Tangent Plane

Tangent Planes From Parameter Curves

\[\gamma(t)=\sigma(u(t),v(t))\]
\[\gamma'(0)=u'(0)\sigma_u+v'(0)\sigma_v\]
Meaning

All surface curve velocities through \(p\) lie in the span of the two parameter-direction velocities at \(p\).

Tangent plane = span of coordinate curve velocities9 / 16
The tangent plane is built from velocities of curves that lie on the surface. In a patch, the coordinate curves give two distinguished tangent directions, and all other surface curve velocities are their linear combinations by the chain rule.
Derivative

Derivatives Push Tangent Velocities

Tangent Map

For a smooth \(f:S_1\to S_2\), the derivative \(D_pf\) sends a tangent velocity \(\gamma'(0)\) to \((f\circ\gamma)'(0)\).

  1. Choose a curve on \(S_1\) with velocity \(X\).
  2. Compose with \(f\) to get a curve on \(S_2\).
  3. Different curves with the same velocity give the same pushed velocity.
Coordinate matrices represent a geometric linear map10 / 16
The derivative of a smooth surface map should be read as a linear map between tangent planes, not merely as a matrix floating between coordinate grids. Coordinates compute it, but the chain rule is what proves the result is independent of the chosen curve and chart description.
Normal Field

Standard Unit Normals

\[N_\sigma=\frac{\sigma_u\times\sigma_v}{\|\sigma_u\times\sigma_v\|}\]
Sphere Patch Lesson

The standard normal is determined by the order of the patch parameters. For common latitude-longitude choices, it can point inward on the chosen domain.

  • Unit length: \(\|N_\sigma\|=1\).
  • Orthogonality: \(N_\sigma\cdot\sigma_u=N_\sigma\cdot\sigma_v=0\).
Normals remember the orientation of the parameter grid11 / 16
Normal vectors come from the ordered pair of parameter directions, so switching chart orientation can reverse the sign. The sphere example is a useful reminder that a standard normal for a patch need not be the outward normal that geometric intuition first expects.
Signed Tangent Geometry

Oriented Angle Needs A Normal

\[\operatorname{angle}_N(v,w)=\operatorname{atan2}\!\big(N\cdot(v\times w),\,v\cdot w\big)\]
Sign Rule

Changing \(N\) to \(-N\) reverses the sign of every oriented angle in the tangent plane.

Signed angles are local orientation data12 / 16
The sign of an angle in a tangent plane depends on the chosen unit normal. With the opposite normal, the same ordered pair of tangent vectors receives the opposite signed angle. This is the local algebraic reason orientability matters.
Orientability

Orientability Means Normals Glue

Atlas Criterion

Choose compatible patches so every transition determinant is positive on overlaps.

Normal Field Criterion

Equivalently, choose a continuous unit normal over the entire surface.

Subtle Point

Local normals are easy. The problem is making all local choices agree after moving around the surface.

Positive transition determinants let normal choices agree13 / 16
Orientability is not the existence of a normal at each individual point; local normals always exist for a regular patch. The point is whether chart choices can be arranged so transition determinants stay positive and the local normal choices agree globally.
Nonorientable Surface

The Mobius Band Obstruction

Obstruction

After one circuit around the median circle, a transported normal comes back as its negative.

\[N_{\mathrm{end}}\cdot N_{\mathrm{start}}=-1\]
  • Every small patch is regular.
  • The global sign choice cannot be made consistently.
  • Negative transition determinant branches reveal the twist.
Local regularity does not imply global orientability14 / 16
The Mobius band is the chapter's clean obstruction example because every small piece is a regular surface patch, yet one circuit reverses a transported normal. Ask students to inspect the end of the trip: the base point returns, but the normal direction does not.
Applied Lab

Patch Doctor Checklist

1. Declare The Domain

Open set, excluded seams, and intended image piece.

2. Differentiate The Formula

Compute \(\sigma_u\), \(\sigma_v\), and \(\sigma_u\times\sigma_v\).

3. Diagnose The Minimum

Zeros identify collapse; small numeric values demand exact analysis.

4. Classify The Exclusion

Coordinate seam, patch boundary, or real singularity?

Read cross-product norm as a regularity meter, then verify symbolically where it matters.
A reliable parametrization passes domain, rank, and overlap checks15 / 16
This slide turns the chapter into a diagnostic workflow. Before trusting a parametrization, identify the domain, compute the two partial derivatives, inspect the cross product, and decide whether excluded points are coordinate seams or actual singularities.
Recap

What Survives A Change Of Patch

  • A surface is an atlas of local coordinate patches, not one universal formula.
  • Regularity is the invariant condition \(\sigma_u\times\sigma_v\ne0\).
  • Tangent planes and derivatives are computed in charts but belong to the surface.
  • Normals require consistent sign choices; orientability is the global test.
Exit Question

Given a new parametrized surface, where could the formula fail: domain, injectivity, regularity, smooth overlap, or orientation?
