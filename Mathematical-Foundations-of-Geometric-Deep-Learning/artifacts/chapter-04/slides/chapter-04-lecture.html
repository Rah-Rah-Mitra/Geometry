Charts and atlasesChapter 04

Manifolds

Definition

A space locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$, with smoothly compatible coordinate charts forming an atlas.

Transition maps

$\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$ smooth on overlaps

Examples in GDL

  • Sphere $S^2$ and tori
  • Rotation group $SO(3)$
  • Mesh-approximated surfaces

Charts make calculus coordinate-independent, so intrinsic operations do not depend on an embedding.

PDF pages 37-50Manifolds
Define a smooth manifold as a space locally homeomorphic to Euclidean space with smoothly compatible charts. Atlases and transition maps make calculus coordinate-independent. Examples relevant to GDL include spheres, the rotation group as a manifold, and surfaces approximated by meshes.
Local linear structureChapter 04

Tangent Spaces

Tangent vector

$v=\dot\gamma(0),\quad \gamma(0)=p$

The tangent space $T_pM$ collects velocities of all curves through $p$; it is a vector space of the manifold's dimension.

Differential

$df_p: T_pM\to T_{f(p)}N$

Why it matters

Gradients and feature directions live in tangent spaces once the domain is curved.

PDF pages 37-50Tangent Spaces
Introduce the tangent space at a point as the vector space of velocities of curves through that point. The differential of a smooth map acts linearly between tangent spaces. Tangent vectors are where gradients and feature directions live once the domain is curved rather than flat.
Length and geodesicsChapter 04

Riemannian Metrics

Metric

$g_p(\cdot,\cdot): T_pM\times T_pM\to\mathbb{R}$

Length

$L(\gamma)=\int_a^b\sqrt{g_{\gamma}(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)}\,dt$

Geodesics

Locally length-minimizing curves; the manifold's straight lines and the basis of geodesic distance.

The metric also gives angles and the volume element for integration.

PDF pages 37-50Riemannian Metrics
Add a Riemannian metric: a smoothly varying inner product on each tangent space. It defines lengths, angles, geodesics, and the volume element. Geodesics generalize straight lines and give the shortest-path distance used to compare points on curved data domains.
Departure from flatnessChapter 04

Curvature

Riemann tensor

$R(X,Y)Z$ measures non-commuting transport

Sectional and Ricci curvature summarize how geodesics spread or converge.

Why it matters

Curvature affects distances, embeddings, and the stability of intrinsic networks; negatively curved spaces, for instance, embed trees efficiently.

PDF pages 37-50Curvature
Discuss curvature as the obstruction to a manifold being flat, captured by the Riemann tensor and summarized by sectional and Ricci curvature. Curvature controls how geodesics spread or converge and influences the design and stability of networks operating intrinsically on manifolds.
