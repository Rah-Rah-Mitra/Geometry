DiagonalizationChapter 06

Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions

Eigen-equation

$T e_n=\lambda_n e_n$

Spectral theorem

$T=\sum_n\lambda_n\langle\cdot,e_n\rangle e_n$

Self-adjoint operators have a complete orthonormal eigenbasis with real eigenvalues.

Payoff

Operator equations decouple into independent scalar equations along eigen-directions.

PDF pages 55-64Eigenvalues
Recall eigenvalues and eigenfunctions and the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators: a complete orthonormal eigenbasis with real eigenvalues. Diagonalization expresses the operator as scaling along eigen-directions, turning hard operator equations into independent scalar equations that are far easier to solve.
Connectivity operatorChapter 06

The Graph Laplacian

Laplacian

$L=D-A$

Normalized

$\mathcal{L}=I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$

Properties

  • Symmetric, positive semidefinite
  • Non-negative eigenvalues
  • Orthogonal eigenvectors

It encodes connectivity and smoothness, the central operator of spectral graph theory.

PDF pages 55-64Graph Laplacian
Define the graph Laplacian as degree minus adjacency, and its normalized variants. It is symmetric positive semidefinite, so its eigenvalues are non-negative and eigenvectors orthogonal. The Laplacian encodes connectivity and smoothness and is the central operator of spectral graph theory.
Frequency on graphsChapter 06

Fourier as Eigenbasis

Graph Fourier transform

$\hat f=U^\top f,\quad L=U\Lambda U^\top$

Classical Fourier modes are Laplacian eigenfunctions on regular domains.

Generalization

On graphs, Laplacian eigenvectors define frequency, extending Fourier analysis to irregular structure.

PDF pages 55-64Fourier Eigenbasis
Reframe the Fourier transform as the eigenbasis of the Laplacian: classical Fourier modes are Laplacian eigenfunctions on regular domains. On graphs, the Laplacian eigenvectors define the graph Fourier transform, generalizing the notion of frequency to arbitrary irregular structure.
Filtering signalsChapter 06

Spectral Convolution

Spectral filter

$g\star f=U\,g(\Lambda)\,U^\top f$

A filter scales each eigen-component, then transforms back to the vertex domain.

Spectral GNNs

This is the principle behind spectral graph networks.

Efficiency

Polynomial filters localize the operation and avoid full eigendecomposition.

PDF pages 55-64Spectral Convolution
Define convolution as multiplication in the spectral domain. A spectral filter scales each Laplacian eigen-component, then transforms back. This is the principle behind spectral graph neural networks; polynomial filters make it local and efficient, avoiding full eigendecomposition.
