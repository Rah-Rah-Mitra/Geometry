DistanceChapter 02

Metric Spaces

Axioms

$d(x,y)\ge 0,\; d(x,y)=0\iff x=y$

$d(x,y)=d(y,x)$

$d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$

A metric turns a set into a space where nearness is quantitative and open balls define which functions are continuous.

Examples in GDL

  • Point clouds with Euclidean distance
  • Graphs with shortest-path distance
  • Manifolds with geodesic distance

Why it matters

The data domain's metric controls neighborhoods, and neighborhoods control which local operations (convolutions, message passing) are well-defined.

PDF pages 20-25Metric Spaces
Define a metric space as a set with a distance function satisfying non-negativity, symmetry, identity of indiscernibles, and the triangle inequality. Open balls generate a topology. Emphasize that data domains in GDL are frequently metric: point clouds, graphs with shortest-path distance, and manifolds with geodesic distance all fit this frame.
Continuity without distanceChapter 02

Topology

A topology

A collection of open sets closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections, containing the empty set and the whole space.

Continuity

$f$ continuous $\iff f^{-1}(U)$ open for every open $U$

Homeomorphism

A continuous bijection with continuous inverse: the topological notion of sameness, blind to stretching and bending.

Topology lets symmetry groups act on spaces that lack a canonical distance, so continuity survives where metrics do not.

PDF pages 20-25Topology
Introduce topological structure abstractly: open sets closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections. Continuity is preimages of open sets being open. Homeomorphism is the topological notion of sameness. This abstraction lets us speak of continuity without a metric, which matters when symmetry groups act on spaces without a canonical distance.
Length and angleChapter 02

Normed and Inner-Product Spaces

Norm

$\|x\|\ge0,\;\|\alpha x\|=|\alpha|\,\|x\|,\;\|x+y\|\le\|x\|+\|y\|$

Inner product

$\langle x,y\rangle,\quad \|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}$

Completeness

Complete normed space is Banach; complete inner-product space is Hilbert.

Why Hilbert spaces

Feature maps, kernels, and spectral decompositions all live in inner-product geometry, making orthogonality and projection available.

PDF pages 20-25Normed & Inner-Product
Cover normed vector spaces and inner-product spaces. A norm measures length; an inner product additionally encodes angle and orthogonality. Completeness gives Banach and Hilbert spaces. Hilbert spaces are central to GDL because feature spaces, kernels, and spectral decompositions all live in inner-product geometry.
AnalysisChapter 02

Convergence and Continuity

Convergence

A sequence converges if it eventually stays within every ball about its limit. Cauchy sequences cluster; completeness guarantees they converge.

Lipschitz

$d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le L\,d_X(x,x')$

Stability

The Lipschitz constant $L$ bounds how much a map stretches distance, controlling robustness of networks to perturbed inputs.

Uniform continuity strengthens continuity to a single modulus across the whole domain, important for approximation guarantees.
