Groups: Symmetry as Algebra

Axioms

Associative; identity \(e\); inverses \(g^{-1}\).

Examples for GDL

\(\mathrm{SO}(d)\), \(\mathrm{O}(d)\), \(S_n\), \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), gauge groups.

Tools

Subgroups, cosets, homomorphisms, quotient groups.

Reading

Choose the group; the architecture's invariance/equivariance follows.

The group is the symmetryChoice of group = design choice
Define a group as a set with an associative binary operation, identity, and inverses. Emphasize examples relevant to GDL: rotation groups SO(d), permutation groups S_n, translation groups, and gauge groups. Subgroups, cosets, and homomorphisms are the structural tools used throughout the chapter.

Rings, Fields, and Modules

Ring

Set with compatible \(+\) and \(\cdot\); not every nonzero element invertible.

Field

Commutative ring with all nonzero elements invertible: \(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}\), finite fields.

Module

Ring-generalization of vector space; coefficients live in a ring instead of a field.

Use in GDL

Tensors over fields; representation theory uses modules over group rings.

Scalars + addition + multiplicationVector spaces live over fields
Cover rings and fields. A ring has addition and multiplication compatible by distributivity; a field is a commutative ring where every nonzero element is invertible. The reals and complex numbers are the standard fields in GDL; finite fields appear in some discrete contexts. Modules over rings generalize vector spaces.
Section 3
G ↔ X

Group Actions: How Symmetry Touches Data

Action

\(\rho: G \to \mathrm{Sym}(X)\), homomorphism into the symmetries of \(X\).

Orbit-stabilizer

\(|G \cdot x| = |G| / |\mathrm{Stab}(x)|\)

Examples

SO(3) on \(\mathbb{R}^3\); \(S_n\) on graph node sets; translation on signals.

Homogeneous spaces

\(X = G/H\) when the action is transitive; the canonical setting for many GDL constructions.

Action is the data-side counterpart of the groupOrbit-stabilizer is the cardinality identity
Introduce group actions. An action of G on X is a homomorphism from G to the symmetries of X. Orbits, stabilizers, and orbit-stabilizer theorem organize the action. In GDL, the data domain X carries a G-action and the learned functions are required to be equivariant under this action.
Section 4
architectural device

Equivariance and Invariance

Equivariant

\(f(g \cdot x) = g \cdot f(x)\)

Invariant

\(f(g \cdot x) = f(x)\)

Layered design

Stack equivariant layers; cap with an invariant pooling to predict graph- or shape-level outputs.

Examples

CNNs: translation-equivariant. GNNs: permutation-equivariant. SE(3)-equivariant networks for molecules.

Equivariance is the architectural constraintInvariance is its terminal special case
Define equivariance. A function f from X to Y between G-spaces is equivariant if f composed with the action on X equals the action on Y composed with f. Invariance is the special case where the Y-action is trivial. Equivariant networks are the principal architectural device in geometric deep learning.
