Printed pp. 35-54 · PDF 33-52Smooth Plane Cubics
Introduce the nonsingular plane cubic and its Weierstrass normal form, obtained by projective coordinate changes. Unlike a conic, a smooth cubic is not rational: it cannot be parametrized by the line. This failure of rationality is the first sign that cubics have genuinely richer geometry and demands a new organizing idea, the group law.
Geometric additionChapter 02

The Chord-and-Tangent Law

Collinearity

$P+Q+R=O \iff P,Q,R\ \text{collinear}$

A line meets the cubic in three points by Bezout's theorem.

Doubling

Tangent lines define $P+P$ when two points coincide.

Result

A well-defined commutative operation on the curve.

Printed pp. 35-54 · PDF 33-52The Chord-and-Tangent Law
Define addition geometrically. A line meets the cubic in three points by Bezout, and the chord-and-tangent rule turns this into a binary operation: collinear points sum to a fixed identity. Tangent lines handle doubling. The construction is entirely geometric, yet it produces a well-defined commutative operation on the points of the curve.
The hard axiomChapter 02

Associativity

Goal

$(P+Q)+R=P+(Q+R)$

Commutativity and inverses are easy; associativity is not.

Nine-point property

Two cubics through eight common points share the ninth.

Heart of the chapter

This synthetic argument is the conceptual high point.

Printed pp. 35-54 · PDF 33-52Associativity
Address the one hard axiom. Commutativity and inverses are easy, but associativity is not obvious from the construction. Prove it using the fact that two cubics through eight common points share the ninth, a consequence of counting intersections. This synthetic argument is the conceptual heart of the chapter and the deepest point in this part.

Printed pp. 35-54 · PDF 33-52Consequences
Explore what the group buys us. Over the rationals the points form a finitely generated abelian group by the Mordell theorem, the source of deep arithmetic. Torsion points and flexes appear as special elements. The same curves underpin modern elliptic-curve cryptography, showing how this classical geometry reaches into computation.
