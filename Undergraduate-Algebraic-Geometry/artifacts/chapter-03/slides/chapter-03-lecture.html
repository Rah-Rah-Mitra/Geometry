Algebraic Sets

Definition

The common zero locus of a family of polynomials in affine space.

Finiteness

By the Hilbert basis theorem, finitely many polynomials always suffice to define any algebraic set.

Vanishing set

$V(S)=\{p\in\mathbb{A}^n : f(p)=0\ \forall f\in S\}$

Ideals

Every algebraic set arises from an ideal, and enlarging $S$ to the ideal it generates leaves the zero set unchanged.

Printed pp. 57-72 · PDF 55-70Algebraic Sets
Define an affine algebraic set as the common zero locus of a collection of polynomials in affine space. Every ideal of the polynomial ring determines such a set, and by the Hilbert basis theorem finitely many polynomials always suffice. These sets are the geometric objects whose algebraic shadows we will study throughout the rest of the course.

The Zariski Topology

Closed sets

Declare the algebraic sets to be the closed sets; this defines a topology on affine space.

Coarse and non-Hausdorff

Open sets are huge and points are not separated, yet the topology fits polynomial geometry perfectly.

Irreducibility

A variety is an irreducible closed set; it cannot split into two smaller closed pieces.

Algebraic mirror

$X\ \text{irreducible}\iff I(X)\ \text{prime}$

Printed pp. 57-72 · PDF 55-70The Zariski Topology
Equip affine space with the Zariski topology, whose closed sets are exactly the algebraic sets. This topology is coarse and far from Hausdorff, yet it is perfectly suited to polynomial geometry. Irreducible closed sets are the varieties proper, and irreducibility corresponds algebraically to the defining ideal being prime.

The Nullstellensatz

Hilbert's theorem

Over an algebraically closed field, the only obstruction to vanishing is the radical.

Points

Maximal ideals of the polynomial ring correspond exactly to points of affine space.

Strong form

$I(V(J))=\sqrt{J}$

Why it matters

This is the keystone that turns the loose analogy between equations and shapes into an exact equivalence.

Printed pp. 57-72 · PDF 55-70The Nullstellensatz
State Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, the foundational theorem of the subject. Over an algebraically closed field the radical of an ideal equals the ideal of all polynomials vanishing on its zero set. This pins down an exact correspondence between radical ideals and algebraic sets, and between maximal ideals and points of affine space.

The Dictionary

Bijection

Algebraic sets correspond, order-reversing, to radical ideals; varieties to prime ideals; points to maximal ideals.

Coordinate ring

The quotient ring becomes the ring of regular functions on the variety.

Functions

$k[X]=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I(X)$

Strategy

Geometric questions translate faithfully into commutative algebra, the recurring method of the course.

Printed pp. 57-72 · PDF 55-70The Dictionary
Read off the consequences. The Nullstellensatz gives an order-reversing bijection between algebraic sets and radical ideals, sending varieties to prime ideals and points to maximal ideals. The coordinate ring becomes the ring of regular functions, and geometric questions translate faithfully into commutative algebra, the strategy that organizes everything to follow.
