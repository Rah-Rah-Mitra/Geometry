Polynomials and Their Zero Sets

Zero set

$V(f)=\{x:f(x)=0\}$

Lines, conics, and surfaces all arise as zero sets of polynomials.

Two sides of a coin

The geometry of the set and the algebra of its defining polynomials are inseparable.

Plan

We study both together throughout the course.

Printed pp. 11-18 · PDF 9-16Polynomials and Their Zero Sets
Start from the basic object: a system of polynomial equations and the set of points where they vanish. Lines, conics, and surfaces are all zero sets of polynomials. The geometry of these sets and the algebra of the polynomials defining them are two sides of one coin, and the course studies them together.

Strategy

Solve geometry with algebra; read algebra geometrically.

Printed pp. 11-18 · PDF 9-16The Algebra-Geometry Dictionary
Preview the central dictionary: geometric objects correspond to algebraic ones. Varieties match ideals, points match maximal ideals, and maps between varieties match homomorphisms of coordinate rings. This translation lets us solve geometric problems with algebra and interpret algebraic facts geometrically, the recurring strategy of the entire book.
