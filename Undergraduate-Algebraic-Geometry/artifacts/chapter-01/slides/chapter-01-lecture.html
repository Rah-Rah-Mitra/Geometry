Printed pp. 19-34 · PDF 17-32Quadratic Equations
Define a plane conic as the zero set of a degree-two polynomial in two variables. The general conic has six coefficients, so conics form a five-dimensional projective family. Degenerate cases, where the quadratic factors, give pairs of lines, and we keep careful track of when a conic is irreducible versus reducible.
Linear algebraChapter 01

Matrix and Discriminant

Conic matrix

$\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T} M\,\mathbf{x}=0,\quad M=M^{\mathsf T}$

A symmetric three-by-three matrix in homogeneous coordinates.

Determinant

$\det M=0$ detects degeneracy; rank distinguishes the cases.

Payoff

Conic questions become symmetric-matrix questions.

Printed pp. 19-34 · PDF 17-32Matrix and Discriminant
Encode a conic by a symmetric three-by-three matrix in homogeneous coordinates. The determinant of this matrix detects degeneracy, and its rank distinguishes the cases. This linear-algebra packaging turns questions about conics into questions about symmetric matrices, where the spectral and congruence theory does the classifying work for us.

Projective Classification

Smooth normal form

Over an algebraically closed field, every smooth conic is one type.

Over the reals

A sign invariant separates real ellipse-type from empty conics.

Few types

The short list is why conics are so tractable.

Printed pp. 19-34 · PDF 17-32Projective Classification
Classify conics up to projective change of coordinates. Over an algebraically closed field every smooth conic is projectively equivalent to a single normal form, while over the reals a sign invariant separates ellipse-type from empty conics. The number of distinct types is small, which is exactly why conics are so tractable.

Parametrization

Printed pp. 19-34 · PDF 17-32Rational Parametrization
Show that a smooth conic with one rational point is rational: lines through that point meet the conic in one further point, giving a one-to-one parametrization by the projective line. This is the conic version of the chord construction and previews how rationality questions dominate the study of higher-degree curves.
