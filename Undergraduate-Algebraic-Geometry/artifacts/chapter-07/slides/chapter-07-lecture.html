The Count of Twenty-Seven

Cayley-Salmon

A smooth cubic surface contains exactly twenty-seven lines, no more and no fewer.

Universal

The same number for every smooth cubic, a sharp algebraic count.

The number

$27=6+15+6$

Subtlety

Both existence and exactness need real argument, not parameter counting alone.

State the theorem of Cayley and Salmon: a smooth cubic surface contains exactly twenty-seven lines, no more and no fewer. The Blow-up Model

The realization

A smooth cubic surface is the plane blown up at six points in general position.

The accounting

Six exceptional curves, fifteen joining lines, six conics through five points.

Decomposition

$6+\binom{6}{2}+6=6+15+6=27$

Elegance

The model explains the count exactly and constructs every line.

Printed pp. 109-120 · PDF 107-118The Blow-up Model
Explain why the lines exist. A smooth cubic surface is the blow-up of the projective plane at six points in general position. The twenty-seven lines arise as the six exceptional curves, the fifteen lines joining pairs of the points, and the six conics through five of them, accounting for the count exactly through this elegant model.
