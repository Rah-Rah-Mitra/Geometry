Projective Space

Two views

Lines through the origin, or affine space plus a hyperplane at infinity.

Homogeneity

Coordinates are defined only up to scaling, so only homogeneous polynomials vanish well.

Coordinates

$[x_0:x_1:\dots:x_n]=[\lambda x_0:\dots:\lambda x_n]$

Payoff

Parallel lines now meet, and Bezout's theorem holds with no exceptions.

Printed pp. 87-100 · PDF 85-98Projective Space
Construct projective space as lines through the origin, or equivalently as affine space completed by a hyperplane at infinity. Homogeneous coordinates are defined only up to scaling, so only homogeneous polynomials have well-defined zero sets. This completion is what makes intersection theory uniform: parallel lines now meet, and Bezout's theorem holds without exceptions.

Projective Varieties

Definition

Zero sets of homogeneous ideals in projective space.

Affine charts

Covered by standard affine pieces, so local questions reduce to the affine theory.

Why projective

The natural compact arena where degree and intersection behave well.

Bezout

$\#(C\cap D)=\deg C\cdot\deg D$

Printed pp. 87-100 · PDF 85-98Projective Varieties
Define projective varieties as zero sets of homogeneous ideals in projective space. They are covered by affine pieces, the standard charts, so local questions reduce to the affine theory already developed. Projective varieties are the natural compact arena for algebraic geometry, where degree and intersection behave far better than in the affine world.

Rational and Birational Maps

Rational maps

Given by ratios of homogeneous polynomials, away from a locus where they fail.

Birational

A rational map with a rational inverse; the varieties agree on dense opens.

Equivalence

$X\dashrightarrow Y,\quad Y\dashrightarrow X$

Central problem

Birational classification is coarser than isomorphism and drives higher geometry.

Printed pp. 87-100 · PDF 85-98Rational and Birational Maps
Introduce rational maps, defined by ratios of homogeneous polynomials away from a locus where they break down. A birational map has a rational inverse, making two varieties birationally equivalent: isomorphic on dense open sets. Birational classification is coarser than isomorphism and is the central organizing problem of higher algebraic geometry.
Undergraduate Algebraic GeometryChapter 05

Blow-ups

The construction

Replace a point by the projective space of directions through it.

Effect

Separates branches and turns many rational maps into genuine morphisms.

Printed pp. 87-100 · PDF 85-98Blow-ups
Resolve the indeterminacy of rational maps with the blow-up, which replaces a point by the projective space of directions through it. Blowing up separates branches and turns many rational maps into genuine morphisms. This single construction underlies resolution of singularities and the birational surgery that classifies surfaces and beyond.
