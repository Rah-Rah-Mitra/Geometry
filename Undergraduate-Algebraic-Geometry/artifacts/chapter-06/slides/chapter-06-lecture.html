The Tangent Space

Definition

The linear space cut out by the differentials of the defining equations at the point.

Computation

Built directly from the Jacobian matrix, no limits required.

Tangent space

$T_pX=\ker\big(Df(p)\big)$

Meaning

It records the first-order behavior of the variety near the point.

Printed pp. 101-108 · PDF 99-106The Tangent Space
Define the Zariski tangent space at a point as the linear space cut out by the differentials of the defining equations. This is the algebraic version of the tangent plane from calculus, computed purely with the Jacobian matrix. It measures the first-order behavior of the variety near the point without any reference to limits.

Nonsingular Points

Smoothness

The tangent space has the smallest possible dimension, equal to that of the variety.

Full rank

Equivalently the Jacobian attains its maximal rank at the point.

Singularities

Where the tangent space jumps up: nodes, cusps, and other failures of local flatness.

Local model

At a smooth point the variety looks locally like affine space.

Printed pp. 101-108 · PDF 99-106Nonsingular Points
Call a point nonsingular, or smooth, when the tangent space has the smallest possible dimension, equal to the dimension of the variety. Equivalently the Jacobian attains full rank there. Singular points, where the tangent space jumps up, are exactly the places where the variety fails to be locally like affine space, such as cusps and nodes.

Dimension

Algebraic definition

The length of the longest chain of irreducible subvarieties.

Function field

Equal to the transcendence degree of the field of rational functions.

Chain

$X_0\subsetneq X_1\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq X_d$

Intuition

A curve is one-dimensional, a surface two; dimension is a birational invariant.

Printed pp. 101-108 · PDF 99-106Dimension
Define the dimension of a variety algebraically, as the length of the longest chain of irreducible subvarieties, matching the transcendence degree of its function field. For nice varieties this agrees with intuition: a curve is one-dimensional, a surface two. Dimension is a birational invariant and the most basic numerical measure of size.

Smoothness and the Jacobian

Criterion

A point is smooth exactly when the Jacobian rank equals the codimension.

Singular locus

A closed subvariety cut out by the vanishing of the relevant minors.

Smoothness

$\dim T_pX=\dim X$

Genericity

For an irreducible variety the singular locus is generically empty.

Printed pp. 101-108 · PDF 99-106Smoothness and the Jacobian
State the smoothness criterion. A point is nonsingular precisely when the rank of the Jacobian equals the codimension, so the tangent space dimension equals the dimension of the variety. The singular locus is itself a closed subvariety, cut out by the vanishing of the relevant minors, and is generically empty for an irreducible variety.
