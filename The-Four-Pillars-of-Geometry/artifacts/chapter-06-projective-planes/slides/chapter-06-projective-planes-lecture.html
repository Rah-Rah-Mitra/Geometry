Configuration theorem

Pappus’ Theorem: Commutativity from Collinearity

Statement

If \(A,B,C\) are collinear and \(D,E,F\) are collinear, then the three cross-join intersections \(AE \cap BD\), \(AF \cap CD\), \(BF \cap CE\) are collinear.

Algebraic Meaning

Pappus holds in a projective plane if and only if the coordinate system is a commutative field. Failure of Pappus means the multiplication is non-commutative.

\(\text{Pappus} \iff \text{commutative field}\)
Visible concept: collinearity of cross-joins tests commutativity.Inspection target: trace each cross-join to its intersection.
Present Pappus theorem: if points A B C are collinear and D E F are collinear, then the three cross-join intersection points are also collinear. This theorem holds in every projective plane coordinatized by a commutative field and fails in planes over non-commutative division rings.
Perspectivity

Desargues’ Theorem: Perspective from Point and Line

Statement

Two triangles are in perspective from a point (corresponding vertices joined by concurrent lines) if and only if they are in perspective from a line (corresponding sides meet in collinear points).

Algebraic Meaning

Desargues holds if and only if the plane can be coordinatized by a division ring. In three dimensions, Desargues is a theorem; in two dimensions, it is an independent axiom.

Implications

  • Desargues + Pappus ⇒ commutative field.
  • Desargues alone ⇒ division ring (possibly non-commutative).
  • Neither ⇒ exotic planes with no algebraic coordinates.
\(\text{Desargues} \iff \text{division ring}\)
Visible concept: point-perspectivity implies line-perspectivity.Inspection target: trace concurrent lines and collinear intersections.
Present Desargues theorem: if two triangles are in perspective from a point, then they are in perspective from a line and conversely. This theorem holds in every projective plane that can be embedded in three-dimensional projective space, and it is equivalent to coordinatizability by a division ring.
Counterexample

The Moulton Plane: Where Desargues Fails

Construction

Keep all lines with non-negative slope. For lines with negative slope crossing the \(y\)-axis, halve the slope on the right side. Lines become “bent” at the \(y\)-axis.

Incidence Holds

Any two distinct points still determine a unique line, and any two distinct lines still meet in exactly one point. The incidence axioms are satisfied.

Desargues Fails

A specific Desargues configuration can be found where the three axis points are no longer collinear. This proves Desargues is independent of the incidence axioms in dimension two.

incidence axioms \(\not\Rightarrow\) Desargues
Visible concept: bent lines preserve incidence but break perspectivity.Inspection target: find the non-collinear axis points.
Show the Moulton plane as a concrete counterexample. In the Moulton plane, lines with negative slope that cross the y-axis are bent at the axis. The incidence axioms still hold, but the Desargues configuration fails because the three intersection points are no longer collinear. This proves that Desargues theorem is independent of the incidence axioms.
Algebra from geometry

Recovering Addition and Multiplication from Incidence

Addition

Choose a line at infinity, a unit point, and an origin. Use parallel lines (through the point at infinity) to translate segments. The construction defines \(a + b\) purely from incidence.

Multiplication

Use a similar-triangle configuration with a unit segment and a point at infinity. The projection from one line to another through a center gives \(a \cdot b\) as a segment length.

Result

If Pappus holds, these operations satisfy all field axioms: associativity, commutativity, distributivity, and existence of inverses. The geometry determines the algebra.

Key Takeaway

Geometry and algebra are two views of the same structure. Configuration theorems like Pappus and Desargues determine the algebraic properties of the coordinate system, and vice versa.
