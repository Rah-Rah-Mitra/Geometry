What Is an Isometry

Distance preserving

Every pairwise distance is unchanged.

Orthogonal part

The linear map satisfies $A^\top A = I$.

Affine form

$x \mapsto A\,x + t,\qquad \det A = \pm 1$

Two signs

The determinant sign splits all motions.

Printed pp. 39-49What Is an Isometry
An isometry is any transformation preserving every distance between points. We write it as an orthogonal matrix acting on coordinates followed by a translation vector. Because the linear part is orthogonal its determinant is plus or minus one, and that single sign organizes the whole classification we develop.

Direct versus Opposite

Direct, $\det A = +1$

Sense preserved: rotations, translations.

Opposite, $\det A = -1$

Sense reversed: reflections, glides.

Signed-area test

$\operatorname{sign}\,[\,T'\,] = \det A \cdot \operatorname{sign}\,[\,T\,]$

Visible check

A directed triangle flips when $\det A = -1$.

Printed pp. 39-49Direct versus Opposite
Direct isometries preserve sense and have determinant plus one; opposite isometries reverse sense and have determinant minus one. A directed triangle exposes the difference instantly: its signed area keeps sign under direct motions and flips under opposite ones, matching the determinant exactly.

Reflection as the Building Block

Mirror line

The fixed set is an entire line.

Universality

Every isometry is a product of $\le 3$ reflections.

Printed pp. 39-49Reflection as the Building Block
A reflection is the simplest opposite isometry, fixing an entire mirror line and reversing sense. With unit mirror direction u the linear part is two u u transpose minus the identity. Every isometry of the plane factors into at most three such reflections, the chapter's central structural theorem.

Two Reflections in Intersecting Mirrors

Composition rule

$R_{m_2}\,R_{m_1} = \mathrm{Rot}\,(O,\,2\theta)$

Result

Direct motion; one fixed point at $O$.

Printed pp. 39-49Two Reflections in Intersecting Mirrors
Reflecting in one mirror then a second that crosses it at angle theta produces a rotation about the intersection point through twice theta. The composite is direct, fixes only the crossing point, and the doubling of the angle is the chapter's most memorable composition rule.

Two Reflections in Parallel Mirrors

Composition rule

$R_{m_2}\,R_{m_1} = \mathrm{Trans}\,(2d\,\hat n)$

Result

Direct motion; no fixed point.

Printed pp. 39-49Two Reflections in Parallel Mirrors
When the two mirrors are parallel and separated by distance d, the product of the reflections is a translation perpendicular to them through twice d. There is no fixed point, the motion is direct, and sliding the mirror pair leaves the same translation unchanged.

Half-Turns and Translations

Half-turn

Rotation by $\pi$: $x \mapsto 2c - x$.

Product of two

Composes to a translation.

Two half-turns

$H_{c_2}\,H_{c_1} = \mathrm{Trans}\,\big(2(c_2 - c_1)\big)$

Consequence

Translations commute and have no fixed point.

Printed pp. 39-49Half-Turns and Translations
A half-turn is a rotation through pi about a center, sending each point to its reflection through that center. The product of two half-turns is a translation by twice the vector joining the centers, mirroring the parallel-mirror rule and showing why translations commute.

The Glide Reflection

Glide map

$G(x,y) = (x + g,\, -y),\quad G^2 = \mathrm{Trans}\,(2g)$

Signature

Opposite, no fixed point, midpoints on axis.

Printed pp. 39-49The Glide Reflection
A glide reflection reflects across an axis then slides along it. It is opposite, has no fixed point, yet its square is a pure translation. For every point the midpoint of the point and its image lands exactly on the glide axis, the cleanest visible fingerprint.

Classification by Reflection Count

One reflection

A reflection (opposite).

Two reflections

Rotation if mirrors meet, translation if parallel.

Three reflections

A glide reflection (opposite).

Structural theorem

$\text{every isometry} = R_{m_3}R_{m_2}R_{m_1},\ \ \le 3$

Printed pp. 39-49Classification by Reflection Count
One reflection gives a reflection; two give a rotation or translation depending on whether mirrors meet or stay parallel; three give a glide reflection. Thus every plane isometry is a product of at most three reflections, sorting the four kinds by parity and reflection count.

Fixed Points and the Hjelmslev Midpoint

Fixed sets

Translation: none. Rotation: one point. Reflection: a line. Glide: none.

Hjelmslev

Midpoints of a line to its image are collinear.

Midpoint locus

$M_i = \tfrac12\big(P_i + f(P_i)\big)\ \Rightarrow\ \text{collinear}$

Half-turn case

All midpoints collapse to the center.

Printed pp. 39-49Fixed Points and the Hjelmslev Midpoint
Fixed-set behavior names the motion: translations fix nothing, rotations fix one point, reflections fix a line, glides fix nothing yet carry an axis. Hjelmslev's theorem says midpoints joining a line to its image are collinear, or coincide for a half-turn.

The Group of Isometries

A group

Closed under composition and inverse.

Index two

Direct isometries form a normal subgroup.

Strip generators

$\langle\,\text{trans},\ \text{mirror},\ \text{half-turn},\ \text{glide}\,\rangle$

Patterns

Small generator sets repeat one motif into a strip.

Printed pp. 39-49The Group of Isometries
The isometries form a group under composition, with direct isometries an index-two subgroup. Strip patterns are generated by small sets of these motions: translations, vertical and horizontal mirrors, half-turns, and glides, each generator doing identifiable geometric work along the strip.

Recap and Classifier

Read four numbers

Determinant, translation, fixed-set rank, distance residual.

Then name it

Translation, rotation, reflection, or glide.

Decision order

$\det A \to t \to \operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{Fix}) \to \|A^\top A - I\|$

Takeaway

The catalog becomes a reliable procedure.

Printed pp. 39-49Recap and Classifier
To classify any candidate map, read four numbers in strict order: the determinant sign, the translation part, the fixed-set rank, and the distance residual. That disciplined habit converts Coxeter's catalog of plane motions into a reliable decision procedure, naming a translation, rotation, reflection, or glide reflection every single time.
