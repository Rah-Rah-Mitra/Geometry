Lines and CirclesIncidence with the center decides the image

Inversion Exchanges Lines and Circles in Four Cases

Line Through \(O\)

The image is the same line direction, with reciprocal radial positions.

Line Missing \(O\)

The image is a circle through \(O\); one sample has center \((0,0.694)\).

Circle Through \(O\)

The image is a line; the limiting point at \(O\) is sent to infinity.

Circle Missing \(O\)

The image is another circle; one sample radius is \(0.172\).

Maximum residual: \(1.998\times10^{-15}\)Classification is geometric, not visual guesswork
The line and circle slide is the practical classification theorem. Lines through the inversion center remain lines, lines missing the center become circles through it, circles through the center become lines, and circles missing the center remain circles. The residuals certify all four cases numerically.

ApolloniusDistance-ratio loci

A Fixed Distance Ratio Is Usually a Circle

Locus

$$ {|PA|\over |PA'|}=\mu $$

\(\mu=1\)

The locus is the perpendicular bisector line.

\(\mu\ne1\)

The locus is an Apollonius circle.

Ratios Sampled

Fixed points: \(A=(-2,0)\), \(A'=(2,0)\)The line case appears exactly at ratio one
Apollonius loci translate a distance ratio into a geometric object. When the ratio is one the locus is a line, and otherwise it is a circle whose center and radius move with the ratio. The table makes the transition around one inspectable rather than mysterious.
