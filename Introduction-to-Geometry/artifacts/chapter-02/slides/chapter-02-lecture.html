IsometryFixed points leave little freedom

Fixing Two Points Forces Identity or Reflection

Distance Constraint

A third point must stay on two circles centered at the fixed points.

Two Candidates

Off the fixed line, the only choices are \(P\) and its mirror image.

Conclusion

More than one fixed point forces either identity or reflection in the fixed line.

Isometry means every distance is preservedFixed points constrain the map
The fixed-point isometry slide explains why reflections are so constrained. If two points are fixed, a third point must preserve both distances to them, leaving only the original point or its mirror across the fixed line. More fixed information leaves very little freedom.

The dihedral group slide converts the symmetry picture into algebra. A regular pentagon has five rotations and five reflections, the rotation has order five, the reflection squares to the identity, and conjugating a rotation by a reflection reverses it. That is the group in one diagram.

First visible noncommutativityReflection products generate rotations
The product of reflections is the chapter's most productive mechanism. Two mirrors through the same point produce a rotation whose angle is twice the angle between the mirrors. Reversing the order gives the inverse rotation, so noncommutativity becomes visible as a geometric effect.
