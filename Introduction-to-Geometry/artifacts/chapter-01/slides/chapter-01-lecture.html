Affine Meaning

Midpoints, straightness, and averaging are enough; angles are irrelevant.

Shape may vary, midpoint logic remainsCentroid as affine invariant
The centroid slide separates affine geometry from metric geometry. Medians use only midpoint and straight-line structure, and the centroid is the average of the vertices. The two-to-one ratios survive shape changes because this construction does not depend on angles or side lengths.

This comparison prevents the common mistake of treating all triangle centers alike. The incenter is controlled by equal distances to side lines, while the circumcenter is controlled by equal distances to vertices. One center is tangent to the sides; the other sees the vertices on a circle.

The extremum problems show how classical geometry can hide optimization. Fagnano asks for a shortest inscribed triangle and lands on the orthic triangle for an acute triangle. Fermat asks for the shortest total distance to the vertices and produces the interior one hundred twenty degree condition.
