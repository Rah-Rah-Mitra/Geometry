Vector Products as Geometry

Dot product

Projection and orthogonality: $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta$.

Cross product

Oriented area and a normal direction $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$.

Triple product

Signed volume; zero exactly when coplanar.

Printed pp. 307-327Vector Products as Geometry
Before differentiating directions we recover the geometry of vector algebra. The dot product is projection and orthogonality, the cross product is oriented area and a normal direction, and the scalar triple product is signed volume that vanishes exactly when three edge vectors are coplanar.

Vector Functions and Derivatives

Constant length

If $\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{r}$ is constant, differentiate to get the key identity.

Orthogonality

$\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{r}'=0$

Circle

Radial position, tangential velocity, inward acceleration.

Printed pp. 307-327Vector Functions and Derivatives
Differentiating a vector function is done component by component, but its geometry matters more. Any vector of constant length has a derivative perpendicular to itself, the identity that forces circular motion to have tangential velocity and inward acceleration, seeding the later definitions of tangents and normals.

Arc Length and the Unit Tangent

Arc length

Integrate speed to measure distance along the curve.

Definition

$s=\displaystyle\int_a^t |\mathbf{r}'(u)|\,du,\quad \mathbf{T}=\dfrac{d\mathbf{r}}{ds}$

Why $s$

Removes the arbitrary speed of a parametrization.

Printed pp. 307-327Arc Length and the Unit Tangent
Reparametrizing by arc length removes the arbitrary speed of a parametrization. Differentiation with respect to s yields a unit tangent that depends only on the geometry of the trace, giving every later frame vector an intrinsic, parametrization-independent meaning we can compute and trust without ambiguity.

Curvature and the Principal Normal

Curvature

How fast the unit tangent turns per unit arc length.

Definition

$\kappa=\left|\dfrac{d\mathbf{T}}{ds}\right|,\quad \dfrac{d\mathbf{T}}{ds}=\kappa\,\mathbf{N}$

Principal normal

$\mathbf{N}$ points toward the centre of bending.

Printed pp. 307-327Curvature and the Principal Normal
Curvature measures how fast the unit tangent turns. Because the tangent has constant length, its derivative is perpendicular to it and points along the principal normal. The magnitude kappa is the reciprocal of the radius of the osculating circle that best hugs the curve at that point.

The Osculating Circle and Evolute

Centre of curvature

$\mathbf{r}_c=\mathbf{r}+\dfrac{1}{\kappa}\,\mathbf{N}$ for each point.

Evolute

The locus of centres of curvature.

Involute

Unwinding a taut string returns the curve.

Printed pp. 307-327The Osculating Circle and Evolute
At each point the osculating circle of radius one over kappa best fits the curve, and its centre is the centre of curvature. As the point moves, these centres trace the evolute; unwinding a taut string from the evolute returns the original curve as one of its involutes.

Catenary and Tractrix

Catenary

The hanging chain: $\big(au,\;a\cosh u\big)$.

Tractrix

Its involute: $\big(a(u-\tanh u),\;a\,\mathrm{sech}\,u\big)$.

Defining property

Tangent segment to the axis has constant length $a$.

Printed pp. 307-327Catenary and Tractrix
The catenary is the curve of a hanging chain, and the tractrix is its involute, obtained by unwinding. The tractrix is pinned down by a vivid property: the tangent segment from any point to the horizontal axis has constant length, a fact we verify numerically rather than by eye.

The Frenet Trihedron in Space

Tangent $\mathbf{T}$

Unit direction of travel along the curve.

Normal $\mathbf{N}$

Toward bending, perpendicular to $\mathbf{T}$.

Binormal $\mathbf{B}$

$\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{T}\times\mathbf{N}$ completes the frame.

Printed pp. 307-327The Frenet Trihedron in Space
A space curve cannot be described by a tangent alone. We attach a moving orthonormal frame: the tangent T, the principal normal N pointing toward bending, and the binormal B equal to T cross N. The plane of T and N is the osculating plane the curve momentarily lives in.

The Serret-Frenet Formulas

A skew system

The frame derivatives close on the frame itself.

Frenet-Serret

$\mathbf{T}'=\kappa\mathbf{N}$
$\mathbf{N}'=-\kappa\mathbf{T}+\tau\mathbf{B}$
$\mathbf{B}'=-\tau\mathbf{N}$

Two invariants

Curvature $\kappa$ and torsion $\tau$ alone drive the motion.

Printed pp. 307-327The Serret-Frenet Formulas
The three frame derivatives close into a skew system. T prime equals kappa N, B prime equals minus tau N, and N prime equals minus kappa T plus tau B. Two invariants, curvature and torsion, drive the entire motion of the trihedron along the curve.

Torsion and the Osculating Plane

Torsion

How fast the osculating plane twists about $\mathbf{T}$.

Reading $\tau$

$\tau=0 \iff$ planar curve

Out of plane

Nonzero $\tau$ lifts the curve from its osculating plane.

Printed pp. 307-327Torsion and the Osculating Plane
Torsion measures how fast the osculating plane twists, equivalently how the binormal swings. A plane curve has zero torsion and never leaves its osculating plane. Nonzero torsion is exactly the curve lifting out of that plane, the signature of genuinely three-dimensional twisting.

The Circular Helix

Parametrization

$\mathbf{r}(u)=\big(a\cos u,\;a\sin u,\;cu\big)$.

Constant invariants

$\kappa=\dfrac{a}{a^2+c^2},\quad \tau=\dfrac{c}{a^2+c^2}$

Pitch

Constant rise $c$ per turn; constant bending and twisting.

Printed pp. 307-327The Circular Helix
The circular helix is the laboratory of the theory. For radius a and pitch c, arc length grows like the square root of a squared plus c squared, and both curvature and torsion are constant, equal to a and c each divided by that same sum of squares.

General Helices and Recap

General helix

Tangent at a fixed angle to a direction.

Lancret condition

$\dfrac{\kappa}{\tau}=\text{constant}$

Concho-spiral

Fixed-angle motion lifted onto a cone.

One recurring theme

  • A curve is special when one invariant stays constant.
  • Circle: constant $\kappa$. Helix: constant $\kappa$ and $\tau$.
  • General helix: constant $\kappa/\tau$. Tractrix: constant tangent length.
Printed pp. 307-327General Helices and Recap
A general helix keeps its tangent at a fixed angle to a direction, equivalently a constant curvature over torsion ratio; the concho-spiral extends this on a cone. The recurring theme of the chapter is that a curve is special precisely when one differential invariant stays constant along it.
