Dilative rotation adds similarity scale to the rotational axis picture. The determinant becomes lambda cubed, so positive lambda is direct and negative lambda is opposite in three dimensions. The two sampled cases share the same rotation angle but have opposite orientation behavior.

A Spatial Transformation Is Classified By Tests, Not Its Name

1. Distance or Scale

Check whether all distances are preserved or all distance ratios share one factor.

2. Determinant Sign

Positive determinant is direct; negative determinant is opposite.

3. Fixed Set

All space, axis, plane, center, or no fixed point separates special cases.

4. Generator

Products of reflections explain rotations, translations, central inversion, and twists.

Special Cases
  • Identity: \(\theta=0\), \(\lambda=1\), fixed set all space.
  • Half-turn: \(\theta=\pi\), \(\lambda=1\), fixed set axis.
  • Plane reflection: \(\theta=\pi\), \(\lambda=-1\), fixed set plane.
  • Central inversion: \(\theta=0\), \(\lambda=-1\), fixed set center.

Three-Dimensional Motions Are Built From Invariants

Orientation

Signed volume distinguishes direct and opposite isometries when distances agree.

Reflections

Three perpendicular reflections give central inversion; two plane reflections rotate or translate.

Twists

A screw motion preserves distances and radius from an axis while translating along it.

Similarities

Dilative rotation and sphere inversion replace exact distance preservation with scale or sphere fitting.
