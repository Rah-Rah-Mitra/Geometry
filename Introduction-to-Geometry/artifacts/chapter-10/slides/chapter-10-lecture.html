Euler ClassificationThe five symbols

Euler Counts Make Most Regular Symbols Impossible

Relations

$$V-E+F=2,\qquad qV=2E=pF$$

Positive Denominator

$$(p-2)(q-2)<4$$

Valid Symbols

\(\{3,3\},\{4,3\},\{3,4\},\{3,5\},\{5,3\}\).

Boundary Cases

\(\{3,6\}\), \(\{4,4\}\), and \(\{6,3\}\) are flat rather than spherical.

Symbolic Euler check simplifies to 2Only five positive spherical cases remain
Euler classification is the proof core of the chapter. The relations qV equals two E equals pF combine with V minus E plus F equals two to produce the denominator condition, and only five positive regular symbols survive before the flat and overfull cases appear.

Duality Check

\(\phi(p,q)=\psi(q,p)\), and tetrahedron plus octahedron dihedral angles sum to \(180^\circ\).

Radii and angles show that the five solids are metrically different even when the same Euler identity holds. Circumradius, midradius, inradius, angular defect, and dihedral angle change with the Schlaefli symbol, and the cube-octahedron values preview duality.
Reciprocal PolyhedraFaces become vertices

Duality Swaps \(\{p,q\}\) With \(\{q,p\}\)

Cube and Octahedron

\(\{4,3\}\leftrightarrow\{3,4\}\); \(12\) shared original edges generate dual edges.

Dodecahedron and Icosahedron

\(\{5,3\}\leftrightarrow\{3,5\}\); \(30\) shared original edges.

Tetrahedron

\(\{3,3\}\) is self-dual.

Orthogonality

Maximum reciprocal edge orthogonality residual is \(4.74\times10^{-17}\).

Dual vertices come from original face centersReciprocity is a geometric construction, not just a table
Reciprocal polyhedra turn faces into vertices and swap the Schlaefli parameters. Cube and octahedron form a dual pair, dodecahedron and icosahedron form another, and the tetrahedron is self-dual. Orthogonality checks show the face-center construction is geometrically aligned.
Regularity Versus EulerWhat the formula does not see

A Rectangular Box Has Euler Characteristic Two But Is Not Platonic

Euler Data

\(V=8,\ E=12,\ F=6,\ \chi=2\).

Metric Failure

Distinct edge lengths are \(1.0,\ 1.45,\ 2.1\).

Face-Center Radii

Radii range from \(0.5\) to \(1.05\), so reciprocal placement is not regular.

Lesson

Euler characteristic and reciprocal graph survive, but metric equality fails.

Regularity needs equal local metric dataTopology alone does not classify Platonic solids
The regularity lab is a useful warning. A rectangular box still satisfies Euler's formula and has a reciprocal graph, but its edge lengths and face-center radii are unequal. This is the moment to separate topological sphere-like behavior from Platonic regularity.
Lecture RecapWhy exactly five?

The Classification Works Because Counts, Metrics, And Duality Agree

Topology

\(V-E+F=2\) holds for the constructed spherical polyhedra.

Regular Symbols

\((p-2)(q-2)<4\) leaves only five positive cases.

Metric Data

Radii and dihedral angles distinguish solids with the same Euler behavior.

Reciprocity

Duality swaps \(\{p,q\}\) and \(\{q,p\}\), with the tetrahedron self-dual.

Final Sanity
  • Five symbols: \(\{3,3\},\{4,3\},\{3,4\},\{3,5\},\{5,3\}\).
  • All Platonic meshes have \(\chi=2\).
  • All construction families are watertight.
  • Max reciprocal orthogonality residual \(4.74\times10^{-17}\).
