Shape kept rigid, size set free

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94Similarity in the Euclidean Plane
This chapter spans printed pages 67 to 76 and places similarity between congruence and affine freedom. A similarity preserves all angles and scales every length by one positive factor, yet it may keep or reverse orientation. We classify these maps, find their fixed centers, and apply them to classical triangle geometry across the plane.
Introduction to GeometryChapter 05

What a Similarity Preserves

Angles

Every angle is carried to an equal angle.

Length ratios

All distances multiply by a single ratio $k$.

Areas

Regions scale by $k^2$.

Defining property

$|S(P)\,S(Q)| = k\,|PQ|, \quad k > 0$

Consequence

$\operatorname{area}(S(F)) = k^2\,\operatorname{area}(F)$

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94What a Similarity Preserves
A similarity is weaker than an isometry but still rigid in shape: it multiplies every distance by the same ratio k and leaves all angles unchanged. Areas therefore scale by k squared. Reading any figure means asking what survives once a single uniform change of scale is permitted across the whole plane consistently.

The Dilatation (Central Similarity)

Definition

$P' = O + \lambda\,(P - O)$

Parallel images

Each line maps to a line parallel to it.

Signed ratio

$|\lambda|$ scales length; $\operatorname{sgn}\lambda$ chooses the side of $O$.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94The Dilatation
The dilatation, or central similarity, fixes a center O and sends each point P along its own ray to O plus k times the displacement. Every line maps to a parallel line, and lengths scale by the absolute value of k. The sign of k decides whether image points fall on the same or opposite side of O.

Two Centers of Similitude

External center

Positive ratio pairs same-direction radius points.

Internal center

Negative ratio pairs opposite radius points.

Centers

$S_{\text{ext}}=\dfrac{r_1 C_2 - r_2 C_1}{r_1 - r_2},\;\; S_{\text{int}}=\dfrac{r_1 C_2 + r_2 C_1}{r_1 + r_2}$

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94Two Centers of Similitude
Two unequal non-concentric circles are related by exactly two dilatations. The external center uses a positive ratio pairing radius points in the same direction, while the internal center uses a negative ratio pairing opposite radius points. Both centers lie on the line of centers, and corresponding points stay collinear with them throughout.

The Spiral Similarity (Dilative Rotation)

Complex form

$z' = a\,z + b, \quad a = \mu(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$

Rotation plus scale

Turn by $\theta$ and scale by $\mu$ about one center.

Orbits

Each step multiplies the radius by $\mu$ and adds angle $\theta$.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94The Spiral Similarity
A direct similarity that is not a pure dilatation is a spiral similarity: a rotation through angle theta combined with a dilatation of ratio mu about one fixed center. In complex coordinates it reads z maps to a z plus b. Every orbit around the center turns by theta and scales by mu at each step.
Introduction to GeometryChapter 05

Every Direct Similarity Has a Center

Fixed point

$z_0 = \dfrac{b}{1 - a}, \quad a \neq 1$

Uniqueness

The $2\times2$ linear system has one solution when $a\neq1$.

Edge case

$a=1$ gives a translation: an isometry with no finite center.

Centered form

$z' - z_0 = a\,(z - z_0)$

Reading

About $z_0$ the map is a pure dilative rotation by $a$.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94The Fixed Center
Writing a direct similarity as z prime equals a z plus b, whenever a is not one the equation has a unique solution z0 equals b over one minus a. This is the invariant center about which the whole transformation is a clean dilative rotation. Only the isometric translation edge case, with a equal to one, lacks such a fixed point.
Introduction to GeometryChapter 05

Direct versus Opposite Similarities

Direct

$z'=az+b$; preserves orientation.

Opposite

$z'=a\bar{z}+b$; reverses orientation.

Determinant test

$\det_{\text{direct}} = |a|^2 > 0, \quad \det_{\text{opp}} = -|a|^2 < 0$

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94Direct versus Opposite
Orientation splits similarities in two. A direct similarity z maps to a z plus b has linear determinant the modulus of a squared, so oriented area keeps its sign. An opposite similarity z maps to a times the conjugate of z plus b has determinant minus that value, reversing every triangle's sense. The determinant sign is the decisive test.

The Dilative Reflection

Opposite form

$z' = a\,\bar{z} + b$

Dilation plus reflection

Scale by $\mu$, then reflect in a line through the center.

Fixed point

One anchor unless it degenerates to a glide reflection.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94The Dilative Reflection
The opposite counterpart of the spiral similarity is the dilative reflection: a dilatation combined with a reflection in a line through the fixed center. It scales by the ratio mu while flipping handedness. Like its direct cousin it possesses a single fixed point unless it degenerates into a pure glide reflection isometry.

The Nine-Point Center as a Similitude

Two half-scale maps

$G$ with $-\tfrac12$ and $H$ with $+\tfrac12$ land on one circle.

Result

$N = \tfrac12(O + H), \quad R_9 = \tfrac12 R$

Euler line

$O,\,G,\,N,\,H$ are collinear.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94The Nine-Point Center
The nine-point center N is a center of similitude in disguise. The half-turn dilatation about the centroid with ratio negative one half, and the dilatation about the orthocenter with ratio one half, both land on one circle of radius half the circumradius. Hence N is the midpoint of O and H along the Euler line.

Ratios of Length and Area

Internal ratios preserved

$\dfrac{|S(P)S(Q)|}{|S(R)S(T)|} = \dfrac{|PQ|}{|RT|}$

Similar triangles

Equal angles, proportional sides.

Area scaling

$\dfrac{[\,S(F)\,]}{[\,F\,]} = k^2$

Fundamental theorem

A line parallel to one side cuts the others proportionally.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94Ratios of Length and Area
Because a similarity multiplies every length by k, any ratio of two corresponding lengths equals k and any ratio of two lengths within a single figure is preserved exactly. Areas scale by k squared, so area ratios are likewise invariant. This is why similar triangles share equal angles and proportional sides throughout the plane.

Summary: Classifying Plane Similarities

Direct, ratio $\neq 1$

Dilatation or spiral similarity; unique fixed center.

Opposite, ratio $\neq 1$

Dilative reflection; one fixed point.

Ratio $=1$

The isometries: rotation, translation, reflection, glide.

Invariants

$\text{angles},\;\; \dfrac{|PQ|}{|RT|},\;\; \dfrac{[F_1]}{[F_2]}$

Applications

Similar triangles, centers of similitude, the nine-point center.

Printed pp. 67-76 · PDF pp. 85-94Summary and Classification
Every plane similarity is a dilatation, a spiral similarity, or a dilative reflection, together with the isometries as the ratio-one case. Direct maps preserve orientation and, when not isometries, have a unique fixed center; opposite maps reverse it. Length and area ratios survive, anchoring applications from similar triangles to the nine-point center.
