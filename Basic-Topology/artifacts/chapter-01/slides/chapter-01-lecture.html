Hypotheses
counterexamples explain the theorem

Euler Theorem Needs Its Hypotheses

Euler Theorem, Chapter 1 Form

If a polyhedral surface has a connected edge graph and every polygonal loop separates the surface, then \(V-E+F=2\).

What Fails Without Them?

  • Disconnected pieces each contribute their own ledger.
  • A tunnel admits a loop whose cut does not split the surface.
  • The theorem's proof needs both conditions to turn a dual graph into a tree.

Notebook checks record the counterexamples as disconnected_pair_chi_four and torus_chi_zero.

Hypotheses are not technical decorationThey identify sphere-like topology
Use this slide to slow down the temptation to memorize V minus E plus F equals two. Connectedness prevents separate pieces from adding their own Euler counts, while the separating-loop condition excludes a tunnel. The point is not that counterexamples are technical; the hypotheses are exactly where topology enters.
Proof State
verified: proof graph acyclic with Euler sink

Proof State: Tree And Dual Tree

Ledger From Two Trees

Choose a primal spanning tree \(T\). The unused edges define a complementary dual tree \(T^*\).

\[(v(T)-e(T))+(v(T^*)-e(T^*))=2.\]

Geometric Meaning

Thicken \(T\) and \(T^*\). Each thickened tree is a disc; the two discs meet along one boundary curve, giving the sphere picture behind Euler's formula.

The artifact checks proof_graph_is_acyclic and proof_graph_has_euler_sink record this proof-state scaffold.

Combinatorial tree identity: vertices minus edges equals oneTopological output: two-disc decomposition
Present the proof as a state machine. A primal spanning tree records connectedness, and the complementary dual graph becomes a tree only when loops separate correctly. Thickening those two trees leaves two discs with a common boundary, so the arithmetic proof and the sphere picture are the same argument in two languages.
Homeomorphism
continuous bijection with continuous inverse

Topological Equivalence As Deformation

Definition

A homeomorphism \(h:X\to Y\) is one-to-one, onto, continuous, and has continuous inverse.

Allowed Intuition

  • Stretching and bending are allowed.
  • Identifying distinct points is forbidden.
  • Tearing a neighborhood apart is forbidden.

Radial projection from a convex polyhedron to a sphere is the chapter's concrete model of this idea.

Rubber intuition is usefulThe formal object is the homeomorphism
Translate the informal rubber picture into the formal homeomorphism habit. A deformation intuition is useful, but the actual claim is the existence of a continuous bijection with continuous inverse. The visual route reminds students that points are tracked one-to-one; no tearing, gluing, or collapsing is allowed.
First Invariant
counts change, chi survives

Euler Number As Invariant

Invariant Pattern

Topologically equivalent polyhedra have the same Euler number.

\[\chi(P)=\chi(Q)\quad\text{when}\quad P\cong Q.\]

Why This Is Surprising

The individual counts \(V\), \(E\), and \(F\) depend on the chosen cell structure. Their alternating combination is the part that survives topological equivalence.

The chapter notebook verifies the sphere-like value across tetrahedron, cube, and icosahedron ledgers.

Compute from one convenient modelInterpret as a property of the space
State the first invariant theorem in operational language. The counts V, E, and F can change when a polyhedron is subdivided or redrawn, but the alternating total is preserved under topological equivalence. This is the prototype for later invariants: compute from a model, then interpret as belonging to the space.
Surfaces
local condition, global topology

Surfaces: Local Plane, Global Difference

Surface Test

Each point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to the plane. With boundary allowed, edge points use a half-plane neighborhood.

Local Is Not Global

A sphere and a torus both look planar under a microscope, but their global loop behavior and Euler characteristic differ.

A Mobius strip is locally a surface with boundary, while a trip around its center reverses the chosen normal direction.

Local neighborhoods define the objectGlobal invariants classify and separate examples
Separate local and global information. Every surface point should have a planar neighborhood, or a half-plane neighborhood when boundary is allowed, yet that local test cannot decide whether the whole surface is a sphere, torus, Mobius strip, or Klein bottle. The classification ledger makes that contrast visible.

Abstract Spaces
continuity without distance

Abstract Spaces And Neighborhoods

Topology As Neighborhood Data

A topological space assigns to each point a collection of neighborhoods satisfying stability under intersection, enlargement, and interior.

Continuity Test

\[f:X\to Y\ \text{is continuous when}\ f^{-1}(N)\ \text{is a neighborhood of }x.\]

Homeomorphism becomes meaningful for subsets, quotient constructions, function spaces, and surfaces that are not presented as rigid objects in Euclidean space.

Distance is optionalNeighborhood structure is enough to define continuity
Connect the pictures to the abstract definition. A topology is the neighborhood data needed to define continuity without relying on distance. The inverse-image test for continuity is the hinge: it lets homeomorphism make sense for subsets, quotient constructions, function spaces, and surfaces presented without a preferred embedding.

Classification Preview

Classification Preview

Every closed connected surface is homeomorphic to exactly one orientable handle surface or one nonorientable crosscap surface.

Orientable genus \(g\)

\(\chi=2-2g\)

Nonorientable \(n\) crosscaps

\(\chi=2-n\)

Present classification as a preview of a later proof and as an organizing theorem for examples. Closed connected surfaces split into orientable handle surfaces and nonorientable crosscap surfaces. The formulas for chi are compact ledger summaries, while orientability records information that chi alone cannot always see.
Surface Surgery
interactive ledger artifact

Surgery Ledger: Handles And Crosscaps

Handle Move

Remove two discs from a sphere and glue in a cylinder. This adds one handle and changes \(\chi\) by \(-2\).

Crosscap Move

Remove one disc and attach a Mobius strip. This adds one crosscap and changes \(\chi\) by \(-1\).

Open the linked artifact if the embedded Plotly view is paused by the host: surface-surgery-invariant-ledger.html.

Handle: orientable and chi decreases by twoCrosscap: nonorientable and chi decreases by one
Make the surgery operations computational. Adding a handle lowers chi by two and preserves orientability; adding a crosscap lowers chi by one and creates nonorientability. The interactive ledger is not a surface renderer, but it is a reliable invariant dashboard for checking moves through the classification list.
Limits Of One Invariant
torus and Klein share chi zero

Same Chi, Different Surface

Single-Invariant Warning

\(\chi(T^2)=0\) and \(\chi(K)=0\), but \(T^2\) is orientable and the Klein bottle is not.

Invariant Suite

  • Euler characteristic separates many surfaces.
  • Orientability separates some ties.
  • Fundamental groups and homology refine the toolbox later.

Grounded in check torus_and_klein_share_chi_not_orientability.

Failure of one invariant is informationAdd another invariant before concluding sameness
Use the torus and Klein bottle to show why one invariant is rarely enough. Both have Euler characteristic zero, so chi alone cannot separate them. Orientability supplies the missing distinction. This prepares students for the larger lesson that topology uses suites of invariants rather than a single universal detector.
Non-Homeomorphism Pattern
connectedness and cut behavior

Invariants Separate Pictures

Reusable Contradiction Pattern

Assume \(h:X\to Y\) is a homeomorphism. Apply an invariant test to \(X\), transfer the test through \(h\), and compare the result in \(Y\).

Cutpoint Example

Removing one point from a cycle-like model remains connected. Removing the shared point from a figure-eight model disconnects it.

Checks: cycle_minus_one_point_connected and figure_eight_center_removal_disconnects.

To prove not homeomorphic, compute a preserved featureDifferent invariant values block every homeomorphism at once
Turn non-homeomorphism into a reusable proof pattern. If a proposed homeomorphism existed, it would carry the chosen operation and its invariant outcome to the other space. The cutpoint artifact models this with graphs: removing a point can preserve connectedness in one model and destroy it in another.
