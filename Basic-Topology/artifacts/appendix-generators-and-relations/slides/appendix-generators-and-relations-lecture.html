Open with the central problem: topology produces loops, but computation needs a finite algebraic record. The appendix explains how to move from a geometric generating set to formal words, then to a quotient where selected loops become trivial. Keep the emphasis on reading a presentation as instructions rather than as a black-box group name.

Presentations In Topology

loops, edges, faces
Topological Reading
Generators name basic loops or directed edges. Relations name closed boundary words that should become homotopically trivial.

1. Name paths

Choose loops such as \(a,b,c\) or edge symbols \(x,y\).

2. Form words

A path is read as a word: \(a b A c\), where capitals denote inverses.

3. Kill boundaries

A filled face contributes a relation word: \(a b A c = e\).

This is the computational bridge used later when topological decompositions produce fundamental-group presentations.

Connect the algebra to cell and loop pictures. Edges or named loops contribute generators, while faces, glued boundaries, or homotopies contribute words that must close up. The slide should make clear that a presentation is not decoration around a group; it is a compact record of the topology that produced the group.

Free Words As A Reduction Machine

no accidental equations
Free Group Normal Form
A reduced word has no adjacent inverse pair. The empty reduced word is the identity.
$$a b B A c C a a B A b \longmapsto a a B A b.$$

Stack rule

  • Read one symbol at a time.
  • If it is inverse to the stack top, pop.
  • Otherwise, push the symbol.
  • The final stack is the reduced word.

Check file: ../checks/free-word-reduction-checks.json

Treat the free group as a deterministic normal-form machine. In a free group there are no hidden relations, so the only automatic simplification is adjacent inverse cancellation. The stack image from the notebook makes the rule inspectable: read left to right, push a letter, and pop only when the next letter is its inverse.

From Free Group To Presentation Quotient

relations become a normal subgroup
Presentation Mechanism
Given generators \(X\) for a group \(G\), the free group maps onto \(G\). The kernel records exactly which free words become identity.
$$F(X) \twoheadrightarrow G,\qquad G \cong F(X)/N.$$

Where the relations live

If \(R\) is a chosen list of relation words, the quotient uses the smallest normal subgroup containing them:

$$N=\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle.$$

Sanity record: ../checks/relation-rewriting-checks.json

Now add relations. Start with F(X), map a free word to the group element it names, and collect the words that map to the identity. Because quotients require a normal subgroup, the relation set R really means its normal closure. This is the algebraic bridge from free syntax to the target group.

Relations Are Closed Paths On The Cayley Graph

geometric validation
Inspection Rule
A relation word \(r\) is valid in the quotient graph when reading \(r\) from any vertex returns to that vertex.

\(x^5\)

Five \(x\)-steps wind around a rotation cycle and return.

\(y^2\)

Two reflection steps undo each other.

\((xy)^2\)

The alternating square closes at every starting state.

The graph checks strong connectivity and relation closure in ../checks/appendix-final-sanity-checks.json.

Read a relation as a closed walk in the Cayley graph. If x^5 is a relation, following five x-edges returns to the starting vertex. If (xy)^2 is a relation, the alternating square also closes. This visual criterion is useful because a failed closure immediately exposes a broken presentation model.

Normal Closure: Relations Must Vanish Everywhere

conjugate loops
Normal Closure
The symbol \(\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle\) means the subgroup generated by every conjugate of every relation word:
$$g r g^{-1},\qquad r\in R,\;g\in F(X).$$
Geometric Meaning
Conjugation moves the basepoint to a new vertex, runs the relation loop, and moves back.

Normal-closure diagnostic: ../checks/normal-closure-checks.json

Stress why conjugates appear. A quotient should make a relation loop vanish no matter where the basepoint has been moved, so g r g^{-1} must also be trivial. The notebook check tests this by moving a relation loop around the dihedral Cayley graph and verifying closure at every sampled vertex.

A Schreier-Style Pentagon Action

orbit graph instead of full Cayley graph
Action Diagnostics
  • \(x\)-edges form one pentagon cycle.
  • \(y\) acts as an involution on the five vertices.
  • The presentation relations still act trivially.

Why use this view?

An action graph can expose relation failures on a smaller state space before one tries to enumerate a full Cayley graph.

Action checks: ../checks/schreier-action-checks.json

Shift from the full Cayley graph to an action graph. The pentagon keeps orbit points rather than all group elements, so x acts as a five-cycle while y acts as an involution. This is a useful lower-dimensional inspection: the presentation relations must still act trivially on the orbit even though the graph has fewer vertices.

Free Products And Alternating Normal Forms

factor groups as alphabets
Free Product Word Rule
A reduced word in \(G*H\) alternates nonidentity elements from \(G\) and \(H\). Adjacent letters from the same factor must be multiplied inside that factor first.

Presentation formula

If \(G=\langle X\mid R\rangle\) and \(H=\langle Y\mid S\rangle\), then the free product keeps both sets of generators and both sets of relations:

$$G*H=\langle X,Y\mid R,S\rangle.$$
factor relation: \(s^2=e\) factor relation: \(r^3=e\) no cross relation
Introduce free products as the same word philosophy with factor groups as alphabets. A reduced word alternates nonidentity pieces from G and H; if two adjacent pieces come from the same factor, first multiply them inside that factor. The tree artifact shows how quickly alternating normal forms branch.

Abelianization is a deliberately lossy inspection. Replace each relation word by exponent sums in the generators and compute the rank of the resulting integer relation matrix. This cannot identify the whole group, but it quickly detects what survives after all commutators have been forced to vanish.

Universal Properties: The Characterization Layer

source span endpoint
Free Group
Any function \(X\to K\) extends uniquely to a homomorphism \(F(X)\to K\).
Free Product
Any pair of homomorphisms \(G\to K\) and \(H\to K\) extends uniquely to one homomorphism \(G*H\to K\).
Teaching Point
These properties say what the constructions do for every target group, not just for the finite examples inspected above.
Close the algebra with the two universal mapping properties stated in the source span. A free group is the unique extension machine for functions defined on its generators. A free product is the unique extension machine for homomorphisms from its two factors. These are characterization principles, not extra examples.

Recap Checklist

what students should be able to inspect
  • Read a presentation as generators plus relation words.
  • Reduce a free word using only adjacent inverse cancellation.
  • Explain why relation words generate a normal closure.
  • Validate relation words as closed paths in a Cayley or action graph.
  • Recognize alternating normal forms in a free product.
  • Use abelianization as a quick shadow calculation.
Exit Prompt
Given \(\langle a,b \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}\rangle\), what does the abelianized relation matrix see, and what has it forgotten?
Use the final slide as an instructor checklist. Students should leave able to reduce free words, interpret a presentation as a quotient by normal closure, test relations on Cayley or action graphs, recognize alternating normal forms in free products, and use abelianization as a quick consistency check rather than a complete invariant.
