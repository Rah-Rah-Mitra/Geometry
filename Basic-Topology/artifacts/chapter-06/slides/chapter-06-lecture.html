1 | Complexes As Data

Simplicial Complexes Need Compatible Intersections

Definition
A complex \(K\) is a collection of simplexes such that every face of a simplex in \(K\) is in \(K\), and any two simplexes meet in a common face or not at all.
Polyhedron
The space \(|K|\) is the union of its simplexes with the topology obtained by gluing along shared faces.

Data Contract

  • Vertices are the primitive labels.
  • Faces are closed downward under inclusion.
  • Topology is read from incidence, not from a decorative drawing.
Spend time on the intersection condition. A drawing can look triangular and still fail to be a simplicial complex if overlaps create extra crossing data. The usable object is the incidence rule plus compatible geometry, so every intersection is already a face of both simplexes.

3 | Subdivision

Subdivision Rule
A simplex of \(\operatorname{Sd}K\) is a chain \(\sigma_0<\sigma_1<\cdots<\sigma_r\) of nonempty faces of \(K\).

Topological Point

Emphasize the chain rule for new simplexes: vertex less than edge less than face. The count increase is deliberate, not noise. The mesh shrinks while the underlying polyhedron and Euler characteristic stay fixed, which is exactly why subdivision is a proof machine.
4 | Why Subdivision Helps

Proof Roadmap: Make Stars Small Enough

Subdivision Strategy
Given an open cover of \(|K|\), sufficiently many barycentric subdivisions make every closed star lie inside one chosen open set.
1. Cover target by open stars.

Use the target complex \(L\) and its vertex stars as the local containers.

2. Pull back along the map.

For \(f:|K|\to |L|\), the inverse images of target stars form an open cover of the domain.

3. Subdivide the domain.

Repeated barycentric subdivision shrinks the mesh until each domain star is carried by one target star.

The proof idea is an open-cover argument written in combinatorial language. Pull target stars back along the continuous map, subdivide until domain stars are small enough, and then make vertex choices. This is where local geometry buys finite combinatorics.
5 | Approximation By Vertex Data

Simplicial Approximation: Continuous Maps Become Vertex Choices

Simplicial Approximation Theorem
After enough subdivision, a continuous map \(f:|K|\to |L|\) has a simplicial map \(g:\operatorname{Sd}^n K\to L\) with \(g(v)\) chosen from a carrier of \(f(v)\).
Star Condition
If \(f(\operatorname{St}(v))\subseteq \operatorname{St}(g(v))\) for every vertex \(v\), then \(g\) is the desired local simplicial model.

6 | What Can Go Wrong

Triangulation Is Stronger Than Drawing Triangles

Restriction 1
Face closure: every edge and vertex of a listed simplex must be part of the complex.
Restriction 2
Common-face intersections: accidental crossings create missing incidence data.
Restriction 3
Topology: infinite or quotient models must carry the intended gluing topology.

Quotients Need Compatibility

An edge-identification polygon gives a recipe. A compatible triangulation must match boundary subdivisions and preserve the prescribed edge word.

\(a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}\) is a quotient instruction, not a complex by itself.

Orbit Spaces Need Repair

For a simplicial group action, barycentric subdivision separates orbit data enough for the quotient to inherit a complex structure.

Notebook Check
The chapter tracks vertex schemes and uses subdivision to avoid bad collapses inside a small simplex.
This is the caution slide. Triangulation is stronger than drawing triangles on a space. The face-closure condition, common-face intersections, quotient compatibility, and local finiteness are all restrictions that prevent a picture from lying about the topology.
7 | Edge Loops

Edge Paths Replace Arbitrary Loops By Finite Words

Edge Path
An edge loop is a word \(v_0v_1\cdots v_n\) in adjacent vertices with \(v_0=v_n\), read inside the 1-skeleton.
Triangle Move
If \(u,v,w\) span a 2-simplex, the path \(u\to v\to w\) is equivalent to the shortcut \(u\to w\).

8 | Presentations

Edge Group Calculations Become Generators And Relations

Edge Group Theorem
For a connected complex, the edge-loop group agrees with the fundamental group of the polyhedron.
1. Choose a maximal tree.

Declare tree edges trivial by contracting them in the edge-path calculation.

2. Keep non-tree edges.

They are the candidate generators; in a graph, their number is \(E-V+1\).

3. Read triangle relations.

Every filled 2-simplex gives a local shortcut relation among edge words.

12 | Quotients By Simplicial Actions

Orbit Triangulations Need Vertex Schemes

Simplicial Action
A group action on \(K\) is simplicial when each group element sends every simplex linearly onto a simplex.
Roadmap
Record vertex orbits, list which orbit sets come from simplexes, then subdivide enough that no small simplex collapses ambiguously.
Repair Move
The chapter's proof uses barycentric subdivision, especially a second subdivision, to make the quotient inherit clean simplicial data.
Explain the vertex scheme slowly: the quotient remembers vertex orbits and which orbit sets came from original simplexes. The second barycentric subdivision is a technical but useful repair, because it prevents bad identifications inside a small simplex from corrupting the quotient complex.
13 | Infinite Complexes

Local Finiteness Keeps Infinite Complexes Topological

Local Finiteness
Each point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many simplexes. In a combinatorial test, every vertex star is finite.
Why It Matters
Without local finiteness, the Euclidean subspace topology and the gluing topology can diverge near an accumulation point.

This is where infinite examples become respectable. Local finiteness keeps neighborhoods from meeting infinitely many unrelated simplexes, so the gluing topology behaves like the Euclidean subspace topology. The accumulation panel shows why this condition is not cosmetic.
14 | Proof Architecture

Four Reusable Proof Roadmaps

Subdivision

Replace \(K\) by \(\operatorname{Sd}^nK\), preserve \(|K|\), shrink mesh, and keep \(\chi\) stable.

Simplicial Approximation

Use stars and carriers to replace a continuous map by a simplicial map after subdivision.

Edge Groups

Choose a tree, keep non-tree generators, and impose triangle shortcut relations.

Orbit Triangulations

Track vertex orbits, subdivide to remove ambiguity, and read quotient simplexes.

Use the dependency map as a proof navigation tool. Subdivision supplies small stars, approximation turns maps into vertex data, edge groups turn loops into presentations, and orbit triangulations make quotient spaces combinatorial after enough controlled refinement.
15 | Simplicial Invariants

What Changes Under Triangulation, And What Should Not

Adjustable Model Data

Number of vertices, edges, and faces.
Mesh size and geometric embedding.
Subdivision level and vertex scheme.
Choice of maximal tree in an edge-group calculation.

Stable Topological Signals

Underlying polyhedron \(|K|\).
Euler characteristic \(\chi\).
Betti pattern and boundary identity \(\partial\partial=0\).
Edge group, hence \(\pi_1\), after the theorem applies.
Chapter Habit
Change the triangulation to make the proof local; check that the invariant being used does not change with the triangulation.
This comparison is the central habit of the chapter. Triangulations are adjustable models, so counts and mesh sizes can change. The polyhedron, Euler characteristic, homotopy information, quotient type, and local finiteness checks are the data that should remain meaningful.
16 | Recap

Triangulations Turn Topology Into Controlled Combinatorics

Takeaways

  • Simplicial complexes are incidence rules with compatible geometry.
  • Barycentric subdivision refines by chains and shrinks mesh.
  • Simplicial approximation turns continuous maps into vertex data.
  • Edge groups make \(\pi_1\) calculations finite and local.
  • Quotient and infinite complexes require compatibility checks.

What To Watch In Proofs

  • Where is the triangulation changed?
  • Which invariant is claimed to survive?
  • Which local condition is being used: star, carrier, triangle relation, vertex orbit, or local finiteness?
Refine the model. Preserve the topology. Compute locally.
