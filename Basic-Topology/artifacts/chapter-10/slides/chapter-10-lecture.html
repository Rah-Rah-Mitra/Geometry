From space curve to annotated immersion

Diagram Data Is Local

Diagram rule

Each crossing is read inside a tiny disk. Outside the disk, the diagram is unchanged while local data are edited or recorded.

  • Finiteness keeps the diagram combinatorial.
  • Transversality rules out triple collisions and tangencies.
  • Over-under data remembers the lost coordinate.
diagram \(=\) immersion of \(S^1\) \(+\) crossing signs
Notebook section: A Trefoil Is Not Its ProjectionInspection target: local crossing disk
Slow down on the distinction between a knot and a diagram. A diagram is an annotated immersion of a circle in the plane, with finitely many local over-under decisions. Students should inspect how each crossing contributes a tiny piece of data while the ambient isotopy class lives in three dimensions.
Diagram changes that preserve the knot

Reidemeister Moves

Theorem pattern

Two nice diagrams represent ambient isotopic knots exactly when one can pass between them by planar isotopy and finitely many Reidemeister moves.

Type I: create or remove a local curl.
Type II: create or remove two opposite crossings.
Type III: slide a strand past a crossing.
Artifact: reidemeister-move-atlas.pngLocal disk outside boundary fixed
Present the three moves as the legal edit operations for diagrams of the same knot. The outside of the small disk is fixed, so all action is local. The point is not to memorize drawings, but to recognize that curl, cancellation, and slide changes are harmless at the level of ambient isotopy.

Crossings become group relations

Wirtinger Relations

Construction

Assign a generator to each overpass in the diagram. At each crossing, read the underpass change as a conjugacy relation in the complement.

\(\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)\)
Trefoil reduction

A standard reduction of the diagram gives \(\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle\).

Artifact: trefoil-wirtinger-presentation-graph.pngNotebook section: Wirtinger Presentations
Introduce Wirtinger presentations as a local-to-global conversion. Each overpass contributes a generator in the complement, and each crossing records how an underpass loop changes when it passes under an overpass. The graph artifact is a proof scaffold: generators feed relations, and relations assemble into the knot group.

Finite quotient check

The trefoil relation aba equals bab is useful because it has a small nonabelian witness. Sending a and b to two transpositions in S3 satisfies the relation but the images do not commute. That finite quotient separates the trefoil group from the infinite cyclic group of the unknot without solving the whole group.
A spanning surface from a diagram

Seifert Surface Model

Algorithmic picture

Smooth oriented crossings, fill the Seifert circles with disks, then restore crossings as twisted bands.

Boundary condition

The resulting orientable surface has boundary equal to the original knot diagram, interpreted back in three-space.

Artifact: trefoil-seifert-surface-band-model.pngConcept: Seifert surface
Now move from the complement to a spanning surface. The Seifert construction smooths crossings according to orientation, fills the resulting circles by disks, and reinstalls crossings as twisted bands. The band model keeps the surface orientable while showing where the genus is forced by the original three crossings.

Computation

For the trefoil, \(\chi=2-3=-1\), so \(-1=2-2g-1\), and hence \(g=1\).

Check: trefoil-seifert-genus-checks.jsonFormula: chi = s - c = 2 - 2g - b
Make the Euler characteristic calculation explicit and mechanical. For the trefoil model, two Seifert circles and three bands give chi equal to minus one. With one boundary component, the equation chi equals two minus two g minus b gives genus one. This is a surface invariant extracted from diagram bookkeeping.
Local sheets, global monodromy

Covering Maps

Definition

A map \(p:E\to B\) is a covering map if every small enough \(U\subset B\) has preimage split into disjoint open sets, each mapped homeomorphically onto \(U\).

Loop action

Lift a loop in \(B\) from a chosen sheet. Its endpoint records how the loop moves through the fiber.

Notebook section: Covering SpacesLocal condition, global sheet motion
Shift vocabulary from knots to covering spaces. A covering map looks locally like identical sheets over each small base neighborhood, even though loops can permute sheets globally. This prepares the bridge to knot groups: fundamental group elements act by lifting paths and watching where endpoints land.
The circle cover as the model calculation

Lifts And Deck Shifts

Universal cover of \(S^1\)

The map \(p:\mathbb R\to S^1\) sends a real parameter to an angle. A loop with winding one lifts to a path ending one integer translate away.

deck shift: \(T_n(x)=x+n\)
Inspection target

Watch the endpoint, not just the projected loop. Endpoint motion is the computational face of monodromy.

Artifact: circle-cover-lifts-and-deck-transformations.htmlDeck group: Z acting by translations
Use the circle cover as the cleanest endpoint-motion model. A loop of winding number one lifts from a chosen starting point to the next integer translate. Deck transformations preserve the covering map, so on the real line they are exactly integer translations. This is the prototype for the deck-shift variable t.
Regularity is extra structure

Deck Transformations

Definition

A deck transformation is a homeomorphism \(h:E\to E\) with \(p\circ h=p\). It permutes each fiber without changing the base point.

Where the variable \(t\) comes from

Infinite Cyclic Cover

\(\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)\longrightarrow H_1(S^3\setminus K)\cong\mathbb Z\)
Meridian rule

Every Wirtinger meridian maps to the same generator of \(\mathbb Z\). The corresponding regular cover has deck group \(\mathbb Z\).

Algebraic translation

Choose one deck generator. Its action on homology is written as multiplication by \(t\).

Notebook section: Alexander PolynomialDeck shift becomes the Laurent variable
Tie the covering-space language back to knots. Abelianizing a knot group sends every meridian to one generator of Z, producing an infinite cyclic cover of the complement. The deck group is Z, and one chosen deck shift is recorded algebraically as multiplication by t in the Alexander module.
Crossing relations over \(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]\)

Alexander Matrix

Trefoil matrix

The notebook uses the matrix \(\begin{bmatrix}1-t&-1\\ t&1-t\end{bmatrix}\) as the two-relation Alexander presentation.

\(\det A=t^2-t+1\)
Unit ambiguity

The polynomial is read up to multiplication by \(\pm t^k\), reflecting choices in the presentation.

Artifact: trefoil-alexander-presentation-matrix.pngCheck: trefoil-alexander-polynomial-checks.json
Explain the matrix as relations after the deck shift has been remembered. The trefoil presentation matrix has entries in the Laurent polynomial ring, so row and column operations carry unit ambiguities. The determinant t squared minus t plus one is the trefoil Alexander polynomial up to multiplication by plus or minus a power of t.
