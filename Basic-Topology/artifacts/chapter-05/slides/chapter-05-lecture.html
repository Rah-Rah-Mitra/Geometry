Construction Step 1

Homotopy Rel Endpoints Pins The Basepoint

Definition
A homotopy \(F:I\times I\to X\) from \(\alpha\) to \(\beta\) rel endpoints satisfies
\(F(s,0)=\alpha(s),\quad F(s,1)=\beta(s)\)
and \(F(0,t)=F(1,t)=p\) for every \(t\).
Computational Translation
The notebook treats a based loop as a sampled path or a word. Homotopy rel endpoints permits deformation, re-timing, and backtracking cancellation while the basepoint remains fixed.
  • Horizontal coordinate \(s\): position along the loop.
  • Vertical coordinate \(t\): deformation time.
  • Pinned sides are what make loop classes multiply cleanly.
Homotopy rel endpointsSource section 5.1
Emphasize that the square is the actual object, not a metaphor. Each horizontal slice is a based loop, while both vertical edges are pinned at the basepoint. This is what makes deformation compatible with loop multiplication later.

Construction Step 2

Quotienting By Homotopy Makes Multiplication Algebraic

Theorem 5.5
Homotopy classes of loops based at \(p\) form a group:
\([\alpha]\,[\beta]=[\alpha\cdot\beta]\)
with identity the constant loop and inverse \([\alpha^{-1}]\).
  • Associativity becomes a reparameterization of the interval.
  • The constant loop only changes how the clock is spent.
  • \(\alpha\cdot\alpha^{-1}\) is invisible after backtracking.
Sanity Data
Word checks pass: associativity matches, left identity holds, and inverse backtracking reduces to the identity.
Fundamental group lawArtifact: loop-product-group-laws.png
The raw concatenation operation remembers the clock, so it is too strict. Passing to homotopy classes hides harmless reparameterizations and backtracking. The checks in the notebook confirm the three group-law claims with reduced loop words.
Functoriality With A Basepoint

Maps Send Based Loops To Based Loops

Induced Homomorphism
For \(f:X\to Y\), define
\(f_*([\alpha])=[f\circ\alpha]\).
Composition is preserved: \((g\circ f)_*=g_*\circ f_*\).
Basepoint Change
A path \(u:p\to q\) reads a loop at \(p\) from \(q\) as \(u^{-1}\alpha u\). Algebraically this is conjugation, so the basepoint is not decorative.
Degree tests: \((-2)\cdot3=-6\), \(2\cdot5=10\), \(0\cdot7=0\), \(1\cdot(-4)=-4\).
Conjugation sample: old word \(a b A\) becomes \(U a b A u\).
Induced mapsArtifact: induced-map-basepoint-diagram.png
Make the basepoint issue explicit before using functoriality. Moving the basepoint along a path conjugates loops, while a continuous map composes with loops and gives a homomorphism. The degree-map checks verify that induced maps compose as expected.
Model Computation

\(\pi_1(S^1)\cong \mathbb{Z}\) By Lifting To The Line

Theorem 5.8
The map \(n\mapsto [s\mapsto e^{2\pi i n s}]\) is an isomorphism \(\mathbb{Z}\to\pi_1(S^1,1)\).
  • Lift a loop \(\alpha:I\to S^1\) to \(\tilde{\alpha}:I\to\mathbb{R}\) with \(\tilde{\alpha}(0)=0\).
  • The endpoint \(\tilde{\alpha}(1)\) must be an integer.
  • Homotopy lifting forces that integer to be invariant.

The Torus Records Two Translation Generators

Covering View
If \(G\) acts properly discontinuously on simply connected \(X\), then the quotient \(X/G\) has \(\pi_1(X/G)\cong G\).
  • \(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\) translations of the plane give the torus.
  • The two deck translations become meridian and longitude.
  • The product theorem gives the same result from \(S^1\times S^1\).

Invariant Beyond Homeomorphism

Homotopy Equivalence Preserves \(\pi_1\)

Theorem 5.18
Path-connected spaces with the same homotopy type have isomorphic fundamental groups.
  • A deformation retraction is a visible homotopy equivalence.
  • The annulus, cylinder core, and punctured plane share the circle group.
  • The basepoint path still matters in the formal proof.
Reusable Move
Replace a thick space by a spine when the homotopy carries every loop to the retained subspace.
Homotopy typeArtifact: deformation-retraction-annulus.png
Now the invariant is no longer restricted to homeomorphism. A deformation retraction gives a concrete homotopy equivalence, so the annulus, cylinder core, and punctured plane inherit the circle group. The visual shows points flowing toward the retained core.
Contractible Does Not Mean Rel Everything

A Global Contraction May Move The Chosen Point

Theorem 5.19
Contractible spaces have the homotopy type of a point, hence are simply connected.
Caveat
A contraction \(1_X\simeq c_p\) may be forced to move \(p\). Homotopy and homotopy rel a subset are different requirements.
  • Do not replace every homotopy by a basepoint-fixed homotopy.
  • Contractible examples can still be geometrically subtle.
  • For \(\pi_1\), the loop endpoints remain pinned throughout.
Contractibility caveatOriginal SVG schematic
Use this slide to slow down a common overreach. Contractible implies simply connected, but the homotopy need not keep a chosen point fixed throughout. The comb-style schematic represents how a space can shrink globally while resisting a basepoint-relative contraction.

Application 3

A Surface Point Is Not Both Interior And Boundary

Theorem 5.23
The interior and boundary of a surface are disjoint.
  • Assume one point has both a plane chart and a half-plane chart.
  • Use the overlap to produce a punctured half-disc containing a circle.
  • Radial projection would retract a contractible punctured half-disc onto that circle.
Contradiction
The source group is trivial, but the target circle has \(\pi_1\cong\mathbb{Z}\). The required onto map cannot exist.
Boundary of a surfaceArtifact: surface-boundary-obstruction.png
The boundary theorem uses the same obstruction pattern locally. If a point were both boundary and interior, a punctured half-disc would retract onto a circle. The punctured half-disc is contractible, so its trivial fundamental group cannot map onto the circle group.
