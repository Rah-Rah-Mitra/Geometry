The Equivalence Principle Has A Curvature Remainder

Local cancellation

At a chosen event, a freely falling observer can make ordinary gravitational acceleration disappear from the frame.

What remains

Neighboring free-fall paths do not stay perfectly parallel. Their relative acceleration is measurable.

Geometric Reading

gravity at a point can be framed away; tidal curvature cannot.

Pedagogical target

The equivalence principle points toward curvature because it leaves behind a second-order comparison.

equivalence-principle motivationLocal flatness versus tidal evidence
Use this slide to separate local cancellation from tidal evidence. A freely falling observer can choose a local frame where ordinary gravitational acceleration disappears at a point. That does not make gravity a coordinate illusion. Neighboring falling particles still accelerate relative to one another, and that relative acceleration is what curvature records. This is the first bridge from physical intuition to the geometric language of the course.

The Metric Draws Causal Geometry

Lorentzian Signature

$$ds^2=-c^2dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2$$

Timelike

possible massive worldlines

Null

light cone boundary

Spacelike

outside causal reach

Invariant

causal sorting survives coordinates

Metric role

The sign pattern is not a convention on a page. It is the local rule that tells clocks, light rays, and observers what counts as a possible direction.

spacetime metric and diagramsSignature is geometry, not decoration
Make the metric signature concrete before introducing tensors. In Euclidean geometry every nonzero direction has positive squared length. In spacetime the Lorentzian sign split partitions tangent directions into timelike, null, and spacelike. The light cone is not just a drawing of light. It is the metric's local sorting rule for possible causal motion, and changing coordinates must preserve that sorting.

Free Fall Means Geodesic Motion

Transport statement

A free particle carries its velocity vector along its own worldline without external forcing.

Coordinate Form

$$\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}=0$$

Same course idea

Parallel transport is now being used in spacetime. The connection tells how local inertial frames change from event to event.

Reading tip

Christoffel symbols are not a new force. They are the coordinate expression of the metric's comparison rule.

geodesics as free fallConnection replaces force language
Now translate force language into geometry. A free particle follows a worldline whose tangent vector is transported along itself. In coordinates this becomes the geodesic equation, where the Christoffel symbols express how the coordinate grid itself changes. Emphasize that this is the same transport idea used earlier, but the path now lives in spacetime rather than on a two-dimensional surface.

Tidal Stretching Reads The Riemann Tensor

What the image measures

A small ball of free-falling test particles deforms into an ellipsoid when neighboring geodesics accelerate differently.

Geodesic Deviation

$$\frac{D^2\xi^\mu}{d\tau^2}=-R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}u^\nu\xi^\alpha u^\beta$$

Objects in the formula

\(\xi\) is the separation vector, \(u\) is the reference velocity, and \(R\) is the curvature tensor.

Inspection target

Gravity becomes visible through relative acceleration, not through a single particle's absolute acceleration.

gravitational tidal forcesCurvature as a measurement on neighboring geodesics
The local figure provides the physical diagnostic. The ellipsoid is a bundle of nearby free-fall trajectories. If all points had the same acceleration, the bundle would translate without deforming. Stretching and squeezing mean neighboring geodesics have different relative accelerations. The geodesic deviation equation is the compact geometric statement: curvature acts on the separation vector and the velocity of the reference worldline.

Stress-Energy Is The Source Ledger

Matter Field Content

$$T_{\mu\nu}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho & \text{flux}\\ \text{momentum} & \text{stress}\end{bmatrix}$$

Energy density

How much energy an observer measures in a local volume.

Momentum and flux

How energy and momentum flow across local surfaces.

Pressure and shear

How matter pushes, pulls, and stores directional stress.

matter field equationSource term is more than mass
Introduce stress-energy as the bookkeeping tensor for matter and radiation, not as a mysterious add-on. Its components record energy density, momentum density, energy flux, and pressure or shear stress. This matters because general relativity does not let mass alone source gravity. The geometry responds to the full local flow of energy and momentum through spacetime.

Einstein's Equation Matches Two Tensors

$$G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$$

\(G\)

curvature summary built from the metric, Ricci curvature, and scalar curvature.

\(\Lambda\)

cosmological term: uniform curvature contribution at large scales.

\(T\)

stress-energy tensor: local matter, radiation, momentum flux, and pressure.

Tensor equality

The equation compares geometric and physical ledgers in a form every coordinate system must respect.

Einstein field equationCurvature side equals matter side
This is the central synthesis slide. The left side is a divergence-free curvature summary built from the metric and its derivatives. The right side is the stress-energy ledger scaled by constants. The equation says that spacetime geometry and physical content must be compared tensor to tensor, so every observer agrees about the geometric relation even when the component table changes.

Schwarzschild Geometry Is A Vacuum Test Case

Outside a spherical mass

The vacuum equation \(T_{\mu\nu}=0\) still admits curved geometry because the central mass sets boundary conditions.

Schwarzschild Line Element

$$ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}\right)c^2dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2$$

Horizon signal

The radial metric factor changes behavior at \(r=2GM/c^2\) in these coordinates.

Geometric lesson

A vacuum region can be Ricci-flat while still carrying curvature measured by tidal effects and geodesics.

Schwarzschild solution and early testsVacuum does not mean flat
Use Schwarzschild as the first named solution rather than as a computational burden. Outside a spherical nonrotating mass the stress-energy tensor is zero, yet curvature remains because the boundary conditions encode the central mass. The metric coefficient marks the horizon in these coordinates, and free-fall worldlines bend inside the causal structure drawn by the light cones.
