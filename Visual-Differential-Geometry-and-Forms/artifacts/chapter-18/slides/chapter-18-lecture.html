Topology LedgerChapter 18

The Euler Ledger Is Stable Under Refinement

Definition

\(\chi = V - E + F\)

Vertices, edges, and faces are not individually invariant; their alternating sum is.

Refine A Face

Add one interior vertex, connect it to a triangular face, and the ledger changes by \(+1-3+2=0\).

Teaching Point

The proof needs a quantity that survives drawing choices. Euler characteristic is that quantity.

A local refinement changes entries, not the alternating balance.

Euler characteristic as \(V-E+F\)Mesh-independent bookkeeping
Euler characteristic is the invariant that lets a purely combinatorial count remember the topology of a surface. The expression V minus E plus F is deliberately insensitive to how fine the mesh is. Refining a triangulation adds vertices, edges, and faces in compensating packages, so the ledger changes its entries without changing its balance.

Cauchy FlatteningChapter 18

Cauchy's Move: Remove One Face, Flatten The Rest

Cut

Open the polyhedron by removing a face and cutting along edges until it becomes a planar net.

Count

The planar net has a boundary, but its vertices, edges, and faces are now easy to enumerate.

Restore

Add the missing face back to recover the closed sphere ledger \(V-E+F=2\).

Flattening changes the picture, not the accounting logic.

Cauchy's proof by flattening polyhedraPolygonal nets
Cauchy's idea is to remove one face and flatten the rest into a polygonal net. The act of cutting changes the visible object dramatically, but it does not create new topological information. The net has boundary, so its ledger is easier to count, and the missing face is restored at the end.
Angular Excess LedgerChapter 18

Triangulate And Sum The Angle Errors

One Triangle

\(E_\Delta = \alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi\)

All Triangles

\(\sum_\Delta E_\Delta = \sum\hbox{all angles} - \pi F\)

Closed Surface

Interior vertex angles total full turns, and every edge is shared by two faces.

The proof works because every triangle contributes one \(-\pi\), while vertices contribute turns.

Angular-excess proofLocal angles to global count
For each geodesic triangle, angular excess is the sum of its three angles minus pi. Summing over all triangles converts face-by-face geometry into a vertex ledger. Interior angles around a regular interior vertex contribute a full turn, while edges are counted twice and faces once. This is the mechanism that turns geometry into V minus E plus F.
Legendre BookkeepingChapter 18

Legendre's Cancellation: Interior Data Disappears

Chapter artifact: object to visual to formula to check.

Local Object

A triangle has angle data attached to its corners and incidence data attached to its edges.

Pairwise Cancellation

Every interior edge belongs to two adjacent faces, so its boundary role is counted twice and cancels in the total ledger.

What Remains

The result depends on the global combinatorics, not the arbitrary route chosen through the mesh.

Legendre's proof of the polyhedral formulaInterior edge cancellation
Legendre's bookkeeping makes the cancellation explicit. The proof does not rely on a single heroic triangle. It relies on the fact that every shared interior edge is seen from two adjacent faces with opposite bookkeeping roles. Once the local entries cancel in pairs, the remaining total is controlled by the global count.

Adding a handle changes the surface type, and the Euler ledger detects it immediately. For an orientable closed surface of genus g, chi equals two minus two g. The angular-excess ledger visualizes the same statement after multiplying by two pi: sphere positive, torus zero, higher genus negative.
The TheoremChapter 18

The Smooth Limit Is Global Gauss-Bonnet

$$\int_M K\,dA = 2\pi\chi(M)$$

1. Triangulate

Choose a geodesic triangulation fine enough that each face is nearly local.

2. Approximate

Each triangle satisfies \(E_\Delta \approx \int_\Delta K\,dA\).

3. Sum And Limit

The exact angle ledger gives \(2\pi\chi\); refinement turns the sum into the integral.

Geometry Side

Total curvature is accumulated from infinitesimal angular excess.

Topology Side

The same accumulated excess equals a number unchanged by smooth deformation.

Angular-excess proof of global Gauss-BonnetTotal curvature equals topology
Now connect the discrete proof to the smooth theorem. For a fine geodesic triangulation, the excess of each small triangle approximates the integral of K over that triangle. Summing over all faces approximates the total curvature, while the exact angle bookkeeping gives two pi chi. Passing to the refinement limit gives the global Gauss-Bonnet theorem.
Recap And CheckChapter 18

What To Remember

Two Ledgers

Angular excess records local geometry; \(V-E+F\) records topology.

One Agreement

After summing and refining, the ledgers agree: \(\int_M K\,dA=2\pi\chi(M)\).

Instructor Exercise

Triangulate a torus and explain why the total angular excess must be zero, even when \(K\) is not zero pointwise.

The theorem is not magic: it is cancellation plus a limiting argument.
