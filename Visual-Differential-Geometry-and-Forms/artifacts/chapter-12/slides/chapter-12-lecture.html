The Gauss Map: Send Each Point To Its Normal

Definition

For an oriented surface $S\subset\mathbb{R}^3$, the Gauss map is

$$N:S\longrightarrow S^2,\qquad p\mapsto N(p).$$

What Changes

A point moving on the surface traces a curve of unit normal vectors on the sphere.

What This Uses

The measurement depends on the embedding in space, so it is extrinsic at the point of construction.

Gauss or spherical mapNormals as data
Define the Gauss, or spherical, map as the rule assigning to each surface point its unit normal vector. Emphasize that the target is the unit sphere, not another copy of the surface. This is why extrinsic curvature sees how the surface sits in space: the measurement changes when the normal field changes.

Curvature As Signed Area Distortion

Gaussian Curvature By The Gauss Map

$$K(p)=\lim_{A\to p}\frac{\operatorname{area}(N(A))}{\operatorname{area}(A)}$$

The oriented version includes a sign from whether the normal map preserves local orientation.

Tiny Patch Logic

A nearly flat patch sends its normals to a tiny spherical patch. A sharply bending patch sweeps more area on the sphere.

Sign Matters

The same area size with reversed orientation gives the opposite sign of curvature.

Area distortion under the spherical mapSigned local ratio
Move from a single normal vector to a tiny surface patch. The Gauss map sends the patch to a patch on the sphere. Gaussian curvature is the limiting signed ratio of spherical image area to surface area. The sign is not cosmetic; it records whether the normal map preserves or reverses local orientation.

Principal Curvatures: Extremal Normal Bends

Directional Normal Curvature

At a fixed point, each tangent direction has a normal curvature: how a normal section bends in that direction.

Principal Curvatures

The maximum and minimum directional normal curvatures are $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$, with orthogonal principal directions.

The Compact Formula

$$K=\kappa_1\kappa_2,\qquad H=\frac{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}{2}$$

Extrinsic curvature as product of principal curvaturesShape operator viewpoint
At a point, every tangent direction has a normal curvature. The two extremal values are the principal curvatures kappa one and kappa two, with orthogonal principal directions. Gaussian curvature is their product, so the chapter turns the Gauss-map area ratio into the compact formula K equals kappa one times kappa two.

Reading The Sign Of $K$

$K>0$

Principal bends have the same sign: locally dome-like or cup-like.

$K<0$

Principal bends have opposite signs: locally saddle-like.

$K=0$

At least one principal bend vanishes: parabolic or planar behavior.

Local

The label belongs to a point or small patch, not automatically to the entire surface.

Common Misread

Positive does not mean "above" and negative does not mean "below." The sign comes from the product of two normal bends and the orientation of the normal map.

Elliptic, hyperbolic, parabolic, planarLocal shape classification
The sign catalog separates elliptic, hyperbolic, parabolic, and planar behavior. Positive K means both principal bends have the same sign; negative K means opposite signs; zero K means at least one principal bend vanishes. This is a local classification, not a statement about the whole surface.

Product Versus Average: $K$ And $H$

Two Summaries Of The Same Pair

$$K=\kappa_1\kappa_2,\qquad H=\frac{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}{2}$$

Gaussian Curvature

Multiplicative: measures signed area distortion of the Gauss map.

Mean Curvature

Additive: measures an averaged bending tendency, central in minimal-surface behavior.

Why Distinguish Them

A surface can have $H=0$ while $K<0$. Averaging and multiplying answer different geometric questions.

Principal product versus mean curvatureDifferent summaries, different meanings
Contrast Gaussian curvature with mean curvature. K equals the product of the principal curvatures and measures area distortion of the Gauss map; H equals their average and measures a different bending tendency. A saddle can have zero mean curvature while still having negative Gaussian curvature.

Takeaways

1. Normals Become A Map

The Gauss map $N:S\to S^2$ packages how the surface sits in space.

2. Curvature Is Area Distortion

$K$ is the limiting signed area ratio between a surface patch and its spherical normal image.

3. Principal Curvatures Compress It

The same measurement is $K=\kappa_1\kappa_2$, so local shape is read from the two principal bends.

4. Sign Predicts Shape

Positive, negative, and zero Gaussian curvature correspond to elliptic, hyperbolic, and parabolic or planar local behavior.
