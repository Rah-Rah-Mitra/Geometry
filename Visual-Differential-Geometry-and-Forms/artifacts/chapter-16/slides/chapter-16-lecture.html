Introduction to the Global Gauss-Bonnet Theorem

How can the total curvature of a closed surface remember only its topology?

The chapter question

Move from pointwise bending to a whole-surface invariant: add every signed bit of Gaussian curvature and compare the result with Euler characteristic.

Destination

$$\int_M K\,dA=2\pi\chi(M)$$

What survives

The shape may flex, bulge, or wrinkle, but for a closed surface the integral is locked to genus.

The accounting problemChapter 16

From Local Curvature to a Global Ledger

Local entry

At each point, \(K=k_1k_2\) records signed Gaussian curvature.

Global total

The surface ledger is \(\displaystyle \int_M K\,dA\), summing positive and negative contributions.

What cancellation means

A zero total does not imply every point is flat. It may mean the positive and negative entries balance exactly.

Local measurement becomes a whole-surface invariant.\(K\,dA\)
Use this slide to slow down the bookkeeping. Gaussian curvature is still the pointwise product of principal curvatures, but the global theorem asks for the signed area under that function over the whole surface. The crucial shift is that positive and negative patches are allowed to cancel before topology enters.

Euler characteristic

For genus \(g\)

\(\chi(M)=2-2g\), so \(\displaystyle \int_M K\,dA=4\pi(1-g)\).

No boundary term here

This introductory form is the clean closed-surface version. Boundary and corner corrections belong to the more general theorem.

Read the equality both ways

Topology predicts total curvature; a computed total curvature audits the claimed topology.

First calibrationChapter 16

Calibrating on the Sphere

Unit sphere

\(K=1\), so \(\displaystyle \int_{S^2}K\,dA=\operatorname{area}(S^2)=4\pi\).

Gauss-Bonnet check

\(2\pi\chi(S^2)=2\pi\cdot 2=4\pi\).

Spherical excess preview

The same normalization makes spherical triangles carry area through angle excess, a local hint of the global theorem.

Artifact: sphere-lune-spherical-map.pngTotal curvature \(4\pi\)
Use the spherical map figure to make the normalization feel inevitable. On the unit sphere, Gaussian curvature is one everywhere, so total curvature is just spherical area, four pi. That matches two pi times Euler characteristic two, giving a first calibration before more subtle cancellation examples.
Zero total, not zero curvatureChapter 16

Torus: Cancellation Without Flatness

Prediction

$$\chi(T^2)=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \int_{T^2}K\,dA=0$$

Outside bands

Parts of an embedded torus bend like a sphere and contribute positive curvature.

Inside bands

The inner saddle-like region contributes negative curvature. The integral cancels exactly.

Genus \(g=1\)\(\int K\,dA=0\)
This slide prevents a common misconception. The torus has total curvature zero, but that does not mean it is flat as an embedded surface. Positive curvature on the outside and negative curvature on the inside cancel in the integral, which is exactly what Euler characteristic zero predicts.
Handle bookkeepingChapter 16

Handles Change the Curvature Budget

Adding one handle

\(g\mapsto g+1\) changes \(\chi\) by \(-2\).

Gauss-Bonnet consequence

\(\Delta\!\left(\int_M K\,dA\right)=2\pi(-2)=-4\pi\).

Interpretation

A handle is not just a local dent. It changes the global accounting rule the entire closed surface must satisfy.

Artifact: pancake-hole-curvature-budget.pngEach handle costs \(4\pi\)
The pancake-and-hole figure makes the sign bookkeeping tactile. Every added handle lowers Euler characteristic by two, so the total curvature decreases by four pi. Speak of handles as changing the accounting rule, not as tiny local defects, because the theorem constrains the whole closed surface.
Conceptual bridgeChapter 16

Gauss Map Degree

Bridge formula

Curvature area measures signed area swept by the normal map.

Degree intuition

\(\displaystyle \int_M K\,dA = 4\pi\,\deg(N)\) in the normal-map picture.

Why topology enters

Degree is stable under continuous deformations. That stability is the topological shadow behind the curvature integral.

Topological degree of the spherical map\(N:M\to S^2\)
Introduce the Gauss map as the bridge between geometry and topology. A normal direction at each point of the surface lands on the unit sphere. Counting signed covering of the sphere gives a degree, and the curvature integral measures that signed covering area. This is the conceptual route into later proofs.
SynthesisChapter 16

What the Theorem Lets Us Predict

Given topology

For a closed genus \(g\) surface, total curvature is fixed before a metric is chosen: \(4\pi(1-g)\).

Given geometry

A measured \(\int K\,dA\) audits which Euler characteristic a closed surface could have.

Given a drawing

Positive and negative curvature patches must balance to the correct total, regardless of visual complexity.

Quick check

A closed orientable surface has three handles. What must its total Gaussian curvature be?

Answer route: \(g=3\), so \(\chi=-4\), hence \(\int K\,dA=-8\pi\).
