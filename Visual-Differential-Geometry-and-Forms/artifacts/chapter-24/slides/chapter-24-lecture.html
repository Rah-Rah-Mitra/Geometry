Holonomy

Curvature made visible as the returned angle after parallel transport around a loop

The measurement

Carry a tangent vector around an oriented loop $L$. Compare the returned vector with the initial vector in the same tangent plane.

Returned angle

$$R(L)=\angle(v_{\mathrm{start}},v_{\mathrm{return}})$$

Curvature accumulated

For nearly constant Gaussian curvature on the enclosed region, $R(L)\approx K\,\mathrm{area}(L)$.

printed pp. 245-251Holonomy as a loop meter
Open by making holonomy a measurement, not a mysterious abstraction. The loop is oriented, the tangent vector is transported all the way around, and only when it returns to the starting tangent plane do we compare the teal and orange arrows. For the notebook's model loop, area is about 4.924, curvature is 0.34, and the returned angle is about 1.674 radians, so the class can see R(L) behaving like curvature accumulated over area.

The Measurement Rules

Not path turning

A boundary tangent may visibly turn because the boundary turns. Holonomy measures the transported probe after it completes the loop.

Not distant comparison

Vectors at different surface points live in different tangent planes. The comparison is made only after returning to the basepoint.

On geodesic sides

The transported vector keeps a constant angle with each geodesic edge.

Why triangles work

For geodesic triangles, the returned angle can be read from the exterior-turn ledger.

printed pp. 245-251How to read transport diagrams
This slide prevents the main misreading. Path arrows show traversal order, while tangent arrows show the transported probe. The vector is not compared pointwise with distant tangent planes; it is carried by the connection and compared only at the basepoint. Along geodesic sides, the transported vector keeps a constant angle with the side, which is why later triangle arguments can become angle bookkeeping.

Sphere Octant: Positive Curvature, Positive Holonomy

Great-circle loop

The octant triangle goes from the north pole to longitude $0$, then to longitude $\pi/2$, then back to the north pole.

Unit sphere check

$$R=\mathrm{area}=\mathrm{excess}=\frac{\pi}{2}$$

Inspection target

The green start tangent and orange returned tangent at $p$ differ by exactly the spherical excess.

printed pp. 245-251Spherical triangle holonomy
Use the unit sphere octant as the first exact computation. The loop follows three great-circle edges, each interior angle is pi over two, and the spherical excess is pi over two. The notebook's transport calculation returns the same angle, so this is the clean positive-curvature case: holonomy equals area equals angular excess.

The Geodesic Triangle Ledger

Keep the angle on each edge

Along a geodesic edge, parallel transport preserves the angle between the probe vector and that edge.

Ledger identity

$$R(\Delta)=2\pi-(\phi_1+\phi_2+\phi_3)$$

$$=\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3-\pi$$

Meaning

The returned angle is the triangle's angular excess, with sign.

Symbolic check

SymPy simplifies the difference between the two expressions to $0$.

printed pp. 245-251Angle defect as holonomy
The ledger turns the geometry into a compact identity. If phi_i are the exterior turns of the geodesic sides, then the returned rotation is 2 pi minus their sum. Substituting phi_i equals pi minus theta_i gives theta_1 plus theta_2 plus theta_3 minus pi. The point is that holonomy is angle defect, not the visible bending of the drawn boundary.

Hyperbolic Sign Test: Defect Means Negative Holonomy

Angle sum below $\pi$

In the upper-half-plane model, geodesics are vertical lines or semicircles orthogonal to the boundary. The model preserves angles, and this triangle has angle sum $2.55071$ rad.

Curvature $K=-1$

$$R=\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3-\pi=-0.590882$$

Area relation

The positive defect area is $0.590882$, so $R=-\mathrm{area}$.

printed pp. 245-251Hyperbolic holonomy
Now the same triangle logic runs in negative curvature. In the upper-half-plane model the drawn angles are conformal, so the Euclidean-looking vertex angles are the hyperbolic angles, but area is hyperbolic area. The sample angle sum is about 2.55071 radians, below pi, giving defect area 0.590882 and returned angle R equals negative 0.590882.

Additivity: Internal Edges Cancel

Subdivide

Inserted edges appear twice with opposite orientations.

Frame cancellation

$$T_e^{-1}T_e=I,\qquad R(L_1)+R(L_2)=R(L)$$

Numerical residual

Sphere subareas add to the whole with error $2.22\times 10^{-16}$.

printed pp. 245-251Additivity over regions
Additivity is a proof mechanism rather than a new example. When a region is subdivided, every inserted internal edge is traversed once in each direction. Transport forward and then backward along that same edge composes to the identity, so the only uncanceled transport is on the outer boundary. The notebook's sphere check has subareas summing to the whole area with residual near 2.22 times 10 to the minus 16.

Curvature From Small Loops

Limit diagnostic

$$K(p)=\lim_{L\to p}\frac{R(L)}{\mathrm{area}(L)}$$

Large loop

$R/\mathrm{area}$ blends curvature over the enclosed region.

Small loop

Shrinking the loop isolates the Gaussian curvature at the basepoint.

printed pp. 245-251Infinitesimal holonomy
This slide connects holonomy to Gaussian curvature itself. For large loops on variable-curvature surfaces, R divided by area is an average-like quantity. Shrink the loop around a point and the ratio approaches K at that point. The notebook's constant-curvature samples are intentionally simple: the sphere ratios sit at plus one and the hyperbolic ratios sit at minus one.

Takeaways: Holonomy Is Curvature With a Needle

Sphere

$R=+\mathrm{area}$ on the unit sphere.

Hyperbolic plane

$R=-\mathrm{area}$ when $K=-1$.

General surface

For small loops, $R/\mathrm{area}$ samples $K(p)$.

One sentence to keep

Holonomy is the angle defect accumulated by parallel transport, and additivity lets that angle behave like the integral of curvature.

printed pp. 245-251Synthesis
Close by threading the chapter together. Holonomy starts as a returned-angle measurement, exact geodesic triangles turn it into angular excess or defect, subdivision makes the measurement additive, and shrinking loops turns the returned angle per area into Gaussian curvature. The final slogan is that curvature can be detected by carrying a tangent vector around a loop and reading the angle it brings back.
