Intrinsic Constructions

How can transport be defined without leaning on the surrounding space?

The Question

Can an inhabitant move tangent arrows using only surface measurements?

The Strategy

Replace ambient projection with tiny geodesic moves, a comparison rule, and a limit.

The Payoff

The same rule prepares holonomy: transport around a loop becomes a curvature probe.

The transport rule must be measurable from inside the surface.

Intrinsic MeasurementChapter 23

Allowed Data: What The Surface Itself Supplies

Intrinsic Rule

A construction is intrinsic when every step can be stated with tangent vectors, the metric, charts, geodesics, and limits inside the surface.

Geodesics

Supply the smallest straight moves available to a surface inhabitant.

Charts

Give coordinates for calculation, but the final vector must not depend on the chosen labels.

The construction is a closed loop of surface-native operations.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Intrinsic Data Contract
This slide separates geometric information from convenient scenery. The metric tells us lengths and angles in each tangent plane, geodesics tell us which small moves count as straight, and charts give labels for computation. The forbidden move is to decide transport by looking from outside the surface and projecting by eye. That projection can be useful as a verification bridge, but not as the intrinsic definition.
Geodesic MovesChapter 23

The Geodesic Ladder Replaces Ambient Projection

Chapter artifact: a ladder of geodesic steps replaces ambient projection.

One Rung

Use a small geodesic quadrilateral to move a vector from one tangent plane to the next.

Refinement

Repeat along the curve and shrink the step. The finite ladder converges to transport.

Finite Approximation

step size \(h\) small: transported vector error is higher order in \(h\)

What To Inspect

The straight pieces are geodesics. No normal vector in the surrounding space is needed.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Geodesic Ladder Construction
Introduce the geodesic ladder as a finite construction that only asks for geodesic segments. Starting at a point with a tangent vector, step along the base curve, close a tiny geodesic quadrilateral, and read the transported vector from the next side. Emphasize that each rung is approximate, but shrinking the step size converts the ladder into a transport rule independent of the chosen drawing.
Limiting ComparisonChapter 23

First Transport Back, Then Subtract

The Subtraction Problem

\(V(t+h)\) and \(V(t)\) live in different tangent planes, so ordinary subtraction is not defined.

Covariant Difference Quotient

$$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V(t)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{P_{t+h\to t}V(t+h)-V(t)}{h}$$

The Repair

\(P_{t+h\to t}\) transports the later vector back into \(T_{\gamma(t)}M\), where comparison makes sense.

The stable value appears only after the geometric comparison has been normalized.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Covariant Derivative Limit
The covariant derivative begins with a subtle subtraction problem: vectors based at different points do not live in the same vector space. The limit formula solves this by transporting the later vector back to the earlier tangent plane before subtracting. The local convergence figure helps students see why first-order comparisons stabilize even when the visible chord and arc measurements disagree at finite scale.
Tangent ChangeChapter 23

The Covariant Derivative Measures Intrinsic Change

A derivative of vector fields must compare arrows after bringing them into one tangent plane.

Parallel Along A Curve

$$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V=0$$

No intrinsic change remains after transport compensation.

If It Is Zero

The field is being carried as parallel as the surface allows.

If It Is Nonzero

The result is tangent data: it lives in the same tangent plane as the original vector.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Intrinsic Derivative
Now state the conceptual definition. A vector field along a curve has covariant derivative zero precisely when its successive values agree after intrinsic transport. If the derivative is nonzero, it measures the remaining tangent-plane change after the base-point motion has been accounted for. The diagram deliberately shows two tangent planes, because the operation is about comparing data after moving it into one common plane.
Correction To Stay TangentChapter 23

Ordinary Derivatives See Too Much

Chapter artifact: object to visual to formula to check.

Temptation

Differentiate the arrow in ambient coordinates and call that the change.

Problem

The ordinary derivative can point partly off the surface, so it is not purely tangent information.

Embedded Check

$$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V=\left(\frac{dV}{dt}\right)^{\top}$$

Projection verifies the intrinsic derivative when an embedding is available.

Teaching Point

The projection formula is a bridge for intuition, not the definition required by intrinsic geometry.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Ordinary Versus Intrinsic Derivative
Use the ordinary derivative as the tempting but wrong first guess. Differentiating an embedded vector field in ambient space generally produces a component normal to the surface, which is not tangent data. Projecting back to the tangent plane gives the embedded formula for the Levi-Civita derivative, but the slide stresses that this is a check on the intrinsic rule, not the rule's source.
Coordinate FormulaChapter 23

Charts Are Bookkeeping, Not Geometry

Component Formula

$$\left(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V\right)^k=\frac{dV^k}{dt}+\Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^iV^j$$

Raw Component Change

\(\frac{dV^k}{dt}\) tracks how the list of coordinates changes.

Basis Correction

\(\Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^iV^j\) tracks how the coordinate basis changes along the curve.

Invariant Output

The sum transforms as a tangent vector, not as an arbitrary list.

Christoffel symbols correct the basis drift introduced by a chosen chart.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Charts And Transport
Coordinates enter only after the intrinsic object is defined. In a chart, the correction terms Gamma record how the coordinate basis itself changes as the point moves. This lets students interpret the formula without treating Christoffel symbols as mysterious forces: they are bookkeeping coefficients that make the component derivative transform like a tangent vector rather than like a list of unrelated numbers.
Holonomy PreviewChapter 23

Transport Around A Loop Detects Curvature

Chapter artifact: transport around a loop measures curvature.

Experiment

Carry a tangent vector around a small loop using the same intrinsic transport rule.

Observation

The vector may return rotated. That failure to close is holonomy.

Small-Loop Reading

$$\text{rotation angle}\approx K\,\text{area}$$

Bridge Forward

Curvature becomes a measured effect of transport, not merely a symbol in a formula.

Printed pages 240-244; PDF pages 269-273Holonomy And Curvature
Close the main arc by returning to loops. If an intrinsic transport rule carries a vector around a small loop and the vector returns rotated, the rotation is holonomy, and it previews curvature as a measured failure of transport to close. The point is operational: curvature is not first introduced as a tensor entry, but as the result of a repeatable experiment with arrows and small geodesic moves.
