Use this route slide as a contract with the audience. The proof is not a black-box integration trick. It is a migration of one idea: count how a direction map covers its target. First the target is the circle of tangent directions for a plane loop. Then the same count becomes normal direction on a surface, and finally the count becomes Euler characteristic through cell-like bookkeeping.
Hopf's Umlaufsatz

Total Turning Is a Degree

plane loop
Turning theorem
For a positively oriented simple closed regular plane curve, the unit tangent direction has degree one.
Measurement
\(T(s)=\gamma'(s)/|\gamma'(s)|,\quad T:S^1\to S^1\)
\(\displaystyle \int_\gamma k_g\,ds = 2\pi\,\deg(T)\)

The formula says that curvature is not just local bending. Integrated around a closed curve, it records how many times the direction field turns.

Slow down on the map \(T:S^1\to S^1\). The domain circle is the curve parameter after one full trip; the target circle is the set of possible unit tangent directions. The integral of signed curvature measures angular velocity of this target point. Hopf's statement is therefore a degree statement: for a simple positive loop, the tangent direction makes one full positive lap.
What Deformation Changes

Inflections Redistribute Curvature, Not Degree

signed curvature ledger
Interpretation
A deformed circle may bend clockwise on some arcs and counterclockwise on others. Those local signs affect the integrand, but the full tangent map still closes with the same winding number.
+ / -local curvature sign can vary
\(2\pi\)total turning for a simple positive loop
\(\deg T\)integer preserved by regular homotopy
0forbidden value of \(|\gamma'|\)
This is the place to prevent a common misconception: positive curvature everywhere is not the invariant. A circle has that convenient property, but deformations can introduce arcs with the opposite sign. The invariant is the total angular change of the tangent direction. As long as the tangent vector never vanishes and the map remains continuous, the degree cannot slide continuously from one integer to another.
Heuristic Proof Mechanism

The Integer Changes Only Through a Bad Event

regular homotopy
Proof roadmap
  1. Turn a curve into its unit tangent map \(T_t:S^1\to S^1\).
  2. Let the curve deform through regular curves, so \(T_t\) varies continuously.
  3. Use discreteness of degree: a continuous path in \(\mathbb Z\) is constant.
Where the proof can fail
A cusp, zero velocity, or discontinuous tangent map removes the target-circle degree. The heuristic proof must keep these events outside the deformation.
Present this slide as the logical core of the chapter's first half. We are not claiming every drawing preserves the invariant by visual resemblance. We are claiming that a regular deformation gives a continuous family of maps into the circle, and degree is integer-valued. The only way to change it is to pass through a state where the tangent map is not defined or no longer belongs to the same class.
From Curves to Surfaces

The Normal Map Replaces the Tangent Map

Gauss map
Gauss map
For an oriented surface in \(\mathbb R^3\), the unit normal defines \(N:M\to S^2\).
Local-to-global bridge
\(\displaystyle N^*(dA_{S^2}) = K\,dA_M\)
Gaussian curvature is the signed area distortion of the normal map.

The curve theorem counted how tangent directions cover \(S^1\). The surface argument asks how normal directions cover \(S^2\).

Make the analogy explicit. For curves, the tangent map sends each point of the loop to a direction on the unit circle, and signed curvature is angular change. For surfaces, the Gauss map sends each point to a unit normal on the sphere, and Gaussian curvature is the signed area distortion of that map. This is the geometric reason an integral of \(K\,dA\) can count covering behavior.
Heuristic Global Step

Total Curvature Counts Topology

closed oriented surface
\(\displaystyle \int_M K\,dA = 2\pi\,\chi(M)\)
Sphere
\(\chi=2,\quad \int K\,dA=4\pi\)

The normal directions cover the unit sphere once.

Torus
\(\chi=0,\quad \int K\,dA=0\)

Positive and negative curvature contributions cancel.

Higher genus
\(\chi=2-2g\)

Handles reduce the total signed curvature by \(4\pi\) each.

State the global theorem as the destination of the analogy, but keep the word heuristic honest. The chapter's route is visual: total curvature is the signed area swept out by normals, and the global count depends on the surface's topological type. The notebook ledger records the sample values \(4\pi\), \(0\), \(-4\pi\), and \(-8\pi\) for Euler characteristics \(2\), \(0\), \(-2\), and \(-4\).
Why Euler Characteristic Appears

Local Turning Defects Add Like a Ledger

vertices, edges, faces
Cancellation pattern
Interior edge contributions are seen twice with opposite orientations. What survives is a vertex defect ledger, and the combinatorics of that ledger is \(V-E+F\).
Teaching point
The proof is global because repeated local turn measurements cancel in pairs, leaving a topological residue rather than a metric detail.
This slide explains why the answer is not merely a normal-map area. When a surface is decomposed into small pieces, each boundary has its own turning contribution. Shared edges are traversed in opposite directions by adjacent pieces, so their boundary bookkeeping cancels. What remains is concentrated at corners or defects, and the surviving count has the Euler-characteristic pattern \(V-E+F\).

Recap

The Heuristic Proof in Four Sentences

carry forward
1. Curves carry a degree.The unit tangent map \(T:S^1\to S^1\) turns total curvature into a winding number.
2. Degree resists deformation.A regular deformation moves the map continuously, while the degree remains an integer.
3. Surfaces use normals.The Gauss map \(N:M\to S^2\) turns \(K\,dA\) into signed target-sphere area.
4. Topology is the residue.Local boundary turns cancel in pairs, leaving \(2\pi\chi(M)\) as the global total.

The chapter's diagnostic habit: identify the representation, identify the invariant, then identify the forbidden event.

Close by compressing the proof without pretending it is fully rigorous. The students should leave with the analogy between tangent degree and normal-map degree, plus the cancellation habit that makes Euler characteristic appear. The final sentence is the reusable method for later differential geometry: representations can bend and change coordinates, but a valid invariant survives exactly because the proof has excluded the events that would destroy it.
