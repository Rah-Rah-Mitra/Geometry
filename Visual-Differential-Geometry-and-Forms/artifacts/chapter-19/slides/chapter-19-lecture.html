The New Counting Object

A tangent vector field may be smooth almost everywhere, yet forced to vanish at isolated points.

The Global Payoff

The sum of these local integers becomes the Euler characteristic, then total curvature.

Open the lecture by naming the chapter's shift in viewpoint. The earlier proofs measured curvature by angle excess and boundary turning. This proof asks a different question: if a tangent vector field is forced to vanish at a few points, can the total failure of the field to be nonsingular recover the same topological number?
Local invariantChapter 19

Index Is Local Winding

Definition

For an isolated zero $p$ of a plane field $X$, choose a small positively oriented loop $\gamma$ around $p$ with no other zeros.

Index

$$\operatorname{ind}_p(X)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_\gamma \arg X$$

Stable Under Deformation

Change the loop or gently perturb the field without crossing a zero, and the integer stays fixed.

isolated zerowinding degree of $X/|X|:\gamma\to S^1$
Pause on the word local. The index of an isolated zero is not the length of the arrows or the shape of the surrounding loop. It is the net number of turns made by the field direction while the loop travels once around the zero. The field can wiggle, but if the loop does not cross another zero, the winding count cannot change.
Plane modelsChapter 19

Model Singularities: Sources, Sinks, Saddles, Powers

Plus-One Behavior

Radial source and sink fields rotate once as the loop rotates once.

Minus-One Behavior

A saddle reverses the directional sweep, contributing a negative local count.

Degree, Not Mood

Attracting, repelling, and shearing impressions are secondary; the invariant is a signed winding number.

Mental model

complex powers: $z^k$ turn directions $k$ times

Use the figure as a catalog of test cases. A source and a sink both contribute plus one because the direction turns once positively around the zero. A saddle contributes minus one because the direction unwinds. Higher power models make clear that index is a signed degree, not a visual impression of whether the field looks attractive or repulsive.
Operational measurementChapter 19

The Loop Index Is an Angle Meter

Sample

Move around a small loop and record the field angle $\theta(t)=\arg X(\gamma(t))$.

Unwrap

Remove artificial jumps at $\pm\pi$ so the angle becomes a continuous trace.

Read the integer

$$\operatorname{ind}=\frac{\theta(1)-\theta(0)}{2\pi}$$

Surface fieldsChapter 19

From Plane Zeros to Surface Ledgers

Chart independence

In a local oriented chart, a surface field looks planar. The integer index survives orientation-preserving coordinate changes.

Many Zeros

A field may have singularities $p_1,\ldots,p_m$; each receives a local index.

Total index

$$I(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{ind}_{p_j}(X)$$

Bookkeeping Principle

Moving the field can split or merge zeros, but the total ledger is the quantity to watch.

surface vector fieldslocal indices become a ledger
Now transfer the local invariant to a surface. In a coordinate chart the field is still a planar vector field, but changing charts preserves the integer index because orientation-preserving coordinate changes do not alter the degree. The important bookkeeping move is that the surface can distribute its obstruction across many isolated zeros.
The topological bridgeChapter 19

Poincare-Hopf Turns Local Counts Into Topology

Theorem

For a compact oriented surface $M$ and a tangent vector field $X$ with isolated zeros,

$$\sum_p \operatorname{ind}_p(X)=\chi(M).$$

Independence

The left side uses a chosen field; the right side belongs to the surface.

Consequences

A sphere cannot support a nowhere-zero tangent field; a torus can.

Poincare-Hopf theoremlocal zeros count $\chi(M)$
State Poincare-Hopf as the bridge from local counts to topology. Any tangent vector field on a compact oriented surface with isolated zeros has total index equal to the Euler characteristic. Different vector fields may put zeros in different places, but the sum of their indices is pinned by the surface itself.

What Can Change?

The metric can redistribute positive and negative curvature. The integral remains tied to $\chi(M)$.

Gauss-Bonnet total curvature ledger$\int K\,dA=2\pi\chi(M)$
The ledger makes the theorem feel less mysterious. The sphere has Euler characteristic two and total curvature four pi. The torus has Euler characteristic zero and zero total curvature in the global theorem. Higher genus surfaces force negative total curvature. A metric can move curvature around, but the total stays locked to the same integer.

What the Vector-Field Proof Adds

Angle Excess Proof

Curvature is accumulated angular defect over geodesic polygons.

Boundary Turning Proof

Curvature balances geodesic curvature and corner turning along a boundary.

Vector-Field Proof

Curvature measures the obstruction to a nonsingular tangent direction.

Common endpoint

$$\int_M K\,dA=2\pi\chi(M)$$

Three visual routes land on the same global identity.

Next Door

The moving direction language prepares connection forms, holonomy, and parallel transport: curvature will be read as rotation after transport around loops.
