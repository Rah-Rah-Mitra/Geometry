Boundary DetectorChapter 36

Exterior Derivative As Boundary Detector

Operational Definition

For a tiny oriented \((p+1)\)-cell \(C\),

$$d\omega(C)\;\approx\;\frac{\int_{\partial C}\omega}{\operatorname{vol}(C)}.$$

What The Quotient Is Saying

  • Evaluate the old form on the boundary.
  • Use boundary orientation for signs.
  • Keep the first-order coefficient that survives.

Stokes Preview

The local statement scales up to \(\int_C d\omega=\int_{\partial C}\omega\) once cells tile a region and interior boundaries cancel.

Teaching Warning

The symbol \(d\) is not a coordinate gradient. It is the coordinate-free boundary-variation operator.

local cell modelboundary readings determine the next-degree form
Present d as a boundary detector rather than as a symbolic decoration. A p-form is evaluated on small p-dimensional probes. Its exterior derivative is evaluated on one-dimension-larger probes and records the first-order failure of the original form's boundary readings to cancel. This is the small-scale reason Stokes' theorem will later identify a bulk integral of d omega with a boundary integral of omega.
One-FormsChapter 36

\(d\) Of A One-Form: Circulation Cell

Local Formula In \(R^2\)

If \(\alpha=P(x,y)\,dx+Q(x,y)\,dy\), then

$$d\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dx\wedge dy.$$

Cell Reading

The top and bottom edges compare \(P\) as \(y\) varies. The right and left edges compare \(Q\) as \(x\) varies. Orientation subtracts the reversed comparisons.

Visual Target

The coefficient of \(dx\wedge dy\) is the signed circulation density around the cell.

Common Misread

Both partial derivatives matter because the form reads tangent directions along every edge, not only arrows inside the region.

\(\alpha=P\,dx+Q\,dy\)circulation density becomes a 2-form
Use the circulation cell to anchor the first formula. A one-form alpha equals P dx plus Q dy. Walk around a tiny coordinate rectangle and add the edge readings. The horizontal edges compare Q in the x direction and the vertical edges compare P in the y direction, with orientation deciding the signs. Dividing by area leaves Q_x minus P_y, the coefficient of dx wedge dy.
Coordinate PatternChapter 36

Coordinates: Variation Becomes The Next Form

Coefficient Rule

For a coordinate \(p\)-form \(\omega=\sum_I a_I\,dx^I\),

$$d\omega=\sum_{I,j}\frac{\partial a_I}{\partial x^j}\,dx^j\wedge dx^I.$$

One-Form To Two-Form

$$d(Pdx+Qdy)=\left(Q_x-P_y\right)dx\wedge dy.$$

Two-Form To Three-Form

$$d(P\,dy\,dz+Q\,dz\,dx+R\,dx\,dy)$$

$$=(P_x+Q_y+R_z)\,dx\,dy\,dz.$$

Algebraic Inspection

Each derivative contributes a new direction; wedges reorder that direction into the oriented basis and assign the sign.

coordinate ruledifferentiate coefficients, wedge directions, collect signs
This slide generalizes the local cell calculation. The exterior derivative differentiates each coefficient, wedges in the coordinate direction of variation, and then collects terms using alternation. For a one-form this produces the curl-like area reader. For a two-form in three dimensions it produces the divergence-like volume reader. The key teaching point is that d does not merely take derivatives; it upgrades the probe dimension.
Product RuleChapter 36

The Graded Product Rule

Exterior Leibniz Rule

For \(\alpha\in\Omega^p(M)\),

$$d(\alpha\wedge\beta)=d\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^p\,\alpha\wedge d\beta.$$

Where The Sign Comes From

The derivative of \(\beta\) has to move past the \(p\) slots of \(\alpha\). Each slot crossing contributes an orientation swap.

Misconception

The sign is not optional notation. It is the orientation cost of moving differential pieces past each other.

graded derivationdegree controls orientation bookkeeping
The product rule for forms must remember degree. When d passes across a p-form alpha to differentiate beta, it crosses p one-form slots, so the second term receives the sign minus one to the p. Students should see the sign as orientation bookkeeping, not as a convention to memorize. Geometric Translation

Taking a boundary twice leaves every lower-dimensional face counted twice with opposite orientations.

Assumption

The coordinate cancellation uses smooth coefficients, so mixed partial derivatives commute.

\(d^2=0\)mixed partials cancel, and boundaries cancel
Make d squared equals zero feel geometric before it feels algebraic. Algebraically, second partial derivatives appear in opposite wedge orders and cancel when the coefficients are smooth. Geometrically, the boundary of a boundary has no remaining edge: each codimension-two piece is visited twice with opposite orientation. This cancellation is the hinge between exact, closed, and later cohomological language.
Closed And ExactChapter 36

Closed Versus Exact

Two Adjectives

$$\omega\ \text{closed}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\omega=0.$$

$$\omega\ \text{exact}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \omega=d\eta.$$

Immediate Consequence

$$\omega=d\eta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\omega=d^2\eta=0.$$

Local Versus Global

Closed says the local boundary detector sees no variation. Exact says the form is globally a derivative of a lower-degree potential.

Topology Enters

On a domain with holes, a closed form can still carry a nonzero period around a noncontractible loop.

closed: \(d\omega=0\)exact: \(\omega=d\eta\)
Define the two adjectives through d. A form is closed when d omega equals zero, so the local boundary detector finds no first-order variation. A form is exact when omega is already d eta for some lower-degree form. Exact always implies closed because d squared is zero, but the reverse can fail globally when topology leaves noncontractible loops or holes for integrals to detect.
ApplicationChapter 36

Cauchy-Riemann As Closedness

Split \(f\,dz\)

For \(f=u+iv\),

$$f\,dz=(u\,dx-v\,dy)+i(v\,dx+u\,dy).$$

Closedness Gives Cauchy-Riemann

$$d(u\,dx-v\,dy)=(-v_x-u_y)\,dx\wedge dy,$$

$$d(v\,dx+u\,dy)=(u_x-v_y)\,dx\wedge dy.$$

Compatibility Equations

\(u_x=v_y\) and \(u_y=-v_x\) say the two boundary detectors vanish together.

Why It Belongs Here

Exterior differentiation turns a familiar analytic condition into a visible cancellation condition for oriented rectangles.

Cauchy-Riemann via formsclosed one-forms package compatibility
This is a compact application: complex differentiability can be translated into closed real one-forms. If f equals u plus i v, the real and imaginary parts of f dz are u dx minus v dy and v dx plus u dy. Requiring both to be closed gives u_x equals v_y and u_y equals minus v_x. Exterior differentiation packages the compatibility equations as a cancellation test.

2 to 3

0

Maxwell Compression

$$dF=0,\qquad d{*F}=J.$$

The Hodge star \(*\) uses the metric; the exterior derivative supplies the boundary law.

grad, curl, divMaxwell equations as exterior derivative statements
Use the ladder to connect familiar vector calculus to the forms sequence. With a metric and orientation in the background, d on zero-forms becomes gradient, d on one-forms becomes curl, and d on two-forms becomes divergence. Maxwell's compact equations use the same language: dF equals zero for the homogeneous equations, while d star F equals J after the Hodge star inserts the metric.
SynthesisChapter 36

Stokes-Style Takeaways

The Governing Picture

$$\int_U d\omega=\int_{\partial U}\omega.$$

What \(d\) Detects

The boundary readings of \(\omega\) fail to cancel exactly when the interior has a nonzero \(d\omega\) density.

Fast Example

For \(\alpha=-y\,dx+x\,dy\), \(d\alpha=2\,dx\wedge dy\). The disk's area reading equals the circulation density accumulated around its boundary.
