The Lab Grammar

1. Name the object

A loop is not a region; a field along a geodesic is not a free vector in one plane; a tensor is not just a table of numbers.

2. Draw the diagnostic

The visual should identify what can be inspected: returned angle, boundary turning, relative acceleration, or failed commutator.

3. Attach the formula

The formula becomes trustworthy only after the geometric role of each term is known.

4. State the check

Use a sign, symmetry, limiting ratio, or conservation law to catch a plausible wrong answer.

object -> visual -> formula -> checkA method for reading exercise sets
Use the lab map to give students a stable method. Every problem family should move from an object to a visual model, then to a formula, and finally to a check. This prevents the common exercise-set failure mode where a formula is applied before the geometric object is clear. The instructor can ask the room to name the object before revealing each formula.

Transport Is A Comparison Test

Transport equation

$$\nabla_T V=0$$

The derivative is judged in the tangent bundle, not in a fixed drawing plane.

Diagnostic prompt

What curve is used, what connection compares tangent spaces, and what returned direction should be expected?

Common failure

Sliding an arrow by eye ignores the tangent-plane change. The exercise must state the comparison rule.

Perturbation check

Change coordinates or resample the path. A correct transport rule changes components but preserves the geometric comparison.

parallel transport constructionCompare tangent data along a path
This slide makes parallel transport operational. A transported vector is not an arrow dragged across a drawing; it is a tangent vector compared by a connection along a path. The exercise diagnostic is to choose the curve, specify the comparison rule, and predict what should happen under rerouting, shrinking a loop, or changing coordinates.

Holonomy Turns Loops Into Curvature

Tiny-loop readout

$$\Delta\theta \approx \int_R K\,dA$$

Shrinking the loop turns returned angle per area into a local curvature estimate.

What is measured

Transport a tangent vector around a closed curve and compare the final vector with the initial one in the same tangent plane.

What can go wrong

Orientation reversals and inconsistent signs can make a correct magnitude look wrong.

Exercise diagnosis

If the loop encloses no curvature in a flat patch, holonomy should vanish up to numerical or approximation error.

holonomy and curvatureReturned angle as a loop diagnostic
Move from along-path comparison to loop comparison. Holonomy asks whether a vector returns to its starting tangent plane with the same direction after being transported around a closed curve. For a tiny loop the returned angle divided by enclosed area estimates curvature, so the problem becomes a meter rather than a symbolic ritual.

Boundary Curvature Has To Be Accounted For

Local Gauss-Bonnet ingredients

$$\int_R K\,dA+\int_{\partial R}k_g\,ds+\sum_i \alpha_i=2\pi\chi(R)$$

Interior term

Gaussian curvature accumulates over the chosen region.

Boundary term

Geodesic curvature records how the boundary turns inside the surface.

Corner term

Piecewise smooth boundaries add jumps in tangent direction. They are not optional bookkeeping.

geodesic-curvature-sphere-cone-lab.pngBoundary and interior terms share the ledger
Use the sphere-cone artifact as a warning that curvature accounting has several entries. An angle excess or defect is not only an interior curvature story; boundary geodesic curvature and corner turns may also contribute. Good solutions state which entries are present, which vanish, and which sign convention is being used.

Gauss-Bonnet Is A Ledger

Topology side

$$2\pi\chi$$

The total target is fixed by the region or surface type.

Curvature

\(\int K\,dA\)

Boundary

\(\int k_g\,ds\)

Corners

\(\sum\alpha_i\)

Topology

\(2\pi\chi\)

Diagnostic use

1

Identify the region and orientation before computing signs.

2

Mark which boundary pieces are geodesics, so their \(k_g\) entries vanish.

3

Use \(\chi\) as the total that catches missing terms.

Exercises in this family are best read as conservation laws: find the missing entry and verify the total against Euler characteristic.

Geodesic Deviation Reads Curvature Dynamically

Geodesic deviation

$$\frac{D^2J}{dt^2}+R(J,T)T=0$$

Flat

Nearby geodesics coast with constant relative velocity.

Positive

Separation can focus; curvature acts like a restoring term.

Negative

Separation can spread faster than the flat model predicts.

Diagnostic prompt

Given \(J(0)\) and \(DJ/dt(0)\), predict the qualitative behavior before computing components.

Jacobi equation and geodesic deviationCurvature as relative acceleration
Connect Act IV transport to neighboring geodesics. A Jacobi field is a moving separation vector along a base geodesic, and the curvature tensor supplies the relative acceleration term. The diagnostic question is whether nearby geodesics coast, focus, or spread. Students should predict the qualitative behavior before manipulating the tensor formula.

Tensor Identities Are Regression Tests

Algebraic curvature checks

$$R_{ijkl}=-R_{jikl}=-R_{ijlk}=R_{klij}$$

$$R_{ijkl}+R_{iklj}+R_{iljk}=0$$

Why identities matter

They encode the geometry of non-commuting comparisons, not arbitrary array symmetry.

Failure mode

An index-order mistake can still produce plausible numbers while breaking the first Bianchi identity.

Exercise diagnosis

Before interpreting a component, check antisymmetry, pair symmetry, and cyclic cancellation.

riemann-bianchi-checker.pngSymmetry before interpretation
Treat the Riemann and Bianchi identities as consistency tests rather than decoration. The tensor has symmetries because it records a geometric failure of comparisons to commute, not because an array happens to have repeated entries. The heatmap stands in for a symbolic regression check: if a computed tensor violates these identities, the connection or index convention is suspect.

Spacetime Uses The Same Diagnostic Habit

Einstein curvature ledger

$$G_{ab}=\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}-\frac12 Rg_{ab}$$

$$\nabla^aG_{ab}=0$$

Geodesic meaning

Free-fall paths are geodesics of the spacetime metric.

Tidal meaning

Relative acceleration of nearby worldlines is geodesic deviation.

Conservation check

The divergence-free Einstein tensor is the spacetime analogue of a curvature ledger with a built-in consistency test.

Einstein tensor and relativity applicationsAct IV diagnostics in spacetime
The relativity exercises extend the same Act IV grammar into spacetime. Free-fall worldlines are geodesics, tidal acceleration is geodesic deviation, and the Einstein tensor packages curvature into a divergence-free source ledger. The goal is not to solve field equations on a slide, but to recognize the same object-measurement-invariant habit in Lorentzian geometry.

One-line takeaway

Act IV is the art of comparing nearby geometric data.

Loops, regions, geodesic families, tensors, and worldlines are different probes of the same curvature story.
