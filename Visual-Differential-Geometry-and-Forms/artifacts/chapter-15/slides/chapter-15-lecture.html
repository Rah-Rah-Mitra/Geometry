Chapter 15

The Shape Operator

How the derivative of the unit normal becomes a linear map on the tangent plane.

$$S_p(v) = -D_v n$$

A surface bends because its normal field changes. The shape operator records that change in tangent coordinates.

Visual Differential Geometry and Forms Printed pages 149-164; PDF pages 178-193
Open by separating the two objects students often merge: the surface point is fixed, but the normal vector is allowed to vary along tangent directions. The lecture question is not 'what is curvature?' in the abstract; it is 'what machine turns a tangent direction into the infinitesimal change of the normal?' Preview the sign convention and say that the minus sign makes the sphere have positive principal curvatures with the outward normal.
The Route

From Normal Drift To Curvature Data

1. Differentiate The Normal

Move on the surface in a tangent direction \(v\). Watch the unit normal \(n\) change.

\(D_v n\)

2. Package As An Operator

The derivative is tangent to the unit sphere, hence tangent to the surface at the contact point.

\(S_p:T_pM\to T_pM\)

3. Read Its Eigenstructure

Eigenvectors are principal directions; eigenvalues are principal curvatures.

\(k_1,\;k_2\)

The chapter is a conversion story: a geometric observation, the drifting normal, becomes a linear algebra object, and that object then produces the classical curvature language. Construction

The Normal Field Already Contains Bending

Normal drift over a patch defines a tangent-plane map.
Local Reading Pick \(p\in M\), \(v\in T_pM\), and a surface curve \(\gamma\) with \(\gamma(0)=p\), \(\gamma'(0)=v\). Then $$D_v n = \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{0} n(\gamma(t)).$$
  • The normal has constant length, so its derivative is perpendicular to \(n(p)\).
  • For a surface in \(\mathbb R^3\), perpendicular to \(n(p)\) means tangent to \(M\) at \(p\).
  • Thus \(v\mapsto -D_vn\) is an endomorphism of \(T_pM\).
Talk through the image as a measurement device: walk a tiny amount in a tangent direction and compare the old and new normals. Constant normal length forces the derivative perpendicular to the normal, so it lands in the tangent plane. That is what makes the shape operator linear algebra rather than only ambient differentiation.
Definition

The Weingarten Map At A Point

Shape Operator For an oriented regular surface \(M\subset\mathbb R^3\) with unit normal \(n\), the shape operator at \(p\) is $$S_p(v)=-D_v n,\qquad v\in T_pM.$$
Self-Adjointness With respect to the first fundamental form, $$\langle S_pu,v\rangle=\langle u,S_pv\rangle.$$ Hence the principal directions can be chosen orthogonal.
State the definition slowly and make the domain and codomain explicit. The vector v is used as the direction in which the normal field is differentiated; the output is another tangent vector. Self-adjointness explains why the operator has real eigenvalues and orthogonal principal directions, preparing the ellipse picture.
Linear Algebra View

The Unit Circle Becomes A Curvature Ellipse

The tangent unit circle maps to an ellipse of normal derivatives.
Matrix Reading After choosing an orthonormal tangent frame, \(S_p\) is represented by a symmetric matrix $$[S_p]=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ b&c\end{pmatrix}.$$

Trace

Mean curvature uses \(H=\frac12\operatorname{tr}S\).

Determinant

Gaussian curvature uses \(K=\det S\).

The ellipse axes reveal the directions where bending is extremal.

Use this slide to connect calculus to linear algebra. The input circle is all unit tangent directions. Applying S_p to that circle produces an ellipse, just as any symmetric linear map does. The long and short axes mark eigenvectors, while their signed scale factors are the principal curvatures. Trace and determinant then become compact summaries, not arbitrary formulas.
Eigenstructure

Principal Directions Diagonalize Bending

Principal Curvatures If \(S_pe_i=k_i e_i\), then \(e_i\) is a principal direction and \(k_i\) is the corresponding principal curvature.
$$k_n(\theta)=\langle S_pu(\theta),u(\theta)\rangle = k_1\cos^2\theta+k_2\sin^2\theta$$
  • The normal curvature in direction \(u\) is a quadratic form.
  • Euler's formula is the diagonal form of that quadratic expression.
  • Changing tangent frames changes the matrix, not the eigenvalues.
Eigenvectors reveal principal directions.
This is the slide where students should see why eigenvectors deserve geometric names. In an arbitrary tangent direction, the normal curvature is a mixture of the two principal values. Along an eigenvector, the shape operator simply stretches or reverses that direction, so the curvature measurement is unmixed. The formula k_1 cos^2(theta)+k_2 sin^2(theta) is best presented as 'linear algebra seen from a rotating unit vector.'
Hyperbolic Points

Zero Normal Curvature Appears Between Opposite Signs

Zero normal curvature directions appear only with mixed signs.
Common Mistake Zero normal curvature does not mean the surface is flat at the point. It means bending cancels in that particular direction.
Asymptotic Direction A nonzero tangent direction \(u\) is asymptotic when $$\langle S_pu,u\rangle=0.$$

If \(k_1k_2<0\), Euler's formula has two real zero directions. If \(k_1,k_2\) have the same sign, it has none.

Frame this as a sign test. At a hyperbolic point, one principal curvature bends one way and the other bends the opposite way. Between those directions, there are two directions where the normal curvature measurement is zero. Be careful to say that these are directional measurements; the point still has nonzero Gaussian curvature because the determinant is negative.
Forms

Three Quadratic Forms, One Operator

At A Fixed Point $$I(u,v)=\langle u,v\rangle,\qquad II(u,v)=\langle S_pu,v\rangle,\qquad III(u,v)=\langle S_pu,S_pv\rangle.$$
  • \(I\) measures tangent lengths and angles.
  • \(II\) measures bending through one use of \(S_p\).
  • \(III\) measures the metric after the normal map derivative.
Coordinate Moral In a principal frame, these forms become diagonal together.
The first, second, and third forms package metric and bending data.
Use the comparison figure to keep the forms connected. The first form is ordinary tangent-plane measurement. The second inserts the shape operator once, converting bending into a bilinear measurement. The third inserts it twice, corresponding to measurement through the normal map derivative. In a principal frame, all three diagonalize together.
Instructor Problem

A Two-Minute Shape Operator Reading

Exercise At a point \(p\), suppose an orthonormal tangent frame gives $$[S_p]=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.$$ For \(u(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), find:
  • the principal curvatures and principal directions,
  • \(H\) and \(K\),
  • the asymptotic directions, if any.
Solution Skeleton $$k_n(\theta)=3\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta.$$ $$H=1,\qquad K=-3.$$ Asymptotic directions solve \(3\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta\), so \(\tan^2\theta=3\).

Discussion prompt: Which part of the answer would change if the frame were rotated? Which part is invariant?

Let students work before showing the skeleton. The operator should feel usable: eigenvalues are visible from the diagonal matrix, mean and Gaussian curvature are trace and determinant summaries, and asymptotic directions come from the quadratic form. Ask what survives a frame change to separate matrix entries from geometric data.
Recap

What The Shape Operator Lets Us See

Normal Derivative

The map \(v\mapsto -D_vn\) turns visible normal drift into a tangent-plane operator.

Principal Geometry

Eigenvectors give principal directions; eigenvalues give the two extremal normal curvatures.

Classical Curvatures

Mean and Gaussian curvature are \(\frac12\operatorname{tr}S\) and \(\det S\).

Chapter Takeaway The shape operator is the bridge from moving normals to the first, second, and third fundamental forms.
Close by returning to the opening question. The derivative of the normal becomes an operator because constant normal length forces the derivative into the tangent plane. Once that operator is available, the chapter becomes structured linear algebra: eigenvectors, eigenvalues, trace, determinant, and associated quadratic forms.
