Metric data

Lengths, angles, areas, and the Levi-Civita rule for transporting tangent vectors are intrinsic.

Embedding data

Bridge quantity

The proof mechanism starts by separating measurements from representations. An isometry may bend the surface in space, so the visible normal field can change. What cannot change is the metric, and therefore the Levi-Civita rule for parallel transport. Holonomy is the metric's way of reporting curvature without asking how the surface sits in three-space.

Where Egregium Enters

Area Distortion

\(dA_{S^2} = K\, dA_S\)

Extrinsic-looking side

The normal map scales tiny surface areas by Gaussian curvature.

Intrinsic side

The same spherical area is a holonomy angle, and holonomy comes from the metric.

Conclusion

The local ratio \(\Delta\theta/A\) is forced by the metric, so \(K\) is forced by the metric.

pp. 252-256The proof mechanism
This is the decisive synthesis. The Gauss map converts the surface patch area into spherical area with local scale factor K. But the same spherical area is also the holonomy angle, and holonomy is intrinsic because parallel transport is determined by the metric. Therefore K can be recovered from metric data alone.

Do Not Confuse The Scaffold With The Invariant

Common mistake

The normal map appears in the argument, so it is tempting to call the normal field intrinsic.

Correction

The normal field is scaffolding. The intrinsic measurement is the holonomy-to-area ratio.

Cylinder Check

plane \(\leftrightarrow\) cylinder by bending, yet \(K=0\) on both.

pp. 252-256Representation versus invariant
Pause on the common misconception. The proof uses the normal map, but it does not conclude that normals are intrinsic. Normals are scaffolding that help compute the answer. The answer is the holonomy-to-area ratio, which survives isometric bending. A plane rolled into a cylinder changes its normals dramatically while leaving K equal to zero.
