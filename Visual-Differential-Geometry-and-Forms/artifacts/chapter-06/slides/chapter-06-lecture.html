The Hyperbolic Plane in Complex Coordinates

Two models, one idea

The disk $|z|<1$ and the upper half-plane $\mathrm{Im}\,z>0$ each store one complex coordinate per point.

Conformal metric

$$ds^2_{\text{disk}} = \frac{4\,|dz|^2}{(1-|z|^2)^2}$$

Half-plane

$$ds^2_{\mathbb{H}} = \frac{|dz|^2}{(\mathrm{Im}\,z)^2}$$

pp. 65-82The Hyperbolic Plane in Complex Coordinates
A Riemannian metric assigns lengths and angles infinitesimally. The Poincare disk and upper half-plane both record one complex coordinate per point, but rescale the Euclidean ruler by a conformal factor that blows up near the boundary. That single factor encodes all of the negative curvature we are about to exploit.

What an Isometry Must Preserve

Definition

A map $f$ is an isometry when it reproduces every infinitesimal length:

$$ds^2\big(f(z)\big) = ds^2(z)$$

Therefore preserved

Distances, angles, geodesics, and hyperbolic area are all carried along unchanged.

Strategy

Find every such $f$, then recognize the family as familiar complex functions.

pp. 65-82What an Isometry Must Preserve
An isometry is a bijection of the surface that leaves the metric invariant: every infinitesimal length, and therefore every distance, angle, geodesic and area, is reproduced exactly. Our entire strategy is to find all such maps for the hyperbolic plane, and to recognize them as a familiar family of complex functions.

Complex Multiplication is a Rotation

Polar form

$$z = r\,e^{i\theta}, \qquad w\cdot z = |w|\,r\;e^{i(\arg w + \theta)}$$

Amplitwist

Multiplying by $w$ scales by $|w|$ and twists by $\arg w$ at once.

Pure rotation

When $|w|=1$ the map $z\mapsto e^{i\alpha} z$ is a rotation about the origin by $\alpha$.

pp. 65-82Complex Multiplication is a Rotation
Writing a complex number in polar form exposes the geometric content of multiplication. Multiplying by a unit modulus number rotates the plane about the origin by its argument; a general factor also scales by its modulus. This amplitwist is the seed from which every rigid motion of the plane will be assembled.

Euclidean Rigid Motions as Complex Maps

Direct isometry

$$f(z) = e^{i\alpha} z + b, \qquad |e^{i\alpha}|=1$$

Reflection (opposite)

$$f(z) = e^{i\alpha}\,\bar{z} + b$$

Whole group

Conjugation $\bar{z}$ flips orientation; everything else is rotation plus translation.

pp. 65-82Euclidean Rigid Motions as Complex Maps
Before the curved case, fix intuition in the flat plane. A direct Euclidean isometry is exactly multiplication by a unit complex number followed by a translation: a rotation-translation. Adding a conjugation gives the orientation-reversing reflections. Complex notation has already compressed the whole rigid-motion group into two short formulas.

Mobius Transformations

Definition

$$M(z) = \frac{az + b}{cz + d}, \qquad ad - bc \neq 0$$

Group

Composition corresponds to matrix product $\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}$; inverses exist.

Three points

A Mobius map is fixed once its values at three points are named.

Circle law

Generalized circles map to generalized circles.

pp. 65-82Mobius Transformations
A Mobius transformation sends z to a fractional linear expression with complex coefficients and nonzero determinant. These maps form a group under composition, are determined by their action on three points, and send the family of generalized circles to itself. They are the natural candidates for the isometries we seek.

Inversion and Generalized Circles

Factorization

Every Mobius map is built from translation, scaling, and one inversion:

$$z \mapsto z+\tfrac{d}{c} \mapsto \tfrac{1}{z} \mapsto \cdots$$

Inversion

$z\mapsto 1/\bar{z}$ swaps interior and exterior of the unit circle.

Consequence

Lines and circles are interchanged, but the combined family is preserved.

pp. 65-82Inversion and Generalized Circles
Every Mobius map factors into translations, a complex scaling, and one inversion in the unit circle. Inversion is the genuinely new ingredient: it swaps inside and outside of the circle and trades lines for circles. Because each factor preserves the family of circles and lines, so does any composition of them.

Isometries of the Upper Half-Plane

Main result

$$z \mapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d}, \quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},\ ad-bc>0$$

Real coefficients

These fix the real axis and keep $\mathrm{Im}\,z>0$.

One reflection

$z\mapsto -\bar{z}$ supplies the orientation-reversing isometries.

pp. 65-82Isometries of the Upper Half-Plane
On the upper half-plane the isometries are precisely the real Mobius transformations, those with real coefficients and positive determinant, together with one reflection. They map the real axis to itself, preserve the imaginary part structure, and therefore leave the hyperbolic metric, with its one over y scaling, completely invariant.

Isometries of the Poincare Disk

Disk automorphisms

$$M(z) = e^{i\alpha}\,\frac{z - a}{1 - \bar{a}\,z}, \qquad |a|<1$$

Blaschke factor

The factor with parameter $a$ slides the center to any chosen point.

Homogeneity

Every point looks like every other: the disk is fully symmetric.

pp. 65-82Isometries of the Poincare Disk
Transporting the result to the disk, the orientation-preserving isometries become the disk-automorphism Mobius maps: a rotation composed with a so-called Blaschke factor that slides the center to any chosen point. This is the main isometry result, and it makes the homogeneity of hyperbolic geometry visible and computable.

Classifying Hyperbolic Motions

Elliptic

One interior fixed point; rotation around it. $|\mathrm{tr}|<2$.

Parabolic

One boundary fixed point; shear-like limit. $|\mathrm{tr}|=2$.

Hyperbolic

Two boundary fixed points; translation along the joining geodesic. $|\mathrm{tr}|>2$.

pp. 65-82Classifying Hyperbolic Motions
Counting fixed points sorts the orientation-preserving isometries into three archetypes. Elliptic maps fix one interior point and rotate around it; parabolic maps fix a single boundary point; hyperbolic maps fix two boundary points and translate along the geodesic joining them. The trace of the matrix decides which type appears.

Spacetime and the Lorentz Connection

Spacetime interval

$$ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + dx^2$$

Boost = isometry

A Lorentz boost preserves the interval just as a hyperbolic motion preserves distance.

Shared algebra

Both act as Mobius maps on a boundary circle or sphere.

pp. 65-82Spacetime and the Lorentz Connection
The same algebra governs special relativity. A Lorentz boost preserves the indefinite spacetime interval just as a hyperbolic isometry preserves hyperbolic distance, and both act as Mobius transformations on a boundary circle or sphere. Complex multiplication thus links the curved metric story to the geometry of light and motion.

Synthesis and Takeaways

Coordinates

A hyperbolic point is one complex number; motions are complex functions.

Main result

Hyperbolic isometries are exactly the disk and half-plane Mobius maps.

Classification

Fixed points and trace split them into elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic.

One transferable lesson

Separate representation from invariant: matrices and charts change freely, but preserved distance, the conformal factor $\frac{4}{(1-|z|^2)^2}$, and circle structure carry the geometry.

pp. 65-82Synthesis and Takeaways
We collapsed rigid motions into complex arithmetic, identified hyperbolic isometries with disk and half-plane Mobius maps, and classified them by fixed points. The recurring lesson is to separate representation from invariant: coordinates and matrices change freely, while preserved distance, the conformal factor, and circle structure carry the real geometry forward.
