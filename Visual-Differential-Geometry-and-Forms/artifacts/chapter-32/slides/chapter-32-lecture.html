DefinitionChapter 32

A Covector Measures A Tangent Vector

1-Form At A Point

$$\omega_p\in T_p^*M,\qquad \omega_p(av+bw)=a\omega_p(v)+b\omega_p(w).$$

Visual Reading

  • Parallel stacks represent equal readings of the covector.
  • A vector crossing more stacks has larger magnitude.
  • Reversing direction reverses the sign.

Common Misread

The blue stacks do not point like a vector field. They test the vector that is handed to them.

Local Coordinates

$$\omega=a\,dx+b\,dy,\qquad v=v^x\partial_x+v^y\partial_y,$$

$$\omega(v)=a\,v^x+b\,v^y.$$

covector space \(T_p^*M\)linear measurement, not displacement
Make the definition operational. A 1-form at p is a linear measurement on the tangent space at p. In the grid picture, the blue parallel slices are not arrows of a vector field; they are level sets of a measuring device. A vector crossing more slices receives a larger value, and reversing the vector reverses the sign. This is the first useful mental model for a covector.
DualityChapter 32

Vectors Point, 1-Forms Test

Tangent Vector

A tangent vector is a directed first-order displacement. It answers: which way, and how fast?

Covector / 1-Form

A covector is a linear test on possible displacements. It answers: how much does this vector count in my measurement?

Same Pairing, Three Notations

$$\omega(v)= \begin{bmatrix}a&b\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}v^x\\ v^y\end{bmatrix} =\langle \omega\mid v\rangle.$$

Basis Changes

Vector components and covector coefficients transform in opposite ways so the scalar \(\omega(v)\) stays fixed.

row vectors and Dirac brasthe value is coordinate-free
This slide separates the two dual objects. A tangent vector is drawn as a directed displacement. A covector is drawn as a family of equally spaced testing lines. Algebraically this is the difference between a column and a row. The pairing is a scalar, and that scalar survives changes of basis because vector components and covector coefficients compensate.
Physical ModelChapter 32

Work Is A 1-Form Field

Small Motion

$$\omega=F_x\,dx+F_y\,dy,\qquad \omega_p(\Delta r)=F(p)\cdot \Delta r.$$

Field Version

A 1-form field assigns one covector to each point. The vector input changes with the motion; the covector changes with position.

From Local Work To Total Work

$$W_\gamma=\int_\gamma \omega=\int_a^b \omega_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma'(t))\,dt.$$

Inspection Target

The integrand is scalar at every instant. A line integral adds readings, not arrows.

work as covector fieldline integrals begin locally
Use work to make the definition physical. A force or gravitational field becomes a covector field when the question is how much work is done by a small displacement. At each point the 1-form evaluates the velocity or displacement vector. Summing those tiny scalar measurements along a path is the line integral, so line integrals start as local one-form evaluations.
DifferentialsChapter 32

The Differential \(df\) Is The Prototype

Directional Change

$$df_p(v)=D_v f=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}f(\gamma(t)),\qquad \gamma(0)=p,\ \gamma'(0)=v.$$

Contour Reading

  • Move along a level set: \(df_p(v)\approx 0\).
  • Move across level sets: \(df_p(v)\) grows.
  • Reverse direction: the sign changes.

Metric Warning

The gradient vector \(\nabla f\) needs a metric. The covector \(df\) is defined before choosing lengths and angles.

topographic maps and gradients\(df\) is the covector; \(\nabla f\) uses a metric
Now connect the measurement picture to functions. The differential df at p eats a tangent vector v and returns the directional change of f in that direction. The contour figure makes this visual: movement tangent to a level curve gives near zero change, while movement across contours gives a larger value. The gradient vector appears only after a metric identifies covectors with vectors.
Coordinate ProbesChapter 32

Basis 1-Forms Are Coordinate Probes

Dual Basis

$$dx(\partial_x)=1,\quad dx(\partial_y)=0$$

$$dy(\partial_x)=0,\quad dy(\partial_y)=1.$$

Coordinate Extraction

If \(v=v^x\partial_x+v^y\partial_y\), then \(dx(v)=v^x\) and \(dy(v)=v^y\).

basis one-forms and componentsprobes first, notation second
Introduce dx and dy as probes rather than mysterious infinitesimals. The form dx asks for the x-component of a tangent vector; dy asks for the y-component. A general 1-form a dx plus b dy combines those probes with weights. This is the practical bridge from diagrams to row-vector algebra.
LinearityChapter 32

1-Forms Add By Superposing Measurements

Linearity Of Evaluation

$$(a\omega+b\eta)(v)=a\,\omega(v)+b\,\eta(v).$$

Scalechange stack spacing or sign
Addcombine readings pointwise
Evaluatereturn one scalar

Geometric Meaning

Two covectors can be combined before the vector arrives, or their scalar answers can be combined after evaluation.

Orientation Check

A negative coefficient reverses the sign of the measurement. That sign matters when integrals appear.

geometric addition of 1-formslinear algebra in the cotangent space
Linearity has a visible interpretation. Scaling a 1-form changes the spacing or sign of its measuring stacks. Adding two 1-forms means measuring with both and adding the scalar answers. The formula is simple, but the slide keeps the geometric meaning in view: covectors form a vector space because measurements can be combined before or after evaluation.
Integration PreviewChapter 32

Line Integrals Add Local Measurements

Riemann Sum Picture

$$\sum_i \omega_{\gamma(t_i)}(\gamma'(t_i))\,\Delta t$$

$$\longrightarrow\quad \int_\gamma \omega.$$

What The Curve Supplies

The path supplies a point \(\gamma(t)\) and a tangent vector \(\gamma'(t)\). The 1-form supplies the scalar reading.

What Gets Added

Only numbers are summed. This is why orientation and sign are part of the data, not bookkeeping decoration.

Next Chapters

The same measurement idea will scale from curves to oriented area and volume pieces.

line integralslocal covector readings become path-level quantities
Build the line integral as a Riemann sum of local covector readings along a curve. The tangent vector gamma prime is the directed piece of motion, and the 1-form reads that tangent at each point. This prevents a common mistake: the integral is not adding arrows along the curve; it is adding scalar readings produced by the 1-form.
