Neighbouring Geodesics Produce A Jacobi Field

Definition

$$J(t)=\left.\frac{\partial \gamma(s,t)}{\partial s}\right|_{s=0}$$

The vector lives in \(T_{\gamma_0(t)}M\), not in a fixed coordinate plane.

What the family supplies

Each \(s\)-curve is a geodesic. The \(s\)-derivative records how the neighboring geodesic changes when the label changes.

What must not be confused

A drawn distance between curves is chart dependent. The Jacobi field is the invariant tangent-vector bookkeeping for that separation.

Initial data

\(J(0)\) is initial offset and \(DJ/dt(0)\) is initial relative velocity. These two pieces determine the field.

geodesic variationSeparation field along a base geodesic
Introduce the geometric object before the equation. A geodesic variation is a sheet of geodesics indexed by a parameter s. Looking along the central geodesic, the derivative in the s direction gives a vector field J that measures separation inside the tangent planes of the surface. The important point is that J is not a coordinate distance drawn in the page; it is a tangent vector field along the base geodesic.

The Jacobi Equation Reads Like Force

Covariant second derivative

$$\frac{D^2J}{dt^2}=-R(J,T)T$$

Left side

Relative acceleration after every comparison has been made inside the moving tangent planes.

Right side

The curvature operator takes the separation direction and the geodesic velocity, then returns the tidal push on \(J\).

Flat-space diagnostic

If \(R=0\), then \(D^2J/dt^2=0\): separation changes linearly.

Jacobi equationCurvature operator as tidal term
Now give the equation its physical reading. The left side is covariant relative acceleration along the base geodesic. The right side is supplied by the curvature tensor acting on the separation vector and velocity. In flat space the force term vanishes, so neighboring geodesics keep a constant relative velocity. In curved space the sign and direction of R(J,T)T decide whether the family focuses or spreads.

The Sign Of Curvature Controls Separation

Two-dimensional normal form

$$j''+Kj=0$$

Here \(J=jN\), with \(N\) a parallel unit normal along the base geodesic.

\(K=0\)

Linear separation; flat geodesic fans keep Euclidean bookkeeping.

\(K>0\)

Oscillatory separation; initially parallel paths tend to refocus.

\(K<0\)

Hyperbolic growth; nearby geodesics diverge faster than flat space predicts.

Lecture prompt

Ask which sign behaves like a restoring force. Students should answer from the fan picture before reading the equation.

flat, positive, negative curvatureSign controls focusing
The constant-curvature fan makes the sign convention inspectable. In the flat model a fan has Euclidean angle sum pi and linear separation. In positive curvature, geodesics bend back toward each other and triangle angle sums have excess. In negative curvature, geodesics pull away and angle sums have defect. The scalar equation j double-prime plus K j equals zero is the two-dimensional normal form of that behavior.

Polar Coordinates Make The Proof Local

Geodesic polar metric

$$ds^2=dr^2+G(r,\theta)^2\,d\theta^2$$

Jacobi equation for G

$$G_{rr}+KG=0$$

Initial Euclidean behavior

\(G(0,\theta)=0\), \(G_r(0,\theta)=1\). Curvature enters in the next terms.

Proof idea

Small-circle circumference is obtained by integrating the angular coefficient \(G\). Deviation of \(G\) from \(r\) records curvature.

geodesic polar coordinatesLocal proof engine
Geodesic polar coordinates turn the visual fan into a single coefficient G. The angular coordinate vector is a Jacobi field, so the same equation appears as G rr plus K G equals zero with Euclidean initial behavior at the origin. This is the local proof engine behind the small circle expansions: curvature enters by changing the rate at which angular arcs grow.

Velocity Holonomy Is The Same Force

Commutator of comparisons

$$(\nabla_s\nabla_t-\nabla_t\nabla_s)V=R(S,T)V$$

Velocity holonomy

Transporting velocities around a tiny geodesic rectangle produces a small failure to return.

Same operator

The Jacobi force uses \(R(J,T)T\). Holonomy uses \(R(S,T)V\). Both ask how transport depends on the order of directions.

Teaching bridge

Previous holonomy chapters measured curvature by loops. This chapter measures it by the acceleration between neighboring geodesics.

The slide should make students see that relative acceleration and holonomy are not separate phenomena; they are two measurements of the same curvature operator.

Small Circles Remember Curvature

Circumference expansion

$$C(r)=2\pi r\left(1-\frac{K(p)}{6}r^2+O(r^3)\right)$$

Area expansion

$$A(r)=\pi r^2\left(1-\frac{K(p)}{12}r^2+O(r^3)\right)$$

Sign check

Positive \(K\) makes small circles smaller than Euclidean circles. Negative \(K\) makes them larger.

Why Jacobi fields appear

The circle is swept out by radial geodesics. Its circumference is the accumulated separation between neighboring rays.

small-circle-defect-series.pngCurvature in circumference and area
Small geodesic circles are a quiet diagnostic for the same deviation. In Euclidean space circumference is two pi r and area is pi r squared. Positive curvature subtracts from both at the first nontrivial order, while negative curvature adds. The coefficients one sixth and one twelfth are the scalar shadows of geodesic deviation near the center.
