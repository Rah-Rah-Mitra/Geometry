Using ambient space as a scaffold for the first model of parallel transport.

The Question

How can an arrow move along a curved surface while remaining a tangent arrow at every point?

The Scaffold

Let the surrounding space move the vector, then project the result back into the surface's tangent plane.

The Projection Rule

Ambient Derivative

Differentiate the moving vector in the surrounding Euclidean space. This derivative may point partly out of the surface.

Extrinsic Covariant Derivative

$$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V=P_{T_{\gamma(t)}M}\left({dV\over dt}\right)$$

Parallel Along The Curve

The vector field is parallel when the tangent projection vanishes: all visible change is normal to the surface.

extrinsic scaffoldprojection defines the first derivative
The key construction is a split of an ordinary ambient derivative into tangent and normal parts. The covariant derivative is the tangent projection of the ambient rate of change. If that projected part is zero, the vector is parallel along the curve according to this extrinsic model.

Discrete Transport Around A Loop

Step Algorithm

  1. Move the current tangent vector through the ambient space.
  2. Project it into the next tangent plane.
  3. Repeat along the sampled curve.
Finite Step

$$V_{i+1}=P_{T_{p_{i+1}}M}(V_i),\qquad \|V_{i+1}\|=1$$

Loop Diagnostic

A closed path need not return the vector to its starting direction. The discrepancy is a curvature signal, not a drawing error.

artifact: extrinsic-projection-transport.pngambient move, tangent acceptance
Use the local artifact to make the algorithm concrete. Move a tangent vector a short step in the surrounding space, project it into the next tangent plane, renormalize if desired, and repeat. Around a closed loop the returned direction can disagree with the starting direction, and that mismatch is a probe for curvature.

Where Projection Happens

At Each Point

The surface offers a different tangent plane and normal. Transport must be checked at the point where the vector lands.

Tangent Acceptance Test

$$W=W^\top+W^\perp,\qquad W^\top\in T_pM,\quad W^\perp\parallel N_p$$

What The Chart Hides

The contour map is a coordinate view of a surface. It helps locate the calculation, but parallelism is judged by tangent spaces, not by ink on the page.

artifact: surface-projection-transport.pngtangent spaces vary along the path
The surface plot emphasizes that projection is not a drawing trick. Each point has its own tangent plane and normal, so the vector must be judged against the tangent space at the point where it lands. The ambient coordinates are convenient bookkeeping, but the accepted component is the tangent one.

Geodesics As Self-Parallel Tangents

Use The Curve's Own Arrow

Set the transported vector equal to the unit tangent $T=\dot\gamma$. A geodesic is a curve whose tangent transports itself.

Self-Parallel Condition

$$\nabla_TT=0$$

Extrinsic Reading

The ambient acceleration can be nonzero, but it has no tangent component. The curve bends only as much as the surface forces it to bend.

artifact: geodesic-self-parallel-tangent.pnggeodesic = self-parallel tangent
A geodesic is the special case where the curve's own tangent vector survives this test. Its ambient acceleration may point away from the surface, but the tangent component of the acceleration vanishes. That is why a geodesic is straight from the surface's point of view even when it visibly bends in space.

Parallel Transport Along Geodesics

Calibration Path

Since $T$ is self-parallel, it provides a reference direction while other tangent vectors are transported beside it.

Angle Preservation Along Transport

$$ {d\over dt}\langle V,W\rangle=0\quad\text{when}\quad \nabla_TV=\nabla_TW=0 $$

Pedagogical Role

The geodesic case shows what a future intrinsic connection must preserve: tangent membership, length, and angles under comparison.

parallel transport along geodesicscomparison without sideways correction
Once the tangent of a geodesic is self-parallel, it becomes a calibration path for other transported vectors. The lesson is not that geodesics make transport trivial, but that the along-path comparison rule can be stated without referring to sideways turning inside the surface.

Potato-Peeler Development

The Physical Model

Peel a narrow strip from the surface and lay it flat without stretching. Along a geodesic, the centerline develops as a straight line.

Metric Ledger

$$ds^2=g_{11}\,du^2+2g_{12}\,du\,dv+g_{22}\,dv^2$$

Why This Matters

The strip is a bridge from extrinsic handling to intrinsic data: the surface can be measured by lengths and angles after flattening.

artifact: potato-peeler-strip-flattening.pngdevelopment as measured flattening
The potato-peeler model makes the same idea tactile. A narrow strip is peeled from the surface and flattened; along a geodesic the strip develops without sideways correction. The metric coefficients tell us how coordinate steps become lengths, so the flattened strip is not a freehand sketch but a measurement device.

Representation Versus Invariant

Common Trap

The extrinsic picture is a scaffold, not the final definition. Do not confuse a convenient embedding with the geometry that survives bending without stretching.

Can Change

  • coordinates
  • ambient drawing
  • chosen frame
  • sampling of the path

Must Survive

  • metric lengths
  • tangent projection rule
  • angle preservation
  • loop comparison result
pitfall checkthe scaffold points toward intrinsic geometry
This chapter is a warning against mistaking the scaffold for the geometry. Coordinates, embeddings, and frame drawings can all change. What survives are metric measurements, tangent projections, and comparison results such as a loop's returned angle. The extrinsic construction is useful exactly because it shows what must later be defined intrinsically.
