Curve Curvature As A Sampling Test

Object

A plane curve \(\gamma(t)\), with a chosen orientation and sampling.

Measurement

$$\kappa=\frac{x'y''-y'x''}{\left((x')^2+(y')^2\right)^{3/2}}$$

Failure Mode

Confusing a fast parameter with a sharply turning curve. Arclength normalization is the antidote.

plane-curve curvaturesampling and orientation check
The first diagnostic separates the curve from its parameterization. A reliable computation samples position, velocity, and acceleration, but the inspected quantity is the signed turning per unit arclength. Ask students to predict what should change under resampling and what should remain the same.

Frenet Frame And Torsion Lab

A helix is the calibration target: both curvature and torsion are nonzero and exactly predictable.

Helix Calibration

For \(\gamma(t)=(a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\):
$$\kappa=\frac{a}{a^2+b^2},\qquad \tau=\frac{b}{a^2+b^2}$$

Diagnostic Question

Does the moving frame rotate at the expected rate, or is the computation responding to the sample grid?

Invariant

Uniformly resampling the same helix should not change \(\kappa\) or \(\tau\); it should only improve numerical stability.

Warning

Torsion is a three-dimensional signed measurement. A planar curve has zero torsion even when its curvature is large.

Frenet framecurvature and torsion targets
The helix turns curve exercises into a controlled experiment because it has exact nonzero targets for both curvature and torsion. The instructor should treat the moving frame as the visible object and the two constants as calibration readings: if either drifts under uniform sampling, the computation is suspect.

Surface Curvature Gallery

Local Surface Test

$$K=\kappa_1\kappa_2,\qquad H=\frac{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}{2}$$

\(K>0\)

The principal curvatures agree in sign: dome or bowl behavior.

\(K<0\)

The principal curvatures disagree: saddle behavior and crossing tangent plane.

\(K=0\)

One direction is flat to second order: cylinder-like or parabolic behavior.

surface curvatureprincipal signs as visual evidence
This slide shifts from curves to surfaces by asking students to classify local bending before computing. The gallery separates positive, zero, and negative Gaussian curvature. The goal is not to memorize shapes; it is to connect the sign of the product of principal curvatures with what crosses or stays on one side of the tangent plane.

Shape Operator Coordinate Check

Representation Test

$$S_{\mathrm{new}}=P^{-1}SP,\qquad \operatorname{tr}S,\ \det S,\ \mathrm{spec}(S)\ \text{unchanged}$$

Principal Basis

The operator is diagonal; entries are the principal curvatures \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2\).

Rotated Basis

Matrix entries change, but eigenvalues, trace, and determinant still report the same geometry.

Exercise Trap

Do not interpret off-diagonal matrix entries as new curvatures; they are basis bookkeeping.

shape operatorcoordinate artifact versus invariant
The shape-operator slide is the main representation test. Principal coordinates diagonalize the operator, while a rotated basis usually introduces off-diagonal terms. The trace, determinant, and eigenvalues should agree across both views, so the exercise becomes a check that the operator is geometric rather than a coordinate artifact.

Gauss-Bonnet Ledger

The global exercises become accounting: local curvature plus boundary turning must close against topology.

Closed Surface Ledger

$$\int_M K\,dA=2\pi\chi(M)$$

With Boundary

$$\int_D K\,dA+\int_{\partial D}k_g\,ds+\sum_i \alpha_i=2\pi\chi(D)$$

Diagnostic Reading

If the topology row changes, the total budget changes. If only the coordinates change, the budget should not.

Gauss-Bonnettopological curvature budget
Use the ledger to make global curvature accounting concrete. Local curvature, corner turns, boundary turning, and topology are not separate facts; they are entries in one conservation statement. The table lets students see Euler characteristic as the number that predicts the total curvature budget.

Vector Field Index

Poincare-Hopf Diagnostic

$$\sum_{p: X(p)=0}\operatorname{index}_p(X)=\chi(M)$$

Local Measurement

Walk once around a zero and count how many times the vector direction winds.

Global Invariant

Zeros can split, move, or cancel in pairs, but the total index is fixed by topology.

Common Pitfall

Counting arrows is not enough. The diagnostic is the winding of their directions around the loop.

vector-field topologyPoincare-Hopf as index accounting
Poincare-Hopf is the topology counterpart to Gauss-Bonnet in this review. Instead of integrating curvature, we add turning numbers around zeros of a vector field. The diagnostic is the same: local measurements are allowed to move under deformation, but their total has to match the Euler characteristic.
