Newton's Ultimate Equality

The relation

Two quantities are ultimately equal when their ratio tends to one as the construction shrinks toward zero.

Newton's idea

This is the language of vanishing to higher order, the engine behind every first-order argument.

Definition

$A \sim B \iff \displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{A}{B}=1$

Why it helps

Inside a shrinking diagram we may replace any quantity by an ultimately equal one with no loss of rigor.

pp. 18-24Newton's Ultimate Equality
Here we introduce the core proof device: ultimate equality, written with the tilde. Two quantities are ultimately equal when their ratio tends to one as the construction shrinks. This is Newton's language for vanishing-to-higher-order, and it lets us replace curves by their tangents within any first-order computation without losing rigor.

Vanishing to Higher Order

First-order agreement

If $A$ and $B$ agree to first order, their difference is of order $\epsilon^2$, which the ratio cannot see.

Stable under algebra

Sums, products, and ratios of ultimately equal pieces remain ultimately equal.

Why the ratio survives

$\dfrac{A}{B}=\dfrac{B+O(\epsilon^2)}{B}=1+O(\epsilon)\to 1$

pp. 18-24Vanishing to Higher Order
We make ultimate equality precise. Quantities agreeing to first order differ by terms of order epsilon squared, so dividing by the leading term sends the discrepancy to zero. We show that sums, products, and ratios of ultimately equal pieces stay ultimately equal, which is exactly what licenses substitution inside a shrinking diagram.

The Tangent Triangle

Reading a derivative straight off a shrinking picture

The construction

A small angle increment $d\theta$ produces an arc, its chord, and a thin triangle whose legs are ultimately differentials.

Result seen, not derived

$d(\tan\theta) \sim \sec^2\theta\, d\theta$

pp. 18-24The Tangent Triangle
We compute the derivative of tangent purely visually. A tiny angular increment produces an arc, a chord, and a thin triangle whose legs are ultimately equal to differentials. Reading the similar triangles off the picture gives the derivative directly, with no formula manipulation. This is the prologue's showcase of seeing replacing symbol shuffling.

Act III — Curvature

From defect to invariant
