Neutral Baseline: What Is Already Available?

Model-local definition

Two lines are parallel when they are distinct line-sets in the same plane and have no common point in the chosen model.

\(m \parallel \ell \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad m \ne \ell,\; m \cap \ell = \varnothing\)
  • Earlier neutral results supply perpendiculars, angle comparison, congruent right triangles, and separation tools.
  • The exterior-point question asks for lines through \(P \notin \ell\) that avoid \(\ell\).
  • Existence and uniqueness are separate claims; a drawing can suggest either one only after the model is named.
Frame this slide as the contract for neutral geometry. We keep the incidence, metric, betweenness, angle, and congruence machinery from earlier chapters, then ask what those tools do not decide. The key habit is to translate parallelism into a model-local predicate: distinct lines, same plane, and no shared point in the model.

The Exterior-Point Test Separates Models

Euclidean pattern

Through \(P\), exactly one candidate line avoids the reference line \(\ell\).

Hyperbolic pattern

Several geodesic candidates through \(P\) can avoid the same reference geodesic.

Use the two panels to separate visual memory from logical content. On the left, Euclidean intuition gives exactly one line through the exterior point that avoids the reference line. On the right, the disk-model sketch suggests that many geodesics through a point can avoid the base geodesic. The definition is the same; the model changes the answer.

Parallel Axioms Are Branch Points

Logical role

A parallel postulate does not define lines. It decides an exterior-point behavior that neutral geometry has left open.

\(\exists m\,(P \in m \ \text{and}\ m \parallel \ell)\quad\) versus \(\quad\exists! m\)
  • The Euclidean branch adds uniqueness of the parallel through an exterior point.
  • The non-Euclidean branch permits more than one nonintersecting line through the same point.
  • Statements proved before this fork must not quietly use uniqueness.
Present the fork as a dependency issue. Neutral theorems can prove existence and many angle facts, but uniqueness of the exterior parallel is a new commitment. Students should hear that the Euclidean and non-Euclidean branches are not competing drawings; they are different completions of the same neutral starting language.

Saccheri Quadrilaterals Make The Branch Measurable

Definition

A Saccheri quadrilateral has a base \(AB\), right base angles at \(A\) and \(B\), and congruent legs \(AD\) and \(BC\).

\(AD \cong BC,\qquad \angle A=\angle B=90^\circ\)
Inspection target

The summit side \(CD\) and summit angles \(\angle C,\angle D\) act as the readout. The shape is diagnostic only when the right-angle and equal-leg constraints are enforced.

Introduce Saccheri quadrilaterals as a measuring device. The right base angles and equal legs are the calibration conditions, while the summit side and summit angles are what we inspect. Stress that a generic four-sided drawing teaches very little; the diagnostic power comes from the constraints that make the summit behavior meaningful.

Summit Angles Encode The Parallel Hypothesis

Saccheri readout

Neutral geometry forces the two summit angles to agree. The question becomes whether that common angle is right or acute.

\(\angle C \cong \angle D,\qquad \sigma=\mu(\angle C)=\mu(\angle D)\)

Right-angle hypothesis

If \(\sigma=90^\circ\) for Saccheri quadrilaterals, the exterior-point test has Euclidean character.

Acute-angle hypothesis

If \(\sigma<90^\circ\), parallel families widen and model-dependent critical behavior appears.

Why this matters

The global parallel axiom becomes a local angle measurement that can be checked in examples.

Walk through the three summit-angle stories without making the slide a historical detour. In the geometry developed here, the equal summit angles become a local readout for the parallel question. Right summit angles point toward Euclidean behavior; acute summit angles point toward hyperbolic behavior; the blocked obtuse route is a lesson in how neutral angle estimates already have force.

The Critical Function Turns Separation Into Data

Definition by role

A critical function records a threshold: as a separation parameter changes, it marks the boundary between candidates that meet the reference line and candidates that remain parallel.

  • Its shape is evidence for model behavior: monotone, bounded, linear, or curved.
  • The notebook profile compares sample responses; the theorem must explain why the observed pattern persists.
  • The data view is useful because parallel divergence becomes something measurable.
Connect the critical function to the notebook's computational profile. It is not just a decorative graph; it records a threshold relation. As the separation parameter changes, the boundary between meeting candidates and nonmeeting candidates changes monotonically. The proof still has to justify the rule, but the graph tells students what kind of theorem they are trying to prove.

Critical Angles Package Families Of Rays

Boundary test

Fix \(P\) and a reference line \(\ell\). Candidate rays through \(P\) fall into three visible roles: they meet \(\ell\), they do not meet \(\ell\), or they sit at the boundary between the two behaviors.

\(\Pi(d) \text{ records a boundary angle at distance } d.\)
meetRays cross the reference line inside the model.
limitBoundary rays are parallel but just barely so.
avoidInterior rays in the parallel region do not meet the reference line.
recordThe critical function stores how the boundary moves.
Use this slide to make the boundary idea geometric. From a fixed exterior point, rays can meet the reference line, fail to meet it, or sit at the transition. The critical angle is the quantity being recorded. Students should not memorize the notation first; they should understand that the function packages a family of exterior-point tests.

Mini-Lab: Vary A Saccheri Parameter

Protocol

Hold the base and equal-leg constraint fixed. Vary a summit-height parameter \(h\), then record the summit length and common summit angle.

\(AD=BC=L,\quad s(h)=CD,\quad \sigma(h)=\mu(\angle C)=\mu(\angle D)\)
  • Check calibration first: right base angles and equal legs must still hold.
  • Track which quantities are invariant and which respond to \(h\).
  • Compare the numerical trend with the proof dependency map.
Treat the lab as a small scientific protocol. Vary one Saccheri parameter at a time, record the base, summit, leg equality, and summit-angle trend, and then ask which observations are invariant. The point is not to collect a polished numerical answer; it is to practice seeing which hypotheses make the diagnostic valid.

Takeaways

Parallel theory separates models

The same neutral definitions support different answers to the exterior-point test.

Saccheri quadrilaterals localize the axiom

Right base angles and equal legs turn a global parallel question into a summit-angle readout.

Critical functions record thresholds

A family of candidate rays becomes a measurable boundary that can vary with separation.

Proof and computation have different jobs

Diagrams and checks expose hypotheses; the theorem still carries the universal claim.

Chapter answer

Parallel behavior reveals which model commitments have entered the argument. The chapter's tools keep those commitments visible before Euclidean or hyperbolic geometry takes over.
