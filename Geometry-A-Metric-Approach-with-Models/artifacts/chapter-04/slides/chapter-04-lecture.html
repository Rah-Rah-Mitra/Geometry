Definition from a coordinate model

A Line Becomes A Side Classifier

Definition 4.1-style Predicate

For a line \(L: ax+by+c=0\), define

\(s_L(P)=ax_P+by_P+c\)

Only points with \(s_L(P)\ne 0\) belong to a side.

Same Side / Opposite Side

Same side: \(s_L(P)s_L(Q)>0\)

Opposite sides: \(s_L(P)s_L(Q)<0\)

Treat the signed equation as the computational version of side. A point not on the line receives a positive or negative label; pairs with matching labels live on the same side, and pairs with opposite labels live on opposite sides. This is an algebraic model of the separation relation, not a replacement for the axioms in arbitrary geometries.
Segment crossing criterion

Opposite Sides Predict A Forced Meeting

Coordinate Test

If \(A\) and \(B\) are not on \(L\), then the segment \(\overline{AB}\) crosses \(L\) when endpoint signs differ.

\(s_L(A)s_L(B)<0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \overline{AB}\cap L\ne \varnothing\)

In a Pasch-style axiom system, the theorem-level content is the existence of this meeting point without relying on coordinates.

Boundary case: if \(s_L(P)=0\), the point is on the line and is not assigned to either open side.
Push students from point classification to segment behavior. When the endpoint signs have opposite values, continuity in the coordinate model forces an intersection with the separating line. The important caveat is that axiomatic plane separation abstracts the conclusion, so the sign formula is a model for intuition and checking, not the definition in every plane.

Pasch geometry

Pasch Converts Local Crossings Into Triangle Exits

Pasch Principle

A line that meets one side of a triangle, under the nonvertex hypotheses, cannot pass through the triangle without meeting an additional side.

Predicate Reading

One side-crossing changes the side relation of two vertices. Pasch asserts that the changed relation has a geometric witness on another side.

entry side \(+\) separation data \(\Rightarrow\) exit side

State Pasch as the theorem that prevents a line from secretly entering a triangle and never leaving. If a transversal meets one side under the usual nonvertex restrictions, the side bookkeeping forces another side intersection. The figure should be read dynamically: the crossing with AC changes which exit side is available and makes the triangle interior observable.
Crossbar theorem

An Interior Ray Must Hit The Opposite Side

Theorem Roadmap

If ray \(\overrightarrow{AD}\) lies in the interior of \(\angle BAC\), then it intersects segment \(\overline{BC}\).

Why Separation Matters

  • Interior of an angle is defined by two side choices.
  • The ray crosses from the vertex into both chosen half-planes.
  • Pasch supplies the forced meeting with the opposite side.
Move from triangle sides to angle interiors. The crossbar theorem says that a ray placed inside an angle at A must meet the opposite side BC of triangle ABC. The teaching point is not just the intersection; it is that the phrase inside an angle carries enough separation information to force a concrete segment hit.
Interior as half-plane intersection

Triangle Interior Is Three Compatible Side Choices

Membership Test

For triangle \(ABC\), a point \(P\) is interior when it lies on the same side of each edge as the opposite vertex.

\(P\in \operatorname{Int}(ABC)\) iff

\(P,C\) same side of \(AB\)

\(P,A\) same side of \(BC\)

\(P,B\) same side of \(CA\)

Build triangle interior as an intersection of three half-plane choices. A point is inside the triangle when it lies on the same side of each edge as the opposite vertex, avoiding the edge lines themselves. This formulation is useful because it makes interior membership testable one edge at a time and prepares the convexity discussion.
Convex quadrilaterals

Convexity Is Consistent Orientation Plus Interior Diagonals

Orientation Criterion

For consecutive vertices \(A,B,C,D\), the signed turns should keep the same sign.

\(\operatorname{orient}(A,B,C),\operatorname{orient}(B,C,D),\operatorname{orient}(C,D,A),\operatorname{orient}(D,A,B)\)

Diagonal Criterion

In a convex quadrilateral, each diagonal lies in the interior of the opposite angle pair and the diagonals meet inside the figure.

Use the quadrilateral artifact to shift from triangles to four-sided figures. Convexity can be diagnosed by consistent orientation, positive signed area, and diagonals that remain in the interior rather than escaping outside an edge. The orange diagonals in the figure are not decoration; they are witnesses for how the boundary encloses the region.

Short board exercise

Classify Sides Before Drawing The Segment

Exercise

Let \(L: x-2y+1=0\). For each point, compute \(s_L(P)=x_P-2y_P+1\), then decide which segments must cross \(L\).

Point Coordinates Sign
\(A\) \((-1,1)\) ?
\(B\) \((3,1)\) ?
\(C\) \((1,-1)\) ?

Discussion Prompts

  1. Which pairs lie on the same side of \(L\)?
  2. Which pairs lie on opposite sides of \(L\)?
  3. Which of \(\overline{AB}\), \(\overline{AC}\), and \(\overline{BC}\) are forced to meet \(L\)?
  4. How would the answer change if one point had sign zero?
Hint: multiply signs before sketching. The sketch should confirm the predicate, not replace it.
Use the exercise to make students perform the side arithmetic themselves. They should compute signs, classify same-side and opposite-side pairs, and predict which segments cross L before drawing anything. The point is to rehearse the Chapter 4 habit: a spatial claim should become a predicate, and the predicate should decide a crossing or interior question.
