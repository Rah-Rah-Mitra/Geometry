Definition layer

An Area Function Is a Rule With Geometric Commitments

Working Definition

An area function assigns a real number \(A(R)\) to a permitted region \(R\), subject to rules that make the number independent of how the region is drawn.

\(A(R) \ge 0,\quad A(gR)=A(R),\quad A(R_1 \cup R_2)=A(R_1)+A(R_2)\)

The last identity assumes \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) have disjoint interiors and meet only along boundary pieces.

What the Rules Protect

  • Nonnegativity prevents cancellation from masquerading as measurement.
  • Congruence invariance makes rigid motion invisible to area.
  • Additivity turns a dissection into a computation.
  • Normalization chooses the unit of measurement.

Test Question

If a proposed formula changes after a rigid motion or double-counts overlapping pieces, it has failed before any advanced theorem begins.

Area is a function constrained by axiomsprinted pages 248-284
This slide should slow the class down before formulas appear. The essential claim is that area is not a primitive number attached to a drawing; it is a function whose behavior is constrained by rules. Nonnegativity prevents cancellation, congruence invariance says motion should not change measurement, additivity lets decompositions compute totals, and normalization fixes the scale. Ask students which rule a proposed measurement violates.
Axioms become computation

Additivity Lets a Region Be Measured Through Its Pieces

Notebook artifact: area-function-additivity.png

Decomposition Rule

If \(R\) is cut into finitely many regions \(R_i\) whose interiors do not overlap, then

\(A(R)=\sum_i A(R_i)\)

Inspection Target

Internal edges are bookkeeping devices. They separate pieces, but they do not contribute a second copy of area.

Computational Translation

Triangulate a polygon, compute each triangle, then verify that another triangulation gives the same total.

visual storyboard: decompose and compare the summed pieces with the wholeartifact checked for nonblank variation
Use the decomposition picture as the first operational test. If a polygon is cut into pieces that overlap only along edges, the sum of piece areas should recover the original area. This is the bridge between synthetic area and computation: triangulation gives many possible sums, but additivity asks all honest cuts to agree. The learner inspection target is the shared internal boundaries, which should not be counted twice.
Euclidean polygon area

The Shoelace Formula Computes Signed Area From Boundary Order

Formula

For vertices \(p_i=(x_i,y_i)\) listed cyclically, with \(p_{n+1}=p_1\),

\(A_{\mathrm{signed}}=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i)\)

Orientation Check

Reversing the vertex order reverses the sign. Ordinary area is \(|A_{\mathrm{signed}}|\).

Why It Fits the Axioms

The boundary sum telescopes across triangulations and behaves predictably under rigid motions.

Notebook artifact: shoelace-orientation-check.png

signed area records orientationabsolute value recovers Euclidean area
The shoelace formula enters as an oriented computation rather than as a magic determinant trick. Walk around the boundary and add cross products of consecutive vertices. Reversing the walk changes the sign while preserving the geometric size, so the absolute value is the ordinary Euclidean area. This slide should make orientation visible enough that students can diagnose sign errors before they become algebra errors.
Worked check

One Quadrilateral, Two Area Computations

Boundary Sum

For \(P_1=(0,0)\), \(P_2=(1,2)\), \(P_3=(3,1.5)\), \(P_4=(3,0)\):

\(A=\frac12|0+1(1.5)+3(0)+3(0)-0-2(3)-1.5(3)-0|=3\)

Diagonal Sum

The diagonal \(P_1P_3\) splits the quadrilateral into two triangles whose areas are \(2.25\) and \(0.75\).

Invariant Reading

Same region, same orientation convention, different decomposition, same total.

small data exposes assumptionsadditivity plus orientation
This worked example keeps the arithmetic small. The quadrilateral has area three by the shoelace sum, and a diagonal cut into two triangles gives the same total. The point is not the particular coordinates; it is the invariant comparison between two computations. Have students identify the exact place where additivity enters and the exact place where orientation enters.
Proof roadmap

Euclidean Area Is Forced Once the Unit Square Is Chosen

Normalization

Declare the unit square to have area \(1\). Rectangles then inherit \(bh\) by subdivision and scaling.

Triangles

Every triangle pairs with a congruent copy inside a parallelogram, giving \(A=\frac12 bh\).

Polygons

Triangulate, sum, and use additivity to show that changing the triangulation does not change the answer.

existence: build from simple regionsuniqueness: axioms make refinements agree
Present the Euclidean area theorem as a proof roadmap rather than a memorized formula. Rectangles set the scale, triangles inherit half-base-times-height, polygons inherit triangulation, and additivity plus invariance makes the result independent of the chosen decomposition. The lecture should emphasize that uniqueness comes from the axioms once the unit square has been normalized.
Hyperbolic model

In Curvature Negative One, Triangle Area Is Angle Defect

Notebook artifact: hyperbolic-area-defect-table.png

Defect Formula

\(A_{\mathbb H^2}(\triangle)=\pi-(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)\)

For curvature \(-\kappa^2\), divide the defect by \(\kappa^2\).

Interpretation

Hyperbolic triangles have angle sum less than \(\pi\). The missing angle is not an error; it measures area.

Contrast

Euclidean area depends on side scale. Hyperbolic defect ties area directly to curvature and angles.

visual storyboard: read defect as an area-like quantitysample defect in checks: 0.7415926535897928
Hyperbolic area should feel surprising but disciplined. In curvature negative one, the area of a geodesic triangle is measured by angle defect: pi minus the angle sum. This converts a metric phenomenon into an angular diagnostic. The figure from the notebook artifact samples several triangles, but the teaching point is conceptual: in hyperbolic geometry, smaller angle sum means larger area.
Why defect acts like area

Cutting a Hyperbolic Triangle Makes Defects Add

Algebra of the Cut

A geodesic split creates two triangles. The new angles along the cut are supplementary.

\(\delta(T_1)+\delta(T_2)=\delta(T)\)

Area Behavior

  • Isometries preserve angles, hence preserve defect.
  • Disjoint interiors make defects additive.
  • Nonnegative defect matches the hyperbolic angle inequality.

Teaching Point

The formula survives cutting because boundary bookkeeping cancels in exactly the way area requires.

defect is not just a formula; it has the area axiomstriangles first, polygons by dissection
Use this slide to explain why defect deserves to be called an area function for triangles. Isometries preserve angles, so they preserve defect. Cutting a triangle along a geodesic creates two new triangles; the two new angles along the cut add to pi, so the defects add exactly. This telescoping argument is the classroom moment where the formula becomes structural.

Conformal Advantage

Angles drawn in the disk are the hyperbolic angles, so defect can be read from the model picture.

Cut and reassemble

Bolyai-Style Scissors Reasoning Is Additivity Plus Isometry

Finite Dissection

Cut \(P\) into pieces \(P_i\), move each piece by an isometry \(g_i\), and reassemble a new region \(Q\).

\(A(P)=\sum_i A(P_i)=\sum_i A(g_iP_i)=A(Q)\)

What Is Being Proved

The total area is insensitive to the chosen finite rearrangement when the pieces fit without overlap.

Why This Matters

Scissors arguments replace direct measurement with an explicit equivalence of regions.

Notebook artifact: bolyai-scissors-invariance.png

visual storyboard: track pieces without changing total areaBolyai-style invariant
Bolyai-style scissors reasoning gives a second way to think about area without relying on a single coordinate formula. Cut a region into finitely many pieces, move each piece by an isometry, and reassemble. Because area is additive and isometry-invariant, the total cannot change. The slide should connect this visual operation back to the axioms from the beginning of the lecture.
Failure cases

Three Common Ways a Proposed Area Rule Breaks

Signed Value as Area

Signed area can be negative. It records orientation, so it is a diagnostic tool rather than a nonnegative area function.

\(A_{\mathrm{signed}}(R)=-A_{\mathrm{signed}}(R^{\mathrm{rev}})\)

Visual Disk Size

In a model, the drawn Euclidean size may not be the intrinsic area. The Poincare disk is the main warning case.

\(\hbox{drawn size} \ne A_{\mathbb H^2}\)

Overlapping Pieces

Additivity needs disjoint interiors. Overlap causes double counting unless an inclusion-exclusion correction is supplied.

\(A(R_1\cup R_2)\ne A(R_1)+A(R_2)\)

Classroom Prompt

For each failure, name the axiom that breaks and state one extra hypothesis that would repair the computation.

misconceptions are axiom failuresgood computations state their hypotheses
This is the misconception slide. Signed area is useful for orientation, but it is not an area function because it can be negative and can cancel. Visual size in a model picture is also not necessarily intrinsic area, especially in the Poincare disk. Finally, additivity requires a clean decomposition; overlapping interiors create double counting. Let students classify each failure by the axiom it breaks.
Chapter synthesis

Area Becomes Geometry When It Survives the Right Tests

  • Picture: a region, a triangulation, a disk-model triangle, or a finite dissection.
  • Predicate: nonnegative value, isometry invariance, additivity, and normalization.
  • Failure case: orientation sign, overlapping interiors, or trusting model-scale visual area.
  • Model contrast: Euclidean area uses polygonal measurement; hyperbolic area uses angle defect.
