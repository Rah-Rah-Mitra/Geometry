Lecture Route

Three Questions For Every Angle Claim

Metric

Can the claim be tested with a distance, dot product, tangent vector, or normalized direction?

Example: \(u\cdot v=0\) signals a right angle in a Euclidean coordinate chart.

Model-Dependent

Does the claim depend on which sets count as lines, rays, or geodesics?

Example: a bent Molton line changes the direction data carried by a ray.

Conformal

Does a representation distort distance while preserving tangent angles at intersections?

Example: Poincare disk geodesics use Euclidean tangent angles.

Working habit. State the primitive, compute the measurement, then name which transformations are allowed to preserve it.

Angle From Directions

Rays Contribute Directions, Not Lengths

\(\displaystyle \theta=\operatorname{atan2}\left(|u_xv_y-u_yv_x|,\;u\cdot v\right)\)

1

Choose nonzero direction vectors \(u\) and \(v\) along the two rays.

2

Normalize if convenient: scaling either vector by a positive number does not change \(\theta\).

3

Use dot and signed-area data together, so the measured opening is independent of the ray lengths.

direction invariant requires ray rule drawing scale irrelevant

Perpendicularity And Congruence

Right Angles Are Measurement Statements

Definition Pattern

Two rays through a common vertex are perpendicular when the accepted angle measure gives a right angle.

Coordinate test: \(u\cdot v=0\), so \(\theta=\pi/2\).

Congruence Pattern

Angles are congruent when their measures agree under the model's accepted measurement rule, not when a drawing merely looks similar.

Common Error

Do not infer a right angle from screen appearance. Identify the metric or angle-measure function first.

The notebook checks which measurement data stay fixed when the coordinate picture moves.

Metric test: dot product zeroCongruence test: same accepted angle measure
Present perpendicularity as a special angle condition rather than as a picture of a cross. Use the congruence graphic to connect synthetic language with computation: a rigid move changes coordinates but should not alter side lengths or angle measures, so the invariant inventory matters more than the screen picture.
Model Dependence

The Molton Plane Changes The Line Rule

A changed line system can make familiar slope intuition unreliable.

Warning

The carrier set may still look like the ordinary plane while the declared lines no longer behave like Euclidean straight lines.

Angle Consequence

An angle built from Molton rays must read direction from the model's ray rule. Ordinary Euclidean slope is no longer a complete description.

Molton Local Test

Same Points, Different Direction Data

Angle claim = vertex + two model rays + accepted measure

Reading Rule

  • Before the bend, a ray carries its familiar local direction.
  • At the bend, the model rule determines the tangent or one-sided direction to use.
  • After the bend, Euclidean-looking slope may not match model direction.

Inspection Target

Where a Molton line changes course, the visible corner is a property of the model line, not an accidental drawing artifact.

Model lesson: define line, then define angleEuclidean picture is not the authority
Explain the diagram as a rule book, not as a defect in drawing. On one side of the vertical divider the familiar line direction is kept; on the other side positive slopes are reinterpreted. Angle statements must therefore name the model before they can be measured.
Conformal Angle Measure

The Poincare Disk Distorts Distance But Preserves Tangent Angles

Geodesic Rule

Disk geodesics are diameters or arcs that meet the boundary circle orthogonally.

Angle Rule

At an interior intersection, measure the Euclidean angle between tangent directions to the two geodesics.

Conformal means local angle data survives the representation.

Separation

Hyperbolic lengths are not read as Euclidean chord lengths, even though hyperbolic angles are read from Euclidean tangents.

Conformal invariant: tangent angleNon-invariant under the display: Euclidean distance
For the Poincare disk, emphasize the contrast between distance and angle. Hyperbolic distances are distorted by the disk model, but angles are measured by Euclidean tangent directions at intersections of geodesics. That conformal feature lets local angle reasoning look familiar inside a non-Euclidean metric.

Euclidean coordinate plane Straight affine lines and their rays. Direction vectors at a vertex; dot and determinant compute the opening. Rigid motions, uniform scaling of rays, coordinate rotations.

A Reproducible Angle Number

\(A=(0,0),\;B=(2,0),\;C=(0.5,1.25)\)

1

Use direction vectors \(u=B-A=(2,0)\) and \(v=C-A=(0.5,1.25)\).

Recap And Diagnostic

Classify The Angle Statement Before Proving It

Takeaways

  • Angle measure begins with two model rays at a vertex.
  • Perpendicularity is a right-angle measurement, not a visual guess.
  • Congruence means equality under the accepted angle measure.
  • Molton lines show that line rules can change angle interpretation.
  • Poincare conformality preserves tangent angle while changing distance.

Exit Diagnostic

Label each claim as metric, model-dependent, conformal, or mixed.

  1. Scaling both ray endpoints away from the vertex leaves the opening fixed.
  2. A Molton ray may carry different direction data after crossing the divider.
  3. Two Poincare geodesics use tangent directions for their angle.
  4. Euclidean chord length in the disk equals hyperbolic distance.

Expected Catch

The fourth claim is false: the disk picture is conformal for angle, not Euclidean for distance.
