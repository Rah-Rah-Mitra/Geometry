Dimension two

Surfaces Collapse Einstein to Constant Gaussian Curvature

Two-dimensional simplification
On a surface, Ric = K g. Thus an Einstein metric is exactly a constant-curvature metric once lambda is constant.
integral K dA = 2 pi chi(M)
  • The sign of curvature points to the sign of Euler characteristic.
  • Topology is read through Gauss-Bonnet, not through a large tensor system.
  • This is the cleanest model for curvature becoming a global invariant.
Surface case03 / 16
The surface case is the chapter in miniature. Because Ricci is just Gaussian curvature times the metric, the Einstein condition says the curvature is constant and Gauss-Bonnet immediately ties the sign to Euler characteristic. This is a good place to warn that the simplicity is special to dimension two; higher dimensions retain much more curvature information outside Ricci.
Dimension three

In Three Dimensions, Einstein Forces Constant Sectional Curvature

Key mechanism
The three-dimensional curvature tensor is determined by the Ricci tensor. If the Ricci tensor is proportional to the metric, every sectional curvature is forced to be the same constant.
Topological reading
Compact examples are modeled by spherical, flat, or hyperbolic space forms according to the sign of lambda.
The low-dimensional lesson: fewer curvature components make the Einstein condition more rigid.
Three-dimensional rigidity04 / 16
In dimension three there is no Weyl curvature, so the Ricci tensor determines the full curvature tensor. That means the Einstein equation is much stronger than it first looks: it gives constant sectional curvature rather than merely constant Ricci in averaged directions. The topological consequences are therefore tied to space-form geometry and to how a group can act on the corresponding model space.
Dimension four

Four Dimensions Keep a Weyl Shadow

Curvature bookkeeping
In dimension four, Einstein removes the traceless Ricci piece, but it does not remove Weyl curvature. Topology sees both scalar curvature and the self-dual or anti-self-dual Weyl parts.
Riem = W+ + W- + scalar part
This is why four dimensions are the first place where the Einstein condition and differentiable topology interact in a genuinely delicate way.
Four-dimensional decomposition05 / 16
Move slowly here because this is the hinge between tensor geometry and topology. In four dimensions, the Einstein condition kills the traceless Ricci block but leaves the Weyl blocks alive. Characteristic numbers can be expressed through curvature integrals, so an Einstein metric must make analytic curvature data compatible with topological quantities such as Euler characteristic and signature.
A four-dimensional obstruction

The Hitchin-Thorpe Inequality Is a Topological Gate

Necessary condition
For an oriented compact Einstein four-manifold, the Euler characteristic and signature must satisfy a curvature-derived inequality.
2 chi(M) >= 3 |tau(M)|
Interpretation
Passing this inequality does not construct an Einstein metric. Failing it rules one out before analytic existence work begins.
Euler characteristic and signature06 / 16
Present the inequality as a necessary gate, not as a classification theorem. The proof idea is that Chern-Weil formulas express topological invariants through curvature, and the Einstein condition simplifies the integrands enough to force a sign inequality. The visual cone helps students distinguish an obstruction from an existence result: the allowed region still contains many manifolds whose Einstein question remains hard.

Group restrictions by curvature sign07 / 16
This slide organizes several group statements by the sign of the Einstein constant. Positive Ricci curvature is restrictive because comparison geometry supplies diameter and compactness consequences; Ricci-flat geometry is more delicate because flat factors can appear after passing to covers. Negative Ricci curvature does not impose the same finiteness pattern, so examples and obstructions must be read with the sign visible.
Bochner mechanism

Topology Is Often Detected by Vanishing

Energy identity pattern
A differential object satisfies an identity of the form: rough Laplacian plus curvature term equals a nonnegative norm. Positive curvature terms can force the object to vanish.
0 = integral ( |nabla alpha|^2 + curvature · |alpha|^2 )
  • Vanishing of harmonic 1-forms constrains the first Betti number.
  • Vanishing of spinors creates spinorial obstructions.
  • The same analytic grammar reappears with different bundles.
Bochner-style reasoning08 / 16
Introduce Bochner reasoning as a reusable proof shape. The argument does not inspect topology directly; it shows that a harmonic or parallel object must vanish, and then translates that vanishing into a topological statement. This prepares the spinorial obstruction slide, where the relevant object is a spinor rather than a 1-form.
Covers and deck transformations

The Universal Cover Reveals the Group Action

Covering principle
The universal cover carries the lifted Einstein metric. The fundamental group acts by deck transformations, so geometric restrictions on the cover become algebraic restrictions on the group.
What to inspect
Look for lines, flat factors, bounded diameter, and volume behavior. These are geometric signals that a group action cannot ignore.
Fundamental group via the cover09 / 16
The universal cover is the natural stage for fundamental group arguments. The lifted metric preserves the curvature condition, while the group appears as isometries of the cover. This lets the proof translate between geometry of geodesics and algebra of deck transformations, which is exactly the translation needed for splitting and finiteness results.
Spinorial obstruction

Spin Geometry Adds a Dirac Test

Lichnerowicz pattern
On a spin manifold, the square of the Dirac operator contains a connection Laplacian plus a scalar-curvature term.
D^2 = nabla* nabla + s / 4
Obstruction logic
If an index theorem predicts a nonzero harmonic spinor but positive scalar curvature would force all harmonic spinors to vanish, the metric cannot exist.
Spinorial obstruction10 / 16
Frame spin geometry as an additional detector, not as a replacement for curvature comparison. The Dirac operator has an analytic square, while its index is topological, so the same object is pulled in two directions. For positive Einstein constant, scalar curvature is positive, and that positivity can contradict a nonzero index prediction.
Scalar curvature sign

The Einstein Constant Decides Which Tests Are Active

lambda > 0
Positive scalar curvature activates Myers and Bochner-style vanishing. It is the natural zone for finiteness and spinorial contradictions.
lambda = 0
Ricci-flat metrics make vanishing terms neutral. Parallel objects and flat factors must be tracked through covers.
lambda < 0
Negative scalar curvature defeats many positive-curvature vanishing arguments. Obstructions usually need different input.
same equation, different topological pressure by sign
Sign discipline11 / 16
Pause on sign because it controls which arguments are legal. A theorem proved with positive scalar curvature cannot simply be carried into the Ricci-flat or negative case. This slide is a practical reading checklist: before applying Myers, Bochner, or a spinorial vanishing argument, first identify the sign of the Einstein constant and the compactness assumptions.
Cheeger-Gromoll

Nonnegative Ricci Curvature Splits Along Lines

Splitting theorem, conceptually
A complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature that contains a line splits isometrically as a product with a real factor.
M tilde = N x R^k
Topological consequence
For compact Ricci-flat manifolds, the universal cover separates an Euclidean factor from a compact simply connected part after passing to finite covers.
Splitting theorem12 / 16
The splitting theorem should be read as geometry discovering an algebraic product. A line is a globally minimizing geodesic in both directions, and nonnegative Ricci curvature prevents the manifold from bending around it in an arbitrary way. In the Ricci-flat Einstein case, this theorem explains why Euclidean factors and virtually abelian pieces of the fundamental group naturally appear.

Worked sorting exercise

Classify the First Obstruction Before Searching for a Metric

Candidate A
A compact oriented 4-manifold has 2 chi < 3 |tau|. The first question is not analytic existence; it is the Hitchin-Thorpe gate.
Candidate B
A spin manifold has an index forcing a harmonic spinor, while the proposed metric has positive scalar curvature. The first test is Dirac vanishing.
Candidate C
A compact Ricci-flat example has infinite fundamental group. The first move is to pass to the cover and inspect splitting.
name the gate, state its hypotheses, then decide what remains open

Recap

What Chapter 6 Adds to the Course

  • In dimension two, Einstein means constant Gaussian curvature and Gauss-Bonnet reads the topology.
  • In dimension three, Einstein metrics are space-form metrics because Ricci determines curvature.
  • In dimension four, Weyl curvature remains and characteristic numbers create obstructions.
  • Fundamental groups are studied through lifted metrics, covering actions, and splitting.
  • Spin and Bochner methods turn analytic vanishing into topological nonexistence tests.
End of lecture16 / 16
Close by returning to the chapter's central promise: Einstein geometry can rule topology in or out before one solves a metric equation. The low-dimensional cases show rigidity, the four-dimensional case shows characteristic-number obstructions, and the group and spin sections show how analysis detects topology indirectly. The takeaway is not a single classification, but a toolkit for asking which topological gate an Einstein metric must pass.
