The Einstein Condition

Definition
A pseudo-Riemannian or Riemannian metric g is Einstein when its Ricci tensor is pointwise proportional to the metric:
Ric(g) = λ g

For connected manifolds of dimension at least three, the contracted Bianchi identity forces λ to be constant.

Definition layerRic = λg
Emphasize that the Einstein condition compares two symmetric bilinear forms on every tangent space. It is not merely a scalar curvature condition. The metric measures lengths, Ricci measures averaged curvature response, and the equation says these measurements are pointwise proportional.

Ricci Curvature Is A Trace

Inspection Formula
If e_1,...,e_n is orthonormal and e_1 = v/|v|, then
Ric(v,v) = |v|² ∑i=2n K(e_1,e_i)

The Einstein equation says this average is the same multiple of |v|² in every direction.

Ricci curvature feelTrace, not total memory
Explain Ricci as a trace operation: choose one direction, average sectional curvatures through that direction, and obtain a number that depends quadratically on the chosen vector. The diagram intentionally compresses many planes through v into three representative slices.

Examples Calibrate The Equation

Space Forms

Constant sectional curvature gives the cleanest Einstein metrics.

Ric = (n-1)κg

Symmetric And Homogeneous

Large isometry groups reduce geometry to algebraic parameters.

Products And Scales

Products are Einstein only when the factors match the same constant after scaling.

λ1 = λ2
Examples are not decoration. They test whether the definition notices curvature, topology, symmetry, and normalization.
Examples layerConcrete witnesses
The examples should be read as calibration points. Constant curvature spaces satisfy the equation for transparent reasons, products show what can go wrong with scale, homogeneous spaces turn symmetry into equations, and Kahler examples point toward later complex geometry.

Worked Model: Constant Curvature

Computation
Suppose every two-plane has sectional curvature κ. Then each direction sees the same trace:
Ric(v,v) = (n-1)κ |v|²
Hence Ric = (n-1)κg.

This explains why round spheres, Euclidean spaces, and hyperbolic spaces are the first mental models.

Model calculationSpace forms
This is the smallest worked model in the lecture. For a space form, every sectional curvature is the same number kappa, so the trace over n minus one orthogonal directions immediately gives Ric equals n minus one times kappa times g.

Existence Has Several Doors

Symmetry Door

Reduce the equation to finitely many parameters or lower-dimensional differential equations.

Analysis Door

Choose a gauge, study a nonlinear elliptic system, and control regularity.

Topology Door

Use characteristic classes, fundamental groups, and comparison theorems to rule out candidates.

Existence layerMethods enter early
Frame existence as a problem with several entry points. Symmetry can reduce the equation to algebra or ordinary differential equations, analysis treats it as a nonlinear elliptic system after gauge choices, and topology supplies obstructions before analysis begins.

Dimension Changes The Subject

Dimension 2
Ric = Kg, so Einstein means constant Gaussian curvature.
Dimension 3
The Weyl tensor vanishes, so Einstein metrics have constant sectional curvature.
Dimension 4+
Weyl curvature can survive, giving richer local and global geometry.
Examples and topologyLow dimensions are special
Dimension changes the personality of the subject. In dimension two the equation is essentially constant Gaussian curvature. In dimension three it forces constant sectional curvature. In dimension four new Weyl curvature phenomena appear, and higher dimensions widen the example landscape.

Moduli: Solutions After Symmetry

Question
After identifying metrics related by diffeomorphism and scaling, what is the shape of the remaining solution space?

The raw set of metrics is enormous. The Einstein equation cuts out a smaller locus, and geometric equivalence collapses redundant directions.

Moduli layerEquation divided by equivalence
Introduce moduli as the space of solutions after removing changes that are only diffeomorphisms and scalings. The visual separates raw metrics, the Einstein locus, gauge orbits, and the quotient one actually wants to understand, especially when asking whether nearby solutions represent new geometry.

Deformations Need Gauge Control

Warning
A family of coordinate changes can move the metric without changing the geometry. Deformation theory must separate genuine motion from pure gauge.
Roadmap
E(g) = Ric(g) - λg
Linearize E, impose a gauge, study the kernel, then check nonlinear obstructions.
Moduli mechanicsFake directions are real work
The introduction points forward to deformation theory. Linearizing the Einstein equation alone is not enough because diffeomorphisms create fake directions. A useful mental model is equation, gauge condition, elliptic operator, kernel, then obstruction, with each step removing one source of ambiguity.

The Sign Of λ Sorts The Landscape

Positive

Compactness phenomena and strong restrictions often appear.

Ric > 0

Zero

Ricci-flat geometry connects to special holonomy and Calabi-Yau type questions.

Ric = 0

Negative

Hyperbolic models, complex methods, and noncompact behavior become central.

Ric < 0
scalar curvature S = nλ

The scalar curvature remembers the sign, but the Einstein equation contains the stronger tensor statement.

Normalization layerSign is a first invariant
Einstein constant sign is a first sorting invariant. Positive, zero, and negative cases have different compactness behavior, fundamental group restrictions, and model geometries. The slide should not be sold as a classification, only as a reliable first triage.

Useful Failure Modes

Common Diagnostic Failures
  • metric loses positivity
  • product factors have mismatched constants
  • deformation is only a diffeomorphism
  • scalar curvature hides anisotropic Ricci curvature
Hypotheses matterFailures teach the definition
Failure modes are pedagogically valuable here. A metric can fail positivity, a product can fail to have one Einstein constant, a deformation can be pure gauge, and a scalar curvature calculation can miss Ricci anisotropy. Each failure clarifies a hypothesis.

Four Questions

Mini Exercise: Diagnose The Claim

Exercise
A product metric g = g_1 ⊕ g_2 has Ric(g_1)=2g_1 and Ric(g_2)=5g_2. Is g Einstein? What change could make the answer different?

Check

The Einstein constant must be the same on every tangent direction of the product.

Repair

Rescale one factor when possible so both Ricci tensors match one common multiple.

Student promptProducts test normalization
Give students a few minutes to solve one prompt aloud. The best answers should use the equation Ric equals lambda g explicitly and should distinguish genuine geometric change from coordinate change or rescaling. The point is disciplined diagnosis.
