Definition

A Riemannian Submersion Is an Isometry in the Horizontal Directions

Definition
A smooth surjection π: (M,g) → (B,ḡ) is a Riemannian submersion when dπ has full rank and restricts to an isometry from H_p onto T_π(p)B.
  • V_p=ker dπ_p is the vertical space.
  • H_p=V_p⊥ is the horizontal space.
  • Only horizontal lengths are directly seen by the base.
Horizontal isometry03 / 16
Emphasize that the definition is metric, not just differential-topological. A submersion gives many possible horizontal complements, but the Riemannian metric selects the orthogonal one. The base metric is exactly the metric that makes horizontal vectors project without changing length.
Tangent bookkeeping

Every Vector Has Two Jobs: Fiber Motion and Base Motion

Projection operators
Write X=X^H+X^V. Calculations in the chapter repeatedly apply covariant derivatives and then keep only the horizontal or vertical component.
Common mistake
Horizontal does not mean tangent to a preferred embedded copy of the base. It means orthogonal to the fiber at the point under discussion.
The splitting changes from point to point, and that changing is exactly what the tensors detect.
Splitting tangent data04 / 16
This is a bookkeeping slide, but it is essential bookkeeping. Students should become comfortable decomposing a vector before asking about curvature. The tensors introduced next are built by differentiating these components and watching where the result lands.
O'Neill tensors

Two Tensors Measure the Failure of Product Geometry

Interaction tensors
Schematically, A records the vertical part of differentiating horizontal fields, while T records the horizontal part of differentiating vertical fields.
AXY=(∇XHYH)V,   TUV=(∇UVVV)H
These formulas are a memory aid for the lecture. The full definitions include the standard component conventions, but the geometric signal is already visible here.
Two defects from being a product05 / 16
The notation can look forbidding, so attach each tensor to a physical question. If horizontal directions are differentiated, do they remain horizontal? If fiber directions are differentiated, do they remain inside the fiber? The answers are precisely the correction terms that later enter curvature.
Curvature formulas

Base Curvature Is Not Merely Upstairs Curvature

Horizontal planes
For a horizontal two-plane, the base sectional curvature equals the corresponding upstairs curvature plus a nonnegative correction governed by A.
KB(dπX,dπY) = KM(X,Y) + 3|AXY|2
Interpretation
The base can look more positively curved because horizontal directions twist through the fibers.
O'Neill's horizontal curvature formula06 / 16
This is one of the lecture's anchor formulas. The sign and the factor are memorable because they say that the base curvature sees twisting of the horizontal distribution. Use it to warn students that projection is not curvature-neutral even when horizontal lengths are preserved.
Plane taxonomy

Curvature Splits by the Type of Two-Plane

Horizontal plane
Both directions project to the base. Curvature compares directly with B, corrected by A.
Vertical plane
Both directions lie in a fiber. Curvature sees the intrinsic fiber curvature plus how the fiber sits inside M.
Mixed plane
One direction is horizontal and one vertical. This is where the coupling is hardest to ignore.
Three curvature channels07 / 16
Pause here to classify every curvature formula by the plane it studies. Horizontal planes are easiest to remember because of the base comparison, but vertical and mixed planes are just as important for Ricci curvature. Ricci is a trace, so it necessarily sums information across all three channels.
From sectional to Ricci

Einstein Conditions Are Trace Balances Across Directions

Ricci decomposition
The Ricci tensor of M traces horizontal-horizontal, vertical-vertical, and mixed curvature contributions. Each trace carries base, fiber, and tensor terms.
Ric_M = Ric_B + Ric_F + traces(A,T) + divergence terms
Einstein reading
The equation Ric_M=λg becomes a system asking whether these traced contributions can be made proportional in every direction.
Tracing toward Einstein08 / 16
Connect this slide back to the course theme. Sectional curvature formulas are local measurements, but the Einstein condition asks for a tensor trace to be proportional to the metric. The lecture's calculations matter because they make that trace balance computable in structured families.
Canonical variation

Scale the Fibers, Then Watch the Einstein Equation Move

One-parameter metric family
Keep horizontal geometry fixed and multiply the vertical metric by a positive parameter t. This produces a controlled family g_t=g_H+t\,g_V.
gt = gH + t gV,   t>0
Why it helps
The Ricci components become functions of t. Solving the Einstein condition often reduces to matching horizontal and vertical eigenvalues.
Canonical variation09 / 16
Canonical variation is one of the chapter's most useful construction devices. It turns an infinite-dimensional metric question into a parameter search inside a geometrically meaningful family. The central teaching point is that scaling fibers changes curvature anisotropically, so an Einstein metric may occur at special parameter values.
Prototype example

Hopf-Type Fibrations Show the Mechanism Without Hiding the Fibers

Model to remember
A sphere can carry a fibration whose fibers are circles and whose base is a lower-dimensional symmetric space. The submersion formulas explain how round and squashed metrics differ.
  • Fiber direction: circle action.
  • Base direction: horizontal quotient geometry.
  • Variation: shrink or expand the circle fibers.
Hopf-type intuition10 / 16
This slide is deliberately schematic rather than a copied figure. The point is to provide a mental model for the chapter's examples: fibers can be genuine geometric circles, not just abstract kernels. Once the fiber direction is visible, canonical variation becomes a concrete operation.
Homogeneous applications

Group Quotients Turn Submersion Formulas Into Algebra

Lie algebra reduction
In homogeneous settings, vertical and horizontal spaces can often be read from an invariant decomposition of Lie algebras.
Einstein search
Symmetry reduces tensor equations to finitely many parameters, while submersion formulas explain how those parameters affect Ricci curvature.
This is why the chapter naturally follows the homogeneous and Kahler homogeneous chapters: quotient geometry turns examples into computable families.
Homogeneous quotients11 / 16
The chapter uses submersions as a bridge from group structure to curvature formulas. In a homogeneous quotient, the decomposition is not merely local; symmetry keeps the same algebraic pattern everywhere. This makes the Einstein equation much more approachable than it would be for an arbitrary metric.
Warped products

Warping Changes Fiber Scale From Point to Point

Metric ansatz
A warped product metric has the form g=g_B+f^2 g_F, where the fiber scale is controlled by a positive function on the base.
g = g_B + f(b)^2\,g_F
New curvature terms
Derivatives of f enter Ricci curvature. The fibers do not just have a different size; their size changes along the base.
Warped product ansatz12 / 16
Warped products should be contrasted with canonical variation. In canonical variation the fiber scale is a constant parameter, while in a warped product the scale is a function on the base. That functional dependence brings gradient and Hessian terms into the Ricci equations.

Warnings

Three Misreadings That Break the Geometry

Projection is not quotient by force
A smooth map with fibers is not automatically a Riemannian submersion. Horizontal lengths must match the base metric.
Horizontal is not integrable by default
If A is nonzero, horizontal vector fields can bracket into vertical directions. Local product intuition then misleads.
Scaling is anisotropic
Changing the fiber scale changes different curvature components in different ways. It is not just a uniform rescaling of the whole metric.
The useful examples are controlled precisely because these pitfalls are visible.
Common failure modes14 / 16
Use this as a diagnostic slide during discussion. Each warning corresponds to a real source of wrong calculations: ignoring the metric condition, assuming a product when the horizontal distribution twists, or treating canonical variation like ordinary scaling. The cure in each case is to return to the splitting and compute the tensor terms.
Learner lab

Three Short Experiments for Active Reading

Experiment 1
Draw a submersion with one-dimensional fibers. Mark V, H, and one vector decomposed into horizontal and vertical components.
Experiment 2
Assume A=0. Predict how the horizontal curvature formula simplifies, and explain what integrability intuition this suggests.
Experiment 3
For g_t=g_H+t g_V, list which quantities should depend on t before solving any Einstein equation.
The goal is to identify the moving part before doing algebra. Good solutions name the relevant distribution and the tensor that measures its behavior.

Recap

What Riemannian Submersions Add to the Einstein Story

  • A submersion preserves metric data only after restricting to horizontal directions.
  • O'Neill tensors are the correction terms for not being a product.
  • Sectional curvature formulas become Ricci formulas after tracing over horizontal and vertical frames.
  • Canonical variation and warped products turn geometry into tunable Einstein equations.
  • Homogeneous examples are effective because symmetry makes the splitting and traces algebraic.
