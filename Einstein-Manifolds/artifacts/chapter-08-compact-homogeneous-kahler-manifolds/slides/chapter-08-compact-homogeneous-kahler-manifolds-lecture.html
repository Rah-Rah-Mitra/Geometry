The Reading Problem

Chapter Question

When a compact manifold is both homogeneous and Kahler, which parts of the Einstein equation are forced by representation theory, and which parts remain metric choices?

  • Homogeneity replaces local variation by algebra at one base point.
  • Kahler geometry turns curvature into a closed real (1,1)-form.
  • Compactness makes cohomology and positivity unavoidable bookkeeping.
Use this slide to keep the lecture from becoming a list of Lie-theoretic definitions. The central move is that homogeneity lets us compute at a base point, while the Kahler condition forces the curvature form to carry complex type and cohomological meaning. Compactness is not a decorative hypothesis; it is what makes positivity, integration, and Chern-class constraints part of the story. Students should leave the slide with a question they can test on every example.

Adjoint Orbits As Manifolds

Model

For a compact Lie group G and xi in g, the orbit O_xi = Ad(G)xi is diffeomorphic to G/G_xi.

\(T_\xi O_\xi = \{[X,\xi] : X\in \mathfrak g\}\)

The isotropy subgroup records exactly which infinitesimal motions do not move the chosen point.

Develop the orbit picture before mentioning flags. A point in the Lie algebra moves under conjugation, and the tangent directions at that point are bracket directions. This makes adjoint orbits ideal homogeneous spaces: the stabilizer is visible, the tangent space has a quotient description, and invariant tensors can be described by their values at one point. The slide prepares the later formulas for symplectic and complex structures.

Roots Split The Tangent Space

Lie Algebra Decomposition

After choosing a maximal torus and a positive root system, the tangent representation of a flag orbit is assembled from root spaces not belonging to the stabilizer.

  • Root pairs supply real two-planes in the tangent space.
  • A choice of positive roots orients those planes.
  • Invariant tensors become diagonal data on these blocks.
The algebraic point of the slide is that the tangent space is not an undifferentiated vector space. It arrives decomposed into root blocks, and those blocks are the natural coordinates for invariant metrics and forms. A positive-root choice is more than notation; it fixes which half of the complexified tangent space is treated as holomorphic. This is the first place where representation theory begins to encode complex geometry.

Canonical Complex Structures

Construction

Declare the positive root spaces to be holomorphic tangent directions and the negative root spaces to be antiholomorphic directions.

Check

Closure of the selected roots under brackets gives the Newlander-Nirenberg integrability condition in this homogeneous setting.

Connect the abstract tensor J to a rule students can inspect on root spaces. On each real root plane, J is a quarter-turn, but the global issue is whether these choices are compatible with brackets. The positive-root convention provides that compatibility for the canonical structures used in the chapter. Emphasize that the construction is invariant, so defining it at the base point defines it everywhere.

The KKS Form Is The Orbit Area Form

Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau Form

At an orbit point xi, define a two-form on bracket tangent vectors by

\(\omega_\xi([X,\xi],[Y,\xi])=\langle \xi,[X,Y]\rangle\)

It is closed and invariant, so it turns the adjoint orbit into a symplectic homogeneous space.

This is the symplectic heart of the chapter. The formula is compact, but it hides a great deal: tangent vectors are represented by brackets, the pairing uses an invariant inner product, and the result is independent of representative choices. Present the green region as the infinitesimal area measured by the orbit itself rather than by an external coordinate chart. The closedness of the form is what lets the orbit enter Kahler geometry rather than merely almost-Hermitian geometry.

Compatibility Produces Kahler Metrics

Kahler Compatibility

An invariant complex structure J and invariant symplectic form omega define a Riemannian metric when omega(X,JX)>0.

  • The metric is automatically invariant.
  • The fundamental form is the original closed form.
  • Metric parameters are root-block weights.
Make the triangle of structures explicit. In general Kahler geometry one often starts with a metric and obtains a form, but on homogeneous orbits it is natural to build the form and complex structure first. Positivity is the remaining condition that converts the construction into a Riemannian metric. This slide is also the place to foreshadow that different root weights produce different invariant Kahler metrics on the same complex manifold.

Invariant Metrics Are Weighted Root Blocks

Parameter Picture

On a generalized flag manifold, an invariant Kahler metric is encoded by positive coefficients assigned to the irreducible tangent summands.

\(g = x_1 Q|_{\mathfrak m_1}+x_2 Q|_{\mathfrak m_2}+\cdots+x_r Q|_{\mathfrak m_r},\quad x_i>0\)

The geometry changes continuously as the weights change, but the symmetry and complex type remain fixed.

Students often need permission to think of invariant metrics as finite-dimensional data. The slide gives that permission: the continuous tensor field is determined by a handful of positive numbers once the homogeneous decomposition is fixed. This is not merely computational convenience; it is why compact homogeneous Kahler-Einstein examples can be studied by algebraic equations. Keep the phrase positive weights in view because it will matter for both Kahler classes and Einstein normalization.

Ricci Form: Curvature With Cohomology Memory

Kahler Ricci Form

The Ricci tensor is equivalently represented by a real closed (1,1)-form rho. Its de Rham class is fixed by the complex structure.

\([\rho]=2\pi c_1(M),\qquad \operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\rho(X,JY)\)

The Einstein condition asks for proportionality of two forms, not just equality of two tensors.

This slide should slow down the transition from metric curvature to topology. In Kahler geometry the Ricci tensor can be encoded as the Ricci form, and that form has a cohomology class determined by the first Chern class. The Einstein equation therefore cannot be separated from the Kahler class. On homogeneous spaces the representative can often be written invariantly, but its class is still the controlling memory of the complex structure.

The Einstein Test In A Kahler Class

Kahler-Einstein Target

For an invariant Kahler metric, the Einstein condition is equivalent to a scalar proportionality of invariant (1,1)-forms.

\(\rho = \lambda\omega\)
  • The sign of lambda is tied to c_1(M).
  • In the Fano homogeneous case, the canonical invariant metric is the natural target.
  • Solving means matching root-block coefficients.
The purpose here is to make the Einstein equation look finite-dimensional without making it look trivial. Because all relevant forms are invariant, one compares their coefficients on the same root-block basis. The proportionality constant is not arbitrary bookkeeping: it reflects the sign and scale of the first Chern class relative to the Kahler class. For compact homogeneous Kahler manifolds, this is why the algebraic matching problem can produce canonical examples.

Flag Manifolds As Running Examples

Example Ladder

Projective spaces and full flag manifolds illustrate the same construction at different levels of isotropy complexity.

  • CP^1: one positive root pair, one weight.
  • CP^n: isotropy irreducible, forced scale.
  • SU(3)/T^2: three root blocks, nontrivial coefficient matching.
Use examples as calibration devices rather than as a detour. The projective line is the simplest orbit where the symplectic, complex, and metric pictures can all be seen at once. Complex projective space shows how isotropy irreducibility collapses the metric parameters. The full flag manifold in three dimensions is the first classroom-friendly case where several root blocks make the Einstein coefficient comparison visibly nontrivial.

Moment-Polytope Intuition

Geometric Reading

A coadjoint orbit carries a Hamiltonian torus action. Its moment image is the convex hull of the Weyl orbit of the selected point.

The polytope is not a replacement for the manifold, but it gives a compact visual summary of weights, symmetry, and positivity.

This visual gives students a second way to remember adjoint and coadjoint orbits. The torus action compresses part of the symplectic geometry into a convex polytope, whose vertices come from the Weyl group orbit. Avoid overclaiming: the polytope does not contain all curvature information. Its lecture value is that it makes positivity cones, root directions, and symmetry visible in one picture.

Proof Roadmap: Why Homogeneity Helps

1. Reduce

Replace tensor fields on G/K by K-invariant algebraic data on m.

2. Split

Use roots to decompose m_C into holomorphic and antiholomorphic summands.

3. Form

Construct invariant closed (1,1)-forms and identify which ones are positive.

4. Compare

Compute the invariant Ricci form and solve rho=lambda omega coefficient by coefficient.

This is a proof-architecture slide, not a theorem statement. The idea is to show where each hypothesis is used: homogeneity gives the reduction, compact Lie theory gives the root decomposition, Kahler geometry gives closed positive forms, and curvature computation gives the Ricci comparison. If students get lost in formulas later, send them back to these four verbs. They form the mental checklist for reading the chapter.

Invariant Ricci Forms Are Algebraic

Base-Point Computation

For homogeneous Kahler metrics, the Ricci form is determined by its values on bracket-generated tangent vectors at the base point.

\(\rho_o([X,o],[Y,o])\quad \text{depends on structure constants and weights}\)

This is the bridge from differential geometry to a finite list of root-block coefficients.

This slide explains why the chapter can discuss Ricci curvature without writing a local coordinate curvature computation on every page. Homogeneity moves the computation to the base point, while the bracket structure tells us how invariant vector fields interact. The metric weights still matter, so the result is not purely topological. The output is algebraic enough to compare with the Kahler form block by block.

Cohomology Fixes The Target Class

Class Constraint

The Ricci form may vary as a representative, but its cohomology class is fixed by the complex manifold.

  • The Kahler form chooses a point in the Kahler cone.
  • The Einstein condition requires that point to lie on the Ricci ray.
  • Homogeneity supplies canonical candidates but not arbitrary freedom.
The class picture prevents a common overemphasis on local curvature formulas. In Kahler geometry the Ricci form remembers the first Chern class, so changing the metric changes the representative but not the cohomology class. The Kahler form, by contrast, records the selected Kahler class. The Einstein equation can therefore be read as an alignment statement inside cohomology before any pointwise coefficient comparison is attempted.

Common Misreadings

Misreading 1

Every invariant almost complex structure on a homogeneous space is integrable.

Correction: integrability is a bracket condition, and the root-choice construction is doing real work.

Misreading 2

The KKS form automatically makes every invariant metric Kahler.

Correction: compatibility and positivity with the chosen J must be checked.

Misreading 3

The Einstein equation is only a Riemannian equation here.

Correction: in the Kahler setting it is also a cohomological proportionality condition.

Pause here before the final exercise and synthesis. These misconceptions are natural because each construction looks canonical once it has been built. The correction is that compatibility conditions are doing the mathematical labor: brackets for integrability, positivity for the metric, and Chern-class information for Ricci curvature. This slide is a useful diagnostic for whether students are following the dependencies rather than memorizing names.

Exercise: The Three-Block Flag Test

Board Problem

Suppose a homogeneous flag manifold has three isotropy summands m_1,m_2,m_3 and an invariant Kahler metric with positive weights x_1,x_2,x_3.

Describe what information is needed to decide whether it can satisfy rho=lambda omega.

  • Identify the root blocks and the chosen complex orientation.
  • Write the Kahler form coefficients in the same basis as the Ricci form.
  • Ask whether all ratios rho_i/x_i agree.
This exercise is deliberately qualitative so that it works at the board without a long computation. The key answer is not a numerical value; it is the list of data required to compare the forms. Students should name the tangent summands, the complex orientation, the Kahler coefficients, and the Ricci coefficients. If they can explain why equal ratios matter, they understand the finite-dimensional reduction of the Einstein equation.

Takeaways

Orbit

Compact homogeneous Kahler manifolds are efficiently modeled by adjoint or coadjoint orbits and flag quotients.

Structure

Positive roots select the complex structure; the KKS form supplies the natural symplectic side.

Einstein

The Kahler-Einstein condition becomes rho=lambda omega, a comparison of invariant form coefficients.
