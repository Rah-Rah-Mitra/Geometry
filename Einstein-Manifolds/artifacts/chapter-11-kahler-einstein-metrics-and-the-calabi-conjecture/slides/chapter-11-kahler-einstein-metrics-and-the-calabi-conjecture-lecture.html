Reading Map: One Equation, Three Languages

Lecture contract

We read the Kahler-Einstein problem as a compatibility problem among curvature, complex volume, and cohomology.

  • First translate \( \operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g \) into \( \rho=\lambda\omega \).
  • Then rewrite the equation using a Kahler potential.
  • Finally separate the negative, zero, and positive \(c_1\) regimes.
Use this map to prevent the lecture from sounding like a sequence of named theorems. The same problem is being translated through three languages: Riemannian curvature, complex differential forms, and nonlinear elliptic equations. Emphasize that the arrows are dependencies, not historical decoration. A student should be able to say which box supplies the unknown, which supplies the equation, and which supplies a necessary obstruction.

Kahler-Einstein Means Proportional Forms

Definition

A Kahler metric is Kahler-Einstein when its Ricci form is a constant multiple of its Kahler form:

\(\rho=\lambda\,\omega.\)
Cohomology check

Since \([\rho]=2\pi c_1(M)\), the equation forces \(2\pi c_1(M)=\lambda[\omega]\).

Make the first serious move slowly. In ordinary Riemannian language, Einstein means the Ricci tensor is proportional to the metric; in Kahler language this becomes the proportionality of two closed real (1,1)-forms. That translation is powerful because it immediately creates a cohomology constraint. Before anyone solves a PDE, the Kahler class has to sit on the correct ray determined by the first Chern class.

The Ricci Form Measures Complex Volume Distortion

Local formula

In holomorphic coordinates, the Ricci form of a Kahler metric is

\(\rho=-i\,\partial\bar\partial\log\det(g_{i\bar j}).\)

The determinant records how complex volume changes from chart to chart; the second derivative turns that volume distortion into curvature.

This slide gives the local computational handle for the whole chapter. The determinant of the Hermitian metric matrix measures complex volume, and applying partial partial-bar to its logarithm produces the Ricci form. This is why the Ricci side of the Einstein equation can be written as a nonlinear expression in one potential. It is also why the equation remembers both analysis and holomorphic geometry.

Fix A Kahler Class, Vary A Potential

Potential ansatz

Within a fixed Kahler class, write

\(\omega_\phi=\omega_0+i\,\partial\bar\partial\phi,\)

with positivity \( \omega_\phi>0 \).

  • The cohomology class stays fixed.
  • The metric coefficients change nonlinearly.
  • The unknown becomes a scalar function.
Explain why potentials are the right variables. If the Kahler class is fixed, adding i partial partial-bar phi changes the representative but not the cohomology class. That converts the geometric unknown from a full tensor into a scalar function subject to a positivity condition. The positivity condition should be heard as part of the equation, because losing it means the form is no longer a Kahler metric.

The Einstein Equation Becomes Complex Monge-Ampere

Schematic equation
\((\omega_0+i\partial\bar\partial\phi)^n=e^{F-\lambda\phi}\omega_0^n.\)

The function \(F\) encodes the chosen background Ricci data.

The left side is a determinant; the right side prescribes a volume form with the correct total mass.

Here the lecture should cross from geometry into analysis. The top exterior power of omega phi is the determinant of the perturbed Hermitian matrix, so the equation is a complex Monge-Ampere equation. The right side is not arbitrary: its integral must match the fixed volume of the Kahler class. Stress that ellipticity is tied to the positivity of omega phi, which is why a priori estimates are central.

Calabi's Prescription Problem

Calabi conjecture, working form

In a fixed Kahler class, prescribe a representative of \(2\pi c_1(M)\) as the Ricci form of a unique Kahler metric in that class.

Yau's resolution

The solution follows from solving the corresponding complex Monge-Ampere equation with the required normalization.

State the Calabi conjecture as a prescription theorem, not merely as a slogan about Ricci-flat metrics. The input is a Kahler class and a Ricci-form representative in the first Chern class; the output is a Kahler metric in the same class with exactly that Ricci form. Yau's theorem supplies existence and uniqueness after the standard normalization. This result is the analytic engine behind the zero and negative first Chern class cases.

Three First-Chern-Class Regimes

Negative \(c_1\)

The canonical class is positive. The Kahler-Einstein constant is negative, and Aubin-Yau theory gives existence in the required class.

Zero \(c_1\)

The Ricci form can be prescribed as zero. Yau's theorem gives Ricci-flat Kahler metrics in each Kahler class.

Positive \(c_1\)

The Fano case is delicate. Cohomology is necessary but not sufficient, and automorphisms can obstruct existence.

Use the sign trichotomy as the memory spine for the middle of the lecture. When the first Chern class is negative or zero, the Calabi-Yau theorem provides a clean existence story after choosing the appropriate class. Positive first Chern class is the unstable case: it is geometrically rich but not controlled by the cohomology condition alone. This prepares the audience for the Futaki obstruction and extremal metrics.

Negative First Chern Class: Coercive Direction

Existence pattern

For \(c_1(M)<0\), the required Kahler class is negative proportional to \(c_1(M)\), and the Einstein constant is negative.

\(\rho=-\omega\)

after the conventional normalization of the chosen class

Do not reduce the negative case to a sign slogan. The important teaching point is that the cohomology constraint identifies the class and the analytic problem has the right coercive behavior. In a lecture, this is the first place to say that the estimates are the theorem's substance, even though the slide only records the normalized target equation. Students should hear that existence is global and analytic, not a local curvature adjustment.

Zero First Chern Class: Ricci-Flat Kahler Metrics

Calabi-Yau theorem

If \(c_1(M)=0\), each Kahler class contains a unique Ricci-flat Kahler metric after normalization.

  • The Ricci form target is zero.
  • The Kahler class is still freely chosen.
  • The holonomy may reduce, producing special geometric structure.
Present the zero case as the cleanest form of the Calabi-Yau theorem. The first Chern class vanishes, so the Ricci form can be prescribed as zero, and the theorem gives a Ricci-flat representative in every Kahler class. It is worth saying that Ricci-flat does not mean flat; the full curvature tensor can remain highly nontrivial. This is the bridge from Einstein geometry to special holonomy examples later in the course.

Positive First Chern Class: Necessary Is Not Sufficient

Fano warning

When \(c_1(M)>0\), the class condition asks for \([\omega]=2\pi c_1(M)\), but this condition alone does not guarantee a Kahler-Einstein metric.

Holomorphic vector fields and stability phenomena enter because the automorphism group can move the would-be minimizer.

This slide should slow down the positive case. It is tempting to say that the same theorem solves everything once the class is fixed, but that is false in the Fano direction. The cohomology condition identifies the natural class, yet holomorphic automorphisms can obstruct the existence of a Kahler-Einstein metric. This is where the lecture turns from Monge-Ampere solvability alone toward invariants such as the Futaki character.

The Futaki Character Detects Drift

Obstruction principle

A holomorphic vector field can pair with the normalized Ricci potential to produce a character. For a positive Kahler-Einstein metric, this obstruction must vanish.

\(\mathfrak F(X)=\int_M X(h)\,\omega^n\)

The formula is schematic for lecture use: \(h\) is the Ricci potential in the chosen class.

Explain the Futaki character as a diagnostic for motion along holomorphic symmetries. If a vector field creates a preferred drift of the Ricci potential, then a genuine positive Kahler-Einstein metric cannot sit still in the required way. The integral formula is included to show that the obstruction is global, not a pointwise defect. Keep the statement necessary rather than sufficient in this chapter-level treatment.

Aubin's Continuity Path: Deform The Equation

Continuity method

Replace the target equation by a family indexed by \(t\). Show the solution set is nonempty, open, and closed under uniform estimates.

  1. Start at a solvable equation.
  2. Use linearized invertibility for openness.
  3. Use a priori bounds to pass to limits.
This is the proof-method slide, and it should be presented as a template rather than as a full proof. Aubin's continuity method deforms an equation known to be solvable into the desired equation. Openness comes from an implicit-function theorem after understanding the linearized operator, while closedness is the hard estimate package. Students should recognize this pattern when later chapters solve geometric PDEs by deformation.

What The Estimates Must Control

Zero order

Bound the potential \(\phi\) after fixing its additive normalization.

Second order

Control the eigenvalues of \(g_\phi\) relative to the background metric.

Higher order

Use elliptic bootstrapping once the equation is uniformly elliptic.

Compactness

Extract a smooth limiting potential and verify the limiting equation.

The estimates slide is meant to demystify the word existence. A normalized potential bound prevents the unknown from escaping by an additive constant; second-order estimates keep the metric positive and comparable to the background; higher-order estimates then follow from elliptic regularity. This sequence is a proof technology, but it also gives a diagnostic for failed solution attempts. If any rung is missing, the continuity path can break before reaching the endpoint.

Extremal Metrics: When Einstein Is Too Rigid

Calabi's broader variational idea

Instead of demanding constant Ricci proportionality, minimize the squared scalar curvature functional within a Kahler class.

\(\mathcal C(\omega)=\int_M S(\omega)^2\,\omega^n.\)

The Euler-Lagrange condition says the gradient of scalar curvature is holomorphic.

Use extremal metrics as the chapter's widening move. If Kahler-Einstein metrics do not exist, Calabi's variational framework still asks for canonical metrics in a Kahler class. The functional integrates scalar curvature squared, and its critical points have holomorphic scalar-curvature gradient. This does not replace Kahler-Einstein geometry; it explains how the same search for canonical representatives continues when the Einstein condition is too restrictive.

Reference Model: Projective Space

Fubini-Study metric

A standard local potential is

\(\phi=\log(1+|z|^2).\)

It gives the model positive Kahler-Einstein metric on complex projective space.

Use this as the sanity check for signs, normalizations, and the meaning of positive \(c_1\).

Give students one stable example before closing the theory. Complex projective space with the Fubini-Study metric is the model where the positive first Chern class case succeeds. The local potential log one plus norm squared makes the metric concrete, while the global construction reminds students that a local formula must glue correctly across projective charts. It is also the reference point for checking normalizations in the positive Einstein equation.

Common Mistakes To Defuse

Class versus form

Matching \(c_1(M)\) in cohomology does not mean the displayed Ricci form already equals the desired multiple of \(\omega\).

Ricci-flat versus flat

Ricci-flat Kahler metrics may have nonzero full curvature; only the trace part vanishes.

Positive case

Fano positivity identifies the natural class, but automorphisms and stability issues still matter.

This slide is a useful pause before the recap. Students often collapse cohomology equality into equality of forms, or Ricci-flatness into complete flatness, or Fano positivity into automatic existence. Each mistake erases one layer of the chapter's logic. Ask the class to rephrase each warning as a positive rule for reading a Kahler-Einstein existence theorem.

Takeaways For The Einstein Manifolds Course

  • Kahler-Einstein metrics satisfy \( \rho=\lambda\omega \), so curvature and cohomology are tied from the start.
  • The potential method turns the equation into complex Monge-Ampere with positivity constraints.
  • Aubin-Yau methods settle the negative and zero first Chern class regimes.
  • The positive case needs extra obstruction and stability information; extremal metrics broaden the target.
