Definition

A Homogeneous Manifold Looks The Same From Every Point

Definition A Riemannian manifold (M,g) is homogeneous when its isometry group acts transitively on M. Fixing a point o gives
M ≅ G/H,    H = {g in G : g o = o}.
The stabilizer H remembers the symmetries that do not move the base point; the quotient remembers all possible positions of that point.
Base point plus orbit equals global model3 / 16
The main definition is geometric rather than coordinate-based. A Riemannian manifold is homogeneous when its isometry group can move any point to any other point. Once a base point is fixed, the entire manifold can be viewed as cosets G over H, where H records the symmetries that keep the base point fixed.
Metric Data

An Invariant Metric Is A Base-Point Inner Product

At The Base PointChoose an inner product < , > on T_oM.
CompatibilityRequire <h u, h v> = <u,v> for the isotropy action of every h in H.
PropagationPush the inner product by the group action to define g at every point of G/H.
Invariant metrics are finite data when the isotropy representation is understood4 / 16
This slide is the key algebraic translation. Instead of choosing a metric tensor independently at every point, choose an inner product on the tangent model at the base point. The condition that this inner product be invariant under the isotropy representation is exactly what lets it propagate consistently across the orbit.
Lie Algebra Model

The Tangent Space Comes From A Complement

Reductive Decompositiong = h + m, where m is identified with T_o(G/H).
Adapted ComplementThe condition [h,m] subset m makes the isotropy action visible on the tangent model.
This is the algebraic place where the quotient geometry starts behaving like a tangent-space calculation.
The quotient tangent space is represented inside the Lie algebra5 / 16
A reductive decomposition is a controlled way to identify tangent vectors with Lie algebra directions. The complement m should be preserved by the isotropy action, so brackets with h do not push m out of the tangent model. This is a useful bookkeeping device, not merely a notational convenience.
Finite-Dimensional Metric Families

Representation Blocks Become Metric Parameters

Block FormWhen the isotropy representation splits, invariant metrics often reduce to positive scale parameters x_i.
RestrictionOnly inner products compatible with the isotropy action define well-posed homogeneous metrics.
PayoffThe Einstein equation becomes a finite system for the x_i, rather than a PDE on all of M.
Metric freedom is representation-theoretic freedom6 / 16
Many homogeneous examples become finite-dimensional because the isotropy representation splits into irreducible pieces. On inequivalent summands, an invariant metric often scales each block by a positive parameter. The Einstein equation then becomes a system of algebraic equations in those scale parameters.
Curvature Mechanism

Curvature Is Read From Noncommuting Directions

Schematic Principle
[X,Y] = [X,Y]h + [X,Y]m

The bracket components, together with the invariant metric on m, determine the connection and curvature at the base point.

Homogeneity means this computation at o is enough; group translations carry the result everywhere.
Bracket algebra replaces coordinate differentiation7 / 16
The important message is that curvature still measures noncommuting directions, but on a homogeneous space the noncommutation is recorded by Lie brackets. Connection and curvature formulas can be assembled from the metric and the bracket table. This is why homogeneous Einstein geometry is computationally tangible.

Einstein Condition

The Equation Is Checked At One Point

Ricg = λ g
Homogeneous ReductionBoth Ric_g and g are invariant symmetric forms on m.
Practical TestCompare eigenvalues block by block after normalizing by the metric parameters.
Invariant tensors can be compared on m9 / 16
The Einstein condition compares two invariant bilinear forms on the same tangent model. Because both sides are invariant, it is enough to check the equation at the base point. For parametrized metrics this usually produces polynomial or rational equations, together with the positivity restrictions on the metric parameters.
Distinguished Subclass

Symmetric Spaces Add A Reflection At Every Point

SymmetryAt each point there is an isometry whose differential is minus the identity on the tangent space.
ConsequenceThe tangent bracket component [m,m]_m vanishes in the symmetric model.
Why It MattersCurvature formulas simplify, and many standard examples become canonical Einstein spaces.
Symmetric spaces are homogeneous spaces with point reflections10 / 16
Symmetric spaces are the cleanest subclass because there is an involution that reverses tangent directions at each point. Algebraically, this forces the bracket of two tangent directions back into the isotropy algebra. That extra bracket structure simplifies curvature and explains why symmetric spaces are standard reservoirs of Einstein metrics.

Fast Example Mechanism

Irreducible Isotropy Leaves Almost No Metric Freedom

Irreducible IsotropyThe tangent representation has no nontrivial invariant subspaces.
Metric ConsequenceInvariant symmetric forms are forced into a very small family, often a single scale.
Einstein ConsequenceWith one invariant scale, Ricci has no independent block ratio to mismatch.
Representation theory can make the Einstein equation automatic12 / 16
This is one of the fastest routes to examples. If the isotropy representation is irreducible, Schur-style reasoning says that invariant symmetric forms have very little room to vary. Under the usual hypotheses this makes an invariant metric automatically Einstein up to scale, which is why representation theory is so efficient here.
Normal Homogeneous Models

Some Metrics Descend From The Group Itself

ConstructionStart with an Ad(G)-invariant inner product on g, then restrict to the complement m.
BenefitGroup-level invariance gives strong curvature control and makes many examples transparent.
ScopeNormal homogeneous metrics are important, but they are not the only homogeneous metrics.
Normal homogeneous is a powerful special construction13 / 16
A normal homogeneous metric is built from a group-level inner product, often coming from an Ad-invariant form. This makes geodesic and curvature calculations especially structured. It is useful to emphasize that normal homogeneous is stronger than homogeneous, so it gives convenient examples but not the full landscape.

Use the two-block model as a board calculation template. The constants in the Ricci expressions stand for bracket contributions between summands, while the variables are metric scales. The Einstein condition asks for the two normalized Ricci values to agree, producing a concrete algebraic constraint.

The chapter is also teaching what can go wrong. A metric may be invariant but not Einstein, a complement may be poorly adapted to the isotropy action, or a parameter choice may leave the positive cone.
