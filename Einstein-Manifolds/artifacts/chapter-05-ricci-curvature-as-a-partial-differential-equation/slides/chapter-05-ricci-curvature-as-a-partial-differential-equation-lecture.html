Roadmap

What Changes When Ricci Becomes an Equation?

Unknown
The metric components g_{ij}(x) are the fields. Their first derivatives enter the connection; their second derivatives enter curvature.
Equation
Prescribing a symmetric tensor T asks for Ric(g)=T. The Einstein case is the special feedback law T=λg.
Constraint
Diffeomorphism invariance hides ellipticity. One must separate coordinate freedom from genuine curvature content.
metric jet → connection → Riemann tensor → contracted Ricci tensor
Operator viewpoint02 / 16
Use this slide to establish the central translation. The unknown is not a function but a positive definite matrix of functions. The equation is second order and nonlinear, but a large part of the difficulty comes from coordinates: pulling a solution back by a diffeomorphism is still a solution in transformed data.
Anatomy of the operator

The Ricci Tensor Sees Second Derivatives Plus Lower Terms

Local form, schematically
Ric(g) = g^{-1} * ∂²g + g^{-2} * ∂g * ∂g
The principal part controls PDE type; the quadratic first-derivative terms control nonlinearity.
  • g^{-1} couples the equation to the unknown itself.
  • Second derivatives appear only through curvature combinations.
  • Coordinate changes can erase or create apparent principal terms.
Nonlinear second-order operator03 / 16
The formula is intentionally schematic. It prevents a common misunderstanding: Ricci is not simply a Laplacian of the metric. Its highest derivative part has a Laplace-like core only after choosing a useful gauge, while the inverse metric and first derivative products keep the equation nonlinear.
Diffeomorphism invariance

The First Obstruction Is Not Geometric: It Is Gauge

Degenerate principal symbol
If h = L_X g comes from an infinitesimal coordinate change, then the linearized Ricci operator contains directions that are not controlled as elliptic unknowns.
Working rule
Solve after adding a coordinate condition, then verify that the auxiliary gauge term vanishes for the geometric problem.
This is the local version of a recurring Einstein-metric pattern: the equation is geometric, but the analysis usually begins only after choosing coordinates or a slice.
Gauge before ellipticity04 / 16
Stress that gauge is not a nuisance added by analysts; it is forced by geometry. The same metric up to pullback gives the same curvature content, so the raw differential operator cannot behave like an elliptic system on every component until this redundancy is controlled.
Pointwise solvability

At One Point, Ricci Prescription Is Mostly Linear Algebra

Jet-level question
Fix g(p) and first-order normalizations. Which symmetric tensors can be realized as Ric_g(p) by choosing second derivatives of g?
  • The target space is S²T*_pM.
  • Curvature symmetries remove impossible components before Ricci is formed.
  • Local PDE obstructions require neighborhoods, not just a point.
Pointwise versus local05 / 16
The important distinction is between prescribing Ricci at a point and prescribing it on an open set. At a point, after choosing normal coordinates, enough second jet freedom remains to hit many targets. On an open set, derivatives of the target and compatibility identities begin to matter.
Local obstruction

The Contracted Bianchi Identity Supplies a Compatibility Test

divg Ric(g) = 1/2 d Scal(g)
If Ric(g)=T
The prescribed tensor cannot be arbitrary relative to the unknown metric: its divergence must match the scalar trace relation determined by g.
For Einstein data T = λg, the identity immediately forces λ to be constant in dimensions greater than two.
Bianchi as PDE compatibility06 / 16
Present Bianchi as a conservation law for curvature. It is not an extra theorem one checks after solving; it is a necessary compatibility condition any target tensor must survive. This also explains why prescribed Ricci curvature is subtler than prescribing a random symmetric matrix field.
Making an elliptic problem

A Coordinate Condition Turns the Principal Part Into a Laplacian

Harmonic-coordinate slogan
In a good coordinate gauge, the Ricci equation has leading term
Ricij(g) ≈ -1/2 gab ∂a∂b gij + lower terms.
DeTurck idea
Add a carefully chosen Lie-derivative correction to kill gauge drift, solve the adjusted system, then prove the correction vanishes.
Gauge-fixed Ricci equation07 / 16
This is the analytical turning point. The slide should not be sold as magic: the Laplacian form is a leading-order statement, not the whole equation. The point is that once a slice is chosen, standard elliptic intuition becomes relevant.
Local construction

Einstein Metrics Can Be Built Locally From Analytic Data

Cauchy-Kowalevski pattern
If the equation is written so one normal second derivative is solved for, analytic initial data can determine a local analytic solution.
What this does not say
Local analytic existence is not a global existence theorem, and it does not remove compatibility or gauge questions in smooth categories.
The pedagogical lesson: local Einstein construction is possible because the equation can be organized as a solvable analytic system, not because curvature is algebraically free everywhere.
Analytic local existence08 / 16
Describe this as a local germ construction. Students should hear the difference between prescribing enough analytic Cauchy data and solving a complete global boundary value problem. The visual slice emphasizes that the solution is grown near a hypersurface.
Regularity

Once the Equation Is Elliptic, Regularity Bootstraps

Bootstrapping principle
If a weak or low-regularity metric satisfies a gauge-fixed Einstein equation, elliptic estimates can improve the differentiability of g.
Why it matters
Regularity converts a formally meaningful solution into a geometric metric whose curvature identities can be interpreted classically.
Elliptic regularity09 / 16
Connect this to the appendix material on elliptic operators without assuming the audience has all estimates memorized. The local message is that the PDE does not merely test existing smooth metrics; under suitable hypotheses it can force improved regularity.
Analyticity

Einstein Metrics Are Locally Analytic in Suitable Coordinates

Core statement
In harmonic-type coordinates, an Einstein metric satisfies an analytic elliptic system. Smooth solutions therefore inherit local real analyticity.
Ric(g) = λg ⇒ analytic coefficients after gauge
Analyticity is a rigidity feature: local data can propagate more strongly than one might expect from a generic smooth nonlinear equation.
Analytic rigidity10 / 16
The phrase suitable coordinates is doing real work. Analyticity is not a coordinate-free claim about arbitrary smooth charts; it is obtained after placing the metric in coordinates where the Einstein equation becomes an analytic elliptic system.
Uniqueness

Local Uniqueness Depends on the Data and the Gauge

Unique continuation intuition
Elliptic analytic systems resist local branching: sufficiently compatible data can pin down a germ.
But uniqueness is modulo diffeomorphism
Two coordinate expressions may represent the same geometry. A uniqueness statement must say which gauge or boundary data are fixed.
The local question compares geometric germs only after the coordinate identifications, normalized data, and regularity class have all been specified.
Uniqueness modulo coordinate freedom11 / 16
This slide should temper the previous existence discussion. A construction theorem and a uniqueness theorem are not opposites; they ask different things. Emphasize that coordinate freedom must be fixed before comparing two local metrics component by component.
Nonexistence

Failure Can Occur Before Solving Begins

Three local failure modes
  • the target tensor violates a differential identity;
  • the chosen initial data are incompatible with constraints;
  • the gauge-fixed system loses ellipticity or positivity of the metric.
Nonexistence in this chapter is not only global topology. Local PDE structure can already prohibit a proposed Ricci prescription.
Local obstruction gates12 / 16
Make nonexistence concrete. A proposed right-hand side may look harmless as a symmetric tensor field, yet fail a necessary identity once one asks for it to be Ricci of some metric. This is a PDE obstruction, not just a topological obstruction.
Worked local model

In Dimension Two, Conformal Coordinates Expose the PDE

Metric ansatz
g = e2u(x,y)(dx² + dy²)
The Ricci tensor is controlled by the Gaussian curvature, and curvature reduces to a scalar second-order equation for u.
K = - e-2u Δu,    Ric = K g
This model is not the whole chapter; it is a small chart where the PDE character of Ricci becomes visible without tensor-index overload.
Scalar reduction in a model case13 / 16
Use the conformal model as a sanity check. In two dimensions, Ricci contains no more information than Gaussian curvature, so the equation collapses to a scalar equation. That makes the second-order nature and the role of coordinates immediately visible.

Learner lab

Three Experiments for Reading the Chapter Actively

Experiment 1
In the conformal model g=e^{2u}(dx²+dy²), choose a quadratic u. Predict the sign of K at the origin from Δu.
Experiment 2
Propose a target tensor T. Before solving, write the Bianchi-style compatibility condition it would have to satisfy.
Experiment 3
Explain why two coordinate formulas for a metric might be the same local Einstein geometry after a diffeomorphism.
The goal is not computation volume; it is knowing which obstruction appears first.
Active checks15 / 16
Use this slide as a structured checkpoint rather than as extra homework. The first prompt asks students to connect a sign computation to geometric curvature in the conformal model. The second prompt makes them test a proposed Ricci target before trying to solve for a metric, which reinforces the role of compatibility identities. The third prompt returns to gauge: two formulas may describe the same local geometry after a diffeomorphism, so comparison requires a chosen normalization. Ask for compact explanations that name the first obstruction or freedom they detect.
Recap

What Chapter 5 Adds to the Einstein-Manifold Story

  • Ric(g) is a nonlinear second-order differential operator on the metric.
  • Pointwise prescription is a jet problem; local prescription is a PDE problem.
  • Bianchi identities and gauge freedom are structural, not optional.
  • Gauge-fixed Einstein equations bring elliptic regularity and analyticity into play.
  • Existence, uniqueness, and nonexistence must be stated with their data and coordinate choices visible.
End of lecture16 / 16
Close by tying the chapter back to the course arc. Earlier chapters built curvature and variational language; this one teaches how local Einstein geometry is constrained by the analytic behavior of the Ricci operator. The key takeaway is that geometric freedom and PDE rigidity coexist.
