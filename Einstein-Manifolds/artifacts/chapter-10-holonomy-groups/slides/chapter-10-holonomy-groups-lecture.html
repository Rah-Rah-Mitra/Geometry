Holonomy Groups: Curvature Remembered by Parallel Transport

A lecture path through definitions, parallel tensors, products, symmetric spaces, Berger-style restrictions, nonsimply connected quotients, and Lorentzian contrast.

Open the lecture by making holonomy feel operational rather than mysterious. A vector is carried around a loop by the Levi-Civita connection, and the possible end transformations form a group. The chapter asks how much geometry is encoded by those transformations and why special holonomy is so restrictive for Einstein metrics.

  • Detection: read invariant tensors and product splittings from fixed vectors and subspaces.
  • Curvature: use infinitesimal holonomy to connect loops with the curvature tensor.
  • Structure: separate symmetric spaces, Berger-style irreducible cases, quotients, and Lorentzian behavior.

Holonomy Starts at One Tangent Space

Definition
Fix p in M. Parallel transport along piecewise smooth loops based at p gives linear maps of T_pM. The holonomy group is the collection of all such maps.
Hol_p(g) ⊂ O(T_pM,g_p)

Changing the base point conjugates the group, so the geometric content is the representation type rather than a preferred matrix list.

Holonomy is a group because loops concatenate and reverseSlide 3
Stress that the definition lives in one tangent space, which makes holonomy an ordinary linear group representation. The geometric input is the connection, and the Riemannian metric ensures the resulting maps are orthogonal. Conjugacy under a change of base point is the reason lecturers usually speak about the holonomy group without naming p every time.

Restricted Versus Full Holonomy

Two layers
The restricted holonomy group uses loops contractible through based loops. It is the identity component of the full holonomy group.
Why it matters
Local curvature controls the connected part. Global topology may add discrete transformations, especially after taking nonsimply connected quotients.
Local geometry and global topology are separated but not independentSlide 4
This distinction prevents a common confusion: curvature is a local infinitesimal source, while full holonomy can remember global loops. The restricted group captures the connected, locally generated part. Later, when quotients enter the lecture, this slide supplies the vocabulary for saying what changed and what stayed locally the same.

Curvature Is Infinitesimal Holonomy

P_{\partial\Sigma} = I + area(\Sigma) R(X,Y) + higher order terms

A tiny loop spanned by X and Y returns a vector changed to first order by the curvature endomorphism.

Reading habit
Holonomy is not just an abstract subgroup. Its Lie algebra is probed by curvature and its covariant derivatives.
Ambrose-Singer intuition: curvature supplies the infinitesimal rotationsSlide 5
Do not turn this into a technical proof of the holonomy theorem; use it as the geometric bridge. Tiny loops measure the failure of parallel transport to be path independent. The curvature tensor is therefore not merely compatible with holonomy; it is the infinitesimal data from which the connected holonomy algebra is built.

Parallel Tensors Are Holonomy Invariants

Equivalence principle
A tensor field T with nabla T = 0 is determined by T_p, and T_p must be fixed by holonomy.
parallel tensor field ↔ fixed tensor in the Hol_p representation

This turns differential geometry into a representation-theoretic fixed-point problem.

Examples: metric, volume form, complex structures, calibrating formsSlide 6
This is the most useful dictionary entry in the lecture. If a tensor is parallel, transporting around a loop cannot change its value at the base point. Conversely, a holonomy-invariant tensor at one tangent space can be extended by parallel transport when the invariance removes path dependence.

Reducible Holonomy Signals a Product

De Rham idea
On a complete simply connected Riemannian manifold, a nontrivial holonomy-invariant orthogonal splitting leads to a global Riemannian product.
Einstein caution
A product metric is Einstein only when the factors have the same Einstein constant after the chosen scalings.
Irreducible holonomy is the natural setting for special holonomy listsSlide 7
Connect reducibility to geometry rather than leaving it as linear algebra. If holonomy preserves a proper subspace, parallel transport propagates that subspace into a parallel distribution. Under the standard completeness and simply connected hypotheses, those distributions integrate into a product splitting, so holonomy has detected global geometry.

Symmetric Spaces: Curvature Does Not Drift

nabla R = 0

Locally symmetric spaces have parallel curvature. The holonomy algebra is tightly controlled because the curvature tensor is transported without change.

Geometric message
Symmetric spaces are a structured branch of the holonomy story, not a minor example class.
Symmetric spaces supply many Einstein examples through homogeneous geometrySlide 8
The phrase locally symmetric should immediately suggest that curvature is parallel. That single condition has large consequences for holonomy because the curvature operators seen at one point are coherently seen everywhere. For the Einstein course, symmetric spaces are important both as examples and as a boundary case for classification discussions.

The Berger Filter

1 Start with irreducible, nonsymmetric Riemannian holonomy.
2 Require compatibility with curvature identities.
3 Ask which compact subgroups of SO(n) survive.
4 Interpret surviving groups as geometric structures.
The filter is structural orientation, not a proof of the full classificationSlide 9
Present Berger's contribution as a powerful restriction mechanism. The important lecture point is not to memorize a table first, but to see why most subgroups cannot occur as irreducible nonsymmetric Riemannian holonomy. Curvature symmetries, the Bianchi identity, and representation theory make the list small.

Special Holonomy Carries Extra Geometry

U(m), SU(m)

Parallel complex structure and Kahler form. The special unitary case also preserves a holomorphic volume form and is Ricci-flat.

Sp(k), Sp(k)Sp(1)

Quaternionic packages of parallel two-forms or a parallel quaternionic span. These lead toward hyperkahler and quaternion-Kahler geometry.

G2, Spin(7)

Exceptional stabilizers of special differential forms in dimensions seven and eight. Their invariant forms encode metric and orientation data.

Invariant tensors explain the names behind the groupsSlide 10
Use the gallery as a concept map rather than a classification table. Each smaller holonomy group preserves additional algebraic data, and that data appears geometrically as parallel forms or structures. For Einstein manifolds, the key payoff is that several special holonomy cases force Ricci-flatness, while quaternion-Kahler geometry typically fits the nonzero Einstein setting.

Einstein Consequences of Holonomy

Ricci-flat branches
Holonomy contained in SU(m), Sp(k), G2, or Spin(7) carries parallel spinor or form structures that force vanishing Ricci curvature in the standard Riemannian setting.
Nonzero branch
Quaternion-Kahler geometry with irreducible holonomy in Sp(k)Sp(1) is naturally Einstein under the usual dimension and irreducibility hypotheses.
Holonomy often predicts the sign or vanishing of Ricci curvatureSlide 11
This slide connects Chapter 10 back to the title of the course. The value is not that holonomy replaces curvature calculations, but that a small holonomy group may force curvature identities. Emphasize the difference between Ricci-flat special holonomy and quaternion-Kahler Einstein geometry, since both are special but they live in different Einstein constant regimes.

Nonsimply Connected Manifolds Add Monodromy

Full holonomy
Deck transformations and noncontractible loops can enlarge holonomy by discrete components without changing the local restricted holonomy.
Diagnostic question
When a quotient appears, ask whether the new holonomy feature is local curvature or global monodromy.
Restricted holonomy stays local; full holonomy may see fundamental groupSlide 12
Nonsimply connected examples are where the restricted-full distinction pays rent. Passing to a quotient may preserve the local metric geometry while adding loops that did not exist upstairs. Those loops can act on tensors or spinors, so global holonomy may destroy a globally parallel object even when the local model still suggests one.

Lorentzian Holonomy Has Different Geometry

Indefinite contrast
In Lorentzian signature, invariant null lines can occur without an orthogonal product splitting. Reducibility is subtler than in the positive definite case.

This is why Riemannian holonomy intuition must be adjusted before entering relativity or wave-type geometries.

signature (-,+,...,+) changes the linear algebra
The chapter extends the holonomy lens beyond Riemannian definitenessSlide 13
This slide should be treated as a warning label for transferring theorems across signatures. In Riemannian geometry, an invariant subspace has a clean orthogonal complement and often points toward a product. In Lorentzian geometry, a null line lies inside its own orthogonal complement, so reducible representations can remain indecomposable.

Worked Model: Constant Curvature Holonomy

R(X,Y)Z = k( <Y,Z>X - <X,Z>Y )

If k is nonzero, the curvature endomorphisms span rotations of tangent two-planes, so the connected holonomy is as large as the orientation allows.

Sanity check
Flat space has zero curvature and trivial restricted holonomy; constant nonzero curvature has maximal local rotational behavior.
This example calibrates zero, special, and generic holonomySlide 14
Use constant curvature as a quick calculation rather than a theorem-heavy example. The displayed curvature formula shows that each two-plane produces the corresponding infinitesimal rotation when k is nonzero. This calibrates the extremes: flat metrics have no restricted holonomy, while space forms have the expected full rotational local behavior.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1
Treating holonomy as a list of loops instead of the transformations induced by those loops.
Mistake 2
Assuming reducible holonomy always means a product without checking signature, completeness, and covering hypotheses.
Mistake 3
Confusing restricted holonomy, full holonomy, and the isotropy group of a homogeneous presentation.
Most errors are bookkeeping errors about which group is being discussedSlide 15
This is a practical teaching slide. Many wrong solutions come from mixing three nearby ideas: the path group of loops, the induced linear transformations, and the isotropy group of a model. The diagnostic pipeline gives students a way to slow down and identify the precise object before invoking a theorem.

Recap: What Holonomy Lets Us Read

  • Loops become orthogonal transformations of one tangent space.
  • Curvature supplies infinitesimal holonomy.
  • Parallel tensors are fixed vectors in tensor representations.
  • Reducible Riemannian holonomy points toward product structure.
  • Special holonomy often forces Einstein or Ricci-flat consequences.
