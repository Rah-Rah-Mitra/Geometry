Generalizations of the Einstein Condition

Natural first-order conditions on Ricci and curvature tensors: Codazzi tensors, harmonic Weyl tensor, and harmonic curvature.

The Einstein equation Ric = λg is rigid. This lecture asks which pieces of its differential behavior can be kept when the algebraic equality is relaxed.

Motivation

Keep The Differential Consequences

Einstein metrics as the anchor
Einstein AnchorRic = λg makes the traceless Ricci tensor vanish and ties scalar curvature to one constant.
Generalization StrategyReplace the equality by a natural linear condition on ∇Ric, δW, or δRm.
Reading WarningThe word natural means invariant under changes of coordinates, not merely aesthetically simple.
Source focus: natural linear conditions on covariant derivatives of Ricci and curvature2 / 16
Use this slide to make the organizing move explicit. Einstein metrics are special because Ricci has no direction other than the metric, but their curvature also satisfies conservation laws. The generalized classes keep one law while allowing controlled non-Einstein behavior.
Vocabulary

The Curvature Ladder

algebra first, then divergence
The full Riemann tensor records sectional curvature in every two-plane.
Ricci is a trace of Riemann and is the tensor appearing in the Einstein condition.
Scalar curvature is the next trace, while Weyl is the trace-free conformal remainder in dimension at least four.
Rm = W + Ricci part + scalar part
The generalized conditions specify which rung has controlled covariant derivative3 / 16
This slide fixes the language needed for the lecture. The chapter's conditions are not all placed on the same tensor: some involve Ricci, some involve Weyl, and harmonic curvature involves full Riemann curvature. The ladder helps students locate each statement.
Mechanism

Bianchi Identities Drive The Story

differential conservation
Second Bianchi, Schematic∇Rm has cyclic symmetry. Tracing this identity produces divergence relations for Ricci and scalar curvature.
div Ric = (1/2) d Scal
Einstein ConsequenceIf Ric = λg on a connected Riemannian manifold of dimension at least three, then λ is constant.
Generalizations often preserve a Bianchi-type output after weakening Ric = λg4 / 16
Present Bianchi as the engine under the hood rather than a detached identity. Once curvature is differentiated, cyclic symmetry and traces create divergence equations. The generalized conditions in this chapter select particular outputs of that identity machine.
Definition

Codazzi Tensors

symmetry of one derivative
DefinitionA symmetric two-tensor T is Codazzi when (∇XT)(Y,Z) = (∇YT)(X,Z) for all vector fields X,Y,Z.
∇iTjk = ∇jTik
  • The condition is linear in T.
  • It is coordinate invariant because it uses the Levi-Civita connection.
  • It measures a failure of first derivatives to depend on the order of the input slot.
Codazzi is a first-order condition on a symmetric tensor, not necessarily on the metric itself5 / 16
The Codazzi condition is the first precise generalization to put on the board. Stress that T is symmetric but need not be the metric or Ricci tensor. The coordinate expression exposes both linearity and the slot symmetry being imposed.
Specialization

Ricci As A Codazzi Tensor

the nearest neighbor to Einstein
Ricci-Codazzi Condition∇iRicjk = ∇jRicik.
Einstein ImplicationIf Ric = λg and λ is constant, then ∇Ric = 0, hence Ricci is Codazzi.
What Becomes VisibleWhen Ricci has distinct eigenvalues, the Codazzi condition can force geometric structure on the corresponding eigenspace distributions.
Relaxing Ric = λg still leaves a first-order symmetry on Ricci6 / 16
Here the abstract Codazzi definition is applied to Ricci itself. The point is not that every Ricci-Codazzi metric is Einstein. The point is that Ricci may vary in a controlled way, and its eigendirections can acquire geometric structure.
Conformal Split

The Schouten Tensor Packages The Trace Terms

preparing for Weyl
Schouten TensorIn dimension n ≥ 3, the Schouten tensor is a normalized combination of Ricci and scalar curvature.
P = 1/(n - 2) ( Ric - Scal/(2(n - 1)) g )
It is useful because the curvature decomposition can be expressed as Weyl plus metric products with P.
The Codazzi behavior of P is closely tied to the divergence of Weyl.
The Schouten tensor is the bookkeeping device behind harmonic Weyl curvature7 / 16
This is a bridge slide rather than a new destination. Weyl curvature is easiest to discuss after the trace terms are packaged cleanly. The Schouten tensor supplies that bookkeeping, leaving Weyl as the conformal trace-free remainder.
Condition

Harmonic Weyl Tensor

divergence-free conformal curvature
Harmonic WeylThe Weyl tensor is harmonic when its divergence vanishes: δW = 0.
Equivalent ViewFor n ≥ 4, this condition can be read as a Codazzi-type condition on the Schouten tensor.
Einstein ImplicationEinstein metrics have harmonic Weyl tensor because the trace part is parallel and the Bianchi identities absorb the divergence.
Harmonic Weyl controls the conformal remainder rather than all of curvature8 / 16
Define harmonic Weyl as a divergence-free condition, not as a Laplace equation on a function. The working test is delta W equals zero. The key comparison is that harmonic Weyl ignores trace pieces and can be weaker than harmonic full curvature.
Condition

Harmonic Curvature

divergence-free Riemann tensor
Harmonic CurvatureA metric has harmonic curvature when δRm = 0.
δRm = 0 ⇔ Ric is Codazzi
The equivalence is a traced Bianchi identity: divergence of full curvature records the skew part of ∇Ric.
Thus harmonic curvature is stronger than simply asking for constant scalar curvature.
Harmonic curvature is the full-curvature analogue of a conservation law9 / 16
This slide gives one central equivalence of the lecture. Harmonic curvature is stated using Riemann curvature, but Bianchi converts it into a Codazzi condition for Ricci. That conversion ties a four-tensor divergence to first derivatives of Ricci.
Relations

Implications And Non-Implications

read arrows carefully
An Einstein metric is the most rigid item in this map.
Harmonic curvature can be identified with Ricci being Codazzi.
Harmonic Weyl asks only for divergence-free trace-free curvature, so it records different information.

This implication map disciplines the vocabulary. Students often hear several curvature conditions and collapse them into the Einstein condition, but the chapter separates them. Treat arrows as reliable directions, and ask for examples or hypotheses before reversing one.

Four-Dimensional SplitOn an oriented four-manifold, two-forms split into self-dual and anti-self-dual parts, and Weyl splits accordingly.
W = W+ + W-
This refines harmonic Weyl questions by letting one inspect each half of the conformal curvature.
It also connects this chapter back to the earlier self-duality unit.

Computation Habit

What A Local Check Looks Like

indices as diagnostics
Codazzi ResidualDefine Cijk = ∇iRicjk - ∇jRicik. Harmonic curvature asks for this residual to vanish.
Weyl ResidualDefine Hjkl = ∇iWijkl. Harmonic Weyl asks for this residual to vanish.
A symbolic or numerical lab can measure the size of these arrays before trying to prove a theorem.
Residual arrays turn geometric definitions into inspectable computations12 / 16
This computational slide translates the lecture into a notebook workflow. Instead of trusting a named condition, define a residual tensor and check whether its components vanish. For generalized Einstein conditions, two similar classes may differ only by which residual is tested.
Examples

Products Help Separate Conditions

a synthetic model
ModelTake a Riemannian product whose factors have constant Ricci curvatures with different constants.
Ricci is parallel on the product, so it is Codazzi and the curvature is harmonic.
The product is not Einstein unless the constants match after the product metric is fixed.
LessonParallel Ricci is weaker than Einstein, even though it has a strong first-order consequence.
Small original models are enough to explain why the classes do not collapse13 / 16
This example is deliberately compact. A product of constant-Ricci factors can have controlled Ricci without being Einstein with one global constant. Students can compute the block Ricci tensor and see why the derivative condition vanishes while the Einstein equality fails.
Proof Pattern

From Bianchi To Harmonic Curvature

one reusable argument
Step 1Start with the second Bianchi identity for Rm.
Step 2Contract one pair of indices to express div Rm using derivatives of Ricci.
Step 3Recognize the skew derivative ∇iRicjk - ∇jRicik.
ConclusionδRm = 0 exactly when Ricci is Codazzi, up to the sign convention chosen for divergence.
The calculation is an identity, not an existence theorem14 / 16
Use this as a proof roadmap rather than a full index computation. A cyclic identity for differentiated Riemann curvature becomes, after contraction, a formula relating curvature divergence to the skew part of covariant Ricci derivative. That pattern explains the harmonic-curvature equivalence.
Classroom Lab

Three Residual Tests

fast board or notebook checks
Test AChoose a diagonal Ricci tensor with constant entries in a product frame. Ask whether the Codazzi residual can be nonzero.
Test BPerturb one eigenvalue by a coordinate-dependent factor. Track which component of ∇Ric detects the perturbation.
Test CAssume scalar curvature is constant. Decide whether that alone forces δRm = 0 or δW = 0.
The goal is not to finish a classification. The goal is to learn which hypothesis is being tested by each residual.
Exercises should ask for implications and counterexamples, not memorized labels15 / 16
This lab slide makes the definitions active. The first test reinforces that parallel tensors are Codazzi, the second shows how coordinate dependence creates a residual, and the third prevents an overclaim about constant scalar curvature. Students should name which residual belongs to each condition.
Takeaways

Chapter 16 In One Page

from equality to controlled derivative
  • Einstein metrics satisfy Ric = λg and therefore strong differential identities.
  • Codazzi tensors impose symmetry on ∇T; Ricci-Codazzi is the key special case.
  • Harmonic curvature is equivalent to Ricci being Codazzi, modulo divergence sign convention.
  • Harmonic Weyl asks for the divergence-free trace-free part of curvature.
  • Examples and dimension-specific decompositions show that these classes are related but not identical.
Einstein ⇒ harmonic curvature ⇒ harmonic Weyl, with hypotheses and converses treated carefully.
