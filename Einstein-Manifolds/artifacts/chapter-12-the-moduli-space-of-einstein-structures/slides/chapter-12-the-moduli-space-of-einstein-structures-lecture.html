Objects And Equivalence

An Einstein Structure Is A Metric Seen Through Symmetry

Ric(g) = lambda g
Working Definition
An Einstein structure is an equivalence class of Einstein metrics under diffeomorphisms, often with scaling normalized or explicitly accounted for.
Common Trap
Changing coordinates produces a different tensor formula, not a new geometry. Moduli counts geometry after these orbit directions are removed.
Einstein structures03 / 16
The phrase Einstein structure means the metric is remembered only up to the symmetries that do not change the geometry under discussion. Scaling and diffeomorphism both matter, but they play different roles depending on whether the Einstein constant is normalized. Stress that moduli begins when one stops treating coordinate presentations as new geometry.
The Moduli Quotient

Solution Locus First, Quotient Second

Moduli Template
M_E approx {g : Ric(g)=lambda g} / Diff(M), with a scale convention when lambda is allowed to vary.
  • The ambient space is all Riemannian metrics.
  • The Einstein equation cuts out a nonlinear subset.
  • Diffeomorphism orbits must be collapsed.
  • Singularities appear when stabilizers are nontrivial.

First Models

In Dimension Two, Einstein Means Constant Curvature

Positive

Spherical type: curvature sign fixes a highly constrained geometry after normalization.

Zero

Flat type: shape parameters survive even though curvature vanishes.

Negative

Hyperbolic type: a large Teichmuller-like parameter space appears for higher genus.

Surfaces05 / 16
Surfaces give the cleanest sanity check because the Einstein equation is equivalent to constant Gaussian curvature. In dimension two, topology separates the spherical, flat, and hyperbolic cases, and moduli is dominated by conformal or Teichmuller data rather than by a complicated Ricci tensor. This is the first place to distinguish normalized geometry from scale.
Flat Structures

Zero Curvature Does Not Mean Zero Moduli

Flat Moduli Data
A compact flat manifold is locally Euclidean, but globally it remembers lattice shape and finite holonomy information.
Riem(g)=0 implies Ric(g)=0
  • Local curvature is trivial.
  • Global quotient data remains visible.
  • Affine equivalence replaces arbitrary coordinate change.
Flat manifolds06 / 16
For flat manifolds, the curvature equation disappears but the quotient problem does not. One still has to divide by affine symmetries and by the discrete holonomy data that identifies points in Euclidean space. The lattice picture is a useful reminder that zero curvature can still produce a nontrivial moduli problem.
Infinitesimal Deformations

The Tangent Problem Comes From Linearizing Einstein

g(t)=g+t h+O(t^2)
Gauge-Fixed Memory Formula
E'_g(h) approx 1/2 Delta_L h - lambda h, with diffeomorphism directions removed by a slice condition.
Infinitesimal Einstein Deformation
A symmetric tensor h that solves the linearized Einstein equation modulo Lie derivatives L_X g.
Linearized Einstein equation07 / 16
Now move from examples to local analysis. An infinitesimal deformation is a symmetric two-tensor that survives the linearized Einstein equation after gauge directions have been removed. The displayed operator should be read as a schematic memory device: the Lichnerowicz Laplacian, curvature action, and Einstein constant combine to decide first-order motion.
Slice Philosophy

A Local Slice Separates Geometry From Coordinates

Slice Condition
Choose a complement to orbit directions, such as a divergence-type gauge, so nearby metrics are compared in a controlled chart.
  • Vertical directions: L_X g.
  • Horizontal directions: candidate moduli parameters.
  • Residual symmetry: isometries that still act inside the slice.
Gauge slice08 / 16
The slice picture is the local replacement for the impossible task of choosing one representative from every orbit globally. The vertical arrows are pure diffeomorphism directions, while the horizontal slice is where the elliptic deformation problem is posed. This prevents students from counting coordinate changes as geometric deformations.

The complex organizes the local tangent calculation. The middle cohomology is the first approximation to the local moduli space.
Beyond First Order

Not Every Infinitesimal Direction Integrates

Formal Integrability
An infinitesimal solution h is formally integrable if higher-order corrections can be chosen so the Einstein equation vanishes order by order.
  • Linear theory gives a candidate tangent cone.
  • Quadratic terms can impose obstruction equations.
  • Unobstructed directions produce actual local families.
Formal integrability10 / 16
First-order solutions are promises, not completed families. Formal integrability asks whether a tangent vector can be extended order by order into an actual curve of Einstein metrics. The amber obstruction layer in the diagram represents the moment where quadratic or higher terms can stop an infinitesimal deformation from becoming real.
Local Analytic Model

Premoduli Is The Slice Solution Before The Final Quotient

Premoduli Space
The nearby Einstein metrics satisfying the chosen slice condition, usually modeled as the zero set of a finite-dimensional obstruction map.
Phi : H -> K, Phi(0)=0
Final Step
Residual isometries may still act on Phi^{-1}(0), so the local moduli space is a quotient of this premoduli set.
Premoduli11 / 16
A premoduli space is the local analytic object obtained after choosing a slice and solving the equation there. It is not yet the final moduli space because residual isometries may still identify points inside the slice. This distinction is especially important near metrics with nontrivial symmetry, where the quotient can have orbifold-like behavior.
Scale And Sign

The Einstein Constant Changes Under Rescaling

Ric(c g)=Ric(g), so lambda(c g)=lambda(g)/c
Normalization Matters
Without fixing volume, scalar curvature, or scale, the same geometric direction can look like a changing Einstein constant.
  • lambda > 0: compact examples are strongly constrained.
  • lambda = 0: Ricci-flat moduli can be rich.
  • lambda < 0: hyperbolic and negatively curved patterns enter.
Einstein constants12 / 16
The Einstein constant is a coordinate on the type of solution, but it is not independent of scaling. Rescaling the metric changes the constant inversely, so normalized moduli problems usually fix volume, scalar curvature, or the sign of the constant. The slide should make students cautious about comparing families before a normalization has been chosen.
When The Local Moduli Space Is A Point

Rigidity Means No Transverse Einstein Directions

Analytic Criterion
If the gauge-fixed linearized operator has trivial kernel in the slice, the implicit-function picture predicts local rigidity.
Geometric Meaning
Every nearby Einstein metric is isometric to the original one, up to the chosen scale convention.
ker E'_g / im(L) = 0
Rigidity13 / 16
Rigidity is the case where the local moduli space collapses to a point after quotienting. Analytically, this often appears when the gauge-fixed linearized operator has no kernel in the relevant complement. Geometrically, it means every sufficiently nearby Einstein metric is obtained from the original one by the permitted symmetries.
Flexible Ricci-Flat Geometry

K3 Shows That Einstein Moduli Can Be Large

K3 Lesson
Ricci-flat Einstein metrics can move in high-dimensional families controlled by complex, Kahler, and cohomological data.
  • Holonomy and topology constrain the structure.
  • The moduli description is not a single metric formula.
  • Period-style coordinates make flexibility visible.
The sketch is schematic: it teaches the role of cohomological coordinates, not a full classification theorem.
K3 examples14 / 16
K3 surfaces sit at the opposite emotional temperature from rigid examples. They show that special holonomy and Ricci-flat geometry can produce rich families rather than isolated points. Instructor Checkpoint

Three Diagnostics For Reading A Moduli Statement

1 · Quotient
Given a one-parameter family g(t), decide whether its initial velocity is geometric or a Lie derivative.
2 · Obstruction
Find the first place a candidate h could fail: linear equation, gauge condition, or higher-order term.
3 · Normalization
Explain whether a changing lambda records new geometry or only a change of scale.

Recap

A Portable Template For Einstein Moduli

1 · Identify The Equivalence
Diffeomorphisms and scale choices decide what counts as the same Einstein structure.
2 · Solve The Linear Slice Problem
The tangent model is the kernel of the gauge-fixed linearized equation modulo pure orbit directions.
3 · Check Nonlinear Integrability
Obstructions decide whether infinitesimal moduli become actual nearby families.
4 · Interpret Examples
Surfaces and flat manifolds calibrate intuition; rigidity and K3 show the range of possible behavior.
Summary16 / 16
Close by returning to the basic question: what does it mean to vary an Einstein structure? The answer has several layers: quotient by diffeomorphisms and scaling, solve an elliptic linearized equation, check nonlinear obstructions, and then interpret examples through rigidity or flexibility. This gives students a portable template for reading later moduli problems in geometry.
