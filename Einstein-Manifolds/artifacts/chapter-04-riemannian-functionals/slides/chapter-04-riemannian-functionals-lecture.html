Configuration Space
Metrics form an infinite-dimensional manifold-like object

The Metric Is The Variable

Point in configuration space

A metric g assigns an inner product to every tangent space of M.

Tangent direction

A variation is a symmetric 2-tensor h = d g / d t, not a vector field on M.

g(t) = g + t h + O(t^2),   h in Gamma(S^2 T^*M)
Functional viewpoint: inputs are metrics, outputs are numbers or functions2 / 16
Emphasize that a functional on metrics is not a function on points of the manifold. A single point of the space of metrics is already a whole Riemannian geometry, and a tangent vector there is a symmetric two-tensor. The first mental move is to replace coordinate changes by metric perturbations.
Einstein-Hilbert Ledger
Local scalar curvature integrated globally

Total Scalar Curvature

S(g) = integral_M R_g dmu_g
Two moving pieces
  • R_g changes under the perturbation.
  • dmu_g also changes under the same perturbation.
  • The integral records a global response.
Scalar curvature is a density only after pairing it with the metric volume form3 / 16
Present the Einstein-Hilbert integral as the simplest global curvature ledger. Scalar curvature is local, the volume form depends on the same metric, and the integral couples the two. The diagram shows that both the integrand and the measuring device move when the metric is varied.
Euler-Lagrange Signal
Ricci enters through the derivative

First Variation: Where Ricci Appears

dS_g(h) = - integral_M < Ric_g - (1/2) R_g g, h > dmu_g + divergence
Interpretation

The coefficient of h is the gradient-like tensor. Vanishing against admissible variations gives the Euler-Lagrange equation.

Closed manifold simplification

Boundary terms disappear on compact closed manifolds; otherwise they must be controlled.

The variational derivative is the chapter's main detector4 / 16
The first variation is the reason Ricci curvature is unavoidable in this chapter. Up to boundary or divergence terms, changing the metric pairs the perturbation with Ricci minus one half scalar curvature times the metric. This is the variational origin of the Einstein tensor.
Scaling Discipline
Normalize before comparing critical points

Volume Constraint And Normalization

Vol(g) = integral_M dmu_g,   S_norm(g) = Vol(g)^((2-n)/n) integral_M R_g dmu_g
Constraint

On the fixed-volume slice, Lagrange multipliers convert the variational equation into Ric_g = lambda g.

Why normalize?

Functionals should compare geometry, not merely the unit chosen for length.

Scaling is a real direction in metric space, not a minor technicality5 / 16
Explain the role of constraints before naming critical metrics. Without normalization, scaling the metric changes the total scalar curvature in a predictable but unhelpful way. Fixing volume, or using a scale-invariant quotient, turns the Euler-Lagrange equation into a geometric condition.
CSC Versus Einstein
Two different target sizes

Constant Scalar Curvature

CSC condition

R_g is constant on M. This controls one scalar function.

Einstein condition

Ric_g = lambda g. This controls the full Ricci tensor.

Einstein implies constant scalar curvature, but the reverse implication usually fails.
This distinction is central to the chapter's variational map6 / 16
Make clear that constant scalar curvature is weaker than the Einstein condition. Inside a conformal class, the normalized total scalar curvature asks for scalar curvature to become constant. Across all metrics with fixed volume, the stronger Ricci proportional to the metric condition appears.
Nonlinear Operator
Metrics to functions

Scalar Curvature Map

R: Met(M) -> C^\infty(M)
DR_g(h) = -Delta_g tr_g h + div_g div_g h - <Ric_g, h>
Question

Which functions occur as scalar curvatures of nearby metrics?

The derivative turns variational geometry into PDE7 / 16
Treat the scalar curvature map as a nonlinear differential operator from metrics to functions. Its derivative turns a metric perturbation into a function, so the slide separates the input tensor from the output scalar field. This is the analytic bridge to solvability and obstruction questions.
Obstruction Detector
Kernel of the adjoint matters

Adjoint Test And Static Obstructions

DR_g^*(f) = Hess_g f + (Delta_g f) g - f Ric_g
If kernel is zero

The scalar curvature map behaves like a submersion near g, modulo analytic hypotheses.

If kernel is nonzero

Prescribing scalar curvature can meet compatibility conditions invisible in pointwise notation.

The adjoint packages global compatibility into an elliptic equation8 / 16
The adjoint is a diagnostic rather than a decoration. If the adjoint has a nontrivial kernel, then nearby prescribed scalar curvature problems can have hidden compatibility conditions. Students should read the right-hand box as the obstruction detector for the scalar curvature map.
Controlled Variations
Same angles, changed scale

Conformal Directions Are A Laboratory

Conformal class

Metrics differ by multiplication with a positive function. This makes one family of variations especially tractable.

Find g' in [g] with R_{g'} = constant
Yamabe-type viewpoint

The normalized total scalar curvature becomes an elliptic variational problem inside the class.

Conformal variation turns global geometry into a scalar equation9 / 16
Use conformal changes as the controlled experiment. They preserve angles while changing scale, so the scalar curvature transformation becomes a tractable elliptic equation. The Yamabe-type question is whether each conformal class contains a representative with constant scalar curvature.
Critical Metrics
Constrained total scalar curvature

Einstein Metrics As Critical Points

Ric_g = lambda g
Full metric variation

On the fixed-volume space of metrics, critical points of total scalar curvature are Einstein metrics.

Conformal variation

Inside a conformal class, the critical condition becomes constant scalar curvature.

Same functional, different admissible variation spaces10 / 16
Now connect the two levels. Constant scalar curvature solves the constrained problem inside conformal classes, while Einstein metrics solve the full fixed-volume variational problem. The slide deliberately draws the Einstein condition as the smaller target inside the larger constant-scalar-curvature target.

Quadratic Curvature Energies

Riemann energy

integral |Rm|^2 dmu

Measures the whole curvature tensor.

Ricci energy

integral |Ric|^2 dmu

Focuses on Ricci curvature and Einstein defect.

Scalar energy

integral R^2 dmu

Tracks variation of the scalar trace.

Quadratic functionals ask which curvature component the energy notices11 / 16
The chapter then raises the degree of the energy. Instead of integrating scalar curvature linearly, it studies integrals of squared curvature quantities. These functionals detect different pieces of curvature and lead to different Euler-Lagrange equations.
Bookkeeping
Decompose before varying

Curvature Decomposition Organizes Energies

Rm = W + Ric_0 part + scalar part
Why it helps

Quadratic integrals become legible when each term has a geometric role: conformal curvature, traceless Ricci defect, or scalar trace.

Decomposition is the slide rule for curvature energies12 / 16
Use the decomposition as bookkeeping for the quadratic functionals. The full curvature tensor splits into Weyl, traceless Ricci, and scalar parts, so an L squared energy can emphasize conformal curvature, Einstein defect, or average scalar curvature. This makes the formulas less opaque.
Second Variation Thinking
Beyond finding critical points

Gradient, Hessian, Stability

Gradient

The tensor whose pairing with h gives the first variation.

Hessian

The quadratic form that measures whether a critical metric is stable, unstable, or degenerate.

F(g + h) = F(g) + (1/2) Hess_g(h,h) + higher order
Critical point analysis feeds directly into rigidity and moduli13 / 16
A functional gives more than an Euler-Lagrange equation. Its gradient tells the direction of steepest change, while the Hessian at a critical metric tests local rigidity or instability. In Einstein geometry, this is where analytic operator theory starts governing moduli behavior.

Work through the three model rows as sanity checks. Space forms make every curvature piece constant, flat tori kill all curvature energies, and products can have constant scalar curvature without being Einstein unless the factors are balanced. These examples prevent overreading a single equation.
Warnings
Three false shortcuts

Common Misreadings

CSC is not Einstein

One scalar equation cannot normally force the whole Ricci tensor to be proportional to the metric.

Functional matters

Critical for total scalar curvature does not automatically mean critical for a squared curvature energy.

Scale matters

Compare normalized functionals or fixed-volume slices before interpreting numerical energy values.

Most errors come from changing the admissible variation space silently15 / 16
Pause on the warning slide because variational language invites false shortcuts. Critical for one functional does not mean critical for every curvature energy, constant scalar curvature does not imply Einstein, and rescaling must be handled before comparing numerical values of integrals.
