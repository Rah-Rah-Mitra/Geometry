Connections

A Connection Differentiates Moving Vector Fields

Definition

A connection ∇ assigns ∇XY to vector fields X,Y and is C∞-linear in X, additive in Y, and satisfies ∇X(fY)=X(f)Y+f∇XY.

Interpretation

It is not just a directional derivative. It also declares how tangent spaces at neighboring points are compared.

Leibniz rule plus comparison rule03 / 16
Pause on the geometric meaning before writing coordinates. A connection is a rule for differentiating vector fields along directions, but the key hidden operation is comparison between different tangent spaces. Without that comparison, subtracting Y at two nearby points is not intrinsic. The Leibniz rule records how scalar multiplication behaves, while the diagram emphasizes that parallel transport is the zero-change case for the connection. This prepares the Levi-Civita specialization on the next slide.
Metric Compatibility And Torsion

The Levi-Civita Connection Is The Metric's Preferred Derivative

Characterization

For a pseudo-Riemannian metric g, there is a unique connection ∇ with T(X,Y)=0 and ∇g=0.

The first condition removes twisting asymmetry. The second says inner products are preserved under parallel comparison.

2g(∇XY,Z) = Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(X,Z)-Zg(X,Y)
- g(X,[Y,Z]) + g(Y,[Z,X]) + g(Z,[X,Y])
Common Trap

The formula is not a definition of curvature. It is a reconstruction formula for the connection from g and Lie brackets.

Fundamental theorem of pseudo-Riemannian geometry04 / 16
Present the theorem as the reason Riemannian geometry is metric geometry with calculus attached. Once g is fixed, the compatible torsion-free connection is fixed, so curvature becomes a metric invariant rather than extra structure. The displayed Koszul-type identity is useful because it shows exactly where metric derivatives and brackets enter. Stress that pseudo-Riemannian metrics are allowed: nondegenerate signature is enough for the uniqueness argument, even if positivity is absent.
Pseudo-Riemannian Manifolds

Nondegenerate Does Not Mean Positive

Riemannian

gp(v,v) > 0 for every nonzero v.

Lengths and angles behave like a smoothly varying Euclidean inner product.

Pseudo-Riemannian

gp is symmetric and nondegenerate with fixed signature.

Vectors may be positive, negative, or null with respect to g.

What Survives

Levi-Civita connection, curvature tensor, Ricci tensor, scalar curvature, geodesic equation, and Einstein equation still make sense.

Signature changes intuition, not the tensor grammar05 / 16
Make the distinction concrete: positive definiteness is a geometric comfort, not a logical requirement for the connection and curvature machinery. In the indefinite case, the metric is still invertible, so one can raise and lower indices and form contractions, but the causal or null structure changes many intuitions. This is important for the broader Einstein-manifold text because both Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian settings appear naturally, especially when relativity is later introduced.
Curvature As Noncommuting Second Derivatives

Curvature Measures Holonomy Around An Infinitesimal Loop

Tensor View

Curvature is the obstruction to second covariant derivatives commuting after correcting for the bracket of directions.

Inspection Target

Transport a vector around a small loop. In flat geometry the returned vector agrees to first curvature order; in curved geometry the defect records R.

Sectional Curvature

K(σ) = g(R(e1,e2)e2,e1) for an orthonormal basis of the plane σ.

Curvature turns local loops into linear defects06 / 16
Connect algebra to motion. The curvature tensor formula can look like bookkeeping, but its role is simple: compare two ways of differentiating along X and Y, then subtract the correction coming from the noncommutativity of the vector fields themselves. The loop picture gives a physical diagnostic: parallel transport around a small loop fails to return a vector exactly when curvature is present. Sectional curvature is the two-plane measurement extracted from the full tensor.
Curvature Contractions

Ricci Curvature Is A Trace Of Directional Curvature

Ric(X,Y) = trace{ Z ↦ R(Z,X)Y }
Scalar Curvature

s = traceg Ric

The scalar curvature compresses all Ricci directions into one number at each point.

Loss Of Information

Riemann curvature contains more data than Ricci in dimension at least four. The Einstein equation controls a contraction, not every curvature component.

ijkl Ricij one function
Trace operations compress curvature07 / 16
Explain Ricci as a systematic averaging of sectional curvature through planes containing a chosen direction. The contraction diagram should make the hierarchy memorable: Riemann curvature is the large tensor, Ricci is a two-tensor trace, scalar curvature is a further trace. This hierarchy is essential for understanding why Einstein geometry is subtle. The equation Ric equals lambda times g is strong, but it does not freeze the entire Riemann tensor in higher dimension.
Symmetries Of The Metric

Killing Fields Generate Infinitesimal Isometries

Killing Equation

LKg = 0, equivalently ∇iKj + ∇jKi = 0.

Geometric Meaning

The flow generated by K moves points while preserving the metric. It is an infinitesimal symmetry rather than just a vector field.

Lecture Use

Killing fields convert symmetry assumptions into equations that interact tightly with Ricci curvature and Einstein metrics.

Symmetry becomes a first-order tensor equation08 / 16
Use the rotating disk as the simplest mental model: a rotational vector field changes points, but it does not change lengths or angles. The Lie derivative condition L_K g equals zero says the metric is stationary under the flow of K. In coordinates, the symmetric part of the covariant derivative of K vanishes. Later, this first-order equation becomes a powerful bridge between geometric symmetry, conservation laws, and restrictions on curvature.
The Central Equation

An Einstein Metric Makes Ricci Proportional To The Metric

Einstein Metric

Ric = λ g

The same scalar λ works in every tangent direction at a point; for connected dimension greater than two, the contracted Bianchi identity forces λ to be constant.

Reading The Equation

Ricci curvature has no trace-free part. Directional Ricci measurements agree after being normalized by the metric.

Einstein means Ricci has only its scalar part09 / 16
Translate the equation into a visual test: compare the bilinear form Ric with the bilinear form g. If Ric is simply a scalar multiple of g, their ellipsoids have the same shape up to scale and sign. The non-Einstein panel shows a remaining trace-free distortion. Mention that in dimension two the equation is closely tied to scalar curvature, while in higher dimensions it imposes a strong system of nonlinear PDE on the metric.
Decomposing Curvature

Separate Conformal Shape, Trace-Free Ricci, And Scalar Parts

Einstein Simplification

For an Einstein metric, the trace-free Ricci component vanishes. The remaining curvature is Weyl plus scalar curvature contribution.

Why This Matters

Curvature decomposition lets the lecture separate what the Einstein equation controls from what it leaves flexible.

R = W + (Ric0 part) + (scalar part)
Einstein kills the trace-free Ricci block10 / 16
This is a structural slide. In dimensions where the Weyl tensor is meaningful, the full curvature tensor can be decomposed into conformal curvature, trace-free Ricci curvature, and scalar curvature pieces. The Einstein equation eliminates the middle piece, but the Weyl tensor can remain. This explains why Einstein metrics can still have rich local geometry. The decomposition also prepares later chapters where conformal geometry and special four-dimensional phenomena become central.
Laplacians And Natural Operators

The Metric Turns Differentiation Into Elliptic Operators

Function Laplacian

Δf = div(grad f) = traceg ∇2f

Tensor Laplacians

On forms or tensors, one must specify the operator: rough Laplacian, Hodge Laplacian, or curvature-corrected variants.

Einstein Geometry Use

Laplacians appear in Bochner identities, deformation theory, and elliptic gauges for curvature equations.

Trace of Hessian is the scalar prototype11 / 16
Keep the discussion anchored in the scalar Laplacian first: the metric defines the gradient, divergence, and trace of the Hessian. Then warn that tensor Laplacians are not all the same. This distinction matters in Einstein geometry because curvature terms enter Weitzenbock formulas and because deformation equations are elliptic only after choosing the right operator and gauge. The contour diagram should make the Laplacian feel like a metric-weighted second derivative.
Conformal Geometry

Scaling A Metric Preserves Angles But Rewrites Curvature

Conformal Change

g̃ = e2u g

Curvature Consequence

Ricci and scalar curvature acquire derivative terms involving u, its Hessian, and |∇u|2.

Use In The Course

Conformal formulas turn geometric curvature questions into nonlinear equations for a scalar scale factor.

Angle-preserving does not mean curvature-preserving12 / 16
Emphasize that conformal changes are deceptively simple. Multiplying the metric by e to the 2u preserves the angle structure, but the Levi-Civita connection and curvature respond through derivatives of u. This is why conformal geometry is a powerful reduction in some problems and a source of nonlinear PDE in others. The visual grid is intentionally warped by scale rather than sheared by angle, so students can separate conformal information from metric size.
First Variation Grammar

To Study Einstein Metrics, Move The Metric

Metric Path

g(t) = g + t h + O(t2)

The symmetric two-tensor h is the infinitesimal direction in the space of metrics.

Linearization Question

How do ∇, R, Ric, scalar curvature, volume, and the Einstein tensor change to first order in h?

Gauge Warning

Some variations are just infinitesimal diffeomorphisms. Analysis must separate geometric deformation from coordinate motion.

Variation turns geometry into linearized operators13 / 16
Introduce variations as the bridge from definitions to analysis. The space of metrics is infinite-dimensional, and a symmetric tensor h is a tangent vector to that space. Linearizing Ricci or scalar curvature along h is how one studies rigidity, moduli, and variational formulas. The warning about gauge is essential: pulling a metric back by a family of diffeomorphisms produces a variation that may look nonzero in coordinates but represents the same geometry.
Worked Micro-Model

Constant Curvature Metrics Pass The Einstein Test Immediately

Model Fact

If sectional curvature K is constant in dimension n, then Ric = (n-1)K g and s = n(n-1)K.

sphere-like: K > 0 -> lambda > 0 flat model: K = 0 -> lambda = 0 hyperbolic-like: K < 0 -> lambda < 0

This example is a diagnostic, not a classification theorem: many Einstein metrics are not constant sectional curvature.

Constant sectional curvature is the cleanest sanity check14 / 16
Use this model to give students one reliable test case. A constant sectional curvature space form is automatically Einstein because every two-plane sees the same curvature. The Ricci tensor becomes n minus one times K times the metric, and the scalar curvature becomes n times n minus one times K. Then immediately prevent overgeneralization: the Einstein condition is weaker than constant sectional curvature in higher dimensions, because the Weyl part may remain.
How To Work With The Basic Material

Four Habits Keep The Tensor Algebra Honest

1. Name The Structure

State whether the metric is Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian, and whether the connection is Levi-Civita or arbitrary.

2. Track Contractions

Write which indices are traced. Riemann, Ricci, and scalar curvature carry different information.

3. Separate Gauge

When varying a metric, identify deformations caused by diffeomorphisms before interpreting them as new geometry.

Board Exercise

Given a two-parameter metric ansatz, compute the Levi-Civita symbols, form Ricci, and ask which parameter values make Ric proportional to g. Then perturb one parameter and describe which block of the pipeline changes first.

Instructor-ready exercise prompt15 / 16
This slide changes from exposition to practice. The four habits are designed to prevent common errors: using a connection without saying which one, treating Ricci as if it were full curvature, interpreting coordinate motion as geometric deformation, and forgetting that the Einstein equation is a metric-dependent PDE. The board exercise can be kept symbolic or numeric depending on the audience. It asks learners to run the whole chapter pipeline on a small ansatz.
Takeaways

Core Chain

g → ∇ → R → Ric → Ric = λg

  • Metric compatibility selects the Levi-Civita connection.
  • Curvature records noncommuting covariant derivatives.
