Graphs and sets

Sets And Graphs Start With Permutations

Symmetry: \( \Sigma_n \)
Set readout

\( f(PX)=f(X) \)

An unordered collection should not depend on row order.

Graph node map

\( F(PX,PAP^\top)=P F(X,A) \)

The output does change, but only by the same relabeling.

Graphs make the domain part of the input. The model must respect both features and adjacency under the same permutation.

Emphasize the difference between forgetting order and respecting relabeling. For a set readout, reordering the rows should change nothing. For a graph node update, the output should relabel in exactly the same way, because the node identities move with the adjacency matrix.
Local graph construction

Local Graph Maps Are Shared Neighborhood Tests

1-hop locality

\( h_u=\phi\!\left(x_u,\{\!\{x_v:v\in N_u\}\!\}\right) \)

  • The same function \( \phi \) is used at every node.
  • The neighbor bag is unordered, so \( \phi \) must be permutation invariant in that argument.
  • Stacking all \(h_u\) gives a permutation equivariant \(F(X,A)\).
Inspection target

Relabel the graph and verify that the highlighted node update moves to the relabeled node without changing its value.

Locality is measured by graph distance; symmetry is measured by node relabeling.
The key move is to apply the same local rule at every node while feeding that rule an unordered multiset of neighbor features. This is why graph neural layers combine locality with permutation equivariance, and why the expressive power of the neighborhood function matters.
Grids and Euclidean spaces

Grids Add Ordered Neighbors

Shift equivariance
Extra structure

The neighbors of \(u\) are not merely a bag. They have offsets \(u-1\), \(u\), and \(u+1\).

\( (x * \theta)_u=\sum_v x_v\,\theta_{u-v \bmod n} \)

  • Translation symmetry replaces full permutation symmetry.
  • Weight sharing appears as repeated diagonals.
  • Local filters keep parameter count independent of grid size.
Contrast the ring grid with the previous graph. The nodes still form a graph, but each node has a left and right neighbor, so the local rule can use ordered offsets. Weight sharing across shifts gives a circulant matrix, and commutation with shift is the algebraic signature of convolution.
Fourier from symmetry

Fourier Modes Are The Coordinates Of Shift Symmetry

Joint diagonalization

\( C(\theta)x=\Phi\,\mathrm{diag}(\hat{\theta})\,\Phi^*x \)

  • All circulant matrices commute, so one eigenbasis diagonalizes them.
  • The shift operator supplies that basis: the discrete Fourier modes.
  • On general graphs, the basis depends on the graph, so cross-domain transfer becomes fragile.
Caution

A fixed grid has one shared Fourier coordinate system. A changing graph or mesh does not.

Changing coordinates turns convolution into componentwise multiplication.
Present the Fourier transform as a consequence of symmetry rather than a lookup table. Every circulant operator commutes with the shift, so the shift eigenvectors diagonalize all of them. This also explains why spectral graph methods become sensitive when the domain itself changes.
Groups and homogeneous spaces

Group Convolution Separates Domain From Symmetry

Global action
Homogeneous space

Every point can be moved to every other point by some group element.

Practical consequence

When \(G\neq\Omega\), a convolution may output a signal on \(G\), not on the original domain.

This is the conceptual jump from shift convolution to spherical or group-equivariant convolution.

The important distinction is whether the symmetry group can be identified with the domain. On a cyclic grid it can, so outputs stay on the same index set. On the sphere, rotating filters produces a response over rotations, so later layers must know which space their signals inhabit.
Geodesics and manifolds

Manifolds Replace Global Shifts With Local Geometry

Metric structure
No global slide rule

A general manifold has no single translation group that moves one shared filter everywhere.

\( \exp_u(X)=\gamma_X(1), \quad d_g(u,v)=\min_\gamma \ell(\gamma) \)

  • Tangent spaces model local displacements.
  • Geodesics define intrinsic shortest paths.
  • Exponential maps pull a local patch back to a tangent plane.
Make the limitation concrete: a general manifold does not let us slide a filter globally as though every point had a shared coordinate system. Instead, the metric supplies tangent spaces, exponential maps, and geodesics, which let us define local neighborhoods and intrinsic distances.
Isometries and spectral filters

Intrinsic Quantities Survive Deformation

Geometric stability
Intrinsic operator

The Laplacian \( \Delta=\nabla^*\nabla \) depends on the metric, so it is preserved by isometries.

\( \hat{p}(\Delta)x=\sum_k \hat{p}(\lambda_k)\langle x,\phi_k\rangle\phi_k \)

Why not raw spectra only?

Individual high-frequency eigenfunctions can move sharply under small perturbations; transfer functions tied to eigenvalues are more stable.

This is the stability bridge between geometry and learning. If two shapes bend without stretching, geodesic distances and Laplace-Beltrami quantities remain meaningful. Direct eigenvectors may still be unstable, so practical filters are often expressed as functions of the operator rather than arbitrary spectral coefficients.
Gauges and bundles

Gauges Are Local Coordinate Choices

Local SO(s) symmetry
Geometric object

A tangent vector field exists independently of coordinates.

Gauge

A frame \( \omega_u:\mathbb{R}^s\to T_u\Omega \) turns an abstract vector into numbers.

\( \omega'_u=\omega_u g_u,\quad x'(u)=g_u^{-1}x(u) \)

The vector is the same; only its coordinate representation changes.

Students often identify a vector with its coordinate pair. Slow down here: the geometric vector is independent of a chosen tangent frame, while the numeric coordinates depend on that frame. Gauge symmetry asks the layer to ignore arbitrary frame choices while transforming features coherently.
Gauge-equivariant convolution

Gauge-Equivariant Filters Need Transport

Bundles plus connection

\( (x * \Theta)(u)=\int_\Omega \Theta(u,v)\rho(g_{v\to u})x(v)\,dv \)

  • Vectors at \(u\) and \(v\) live in different fibers.
  • Parallel transport compares them in one tangent space.
  • The filter is local because transport along a unique geodesic is local.
Gauge condition

The kernel must commute with the structure group representation so a frame change simply changes output coordinates.

The reason transport appears is simple but subtle: vectors at different points live in different fibers. Before a filter can compare them, it must move them to a common tangent space along a local path. The remaining filter must commute with the structure group representation.
Geometric graphs and meshes

Meshes Sit Between Graphs And Manifolds

Graph + faces + metric
Discrete surface

A triangular mesh \(T=(V,E,F)\) has graph connectivity plus oriented triangular faces.

\( (\Delta X)_u=\sum_{v\in N_u}w_{uv}(x_u-x_v) \)

  • Combinatorial weights use connectivity.
  • Geometric weights use edge lengths and triangle angles.
  • Cotangent weights approximate the manifold Laplacian under refinement.
A triangular mesh is graph-like because it has vertices and edges, but the faces and edge lengths carry geometric information. The cotangent Laplacian is the teaching example: it is local and permutation-aware like a graph operator, while approximating an intrinsic manifold operator.
Spectral analysis on meshes

Stable Mesh Filters Avoid Raw Eigenvectors

Operator transfer functions

\( \hat{p}(\Delta)X=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\alpha_k\Delta^kX \)

  • Sparse matrix products avoid explicit eigendecomposition.
  • Degree \(r\) gives an \(r\)-hop support on the mesh graph.
  • Rational filters can reduce resolution dependence by replacing compact support with decay.
Resolution warning

Two hops on a coarse mesh and two hops on a fine mesh may cover different physical radii.

Connect the algebra back to computation. Polynomial filters apply repeated sparse Laplacian multiplications, so they are local and efficient. Rational filters trade compact support for resolution robustness. In both cases the filter is tied to the operator, not to fragile individual high-frequency eigenvectors.
Meshes as operators

Functional Maps Reframe Remeshing

Signal correspondence
Beyond permutation

Different meshes can represent the same underlying surface with different vertices and connectivity.

Functional map

A linear map \(C:\mathcal{X}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{X}(\Omega')\) transports signals between discretizations.

Invariant view

Functions of intrinsic operator spectra remain stable under remeshing when the correspondence preserves the relevant geometry.

Permutation is only the simplest correspondence between two discretizations. Functional maps let us compare meshes with different vertices or connectivity by moving signals rather than matching point labels one by one. This is why spectra of intrinsic operators become remeshing-stable descriptors.
