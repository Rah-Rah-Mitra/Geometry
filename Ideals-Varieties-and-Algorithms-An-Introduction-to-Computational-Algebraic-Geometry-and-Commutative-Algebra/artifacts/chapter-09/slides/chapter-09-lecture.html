The Hilbert Function

Definition

Dimension of degree pieces modulo the ideal.

Eventually

Equals the Hilbert polynomial.

Hilbert function

$H_I(s) = \dim (k[x]/I)_s$

Reads off

Its degree is the dimension.

Printed pp. 477-546The Hilbert Function
The Hilbert function records the dimension of the space of polynomials of each degree modulo a homogeneous ideal. For large degree it agrees with a polynomial, the Hilbert polynomial, whose degree is the dimension of the variety and whose leading coefficient encodes the degree.

Dimension via Leading Terms

Key fact

Printed pp. 477-546Dimension via Leading Terms
Remarkably, the Hilbert function of an ideal equals that of its leading-term ideal, a monomial ideal. So dimension can be read combinatorially from the staircase of standard monomials in a Grobner basis, reducing a subtle invariant to a finite count of exponent patterns.

Dimension and Chains

Geometric

Longest chain of subvarieties.

Algebraic

Krull dimension of the ring.

Prime chain

$\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_d$

Agreement

Printed pp. 477-546Dimension and Chains
Dimension also equals the length of the longest chain of irreducible subvarieties, matching the Krull dimension of the coordinate ring as a chain of prime ideals. These geometric and ring-theoretic definitions agree, anchoring dimension across the algebra-geometry dictionary.

Transcendence Degree

For irreducibles

Dimension is transcendence degree.

Meaning

Algebraically independent functions.

Function field

$\dim V = \mathrm{trdeg}_k\, k(V)$

Intuition

Free parameters at a generic point.

Printed pp. 477-546Transcendence Degree
For an irreducible variety the dimension equals the transcendence degree of its function field over the base field, the maximal number of algebraically independent coordinate functions. This links dimension to the number of free parameters a generic point of the variety carries.
