The Coordinate Ring

Definition

Polynomials modulo the vanishing ideal.

Reduced

When the ideal is radical.

Coordinate ring

$k[V] = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I(V)$

Meaning

Functions agreeing everywhere are equal.

Printed pp. 237-296The Coordinate Ring
The coordinate ring of a variety is the polynomial ring modulo the ideal of functions vanishing on it. Its elements are the polynomial functions on the variety, with two polynomials identified when they agree everywhere. This quotient ring encodes the variety's geometry algebraically and is reduced when the ideal is radical.

Polynomial Mappings

Printed pp. 237-296Polynomial Mappings
A polynomial map between varieties is given in coordinates by polynomials and induces, by composition, a ring homomorphism between coordinate rings in the opposite direction. This contravariant correspondence makes geometry and algebra into mirror categories, with isomorphic varieties having isomorphic coordinate rings.

Printed pp. 237-296Quotient Rings and Remainders
Computing in the coordinate ring means computing modulo the ideal, which a Grobner basis makes effective: each class has a canonical remainder under division by the basis. The standard monomials, those not divisible by any leading term, form a vector-space basis for the quotient ring.

Rational Functions

Domain a field

Irreducible variety, coordinate ring is a domain.

Defined where

The denominator is nonzero.

Function field

$k(V) = \mathrm{Frac}(k[V])$

Domain

An open dense subset.

Printed pp. 237-296Rational Functions
On an irreducible variety the coordinate ring is a domain, so we may form its field of fractions, the field of rational functions. A rational function is defined wherever its denominator is nonzero, and its domain is an open dense subset of the variety.
