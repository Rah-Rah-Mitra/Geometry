Each algorithm begins by naming its inputs and the output it returns. Specifying types up front, polynomials, lists, or ideals, and the guaranteed properties of the result makes every procedure a clear contract that can be verified and reasoned about independently of implementation.

Printed pp. 607-610Assignment and Variables
Assignment stores a value in a variable, and the symbols distinguish updating a variable from testing equality. Variables hold polynomials, sets, or numbers, and clear naming conventions let the reader follow the state of a computation step by step through the procedure.

Termination

Each loop must finish.

Argument

A decreasing or stabilizing quantity.

Measure

$m_0 > m_1 > \dots \ge 0$

Theme

Correctness versus termination.

Printed pp. 607-610Loops and Termination
Because algorithms must finish, each loop is paired with an argument that it terminates, usually a quantity that strictly decreases or an ascending chain that must stabilize. Distinguishing correctness from termination is a recurring theme throughout the algorithmic chapters of the book.
