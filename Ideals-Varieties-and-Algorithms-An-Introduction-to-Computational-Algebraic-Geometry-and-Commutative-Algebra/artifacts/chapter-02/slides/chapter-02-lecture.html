Grobner Bases

A computable generating set for an ideal

Goal

Make ideal membership decidable.

Tools

Orderings, division, Buchberger's algorithm.

Printed pp. 49-120Grobner Bases
This chapter spans printed pages 49 through 120 and builds the central computational tool of the subject. A Grobner basis is a special generating set for an ideal, adapted to a chosen monomial ordering, that makes ideal membership and many geometric questions decidable through a multivariable division algorithm and Buchberger's algorithm.

Monomial Orderings

Requirements

Total, multiplicative, well ordered.

Choices

Lex, graded lex, graded reverse lex.

Leading term

$\mathrm{LT}(f)=c\,x^{\alpha},\ \alpha=\max_{>}$

Role

Fixes the leading term of each polynomial.

Printed pp. 49-120Monomial Orderings
To divide in several variables we first order monomials in a way compatible with multiplication and well founded. Lexicographic, graded lexicographic, and graded reverse lexicographic orderings are the standard choices, each fixing a leading term for every polynomial and thereby determining how the division algorithm proceeds.

Multivariable Division

Process

Cancel leading terms against an ordered list.

Caveat

Remainder can depend on divisor order.

Division

$f=\sum_i q_i f_i + r$

Membership

Zero remainder suffices but is not necessary.

Printed pp. 49-120Multivariable Division
The division algorithm in several variables divides a polynomial by an ordered list of divisors, repeatedly cancelling leading terms, and yields quotients plus a remainder. Unlike the one-variable case, the remainder can depend on the order of the divisors, so a zero remainder is sufficient but not necessary for membership.

Monomial Ideals and Dickson's Lemma

Monomial ideal

Generated by monomials alone.

Dickson

A finite generating set always exists.

Hilbert basis theorem

$I=\langle g_1,\dots,g_t\rangle$

Consequence

Every polynomial ideal is finitely generated.

Printed pp. 49-120Monomial Ideals and Dickson
Monomial ideals are generated by monomials alone, and Dickson's lemma guarantees every such ideal has a finite generating set. Combined with the leading-term ideal of an arbitrary ideal, this finiteness underlies the Hilbert basis theorem: every polynomial ideal is finitely generated, so the descending search terminates.

Buchberger's Criterion

S-polynomial

Cancels leading terms of a pair.

Test

All S-polynomials reduce to zero.

S-polynomial

$S(f,g)=\frac{x^{\gamma}}{\mathrm{LT}(f)}f-\frac{x^{\gamma}}{\mathrm{LT}(g)}g$

Meaning

Exposes hidden lower-order relations.

Printed pp. 49-120Buchberger's Criterion
A generating set is a Grobner basis exactly when every S-polynomial reduces to zero on division by the set. The S-polynomial cancels leading terms of a pair to expose hidden lower-order relations. This concrete criterion turns the abstract definition into a finite, checkable test for the basis property.

Printed pp. 49-120Buchberger's Algorithm
Buchberger's algorithm computes a Grobner basis by adjoining nonzero S-polynomial remainders until all reduce to zero. Reducing to a minimal, then reduced, basis yields a canonical form for the ideal. With this in hand, ideal membership becomes a single division test. Next: elimination theory.
