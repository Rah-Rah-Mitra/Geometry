Buchberger's Criteria

Coprime criterion

Disjoint leading terms reduce to zero.

Chain criterion

Skip pairs covered by others.

Coprime case

$\gcd(\mathrm{LT}(f),\mathrm{LT}(g))=1$

Effect

Prunes work, keeps correctness.

Printed pp. 547-600Buchberger's Criteria
Two criteria of Buchberger let us skip many S-polynomial reductions in advance. Pairs whose leading terms are relatively prime reduce to zero automatically, and a chain criterion discards pairs already covered by others, dramatically pruning the work without affecting correctness.

Selection Strategies

Normal strategy

Least common multiple first.

Sugar strategy

Track a degree surrogate.

Goal

$\min \deg \mathrm{lcm}(\mathrm{LT}(f),\mathrm{LT}(g))$

Benefit

Keeps polynomials small.

Printed pp. 547-600Selection Strategies
The order in which S-pairs are processed greatly affects performance. The normal selection strategy, choosing pairs of least leading common multiple, and the sugar strategy, tracking a degree surrogate, keep intermediate polynomials small and speed termination in practice.

The FGLM Algorithm

Setting

Zero-dimensional ideals.

Method

Linear algebra in the quotient.

Order conversion

$G_{\mathrm{grevlex}} \longrightarrow G_{\mathrm{lex}}$

Payoff

Cheap elimination-friendly basis.

Printed pp. 547-600The FGLM Algorithm
For a zero-dimensional ideal the FGLM algorithm converts a Grobner basis from one monomial order to another using linear algebra in the finite-dimensional quotient ring. This makes it cheap to obtain an elimination-friendly lexicographic basis from a fast graded one.
