Printed pp. 351-392Group Actions on Polynomials
A finite matrix group acts on the polynomial ring by linear changes of variable. A polynomial is invariant when every group element leaves it fixed. The symmetric group permuting variables is the guiding example, and its invariants are the classical symmetric polynomials studied since antiquity.

Printed pp. 351-392The Reynolds Operator
Averaging a polynomial over the group, the Reynolds operator, projects onto the invariants and produces invariant polynomials from arbitrary ones. Applied to all monomials up to the group order, it generates the full invariant ring, made finite by Noether's degree bound.

Generators of the Invariant Ring

Noether's bound

Degrees at most the group order.

Finiteness

A finite generating set exists.

Invariant ring

$k[x]^G = k[g_1,\dots,g_m]$

Compute

Linear algebra plus Grobner bases.

Printed pp. 351-392Generators of Invariants
Noether's theorem guarantees the invariant ring of a finite group is generated by finitely many invariants of degree at most the group order. The Reynolds operator together with this bound yields an explicit finite generating set, computable directly with linear algebra and Grobner bases.
