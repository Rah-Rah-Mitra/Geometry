Printed pp. 1-48Polynomials and Affine Space
We work over a field and consider polynomials in several variables, whose coefficients live in that field. Affine space is the set of all coordinate tuples over the field. A polynomial determines a function on affine space, and our objects of study are the common solution sets of collections of such polynomials.

Affine Varieties

Definition

Common zero set of a family of polynomials.

Examples

Lines, conics, the twisted cubic.

Variety

$V(f_1,\dots,f_s)=\{p: f_i(p)=0\ \forall i\}$

Closure

Printed pp. 1-48Affine Varieties
An affine variety is the set of points in affine space where a given collection of polynomials all vanish simultaneously. Lines, planes, circles, and twisted cubics are all examples. Unions and intersections of varieties are again varieties, giving the geometry a rich structure mirrored by algebraic operations on the defining equations.

Ideals

Definition

Polynomial combinations of generators.

Link

Every ideal element vanishes on the variety.

Ideal

$\langle f_1,\dots,f_s\rangle=\big\{\textstyle\sum h_i f_i\big\}$

Robustness

Independent of how equations are written.

Printed pp. 1-48Ideals
The set of all polynomial combinations of a fixed collection forms an ideal, the algebraic shadow of a variety. Every polynomial in the ideal vanishes on the variety. Studying the ideal generated by the defining equations, rather than the equations themselves, gives a robust algebraic handle independent of how the equations were written.

Division in One Variable

Division algorithm

Quotient plus a lower-degree remainder.

Consequence

Ideals are generated by a single polynomial.

Division

$f = q\,g + r,\quad \deg r < \deg g$

Euclid

The gcd is computed efficiently.

Printed pp. 1-48Division in One Variable
In one variable the division algorithm expresses any polynomial as a multiple of a divisor plus a remainder of lower degree. This makes the polynomial ring in one variable a principal ideal domain, where every ideal is generated by a single greatest common divisor, computed efficiently by the Euclidean algorithm.
