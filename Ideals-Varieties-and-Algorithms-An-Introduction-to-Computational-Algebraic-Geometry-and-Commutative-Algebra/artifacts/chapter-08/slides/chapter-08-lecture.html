Printed pp. 393-476Projective Space
Projective space consists of lines through the origin in one higher dimension, recorded by homogeneous coordinates determined up to a common nonzero scalar. The affine space embeds as a dense open set, and its complement, the hyperplane at infinity, captures asymptotic directions of affine objects.

Printed pp. 393-476Homogeneous Ideals
Projective varieties are defined by homogeneous polynomials, those whose terms all share one degree, since vanishing must be independent of the chosen scalar. The ideals generated by homogeneous polynomials are homogeneous ideals, and the dictionary between varieties and ideals carries over with this grading.

Printed pp. 393-476Homogenization
Homogenizing an affine polynomial by inserting an extra variable produces its projective closure, while setting that variable to one dehomogenizes back. These operations move freely between affine and projective views, with a homogenizing Grobner basis computing the projective closure of an affine variety.

Bezout's Theorem

Statement

Degrees multiply to count intersections.

Conditions

With multiplicity, over a closed field.

Intersection count

$\#(C \cap D) = m\,n$

Meaning

The cleanest intersection theory.

Printed pp. 393-476Bezout's Theorem
In the projective plane two curves of degrees m and n meet in exactly m times n points, counted with multiplicity and over an algebraically closed field. Bezout's theorem epitomizes the clean intersection theory that motivates the projective viewpoint and underlies the study of plane curves.
