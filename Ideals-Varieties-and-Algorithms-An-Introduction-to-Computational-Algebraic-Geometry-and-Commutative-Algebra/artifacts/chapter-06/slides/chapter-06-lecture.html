Printed pp. 297-350Forward Kinematics
A robot arm is modeled as linked segments with revolute or prismatic joints. The forward kinematics problem expresses the position and orientation of the hand as polynomial functions of the joint parameters, using trigonometric identities recast as polynomial relations between sines and cosines on the unit circle.

Printed pp. 297-350Inverse Kinematics
The inverse kinematics problem asks which joint settings achieve a desired hand position, that is, to solve the kinematic equations for the joint variables. Elimination theory and Grobner bases reduce this nonlinear system, revealing the configuration space and the number of distinct solutions for a given target.

Printed pp. 297-350Geometry as Polynomials
Automatic theorem proving translates a Euclidean configuration into coordinates, turning hypotheses and the conclusion into polynomial equations. A theorem holds when the conclusion polynomial vanishes on the variety of the hypotheses, a condition tested by ideal membership or by computing a remainder.
