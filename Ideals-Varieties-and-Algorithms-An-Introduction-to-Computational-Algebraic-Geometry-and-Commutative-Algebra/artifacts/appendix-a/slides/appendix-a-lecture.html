Fields

Definition

Every nonzero element is invertible.

Examples

Rationals, reals, complexes.

Algebraic closure

$\overline{k}:\ \text{every } f \text{ has a root}$

Role

Hypothesis of the Nullstellensatz.

Printed pp. 601-606Fields
A field is a set with addition and multiplication where every nonzero element is invertible. The rational, real, and complex numbers are the running examples, and algebraic closure, in which every polynomial has a root, is the hypothesis under which the Nullstellensatz holds.

Rings and Ideals

Ring

Add and multiply, no inverses needed.

Ideal

Absorbs multiplication by the ring.

Ideal axiom

$r \in R,\ a \in I \Rightarrow ra \in I$

Quotients

Give coordinate rings.

Printed pp. 601-606Rings and Ideals
A commutative ring has addition and multiplication but need not invert elements; the polynomial ring is the central example. An ideal is a subset closed under addition and under multiplication by any ring element, exactly the structure whose quotients give coordinate rings.

Groups

Axioms

Associativity, identity, inverses.

Use

Symmetry and group actions.

Group law

$(ab)c = a(bc),\ \ e,\ \ a^{-1}$

Order

Bounds invariant degrees.

Printed pp. 601-606Groups
A group is a set with one associative operation, an identity, and inverses. Groups capture symmetry, acting on polynomials in invariant theory and indexing coverings in topology. The order of a finite group bounds invariant degrees through Noether's theorem.

Unique Factorization

UFD

Irreducible factorization is unique.

Inheritance

Polynomial rings over a field qualify.

Factorization

$f = u\, p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_r^{a_r}$

Supports

Greatest common divisors.

Printed pp. 601-606Unique Factorization
In a unique factorization domain every element factors into irreducibles essentially uniquely. Polynomial rings over a field inherit this property, underwriting greatest common divisors, the one-variable theory, and many of the structural results used across the chapters.
