Hilbert's Nullstellensatz

Weak form

No solutions means the ideal is everything.

Strong form

Vanishing polynomials form the radical.

Strong Nullstellensatz

$I(V(I)) = \sqrt{I}$

Hypothesis

Printed pp. 177-236Hilbert's Nullstellensatz
The weak Nullstellensatz says a system of polynomials has no common solution over an algebraically closed field exactly when the constant one lies in their ideal. The strong form identifies the polynomials vanishing on a variety as the radical of the defining ideal, the cornerstone of the entire dictionary.

Radical Ideals

Radical

All roots of ideal elements.

Bijection

Varieties match radical ideals.

Radical

$\sqrt{I}=\{f: f^m \in I \text{ some } m\}$

Effect

No geometric information is lost.

Printed pp. 177-236Radical Ideals
The radical of an ideal collects every polynomial some power of which lies in the ideal. The Nullstellensatz gives an inclusion-reversing bijection between affine varieties and radical ideals, so passing to the radical loses no geometric information while making the algebra and geometry correspond perfectly.

Printed pp. 177-236Sums, Products, Intersections
Set-theoretic operations on varieties correspond to ideal operations: unions match intersections of ideals, intersections match sums, and products of ideals also cut out unions. These dictionary entries let us compute geometric constructions purely algebraically using Grobner basis techniques for each operation.

Zariski Closure and Ideal Quotients

Zariski closure

Geometry

Closure of a set difference.

Printed pp. 177-236Zariski Closure and Quotients
The Zariski closure of a set is the smallest variety containing it, computed as the variety of the ideal of all vanishing polynomials. The ideal quotient, or colon ideal, corresponds to closure of a set difference, allowing us to remove unwanted components from a variety algebraically.
