Idea

Printed pp. 121-176Elimination Theory
This chapter runs from printed pages 121 through 176 and applies Grobner bases to solving systems. Elimination theory systematically removes variables from a system of polynomial equations, reducing it to fewer variables so that solutions can be found by back-substitution, much as Gaussian elimination triangulates a linear system.

Printed pp. 121-176Elimination and Extension
The elimination ideal collects all consequences of a system that involve only the later variables. The Elimination Theorem says a lexicographic Grobner basis instantly exposes this ideal, while the Extension Theorem governs when a partial solution of the eliminated system extends to a full solution upstairs.

With lexicographic order the Grobner basis is structured so that some generators involve only the variables we wish to keep. Intersecting the basis with the smaller polynomial ring yields the elimination ideal directly, turning the geometric act of projecting away variables into a purely mechanical computation.

Geometry of Elimination

Projection

Eliminating drops to a coordinate subspace.

Closure

The image's closure is a variety.

Projection

$\pi(V)\subseteq V(I_\ell)$

Missing points

Where leading coefficients vanish.

Printed pp. 121-176Geometry of Elimination
Geometrically, eliminating variables projects a variety onto a coordinate subspace. The projection's closure is the variety of the elimination ideal, but the projection itself may miss points where leading coefficients vanish. The Extension Theorem characterizes precisely which partial solutions lift back to the full variety.

Implicitization

Method

Eliminate the parameters.

Parametrization

$x_i = f_i(t_1,\dots,t_m)$

Use

Printed pp. 121-176Implicitization
A parametrized surface or curve can be converted to defining equations by elimination. Introducing the parameters as extra variables and eliminating them produces the implicit polynomial equations of the image, solving the implicitization problem central to computer-aided geometric design and modeling.
