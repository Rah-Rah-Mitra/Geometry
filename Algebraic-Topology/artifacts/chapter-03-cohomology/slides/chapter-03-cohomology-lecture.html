Cohomology Adds Structure

Cohomology starts as dual homology, but contravariance turns the dual theory into a calculus of products, duality pairings, coefficient operations, and twisted coefficient systems.

Lecture Thesis

  • Dualizing chains reverses functorial direction.
  • The diagonal map supplies multiplication.
  • Orientations turn multiplication into intersection numbers.
  • Coefficient exact sequences and monodromy record information ordinary homology can miss.

1. Cochains, UCT, and Kunneth

From Chains To Cochains

Local artifact: cochain-duality-pipeline.png
Definition
For a coefficient ring \(R\), a degree \(n\) cochain is a homomorphism \(C_n(X) \to R\). Thus \(C^n(X;R)=\operatorname{Hom}(C_n(X),R)\).
Dual Boundary Rule
The coboundary is defined by \((\delta\varphi)(c)=\varphi(\partial c)\), or briefly \(\delta\varphi=\varphi\partial\).

Contravariance

A map \(f:X\to Y\) pushes chains forward but pulls cochains back: \(f^*:C^n(Y;R)\to C^n(X;R)\).

check: cochain_arrows_reverse visible formula: delta phi = phi partial
Cochains evaluate chainsChapter 3 begins by reversing the arrows
Use the image as the first invariant anchor. A cochain is not a new geometric simplex; it is a tester placed on chains. The formula delta phi equals phi after partial is the whole mechanism: the boundary is applied in chains, then the cochain evaluates. That reversal is why pullback becomes natural.
Finite model sanity check

The First Invariant Check

Boundary Matrices

In a finite cellular or simplicial model, the boundary maps are matrices \(B_n:C_n\to C_{n-1}\). The homology check is \(B_{n-1}B_n=0\).

Coboundary Matrices

Dualizing turns the coboundary into a transpose lane: \(\delta^{n-1}=B_n^T\). Therefore \(\delta^n\delta^{n-1}=(B_{n+1}B_n)^T=0\).

Homology information, corrected by torsion

Universal Coefficients

Local artifact: universal-coefficient-short-exact.png
Universal Coefficient Sequence
For ordinary cohomology with coefficients \(G\), there is a natural short exact sequence $$0\to \operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X),G)\to H^n(X;G)\to \operatorname{Hom}(H_n(X),G)\to 0.$$

Free Lane

Free homology classes dualize into Hom terms and behave like linear functionals on cycles.

Torsion Lane

Torsion from \(H_{n-1}\) contributes through Ext, so coefficient choice can create cohomology in the next degree.

The route to multiplication

Why The Diagonal Matters

Construction
The cup product is the composite: first form an external product on \(X\times X\), then pull back by the diagonal \(\Delta:X\to X\times X\).

Why Homology Does Not Get This For Free

To multiply homology classes on \(X\), one would need a natural multiplication map from \(X\times X\) back to \(X\). Most spaces do not have one.

Why Cohomology Does

Every space has a diagonal map. Contravariance turns that map into a pullback, exactly the direction needed for multiplication.

The diagonal is the structural reason cup products exist broadlyOriginal slide diagram
Make the diagonal feel inevitable rather than technical. Homology classes naturally form external products on a product space, but to multiply them back on X one would need a multiplication on X itself. Cohomology reverses direction, so the universal diagonal map is enough. This is the first real payoff of contravariance.
Alexander-Whitney splitting

Cup Product On A Simplex

Formula
$$(\varphi\smile\psi)([v_0,\ldots,v_{k+\ell}])=\varphi([v_0,\ldots,v_k])\,\psi([v_k,\ldots,v_{k+\ell}]).$$

Front Face

The \(k\)-cochain reads the ordered front block \([v_0,\ldots,v_k]\).

Back Face

The \(\ell\)-cochain reads the ordered back block \([v_k,\ldots,v_{k+\ell}]\), sharing the middle vertex.

degree addition: k plus ell unit in H0
Cup product is local before it becomes a ring operationOriginal simplex split diagram
Read the formula geometrically. The simplex is not duplicated; it is split into a front face and a back face with an overlapping vertex. One cochain evaluates the front part, the other evaluates the back part, and the values multiply. This makes degree addition concrete before the ring language arrives.

Signs come from moving blocks past each other

Graded Commutativity

Graded Commutativity
For \(\alpha\in H^k(X;R)\) and \(\beta\in H^\ell(X;R)\), $$\alpha\smile\beta=(-1)^{k\ell}\beta\smile\alpha.$$

When The Sign Matters

Odd-degree classes anticommute over rings where \(-1\neq 1\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), the sign disappears but degree constraints remain.

Teaching Translation

The sign counts how many degree-one swaps are needed to move one block past the other.

Cohomology rings are graded-commutative, not usually commutative on the noseOriginal sign diagram
Treat the sign as a geometric bookkeeping cost. Swapping a degree k object past a degree ell object produces k times ell elementary crossings, and each crossing contributes a minus sign. This is why the ring language must always carry degrees. It also explains why mod two examples can look deceptively commutative.

Same groups, different multiplication

Cup Products Detect Spaces

Detection Principle
A graded ring can distinguish spaces whose cohomology groups alone look the same.

Wedge Behavior

In \(S^2\vee S^4\), although \(H^4\) exists, the degree-two class lives on the \(S^2\) wedge summand, so its square vanishes.

Projective Behavior

In \(\mathbb CP^2\), the degree two generator \(u\) satisfies \(u^2\neq 0\) and reaches the top class.

cup products detect spaces same additive degrees: 0, 2, 4
Multiplication is extra topological informationOriginal comparison diagram
This comparison is the conceptual center of the lecture. If students remember only additive groups, the wedge of a two-sphere and a four-sphere can look too similar to the complex projective plane. The cup square separates them. Cohomology rings therefore see attachment and intersection data that group ranks alone erase.
Orientation creates a fundamental class

Poincare Duality Map

Local artifact: poincare-duality-orientation-pairing.png
Poincare Duality
For a closed orientable \(n\)-manifold \(M\), cap product with the fundamental class gives $$D_M(\alpha)=\alpha\cap[M]\in H_{n-k}(M;R).$$

Complementary Degrees

Degree \(k\) cohomology pairs with degree \(n-k\) homology. Betti numbers mirror across the middle dimension.

Orientation Role

The fundamental class is the global cycle that lets local evaluations assemble into a manifold-wide pairing.

T2_betti_symmetric pairing antidiagonal nonzero
Duality turns cohomology classes into homology cyclesClosed orientable manifolds are the clean setting
Frame duality as the moment algebra touches manifold geometry. A cohomology class evaluates, but after capping with the fundamental class it also corresponds to a complementary-dimensional homology class. Stress the hypotheses: closedness and orientability are not decorative, because the fundamental class is what makes the global pairing possible.

Proof Roadmap For Duality

Proof Strategy
Duality is proved locally first, then propagated through exact sequences and excision-style gluing until the closed orientable case is reached.

Local To Global

Local orientation data supplies compatible local fundamental classes. Compact support controls noncompact pieces during the gluing argument.

Exactness As Engine

Mayer-Vietoris lets the local isomorphism survive unions when the overlaps are already understood.

This roadmap is structural, not a full proof transcriptionOriginal dependency diagram
Do not try to prove Poincare duality line by line in one slide. Instead, show the dependency architecture. The proof begins with local orientation, uses compactly supported cohomology to handle pieces, and relies on Mayer-Vietoris to glue. The closed case is then the clean statement students usually quote.

Bockstein maps expose whether a mod coefficient class lifts across a coefficient sequence or leaves a one-degree obstruction.

Coefficients can twist around loops

Local Coefficients And Monodromy

Open artifact: local-coefficients-monodromy.html
Local System
A local coefficient system attaches a coefficient group to each point and transports it along paths. A loop may return with a nontrivial automorphism.

Monodromy Warning

One loop can flip the coefficient sign, so the same geometric loop can act as multiplication by \(-1\) on the fiber.

Two Loops Restore

In the sign example, two loops act by \(1\), so the second circuit cancels the first flip.

monodromy one loop: -1 monodromy two loops: 1 trivial monodromy: 1
Local coefficients remember path-dependent coefficient transportEmbedded local HTML
Use the lab to make twisting visible. With ordinary coefficients, a coefficient returns unchanged after moving around a loop. With a local system, the loop can act nontrivially, for example by a sign flip. The recorded values, minus one after one loop and one after two loops, give a compact model of monodromy.
