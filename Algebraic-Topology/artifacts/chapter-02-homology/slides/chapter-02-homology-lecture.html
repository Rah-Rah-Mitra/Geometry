Homology: Cycles, Boundaries, And Computable Holes

Cells create chains, boundary maps test closure, and homology records the cycles that survive after filled-in boundaries are ignored.

Definition
The homology group in degree n

$$H_n(X)=\ker(\partial_n)/\operatorname{im}(\partial_{n+1}).$$

Lecture Arc

Build chains, prove invariance, move through exact sequences, compute with cells, then change coefficients to expose torsion.

Use the ladder to motivate chains as a more flexible language than loops alone. A zero-cell creates a component, a one-cell can create a loop, and a two-cell can kill that loop by making it a boundary. Higher cells repeat the same create or kill logic.
Boundary maps as matrices

Chain Complexes

Chain Complex Condition

$$\partial_n\circ\partial_{n+1}=0.$$

Why it matters

Every boundary is already a cycle: \(\operatorname{im}(\partial_{n+1})\subseteq \ker(\partial_n)\). Homology is the leftover part of the kernel.

B1 times B2 is zero triangle boundary squared zero
matrix viewpointboundary maps are the computable part of the theory
Emphasize that the algebraic object is the whole sequence, not a single group. The condition boundary after boundary equals zero is the structural guarantee that boundaries are automatically cycles, so the quotient defining homology is meaningful rather than a lucky accident.
Quotient intuition

Cycles Modulo Boundaries

Cycles

\(Z_n(X)=\ker(\partial_n)\): chains with no boundary.

Boundaries

\(B_n(X)=\operatorname{im}(\partial_{n+1})\): chains that are already the boundary of something one dimension higher.

\(H_n(X)=Z_n(X)/B_n(X)\)

Common trap

Closed is not the same as nonzero in homology; closed cycles can disappear after a higher-dimensional cell is attached.

same loop shape, different ambient chain dataquotients identify cycles that differ by boundaries
Read the left picture as a loop with no filled disk available and the right picture as the same loop after a two-cell has been attached. The difference is not whether the path closes; both close. The difference is whether it bounds a higher-dimensional chain.

Boundary Signs

Simplicial Boundary

$$\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_n]=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^i[v_0,\ldots,\widehat{v_i},\ldots,v_n].$$

For a triangle: \(\partial[v_0,v_1,v_2]=[v_1,v_2]-[v_0,v_2]+[v_0,v_1]\).

A functor for arbitrary spaces

Singular Homology

Singular n-chain

\(S_n(X)\) is the free abelian group generated by continuous maps \(\sigma:\Delta^n\to X\).

Why this is powerful

  • Works for spaces without a chosen triangulation.
  • Continuous maps induce chain maps by composition.
  • The same boundary formula applies to simplex faces.
A map \(f:X\to Y\) sends \(\sigma\) to \(f\circ\sigma\), hence \(H_n(X)\to H_n(Y)\).
singular chains are large but functorialface maps provide the boundary
Move from triangulated or cellular models to arbitrary spaces by allowing every continuous simplex map into the space. This creates a huge chain group, but the boundary is still computed on the faces of the standard simplex, which is why functoriality is available.
Prisms give chain homotopies

Homotopy Invariance

Homotopy Invariance

If \(f_0,f_1:X\to Y\) are homotopic, then \((f_0)_*=(f_1)_*\) on all homology groups.

Prism Identity

At chain level, the prism operator \(P\) satisfies

$$\partial P+P\partial=(f_1)_{\#}-(f_0)_{\#}.$$

Instructor cue

On cycles, the difference between the two pushed-forward chains is a boundary, so they represent the same homology class.

homotopy is converted into a boundary correctionchain homotopy is the algebraic bridge
Present the prism as an organized chain homotopy. A homotopy between maps sweeps each simplex through a prism, and the prism identity says the two induced chain maps differ by a boundary-level correction. Therefore they induce the same map on homology.
Moving information between spaces

Exactness And Relative Homology

Relative homology tracks chains in X after chains lying in A are treated as already accounted for.

Exactness

A sequence is exact at a term when

$$\operatorname{im}(\text{incoming})=\ker(\text{outgoing}).$$

Relative View

\(H_n(X,A)\) measures n-cycles in X up to chains already living inside A.

image equals kernel at HnX sequence terms verified
exactness is a local conservation law for classesrelative groups isolate new information
Use the exact-sequence machine to make relative homology feel operational. The phrase image equals kernel means every class that dies at the next station was already produced by the previous station. Relative groups measure what remains after the subspace is collapsed out of the calculation.
The connecting homomorphism

Long Exact Sequence Of A Pair

$$\cdots\to H_n(A)\to H_n(X)\to H_n(X,A)\xrightarrow{\delta}H_{n-1}(A)\to H_{n-1}(X)\to\cdots$$
Connecting Map

A relative cycle \([c]\in H_n(X,A)\) has \(\partial c\in C_{n-1}(A)\), and \(\delta[c]=[\partial c]\).

Attach a cell

The boundary of the new cell becomes the class that may kill something in the older subspace.

relative cycles create lower-dimensional boundary dataexactness stitches the pair together
The connecting homomorphism is the star of this slide. A relative n-cycle may have a boundary that lies inside A, and that boundary becomes a class one degree lower in A. This is the algebraic bookkeeping behind attaching-cell computations.

Excision By Subdivision

Excision

If \(U\subset A\) and \(\overline U\subset \operatorname{int}(A)\), then removing \(U\) induces \(H_n(X-U,A-U)\cong H_n(X,A)\).

Subdivision idea

Barycentric subdivision replaces a chain by homologous chains whose simplices fit inside the chosen cover.

Degree And Local Degree

Degree

For \(f:S^n\to S^n\), \(f_*\) on \(H_n(S^n)\cong \mathbb{Z}\) is multiplication by \(\deg f\).

\(\deg(f\circ g)=\deg(f)\deg(g)\)
composition 2 then 3 gives 6 reflection -1 constant 0

Degree is homology of top-dimensional spheres turned into an integer. Local degree decomposes the global count into signed preimage contributions, which explains why reflection has degree negative one, a constant map has degree zero, and composition multiplies degrees.
A small chain complex from a CW structure

Cellular Homology

A cellular differential is built from degrees of attaching maps onto lower-dimensional cells.

Cellular Chain Group

\(C_n^{CW}(X)\) is free abelian on the n-cells of X.

$$d_n(e_\alpha^n)=\sum_\beta \deg(q_\beta\circ \varphi_\alpha)\,e_\beta^{n-1}.$$

Computational payoff

Many singular simplices collapse to one generator per cell and one integer matrix per dimension.

RP2 d2 = 2 Moore d2 = 3 Euler S2 = 2
attaching maps become integer degreescellular homology is the chapter's computation engine
Cellular homology is the computational payoff of homotopy invariance and excision. The chain group has one generator per cell, and the boundary coefficient is the degree of the attaching map onto a lower cell. This compresses many singular chains into a small matrix.

A consistency check for finite complexes

Euler Characteristic

$$\chi(X)=\sum_n(-1)^n c_n=\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}H_n(X).$$

\(S^2\)

cells 1 + 1 in dimensions 0 and 2, so \(\chi=2\).

Torus

Betti ranks 1, 2, 1 give \(\chi=0\).

\(\mathbb{R}P^2\)

integer torsion does not alter rank, and \(\chi=1\).

Moore \(M(\mathbb{Z}/3,1)\)

torsion in \(H_1\) but rank contribution remains zero.

Euler characteristic checks ranks, not torsionfinite CW complexes make both sums finite
Euler characteristic is the alternating trace left after the chain complex cancels boundaries against cycles. For finite CW complexes, the alternating cell count agrees with the alternating Betti count. It is not a complete invariant, but it is a fast consistency check for computations.
Exactness for a union

Mayer-Vietoris

$$\cdots\to H_n(A\cap B)\to H_n(A)\oplus H_n(B)\to H_n(X)\xrightarrow{\delta}H_{n-1}(A\cap B)\to\cdots$$
Use Case

Compute a space by covering it with two simpler subspaces and correcting for their overlap.

intersection term present same exactness rule
the overlap prevents double-countinggluing creates the connecting homomorphism
Describe Mayer-Vietoris as exactness for a union. The intersection term prevents double-counting, the direct sum records the two pieces separately, and the connecting arrow detects cycles that become visible only after gluing. The same image-kernel rule drives the sequence.

\(H_1\) And Abelianization

The functorial pipeline routes spaces, maps, chain complexes, and homology groups through the same algebraic interface.

Path-Connected Space

$$H_1(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong \pi_1(X)^{\mathrm{ab}}.$$

Meaning

First homology keeps loop classes after all commutators are forced to vanish.

genus2 H1 rank 4 H1 rank from abelianization 4 functor pipeline edges 4
homology is functorial and abeliancommutators disappear in degree one
First homology is the abelian shadow of the fundamental group. Commutators vanish because chains add without remembering word order, so H1 keeps the loop classes that survive after forcing multiplication to commute. The genus two check gives rank four, matching four standard generators.
