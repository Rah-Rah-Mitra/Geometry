Moving maps versus moving points

Homotopy And Deformation Retraction Are Different Data

Homotopy. A continuous map \(F:X\times I\to Y\) with \(F(x,t)=f_t(x)\) is a path from \(f_0\) to \(f_1\) in the space of maps.
Deformation retract. A homotopy \(H:X\times I\to X\) moves \(X\) onto a subspace \(A\), with the points of \(A\) kept fixed in the strong version.
Watch the codomain. General homotopy deforms maps into \(Y\); deformation retraction deforms the ambient space \(X\) onto a chosen core.
Formal signal: \(F(x,t)=f_t(x)\) Visual signal: annulus slides onto its core
Slow down on the word homotopy. A homotopy is a path in a mapping space, so the formula records moving maps rather than a cloud of independent moving points. Deformation retraction is stronger because the motion happens inside one space and fixes the core at the end.
Two-sided comparison

Homotopy Equivalence Is Data In Both Directions

Definition. Spaces \(X\) and \(Y\) are homotopy equivalent if there are maps \(f:X\to Y\) and \(g:Y\to X\) such that \(gf\simeq id_X\) and \(fg\simeq id_Y\).

Deformation Retract Warning

A deformation retract is special one-sided geometry: \(A\subset X\), \(r:X\to A\), \(r i=id_A\), and \(i r\simeq id_X\). It proves an equivalence, but it is not the general definition.

Use homotopy equivalence when the replacement may not sit visibly inside the original space.

Replacement standard: two maps and two homotopies Later invariants respect this relation
This slide separates two-sided and one-sided data. Homotopy equivalence needs maps both ways whose composites are homotopic to identities. A deformation retract supplies a special case, but the retract data includes an inclusion and retraction tied to a chosen subspace.
A warning before finite models

Contractible Need Not Look Instantly Collapsible

Contractible. A space \(X\) is contractible when \(id_X\simeq c_p\) for some constant map \(c_p:X\to X\).
  • The proof is a global homotopy, not a visual hunch.
  • Apparent passages or pockets can hide the actual contraction strategy.
  • CW data gives a finite way to inspect when a collapse is legitimate.

Teaching move

Ask what data would prove this. The answer is not a prettier picture; it is a continuous family of maps from the whole space to one point.

Notebook theme: contractibility can be subtle Next: finite CW data makes collapses inspectable
The point of this schematic is humility. Tie it to the source's house with two rooms example: contractible does not always mean visually crushable from the picture in front of us. Ask students to identify the target point, then emphasize that the proof is a continuous family of maps from the whole space to itself.
Replacing maps by inclusions

The Mapping Cylinder Packages A Map Into Geometry

\(M_f=(X\times I\amalg Y)/(x,1)\sim f(x)\)
Proof use. \(X\hookrightarrow M_f\) is an inclusion, and \(M_f\) deformation retracts to \(Y\).
  • The bottom copy of \(X\) remembers the original domain.
  • The top copy is glued into \(Y\) by \(f\).
  • The cylinder fibers provide the deformation retraction.

First numerical trace

Cell Counts Give Euler Characteristic Before Homology

\(\chi(X)=\sum_n (-1)^n c_n\)

Spheres \(S^n\)

\(\chi(S^n)=1+(-1)^n\). The parity of the top cell decides the trace.

Projective Spaces \(\mathbb{R}P^n\)

\(\chi(\mathbb{R}P^n)=1\) for even \(n\), and \(0\) for odd \(n\).

Complex Projective Spaces \(\mathbb{C}P^n\)

\(\chi(\mathbb{C}P^n)=n+1\). No signs alternate away.

Extension as a rectangle-filling problem

Homotopy Extension Property For CW Pairs

HEP. For a CW pair \((X,A)\), a homotopy specified on \(X\times\{0\}\cup A\times I\) extends to \(X\times I\).
  • The bottom is the starting map on all of \(X\).
  • The side strip is the prescribed homotopy on \(A\).
  • The extension fills the remaining rectangle compatibly.
relative homotopy tracks A: true CW pairs have HEP
Proof technology for attaching and collapse arguments Rectangle model: \(X\times\{0\}\cup A\times I\subset X\times I\)
HEP is best taught as a rectangle-filling problem. The bottom edge already has a homotopy on X at time zero, and the side strip has a homotopy on A. For CW pairs, those compatible boundary data extend across the full rectangle.
