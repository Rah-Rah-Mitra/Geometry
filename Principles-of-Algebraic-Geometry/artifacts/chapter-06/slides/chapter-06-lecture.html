Section 1
Grassmannian setup

Lines in Projective Three-Space

Grassmannian

\(\mathrm{Gr}(2, 4)\): lines in \(\mathbb{P}^3 = \mathbb{P}(V)\) with \(\dim V = 4\).

Dimension

\(\dim \mathrm{Gr}(2, 4) = 4\).

Incidences

Two lines in \(\mathbb{P}^3\) generically miss each other; meeting is a codimension-one condition on the Grassmannian.

Reading

The geometry of lines is governed by the geometry of the Grassmannian.

Lines ↔ points on a Grassmannian4-dimensional smooth projective variety
Set up lines in projective three-space. The Grassmannian of lines in P^3 is a four-dimensional projective variety, and the Plucker embedding realizes it as a smooth quadric in P^5. Points on this quadric are in bijection with lines in P^3, and the geometry of the quadric encodes incidence relations among the lines.
Section 2
Klein quadric

The Plucker Embedding

Coordinates

Line spanned by \(p, q\) has Plucker coordinates \(p_{ij} = p_i q_j - p_j q_i\), \(0 \le i < j \le 3\). Six coordinates.

Plucker relation

\(p_{01} p_{23} - p_{02} p_{13} + p_{03} p_{12} = 0\)

Image

Smooth quadric \(\mathbb{Q}_4 \subset \mathbb{P}^5\) (Klein quadric); identifies \(\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)\) with this quadric.

Use

Geometric questions about lines translate to intersection questions on the quadric.

Six coordinates, one relation4-fold Q in P^5
Walk through the Plucker embedding. Given a line spanned by two points with homogeneous coordinates, the six pairwise minors form a point in P^5. These coordinates satisfy a single quadratic Plucker relation. The image is a smooth four-dimensional quadric, the Klein quadric, and is the Grassmannian of lines.
Section 3
complete intersection

The Quadric Line Complex

Definition

\(X = \mathbb{Q}_4 \cap \mathbb{Q}'_4 \subset \mathbb{P}^5\), intersection of the Klein quadric with a second generic quadric.

Dimension

\(\dim X = 3\); generically smooth, Fano of index two.

Geometric meaning

Parametrizes lines in \(\mathbb{P}^3\) subject to a single quadratic condition.

Reading

Classical 19th-century object; foundational example for modern Hodge-theoretic computations.

Two quadrics in P^5 ⇒ smooth 3-foldConcrete instance of complete intersection
Define the quadric line complex. Intersect the Klein quadric with a second generic quadric in P^5 to obtain a smooth three-dimensional intersection. This is the quadric line complex. It parametrizes a special family of lines in P^3 and is a classical object in nineteenth-century algebraic geometry.
Section 4

Cohomology and Hodge Structure

Lefschetz hyperplane

Cohomology of \(X\) in degrees \(\ne 3\) matches the ambient \(\mathbb{Q}_4\).

Middle cohomology

\(H^3(X, \mathbb{C})\) carries a Hodge structure of weight three with interesting (1,2), (2,1) parts.

Monodromy

Picard-Lefschetz applies to one-parameter degenerations; vanishing cycles act by reflection.

Hodge diamond

Explicit: most classes algebraic, with a transcendental piece in the middle.

Cohomology pinned down by LefschetzMiddle Hodge structure carries the interest
Compute Hodge numbers of the quadric line complex. By Lefschetz hyperplane theorem applied to intersections in P^5, the cohomology in low degrees matches the ambient quadric. The middle cohomology carries interesting Hodge structure; the chapter uses Picard-Lefschetz arguments to track its monodromy and identifies the Hodge diamond explicitly.
