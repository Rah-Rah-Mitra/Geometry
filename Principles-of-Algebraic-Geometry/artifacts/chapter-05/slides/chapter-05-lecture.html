One-Variable Residues Revisited

Local residue

\(\mathrm{Res}_p \omega = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|z - p| = \varepsilon} \omega\)

Global theorem

On a compact Riemann surface \(C\) and any meromorphic 1-form \(\omega\), \(\sum_{p \in C} \mathrm{Res}_p \omega = 0\).

Why useful

Forces nontrivial relations among local data; recovers degree of a meromorphic function as a residue sum.

Goal

Lift to several variables; same vanishing structure should hold.

Single-variable case is the modelVanishing of total residue is the punchline
Recall the one-variable residue. For a meromorphic one-form on a Riemann surface, the residue at a point is the coefficient of dz over z minus a in a local Laurent expansion. The global residue theorem on a compact Riemann surface states that the sum of residues of any meromorphic differential is zero.

higher dim

The Grothendieck Residue

Symbol

\(\mathrm{Res}_p \left[\begin{matrix} h \, dz_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_n \\ f_1, \ldots, f_n \end{matrix}\right]\)

Integral

\(= \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n} \oint_{|f_i| = \varepsilon_i} \frac{h \, dz_1 \cdots dz_n}{f_1 \cdots f_n}\)

Properties

Multilinear in numerator, sign-skew under permutation of \(f_i\), well-behaved under base change.

Reading

The Grothendieck residue is the right higher-dimensional generalization of the Laurent coefficient.

Symbol with n functions and an n-formIntegration over a real n-torus
Define the Grothendieck residue. For a meromorphic n-form on an n-dimensional manifold with an isolated zero of the denominator at a point, the residue is given by an integral over a small real n-torus. The symbol is multilinear and behaves well under base change. It provides the higher-dimensional analog of the Laurent coefficient.
Section 3
global vanishing

Global Residue Theorem

Statement

On compact \(X\) of dim \(n\) with meromorphic \(n\)-form having isolated poles, the sum of Grothendieck residues is zero.

Consequence

Local residue data must satisfy global linear constraints; many enumerative computations reduce to this vanishing.

Generalizations

Trace formulas for direct images and Atiyah-Bott style fixed-point identities follow from residue calculus.

Compact manifold + isolated poles ⇒ sum vanishesGlobal analog of the curve case
State the global residue theorem in several variables. For a compact complex manifold and a meromorphic n-form with isolated poles, the sum of all Grothendieck residues equals zero. This is the higher-dimensional analog of the curve sum-of-residues vanishing and produces nontrivial relations among local data.
Section 4
local-to-global

Cousin Problems

First Cousin

Given local principal parts, find a global meromorphic function realizing them.

Second Cousin

Given local zero-pole divisor data, find a global meromorphic function or section.

Obstruction

First Cousin obstruction lies in \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O})\); second Cousin in \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}^*)\), i.e. line-bundle data.

Resolution

On Stein manifolds and on suitably ample projective varieties, the obstructions vanish.

Sheaf cohomology controls solvabilityCousin problems are the classical motivation for sheaf theory
Cover the Cousin problems. The first Cousin problem asks whether prescribed principal parts of a meromorphic function can be globally realized; the second asks the same for divisor data. Solvability is controlled by sheaf cohomology vanishing. On Stein manifolds and on suitably positive projective varieties, the problems have positive answers.
Section 5
trace + intersection

Applications of Residue Calculus

Trace formulas

Direct images of meromorphic forms compute as residue sums; underlies Atiyah-Bott style fixed-point identities.

Intersection numbers

Local Grothendieck residues compute multiplicities for proper intersections in projective space.

Riemann-Roch

Low-dimensional Riemann-Roch identities can be derived from residue arguments via the trace map.

Reading

Residues package transcendental data in a way compatible with algebraic constructions.

Bridge between analytic and algebraicConcrete computation engine
Walk through applications. Residue methods recover trace formulas for direct images, intersection number computations via local Grothendieck residues, and the explicit form of the Riemann-Roch theorem in low dimensions. Residues package transcendental analytic data in a way that is compatible with algebraic constructions.
