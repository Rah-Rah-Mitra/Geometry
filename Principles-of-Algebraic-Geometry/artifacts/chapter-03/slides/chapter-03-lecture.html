Section 1
Leray for fibrations

Spectral Sequences

Object

Sequence \(E_r^{p,q}\) of bigraded groups with differentials \(d_r\), converging to a filtered cohomology.

Leray

For \(\pi: X \to B\): \(E_2^{p,q} = H^p(B, R^q\pi_* \mathcal{F}) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \mathcal{F})\).

Use

Compute cohomology of a fibration when fiber and base are known. Differentials encode the twisting.

Pattern

Build double complex, take cohomology in stages, read off limit via converging pages.

Computational engineWorkhorse for cohomology splittings
Introduce spectral sequences. A spectral sequence is a sequence of differential bigraded objects converging to a cohomology of interest. The Leray spectral sequence for a fibration packages how cohomology of fibers builds up to cohomology of the total space. It is the main computational engine in this chapter.
Section 2
trace = fixed points

Lefschetz Fixed-Point and Hyperplane Theorems

Fixed-point

\(L(f) = \sum_i (-1)^i \mathrm{tr}(f^*|H^i) = \sum_{p \text{ fixed}} \mathrm{ind}_p(f)\)

Hyperplane

For \(X \subset \mathbb{P}^N\) smooth projective of dim \(n\), \(H \subset X\) a smooth hyperplane section: restriction \(H^i(X) \to H^i(H)\) is iso for \(i < n-1\), injective for \(i = n-1\).

Reading

Cohomology of \(X\) is forced by cohomology of generic hyperplane sections below the middle dimension.

Use

Drives induction on dimension and enumerative geometry on varieties.

Two Lefschetz statements, two usesTrace identity + dimension reduction
Present the Lefschetz fixed-point formula. The alternating sum of traces on cohomology equals the count of fixed points weighted by local indices. On algebraic varieties this becomes a powerful enumerative tool, especially combined with the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem for restriction of cohomology to ample divisors.
Section 3
surgery tool

Blowups Along Smooth Subvarieties

Blowup

For \(Y \subset X\) smooth of codim \(c\), \(\widetilde X = \mathrm{Bl}_Y X\); exceptional divisor \(E = \mathbb{P}(N_{Y/X})\).

Cohomology

\(H^*(\widetilde X) \cong H^*(X) \oplus H^*(Y)[1,2,\ldots,c-1]\).

Use

Resolves rational maps, separates singularities, and produces explicit cohomology splittings.

Reading

Blowups change the variety but not its birational class; intersection numbers track transparently.

Replace a subvariety by its directionsStandard birational surgery
Cover blowups along smooth subvarieties. The blowup replaces a smooth subvariety by its projectivized normal bundle. Cohomology of the blowup splits cleanly. The exceptional divisor records the geometry of the subvariety. This is the standard surgery tool when working with rational maps and resolutions.
Section 4
Chow ring + cup

Refined Intersection Theory

Chow ring

\(A^*(X)\) of cycles modulo rational equivalence; for smooth complex \(X\), maps to \(H^{2*}(X)\).

Projection formula

\(f_*(\alpha \cdot f^*\beta) = f_*\alpha \cdot \beta\)

Excess intersection

Formula handles non-transverse intersections via the excess normal bundle.

Pattern

Move cycles to general position, then intersect; obstructions track via normal bundles.

Cycles are the algebraic-geometric numbersIntersection products organize them
Refine intersection theory. On smooth projective varieties, intersection of cycles is well-defined in the Chow ring; for complex varieties this agrees with cup product in cohomology. The chapter introduces moving lemmas, excess intersection formulas, and the projection formula that drive computations.
