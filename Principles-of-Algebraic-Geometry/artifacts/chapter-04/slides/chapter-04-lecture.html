The Intersection Form on a Surface

Pairing

\(D \cdot D' = \deg \mathcal{O}_S(D)|_{D'}\)

Self-intersection

\(D \cdot D = \deg N_{D/S}\) for a smooth curve \(D \subset S\).

Use

Detects negativity of curves (e.g. exceptional divisors of blowups have \(D^2 = -1\)).

Reading

Intersection numbers are the discrete data of surface geometry.

Bilinear pairing on Pic(S)Equals cup product in \(H^2\)
Discuss the intersection form on a surface. The symmetric bilinear pairing on the Picard group records how divisors meet. For a smooth projective surface, the form is well-defined modulo numerical equivalence and reduces to the cup product in two-dimensional cohomology. Self-intersections and intersection numbers of curves drive the rest of the chapter.

The Hodge Index Theorem

Statement

On a smooth projective surface, the intersection form on \(\mathrm{NS}(S) \otimes \mathbb{R}\) has signature \((1, \rho - 1)\).

Implication

If \(H\) is ample and \(D \cdot H = 0\), then \(D^2 \le 0\), with equality iff \(D \equiv 0\) numerically.

Why it matters

Pins down the geometry of the ample cone; constrains numerical equivalence classes.

Connection

Reflects the Lefschetz (1,1)-decomposition of \(H^2\) on a Kahler surface.

One positive direction; all else negativeStructural fingerprint of surface geometry
State the Hodge index theorem. The intersection form on the Neron-Severi group of a smooth projective surface has signature one positive plus the rest negative. Equivalently, the ample cone is one-dimensional positive direction; orthogonal directions to an ample class are negative. This is a structural fingerprint of polarized surface geometry.

Picard-Lefschetz Monodromy

Setup

Family \(\pi: \mathcal{X} \to \Delta\) of surfaces with one nodal central fiber.

Formula

\(T(\alpha) = \alpha + \langle \alpha, \delta \rangle \cdot \delta\)

where \(\delta\) is the vanishing cycle.

Reading

Monodromy is a reflection in the vanishing cycle.

Generalization

For higher-dim families with isolated nodes, the same reflection formula governs monodromy.

Building block for monodromyConnects to mirror symmetry intuition
Present the Picard-Lefschetz formula. For a family of surfaces with a single nodal degeneration, monodromy around the singular fiber acts on cohomology by a reflection in the vanishing cycle. This is the building block for monodromy representations of more complex degenerations and connects to mirror symmetry intuition.
Section 4
classification by kappa

Kodaira Dimension

Definition

\(\kappa(S) = \limsup_m \frac{\log h^0(m K_S)}{\log m}\)

Surface values

\(\kappa \in \{-\infty, 0, 1, 2\}\) for smooth projective surfaces.

Classification buckets

\(-\infty\): rational/ruled. \(0\): K3, abelian, Enriques, bielliptic. \(1\): elliptic surfaces. \(2\): general type.

Reading

Asymptotic growth of pluricanonical sections sorts every surface into one of four boxes.

Enriques-Kodaira classification's organizing invariantDiscrete and birationally invariant
Introduce Kodaira dimension. The Kodaira dimension of a smooth projective variety classifies the asymptotic growth of pluricanonical sections: H zero of m K. For surfaces it takes values negative infinity, zero, one, and two. The Enriques-Kodaira classification organizes surfaces by this discrete invariant.

Rational and Ruled Surfaces

Ruled

\(S \to C\) with fibers \(\cong \mathbb{P}^1\); birationally a \(\mathbb{P}^1\)-bundle over a curve.

Rational

Birational to \(\mathbb{P}^2\).

Hirzebruch surfaces

\(\mathbb{F}_n = \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(n))\) over \(\mathbb{P}^1\); basic family of ruled surfaces.

Reading

Castelnuovo's contractibility theorem: \(-1\)-curves can be blown down; reaches minimal models.

Most elementary classBirational classification largely understood
Cover rational and ruled surfaces. A ruled surface is a smooth projective surface with a morphism to a curve whose fibers are projective lines. A rational surface is birational to projective two-space. These form the Kodaira-dimension-minus-infinity bucket and include Hirzebruch surfaces as the basic family.
