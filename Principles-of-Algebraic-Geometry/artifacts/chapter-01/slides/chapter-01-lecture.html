Complex Projective Space \(\mathbb{P}^n\)

Definition

\(\mathbb{P}^n = (\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \setminus 0) / \mathbb{C}^*\)

Charts

Affine charts \(U_i = \{[z]: z_i \ne 0\}\); transitions are rational and biholomorphic.

Hyperplanes

Zero loci of homogeneous linear forms; generate the Picard group.

Why projective

Compactness + algebraic structure: a smooth projective variety is automatically Kahler via Fubini-Study.

The default ambient spaceAlgebraic + compact + Kahler
Set up projective space. P^n is the space of lines in C^(n+1) through the origin. Homogeneous coordinates parametrize points, hyperplanes are zero loci of linear forms, and the standard affine cover trivializes the manifold structure. Every classical algebraic variety in the chapter sits inside some P^n.
Definitions
smoothness + Bezout

Projective Varieties and Bezout's Theorem

Variety

\(V = Z(f_1, \ldots, f_r) \subset \mathbb{P}^n\), zero locus of homogeneous polynomials.

Smoothness

Jacobian of \(f_i\) has maximal rank at each point.

Bezout

Curves of degrees \(d, e\) in \(\mathbb{P}^2\) meet in \(d e\) points, counted with multiplicity.

Generalization

Hypersurfaces \(H_1, \ldots, H_n \subset \mathbb{P}^n\) in general position meet in \(\prod \deg H_i\) points.

Singular case

Multiplicities must be computed locally; equality requires general position.

Numerical handle on intersectionsDegrees multiply in projective space
Define a projective variety as the zero locus of homogeneous polynomials in P^n. Smoothness is the rank condition on the Jacobian. Bezout's theorem counts intersections of curves and hypersurfaces with multiplicity, and the chapter uses it repeatedly to compute degrees of secant and tangent varieties.

Tangent Spaces and Singularities

Zariski tangent

\(T_p V = (\mathfrak{m}_p / \mathfrak{m}_p^2)^\vee\)

Smoothness criterion

\(\dim T_p V = \dim V\). Otherwise \(p\) is singular.

Blowup

Replace a point by the projective space of directions at it; resolves simple singularities.

Embedded vs intrinsic

Embedded singularity types (node, cusp) are classified by local equations; intrinsic resolution exists generically.

Singularities are where the rank dropsBlowups are the surgery tool
Discuss tangent spaces. The Zariski tangent space at a smooth point is the algebraic cotangent kernel; for an analytic variety it agrees with the holomorphic tangent space. Singular loci are where the rank drops. The chapter introduces resolution of singularities and blowups as the surgery tool.

Plucker Formulas for Plane Curves

Smooth curve

Degree \(d\), genus \(g = \tfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}\).

Singular curve

With \(\delta\) nodes and \(\kappa\) cusps: \(g = \tfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2} - \delta - \kappa\).

Dual degree

\(d^\vee = d(d-1) - 2\delta - 3\kappa\)

Reading

Singularities cost arithmetic genus; flexes and bitangents are counted by intersection with auxiliary curves.

Use

Plucker numbers are the first numerical invariants of classical curve geometry.

Numerical handle on curve singularitiesBridge between geometry and arithmetic
Cover the Plucker formulas. For a smooth plane curve of degree d, the number of nodes, cusps, bitangents, and flexes is constrained by d and the genus. The formulas express singularity counts as intersection numbers, providing a numerical handle on classical curve geometry.
