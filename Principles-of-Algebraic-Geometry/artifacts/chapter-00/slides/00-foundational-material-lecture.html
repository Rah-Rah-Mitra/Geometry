Section 1
Wirtinger operators

Calculus on Complex Manifolds

Wirtinger

\(\partial_z = \tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_x - i \partial_y),\quad \partial_{\bar z} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_x + i \partial_y)\)

Cauchy-Riemann

\(\partial_{\bar z} f = 0 \iff f \text{ holomorphic}\)

Type decomposition

\(d = \partial + \bar\partial\) on complex manifolds; forms split by holomorphic/antiholomorphic type.

Why bother

The type decomposition is the prerequisite for Dolbeault cohomology and the Hodge decomposition.

Dolbeault

\(H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial}\) computed via the \(\bar\partial\)-complex; equals Cech cohomology of the holomorphic forms sheaf \(\Omega^p\).

Complex calculus is the analytic backboneWirtinger operators are the daily tools
Set up complex calculus. Holomorphic functions satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations and the d-bar operator vanishes on them. The Wirtinger operators partial-z and partial-z-bar give a clean way to decompose differentials on complex manifolds. The d-equals-partial-plus-partial-bar decomposition is the workhorse of the rest of the chapter.
Section 2
local-to-global

Sheaves and Cohomology

Sheaf \(\mathcal{F}\)

Assigns to each open set a group/ring of sections, with restriction maps and a gluing axiom.

Cohomology

\(H^i(X, \mathcal{F})\)

measures the failure of taking global sections to be exact.

Tools

Cech cohomology + derived functors agree on reasonable spaces; long exact sequences from short exact sheaf sequences.

Workhorse examples

Exponential sheaf sequence \(0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}^* \to 0\) connects line bundles to first Chern classes.

Pattern

Geometry → sheaf sequence → long exact sequence → numerical invariants.

Sheaves manage local dataCohomology counts obstructions
Introduce sheaves as the bookkeeping for local-to-global data. Sheaf cohomology measures the obstruction to lifting local sections to global ones. The Cech and derived-functor pictures agree under mild hypotheses. The chapter uses sheaf cohomology to manage line bundles, divisors, and holomorphic forms.
Section 3
Kahler = closed (1,1)

Hermitian Metrics and the Kahler Condition

Hermitian metric

Positive-definite Hermitian inner product on each tangent space, varying smoothly.

Kahler form

\(\omega(X, Y) = g(JX, Y)\) is a real \((1,1)\)-form; Kahler condition: \(d\omega = 0\).

Consequence

\(\Delta_d = 2 \Delta_{\bar\partial} = 2 \Delta_{\partial}\) on a Kahler manifold; harmonic forms split by type.

Examples

Projective space and any submanifold (Fubini-Study); complex tori; smooth projective varieties.

Non-example

The Hopf surface is complex but not Kahler — proves the condition is nontrivial.

Kahler = compatible complex + Riemannian + symplecticDrives the Hodge decomposition
Define Hermitian and Kahler structures. A Hermitian metric on a complex manifold gives a real two-form omega; the Kahler condition is that omega is closed. On a Kahler manifold, Laplacians for d, partial, and partial-bar agree up to constants, which is the engine behind Hodge decomposition.
Section 4
Chern classes

Holomorphic Vector Bundles

Definition

Locally trivial sheaf of holomorphic sections of a rank-\(r\) complex bundle.

Line bundles

\(\mathrm{Pic}(X) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}^*)\); first Chern class lands in \(H^2(X, \mathbb{Z})\).

Chern-Weil

Chern classes via curvature of a connection; produces explicit cohomology representatives.

Use

Intersection numbers, ampleness, vanishing theorems, and Riemann-Roch all express in Chern classes.

Higher rank

Total Chern class \(c(E) = 1 + c_1 + c_2 + \ldots\); splitting principle reduces calculations to line bundles.

Vector bundles are the geometric carrier of cohomologyChern classes are the numbers
Describe holomorphic vector bundles and their cohomology. Line bundles are classified up to isomorphism by their first Chern class in the appropriate cohomology. Higher-rank bundles carry Chern classes that obstruct triviality. The chapter sets up Chern-Weil theory for explicit computations later.
Section 5
D → line bundle

Divisors and Linear Systems

Divisor

\(D = \sum n_i V_i\), formal integer sum of codimension-one subvarieties \(V_i\).

Cartier ↔ line bundle

Each Cartier divisor \(D\) gives a line bundle \(\mathcal{O}(D)\); equivalence \(D \sim D'\) iff \(\mathcal{O}(D) \cong \mathcal{O}(D')\).

Linear systems

\(|D| = \mathbb{P}(H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(D)))\); base-point-free systems give maps to projective space.

Workhorse

Embedding criterion: very ample divisors embed \(X\) into projective space.

Reading

Algebraic geometry computations on a variety mostly happen through divisors and line bundles.

Divisors are the dual of curves and surfacesLinear systems realize projective maps
Cover divisors. A divisor is a formal sum of codimension-one subvarieties with integer coefficients. Cartier divisors correspond to line bundles. Linear systems are projective parameter spaces of divisors in a given class, and they realize maps from the variety to projective spaces.
Section 6
harmonic = unique rep

Hodge Decomposition on Compact Kahler Manifolds

Hodge Theorem

Each de Rham class has a unique harmonic representative.

Decomposition

\(H^n(X, \mathbb{C}) = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^{p,q}(X)\)

Symmetries

\(h^{p,q} = h^{q,p}\) (conjugation), \(h^{p,q} = h^{n-p,n-q}\) (Serre duality).

Hodge diamond

Records all the Hodge numbers in a single shape; the structural rigidity of the diamond is the chapter's payoff.

Connection

Hodge theory will produce vanishing theorems, period maps, and global Torelli-type results in later chapters.

Topology splits by holomorphic typeKahler is the structural hypothesis that makes this work
Sketch the Hodge theorem on compact Kahler manifolds. Every cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative, and harmonic forms split by type into the Hodge decomposition. The diamond of Hodge numbers obeys conjugation symmetry and Serre duality. This is the climactic structural result of the foundational chapter.
