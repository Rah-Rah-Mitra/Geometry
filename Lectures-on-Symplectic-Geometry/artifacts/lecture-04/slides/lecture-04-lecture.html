Generating Functions

One function, a whole Lagrangian, and through the graph trick a symplectomorphism

The promise

Describe a Lagrangian submanifold of $T^*X$ by a single scalar function.

The payoff

Twist the construction and it produces canonical transformations $(q,p)\mapsto(Q,P)$.

The link

This is the geometry behind Hamilton-Jacobi theory and classical mechanics.

A Function Cuts Out a Lagrangian

The recipe

Pick $f\in C^\infty(X)$. Its differential is a one-form, hence a section $x\mapsto(x,df_x)$ of $T^*X$.

The object

The image is the Lagrangian section $L_f=\{(x,df_x):x\in X\}$.

Generated Lagrangian

$$L_f=\{(x,df_x)\}\subset T^*X$$

Reading it

Each point of $X$ contributes exactly one covector, the slope of $f$ there.

Printed pp. 20-26A Function Cuts Out a Lagrangian
Start with the cleanest case. Any smooth function on the base has a differential, and the image of that differential is a section of the cotangent bundle. Because the differential of a function is closed, that section is automatically Lagrangian, so functions are the simplest source of Lagrangians.

Why the Section Is Lagrangian

Tautological form

Pull back $\alpha$ along a section $\mu$: one finds $\mu^*\alpha=\mu$ as a form.

Closedness test

The section is Lagrangian iff $\mu^*\omega=-d\mu=0$, i.e. $\mu$ is closed.

Exact is closed

$$\mu=df\ \Rightarrow\ d\mu=d(df)=0$$

Conclusion

$L_f$ is Lagrangian because $df$ is closed; conversely closed one-forms give all sections.

Printed pp. 20-26Why the Section Is Lagrangian
The tautological one-form pulls back along a section to the section itself viewed as a form. The graph of df is Lagrangian precisely when that form is closed, and df is closed because it is exact. This is the computation that makes a function a generator and explains the role of the canonical form.

Generating Functions With Parameters

The obstruction

Most Lagrangians are not graphs over $X$; sections alone are too rigid.

Add parameters

Take $f:X\times \mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R}$, with auxiliary fiber variables $u$.

Generating family

$$f(x,u),\qquad u\in\mathbb{R}^k$$

The idea

Only the points where $f$ is critical in $u$ survive to define the covector.

Printed pp. 20-26Generating Functions With Parameters
Not every Lagrangian is a section, so we enlarge the toolkit. Allow auxiliary parameters and consider a function on the base times a parameter space. The fiber-critical set, where the parameter derivative vanishes, projects to a Lagrangian that need not be a graph over the base, capturing folds and caustics.

The Fiber-Critical Set

Critical fiber

$C_f=\{(x,u):\partial_u f=0\}$ — the locus where $f$ is stationary in the parameters.

Regularity

Assume $0$ is a regular value of $\partial_u f$ so $C_f$ is a submanifold of dimension $\dim X$.

Projected Lagrangian

$$(x,u)\in C_f\ \longmapsto\ (x,\,\partial_x f)\in T^*X$$

Result

The image is Lagrangian; no longer a graph, it can fold over $X$.

Printed pp. 20-26The Fiber-Critical Set
The construction lives or dies on a transversality condition. Demand that zero is a regular value of the fiber derivative so the critical set is a genuine submanifold of the right dimension. Then the horizontal differential supplies the covector, and the projection lands in a Lagrangian. This is the method of generating families.

The Graph Trick

Twisted product

On $T^*X_1\times T^*X_2$ use $\tilde\omega=\omega_1\ominus\omega_2$, flipping the second factor's sign.

Criterion

$\varphi$ is a symplectomorphism $\iff$ its graph is Lagrangian for $\tilde\omega$.

Printed pp. 20-26The Graph Trick
Recall how symplectomorphisms become Lagrangians. Equip the product of two cotangent bundles with the twisted form by flipping the sign on the second factor. A map is symplectic exactly when its graph is Lagrangian for the twisted form. So generating a Lagrangian here generates a symplectomorphism.

$S(q,Q)$ Generates a Symplectomorphism

The generator

Take $S(q,Q)$ on $X\times X$. Its differential cuts out a Lagrangian in the twisted product.

Momentum rules

Old momentum from the old slot, new momentum from the new slot with a sign.

Canonical transformation

$$p=\frac{\partial S}{\partial q},\qquad P=-\frac{\partial S}{\partial Q}$$

Printed pp. 20-26S(q,Q) Generates a Map
Now write the generator in mechanics notation. A function of old and new positions defines momenta by partial differentiation, positive in the old slot and negative in the new. Solving these relations exchanges position pairs for momentum pairs and produces the canonical transformation. This is the classical generating function of the first kind.

The Solvability Gate: Mixed Hessian

What we need

To get a map $(q,p)\mapsto(Q,P)$ we must solve $p=\partial_q S$ for $Q$.

The condition

The mixed Hessian $\partial^2 S/\partial q\,\partial Q$ must be nondegenerate.

Implicit function gate

$$\det\!\Big(\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial q_i\,\partial Q_j}\Big)\neq 0$$

Printed pp. 20-26The Solvability Gate
The relations only define a map where they can be inverted. The mixed Hessian, the matrix of second derivatives in old and new positions, must be nondegenerate. The implicit function theorem then solves for the new position in terms of old position and momentum, exactly the gate seen in the notebook.

Hamilton-Jacobi and Free Motion

Hamilton-Jacobi

A time-dependent $S$ generating the flow solves $\partial_t S + H(q,\partial_q S)=0$.

Worked example

The quadratic generator gives free translation, verified symbolically in the notebook.

Free motion

$$S=-\tfrac12(q-Q)^2\ \Rightarrow\ (x,\xi)\mapsto(x+\xi,\xi)$$

Printed pp. 20-26Hamilton-Jacobi and Free Motion
Time-dependent generators tie directly to Hamilton-Jacobi theory, where the action solves a first-order partial differential equation and generates the flow. The quadratic generator minus half the squared displacement reproduces free translation, the worked example computed symbolically in the notebook with a unit mixed Hessian.

Geodesic Flow and Existence Near the Identity

Riemannian case

The metric gives $g_x:T_xX\to T_x^*X$; $S=-\tfrac12\,d(q,Q)^2$ generates geodesic flow.

Action principle

Constant-speed minimizing geodesics are the action minimizers the generator records.

Distance-squared generator

$$S(q,Q)=-\tfrac12\,d(q,Q)^2$$

Existence

Every symplectomorphism close to the identity has a generating function: the identity sits just off the degenerate locus, so the mixed Hessian stays invertible.

Printed pp. 20-26Geodesic Flow and Existence
On a Riemannian manifold the metric identifies tangent with cotangent vectors, and minus half the squared distance generates geodesic flow, with constant-speed minimizers as the action minimizers. Finally, near the identity every symplectomorphism admits a generating function, since the identity sits just outside the degenerate locus.

Recap and Outlook

The through line

  • $f$ gives the exact Lagrangian $L_f=\{(x,df_x)\}$.
  • Parameters and the fiber-critical set reach non-graph Lagrangians.
  • The twist turns Lagrangians into symplectomorphisms.

Mechanics dictionary

  • $p=\partial_q S,\ P=-\partial_Q S$ is a canonical transformation.
  • Mixed Hessian nondegeneracy is the solvability gate.
  • Hamilton-Jacobi and geodesic flow are the flagship examples.
