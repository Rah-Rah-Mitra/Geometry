Existence and Uniqueness of Moment Maps

Two cohomological questions about a symplectic action

Existence

When does a symplectic action admit a moment map? Obstructions live in $H^1(M)$ and in $H^1(\mathfrak{g}),H^2(\mathfrak{g})$.

Uniqueness

Two moment maps differ by an invariant constant in $(\mathfrak{g}^*)^G$.

Printed pp. 162-167Existence and Uniqueness of Moment Maps
Lecture 26 asks two questions about a symplectic action: when does it admit a moment map, and how unique is that map. The answers are cohomological. Existence is blocked in the first de Rham group of the manifold and the first two cohomologies of the acting Lie algebra; uniqueness is governed by the invariants of the coadjoint action.

Symplectic versus Hamiltonian Fields

Symplectic

$\iota_{X}\omega$ is closed: the flow preserves $\omega$.

Hamiltonian

$\iota_{X}\omega$ is exact: $\iota_{X}\omega = dH$ for some function $H$.

The gap

$\dfrac{\{\text{closed }\iota_X\omega\}}{\{\text{exact }\iota_X\omega\}}\;\cong\;H^1(M)$

Consequence

Every generating field $X^{\#}$ is symplectic; being Hamiltonian is the extra demand.

Printed pp. 162-167Symplectic vs Hamiltonian Fields
Each generator of the action produces a vector field whose contraction with the form is closed, so the field is symplectic. Being Hamiltonian is stronger: the contraction must be exact. The gap between closed and exact one-forms is exactly the first de Rham cohomology, so that group is the first place an obstruction can live.

The First Obstruction Lives in $H^1(M)$

Short exact sequence

$0 \to \chi_{\mathrm{ham}} \to \chi_{\mathrm{sympl}} \to H^1(M) \to 0$

Class of a field

$X^{\#}$ is Hamiltonian $\iff [\iota_{X^{\#}}\omega]=0$ in $H^1(M)$.

Clean case

If $H^1(M)=0$, every symplectic field is Hamiltonian.

Printed pp. 162-167The First Obstruction in H^1(M)
Walk through the short exact sequence relating Hamiltonian fields, symplectic fields, and the first cohomology of the manifold. A symplectic field is Hamiltonian precisely when its class in that cohomology vanishes. If the whole group is zero, every symplectic field is automatically Hamiltonian and this obstruction disappears.

From Action to Comoment Lift

Infinitesimal action

$\mathfrak{g}\to\chi_{\mathrm{sympl}}(M),\; X\mapsto X^{\#}$, a Lie algebra map.

Comoment

A lift $\mu^*:\mathfrak{g}\to C^\infty(M)$ assigning each $X$ a Hamiltonian.

Required compatibility

$\mu^*[X,Y]=\{\mu^*X,\mu^*Y\}$

Goal

Make the lift a Lie algebra homomorphism into the Poisson algebra, simultaneously for all of $\mathfrak{g}$.

Printed pp. 162-167From Action to Comoment Lift
A symplectic action gives a Lie algebra map from g into symplectic vector fields. To build a moment map we lift it to functions, choosing a Hamiltonian for each generator. The lift must be a Lie algebra homomorphism into the Poisson algebra, and assembling it equivariantly across all of g is where the second obstruction appears.

Lie Algebra Cohomology: $H^1$ and $H^2$

Chevalley-Eilenberg

$(\delta c)(X,Y)=-\,c([X,Y])$

$H^1(\mathfrak{g})$

The annihilator $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^0$ of the commutator ideal: the abelianization.

$H^2(\mathfrak{g})$

Classifies the defect cocycle of an arbitrary Hamiltonian lift.

Printed pp. 162-167Lie Algebra Cohomology H^1 and H^2
Introduce the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential on constant cochains. The first cohomology of g is the annihilator of the commutator ideal, measuring missing brackets. The second cohomology classifies the defect cocycle of an arbitrary lift. These two finite-dimensional groups encode the algebraic side of the existence question.

The Defect Cocycle and its Correction

Defect of a lift $\tau$

$c(X,Y)=\tau_{[X,Y]}-\{\tau_X,\tau_Y\}$

It is constant-valued

Both terms have the same Hamiltonian field, so the difference is locally constant.

It is a cocycle

The Jacobi identity gives $\delta c=0$.

Correction when $[c]=0$

$c=\delta b\;\Rightarrow\;\mu^*_X=\tau_X+b(X)$

What changes

Adding a constant $b(X)$ leaves $X^{\#}$ untouched but repairs the bracket relation.

Printed pp. 162-167The Defect Cocycle
After picking arbitrary Hamiltonians, the failure to preserve brackets is a constant two-cochain. The Jacobi identity forces it to be a cocycle. If its class in the second Lie algebra cohomology vanishes, a one-cochain correction adjusts the Hamiltonians by constants, repairing the bracket relation without changing any vector field.

Existence Theorem

Sufficient conditions

$H^1(M)=0 \;\;\text{and}\;\; H^2(\mathfrak{g})=0$

First condition

$H^1(M)=0$ makes each $X^{\#}$ Hamiltonian.

Second condition

$H^2(\mathfrak{g})=0$ makes the Hamiltonians bracket-compatible.

Printed pp. 162-167Existence Theorem
Collect the conditions. A moment map exists when the first de Rham cohomology of the manifold vanishes and the second cohomology of the acting Lie algebra vanishes. The first condition makes each field Hamiltonian; the second makes the choices compatible. Either vanishing alone removes one of the two obstructions.

Semisimple Groups Always Admit Moment Maps

Whitehead's lemmas

$\mathfrak{g}\text{ semisimple}\;\Rightarrow\;H^1(\mathfrak{g})=H^2(\mathfrak{g})=0$

Consequence

The algebraic obstruction vanishes for any symplectic action, independent of $M$.

Compact case

Compact semisimple $G$ always has a moment map.

Example algebras

$\mathfrak{su}(2),\;\mathfrak{su}(n),\;\mathfrak{so}(n)$

Why it matters

$[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}$ here, so the abelianization is trivial and lift defects are always coboundaries.

Printed pp. 162-167Semisimple Groups Always Work
Whitehead's lemmas say a semisimple Lie algebra has vanishing first and second cohomology with real coefficients. Both obstructions vanish at the algebra level regardless of the manifold, so a symplectic action of a compact semisimple group always admits a moment map. This is the cleanest existence statement in the lecture.

The Torus: Symplectic but not Hamiltonian

Generator on $T^2$

$X=\partial_{\theta_1},\quad \iota_X\omega=\iota_X(d\theta_1\wedge d\theta_2)=d\theta_2$

Closed, not exact

$d\theta_2$ is closed, but its period around the $\theta_2$ cycle is $2\pi\neq 0$.

Verdict

The class in $H^1(T^2)$ is nonzero, so no Hamiltonian exists.

Printed pp. 162-167The Torus Counterexample
On the two-torus with its standard area form, rotation in the first angle has contraction equal to the second angle one-form. That form is closed but not exact: its period around the second cycle is two pi. So the action is symplectic but not Hamiltonian, showing the de Rham obstruction is genuinely nonzero.

Uniqueness up to an Invariant Constant

Adding a constant

$d(\mu+c)=d\mu \;\Rightarrow\; \text{same } X^{\#}$

Invisible to fields

A constant shift $c\in\mathfrak{g}^*$ changes values but not Hamiltonian vector fields.

Equivariance filters

Only constants fixed by the coadjoint action are allowed.

Allowed shifts

$c\in(\mathfrak{g}^*)^G=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^0$

Difference of two maps

Any two moment maps for the same action differ by exactly such an invariant constant.

Printed pp. 162-167Uniqueness up to Invariant Constants
Adding a constant element of the dual algebra to a moment map does not change any Hamiltonian vector field. Equivariance restricts the allowed constants to those fixed by the coadjoint action, equivalently those vanishing on every bracket. So two moment maps for the same action differ by such an invariant constant and nothing more.

The Ambiguity Space $(\mathfrak{g}^*)^G$

Affine structure

$\{\text{moment maps}\}\;\text{is affine over}\;(\mathfrak{g}^*)^G$

Semisimple

$[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{g}$ so $(\mathfrak{g}^*)^G=0$: the moment map is unique.

Abelian

All brackets vanish, so the ambiguity is all of $\mathfrak{g}^*$.

Printed pp. 162-167The Ambiguity Space
The space of moment maps is affine, modeled on the coadjoint-invariant part of the dual algebra, which is the annihilator of the commutator ideal. For semisimple g this is zero, so the moment map is unique. For abelian g it is all of the dual, the maximal ambiguity, matching the abelianization picture.

Summary: The Cohomological Dictionary

$H^1(M)$

Separates symplectic from Hamiltonian fields.

$H^1(\mathfrak{g}),H^2(\mathfrak{g})$

Control the equivariant comoment lift.

$(\mathfrak{g}^*)^G$

Measures uniqueness of the moment map.

Clean corner

$G\text{ compact semisimple}\Rightarrow\;\text{exists and unique}$

Delicate corner

Abelian $G$: existence can fail, and when it holds the constant is unconstrained.

Printed pp. 162-167Summary and Dictionary
Summarize the dictionary: first de Rham cohomology separates symplectic from Hamiltonian fields; first and second Lie algebra cohomology control the equivariant lift; the invariant part of the dual algebra measures uniqueness. Compact semisimple groups land in the clean corner with existence and uniqueness both guaranteed.
