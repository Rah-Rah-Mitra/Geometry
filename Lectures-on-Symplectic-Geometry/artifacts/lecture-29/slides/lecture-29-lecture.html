The Delzant Construction

From a polytope to its symplectic toric manifold, by reduction

Goal

Given a Delzant polytope $\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, build a compact symplectic toric manifold with moment image exactly $\Delta$.

Why it matters

This proves the surjectivity half of the Delzant classification: every Delzant polytope is realized.

Printed pp. 181-188The Delzant Construction
Lecture 28 classified compact symplectic toric manifolds by Delzant polytopes. Today we prove surjectivity: every Delzant polytope is realized by an explicit manifold. The recipe reads the facet normals, builds a subtorus of a big torus, and reduces complex space down to the toric manifold whose moment image is the polytope.

The Input: A Delzant Polytope

Half-space form

$\Delta=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:\langle x,v_i\rangle\ge-\lambda_i,\ i=1,\dots,d\}$

Facet data

$d$ facets; each has a primitive inward normal $v_i\in\mathbb{Z}^n$ and a constant $\lambda_i\in\mathbb{R}$.

Smoothness

At each vertex the $n$ meeting normals form a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of the lattice (unimodularity).

Printed pp. 181-188The Input: A Delzant Polytope
We fix notation for the input. A Delzant polytope has d facets, each carrying a primitive inward normal vector in the integer lattice, and is cut out by the half-space inequalities pairing points against those normals. Smoothness means the normals meeting at each vertex form a lattice basis, the unimodularity condition that powers everything later.

Normals Define a Map of Tori

On lattices

$\pi:\mathbb{Z}^d\to\mathbb{Z}^n,\qquad e_i\mapsto v_i$

Subtorus

Exponentiate: $\pi$ induces $T^d\twoheadrightarrow T^n$. Set $N=\ker(T^d\to T^n)$, a subtorus we reduce by.

Dual side

The dual $\pi^*:(\mathbb{R}^n)^*\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^d)^*$ transports moment data downward.

Printed pp. 181-188Normals Define a Map of Tori
The normals assemble into a surjection from the standard lattice onto the polytope lattice, sending the i-th basis vector to the i-th inward normal. Exponentiating gives a surjection of tori, and its kernel N is the subtorus we reduce by. The dual sequence transports moment maps from the big torus down to N.

The Universal Source: $\mathbb{C}^d$

Standard action

$T^d$ rotates each coordinate: $(t_1,\dots,t_d)\cdot z=(t_1 z_1,\dots,t_d z_d)$, Hamiltonian for $\omega_0=\tfrac{i}{2}\sum dz_k\wedge d\bar z_k$.

Moment map

$\phi(z)=-\tfrac12\big(|z_1|^2,\dots,|z_d|^2\big)+\lambda$

Image

Shifted negative orthant: every $\phi_i\le\lambda_i$.

Printed pp. 181-188The Universal Source
Complex space carries the standard symplectic form and a Hamiltonian action of the d-torus rotating each coordinate independently. Its moment map records half the squared modulus of each coordinate, shifted by the constants lambda. This single well-understood space is the universal source from which every Delzant manifold is carved by reduction.

Restrict, Then Cut the Zero Level

N-moment map

$\phi_N=\iota^*\circ\phi:\mathbb{C}^d\to\mathfrak{n}^*$

Zero level

$Z=\phi_N^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbb{C}^d$. We will reduce $\mathbb{C}^d$ by $N$ at this level.

Plan

Show $Z$ compact, $N$ acts freely, then form $M_\Delta=Z/N$.

Printed pp. 181-188Restrict, Then Cut the Zero Level
Restricting the big-torus action to the subtorus N gives N a Hamiltonian action whose moment map is the dual projection composed with the standard moment map. We cut out the zero level set Z of this N-moment map. Reducing complex space by N at level zero is the heart of the construction.

Why $Z$ is Compact

Key identity

$\phi(Z)=\{\,\phi_i\le\lambda_i\,\}\cap\ker\iota^*=\pi^*(\Delta)$

Lifted polytope

The orthant meets the plane $\mathrm{im}\,\pi^*$ exactly in a copy of $\Delta$, which is compact.

Properness

$\phi$ is proper, so the preimage $Z$ of a compact set is compact.

Printed pp. 181-188Why $Z$ is Compact
A point lies in the image of Z under the big moment map exactly when it sits in the shifted negative orthant and is killed by the dual projection. That intersection is precisely the lifted polytope, which is compact. Since the moment map is proper, the closed level set Z is compact too.

Faces $\leftrightarrow$ Vanishing Coordinates

Stratification rule

$\phi_i(z)=\lambda_i\iff z_i=0$

Facets

Landing on facet $i$ forces the single coordinate $z_i=0$.

Vertices

A vertex activates $n$ facets, forcing $n$ coordinates to vanish: largest isotropy.

Printed pp. 181-188Faces and Vanishing Coordinates
Stratify by which facet inequalities are active. A point of the quotient lands on the facet indexed by i exactly when the i-th coordinate of complex space vanishes, since the moment component equals lambda only there. Vertices force n coordinates to vanish at once, producing the largest isotropy and the toughest freeness test.

$N$ Acts Freely: the Smoothness Payoff

Worst case

Isotropy is largest over vertices, where $n$ coordinates vanish. Freeness need only be checked there.

Unimodularity

$\det[\,v_{i_1}\,\cdots\,v_{i_n}\,]=\pm1$

Conclusion

Active normals form a basis, so the stabilizer maps onto $T^n$ and meets $N$ only at the identity.

Printed pp. 181-188N Acts Freely
Freeness of the N-action is checked where isotropy is worst, over vertices. There the n active normals form a lattice basis by smoothness, so the projection identifies the coordinate stabilizer torus with the full quotient torus. Its intersection with N is therefore trivial, the action is free, and the quotient is a manifold.

The Reduced Space $M_\Delta=Z/N$

Marsden-Weinstein

$M_\Delta=\mathbb{C}^d/\!/N=\phi_N^{-1}(0)/N$

Smooth & compact

$Z$ compact, $N$ free $\Rightarrow M_\Delta$ is a compact manifold.

Dimension

$\dim M_\Delta=2d-2(d-n)=2n$, as a symplectic manifold should be.

Printed pp. 181-188The Reduced Space
With N acting freely on the compact level Z, the Marsden-Weinstein quotient is a compact symplectic manifold of dimension twice n. We name it the Delzant manifold associated to the polytope. The construction is a reduction machine: feed it normals, return a smooth symplectic space of exactly the right dimension.

The Residual $T^n$ and Image $\Delta$

Residual action

$T^n=T^d/N\ \curvearrowright\ M_\Delta,\qquad \mu(M_\Delta)=\Delta$

Hamiltonian

The quotient torus descends to a Hamiltonian action with induced moment map $\mu$.

Surjectivity

$M_\Delta$ is a symplectic toric manifold realizing $\Delta$: Delzant's map is onto.

Printed pp. 181-188Residual Torus and Image
The big torus modulo N is an n-torus that descends to a Hamiltonian action on the quotient. Its moment map is induced by the standard one, and its image is exactly the polytope we started from. So the quotient is a symplectic toric manifold realizing the polytope, completing the surjectivity half of Delzant's theorem.

Synthesis and a Worked Example

Pipeline recap

Normals $\to$ torus sequence $\to$ cut $Z\subset\mathbb{C}^d$ $\to$ quotient by $N$ $\to$ toric $M_\Delta$ with $\mu(M_\Delta)=\Delta$.

Example

Standard 2-simplex, $v=(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,1)$: $\mathbb{C}^3/\!/S^1=\mathbb{CP}^2$.

Break smoothness

Swap $(1,1)\to(1,2)$: vertex determinant $\ne\pm1$, $N$ no longer free, quotient is an orbifold.

Printed pp. 181-188Synthesis and Example
Putting it together: normals give the torus sequence, complex space supplies the universal orthant, the zero level is compact, unimodularity makes N act freely, and the quotient is toric with moment image the polytope. The standard two-simplex returns complex projective plane; replacing a normal to break smoothness breaks the manifold property exactly as predicted.
