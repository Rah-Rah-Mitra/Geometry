A Holomorphic Atlas

Definition

A complex manifold of complex dimension $n$ has charts $\varphi_\alpha:U_\alpha\to\mathbb{C}^n$ whose transitions

$\psi=\varphi_\beta\circ\varphi_\alpha^{-1}$

are biholomorphic on overlaps.

Stronger than smooth

Each $\psi$ and $\psi^{-1}$ obey the Cauchy-Riemann equations.

Real picture

$\mathbb{C}^n\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}$, so a complex $n$-manifold is a real $2n$-manifold with extra rigidity.

Cauchy-Riemann

$\partial_x u=\partial_y v,\quad \partial_y u=-\partial_x v$

Printed pp. 81-87A Holomorphic Atlas
A complex manifold of dimension n carries charts into open sets of complex n-space, with all transition maps biholomorphic. Emphasize that the requirement is genuinely stronger than smoothness: each transition and its inverse must satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations, so they are holomorphic in every variable simultaneously.

The Induced Structure $J$

Local rotation

In each chart, $J\,\partial_{x_k}=\partial_{y_k}$ and $J\,\partial_{y_k}=-\partial_{x_k}$.

Why it glues

Holomorphic transitions have $D\psi\,J=J\,D\psi$, so charts agree.

Result

$J^2=-\mathrm{Id},\quad J\ \text{globally defined}$

Printed pp. 81-87The Induced Structure J
Each holomorphic chart carries the standard rotation that sends the x partials to the y partials. Because transition maps are holomorphic, their differentials commute with this rotation, so the local definitions agree on overlaps. The result is a globally well-defined almost complex structure J squaring to minus the identity.

Type Decomposition

Tangent split

$TM\otimes\mathbb{C}=T^{1,0}\oplus T^{0,1}$

$\pm i$ eigenspaces of $J$; spanned by $\partial_z$ and $\partial_{\bar z}$.

Cotangent split

$dz=dx+i\,dy,\quad d\bar z=dx-i\,dy$

Printed pp. 81-87Type Decomposition
Complexifying the tangent bundle and diagonalizing J splits it into the plus-i eigenspace and the minus-i eigenspace, the holomorphic and antiholomorphic tangents. Dually the one-forms split into dz and dz-bar pieces, and k-forms refine into bidegree types counting holomorphic versus antiholomorphic factors.

Splitting the Differential

Decomposition

$d=\partial+\bar\partial$

$\partial$ raises holomorphic degree, $\bar\partial$ raises antiholomorphic degree.

Local form

$\bar\partial f=\sum_k \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\bar z_k}\,d\bar z_k$

Consequence of $d^2=0$

$\partial^2=0,\quad \bar\partial^2=0,\quad \partial\bar\partial+\bar\partial\partial=0$

Why it closes

The splitting $d=\partial+\bar\partial$ holds because holomorphic coordinates exist; this is the integrable case.

Printed pp. 81-87Splitting the Differential
On a complex manifold the exterior derivative splits cleanly as partial plus dbar, raising holomorphic and antiholomorphic degree respectively. This is exactly where integrability matters: the splitting closes only because holomorphic coordinates exist. From d squared equals zero we extract the crucial relation dbar squared equals zero.

Integrability

The question

Given an abstract $J$ with $J^2=-\mathrm{Id}$, does it come from a complex atlas?

Definition

$J$ is integrable if holomorphic coordinates exist near every point.

Involutivity

$[T^{1,0},\,T^{1,0}]\subseteq T^{1,0}$

The holomorphic tangent distribution is closed under bracket.

Frobenius flavor

Integrability is an involutivity obstruction, measured by a tensor.

Printed pp. 81-87Integrability
An abstract almost complex structure need not come from any atlas. Integrability is precisely the condition that holomorphic coordinates exist locally. Walk students through the chain: complex charts give a closed type splitting, which forces the bracket of two holomorphic vector fields to stay holomorphic, the Frobenius-style obstruction.

The Nijenhuis Tensor

Definition

$N_J(X,Y)=[JX,JY]-J[JX,Y]-J[X,JY]-[X,Y]$

It is a tensor

Depends only on $X,Y$ pointwise, not their derivatives — computable from $J$.

Measures

The failure of $T^{1,0}$ to be involutive under the Lie bracket.

Dimension two

$N_J\equiv 0$ automatically on a real surface, so every $J$ there is integrable.

Printed pp. 81-87The Nijenhuis Tensor
The Nijenhuis tensor measures the failure of the holomorphic tangent distribution to be involutive. It is a genuine tensor, built from brackets of J-twisted vector fields, vanishing automatically in real dimension two. Stress that it is computable pointwise, turning integrability into an algebraic condition on J and its first derivatives.

Newlander-Nirenberg

Theorem

An almost complex structure $J$ is integrable — arises from a complex atlas — if and only if

$N_J=0 \iff \bar\partial^2=0$

Printed pp. 81-87Newlander-Nirenberg
The Newlander-Nirenberg theorem is the deep payoff: an almost complex structure is integrable, hence arises from a genuine complex atlas, if and only if its Nijenhuis tensor vanishes, equivalently dbar squared equals zero. This converts a hard PDE existence problem into a finite algebraic test on J.

Holomorphic Functions, Forms, Bundles

Holomorphic function

$f\ \text{holomorphic}\iff \bar\partial f=0$

Holomorphic forms

$(p,0)$-forms with holomorphic coefficients; closed under $\partial$.

Holomorphic bundles

Transition functions are holomorphic matrix-valued maps on overlaps.

Dolbeault complex

$\Omega^{p,0}\xrightarrow{\bar\partial}\Omega^{p,1}\xrightarrow{\bar\partial}\Omega^{p,2}\cdots$

$\bar\partial^2=0$ yields Dolbeault cohomology.

Printed pp. 81-87Holomorphic Objects
With integrability secured, holomorphic functions, forms, and vector bundles all make invariant sense. A function is holomorphic when dbar kills it; transition functions of a holomorphic bundle are holomorphic matrix-valued maps. The dbar operator then organizes these objects into the Dolbeault complex and its cohomology.

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ via Homogeneous Coordinates

Charts

$U_i=\{[z_0:\cdots:z_n]: z_i\neq 0\}$

Affine coordinates $z_j/z_i$ on $U_i$.

Transition on $\mathbb{CP}^1$

$w=\dfrac{1}{z}$

Holomorphic away from the excluded point.

Printed pp. 81-87CP^n via Homogeneous Coordinates
Complex projective space is the workhorse example. Homogeneous coordinates define standard affine charts by dividing by a nonzero coordinate. On overlaps the transition maps are holomorphic, the model being w equals one over z on the projective line, holomorphic away from the excluded point. Projective varieties inherit this structure.
