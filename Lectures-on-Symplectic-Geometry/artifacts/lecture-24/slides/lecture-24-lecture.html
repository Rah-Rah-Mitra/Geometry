Marsden-Weinstein Recap

The theorem

Free, proper, regular zero level $\Rightarrow$ the quotient is symplectic.

Dimension drop

We lose $2\dim G$: one direction to the constraint, one to the orbit.

Reduced space

$$M_0 = \mu^{-1}(0)/G,\qquad \iota^*\omega = \pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$$

Today

We move beyond the zero level and beyond abstractions to worked examples.

Remind the audience that the quotient of a regular zero level by a free proper action carries a unique symplectic form pulled back from the inclusion. Stress the dimension drop of twice the group rank: we lose one direction to the constraint and one to the orbit for each independent symmetry generator.

Why the Flow Stays on a Level

Invariance

$G$-invariance: the group field $X^\#$ kills the Hamiltonian $H$.

Noether

The moment-map component is conserved along the Hamiltonian flow.

The cancellation

$$\{\mu^X, H\} = \mathrm{d}\mu^X(X_H) = -\mathrm{d}H(X^\#) = 0$$

Printed pp. 145-152Why Levels Are Preserved
Walk through the Noether cancellation: invariance kills the Hamiltonian along the group field, the moment map turns that field into a component differential, and the Hamiltonian vector field contracts it into the form. The residual derivative of the moment value along the flow vanishes, so each level is flow-invariant.

Reduction at a General Value

The obstruction

A value $\xi$ need not be fixed by $\mathrm{Ad}^*$, so only its stabilizer $G_\xi$ acts on the level.

Point reduction

$\mu^{-1}(\xi)/G_\xi$ when $G_\xi$ acts freely and properly.

Orbit reduction

$\mu^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_\xi)/G$, quotienting the whole coadjoint-orbit preimage.

Printed pp. 145-152Reduction at a General Value
Explain that an arbitrary value need not be fixed by the coadjoint action, so point reduction at it can fail. Two cures: orbit reduction quotients the whole coadjoint orbit preimage, and the shifting trick trades a nonzero value for the zero level of an enlarged space crossed with the orbit.

The Shifting Trick

Idea

Trade a nonzero value for the zero level of an enlarged space.

Torus shortcut

For $T^k$ every level is central, so simply subtract a constant from $\mu$.

Shift to zero

$$M_\xi \;\cong\; \big(M \times \mathcal{O}_\xi^{-}\big) /\!/_0\, G$$

Printed pp. 145-152The Shifting Trick
Make the shifting trick concrete. Replace reduction of M at the value xi by reduction of the product of M with the coadjoint orbit through xi, carrying the opposite symplectic form, at the zero level. For a torus every level is central, so we may simply subtract a constant from the moment map.

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ as a Symplectic Reduction

The action

$S^1$ rotates $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ by a common phase $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z$.

The moment map

$\mu(z)=\tfrac12|z|^2$ generates this circle action.

The reduction

The regular level is a sphere; its quotient is $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

Reduction at the unit sphere

$$\mu^{-1}(\tfrac12)=S^{2n+1},\qquad \mathbb{CP}^n = S^{2n+1}/S^1$$

Why it works

$S^1$ acts freely on the sphere, so the quotient is a smooth symplectic manifold.

Printed pp. 145-152CP^n by Reduction
Introduce the marquee example. The circle acts on complex n-plus-one space by simultaneous phase rotation with moment map one half the squared norm. The regular level is the odd sphere, the action is free there, and the quotient is complex projective space. Emphasize that this is reduction done explicitly.

The Sphere Quotiented by the Circle

Fix the norm

$|z|^2=1$ pins points to the odd sphere $S^{2n+1}$.

Identify phases

Each complex line $\mathbb{C}\cdot z$ becomes a single point.

Dimensions

$2(n{+}1)-1-1 = 2n$, the real dimension of $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

Printed pp. 145-152The Sphere and the Circle
Detail the geometry: fixing the squared norm pins points to a sphere, and quotienting by the common phase identifies each complex line to a point. The fibers are circles, so projective space is the base of a circle bundle over the sphere. Count dimensions: two n plus one minus one gives two n.

The Reduced Form is Fubini-Study

Descent

The flat form $\omega_0=\tfrac{i}{2}\sum \mathrm{d}z_j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_j$ descends to the quotient.

Kahler

The reduced form is the canonical Fubini-Study Kahler form $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$.

In an affine chart

$$\omega_{\mathrm{FS}} = \frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar\partial \log\big(1+|w|^2\big)$$

Same object elsewhere

$\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is the symplectic form on a $U(n{+}1)$ coadjoint orbit.

Printed pp. 145-152Fubini-Study Form
State that the reduced symplectic form on projective space is the Fubini-Study form, the canonical Kahler form descending from the flat form upstairs. Note the normalization tied to the chosen level radius. This is the same form appearing as the symplectic structure on coadjoint orbits of the unitary group.

The Hopf Picture: $\mathbb{CP}^1 = S^2$

Specialize

For $n=1$ the level set is $S^3$ and the circle fibers are Hopf circles.

Hopf fibration

$$S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} S^2 = \mathbb{CP}^1$$

Specialize to n equal one. The five sphere becomes the three sphere, the circle fibers are Hopf circles, and the quotient is the two sphere, the Riemann sphere CP one. Draw the Hopf map so the audience sees reduction as collapsing each linked fiber circle to one base point.

Reduction in Mechanics

Cyclic coordinate

If $x_n$ is ignorable, its momentum $\xi_n$ is conserved.

Lower the dimension

Freeze $\xi_n=c$, drop the angle; phase space shrinks by two.

Reduction in stages

Quotient by $G_1$, then by $G_2$; equals quotient by $G_1\times G_2$ when free.

Reduced then reconstructed

$$\dim M_{\mathrm{red}} = \dim M - 2,\quad x_n(t)=\int \partial_{\xi_n}H\,\mathrm{d}t$$

Payoff

Eliminating symmetry leaves a solvable lower-dimensional system; reconstruct the angle by one integration.

Printed pp. 145-152Reduction in Mechanics
Connect to dynamics. A cyclic coordinate makes its conjugate momentum conserved; freezing that level and dropping the ignorable angle lowers the phase space dimension by two. Reduction in stages handles a product group one factor at a time, and the staged quotient matches the direct product quotient under freeness.

Symplectic Quotients and GIT

$M/\!/G$

The symplectic quotient is $\mu^{-1}(0)/G$, written $M/\!/G$.

Kempf-Ness

Zeros of $\mu$ meet each polystable $G^{\mathbb C}$-orbit in one $G$-orbit.

Two quotients agree

$$\mu^{-1}(0)/G \;\cong\; M^{\mathrm{ss}}/\!\!/\,G^{\mathbb{C}}$$

Printed pp. 145-152Symplectic Quotient and GIT
Bridge to algebraic geometry. The symplectic quotient M-double-slash-G equals, for the complexified group action, the geometric invariant theory quotient. The Kempf-Ness theorem identifies polystable orbits with zeros of the moment map, so the symplectic and algebraic quotients agree as the same space.

Orbifold Warning and Recap

Finite stabilizers survive

A regular level kills infinitesimal stabilizers, but finite ones remain as singularities.

Weighted actions

Weighted $S^1$ actions give teardrop and football orbifolds.

Workflow

Fix a level, quotient the orbit, read the reduced form, recognize $\mathbb{CP}^n$ and GIT quotients.

Printed pp. 145-152Orbifold Warning and Recap
Close with the caveat: a regular level removes infinitesimal stabilizers, but finite stabilizers survive as orbifold singularities, as weighted circle actions show with teardrop and football quotients. Recap the workflow: fix a level, quotient by the orbit, read off the reduced form, and recognize CP^n and GIT quotients as instances.
