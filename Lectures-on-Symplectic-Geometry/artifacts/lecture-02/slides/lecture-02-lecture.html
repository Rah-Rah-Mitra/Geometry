The Symplectic Form on $T^*X$

Every cotangent bundle is the universal phase space

The claim

For any smooth manifold $X$, the cotangent bundle $T^*X$ carries a canonical symplectic form, with no extra choices.

Why it matters

Positions live on the base $X$; momenta live in the fibers. This is the geometric home of classical mechanics.

Coordinates on $T^*X$

From a base chart

A chart $(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ on $X$ induces a chart $(q_i, p_i)$ on $T^*X$: the $p_i$ are the components of a covector in the basis $dq_i$.

Transformation rule

Base coordinates change by a diffeomorphism; fiber coefficients change by the inverse-transpose Jacobian.

A point of $T^*X$

$$\big(q,\,p\big),\quad p=\textstyle\sum_i p_i\,dq_i \in T_q^*X$$

Projection

$\pi:T^*X\to X$ sends $(q,p)\mapsto q$, forgetting the covector.

Printed pp. 7-12Cotangent Coordinates
Explain how a chart on X induces a chart on the cotangent bundle. The base coordinates are positions and the fiber coefficients are the components of a covector in the dual basis of differentials. Warn that fiber coefficients transform by the inverse-transpose Jacobian, unlike tangent vectors.

The Tautological One-Form $\alpha$

The idea

A point $p\in T^*X$ is already a covector. Use it to evaluate any tangent vector — after pushing that vector down to the base.

Intrinsic definition

$$\alpha_p(v)=p\big(d\pi_p(v)\big),\quad v\in T_p(T^*X)$$

Printed pp. 7-12The Tautological One-Form
This is the heart of the lecture. A point of the cotangent bundle is itself a covector sitting over a base point. The tautological form feeds a tangent vector upstairs through the projection's differential, then lets that resident covector evaluate it. Emphasize that no auxiliary data enters the definition.

$\alpha$ in Local Coordinates

The collapse

Choosing a chart, the intrinsic recipe becomes a single clean sum over the base differentials.

Liouville one-form

$$\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i\,dq_i$$

Why only $dq_i$?

The projection $\pi$ kills the vertical $dp_i$ directions, so applying $d\pi$ leaves only base differentials for $p$ to act on.

Names

$\alpha$ is called the tautological, Liouville, or canonical one-form — a coordinate shadow, not a coordinate accident.

Printed pp. 7-12Alpha in Coordinates
Show that the intrinsic recipe collapses to the clean formula sum of p dq once a chart is chosen. Walk through why the projection kills the vertical part and why only the base differentials survive. The formula is a coordinate shadow, not a coordinate accident.

Why $\alpha$ Survives Chart Changes

The test

Change the base chart $q\mapsto q'=f(q)$. Covectors follow by $p'=p\,(f')^{-1}$, the inverse-transpose rule.

Invariance

$$\sum_i p_i'\,dq_i' = \sum_i p_i\,dq_i$$

The cancellation

The chain-rule factor $f'$ in $dq_i'$ cancels the $(f')^{-1}$ in $p_i'$ exactly — the form is the same in every chart.

Notebook check

The symbolic pullback with $Q=\theta+a\sin\theta$ simplifies the coefficient back to $\xi$, confirming invariance.

Printed pp. 7-12Coordinate Independence
Demonstrate that alpha is well defined by changing charts on the base. The base map and the inverse-transpose fiber rule conspire so that the new sum of p dq equals the old one. Mention the notebook's symbolic pullback check, where the coefficient simplifies exactly back to xi.

The Canonical Form $\omega=-d\alpha$

Definition

Set $\omega=-d\alpha$. Since $\omega$ is exact, it is automatically closed: $d\omega=-d^2\alpha=0$.

In coordinates

$$\omega=\sum_{i} dq_i\wedge dp_i$$

Printed pp. 7-12The Canonical Symplectic Form
Define omega as minus the exterior derivative of alpha. Because omega is exact it is automatically closed, so half the symplectic axiom comes for free. In coordinates omega becomes the standard sum of dq wedge dp, the model two-form from the previous lecture.

Nondegeneracy and Symplectic Volume

The matrix

In $(q,p)$ coordinates $\omega$ has the standard block form, whose determinant is nonzero — so $\omega$ is nondegenerate.

Standard form

$$\Omega_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \det\Omega_0=1$$

Volume form

The top power $\omega^{\wedge n}$ never vanishes, giving a canonical orientation and volume on $T^*X$.

Homework hook

On compact manifolds an exact $\omega$ clashes with Stokes — so spheres $S^{2n}$, $n>1$, admit no symplectic form.

Printed pp. 7-12Nondegeneracy and Volume
Confirm the second axiom: the coordinate matrix of omega is the standard block form with nonzero determinant, so omega is nondegenerate. Its top exterior power omega to the n is a nowhere-vanishing volume form, orienting the manifold. Reference the notebook determinant ledger across dimensions.

Naturality: The Cotangent Lift

The lift

A diffeomorphism $f:X\to X$ lifts to $f^{\#}:T^*X\to T^*X$, pushing covectors by the inverse-transpose differential.

Functoriality

$$(f^{\#})^*\alpha=\alpha\;\Longrightarrow\;(f^{\#})^*\omega=\omega$$

Symplectomorphism

Because the lift preserves the tautological form, it preserves $\omega$ exactly — every $f^{\#}$ is a symplectomorphism.

Slogan

The construction $X\rightsquigarrow(T^*X,\omega)$ is natural: base symmetries become phase-space symmetries for free.

Printed pp. 7-12Naturality of the Lift
State the functoriality theorem. Any diffeomorphism of the base lifts canonically to the cotangent bundle by pushing covectors with the inverse-transpose differential. This cotangent lift pulls the tautological form back to itself, hence pulls omega back to omega, so it is a symplectomorphism.

Example: $T^*S^1$ is a Cylinder

Setup

Coordinates $(\theta,\xi)$ on the cylinder. A circle map $\theta\mapsto f(\theta)$ lifts with fiber rule $\xi\mapsto\xi/f'(\theta)$.

Area preserved

$$dQ\wedge d\Xi = d\theta\wedge d\xi$$

Not Every Symplectomorphism is a Lift

A non-example

Fiber translation $(\theta,\xi)\mapsto(\theta,\xi+c)$ preserves $\omega$, yet it comes from no base diffeomorphism.

Preserves $\omega$

$$d\theta\wedge d(\xi+c)=d\theta\wedge d\xi$$

Injective, not onto

Cotangent lifts inject into $\mathrm{Symp}(T^*X)$ but do not exhaust it — the symmetry group is strictly larger.

Takeaway

Naturality is powerful but partial: it identifies a distinguished family, not all symplectomorphisms.

Printed pp. 7-12A Symplectomorphism Caveat
Sharpen the theorem with a non-example. Translating each fiber vertically preserves omega yet does not come from any base diffeomorphism. So cotangent lifts inject into symplectomorphisms but do not surject. This separates the natural family from the full symmetry group.

First Lagrangians: Zero Section and Fibers

Zero section

$X_0=\{(q,0)\}$ has $p=0$, so $\alpha$ vanishes there; it is half-dimensional and $\omega|_{X_0}=0$.

Cotangent fiber

$T_q^*X$ over a fixed base point also kills $\alpha$, since $d\pi=0$ along the fiber.

Printed pp. 7-12Lagrangian Submanifolds
Introduce the two distinguished half-dimensional submanifolds. The zero section, covectors equal to zero, and each cotangent fiber over a fixed base point both make alpha and hence omega vanish on them. They are the first Lagrangian submanifolds, a theme that will recur throughout the course.

Recap and Outlook

The chain

  • Intrinsic $\alpha_p(v)=p(d\pi_p v)$, locally $\sum p_i\,dq_i$.
  • Canonical $\omega=-d\alpha$, locally $\sum dq_i\wedge dp_i$.
  • Closed (exact) and nondegenerate by construction.
  • Natural under cotangent lifts $f^{\#}$.

Looking ahead

These cotangent models seed the Lagrangian-neighborhood and tubular-neighborhood theorems to come.
