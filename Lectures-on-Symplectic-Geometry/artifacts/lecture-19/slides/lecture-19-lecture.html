From Newton to the Principle of Least Action

Conservative force

A force is conservative when it is the negative gradient of a potential.

Force law

$F = -\nabla V, \qquad m\ddot{x} = F$

Reformulation

Newton's equation is the stationarity of an action built from energy.

Action

$\mathcal{A} = \int_a^b \big(T - V\big)\,dt$

Shift of viewpoint

From pointwise dynamics to selecting a whole curve.

Printed pp. 111-118From Newton to Action
Start with a conservative force, the negative gradient of a potential. Newton's equation is recovered, but reformulated: instead of integrating a force in time, nature selects the path that makes the time integral of kinetic minus potential energy stationary. Emphasize that this is a statement about whole curves, not pointwise dynamics.

The Lagrangian Lives on $TM$

Tangent bundle

A point and a velocity together form a point of $TM$.

Lagrangian

$L:TM\to\mathbb{R}, \quad L\big(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t)\big)$

Lift

The curve lifts to $\tilde\gamma = (\gamma,\dot\gamma)$ in $TM$.

Printed pp. 111-118The Lagrangian on TM
The natural home of the Lagrangian is the tangent bundle. A point and a velocity together, the lift of the curve, are the arguments of L. Make clear that L lives on TM while the conjugate momentum and Hamiltonian will later live on the cotangent bundle; the Legendre transform is the bridge between them.

The Action Functional

Definition

$\mathcal{A}(\gamma) = \int_a^b L\big(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t)\big)\,dt$

Domain

An infinite-dimensional space of curves with $\gamma(a),\gamma(b)$ fixed.

A function on path space

$\mathcal{A}$ assigns one real number to each candidate path.

The question

Which path makes $\mathcal{A}$ stationary, and when is it a minimum?

Calculus of variations

Differentiate $\mathcal{A}$ along families of competing curves.

Printed pp. 111-118The Action Functional
Define the action as the time integral of the Lagrangian along the lifted curve. Stress that the action is a function whose domain is an infinite-dimensional space of paths with fixed endpoints. Minimizing or making it stationary is a calculus problem on that path space, which motivates the variation we set up next.

Endpoint-Fixed Variation

Family

$\gamma_s = \gamma + s\,c, \qquad c(a)=c(b)=0$

First variation

Differentiate $\mathcal{A}(\gamma_s)$ in $s$ at $s=0$.

Stationary path

The derivative vanishes for every admissible $c$.

Printed pp. 111-118Endpoint-Fixed Variation
Introduce a one-parameter family of paths sharing the same endpoints, so the variation field vanishes at both ends. Differentiate the action in the family parameter at zero. The picture shows perturbed paths fanning around the true trajectory; the action graph is flat at the center, which is exactly the stationarity we want to characterize.

The Euler-Lagrange Equations

Differentiate and integrate by parts

Move the time derivative off the variation field $c$.

Boundary term dies

Because $c(a)=c(b)=0$, only the interior survives.

Arbitrary $c$

The bracket must vanish identically along $\gamma$.

First variation

$\delta\mathcal{A} = \int_a^b\!\Big(\tfrac{\partial L}{\partial x} - \tfrac{d}{dt}\tfrac{\partial L}{\partial v}\Big)c\,dt$

Critical-point condition

$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial x^i} - \dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial v^i} = 0$

Printed pp. 111-118Euler-Lagrange Equations
Differentiate under the integral, then integrate by parts to move the time derivative off the variation field. The boundary term dies because endpoints are fixed. Since the variation is otherwise arbitrary, the bracket must vanish, giving the Euler-Lagrange equation. This is the precise critical-point condition for the action functional.

The Legendre Condition

Three cases, sorted by the velocity Hessian $L_{vv}$

No velocity dependence

$\det L_{vv}=0$: E-L is a pointwise critical-set condition.

Affine in velocity

$\det L_{vv}=0$: only first-order content, no IVP.

Quadratic in velocity

$\det L_{vv}\neq0$: genuine second-order law.

Nondegeneracy

$\det\Big(\dfrac{\partial^2 L}{\partial v^i\,\partial v^j}\Big)\neq 0$

Model: $L=\tfrac12 v^2 - \tfrac12\omega^2 x^2$

$\ddot x = -\omega^2 x, \qquad L_{vv}=1$

Doorway

Nondegeneracy lets initial position and velocity fix the motion.

Printed pp. 111-118The Legendre Condition
Walk through the ledger of three cases from the notebook. With no velocity dependence the equation is a pointwise critical-set condition; affine velocity dependence gives a singular velocity Hessian and only first-order content; a nondegenerate velocity Hessian is what makes Euler-Lagrange a genuine second-order law solvable from initial position and velocity.

Stationary Is Not the Same as Minimal

Second variation decides

A critical path is a minimum only if $\delta^2\mathcal{A}>0$.

Oscillator window, $c=\sin(\pi t/T)$

$Q(T)=\tfrac{T}{2}\big((\pi/T)^2-\omega^2\big)$

Threshold

$Q>0 \iff T<\pi/\omega$. Longer intervals give a saddle.

Printed pp. 111-118Stationary Is Not Minimal
A solution of Euler-Lagrange is only a critical point. The second variation decides whether it is a local minimum. Show the harmonic-oscillator window formula: the second variation is positive precisely when the interval is shorter than pi over omega. Beyond that length the critical path becomes a saddle, so length of the interval genuinely matters.

Convexity and the Existence of Minimizers

Tonelli hypotheses

Strict convexity of $L$ in $v$ plus superlinear growth.

Convexity in velocity

$\dfrac{\partial^2 L}{\partial v^i\,\partial v^j}\succ 0$

Consequence

Lower semicontinuity and coercivity force a minimizer to exist.

Printed pp. 111-118Tonelli: Minimizers Exist
State the Tonelli philosophy: strict convexity of L in the velocity variable plus suitable growth gives lower semicontinuity and coercivity, so a minimizer exists in the path space. Convexity in velocity is the same hypothesis seen two ways, locally as a positive velocity Hessian and globally as existence of minimizers.

From $TM$ to $T^*M$: Hamiltonian Dynamics

Fiber derivative

$p_i = \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial v^i}, \quad H = p_i v^i - L$

Legendre transform

Nondegeneracy makes $TM\to T^*M$ a local diffeomorphism.

Hamilton's equations

$\dot x = \partial H/\partial p,\ \dot p = -\partial H/\partial x$.

Printed pp. 111-118TM to T*M
Connect the variational and Hamiltonian pictures. The fiber derivative of L sends a velocity to a momentum, mapping TM to the cotangent bundle. When the Legendre condition holds this is a local diffeomorphism, and the Hamiltonian is the Legendre transform of L. Euler-Lagrange on TM becomes Hamilton's equations on T-star-M.

Geodesics from the Kinetic Lagrangian

Kinetic Lagrangian

$L = \tfrac12\,g_{ij}(x)\,v^i v^j$

Geodesic equation

Its Euler-Lagrange equation is the geodesic equation.

Action and length

Constant-speed geodesics minimize both on short arcs.

Recap of the arc

  • $\mathcal{A}=\int L\,dt$ on paths with fixed endpoints.
  • Euler-Lagrange = vanishing first variation.
  • Legendre nondegeneracy = second-order law.
  • Convexity in $v$ = local minimum and existence.
  • Legendre transform bridges to $T^*M$.

Sphere check

The great-circle arc beats a same-endpoint detour in length and action.

Printed pp. 111-118Geodesics and Recap
Close with the kinetic Lagrangian, one half the metric norm squared. Its Euler-Lagrange equation is the geodesic equation, and constant-speed geodesics minimize both action and length on short arcs, as the sphere comparison confirms. Recap the arc: action, Euler-Lagrange, Legendre, convexity, and the bridge to Hamiltonian dynamics.
