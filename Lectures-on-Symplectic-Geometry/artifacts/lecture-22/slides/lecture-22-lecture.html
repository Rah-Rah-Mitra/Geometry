Hamiltonian Actions

When a symmetry comes with a moment map into $\mathfrak{g}^*$

Symplectic action

A group $G$ acts preserving $\omega$.

Moment map

$\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^*$ packages the conserved quantities.

Noether

Symmetry $\Rightarrow$ conservation, made geometric.

Printed pp. 131-138Hamiltonian Actions
Open by recalling that a symplectic action only preserves the form; today we demand much more. A Hamiltonian action carries a moment map into the dual Lie algebra whose components generate the infinitesimal action. This packages every conserved quantity of the symmetry into one geometric object, and it is the doorway to reduction across printed pages 131 to 138.

From Symplectic to Hamiltonian

Symplectic only

Each $g\in G$ pulls $\omega$ back to itself; nothing more is guaranteed.

The upgrade

Hamiltonian asks each generator to lift to an actual function.

Obstruction

Whether the lift exists is a cohomological question.

Invariance of the form

$$g^*\omega=\omega\quad\text{for all }g\in G$$

When it clears

Abelian or simply connected $G$ often admit the lift automatically.

Printed pp. 131-138From Symplectic to Hamiltonian
Stress the upgrade in structure. A symplectic action merely preserves omega; a Hamiltonian action additionally lifts each generator to an honest Hamiltonian function. Without this lift there is no conserved quantity and no moment map. Emphasize that the obstruction is cohomological, and that abelian and simply connected groups frequently clear it automatically.

The Moment Map: Two Conditions

Condition (i) — generation

$$d\mu^X=\iota_{X^\#}\,\omega,\qquad \mu^X:=\langle\mu,X\rangle$$

Condition (ii) — equivariance

$$\mu\circ g=\mathrm{Ad}^*_g\circ\,\mu$$

Local / differential

The first condition holds pointwise: each component is a Hamiltonian for $X^\#$.

Global / equivariant

The second condition ties the action on $M$ to the coadjoint action on $\mathfrak{g}^*$.

Definition

An action with such a $\mu$ is Hamiltonian; $(M,\omega,G,\mu)$ is a Hamiltonian $G$-space.

Printed pp. 131-138The Moment Map Definition
State the two defining conditions slowly. First, for every algebra element the scalar component is a Hamiltonian generating the fundamental vector field through the contraction identity. Second, the map intertwines the action on the manifold with the coadjoint action on the dual. Make clear that the first condition is local and differential while the second is global and equivariant.

The Generating Vector Field $X^\#$

From group to field

$X\in\mathfrak{g}$ flows points via $\exp(tX)$; differentiating at $t=0$ gives $X^\#$.

Fundamental field

$$X^\#_p=\left.\tfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\exp(tX)\cdot p$$

Contraction

$\omega$ turns the symmetry direction into $d\mu^X$.

Printed pp. 131-138The Generating Vector Field
Explain how a one-parameter subgroup flows points and differentiates to the fundamental vector field X-sharp. The defining identity d mu-X equals iota X-sharp omega says omega converts this infinitesimal symmetry into the differential of a function. Draw the contraction as feeding the symmetry direction into the form to recover the conserved Hamiltonian.

Equivariance and the Coadjoint Action

Equivariance

$$\mu(g\cdot p)=\mathrm{Ad}^*_g\big(\mu(p)\big)$$

Abelian case

For $\mathbb{R}$, $S^1$, and tori the coadjoint action is trivial.

So it reduces to

Equivariance becomes plain invariance: $\mu$ is constant along orbits.

Nonabelian case

For $SO(3)$ the coadjoint action genuinely rotates covectors.

Printed pp. 131-138Equivariance and Coadjoint Action
Equivariance is the global half of the definition. Moving a point by the group must move its moment value by the coadjoint representation. For tori and abelian groups the coadjoint action is trivial, so equivariance collapses to plain invariance. For nonabelian groups like SO(3) it becomes genuine geometry, rotating angular momentum vectors on spheres.

The Comoment Map

Same data, read backward

A comoment map $\mu^*:\mathfrak{g}\to C^\infty(M)$ sends $X\mapsto\mu^X$.

Bracket compatibility

$$\{\mu^X,\mu^Y\}=\mu^{[X,Y]}$$

Lie algebra homomorphism

The assignment intertwines the Lie bracket on $\mathfrak{g}$ with the Poisson bracket on functions.

Why it matters

This compatibility is what makes the moment map natural rather than mere bookkeeping.

Printed pp. 131-138Comoment Map
Reframe the same data backward as a comoment map sending each algebra element to its Hamiltonian function. The key requirement is that this assignment is a Lie algebra homomorphism: the Poisson bracket of two component functions equals the function attached to their Lie bracket. This bracket compatibility is what makes the construction natural and not just bookkeeping.

Example: Rotation $\to$ Angular Momentum

Moment map on $T^*\mathbb{R}^3$

$$\mu(x,y)=x\times y$$

The check

For an infinitesimal rotation $a$, the component is $\langle a,\,x\times y\rangle$.

Noether

$\mu$ is constant on trajectories: angular momentum is conserved on level sets.

Printed pp. 131-138Angular Momentum
This is the historical reason for the name moment map. On the cotangent bundle of three-space, rotational symmetry produces the moment map x cross y, classical angular momentum. Verify the defining identity against an infinitesimal rotation a, whose Hamiltonian component is the pairing of a with x cross y. Conservation on level sets is Noether's theorem in geometric dress.

Linear Momentum & Cotangent Lifts

Translation

Translational symmetry gives linear momentum, the partner example.

Cotangent-lift moment map

$$\mu(x,p)(X)=p\big(X^\#\big)$$

General principle

Any $G$-action on a base lifts to $T^*$ and is automatically Hamiltonian.

Convention note

Sign choices for cotangent coordinates vary; the lecture uses $-p$ for translation.

Printed pp. 131-138Linear Momentum and Cotangent Lifts
Translation symmetry yields linear momentum, the partner example. More generally any group acting on a base lifts to its cotangent bundle, and that lifted action is automatically Hamiltonian with moment map mu of x and p evaluated on X equal to p paired with the generator. Note that sign conventions for cotangent coordinates vary between sources.

Circle Action on $\mathbb{C}^n$: Reduction Preview

Radial moment map

$$\mu(z)=-\tfrac12|z|^2+\tfrac12$$

Zero level

$\mu^{-1}(0)$ is the unit sphere $S^{2n-1}$.

Reduced space

$$S^{2n-1}/S^1=\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$$

Printed pp. 131-138Torus Moment Maps and Reduction
Specialize to the diagonal circle action on complex n-space with the radial moment map. Choosing the additive constant one half places the zero level exactly on the unit sphere. Quotienting that sphere by the phase action gives complex projective space, the very first model of Marsden-Weinstein reduction and a preview of next lecture.

Orbit Spaces Can Be Bad

Scaling on $\mathbb{R}$

$t\cdot x=e^t x$ has three orbits: positive, negative, fixed point $0$.

Hausdorff fails

Every saturated neighborhood of $\{0\}$ touches both sides.

$\mathbb{C}^*$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$

Well-behaved only after deleting the unstable origin.

Printed pp. 131-138Orbit Spaces Can Be Bad
Warn that quotients are not automatically manifolds. The scaling action on the line has three orbits and fails Hausdorff separation at the fixed point. The C-star action on complex space behaves only after deleting the unstable origin. This is exactly why reduction theorems demand regular values, free actions, and proper actions before quotienting.

Coadjoint Orbits of $SO(3)$

Identification

$\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong\mathfrak{so}(3)^*\cong\mathbb{R}^3$; (co)adjoint actions are rotations.

Kostant-Kirillov form

$$\omega_\xi(a,b)=\xi\cdot(a\times b)$$

Nondegeneracy

Kernel is the radial stabilizer; on the tangent plane $\omega_\xi$ is nondegenerate.

Printed pp. 131-138Coadjoint Orbits
Close with the coadjoint orbits of SO(3): concentric spheres carrying the Kostant-Kirillov form omega-xi of a and b equal to xi dotted with a cross b. Its kernel is the radial stabilizer direction, but on the tangent plane it is nondegenerate. These orbits already carry the symplectic structure that reduction and convexity theorems will exploit next.

Recap and Outlook

Definition

Hamiltonian action $=$ symplectic action $+$ equivariant moment map $\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^*$.

Comoment

The components form a Lie algebra homomorphism into Poisson functions.

Examples

Linear and angular momentum are the model cases, not afterthoughts.

Coadjoint orbits

Already symplectic via the Kostant-Kirillov form.

Next

$$M_{\mathrm{red}}=\mu^{-1}(0)/G$$

Ahead

Printed pp. 131-138Recap and Outlook
Summarize the throughline: a Hamiltonian action is a symplectic action plus a moment map, the comoment map is a Lie algebra homomorphism, classical momenta are the model examples, and coadjoint orbits supply ready-made symplectic spaces. Point ahead to reduction at a level set, convexity of moment images, and the Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg theorem.
