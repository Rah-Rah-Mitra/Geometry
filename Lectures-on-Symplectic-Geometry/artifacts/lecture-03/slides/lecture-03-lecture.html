Lagrangian Submanifolds

Half-dimensional submanifolds where $\omega$ restricts to zero

The definition

An $n$-dimensional $L \subset M^{2n}$ with $\omega|_L = 0$.

The method

Every test is a pullback: restrict a form, check it vanishes.

The creed

"Everything is a Lagrangian submanifold."

Printed pp. 13-19Lagrangian Submanifolds
This lecture covers printed pages 13 through 19. We meet Lagrangian submanifolds, the half-dimensional submanifolds on which the symplectic form restricts to zero. The recurring theme is that every Lagrangian test reduces to pulling back a form and checking that it vanishes, turning geometry into differential identities.

Submanifolds and Pullbacks

Closed embeddings

Immersion injects tangents; embedding fixes the topology; a closed embedding gives our submanifolds.

One uniform test

Pull the form $\alpha$ or $\omega$ back to the candidate and read it on tangent vectors.

Pullback criterion

$\iota: L \hookrightarrow M, \qquad \iota^{*}\omega = 0$

Printed pp. 13-19Submanifolds and Pullbacks
Before testing Lagrangians we fix submanifold language. An immersion injects tangent vectors, an embedding fixes the topology, and a closed embedding gives the submanifolds used here. Every later test is a pullback: restrict the tautological one-form or the symplectic form to the candidate and read whether it dies on tangent vectors.

The Lagrangian Condition

Isotropic

$\omega(u,v)=0$ for all $u,v \in T_pL$; isotropy forces $\dim L \le n$.

Maximal

Lagrangian $=$ isotropic of the largest possible dimension $n$.

Two clauses

$\dim L = \tfrac{1}{2}\dim M, \qquad \omega|_L = 0$

Matrix residual

$B^{\top}\,\Omega\,B = 0$

$B$ stacks a tangent basis of $L$; the residual must be the zero matrix.

Printed pp. 13-19The Lagrangian Condition
A Lagrangian submanifold is isotropic and half-dimensional: the restricted symplectic form vanishes and the dimension is exactly n inside the two-n manifold. Isotropy alone forces dimension at most n, so Lagrangians are the maximal isotropic submanifolds. Numerically the residual matrix B transpose Omega B must be the zero matrix.

The Cotangent Bundle $T^{*}X$

Tautological form

$\alpha$ is defined pointwise on $T^{*}X$, and $\omega = -d\alpha$.

First Lagrangians

The zero section $X$ and each cotangent fiber $T^{*}_xX$.

Why

$\alpha$ restricts to zero on both, so $\omega$ does too.

Printed pp. 13-19Cotangent Bundle and Tautological Form
On the cotangent bundle the tautological one-form alpha is defined point by point, and omega equals minus d alpha. The zero section and the individual cotangent fibers are the first Lagrangians. Both have dimension n, and alpha restricts to zero on each, so the canonical two-form restricts to zero as well.

Graphs of One-Forms

A section

$s_{\mu}(x)=(x,\mu_x)$ has image the graph $X_{\mu}\subset T^{*}X$.

Key pullback

$s_{\mu}^{*}\alpha = \mu$, hence $s_{\mu}^{*}\omega = -d\mu$.

Graph criterion

$X_{\mu}\text{ is Lagrangian} \iff d\mu = 0$

Printed pp. 13-19Graphs of One-Forms
A one-form mu is a section of the cotangent bundle whose image is its graph. The key computation is that pulling the tautological form back along the section returns mu itself. Therefore pulling omega back returns minus d mu, and the graph is Lagrangian exactly when mu is closed. This is the heart of the lecture.

Closed Versus Exact

Closed

$d\mu=0$ gives a Lagrangian graph for every closed one-form.

Exact

On simply connected $X$, $\mu = df$ for a generating function $f$.

Intersections

$X_{\mu}\cap X = \{\,df_x = 0\,\}$ are the critical points of $f$.

Cohomology link

$d\mu=0 \;\longleftrightarrow\; [\mu]\in H^{1}(X), \quad \mu=df \Rightarrow [\mu]=0$

Printed pp. 13-19Closed Versus Exact
The graph criterion links Lagrangians to de Rham cohomology. Closed one-forms give Lagrangian graphs; on a simply connected base every closed form is exact, mu equals df. Then the generating function f produces the Lagrangian, and intersections of the graph with the zero section are exactly the critical points of f.

A Worked Graph Test

Closed example

$\mu = x\,dx + y\,dy = d\!\big(\tfrac{x^2+y^2}{2}\big)$; $d\mu=0$, graph is Lagrangian.

Failure mode

$\mu = -y\,dx + x\,dy$ has $d\mu = 2\,dx\wedge dy \ne 0$.

One coefficient

$s_{\mu}^{*}\omega = -(\partial_xQ-\partial_yP)\,dx\wedge dy$

Printed pp. 13-19A Worked Graph Test
We make the criterion concrete in two dimensions. The gradient one-form of a radial potential is closed, so its graph is Lagrangian; the rotational one-form minus y dx plus x dy has constant nonzero exterior derivative, so its graph fails. The same coefficient that measures d mu measures the pullback of omega, side by side.

The Conormal Bundle

Definition

$N^{*}S = \{\,(x,\xi): x\in S,\ \xi|_{T_xS}=0\,\}\subset T^{*}X$.

Adapted coordinates

Tangent motion has no normal differential; $\xi$ has no tangent part.

Why Lagrangian

$\alpha|_{N^{*}S}=0,\quad \dim N^{*}S = n$

Printed pp. 13-19The Conormal Bundle
Given a submanifold S of the base, the conormal bundle collects covectors that annihilate the tangent space of S. In coordinates adapted to S, tangent motion has no normal differential and conormal covectors have no tangent component. Both facts together make the tautological form vanish, so the conormal bundle is Lagrangian and has dimension n.

The Twisted Product $M_1\times M_2$

Twist by subtraction

Equip $M_1\times M_2$ with $\widetilde{\omega}=\mathrm{pr}_1^{*}\omega_1 - \mathrm{pr}_2^{*}\omega_2$.

Symplectic

$\widetilde{\omega}$ is closed and nondegenerate on the product.

Twisted form

$\widetilde{\omega}=\omega_1\ominus\omega_2=\mathrm{pr}_1^{*}\omega_1-\mathrm{pr}_2^{*}\omega_2$

Printed pp. 13-19The Twisted Product
To compare two symplectic manifolds we form their product and twist the form by subtracting the second factor: pr-one-star omega-one minus pr-two-star omega-two. This twisted form is symplectic on the product. It is the arena in which maps between the two manifolds become submanifolds, namely their graphs sitting inside the product.

Symplectomorphisms as Lagrangians

The graph

$\Gamma_{\varphi}=\{(p,\varphi(p))\}\subset M_1\times M_2$ for a diffeomorphism $\varphi$.

The pullback

Restricting $\widetilde{\omega}$ gives $\omega_1-\varphi^{*}\omega_2$.

Equivalence

$\Gamma_{\varphi}\text{ Lagrangian} \iff \varphi^{*}\omega_2=\omega_1$

Printed pp. 13-19Symplectomorphisms as Lagrangians
The graph of a diffeomorphism phi is Lagrangian for the twisted product form exactly when phi is a symplectomorphism. Pulling the twisted form back over the graph yields omega-one minus phi-star omega-two, whose vanishing is precisely the symplectomorphism condition. This is the symplectic creed: everything is a Lagrangian submanifold.

Recap: The Symplectic Creed

Canonical Lagrangians

  • Zero section and cotangent fibers of $T^{*}X$
  • Graph of a closed one-form $\mu$
  • Conormal bundle $N^{*}S$ of any $S$
  • Graph of a symplectomorphism

One certificate

Each was confirmed by a single vanishing pullback of $\alpha$ or $\widetilde{\omega}$.

The creed

$\text{Everything is a Lagrangian submanifold.}$

Printed pp. 13-19Recap and the Creed
We collected the canonical Lagrangians: the zero section, the fibers, graphs of closed one-forms, conormal bundles, and graphs of symplectomorphisms. Each was certified by a single vanishing pullback. Carry forward the slogan that turning maps into Lagrangian graphs lets submanifold techniques solve problems about symplectomorphisms.
