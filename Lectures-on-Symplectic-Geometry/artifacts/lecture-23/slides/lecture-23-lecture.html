The Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer Theorem

Symplectic reduction: cut to a level set, then quotient by the symmetry

Step one

Restrict $\omega$ to the zero level $\mu^{-1}(0)$. The restricted form acquires null directions.

Step two

Quotient by the $G$-action. The orbits are exactly those null directions, so collapsing them yields a symplectic space.

The Setup: A Hamiltonian $G$-Space

The data

$(M,\omega)$ symplectic, $G$ a compact Lie group acting symplectically, with moment map $\mu\colon M \to \mathfrak{g}^*$.

Equivariance

$\mu$ intertwines the $G$-action on $M$ with the coadjoint action on $\mathfrak{g}^*$.

Why compact

Compactness (or properness) makes the orbit space Hausdorff and the quotient well behaved.

Moment map condition

$d\mu^{\xi} = \iota_{\xi^{\#}}\,\omega, \quad \xi \in \mathfrak{g}$

Reading the identity

The function $\mu^\xi = \langle \mu,\xi\rangle$ is a Hamiltonian for the generating vector field $\xi^{\#}$. The moment map packages every symmetry direction at once.

Recall the data: a symplectic manifold, a compact Lie group acting in a Hamiltonian way, and a moment map into the dual Lie algebra. Emphasize compactness or properness guarantees the quotient is Hausdorff. Everything downstream rests on two hypotheses about the zero level that we isolate next.

The Theorem

Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer

If $0$ is a regular value of $\mu$ and $G$ acts freely on $\mu^{-1}(0)$, then the reduced space

$M_{\mathrm{red}} = \mu^{-1}(0)/G$

is a symplectic manifold with a unique form $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$.

Two hypotheses

(1) $0$ is a regular value; (2) $G$ acts freely on the level set.

Dimension count

$\dim M_{\mathrm{red}} = \dim M - 2\dim G$

Defining property

$\iota^{*}\omega = \pi^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$, where $\iota$ is the inclusion of the level set and $\pi$ is the quotient projection.

Printed pp. 139-144Statement of the Theorem
State Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer precisely. Two hypotheses: zero is a regular value and the action on the level set is free. Conclusion: the reduced space is a smooth symplectic manifold whose dimension drops by twice the group dimension. Point at the dimension formula and promise to explain every clause.

Why $0$ Is a Regular Value

Image of the differential

At $p \in \mu^{-1}(0)$, the image of $d\mu_p$ is the annihilator of the stabilizer subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_p$.

Surjectivity

$\operatorname{im} d\mu_p = \mathfrak{g}_p^{\circ}$

Freeness pays off

A free action means $\mathfrak{g}_p = 0$, so the annihilator is all of $\mathfrak{g}^*$ and $d\mu_p$ is onto.

Printed pp. 139-144Why Zero Is Regular
Explain the moment-map differential. The image of d-mu at a point is the annihilator of the stabilizer algebra. Freeness kills the stabilizer, so the differential is surjective and zero is regular. This makes the level set a submanifold of codimension equal to the group dimension.

The Level Set Is Coisotropic

Key identity

$\ker d\mu_p = \big(T_p\,\mathcal{O}_p\big)^{\omega}$

Reading it

The tangent space to the level set is the symplectic orthogonal of the orbit tangent space $T_p\mathcal{O}_p$.

Orbit is isotropic

Since $\mathcal{O}_p \subseteq \mu^{-1}(0)$, the orbit tangent sits inside its own orthogonal: $T_p\mathcal{O}_p \subseteq (T_p\mathcal{O}_p)^\omega$.

Printed pp. 139-144The Coisotropic Level Set
Derive the key identity: the kernel of d-mu equals the symplectic orthogonal of the orbit tangent space. Because the orbit is isotropic, its orthogonal contains it, so the level set is coisotropic. The orbit directions sit inside the level set as the null directions of the restricted form.

The Linear Algebra Lemma

Lemma

Let $(V,\Omega)$ be symplectic and $I \subseteq V$ isotropic. Then $\Omega$ descends to a symplectic form on

$I^{\Omega}/I$.

Tangent model

Apply with $V = T_pM$, $\Omega = \omega_p$, $I = T_p\mathcal{O}_p$. Then $I^\omega = \ker d\mu_p$ and

$T_{[p]}M_{\mathrm{red}} \cong \ker d\mu_p / T_p\mathcal{O}_p.$

Well defined

If $u' = u + a$, $v' = v + b$ with $a,b \in I$, then $\Omega(u',v') = \Omega(u,v)$ because $I$ is null inside $I^\Omega$.

Nondegenerate

If $[u]$ pairs to zero with every $[v]$, then $u \in (I^\Omega)^\Omega = I$, so $[u]=0$. This is exactly where nondegeneracy of $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ is born.

Printed pp. 139-144The Linear Lemma
Do the pure linear algebra first. Given an isotropic subspace of a symplectic vector space, the form descends to the quotient of its orthogonal by itself, and is nondegenerate there. This is the tangent-space model for the reduced space, and it is where nondegeneracy is actually proven.

Collapsing the Null Foliation

Null foliation

The null directions of $\omega|_{\mu^{-1}(0)}$ integrate to a foliation whose leaves are the $G$-orbits.

Slice theorem

A free, proper action admits slices: locally $\mu^{-1}(0) \cong G \times S$. Dividing by $G$ leaves the slice $S$ as a chart.

Principal bundle

$\pi\colon \mu^{-1}(0) \to M_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a principal $G$-bundle, not just a set-theoretic quotient.

Printed pp. 139-144Quotient by the Null Foliation
Geometrize the lemma. The null directions of the restricted form foliate the level set, and the leaves are exactly the group orbits. Collapsing each orbit to a point gives the reduced space. The slice theorem supplies charts and makes the projection a principal bundle with fiber G.

Defining $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$

Defining identity

$\iota^{*}\omega = \pi^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$

Existence

The restricted form $\iota^*\omega$ is basic: it is $G$-invariant and annihilates orbit directions, so it descends through $\pi$.

Uniqueness

$\pi$ is a surjective submersion, so $\pi^*$ is injective on forms; at most one $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ can satisfy the identity.

Nondegeneracy

Pointwise, $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ is the form from the linear lemma on $\ker d\mu_p / T_p\mathcal{O}_p$, which is nondegenerate.

Pullback chain

$T\mu^{-1}(0) \xrightarrow{\ d\pi\ } T M_{\mathrm{red}}$

$d\pi$ is onto with kernel the orbit directions, matching the linear quotient exactly.

Printed pp. 139-144The Reduced Symplectic Form
Define omega-red by the pullback identity: i-star omega equals pi-star omega-red. Uniqueness follows because pi is a surjective submersion, so its pullback is injective on forms. Nondegeneracy is the linear lemma applied pointwise. This is the heart of the construction.

Why $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ Is Closed

Naturality chase

$\pi^{*}(d\omega_{\mathrm{red}}) = d(\pi^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}})$

$= d(\iota^{*}\omega) = \iota^{*}(d\omega) = 0$

Conclude

$\pi^*$ is injective, so $\pi^*(d\omega_{\mathrm{red}})=0$ forces $d\omega_{\mathrm{red}}=0$.

Two facts used

Exterior $d$ commutes with pullback, and $\omega$ is already closed on $M$, so $\iota^*(d\omega) = 0$.

The verdict

Closed plus nondegenerate means $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a genuine symplectic form. The reduced space is symplectic.

Dimension check

$\dim M - 2\dim G$

codim $\dim G$ from the level set, another $\dim G$ from the quotient.

Printed pp. 139-144Closedness of omega-red
Walk through the naturality chase. Pi-star of d-omega-red equals d of pi-star omega-red equals d of i-star omega equals i-star of d-omega, which is zero. Since pi-star is injective, d-omega-red vanishes. Closedness plus nondegeneracy means omega-red is genuinely symplectic.

Level set

$\mu^{-1}(0) = S^3$, the unit sphere. The diagonal circle acts freely on it.

Reduced space

$S^3/S^1 = \mathbb{C}P^1 \cong S^2$, with $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ a multiple of the Fubini-Study form.

Descending Symmetries & Recap

Commuting actions

If a second Hamiltonian $H$-action commutes with $G$ and its moment map is $G$-invariant, then both descend to $M_{\mathrm{red}}$.

Why it descends

The data is constant along $G$-orbits, hence factors through $\pi$.

Next time

Reduction at nonzero levels, staged reduction, and the singular case when freeness fails.

The pipeline
  • Cut to $\mu^{-1}(0)$: codimension $\dim G$, coisotropic.
  • Quotient by $G$: kill the null orbit directions.
  • Inherit $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ via $\iota^*\omega = \pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$.
  • Result: symplectic, $\dim M - 2\dim G$.

One sentence

Reduction trades a symmetry for two lost dimensions, repairing a degenerate restricted form into a symplectic quotient.
