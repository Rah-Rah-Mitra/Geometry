The Legendre Transform

From the Lagrangian picture on $TM$ to the Hamiltonian picture on $T^*M$

Velocities live on $TM$

The Lagrangian $L(q,v)$ is a function on the tangent bundle.

Momenta live on $T^*M$

Symplectic mechanics wants the cotangent bundle and its canonical form.

The bridge

Fiberwise convex duality carries $v \mapsto p$ and $L \mapsto H$.

Printed pp. 119-124The Legendre Transform
Open by framing the lecture as a bridge: mechanics on the tangent bundle uses velocities, but symplectic geometry lives on the cotangent bundle and uses momenta. The Legendre transform is the convex-analytic device that carries us across, and we will build it carefully from strict convexity upward.

Strict Convexity Is the Local Engine

Positive-definite Hessian

$\partial^2 L/\partial v^2 \succ 0$ on every fiber makes $L$ fiberwise strictly convex.

Supporting hyperplane

$L(w) > L(v) + \tfrac{\partial L}{\partial v}(v)\,(w-v)$

Local, not global

Convexity certifies invertibility near a point; it does not yet give a change of coordinates.

Printed pp. 119-124Strict Convexity
Stress that strict convexity is the local engine. A positive-definite Hessian makes every tangent hyperplane a strict support of the graph and makes the gradient map locally injective. Warn that this is purely local information; it certifies invertibility near a point, not yet a global change of coordinates.

The Stability Set $S_L$

Stable slopes

A momentum $p$ is stable when the tilted function $L(v) - p\cdot v$ keeps a unique proper minimum.

Image of the gradient

$S_L = \big\{\, p : p = \tfrac{\partial L}{\partial v}(v)\ \text{some } v \,\big\}$

Convex but unstable

$e^{v} + a v$ is strictly convex yet only reaches slopes in $(a,\infty)$.

Printed pp. 119-124Stability Set
Introduce the stability set as the global complement to the local picture. A slope is stable only when the tilted function keeps a genuine proper minimum, and the set of such slopes is exactly the image of the gradient map. Use the exponential example to show convexity without full stability.

The Legendre Map (Fiber Derivative)

Definition

$\mathcal{L}: TM \to T^*M, \quad v \mapsto \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial v}$

Diffeomorphism

If $L$ is fiberwise strictly convex, $\mathcal{L}$ maps each fiber diffeomorphically onto $S_L$.

Inverse

Recover $v$ from $p$ by solving $p = \partial L/\partial v$ uniquely.

Printed pp. 119-124The Legendre Map
Define the fiber derivative as velocity to momentum, sending a vector to the differential of the Lagrangian in that vector. Under fiberwise strict convexity this map is a diffeomorphism from the tangent space onto the stability set, with the inverse recovering velocity from momentum by solving the gradient equation.

The Convex Conjugate

Legendre dual

$H(q,p) = \sup_{v}\big(\, p\cdot v - L(q,v)\,\big)$

Where the sup is hit

The supremum is attained at the velocity with $\partial L/\partial v = p$.

Always convex

The conjugate $H$ is convex in $p$ even when $L$ is only barely convex.

Printed pp. 119-124The Dual Function
Present the convex conjugate as a supremum of linear minus convex. Explain that the supremum is attained where the slope of the Lagrangian equals the chosen momentum, so the dual value is read off the supporting line. The conjugate is always convex, even when the primal barely is.

Young–Fenchel Inequality

Inequality

$L(q,v) + H(q,p) \;\ge\; p\cdot v$

The gap

The Young gap $L + H - p\cdot v \ge 0$ measures failure of duality.

Equality case

Equality holds exactly when $p = \partial L/\partial v$, i.e. on the graph of $\mathcal{L}$.

Printed pp. 119-124Young-Fenchel Inequality
State the Young inequality and emphasize the equality case, which is the whole point. The gap between the sum of dual values and the pairing is nonnegative and vanishes exactly along the graph of the Legendre map. This equality curve is the certificate that the transform is exact.

Lifting to the Bundles

Fiber by fiber

Apply $\mathcal{L}$ on every tangent space at once; it covers the identity on $M$.

Global map

$\mathcal{L}: TM \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} T^*M, \quad (q,v)\mapsto(q,p)$

Coordinate swap

Momenta replace velocities everywhere when $L$ is fiberwise convex.

Printed pp. 119-124From TM to T*M
Lift the construction to bundles. Applying the fiber derivative on each tangent space simultaneously gives a global map from the tangent bundle to the cotangent bundle covering the identity on the base. Fiberwise convexity guarantees it is a diffeomorphism, so momentum coordinates replace velocity coordinates everywhere.

Building the Hamiltonian

Fiberwise conjugate

$H(q,p) = p\cdot v(q,p) - L\big(q,v(q,p)\big)$

Mechanical model

$L = \tfrac12 m(q)v^2 - U(q)$ gives momentum $p = m(q)\,v$.

Energy form

$H = \dfrac{p^2}{2m(q)} + U(q)$: kinetic in $p$ plus potential.

Printed pp. 119-124The Hamiltonian
Build the Hamiltonian as the conjugate of the Lagrangian taken fiberwise, the supremum of momentum paired with velocity minus the Lagrangian. Walk through the worked mechanical model where momentum is mass times velocity and the Hamiltonian becomes kinetic energy in momentum form plus potential energy.

Euler–Lagrange $\Leftrightarrow$ Hamilton

Two identities

$\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p} = v, \qquad \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q} = -\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q}$

Second order to first order

The E–L equation $\frac{d}{dt}\partial_v L = \partial_q L$ splits into a first-order system.

Hamilton's equations

$\dot q = \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p}, \qquad \dot p = -\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q}$

Printed pp. 119-124Euler-Lagrange to Hamilton
Show the payoff: under the Legendre transform the second-order Euler-Lagrange equations become Hamilton's first-order system. The two chain-rule identities, partial H by partial momentum equals velocity and partial H by partial position equals minus the Lagrangian derivative, do the entire conversion exactly.

$L$ and $H$ Are Legendre Duals

Envelope of tangents

Each function is the upper/lower envelope of supporting lines encoding the other.

Involution

$H = L^{*}, \qquad (L^{*})^{*} = L$

Slope $\leftrightarrow$ value

Reflecting slopes against intercepts is the geometric heart of the transform.

Printed pp. 119-124Convex Duality Picture
Tie the threads together with the supporting-line picture. The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian are Legendre duals: each is the envelope of tangent lines whose slopes and intercepts encode the other. Reflecting slopes and values across this construction is the geometric content of the whole transform.

Load-bearing hypothesis

Fiberwise convexity is essential: lose it and $\mathcal{L}$ stops being invertible.
