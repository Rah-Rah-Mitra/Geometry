The Liouville Volume

Top power

On a $2n$-manifold $\omega^n$ never vanishes.

Orientation

It fixes a canonical orientation and volume.

Local model

In Darboux coordinates it is the flat volume.

Liouville form

$$\mathrm{vol}_\omega=\frac{\omega^n}{n!}=dq_1\wedge dp_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dq_n\wedge dp_n$$

Why $n!$

It cancels the combinatorial overcount in $\omega^n$.

Printed pp. 27-32The Liouville Volume
Start by building the canonical volume from the symplectic form. The top power omega to the n, divided by n factorial, is a nowhere-vanishing form that orients the manifold. Because a symplectomorphism pulls back omega to itself, it pulls back every power, so it preserves this volume exactly.

Symplectomorphisms Preserve Volume

Pullback chain

$$f^*\omega=\omega\;\Longrightarrow\;f^*(\omega^n)=(f^*\omega)^n=\omega^n$$

Consequence

$f^*\,\mathrm{vol}_\omega=\mathrm{vol}_\omega$: volume is preserved.

Printed pp. 27-32Symplectomorphisms Preserve Volume
Spell out the pullback computation so students see there is no slack. Preserving omega forces preservation of the wedge of n copies, hence of the Liouville form. This is the rigorous sense in which symplectic maps are volume-preserving, and it is the hinge for everything that follows in the lecture.

Hamiltonian Flows Are Volume-Preserving

Each $\phi_t$ is symplectic

The time-$t$ flow satisfies $\phi_t^*\omega=\omega$.

Liouville's theorem

$$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\,\mathrm{vol}_\omega=0,\qquad \operatorname{div}X_H=0$$

Phase space

Energy shells inherit an invariant measure too.

Contrast

Dissipative flows contract volume onto attractors.

Printed pp. 27-32Hamiltonian Flows Are Volume-Preserving
Apply the previous slide to time-t Hamiltonian flows, which are symplectomorphisms for every t. Their generator has zero divergence with respect to the Liouville volume, so phase-space volume is an exact invariant of the motion. Contrast this with dissipative flows that contract volume onto attractors.

Poincare Recurrence Theorem

Theorem

Let $f$ preserve a finite measure $\mu$ on $X$. For a.e. $x\in U$ there are infinitely many $k$ with $f^k(x)\in U$.

Reading

Almost every point returns, infinitely often.

Hypotheses

Finite total volume; a volume-preserving map.

What it does not say

No bound on the return time is promised.

Exceptions

The non-returning set has measure zero.

Printed pp. 27-32Poincare Recurrence Theorem
State the theorem carefully: a volume-preserving map of a finite-volume space returns almost every point arbitrarily close to its start, and does so infinitely often. Emphasize the measure-zero exceptional set and that no rate of return is promised, only its eventual certainty for almost every initial condition.

The Pigeonhole Proof

Step 1

Take $U$ with $\mu(U)>0$; iterate to get $U,f(U),f^2(U),\dots$

Step 2

Each has the same measure $\mu(U)$.

Step 3

If all disjoint, $\sum\mu(f^k U)=\infty>\mu(X)$ — impossible.

Overlap

$$f^j(U)\cap f^k(U)\neq\varnothing\;\Rightarrow\;U\cap f^{k-j}(U)\neq\varnothing$$

Conclusion

Some iterate of $U$ meets $U$: a point returns.

Printed pp. 27-32The Pigeonhole Proof
Walk through the volume pigeonhole. Take a positive-volume neighborhood and iterate it. All images share the same volume, so inside a finite total volume they cannot stay pairwise disjoint forever. Two images must overlap, and pulling back gives a point of the set returning to the set.

Visual: The Area Ledger Forces Overlap

Counting

$$N\cdot\mu(U)\le\mu(X)\;\Rightarrow\;N\le\frac{\mu(X)}{\mu(U)}$$

Read-off

Only finitely many iterates can be pairwise disjoint.

Printed pp. 27-32Visual: Forced Overlap
Read the diagram as a running area ledger. Each iterate of the neighborhood carries identical area, so the cumulative demand grows linearly without bound. Against a fixed finite ceiling that is impossible, forcing some pair of iterates to intersect. The intersection is the geometric heart of recurrence.

Consequences for Hamiltonian Dynamics

No universal attractor

An attractor would shrink volume — forbidden.

Eternal return

Almost every orbit revisits its start infinitely often.

Irreversibility puzzle

Recurrence times can be astronomically long.

Takeaway

Conservative dynamics wander, but never settle.

Printed pp. 27-32Consequences for Dynamics
Draw the dynamical morals. Conservative Hamiltonian systems cannot possess universal attractors, since an attractor would shrink volume. Almost every orbit threads back near its origin endlessly. This also explains the apparent tension with thermodynamic irreversibility, resolved by astronomically long recurrence times.

From Return to Exact Periodicity

Definition

A point is $N$-periodic if $f^N(x)=x$, i.e. a fixed point of the iterate $f^N$.

Sharper than return

Recurrence comes near; periodicity comes back exactly.

Surface maps

Area preservation makes periodic points abundant.

Generating link

Fixed points match critical points of $\psi(x)=f(x,x)$.

Printed pp. 27-32Periodic Points and Fixed Points
Shift from approximate return to exact return. Periodic points are fixed points of an iterate, and area-preserving surface maps are unexpectedly rich in them. This is where recurrence sharpens into the existence of genuine closed orbits, a theme that dominates the rest of the symplectomorphism story.

Poincare-Birkhoff Twist Theorem

Theorem

An area-preserving map of the annulus that twists the two boundary circles oppositely has $\ge 2$ fixed points.

What does the work

Area preservation, not smoothness, forces the count.

Printed pp. 27-32Poincare-Birkhoff Theorem
Present the twist theorem flavor. An area-preserving homeomorphism of the annulus that rotates the two boundary circles in opposite senses must fix at least two interior points. Area preservation, not smoothness, supplies the count, which is why it foreshadows the rigidity phenomena of later lectures.
