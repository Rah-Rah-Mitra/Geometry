Contact Forms

The odd-dimensional companion of symplectic geometry

Setting

A manifold $M^{2n+1}$ of odd dimension.

Object

A hyperplane field $\xi = \ker\alpha$ that is maximally non-integrable.

Slogan

Symplectic geometry shifted up by one dimension.

Printed pp. 55-60Contact Forms
This lecture opens the odd-dimensional half of the course. Where symplectic geometry lives on even-dimensional manifolds, contact geometry lives on dimension two n plus one and replaces the symplectic form with a maximally non-integrable hyperplane field. Stress that the whole subject is governed by a single nonvanishing condition we will meet on the next slide.

The Contact Condition

Definition

$\alpha \wedge (d\alpha)^n \neq 0$ everywhere

Equivalent test

$d\alpha$ restricts to a symplectic form on each hyperplane $\xi_p = \ker\alpha_p$.

It is a volume form

On $M^{2n+1}$ the top form $\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n$ orients $M$ when $\alpha$ is contact.

Why it is special

Any nonvanishing $\alpha$ defines a plane field; only contact forms force it to twist.

Printed pp. 55-60The Contact Condition
Define a contact form as a one-form whose top wedge product is a volume form. Emphasise that alpha wedge d-alpha to the n must be nowhere zero, an open and pointwise condition. Contrast this with merely defining a plane field: any nonvanishing one-form does that, but only contact forms force the planes to twist.

Maximal Non-Integrability

Frobenius

$\xi$ integrable $\iff$ it has integral surfaces $\iff$ $\alpha\wedge d\alpha = 0$.

Contact is the opposite

The condition is the strongest possible failure of Frobenius in odd dimension.

R^3 bracket test

$[\,\partial_x,\ \partial_y - x\,\partial_z\,] = -\partial_z$

Printed pp. 55-60Maximal Non-Integrability
Connect the volume condition to Frobenius. An integrable plane field admits integral surfaces, but the contact condition is exactly the strongest failure of integrability in odd dimensions. Walk through the R3 model, computing the bracket of the two spanning fields and showing alpha evaluated on it is nonzero, so the bracket escapes the distribution.

The Standard Form on $\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}$

Standard contact form

$\alpha = dz + \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i\, dy_i$

Volume check

$\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n = dz\wedge dx_1\wedge dy_1\wedge\cdots$, the Euclidean volume.

Darboux for contact

Every contact structure looks locally like this one in suitable coordinates.

n = 1 case

On $\mathbb{R}^3$ this is $dz + x\,dy$, the model we draw and compute with.

Printed pp. 55-60The Standard Form on R^{2n+1}
Present the Darboux normal form. Every contact structure looks locally like the standard one, so this single model carries all local information. Compute that alpha wedge d-alpha to the n equals the Euclidean volume form for the standard coordinates, confirming the model is genuinely contact and giving students a concrete formula to anchor intuition.

Twisting Planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$

Spanning fields

$\xi = \mathrm{span}\{\,\partial_x,\ \partial_y - x\,\partial_z\,\}$

The twist

As $x$ grows the second vector tips downward, rotating the plane.

Comparison

$\ker(dz)$ stays horizontal and integrates to the planes $z=\text{const}$.

Printed pp. 55-60Twisting Planes in R^3
Spend time on the picture. As x increases the second spanning vector tips downward, rotating the contact plane about the x-axis. No surface can stay tangent to this rotating family, which is the visual meaning of non-integrability. Point to the integrable comparison ker dz where the planes stay flat and surfaces exist.

The Reeb Vector Field

Defining equations

$\iota_R\, d\alpha = 0, \qquad \alpha(R) = 1$

Uniqueness

These two conditions pin $R$ down exactly, given the contact form $\alpha$.

Transverse

$R$ points out of the contact planes and generates the Reeb flow.

Printed pp. 55-60The Reeb Vector Field
Introduce the Reeb field as the canonical direction transverse to the contact planes. Its two defining equations pin it uniquely: it lies in the kernel of d-alpha and is normalised by alpha of R equals one. Note that the Reeb field depends on the chosen form, not only on the contact structure, and its flow generates contact dynamics.

The Structure is the Kernel

Conformal rescaling

$\ker(f\alpha) = \ker(\alpha)$ for $f \neq 0$

Volume scales

$(f\alpha)\wedge(d(f\alpha))^n = f^{\,n+1}\,\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n \neq 0$.

Consequence

A contact structure $\xi$ remembers the hyperplanes, not the chosen $\alpha$.

Caveat

The Reeb field $R$ is not intrinsic: it changes when $\alpha$ is rescaled.

Printed pp. 55-60Conformal Ambiguity
Explain that the contact structure is the kernel, not the particular form. Replacing alpha by f alpha for a nowhere-zero f leaves the planes unchanged and rescales the contact volume by f to the n plus one, which stays nonzero. This conformal freedom is why Gray's theorem carries an extra scalar factor and why the Reeb field is not intrinsic.

Examples

$\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}$

The standard $\alpha = dz + \sum x_i\,dy_i$.

Cotangent sphere bundle

$S(T^*X)$ carries the tautological form restricted to the unit level set.

One-jet space $J^1(X)$

$\alpha = dz - \sum p_i\,dq_i$ governs jets of functions on $X$.

Origin

$\alpha = \theta_{\text{taut}}\big|_{\text{unit level}}$

Printed pp. 55-60Examples
Survey the standard examples. The unit cotangent bundle and unit sphere bundle carry the contact form from the tautological one-form restricted to the unit level. The one-jet space of functions on X is contact with alpha equal to dz minus the canonical sum. These examples recur throughout the rest of the course.

Contact Elements

Definition

A point of $X$ together with a tangent hyperplane there.

As covectors

A hyperplane is $\ker\alpha_p$ for a nonzero covector, defined up to scale.

Projective chart

$\alpha = dx_1 + \xi\, dx_2$ on $\mathbb{P}(T^*X)$

Printed pp. 55-60Contact Elements
Describe a contact element as a point together with a tangent hyperplane, equivalently a covector kernel up to scale. The projectivised cotangent bundle is the space of all contact elements and carries a canonical contact structure with local form dx1 plus xi dx2. This makes the homework's contact-element language precise without quoting it.

The Symplectization

The cone

$(M\times\mathbb{R},\ \omega = d(e^{t}\alpha))$

It is symplectic

$\omega^{n+1} = (n+1)\,e^{(n+1)t}\,dt\wedge\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n \neq 0$ iff $\alpha$ is contact.

Printed pp. 55-60The Symplectization
Build the symplectization as the cone M times R with the two-form d of e-to-the-t alpha. Verify it is symplectic exactly when alpha is contact: the top power reproduces the contact volume times an exponential factor. This functor turns contact questions into symplectic ones and underlies much of the deeper theory.

Gray Stability

Theorem (Gray)

$\{\xi_t\}_{t\in[0,1]}$ on closed $M \Rightarrow \exists\ \rho_t:\ \rho_t{}_*\xi_0 = \xi_t$

Contact Moser

Solve $\iota_v\, d\alpha_t|_{\xi_t} = -\partial_t\alpha_t|_{\xi_t}$ for $v$ inside the planes.

Why solvable

$d\alpha_t$ is nondegenerate on $\xi_t$, so $v_t$ is unique; integrate to $\rho_t$.

The scalar

$\rho_t^*\alpha_t = u_t\,\alpha_0$: only the kernel must match, hence a conformal factor.

Printed pp. 55-60Gray Stability
State Gray's theorem as the contact analogue of Moser stability. A smooth family of contact structures on a closed manifold is carried by an ambient isotopy. The proof solves a Moser equation inside the moving hyperplane field, using nondegeneracy of d-alpha there, and records a conformal factor because only the kernel must be preserved.

Recap

Core idea

$\xi=\ker\alpha$ with $\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n\neq 0$: a maximally non-integrable hyperplane field.

Machinery

Standard form, Reeb field $R$, conformal freedom, Gray stability.

Examples

$\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}$, $S(T^*X)$, $J^1(X)$, and contact elements on $\mathbb{P}(T^*X)$.

Bridge

The symplectization $d(e^t\alpha)$ turns contact problems into symplectic ones.

Printed pp. 55-60Recap
Recapitulate the chain of ideas: hyperplane field, the volume condition, maximal non-integrability, the standard model, the Reeb field, examples, and the symplectization. Stress that contact geometry is symplectic geometry shifted by one dimension, and preview that the next lectures exploit the symplectization and Reeb dynamics in earnest.
