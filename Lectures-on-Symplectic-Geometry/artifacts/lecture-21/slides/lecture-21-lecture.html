One-Parameter Groups and Flows

Flow of a complete field

An integral curve through $q$ defines $\rho_t(q)$ for all real $t$.

Why a group law

Composing flows and flowing directly solve the same ODE — uniqueness forces equality.

Flow law

$$\rho_t \circ \rho_s = \rho_{t+s}, \qquad \rho_0 = \mathrm{id}$$

An $\mathbb{R}$-action

The flow is exactly a smooth action of $\mathbb{R}$ on $M$.

Printed pp. 125-130One-Parameter Groups and Flows
Explain that a complete vector field integrates to a flow indexed by real time. The group law is not a new axiom; it follows because composing flows and flowing directly solve the same initial value problem, so uniqueness of integral curves forces them to agree. This is the seed of every action.

Lie Groups and Smooth Actions

Lie group

A manifold $G$ with smooth multiplication and inversion.

Smooth action

A homomorphism $\psi:G\to\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ with smooth evaluation $G\times M\to M$.

Action axioms

$$g\cdot(h\cdot p)=(gh)\cdot p,\qquad e\cdot p = p$$

Working models

$S^1=SO(2)$ rotations, $U(n)$ unitaries, $GL(V)$ invertibles.

Printed pp. 125-130Lie Groups and Smooth Actions
Define a Lie group as a manifold whose multiplication and inversion are smooth, and a smooth action as a homomorphism into diffeomorphisms with smooth evaluation. Emphasize the associativity and identity axioms, and that small matrix groups like rotations and unitaries are our reliable working models throughout the lecture.

Free, Proper, Effective Actions

Effective

Only the identity acts as $\mathrm{id}_M$: $\bigcap_p G_p=\{e\}$.

Free

No nontrivial element fixes any point: every $G_p=\{e\}$.

Proper

The map $(g,p)\mapsto(g\cdot p,p)$ is proper — no escape to infinity.

Why it matters

free $+$ proper $\Rightarrow$ $M/G$ is a smooth manifold

Printed pp. 125-130Free, Proper, Effective
Carefully separate the three adjectives. Effective means only the identity acts trivially everywhere; free means no nontrivial element fixes any point; proper controls escape to infinity so quotients stay Hausdorff manifolds. Stress that free plus proper is the standard package guaranteeing the orbit space is a smooth manifold.

Symplectic Actions

Definition

Each $g$ acts by a symplectomorphism: $\psi_g^{*}\omega=\omega$.

Linear test on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$

$$A^{\top} J A = J$$

Printed pp. 125-130Symplectic Actions
Define a symplectic action: every group element pulls the form back to itself, acting through symplectomorphisms. For a linear action on standard space this is exactly the matrix identity A transpose J A equals J. Demonstrate with a rotation; the residual vanishes, so rotations of the plane are genuinely symplectic.

The Infinitesimal Action

From $\mathfrak{g}$ to vector fields

Each $X\in\mathfrak{g}$ generates a one-parameter subgroup $\exp(tX)$.

Anti-homomorphism

$X\mapsto X^{\#}$ reverses the bracket: $[X,Y]^{\#}=-[X^{\#},Y^{\#}]$.

Generating vector field

$$X^{\#}_p=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\,\exp(tX)\cdot p$$

Tangent to orbits

$X^{\#}$ is the velocity of the subgroup's flow on $M$.

Printed pp. 125-130The Infinitesimal Action
Differentiate a one-parameter subgroup to get the generating vector field X-sharp at each point. This is the bridge from the Lie algebra to vector fields on the manifold: each algebra element produces a flow whose velocity is X-sharp, tangent to the orbit. The map is a Lie algebra anti-homomorphism into vector fields.

Cotangent Lift: Always Symplectic

Lift to phase space

An action on base $X$ lifts to $T^{*}X$ by pushing covectors along.

Tautological form fixed

The lift preserves $\alpha$, hence the canonical $\omega=-d\alpha$.

Automatic symplectic action

$$\hat{\psi}_g^{*}\,\alpha=\alpha \;\Rightarrow\; \hat{\psi}_g^{*}\,\omega=\omega$$

Workhorse example

Any $G$-action on configuration space is symplectic, indeed Hamiltonian, on phase space.

Printed pp. 125-130Cotangent Lift: Always Symplectic
Show that any action on a base manifold lifts to its cotangent bundle and that lift automatically preserves the tautological one-form, hence the canonical symplectic form. This is the workhorse example: every smooth group action on configuration space gives a symplectic, indeed Hamiltonian, action on phase space.

Rotation Action on $\mathbb{R}^2=\mathbb{C}$

Hamiltonian generator

$$X^{\#}=(-y,\,x),\quad \iota_{X^{\#}}\omega=d\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\right)$$

Torus actions

$\mathbb{T}^n$ acting coordinate-wise is a product of such circle rotations.

Printed pp. 125-130Rotation Action on R^2 = C
Work the rotation of the plane in detail. The generator X-sharp is the rotational field minus y, x, and contracting the area form with it gives the differential of minus one half r squared. So the circle action is not just symplectic but Hamiltonian, with that radial function as Hamiltonian. Preview torus actions as products of such circles.
