Hamiltonian Vector Fields

Turning a function $H:M\to\mathbb{R}$ into a flow that preserves the symplectic form

One equation

Nondegeneracy of $\omega$ gives a unique $X_H$ from $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$.

One chain of invariants

Closedness of $\omega$ makes the flow preserve $\omega$ and $H$ — energy is conserved.

One algebra

The Poisson bracket $\{f,g\}$ makes $C^\infty(M)$ a Poisson algebra and detects first integrals.

Printed pp. 103-110From Functions to Dynamics
Open by framing the lecture as the moment geometry becomes mechanics. A single function on a symplectic manifold becomes a flow, and every structural fact we prove is an invariant of that flow. Tell students to read the whole chapter as a chain of conserved quantities rather than isolated definitions.

Defining the Hamiltonian Vector Field

The defining identity

Given $H\in C^\infty(M)$, contract $\omega$ to recover $dH$.

Why it is unique

Nondegeneracy makes $X\mapsto \iota_X\omega$ a bundle isomorphism $TM\to T^*M$.

Musical view

$X_H = \omega^{\sharp}(dH)$ — invert the form, apply to $dH$.

Definition

$$\iota_{X_H}\,\omega = dH$$

Isomorphism

$$\tilde\omega: TM \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } T^*M,\quad X\mapsto \iota_X\omega$$

Printed pp. 103-110Defining the Hamiltonian Vector Field
Stress that nondegeneracy of omega is what makes the defining equation solvable and the solution unique. The musical isomorphism turns the one-form dH into a vector field. Without nondegeneracy there would be no inverse, so the entire construction rests on the symplectic condition we assumed at the start.

Hamilton's Equations as the Flow

Darboux coordinates

With $\omega=\sum dq^i\wedge dp_i$ the defining identity becomes the classical equations.

The minus sign

The antisymmetric matrix $J$ supplies the asymmetry between $q$ and $p$.

Recovers Newton

For $H=\tfrac{|p|^2}{2m}+V(q)$ this is $m\ddot q = -\nabla V$.

Equations of motion

$$\dot q^i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad \dot p_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}$$

In coordinates

$$X_H = \sum_i \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\partial_{q^i} - \frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}\partial_{p_i}$$

Printed pp. 103-110Hamilton's Equations
Work the Darboux computation slowly so students see canonical coordinates turn the abstract identity into the textbook equations of motion. Emphasize the antisymmetric symplectic matrix J supplying the crucial minus sign. This is where the geometry reproduces the dynamics they already know from a first mechanics course.

The Symplectic Gradient

Tangency

$$dH(X_H)=\iota_{X_H}\iota_{X_H}\omega = \omega(X_H,X_H)=0$$

Symplectic gradient

$X_H$ is $\nabla H$ rotated by $\omega$, so it points along level sets, not across them.

Oscillator

$H=\tfrac12(q^2+p^2)$ gives $X_H=(p,-q)$, tangent to every circle.

Printed pp. 103-110Tangent to Level Sets
This is the visual heart of the lecture. Show that dH applied to X_H vanishes, so the field never climbs the energy function. Connect the harmonic oscillator picture, where the field is the ninety-degree rotation of the gradient, to the phrase symplectic gradient that recurs throughout the rest of the course.

The Flow Preserves $\omega$

Cartan's formula

The Lie derivative splits into a contraction of $d\omega$ and an exact term.

Closedness

$d\omega=0$ kills the first term outright.

Exactness

$d(\iota_{X_H}\omega)=d(dH)=0$ kills the second.

Computation

$$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = \iota_{X_H}\,d\omega + d\,\iota_{X_H}\omega = 0 + d(dH)=0$$

Conclusion

Every Hamiltonian flow $\rho_t$ is a symplectomorphism: $\rho_t^*\omega=\omega$.

Printed pp. 103-110The Flow Preserves omega
Walk through the Cartan magic formula computation. Closedness of omega kills one term and the defining identity makes the other an exact differential of an exact form, so the Lie derivative vanishes. Underline that closedness, not just nondegeneracy, is doing the work here, completing the role of both axioms.

Conservation of Energy

Two invariants

The flow fixes both $\omega$ and $H$.

Along the flow

$\tfrac{d}{dt}(H\circ\rho_t)=dH(X_H)=0$.

Physical meaning

For autonomous systems, total energy never changes.

Energy is constant

$$X_H(H)=\omega(X_H,X_H)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; H\circ\rho_t = H$$

Printed pp. 103-110Conservation of Energy
Tie the two preservation statements together. The flow keeps omega fixed and keeps H fixed, so trajectories stay on energy level sets forever. Note that H being constant along its own flow is just the level-set tangency restated, and physically this is conservation of energy in autonomous systems.

The Poisson Bracket

Three faces

One quantity, written via $\omega$, via differentiation, and in coordinates.

Derivative reading

$\{f,g\}=X_f(g)$ measures how $g$ changes along the flow of $f$.

Antisymmetry

$\{f,g\}=-\{g,f\}$ reads straight off $\omega(X_f,X_g)$.

Definition

$$\{f,g\} = \omega(X_f,X_g) = X_f(g) = -X_g(f)$$

Canonical coordinates

$$\{f,g\}=\sum_i \frac{\partial f}{\partial q^i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_i}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q^i}$$

Printed pp. 103-110The Poisson Bracket
Introduce the bracket through three equivalent faces: as omega evaluated on two Hamiltonian fields, as one field differentiating the other function, and as the canonical coordinate formula. Have students verify the antisymmetry directly from the omega definition before moving on to the deeper algebraic identities on the next slide.

$C^\infty(M)$ is a Poisson Algebra

Bilinear & antisymmetric

$\{,\}$ is $\mathbb{R}$-linear in each slot and $\{f,g\}=-\{g,f\}$.

Jacobi

The cyclic sum vanishes — equivalent to $d\omega=0$.

Leibniz

$\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\}$ — a derivation in each argument.

Jacobi identity

$$\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=0$$

Lie homomorphism

$$X_{\{f,g\}} = -[X_f,X_g]$$

Printed pp. 103-110A Poisson Algebra
List the four defining properties and emphasize that Jacobi is the subtle one, equivalent to closedness of omega. The Leibniz rule makes the bracket a derivation in each slot. Mention that the bracket map sending f to X_f is a Lie algebra homomorphism up to sign, linking functions to vector fields.

Brackets Detect First Integrals

The criterion

$f$ is conserved along $X_g$ exactly when $\{f,g\}=0$.

First integrals

Conserved quantities of $H$ are the functions Poisson-commuting with $H$.

$H$ commutes with itself

$\{H,H\}=0$ recovers energy conservation.

Conservation law

$$\frac{d}{dt}\big(f\circ\rho_t^{X_g}\big)=\{f,g\} = 0$$

Printed pp. 103-110Conserved Quantities
State the key corollary: f is conserved along the flow of g exactly when their bracket vanishes. So first integrals of a Hamiltonian system are the functions Poisson-commuting with H. This reframes the search for conservation laws as a purely algebraic problem inside the Poisson algebra of observables.

Symplectic vs Hamiltonian Fields

Symplectic field

$\mathcal{L}_X\omega=0 \iff \iota_X\omega$ is closed

Hamiltonian field

$\iota_X\omega = dH$ is exact

The obstruction

The class $[\iota_X\omega]\in H^1(M)$ vanishes iff $X$ is globally Hamiltonian. Locally, every symplectic field is Hamiltonian.

Printed pp. 103-110Symplectic vs Hamiltonian Fields
Draw the distinction carefully. A symplectic field only needs iota X omega closed, a Hamiltonian field needs it exact. The cohomology class in H one is the precise obstruction. Use the torus with a constant field to show a symplectic field that is only locally, never globally, Hamiltonian because the class is nonzero.

A First Look at Integrable Systems

Complete integrability

On $M^{2n}$, find $n$ independent integrals $f_1,\dots,f_n$ with $\{f_i,f_j\}=0$.

Lagrangian fibers

A common regular level is isotropic and half-dimensional, hence Lagrangian.

Liouville tori

Compact connected components are tori with linear action-angle flow.

Printed pp. 103-110Integrable Systems
Close with the payoff. A system on dimension two n is completely integrable when it has n independent Poisson-commuting integrals. Their common regular level is isotropic and half-dimensional, hence Lagrangian, and compact components are Liouville tori carrying linear action-angle flow. This previews the rest of the course.
