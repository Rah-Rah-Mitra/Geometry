Compatible Triples

One package: $(\omega, J, g)$ — symplectic form, almost complex structure, metric

The thesis

Three structures, but only two are free. Compatibility lets any pair rebuild the third.

The reward

The structure group drops to $U(n)$, and an auxiliary $J$ can be chosen without losing generality.

This short lecture spans printed pages 72 through 75. The aim is to stop treating the symplectic form, the almost complex structure, and the metric as three separate gadgets. Once compatibility holds, they form one package in which any two of the three reconstruct the missing one.

The Three Structures

Symplectic form $\omega$

A closed, nondegenerate antisymmetric two-form on each tangent space.

Almost complex $J$

A bundle endomorphism with $J^2 = -\mathrm{Id}$, a pointwise multiplication by $i$.

Metric $g$

A symmetric, positive-definite inner product on tangent vectors.

Defining relations

$$\omega(u,v) = -\omega(v,u), \quad J^2 = -\mathrm{Id}, \quad g(u,v)=g(v,u)$$

Apart, then together

Each is natural on its own. Compatibility is the extra hypothesis that binds them.

Printed pp. 72-75The Three Structures
Set the stage by naming the three players: a nondegenerate two-form omega, an almost complex structure J squaring to minus one, and a Riemannian metric g. Each lives in its own world, yet the lecture will tie them together by a single bilinear identity that links all three at once.

The Binding Identity

Compatibility

$$g(u,v) = \omega(u, Jv)$$

What it asks

This $g$ must be symmetric and positive definite: $\omega(v, Jv) > 0$ for all $v \neq 0$.

Twisting antisymmetry into symmetry

$\omega$ is antisymmetric; inserting $J$ in one slot turns it into the symmetric pairing $g$.

Printed pp. 72-75The Binding Identity
The whole lecture pivots on one formula, g of u and v equals omega of u and J v. Compatibility means this g is symmetric and positive definite. Stress that this single equation is the bridge: it converts the antisymmetric omega into the symmetric g using J as the twist.

Any Two Determine the Third

Three routes

$$g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv)$$ $$\omega(u,v)=g(Ju,v)$$ $$\omega,\,g \;\Rightarrow\; J \text{ by polar decomposition}$$

One degree of freedom

Fix any two compatible structures and the third is forced uniquely.

Printed pp. 72-75Any Two Determine the Third
Walk the triangle. Given omega and J, define g by the identity. Given g and J, recover omega through omega of u and v equals g of J u and v. Given omega and g, polar decomposition extracts J. The point is closure: no vertex is independent of the other two.

Compatibility Conditions

Taming

$\omega(v, Jv) > 0$ for every nonzero $v$: $J$ is tamed by $\omega$.

Invariance

$\omega(Ju, Jv) = \omega(u,v)$: $J$ preserves the form.

Together give a metric

$$g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv) \;\text{ is symmetric and } g(v,v)>0$$

Positivity, not just nondegeneracy

Nondegeneracy alone is too weak. Positivity is what upgrades $g$ to an inner product.

Printed pp. 72-75Compatibility Conditions
Spell out what compatibility actually demands. The form must tame J, meaning omega of v and J v is positive for nonzero v, and J must preserve omega. Together these give a genuine inner product. Emphasize that positivity, not mere nondegeneracy, is what makes the metric usable.

$J$ Is a $g$-Isometry

Orthogonality

$$g(Ju, Jv) = g(u,v), \qquad J^{\mathsf T} g\, J = g$$

Rotation that preserves length

$J$ turns each tangent space by ninety degrees and keeps every norm fixed.

Printed pp. 72-75J Is an Isometry
Show that compatibility forces J to be orthogonal for g, that is J transpose g J equals g. So J rotates each tangent space by ninety degrees while preserving lengths. This orthogonality is the linear-algebra heart of why the triple behaves like multiplication by i on a Hermitian space.

The Hermitian Analogy

On $\mathbb{C}^n$

A Hermitian form $h$ splits into a real and an imaginary part.

Splitting

$$h(u,v) = g(u,v) - i\,\omega(u,v)$$

The role of $i$

Multiplication by $i$ is exactly the model $J$ joining the two parts.

Printed pp. 72-75Analogy with C-n
Anchor intuition in the model. On complex n-space the Hermitian form splits into a real symmetric part, the metric, and an imaginary antisymmetric part, the symplectic form, joined by multiplication by i. The compatible triple on a manifold is exactly this Hermitian package carried pointwise on tangent spaces.

Reduction of the Structure Group to $U(n)$

Bare bundle

Frames are related by all of $GL(2n,\mathbb{R})$.

Respecting all three

Demand frames preserve $\omega$, $J$, and $g$ at once.

Intersection

$$Sp(2n)\cap GL(n,\mathbb{C})\cap O(2n) = U(n)$$

Printed pp. 72-75Reduction to U(n)
Explain the structure-group payoff. A bare tangent bundle has frames related by the full linear group. Asking frames to respect omega, J, and g simultaneously forces transitions into the unitary group, the common intersection of the symplectic, complex-linear, and orthogonal groups. The triple is a U(n) structure.

The Space of Compatible $J$

Theorem

$$\mathcal{J}(\omega) \neq \varnothing \quad\text{and is contractible.}$$

Existence

Every symplectic form admits a compatible almost complex structure.

Contractible

The space deformation retracts to a point — stronger than connected.

Printed pp. 72-75The Space of Compatible J
State the existence and contractibility theorem. For any symplectic form the space of compatible almost complex structures is nonempty and contractible. Contractibility is stronger than connectedness: it means the space deformation retracts to a point, so no topological choice is hidden in picking a J.

Why Contractibility Matters

Choice-free invariants

Build invariants using any auxiliary compatible $J$; the answer cannot depend on the choice.

Chern classes

The Chern classes of $(TM, J)$ become honest symplectic invariants.

Independence

$$c_k(TM, J_0) = c_k(TM, J_1) \;\text{ for any } J_0, J_1 \in \mathcal{J}(\omega)$$

The licensing principle

A contractible parameter space means the construction is well defined up to that contractible ambiguity.

Printed pp. 72-75Why Contractibility Matters
Draw the consequence for invariants. Because the choice of compatible J is contractible, any invariant built using an auxiliary J cannot depend on which J was chosen. This licenses Chern classes and almost-complex machinery as honest symplectic invariants, defined up to the contractible ambiguity.

A Deformation Caution

Forward direction

If $\omega_0, \omega_1$ share a compatible $J$, the convex path $\omega_t$ stays symplectic.

Why

$$g_t(v,v)=\omega_t(v, Jv)>0 \;\text{ along the path}$$

Converse fails

A path of symplectic forms need not share any single compatible $J$.

Printed pp. 72-75A Deformation Caution
Separate two statements that are easy to conflate. If two symplectic forms share a compatible J, their convex path stays symplectic because the induced metrics stay positive. But a path of symplectic forms alone does not force one common compatible J; the standard four-dimensional example shows the converse fails.
