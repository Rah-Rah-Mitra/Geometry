Moment Map in Gauge Theory

The Atiyah-Bott picture: reduction in infinite dimensions

The slogan

Connections form a symplectic space, the gauge group acts, and curvature is the moment map.

The payoff

Flat connections are the zero level; their quotient is a moduli space.

The method

Marsden-Weinstein reduction, transplanted to a function space over a surface.

Printed pp. 153-161The Atiyah-Bott Picture

Stress that nothing new is invented; the finite-dimensional machinery of earlier lectures is simply transplanted onto an affine function space over a surface.

Principal Bundles and Connections

Vertical directions

A principal bundle $G \to P \to B$ supplies infinitesimal group orbits inside $TP$.

Connection

A $G$-invariant horizontal complement $H$, encoded by a Lie-algebra-valued one-form $A$.

Affine

Connections form an affine space; differences are horizontal one-forms on $B$.

Printed pp. 153-161Principal Bundles and Connections
Recall the bundle data students need. A principal bundle gives vertical directions inside the total space, and a connection is a gauge-invariant choice of horizontal complement, encoded by a Lie-algebra-valued one-form. Emphasize that connections form an affine space, so its tangent vectors are honest horizontal one-forms on the base. That affineness is what later makes everything linear.

Curvature of a Connection

Definition

Curvature is the horizontal part of $dA$, with the quadratic bracket correction.

Abelian case

For a circle bundle the bracket vanishes and $F = dA$ is an ordinary two-form on $B$.

Flatness

$F_A = 0$ is the zero-curvature condition; these are the flat connections.

Curvature

$$F_A = dA + \tfrac12[A,A]$$

Circle bundle

$$F = dA = (Q_x - P_y)\, dx\wedge dy$$

Density

$a = P\,dx + Q\,dy$ on the base, curvature is its scalar curl density.

Printed pp. 153-161Curvature of a Connection
Define curvature as the horizontal part of the exterior derivative of the connection form, plus the quadratic bracket term. For an abelian circle bundle the bracket dies and curvature is just the ordinary two-form dA on the surface. Flag flatness as the zero-curvature condition; that vanishing locus is the eventual target of reduction.

The Affine Space of Connections

One global chart

Fix a reference $A_0$; every connection is $A_0 + a$ with $a$ a Lie-algebra-valued one-form.

Constant tangent space

$T_A\mathcal{A} \cong \Omega^1(B;\mathfrak{g})$ at every point — a flat model space.

Infinite-dimensional

$\mathcal{A}$ is a function space, yet behaves like a vector space.

Printed pp. 153-161The Affine Space of Connections
Explain why the space of connections is the right symplectic manifold. Fixing a reference connection identifies the whole space with the vector space of Lie-algebra-valued one-forms. The constant tangent space lets us write one global symplectic form by integrating a wedge product over the surface, paired through the Killing or trace inner product on the algebra.

The Atiyah-Bott Symplectic Form

Symplectic form

$$\omega(a,b) = \int_B \langle a \wedge b\rangle$$

Skew

Antisymmetry comes directly from the wedge of two one-forms.

Closed

$\omega$ is constant in affine coordinates, so $d\omega = 0$ automatically.

Nondegenerate

Poincare duality on the closed surface detects any nonzero variation.

Printed pp. 153-161The Atiyah-Bott Symplectic Form
Present the symplectic form explicitly. Pair two connection variations by wedging them and integrating over the closed surface. Skewness comes from the wedge of one-forms; closedness is automatic because the form is constant in affine coordinates; nondegeneracy follows from Poincare duality on the surface. Mention the Fourier diagnostic from the notebook that makes the skew blocks visible.

The Gauge Group Acts

Gauge group

$\mathcal{G}$ acts on $\mathcal{A}$ by conjugation plus a derivative term.

Abelian case

A phase $\theta$ shifts $A \mapsto A - d\theta$; components become $(P-\theta_x,\,Q-\theta_y)$.

Two facts

The action preserves $\omega$, and curvature is equivariant.

Printed pp. 153-161The Gauge Group Acts
Introduce the gauge group as the symmetry. Gauge transformations act on connections by conjugation plus a derivative term; in the abelian circle case a phase function theta shifts A by minus d-theta. Stress two facts: the action preserves the Atiyah-Bott form, and curvature is equivariant. For the circle bundle equivariance degenerates into plain invariance of curvature.

Curvature is the Moment Map

Moment map

$$\mu(A) = F_A \in \Omega^2(B;\mathfrak{g})$$

Pairing

Lie algebra of $\mathcal{G}$ is $\Omega^0(B;\mathfrak{g})$; pair $\mu$ with $X$ by integration over $B$.

Defining identity

$d\mu_X(a) = \omega(X^{\#}, a)$ for every variation $a$.

Moment-map condition

$$d\langle\mu, X\rangle = \iota_{X^{\#}}\,\omega$$

Equivariance

$\mu$ intertwines the gauge action with the coadjoint action — Hamiltonian action.

Reading

Curvature is literally the moment map of the gauge group on connections.

Printed pp. 153-161Curvature is the Moment Map
This is the heart of the lecture. State that the curvature map sending a connection to its curvature is a moment map for the gauge action. Verify the defining identity: the derivative of the paired moment map equals the symplectic contraction with the infinitesimal gauge vector. Show on the board that this identity is integration by parts, Stokes with no boundary.

The Stokes Identity

Left side

$$d\mu_X(a) = \int_B X\,da$$

Right side

$$\omega(X^{\#}, a) = \int_B (-dX)\wedge a$$

Why equal

$\int_B d(Xa) = 0$ on a closed oriented surface — no boundary.

Printed pp. 153-161The Stokes Identity
Slow down on the computation that powers the moment-map property. Choose a gauge element X, a function on the surface, and a variation a. The left side integrates X against the linearized curvature; the right side integrates the gauge vector against a through the form. They agree by moving a derivative across the integral. On a closed surface the boundary term vanishes identically.

Flat Connections as the Zero Level

Symplectic quotient

$$\mathcal{M} = \mu^{-1}(0)\,/\,\mathcal{G}$$

Zero level

$\mu^{-1}(0) = \{A : F_A = 0\}$ is exactly the flat connections.

Quotient

Dividing by $\mathcal{G}$ is Marsden-Weinstein reduction in infinite dimensions.

Result

$\mathcal{M}$ is the finite-dimensional moduli space of flat connections.

Printed pp. 153-161Flat Connections as the Zero Level
Assemble the reduction. The moment map is curvature, so its zero level set is exactly the flat connections. Quotienting that level by the gauge group is Marsden-Weinstein reduction performed in infinite dimensions. The result is the moduli space of flat connections, a finite-dimensional symplectic space sitting inside an infinite-dimensional construction.

Yang-Mills and Representation Varieties

Yang-Mills functional

$$\mathrm{YM}(A) = \tfrac12\|\mu(A)\|^2 = \tfrac12\int_B |F_A|^2$$

Norm-squared

YM is the squared norm of the moment map; its minima are the flat connections.

Holonomy

A flat connection is determined by its holonomy representation of $\pi_1(B)$.

Representation variety

$$\mathcal{M} \cong \mathrm{Hom}(\pi_1(B), G)\,/\,G$$

Surface group

Moduli of flat connections is the representation variety of the surface group.
