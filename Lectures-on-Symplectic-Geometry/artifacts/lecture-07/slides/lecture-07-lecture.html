The Moser Theorems

Equating symplectic forms by integrating one linear equation

The trick

Turn a path of forms into an isotopy whose pullback is constant.

The engine

A single pointwise solve: $\iota_{v_t}\omega_t = -\mu_t$.

The payoff

Global and local equivalence theorems for symplectic structures.

Four Notions of Equivalence

Symplectomorphic

Some diffeomorphism $\varphi$ with $\varphi^*\omega_1=\omega_0$.

Strongly isotopic

An isotopy $\rho_t$ realizing $\rho_1^*\omega_1=\omega_0$.

Isotopic / deformation-equivalent

A path of symplectic forms; class fixed, or only nondegeneracy kept.

Printed pp. 40-43Four Notions of Equivalence
Pin down the vocabulary before any proof. Symplectomorphic asks only for some diffeomorphism, strongly isotopic demands an isotopy with a fixed class, isotopic keeps the cohomology class along a path, and deformation equivalent only keeps nondegeneracy. Drawing the implication arrows now stops students from collapsing four distinct words into one fuzzy idea.

The Moser Question

Given

$\omega_t,\ t\in[0,1]$, a smooth path of symplectic forms with $[\omega_t]$ constant.

Wanted

An isotopy $\rho_t$ with $\rho_t^*\,\omega_t = \omega_0.$

Strategy

Do not guess $\rho_t$ directly. Build the generating vector field $v_t$, then integrate it.

Generator

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\rho_t = v_t\circ\rho_t,\qquad \rho_0=\mathrm{id}.$

Printed pp. 40-43The Moser Question
State the goal cleanly. Given a smooth path of symplectic forms sharing a cohomology class, we want an isotopy whose pullback freezes the form at time zero. Emphasize that we are not guessing the diffeomorphism directly; we will manufacture its generating vector field and then integrate, which is the entire strategic move of the lecture.

Differentiating the Pullback

Set the derivative to zero

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\big(\rho_t^*\omega_t\big)=0$

Product rule

$\rho_t^*\!\left(\mathcal{L}_{v_t}\omega_t + \dfrac{d\omega_t}{dt}\right)=0$

Two terms

A Lie-derivative term from moving the map, and the explicit time derivative of the form.

Reduction

Since $\rho_t$ is a diffeomorphism, it suffices that the bracket vanish pointwise.

Printed pp. 40-43Differentiating the Pullback
Walk through the calculation slowly. Write the pullback of omega t under rho t and differentiate in time. The product rule splits into a Lie derivative term and the explicit time derivative of the form. Our target is that this whole expression vanish identically, which converts a global demand into a pointwise algebraic condition we can actually solve.

Three Hypotheses, Three Cancellations

Closedness (Cartan)

$\mathcal{L}_{v_t}\omega_t = d\,\iota_{v_t}\omega_t + \iota_{v_t}\,d\omega_t = d\,\iota_{v_t}\omega_t.$

Fixed cohomology

$\dfrac{d\omega_t}{dt}$ is exact: write it as $d\mu_t$.

Nondegeneracy

$\omega_t$ inverts $\mu_t$ into a unique $v_t$.

Printed pp. 40-43Three Hypotheses, Three Cancellations
This is the heart of the argument, the cancellation ledger. Closedness turns the Lie derivative into d of an interior product via Cartan. Fixed cohomology writes the time derivative as d of a primitive mu t. Nondegeneracy then inverts omega t to solve for the vector field. Each hypothesis retires exactly one term; line them up so the bookkeeping is transparent.

The Moser Equation

Solving one identity makes the pullback derivative vanish

The Moser equation

$\iota_{v_t}\,\omega_t = -\,\mu_t$

Why it solves

$d\iota_{v_t}\omega_t + d\mu_t = d(-\mu_t)+d\mu_t = 0$, so $\frac{d}{dt}(\rho_t^*\omega_t)=0$.

Unique solution

$\omega_t$ nondegenerate $\Rightarrow$ the map $v\mapsto\iota_v\omega_t$ is a bundle isomorphism. So $v_t$ exists and is unique at each point.

Then integrate

Flow $v_t$ to get $\rho_t$; at $t=1$, $\rho_1^*\omega_1=\omega_0$.

Printed pp. 40-43The Moser Equation
Highlight the equation that names the lecture. Interior product of v t with omega t equals minus mu t. Because omega t is nondegenerate, the bundle map sending a vector to its contraction is an isomorphism, so v t exists and is unique at every point. This pointwise linear solve is the engine; everything else is integration and bookkeeping.

A Two-Dimensional Model

Toy path

$\omega_t = (1+\varepsilon t\,x)\,dx\wedge dy$

Primitive

$\dfrac{d\omega_t}{dt}=d\mu_t$ with $\mu_t=\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}x^2\,dy$.

Solution

$v_t = -\dfrac{\varepsilon x^2/2}{1+\varepsilon t x}\,\partial_x$; residual is machine zero.

Printed pp. 40-43A Two-Dimensional Model
Make it concrete with the notebook's toy path. In two dimensions every form is a density times dx wedge dy, so the Moser equation collapses to one scalar cancellation. The primitive is a quadratic, the vector field points along x, and the residual is machine zero. Encourage students to run the cell and watch the arrows cancel the density's drift.

The Nondegeneracy Gate

Fixed class is not enough

The path must stay inside the open cone of nondegenerate forms.

Failure

If the density crosses zero, $\omega_t$ is not symplectic and cannot be inverted.

Singular point

$\lambda_t(p)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \iota_{v}\omega_t=-\mu_t$ has no solution at $p$.

Printed pp. 40-43The Nondegeneracy Gate
Show why fixed cohomology alone is not enough. If the density crosses zero the form stops being symplectic and omega t can no longer be inverted, so the Moser equation loses its unique solution. The path must stay inside the open cone of nondegenerate forms. The failure plot is as instructive as the success plot; do not skip it.

Moser's Global Theorem

Theorem (stability)

$M$ compact, $[\omega_0]=[\omega_1]$, joined by symplectic $\omega_t$ in that class $\Rightarrow$ $\exists\,\varphi$ with $\varphi^*\omega_1=\omega_0$.

Role of compactness

It guarantees $v_t$ integrates over all of $[0,1]$, producing an isotopy $\rho_t$.

Consequence

$\varphi=\rho_1$ is isotopic to the identity; cohomologous forms in one class are strongly isotopic.

Reading

The cohomology class is the only deformation invariant that matters on a compact manifold.

Printed pp. 40-43Moser's Global Theorem
State the payoff theorem for compact manifolds. If two symplectic forms are cohomologous and joined by a path of symplectic forms in that fixed class, they are equivalent by a diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity. Compactness is what guarantees the time-dependent vector field integrates over the whole unit interval, giving a genuine isotopy.

The Local Moser Theorem

Relative version

$\omega_0=\omega_1$ on a compact $X\subset M \;\Rightarrow\; \exists\,\varphi$ fixing $X$ with $\varphi^*\omega_1=\omega_0$ near $X$.

Key choice

The homotopy formula gives a primitive $\mu_t$ that vanishes on $X$.

Therefore

$\iota_{v_t}\omega_t=-\mu_t=0$ on $X$, so $v_t|_X=0$ and the flow fixes $X$.

Printed pp. 40-43The Local Moser Theorem
Present the relative version. Near a compact submanifold where two forms agree, the homotopy formula lets us pick a primitive that vanishes on the submanifold. Then the Moser vector field vanishes there too, so the resulting isotopy fixes the submanifold pointwise. This relative trick is the template for Darboux and the tubular neighborhood theorems coming next.

Recap and Outlook

One equation

Everything hinges on solving $\iota_{v_t}\omega_t=-\mu_t$ pointwise.

Three hypotheses

Fixed cohomology gives $\mu_t$; nondegeneracy gives $v_t$; compactness gives global flow.

One refinement

A primitive vanishing on $X$ yields the relative theorem.

Master identity

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\big(\rho_t^*\omega_t\big)=\rho_t^*\,d\big(\iota_{v_t}\omega_t+\mu_t\big)=0$
