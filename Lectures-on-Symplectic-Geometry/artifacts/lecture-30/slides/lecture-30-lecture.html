Liouville Measure and Its Pushforward

Liouville measure

The symplectic volume form on a $2n$-manifold.

Pushforward

A proper moment map sends it to a measure on $\mathfrak{g}^*$.

Density

Its Radon-Nikodym derivative against Lebesgue is the DH density.

Liouville volume

$$\frac{\omega^n}{n!}$$

DH measure

$$m_{\mathrm{DH}}(U) = \int_{\mu^{-1}(U)} \frac{\omega^n}{n!}$$

Printed pp. 189-196Liouville and Pushforward
Start from the Liouville measure built from omega to the n over n factorial, the canonical symplectic volume. Pushing it forward by a proper moment map gives the Duistermaat-Heckman measure on the dual Lie algebra. Its Radon-Nikodym density against Lebesgue is the object whose regularity the whole lecture explains.

The Sphere: Archimedes' Theorem

Setup

$(S^2,\ \omega = d\theta\wedge dh)$ with circle action and $\mu = h$.

Band area

The preimage of $[a,b]$ is a band of area $2\pi(b-a)$.

Constant density

So $m_{\mathrm{DH}} = 2\pi\,dh$ on $[-1,1]$.

Printed pp. 189-196The Sphere Example
The sphere with height moment map is the cleanest illustration. A horizontal band over an interval has area exactly two pi times the height difference, independent of latitude. So the pushforward density is the constant two pi on the interval from minus one to one, our first explicit Duistermaat-Heckman measure.

Piecewise Polynomial on the Polytope

Chambers

Regular values split $\mu(M)$ into chambers.

Breaks

Density bends only at critical values, where level-set topology changes.

Theorem I

Density is polynomial on each chamber, piecewise across them.

Printed pp. 189-196DH Measure on the Polytope
In general the density is constant on each chamber of regular values but jumps or bends at critical values, where the topology of the level set changes. The graph is polynomial on each chamber and only piecewise polynomial across breaks. That qualitative shape is the heart of the first Duistermaat-Heckman theorem.

Local Form for Nearby Reductions

Tubular model

Near a regular level, $M \cong Z \times (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$.

Connection

$Z \to M_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a principal $S^1$ bundle with connection $\alpha$.

One model, all levels

A single $\sigma$ governs every reduction in the slab.

Equivariant normal form

$$\sigma = \pi^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}} - d(x\,\alpha)$$

Reading it

$x$ is the slab coordinate; $\pi$ projects $Z$ onto $M_{\mathrm{red}}$; $d\alpha = \pi^{*}\beta$.

Printed pp. 189-196Local Form for Reductions
For a circle action the proof builds a local model near a regular level. A tubular neighborhood looks like Z times an interval, and we write sigma as pi star omega-red minus d of x alpha, with alpha a connection. This single normal form controls every reduced space in the interval at once.

Nondegeneracy and the Moment Projection

At $x=0$

$\sigma = \pi^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}} + \alpha\wedge dx$, nondegenerate, hence symplectic for small $x$.

Projection is moment

$\iota_{X^{\#}}\sigma = dx$, so $x$ is the moment map of the slab.

Same quotient

Each level $x=t$ reduces to the fixed underlying space.

Printed pp. 189-196Nondegeneracy and Projection
At x equal to zero the form sigma reduces to the reduced symplectic form plus alpha wedge dx, which is nondegenerate, so it stays symplectic for small x. The same form gives i of X applied to sigma equal to dx, so the projection x is itself the moment map. The level x equals t reduces to the same quotient.

Linear Variation of the Reduced Class

Theorem II

The reduced class moves affinely with the level $c$.

Slope is Chern

$\beta$ is the curvature; $[\beta]$ is the Euler/Chern class of the bundle.

DH theorem

$$[\omega_{\mathrm{red}}(c)] = [\omega_{\mathrm{red}}(c_0)] + (c-c_0)\,e$$

Printed pp. 189-196Linear Variation of the Class
Reducing at level t yields omega-red minus t beta, where beta is the curvature of the connection and represents the Chern class of the circle bundle. Hence the reduced cohomology class varies affinely: it is the class at c-zero plus c minus c-zero times the fixed Euler class. This is the second Duistermaat-Heckman theorem.

Reduced Volume Is Polynomial

Top power

Raise the affine class to the top dimension of $M_{\mathrm{red}}$.

Degree bound

A polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $n-1$.

Fubini step

This reduced volume equals the DH density $f(t)$.

Volume of reductions

$$\mathrm{vol}(M_t) = \int_{M_{\mathrm{red}}} \frac{(\omega_{\mathrm{red}} - t\beta)^{\,n-1}}{(n-1)!}$$

Consequence

The DH density is genuinely polynomial on each chamber.

Printed pp. 189-196Polynomial Volume
Take the top power of the affine class to get the symplectic volume of the reduced space. Expanding omega-red minus t beta to the n minus one over factorial gives a polynomial in t of degree at most n minus one. By a Fubini computation this reduced volume equals the Duistermaat-Heckman density near the regular level.

Toric Case: Constant Density

Full torus

For an $n$-torus the density is the constant $(2\pi)^n$ on $\Delta$.

Volume law

$\mathrm{vol}(M) = (2\pi)^n \cdot \mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{Euc}}(\Delta)$.

Toric DH

$$f(c) \equiv (2\pi)^n \ \text{ on } \ \Delta$$

Printed pp. 189-196Toric Constant Density
On a toric manifold the density is the constant two pi to the n on the whole moment polytope. So the symplectic volume of the manifold is two pi to the n times the Euclidean volume of the polytope. The sphere example is exactly this statement in dimension two, with the segment as polytope.

Equivariant Cohomology and Localization

Cartan model

Invariant forms tensored with polynomials in $x$, $\deg x = 2$.

Differential

$d_{S^1} = d - x\,\iota_X$ squares to zero on invariant forms.

Localization

Equivariant integrals reduce to fixed-point data: exact stationary phase.

Equivariant symplectic form

$$d_{S^1}(\omega + x\,\mu) = 0 \iff d\mu = \iota_X\omega$$

Homework 23

Closed exactly when $\mu$ is a moment map.

Printed pp. 189-196Equivariant Cohomology Bridge
Homework twenty-three frames everything through the Cartan model. The differential d minus i of X times x squares to zero on invariant forms, and omega plus mu x is equivariantly closed precisely when mu is a moment map. Localization then computes such integrals from fixed point data, explaining the stationary phase connection.

Synthesis

Measure side

Pushforward density is piecewise polynomial across chambers.

Class side

Reduced symplectic classes move affinely; top powers are polynomial.

Extremes

Toric manifolds give constant density on the polytope.

Why it matters

Reduction, volume, and equivariant localization unify into one statement: the geometry of the moment image controls symplectic volume exactly.

One line summary

$$f(c) = \mathrm{vol}\big(\mu^{-1}(c)/T\big) \ \text{is piecewise poly.}$$

Printed pp. 189-196Synthesis
To close the course: moment maps cannot scatter symplectic volume arbitrarily. On regular chambers the pushforward density is polynomial because reduced symplectic classes move affinely and their top powers are polynomial. Toric manifolds are the constant-density extreme, and equivariant localization gives the exact stationary phase formula underneath it all.
