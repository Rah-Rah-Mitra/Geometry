Isotopies

One-parameter family

A smooth path of diffeomorphisms $\rho_t:M\to M$ with $\rho_0=\mathrm{id}$.

The path is the object

We never fix a single $\rho_t$; the entire deformation carries the geometry.

Definition

$\rho:M\times\mathbb{R}\to M,\quad \rho_t=\rho(\cdot,t),\ \rho_0=\mathrm{id}_M$

Picture

A coordinate grid is dragged continuously; each point traces a trajectory.

Printed pp. 33-39Isotopies
Define an isotopy as a smooth one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms starting at the identity. Stress that we never freeze a single map; we track the whole path. The derivative of this path at each instant is the data we actually compute with, so the path is the object, not any one frame.

Time-Dependent Vector Fields

Generating field

Differentiating the isotopy gives a field read at the moved point.

Equivalent data

Integrating a complete field $v_t$ recovers $\rho_t$.

Flow equation

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\rho_t(p) = v_t\big(\rho_t(p)\big),\quad \rho_0=\mathrm{id}$

Printed pp. 33-39Time-Dependent Vector Fields
Differentiating the isotopy in time produces a time-dependent vector field read at the moved point. Emphasize the composition order in the flow equation: the field is evaluated where the point has already gone. Conversely, integrating a complete time-dependent field recovers the isotopy, so the two encodings are equivalent.

The Lie Derivative

Rate of change along a flow

$\mathcal{L}_X\omega$ measures how $\omega$ changes when dragged by the flow of $X$.

Invariance test

$\mathcal{L}_X\omega=0$ exactly when $\omega$ is flow-invariant.

Limit definition

$\mathcal{L}_X\omega = \dfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\,(\phi_t^{X})^{*}\omega$

Why it matters

It is the bridge from the geometry of motion to the algebra of forms.

Printed pp. 33-39The Lie Derivative
Introduce the Lie derivative as the infinitesimal rate of change of a form dragged along a flow. Give the limit definition through the pullback, then note it measures how a tensor fails to be invariant under the flow. This is the bridge between the geometry of motion and the algebra of differential forms.

Cartan's Magic Formula

The engine relating a flow to the differential of a form

Cartan

$\mathcal{L}_X\omega = d\,\iota_X\omega + \iota_X d\omega$

Two algebraic moves

An exact piece $d\iota_X\omega$ plus a contraction of $d\omega$.

Payoff

Trade a flow derivative for purely algebraic operations.

Printed pp. 33-39Cartan's Magic Formula
Present Cartan's formula as the engine of the whole lecture: the Lie derivative splits into an exact piece and a contraction of the exterior derivative. Walk through why each term appears and stress that it lets us trade a flow derivative for purely algebraic operations on forms.

Differentiating Pullbacks

Two contributions

The form moves and the form itself changes in time.

Common error

Dropping the explicit $\partial_t\omega_t$ term breaks the bookkeeping.

Time derivative of a pullback

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\,\rho_t^{*}\omega_t = \rho_t^{*}\!\left(\mathcal{L}_{v_t}\omega_t + \dfrac{d\omega_t}{dt}\right)$

Where it leads

This identity is exactly what the Moser equation will balance to zero.

Printed pp. 33-39Differentiating Pullbacks
For a time-dependent form pulled back along an isotopy, the time derivative carries two terms: the Lie derivative of the spatial part plus the explicit time derivative of the form. Warn students that forgetting the explicit term is the most common error and is exactly what the Moser equation balances.

The Homotopy Formula

Chain homotopy

Integrating the contraction along the flow builds an operator $Q$.

Algebraic shadow

A geometric homotopy becomes the identity below.

Homotopy identity

$\mathrm{id}^{*} - \rho_0^{*} = dQ + Qd,\quad Q\omega=\!\int_0^1\!\rho_t^{*}\,\iota_{v_t}\omega\,dt$

Reading it

The difference of endpoint pullbacks is exact plus closed-controlled.

Printed pp. 33-39The Homotopy Formula
State the chain-homotopy formula relating the pullbacks at the two endpoints through an operator built by integrating the contraction along the flow. Show that the operator Q satisfies the identity minus pullback equals dQ plus Qd, the algebraic shadow of the geometric homotopy.

The Poincare Lemma

Contract to a point

On a contractible set one endpoint pullback kills positive-degree forms.

Closed becomes exact

If $d\omega=0$ then $\omega=d(Q\omega)$ locally.

Local exactness

$d\omega=0 \ \Longrightarrow\ \omega = dQ\omega\quad(\deg\omega>0)$

Printed pp. 33-39The Poincare Lemma
Apply the homotopy formula on a contractible set: contracting to a point makes one endpoint pullback vanish on positive-degree forms, so every closed form becomes exact. Stress that this is local exactness, the precise tool that produces primitives for nearby forms in the Moser argument.

Tubular Neighborhoods

Normal bundle model

A neighborhood of $X$ looks like a neighborhood of the zero section of $NX$.

Deformation retract

Radial contraction $(x,v)\mapsto(x,tv)$ retracts onto $X$.

Printed pp. 33-39Tubular Neighborhoods
Introduce the tubular neighborhood theorem: a neighborhood of a submanifold is diffeomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section of its normal bundle. The radial contraction of fibers then makes the submanifold a deformation retract, supplying the homotopy the Poincare argument needs near a submanifold.

When Projection Fails

Hypothesis bites

The theorem needs a sufficiently small normal radius.

Noncompact case

Use a shrinking radius function, not a constant width.

Printed pp. 33-39Projection Failure Mode
Use the circle picture to show the hypothesis bites: the center is equidistant from every boundary point, so nearest-point projection is not unique once the radius is too large. This is why the theorem demands a sufficiently small, or shrinking, normal neighborhood in the noncompact case.

Assembling the Moser Trick

Isotopy → field

A path of diffeomorphisms gives $v_t$.

Cartan differentiates

$\tfrac{d}{dt}\rho_t^{*}\omega_t$ becomes algebraic.

Tubular retract

Supplies the homotopy near $X$.

Exact primitive

The input for Lecture 7's Moser equation.

The comparison engine

$\mathcal{L}_{v_t}\omega_t + \dfrac{d\omega_t}{dt} = 0 \ \Longrightarrow\ \rho_t^{*}\omega_t \equiv \omega_0$

Takeaway

Nearby forms are compared by a controlled flow plus local exactness.

Printed pp. 33-39Assembling the Moser Trick
Close by chaining the pieces: isotopy gives a field, Cartan differentiates the pullback, the tubular retract supplies a homotopy, and the homotopy formula yields an exact primitive. That primitive is precisely the input the next lecture feeds into the Moser equation to compare nearby forms.
