Kahler Forms

Symplectic, complex, and Riemannian geometry in agreement

Definition in one line

A closed, real, positive $(1,1)$-form compatible with an integrable $J$.

Why it matters

The Fubini-Study form makes $\mathbb{CP}^n$ the model compact example; projective varieties inherit it.

Printed pp. 88-95Kahler Forms
This lecture sits at the meeting point of symplectic, complex, and Riemannian geometry. A Kahler form is a symplectic form that respects an integrable complex structure. Stress at the outset that the whole subject is governed by one matrix of Hermitian coefficients being positive definite, which is the engine for every later result.

One Manifold, Three Compatible Structures

From two, a third

An integrable $J$ plus a compatible symplectic $\omega$ already determines a Riemannian metric $g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv)$.

What is new

Kahler demands integrability of $J$, unlocking holomorphic charts and Dolbeault calculus.

The compatible triple

$g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv),\quad \omega(u,v)=g(Ju,v)$

Kahler manifold

A complex manifold carrying a symplectic $\omega$ compatible with its integrable $J$.

Printed pp. 88-95Three Geometries Agree
Open by reminding students that a complex structure plus a compatible symplectic form already builds a Riemannian metric. Kahler is the case where all three structures are simultaneously parallel. The novelty over almost complex compatibility is integrability of J, which lets us use holomorphic coordinates and Dolbeault calculus.

Bidegree and the $(1,1)$ Constraint

Splitting by $J$

The complexified forms split by holomorphic and antiholomorphic factors into types $(p,q)$.

Compatibility

Invariance $J^*\omega=\omega$ kills the $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$ parts, leaving pure type $(1,1)$.

The Five Gates of a Kahler Form

Definition

$\omega$ is Kahler iff it is a closed, real, positive form of type $(1,1)$.

The hard gate

Closedness $d\omega=0$ is the truly restrictive condition: it separates Kahler from merely Hermitian.

Reading Positivity from $h_{jk}$

Local coefficient matrix

Write $\omega = \tfrac{i}{2}\sum h_{jk}\,dz^j\wedge d\bar z^k$ with $h$ Hermitian.

Metric and form

The Kahler metric is the real part of $h$; the form is the imaginary part.

Positivity certificate

$h_{jk}=\overline{h_{kj}},\qquad \omega(v,Jv)>0 \iff h \succ 0$

Consequence

Positive definiteness makes the induced $g$ a genuine Riemannian metric, not just nondegenerate.

Printed pp. 88-95Positivity and the Hermitian Matrix
Tie positivity of the form to positive definiteness of the Hermitian matrix h. Explain that the associated metric is the real part and the form is the imaginary part. Nondegeneracy is just the determinant being nonzero, and positivity strengthens this so the metric is genuinely Riemannian.

The Local Kahler Potential

Construction machine

A strictly plurisubharmonic real $\rho$ produces a Kahler form.

Closed for free

Since $\partial^2=\bar\partial^2=0$, any $\omega=i\,\partial\bar\partial\rho$ is automatically closed.

Potential formula

$\omega = i\,\partial\bar\partial\rho,\qquad \Big(\tfrac{\partial^2\rho}{\partial z^j\partial\bar z^k}\Big)\succ 0$

Converse

Locally every Kahler form arises this way, by Dolbeault triviality.

Printed pp. 88-95The Local Kahler Potential
This is the construction machine of the theory. A strictly plurisubharmonic real function rho yields a Kahler form by applying i partial partial-bar. Because partial squared and partial-bar squared vanish, the form is automatically closed. Stress the converse: locally every Kahler form arises this way, by Dolbeault triviality.

Two Potentials, Two Densities

Flat potential

$\rho=|z|^2$ gives the standard flat form on $\mathbb{C}^n$; complex Hessian is constant.

Fubini-Study potential

$\rho=\log(1+|z|^2)$ bends the density to finite total area.

Printed pp. 88-95Potentials in Action
Compare the flat potential, whose complex Hessian is constant, with the Fubini-Study potential, whose Hessian bends the density. The contour pictures make the difference visible. The point is that the eigenvalues of the complex Hessian are exactly the positivity certificate for the resulting two-form.

The Fubini-Study Form on $\mathbb{CP}^n$

Model compact Kahler

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ with the Fubini-Study form is the canonical compact Kahler manifold.

Globally well defined

Chart transitions add pluriharmonic terms that $i\,\partial\bar\partial$ annihilates.

Local potential

$\omega_{FS}=\tfrac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar\partial\,\log\!\big(1+|z|^2\big)$

Subtle point

Local potentials differ across charts, yet $\omega_{FS}$ is a single global form.

Printed pp. 88-95Fubini-Study on CP^n
Introduce the model compact Kahler manifold. The potential log of one plus norm squared gives a globally defined form despite being only a local potential, because chart transitions add pluriharmonic terms that i partial partial-bar annihilates. This well-definedness is the subtle heart of the projective example.

$\mathbb{CP}^1=S^2$: The Round Area Form

Chart density

On the affine chart the density is $(1+r^2)^{-2}$, with $r=|z|$.

Finite area

$\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}} \frac{2\pi r\,dr}{(1+r^2)^2}=\pi$ — the total projective area.

Printed pp. 88-95CP^1 is the Round Sphere
Ground the abstraction in the simplest case. On the one chart of CP^1 the Fubini-Study density is one over one plus r squared, all squared, and integrating it gives finite total area pi. This is the round area form on the two-sphere up to scale, the most familiar Kahler manifold of all.

Complex Submanifolds Stay Kahler

Restriction

Restricting $\rho$ to a complex submanifold takes a principal minor of the positive Hessian.

Minors stay positive

Principal minors of a positive-definite matrix are positive, so the induced form is Kahler.

Corollary

$X\subset(M,\omega)\ \text{complex}\ \Rightarrow\ (X,\omega|_X)\ \text{Kahler}$

Big payoff

Nonsingular projective varieties sit in $\mathbb{CP}^n$, so they are Kahler via Fubini-Study.

Printed pp. 88-95Submanifolds and Projective Varieties
Close the logical loop. Restricting a strictly plurisubharmonic potential to a complex submanifold takes a principal minor of a positive matrix, which stays positive. Hence complex submanifolds of Kahler manifolds are Kahler, and nonsingular projective varieties inherit Kahler structure from the Fubini-Study form on the ambient projective space.

Recap and Exploration

The chain of ideas

  • $(1,1)$, closed, real, positive
  • Local potential $\omega=i\,\partial\bar\partial\rho$
  • Fubini-Study on $\mathbb{CP}^n$, with $\mathbb{CP}^1=S^2$
  • Submanifolds and projective varieties

One unifying picture

Kahler = symplectic + complex + Riemannian, all mutually compatible.

Exploration prompt

Take $\rho=\log(1+a|z|^2)$. Vary $a$ in the Hessian check and predict when the local form stays positive.

Recap the chain of ideas: type one-one, closed, real, positive, then potentials, then Fubini-Study, then submanifolds.
