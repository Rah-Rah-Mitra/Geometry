Skew-Symmetric Bilinear Forms

Definition

A bilinear $\Omega:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}$ that flips sign under a swap.

Self-pairing vanishes

$\Omega(v,v)=0$ for every $v$: a vector is always neutral against itself.

Reads as area

Skewness is exactly the algebra of oriented two-dimensional area.

Skew-symmetry

$$\Omega(u,v) = -\,\Omega(v,u)$$

Printed pp. 1-6Skew-Symmetric Bilinear Forms
Start purely algebraically. A bilinear map on a vector space is skew when swapping arguments flips the sign, so every vector pairs to zero with itself. Emphasize that skewness already feels like oriented area, which is exactly the geometric reading we will lean on for the rest of the course.

The Standard Normal Form

Structure theorem

Every skew $\Omega$ admits a basis splitting $V$ into a kernel plus area pairs.

Basis

Vectors $u_k$ in the kernel and pairs $e_i,f_i$ with $\Omega(e_i,f_i)=1$.

Even rank

The paired block has even rank; only the kernel dimension is left over.

Decomposition

$$V = U \oplus W,\quad U=\ker\Omega$$

$\Omega(e_i,f_j)=\delta_{ij}$, all other basis pairings zero.

Printed pp. 1-6The Standard Normal Form
This is the structural theorem of the day. By a Gram-Schmidt style induction every skew form splits into a kernel block plus area pairs. Stress that the kernel dimension and the number of pairs are basis-independent invariants, and that the paired part always has even rank no matter how the form looked initially.

Symplectic Vector Spaces

Nondegenerate

The only vector orthogonal to all of $V$ is zero: $\ker\Omega=\{0\}$.

Dual isomorphism

$\tilde\Omega:V\to V^{*}$, $v\mapsto\Omega(v,\cdot)$, is invertible.

Even dimension

No kernel means no leftover block, so $\dim V = 2n$.

Symplectic vector space

$$(V,\Omega),\qquad \tilde\Omega \text{ an isomorphism}$$

A symplectic basis $e_1,\dots,e_n,f_1,\dots,f_n$ identifies $V$ with the standard model.

Symplectic basis

$\Omega(e_i,e_j)=\Omega(f_i,f_j)=0$ and $\Omega(e_i,f_j)=\delta_{ij}$.

Printed pp. 1-6Symplectic Vector Spaces
Nondegeneracy is the no-kernel condition: the only vector orthogonal to everything is zero. Equivalently the induced map to the dual space is an isomorphism. Point out that nondegeneracy plus the normal form immediately forces even dimension and hands us a symplectic basis identifying the space with the standard model.

The Standard Form on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$

Conjugate coordinates

Split coordinates into position $x_i$ and momentum $y_i$.

Prototype

$\omega_0$ is the sum of elementary area forms over the pairs.

Matrix

Identity and minus-identity blocks: nondegenerate by inspection.

Standard symplectic form

$$\omega_0 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dx_i \wedge dy_i$$

In coordinates

$$\Omega_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_n \\ -I_n & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

Printed pp. 1-6The Standard Form on R^{2n}
Here is the prototype every later example imitates. Pair the coordinates into conjugate planes and let omega-zero be the sum of the elementary area forms. Walk through the matrix: identity and minus-identity blocks. This is the concrete object the abstract symplectic basis was secretly describing all along.

Conjugate Planes and Oriented Area

Plane by plane

$\omega_0$ measures signed area in each $(x_i,y_i)$ plane.

No cross terms

Vectors in different conjugate planes pair to zero.

Area reading

$\omega_0(u,v)=\sum_i (u_{x_i}v_{y_i}-u_{y_i}v_{x_i})$

Printed pp. 1-6Conjugate Planes and Oriented Area
Spend time on the picture. The form reads off signed area in each conjugate plane and ignores cross terms between different planes. Have students compute omega-zero on two sample vectors so the determinant-as-area interpretation lands. This visual is the intuition they should carry into every nondegeneracy check.

Subspaces and the Symplectic Orthogonal

Orthogonal of a subspace

$$Y^{\Omega}=\{v: \Omega(v,u)=0\ \forall u\in Y\}$$

$\dim Y + \dim Y^{\Omega} = \dim V$ always holds.

Two extremes

Isotropic: $Y\subseteq Y^{\Omega}$. Coisotropic: $Y^{\Omega}\subseteq Y$.

Symplectic

$\Omega$ restricts nondegenerately to $Y$.

Lagrangian

$Y=Y^{\Omega}$ and $\dim Y=\tfrac12\dim V$: the half-dimensional self-orthogonal case.

Why it matters

Lagrangians are the central objects of the whole subject.

Printed pp. 1-6Subspaces and the Symplectic Orthogonal
Introduce the orthogonal of a subspace under the form and the dimension count that always holds. The four names follow from comparing a subspace to its orthogonal. Lagrangian, the half-dimensional self-orthogonal case, is the one that dominates the subject, so flag it as the case to remember.

Symplectic Manifolds

Smooth two-form

$\omega$ assigns a skew bilinear form to each tangent space, varying smoothly.

Nondegenerate

At every point $p$, the form $\omega_p$ has no kernel.

Closed

$d\omega=0$: the differential condition that powers Darboux's theorem.

Definition

$$d\omega = 0,\qquad \omega_p \text{ nondegenerate } \forall p$$

Printed pp. 1-6Symplectic Manifolds
Now go smooth. A symplectic manifold carries a two-form that is closed and nondegenerate at every point. Explain why both conditions matter: nondegeneracy is the pointwise linear-algebra input, closedness is the differential condition that later yields Darboux's theorem and the whole rigidity of the theory.

First Examples

$\mathbb{R}^{2n}$

The Euclidean model with $\omega_0=\sum dx_i\wedge dy_i$.

Oriented surfaces

Any area form on a surface is automatically closed and nondegenerate.

The sphere $S^2$

$\omega_p(u,v)=\langle p,\,u\times v\rangle$ pairs tangents by the triple product.

Printed pp. 1-6First Examples
Give the three founding examples. Euclidean space with the standard form, any oriented surface with an area form, and the two-sphere whose form pairs tangent vectors by the triple product with the outward normal. The sphere is the first genuinely curved example, so let students dwell on it.

Symplectic Volume and Symplectomorphisms

Symplectic volume

The top power gives a nowhere-vanishing volume form.

Volume form

$$\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\neq 0 \;\Rightarrow\; M \text{ is orientable}$$

Symplectomorphism

A diffeomorphism $\varphi$ pulling one form back to the other.

Form-preserving map

$$\varphi^{*}\omega_2 = \omega_1$$

Linear test

The derivative satisfies $A^{\top}\Omega A=\Omega$; the residual must vanish.

Printed pp. 1-6Volume and Symplectomorphisms
Two payoffs of nondegeneracy. The top power of omega divided by n factorial is a nowhere-vanishing volume form, so symplectic manifolds are orientable. A symplectomorphism is a diffeomorphism pulling one form back to the other, and its linearization must preserve the pairing, which the matrix residual checks.
