Setup and Hypotheses

The data

$(M,\omega)$ compact connected; effective Hamiltonian action of $T^k=\mathbb{T}^k$.

Moment map

$\mu:M\to\mathbb{R}^k\cong(\mathfrak{t}^k)^*$ is $T^k$-invariant.

Defining property

$d\langle\mu,X\rangle = \iota_{X^\#}\omega,\quad X\in\mathfrak{t}^k$

Why each hypothesis

Compactness bounds the image; effectiveness fixes the polytope dimension.

Emphasize that every hypothesis earns its keep. Compactness and connectedness keep the image bounded and convex, the torus makes components of the moment map commute, and effectiveness pins the polytope dimension. Stress that the codomain is the dual of the torus Lie algebra, here identified with real Euclidean space.

The Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg Theorem

Theorem

Let $T^k$ act effectively and Hamiltonianly on compact connected $(M,\omega)$ with moment map $\mu$. Then:

(1) the fibers $\mu^{-1}(c)$ are connected;

(2) $\mu(M)$ is a convex polytope.

The polytope

$\mu(M) = \mathrm{conv}\,\big(\mu(\mathrm{Fix})\big)$

Finite data

The whole image is recovered from images of the fixed-point set $F$.

Printed pp. 168-174The AGS Theorem
Read the two conclusions slowly. Connected fibers is the analytic heart; the convex polytope image is the visible payoff. The punchline is that the polytope equals the convex hull of the finite set of fixed-point images, so a finite amount of data determines the entire moment picture for the action.

Local Structure Near a Fixed Point

Linearized action

Near $p\in F$ the action is rotations in symplectic planes with integer weights $\alpha_j$.

Moment map locally

$\mu(z) \approx \mu(p) - \tfrac12\sum_j \alpha_j\,|z_j|^2$

Printed pp. 168-174Local Normal Form
Near a fixed point the action linearizes into rotations in symplectic planes with integer weights. The moment map becomes an affine projection in those squared radial coordinates, so locally the image is a corner of a cone. This local model is what makes the global components behave like Morse functions.

Components as Morse-Bott Functions

Generic component

$\mu^X = \langle\mu,X\rangle,\quad X\ \text{rationally indep.}$

Critical set

$\mathrm{Crit}(\mu^X)=$ fixed-point set; critical manifolds are Morse-Bott.

Even index

The local normal form forces every index and coindex to be even.

Printed pp. 168-174Morse-Bott Components
Pick a generic direction and pair it with the moment map to get a real function. Its critical set is the fixed-point set, and the local normal form forces every index and coindex to be even. Even indices mean level sets never disconnect, which is the connectedness engine driving the whole proof.

Connectedness by Induction

Base case

$A_1$: a single component $\mu^X$ has connected level sets (even-index Morse theory).

Subtorus step

An injective $A\in\mathbb{Z}^{k\times(k-1)}$ selects a codimension-one subtorus.

Induction $A_{k-1}\Rightarrow A_k$

$(A^{\top}\mu)^{-1}(\xi)\ \text{connected}\ \Rightarrow\ \mu^{-1}(c)\ \text{connected}$

Climb

Connectedness propagates from circles up to the full $T^k$.

Printed pp. 168-174Connectedness of Fibers
Walk through the induction. Connectedness for subtori of dimension one comes from Morse theory with even indices. An integer matrix selects a codimension-one subtorus, and connectedness of its projected fibers feeds the next dimension. The induction climbs from circles up to the full torus.

From Connectedness to Convexity

Slice

A connected projected fiber maps under $\mu$ onto a line in $\mathbb{R}^k$.

Segment property

$p,q\in\mu(M)\ \Rightarrow\ [p,q]\subset\mu(M)$

Printed pp. 168-174Convexity from Slicing
Here connectedness becomes geometry. A connected projected fiber maps under the moment map onto a line segment, because a connected subset of a line containing two endpoints contains everything between. Sliding the slicing direction sweeps these segments across the image, proving the whole image is convex.

The Image is the Fixed-Point Hull

Separating functional

For rationally independent $X$, $\mu^X$ attains its max on a fixed component.

Containment

$\max_M \langle\mu,X\rangle = \max_{C_j}\langle\eta_j,X\rangle$

No outsiders

No image point lies beyond the hull of $\{\eta_j=\mu(C_j)\}$.

Conclusion

Convex image $+$ supporting fixed values $\Rightarrow\ \mu(M)=\mathrm{conv}(\mu(F))$.

Printed pp. 168-174Fixed-Point Hull
Explain the converse containment. A separating functional in a rationally independent direction attains its maximum on a fixed-point component, so no point outside the convex hull of fixed-point images can lie in the image. Together with convexity this pins the polytope exactly as that hull.

Example: $S^2$ and $\mathbb{CP}^1$

Height map

$S^2$ rotating about its axis; $\mu=h$ the height function.

Image

$\mu(S^2)=[-1,1],\quad \mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2$

Printed pp. 168-174Sphere and CP^1
The height function on the two-sphere is the moment map for the rotation circle, with image the interval between the south and north pole values. The same picture is the standard circle action on complex projective line. One interval, two fixed points at its endpoints, the simplest convexity instance.

Effectiveness and Recap

Vertex bound

Effective action $\Rightarrow$ nondegenerate polytope with $\ge k+1$ vertices.

Dimension bound

$\dim M \ge 2k$ (orbits are isotropic)

Logic chain

Even indices $\to$ connected fibers $\to$ convex image $\to$ fixed-point hull.
