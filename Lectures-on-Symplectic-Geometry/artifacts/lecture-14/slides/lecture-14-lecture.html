Dolbeault Theory

From an almost complex structure to a bigraded calculus of forms

The splitting

$J$ cuts $TM\otimes\mathbb{C}$ into $\pm i$ eigenspaces $T^{1,0}\oplus T^{0,1}$.

The grading

Complex forms acquire a type $(p,q)$; $d$ splits as $\partial+\bar\partial$.

The payoff

Holomorphicity is $\bar\partial f=0$; cohomology becomes Dolbeault $H^{p,q}$.

Complexifying the Tangent Space

Over the reals

An almost complex structure is $J:TM\to TM$ with $J^2=-\mathrm{Id}$. It has no real eigenvalues.

Over $\mathbb{C}$

Extend $J$ complex-linearly to $TM\otimes\mathbb{C}$. Now $J$ is diagonalizable.

Eigenvalues appear

$$J^2=-\mathrm{Id}\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathrm{spec}(J)=\{+i,\,-i\}$$

Why complexify

Tensoring with $\mathbb{C}$ is the device that turns a rotation-like $J$ into a clean eigenspace decomposition.

Printed pp. 76-80Complexifying the Tangent Space
We tensor the real tangent space with the complex numbers so that the operator J, which squares to minus the identity, can be diagonalized. Over the reals J has no eigenvalues; over the complexification it gains the eigenvalues plus i and minus i, and those eigenspaces are the whole story.

The Eigenspace Splitting

Projection formulas

$$P^{1,0}=\tfrac12(\mathrm{Id}-iJ),\quad P^{0,1}=\tfrac12(\mathrm{Id}+iJ)$$

Complementary idempotents

$(P^{1,0})^2=P^{1,0}$, $P^{1,0}+P^{0,1}=\mathrm{Id}$, $P^{1,0}P^{0,1}=0$.

Pointwise

Valid for any almost complex $J$ — no integrability needed yet.

Printed pp. 76-80The Eigenspace Splitting
The projections P10 and P01 are built directly from J as one half of the identity minus or plus i times J. They are complementary idempotents whose images are the plus i and minus i eigenspaces. Emphasize that this splitting is pointwise linear algebra valid for any almost complex structure.

Dual Splitting of One-Forms

Dualize

The cotangent space inherits $T^*M\otimes\mathbb{C}=\Lambda^{1,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,1}$.

In a complex chart

$$\Lambda^{1,0}=\langle dz^j\rangle,\qquad \Lambda^{0,1}=\langle d\bar z^j\rangle$$

A one-form decomposes

$\alpha=\alpha^{1,0}+\alpha^{0,1}$: a holomorphic-type piece plus an antiholomorphic-type piece.

Coordinate one-forms

$$dz^j=dx^j+i\,dy^j,\quad d\bar z^j=dx^j-i\,dy^j$$

Printed pp. 76-80Dual Splitting of One-Forms
Dualizing the tangent splitting gives the cotangent decomposition into the one-zero and zero-one parts. A complex-valued one-form is the sum of a holomorphic-type and an antiholomorphic-type piece. In a complex chart these are spanned by the differentials of the coordinates and their conjugates.

Forms of Type $(p,q)$

Wedge powers

Extend the splitting to higher degree by taking exterior products of the two pieces.

Bigraded decomposition

$$\Lambda^k(T^*M\otimes\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\Lambda^{p,q}$$

What a $(p,q)$-form is

$p$ holomorphic legs $dz$ and $q$ antiholomorphic legs $d\bar z$.

Local model

$$\beta=\sum b_{IJ}\,dz^I\wedge d\bar z^J,\;\; |I|=p,\,|J|=q$$

Printed pp. 76-80Forms of Type (p,q)
Taking wedge powers extends the splitting to all degrees. A form of type p,q has p holomorphic legs and q antiholomorphic legs, and the complex k-forms decompose as the direct sum over p plus q equal to k. This bigrading is the coordinate system for everything that follows.

The Bigraded Diamond

One grid, all types

Index $\Lambda^{p,q}$ by $p$ across and $q$ down. Total degree $k=p+q$ runs along anti-diagonals.

Operators as arrows

Everything we define next is read as a move between neighboring nodes.

Printed pp. 76-80The Bigraded Diamond
Lay the type spaces out on a grid indexed by p horizontally and q vertically. This is the Hodge diamond picture. Every operator we meet will be read as an arrow on this grid, so getting students comfortable moving between a node and its neighbors pays off immediately.

Splitting the Exterior Derivative

Project $d$ by type

Apply $d$ to a $(p,q)$-form and read off each type component of the result.

General almost complex case

$$d:\Lambda^{p,q}\to\Lambda^{p+2,q-1}\!\oplus\Lambda^{p+1,q}\!\oplus\Lambda^{p,q+1}\!\oplus\Lambda^{p-1,q+2}$$

Two terms vanish

The $(p+2,q-1)$ and $(p-1,q+2)$ pieces die exactly when $J$ is integrable.

What survives

The two middle pieces: $\partial$ (raises $p$) and $\bar\partial$ (raises $q$).

Printed pp. 76-80Splitting the Exterior Derivative
Apply d to a p,q form and project the result onto each type component. In general d lands in four neighboring boxes, but two of them vanish exactly when the structure is integrable. The surviving pieces are partial, raising p, and dbar, raising q.

The Dolbeault Operators

Definitions

$$\partial=\pi^{p+1,q}\!\circ d,\qquad \bar\partial=\pi^{p,q+1}\!\circ d$$

Integrable case

$$d=\partial+\bar\partial$$

$\partial$ raises $p$

$\partial:\Lambda^{p,q}\to\Lambda^{p+1,q}$ — moves east on the diamond.

$\bar\partial$ raises $q$

$\bar\partial:\Lambda^{p,q}\to\Lambda^{p,q+1}$ — moves south on the diamond.

Local form

$\bar\partial b=\sum\partial_{\bar z^k}b\,\,d\bar z^k\wedge\cdots$

Printed pp. 76-80The Dolbeault Operators
Define partial as the type p plus one, q component of d and dbar as the type p, q plus one component. In the integrable case d equals partial plus dbar with no extra terms. On the grid partial moves east and dbar moves south, exactly as the lattice figure shows.

$J$-Holomorphic Functions

The test

$$f \text{ is } J\text{-holomorphic} \iff \bar\partial f=0$$

Cauchy-Riemann

In a chart, $\bar\partial f=\tfrac12(\partial_x f+i\,\partial_y f)\,d\bar z$. Vanishing recovers the classical equations.

Symbolic checks

$z^2$ is killed by $\bar\partial$; $\bar z^2$ is killed by $\partial$; $z\bar z$ is killed by neither.

Wirtinger derivatives

$$\partial_z=\tfrac12(\partial_x-i\partial_y),\;\; \partial_{\bar z}=\tfrac12(\partial_x+i\partial_y)$$

Printed pp. 76-80J-Holomorphic Functions
On functions the test is small and powerful. A function is J-holomorphic precisely when dbar f vanishes, which in a chart is the Cauchy-Riemann condition. The symbolic checks confirm that polynomials in z are killed by dbar while polynomials in z-bar are killed by partial.

The Dolbeault Complex

From $d^2=0$ by type

$$0=d^2=\partial^2+(\partial\bar\partial+\bar\partial\partial)+\bar\partial^2$$

Separate by type

Each summand lands in a different bigraded box, so each must vanish on its own.

Consequence

$\partial^2=0,\quad \bar\partial^2=0,\quad \partial\bar\partial=-\bar\partial\partial$.

Printed pp. 76-80The Dolbeault Complex
Expanding d squared equals zero by type, and using that the cross terms vanish under integrability, forces dbar squared to equal zero. That makes each row of the diamond a cochain complex. Its cohomology is the Dolbeault cohomology, the bigraded refinement of de Rham cohomology.

Nijenhuis & Newlander-Nirenberg

The obstruction

The clean $d=\partial+\bar\partial$ is not automatic. The Nijenhuis tensor $N_J$ measures the failure.

Nijenhuis tensor

$$N_J(X,Y)=[JX,JY]-J[JX,Y]-J[X,JY]-[X,Y]$$

Bracket-closure

$N_J=0$ iff $T^{1,0}$ is closed under the Lie bracket — the involutivity condition.

Newlander-Nirenberg

$$N_J\equiv 0 \iff J \text{ comes from holomorphic charts}$$

Then

Integrable $J$ gives genuine complex coordinates and the two-term splitting.

Printed pp. 76-80Nijenhuis and Newlander-Nirenberg
The clean two-term splitting is not automatic. The Nijenhuis tensor measures the failure of the one-zero distribution to be closed under bracket, and it is the precise obstruction. Newlander-Nirenberg says its vanishing is equivalent to the existence of holomorphic charts making J integrable.

Recap & Outlook

The pipeline

  • Complexify, then split into $T^{1,0}\oplus T^{0,1}$.
  • Grade forms by type $(p,q)$ on the diamond.
  • Project $d$ into $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$.
  • Read holomorphicity as $\bar\partial f=0$.

The cohomology

When $\bar\partial^2=0$ holds, $H^{p,q}$ refines de Rham cohomology row by row.

The gate

$N_J=0$ is the hinge: it licenses complex charts and the clean Dolbeault complex.

Next

We take up the integrability tests this obstruction opens, on the way to compatible structures.
