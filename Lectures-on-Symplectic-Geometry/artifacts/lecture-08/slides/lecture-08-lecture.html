No Local Invariants

Symplectic geometry is locally "floppy"

Riemannian world

Curvature is a genuine local invariant; you cannot flatten a sphere isometrically.

Symplectic world

No curvature analogue. All symplectic forms look identical near a point.

Printed pp. 44-48No Local Invariants

Darboux's Theorem

Theorem (Darboux)

Near any $p\in(M,\omega)$ there is a chart with $$\omega = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dq_i \wedge dp_i = \omega_0.$$

Consequence

$(M,\omega)$ is locally symplectomorphic to $(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\omega_0)$.

Reading

There is one local model. Any difference between symplectic manifolds is global, never pointwise.

Strategy

Anchor the form at $p$ by linear algebra, then deform with Moser.

Printed pp. 44-48Darboux's Theorem
State Darboux precisely: around any point there are coordinates in which omega becomes the constant standard form. Emphasize that the chart is local and that the conclusion is a symplectomorphism onto an open set of standard space, so no two symplectic manifolds differ locally.

The Moser Trick

Deformation

$$\omega_t=(1-t)\omega_0+t\,\omega_1,\quad \rho_t^*\omega_t=\omega_0$$

Homological equation

$$\mathcal{L}_{v_t}\omega_t+\tfrac{d}{dt}\omega_t=0$$

Cartan

$\mathcal{L}_{v_t}\omega_t=d\,\iota_{v_t}\omega_t$, so solving reduces to a primitive of $\omega_1-\omega_0$.

Output

Integrate $v_t$ to a flow $\rho_t$ that transports $\omega_0$ to $\omega_1$.

Printed pp. 44-48The Moser Trick
Lay out Moser's deformation idea. Interpolate linearly between the two forms, demand the path of diffeomorphisms pull each back to the start, and differentiate to get a homological equation. Cartan's magic formula converts that equation into solving for a time-dependent vector field.

Moser Relative Theorem (at a point)

Statement

If $\omega_0|_p=\omega_1|_p$, a local diffeo $\rho$ with $\rho(p)=p$ satisfies $\rho^*\omega_1=\omega_0$ near $p$.

Poincare lemma

Gives a primitive $\mu$ with $\omega_1-\omega_0=d\mu$ and $\mu_p=0$.

Printed pp. 44-48Moser Relative Theorem
State the relative version: if two symplectic forms agree at a point, they are equivalent near it by a diffeomorphism fixing that point. The key input is the Poincare lemma, which produces a primitive vanishing to the needed order at the anchor point.

Proof of Darboux, Assembled

Step 1 — Anchor

A symplectic basis at $p$ makes $\omega|_p=\omega_0|_p$ after a linear change of coordinates.

Step 2 — Deform

Moser's relative theorem upgrades equality at $p$ to a chart with $\omega=\omega_0$ nearby.

Pattern

$\text{linear anchor} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Moser correction}$

Reuse

This anchor-then-deform pattern recurs for submanifolds and Weinstein.

Printed pp. 44-48Proof of Darboux
Assemble Darboux from the pieces. Pick a symplectic basis to standardize the form at the point by linear algebra, then apply the Moser relative theorem to upgrade pointwise agreement to a genuine local chart. The two-step pattern, anchor then deform, recurs throughout.

Relative Moser on a Submanifold

Theorem

If $\omega_0|_X=\omega_1|_X$ along $X$, then $\exists\,\rho$ with $\rho|_X=\mathrm{id}$ and $\rho^*\omega_1=\omega_0$ near $X$.

Anchor upgraded

The single point becomes a whole submanifold $X$.

Printed pp. 44-48Relative Moser on a Submanifold
Generalize the anchor from a point to a submanifold. If the two forms agree along all of a submanifold X, Moser produces a diffeomorphism fixing X pointwise and matching the forms nearby. The Poincare lemma is replaced by a relative version with parameters along X.

Lagrangian Subspaces

Definition

$L$ Lagrangian $\iff$ $\omega|_L=0$ and $\dim L=\tfrac12\dim V$.

Complement repair

A complement to $L$ becomes Lagrangian after symmetrizing its graph matrix $C\mapsto\tfrac12(C+C^{\!\top})$.

Why it matters

This pointwise linear algebra is the seed the submanifold Moser theorem grows into Weinstein's chart.

Obstruction

Skew part $C-C^{\!\top}$ measures failure to be Lagrangian.

Printed pp. 44-48Lagrangian Subspaces
Recall the linear model. A Lagrangian subspace is isotropic of half dimension, and a complement to one can be repaired into a Lagrangian complement by symmetrizing the graph matrix. This pointwise algebra is the seed that the submanifold Moser theorem grows into a normal form.

Toward Weinstein's Neighborhood Theorems

Lagrangian neighborhood

A tubular neighborhood of a Lagrangian $L$ is symplectomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section in $T^*L$.

Coisotropic variant

Replace the Lagrangian hypothesis with equal restricted forms and $U^\omega\subset U$.

Printed pp. 44-48Toward Weinstein
Preview the payoff. The relative Moser theorem on a submanifold is the machine behind Weinstein's neighborhood theorems: a tubular neighborhood of a Lagrangian looks like its cotangent bundle. Mention the coisotropic variant, where equal restricted forms and orthogonal containment replace the Lagrangian hypothesis.

Darboux

Coordinates $(q_i,p_i)$ flatten any $\omega$ to the standard form.

Moser trick

Deform along $\omega_t$; the relative theorem anchors at a point or along $X$.

Weinstein next

Submanifold Moser drives the Lagrangian and coisotropic neighborhood theorems.

Recap the chain from pages 44 to 48: no local invariants, Darboux flattening, the Moser trick, the relative theorem at a point and along a submanifold, and the setup for Weinstein.
