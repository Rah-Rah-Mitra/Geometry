The Reeb Vector Field

Definition

Given a contact form $\alpha$ on $M^{2n+1}$, the Reeb field $R$ solves two equations.

Reading the equations

$R$ lies in the kernel of $d\alpha$ and is normalized against $\alpha$.

Defining conditions

$$\iota_R\, d\alpha = 0, \qquad \alpha(R) = 1$$

Consequence

$R$ is transverse to $\xi=\ker\alpha$ and points vertically out of it.

Printed pp. 61-64The Reeb Vector Field
The Reeb field is pinned down by two linear conditions on the tangent space at each point. The interior product with d-alpha kills it, which forces it into the kernel of d-alpha, and the normalization alpha of R equals one fixes its length transverse to the contact planes. Together these give a unique R.

Existence and Uniqueness of $R$

On the planes

$d\alpha|_\xi$ is nondegenerate — that is what contact means.

The kernel line

So $\ker(d\alpha)$ in $TM$ is exactly one dimensional and transverse to $\xi$.

Dimension count

$$\dim\ker(d\alpha) = (2n{+}1) - 2n = 1$$

Pick one

Normalization $\alpha(R)=1$ selects a single vector on that line.

Printed pp. 61-64Why R Is Well Defined
Stress why the two equations have exactly one solution. On a contact manifold d-alpha restricted to the contact hyperplane is nondegenerate, so its kernel in the full tangent space is one dimensional and transverse to that hyperplane. The normalization then selects a single vector from that line, giving a globally defined smooth field.

The Flow Preserves $\alpha$

Cartan's formula

$\mathcal L_R\alpha = \iota_R d\alpha + d(\iota_R\alpha)$.

Both terms die

First term is $0$; second is $d(1)=0$.

Invariance

$$\mathcal L_R\,\alpha = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \phi_t^*\alpha = \alpha$$

Meaning

The Reeb flow $\phi_t$ is a one-parameter group of strict contactomorphisms.

Printed pp. 61-64The Reeb Flow Preserves Alpha
Run Cartan's magic formula on the Lie derivative of alpha along R. The exterior derivative term and the interior product term both vanish by the defining equations, so the Lie derivative is zero. The flow therefore preserves both alpha and d-alpha, making it a one parameter group of strict contactomorphisms.

$R$ Is Transverse to $\xi$

Two roles

Inside $\xi$, $d\alpha$ is a symplectic pairing. Across $\xi$, $R$ provides the missing direction.

Splitting

$TM = \xi \oplus \mathbb{R}R$ at every point.

Printed pp. 61-64Transverse to the Contact Planes
This is the key picture: at every point R pokes straight through the contact plane while d-alpha is nondegenerate inside the plane. Have students trace one flow line climbing through a stack of tilted planes. Emphasise that R is never tangent to the distribution, unlike Legendrian directions coming later.

Two Model Reeb Flows

Euclidean $\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}$

$\alpha = dz + \sum x_i\,dy_i \Rightarrow R = \partial_z$. Orbits are vertical lines.

Odd sphere $S^{2n+1}$

$R$ is the Hopf field rotating each $(x_i,y_i)$ plane equally. Orbits are circles.

Printed pp. 61-64Model Reeb Flows
Two anchoring examples. In standard contact Euclidean space the Reeb field is just vertical translation, so orbits are open lines climbing in z. On the odd sphere the same equations yield the Hopf field, rotating every complex coordinate at equal speed, so every orbit is a closed Hopf circle. Same recipe, very different dynamics.

Contact Vector Fields and Hamiltonians

Contact vector field

$X$ whose flow preserves $\xi$: $\mathcal L_X\alpha = \mu\,\alpha$ for some function $\mu$.

The group

Their flows fill out the contactomorphism group of $(M,\xi)$.

Contact Hamiltonian

$$X \;\longleftrightarrow\; H = \alpha(X)$$

Correspondence

$X\mapsto\alpha(X)$ is a bijection with functions $H\in C^\infty(M)$. The Reeb field is $H\equiv 1$.

Printed pp. 61-64Contact Vector Fields and Hamiltonians
Generalise from R to all contact vector fields, whose flows preserve the contact distribution up to scale. The crucial fact is that these are in bijection with smooth functions, the contact Hamiltonians, via H equals alpha of X. So the contactomorphism group is parametrised by functions, exactly as symplectic flows are generated by Hamiltonians.

Legendrian Submanifolds

Definition

$L^n\subset M^{2n+1}$ with $TL\subset\xi$: everywhere tangent to the contact planes.

Maximal isotropic

$n$ is the largest possible dimension of a $\xi$-tangent submanifold.

Contrast

Legendrians are tangent to $\xi$; Reeb orbits are transverse to it.

Printed pp. 61-64Legendrian Submanifolds
Legendrians are the contact analogue of Lagrangians: n dimensional submanifolds everywhere tangent to the contact hyperplane field, which is the maximal possible isotropic dimension. Contrast them sharply with Reeb orbits, which are always transverse. The Legendrian condition pulls alpha back to zero, and these submanifolds carry their own rich knot theory.

Symplectization

Add a coordinate

Glue a real factor $\tau$ onto $M$ to form $\mathbb{R}\times M$.

Nondegenerate

The $e^\tau$ factor makes $\omega$ symplectic across all layers.

Symplectic form

$$\omega = d\!\left(e^{\tau}\,\pi^*\alpha\right)$$

Payoff

$M$ becomes a contact-type hypersurface in a symplectic cone; Reeb dynamics is restricted Hamiltonian flow.

Printed pp. 61-64Symplectization
Symplectization is the bridge back to symplectic geometry. Adjoin a real coordinate tau and form omega as the exterior derivative of e-to-the-tau times alpha. A short computation shows omega is nondegenerate, so the contact manifold becomes a hypersurface in a symplectic cone and Reeb dynamics is the restricted Hamiltonian flow on that level.

Periodic Orbits and the Weinstein Conjecture

Seifert's question

Closed orbits for arbitrary fields on $S^3$? — has counterexamples.

Weinstein conjecture

Every contact form on a closed $M$ has a closed Reeb orbit.

Landmark cases

Hofer, Viterbo; Taubes proved it in dimension three.

Printed pp. 61-64Reeb Orbits and Weinstein
Periodic Reeb orbits are the central objects. Seifert asked about closed orbits for general vector fields and there are counterexamples, but the Weinstein conjecture concerns the rigid Reeb fields of contact forms on closed manifolds and predicts a closed orbit always exists. Mention Hofer's and Viterbo's landmark cases and Taubes's three dimensional theorem.

Geodesic Flow as a Reeb Flow

The setting

On the unit cotangent bundle $S^*Q$ the tautological form $\lambda$ restricts to a contact form.

The punchline

Its Reeb flow is exactly the geodesic flow of the metric on $Q$.

Correspondence

$$\text{Reeb orbits on } S^*Q \;\longleftrightarrow\; \text{geodesics on } Q$$

Consequence

Closed Reeb orbits are closed geodesics — Weinstein-type results recover classical existence theorems.

Printed pp. 61-64Geodesic Flow as a Reeb Flow
The flagship example: on the unit cotangent bundle the canonical one form restricts to a contact form whose Reeb flow is exactly the geodesic flow of the base metric. Closed Reeb orbits become closed geodesics, so Weinstein type existence results recover and generalise classical theorems on the existence of periodic geodesics.
