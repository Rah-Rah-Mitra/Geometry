What Is a Symplectic Toric Manifold?

Definition

A compact connected symplectic $(M^{2n},\omega)$ with an effective Hamiltonian action of $T^n$ and moment map $\mu$.

Why half-dimensional?

$\dim T^n = \tfrac12 \dim M$ is the largest torus that can act with isotropic orbits.

Effective

No nontrivial subtorus acts trivially, so symmetry is fully used.

Data

$(M^{2n},\omega,\,T^n,\,\mu)$

$\mu: M \to (\mathfrak{t}^n)^* \cong \mathbb{R}^n$

Printed pp. 175-180What Is a Symplectic Toric Manifold
Fix the objects we classify. A symplectic toric manifold is a compact connected symplectic manifold of dimension twice n, carrying an effective Hamiltonian action of the n-torus with a moment map. Effectiveness and the half-dimensional torus are exactly what make the action rigid enough to be encoded combinatorially without losing information.

The Moment Image Is a Polytope

Convexity

By Atiyah-Guillemin-Sternberg, $\mu(M)$ is a convex polytope.

Vertices

It is the convex hull of the images of the fixed points of the action.

Fibres

Each face is the image of a connected isotropy submanifold.

Convexity theorem

$\mu(M)=\operatorname{conv}\big(\mu(M^{T^n})\big)$

Printed pp. 175-180The Moment Image Is a Polytope
By the Atiyah and Guillemin-Sternberg convexity theorem, the moment map image of a compact connected Hamiltonian torus space is a convex polytope, the convex hull of the images of the fixed points. For toric manifolds this polytope lives in the dual of the torus Lie algebra and carries all the data we need.

Delzant Polytopes: Three Local Conditions

Simple

Exactly $n$ edges meet at each vertex.

controls degree of corners

Rational

Edge directions are lattice vectors $u_i \in \mathbb{Z}^n$.

controls slopes

Smooth

Primitive edge vectors at each vertex form a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis.

unimodular corner

Delzant polytope

simple $\;\wedge\;$ rational $\;\wedge\;$ smooth, at every vertex

All three tests are independent and purely local to a vertex.

Printed pp. 175-180Delzant Polytopes: Three Conditions
Not every polytope arises. A Delzant polytope satisfies three local conditions at every vertex: simplicity, meaning exactly n edges meet there; rationality, meaning those edge directions are lattice vectors; and smoothness, meaning the primitive edge vectors form a basis of the integer lattice. These tests are independent and entirely local.

Smoothness = Unimodular Corners

Smooth vertex test

$\det[\,u_1\ \cdots\ u_n\,] = \pm 1$

Pass (left)

Edge vectors $\binom{1}{0},\binom{0}{1}$ give $\det=1$. The corner is a $\mathbb{Z}^2$ basis.

Fail (right)

Edge vectors $\binom{1}{0},\binom{1}{2}$ give $\det=2$. Rational, but not smooth.

Printed pp. 175-180Smoothness as Unimodularity
Smoothness is the subtle condition, so make it concrete. At each vertex the n primitive edge vectors must span the full integer lattice, equivalently their matrix has determinant plus or minus one. In the plane this is the determinant test on adjacent primitive normals. The right-hand corner with edge slope two fails: its determinant is two.

Half-Spaces and Primitive Normals

Facet presentation

$\Delta=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:\ \langle x,v_i\rangle\le \lambda_i\}$

$v_i$ primitive outward normal, $\lambda_i\in\mathbb{R}$, one per facet.

Standard simplex

$x_1\ge0,\ x_2\ge0,\ x_1+x_2\le1$.

Normals

$v_1{=}(-1,0),\ v_2{=}(0,-1),\ v_3{=}(1,1)$.

Printed pp. 175-180Half-Space Description
The construction never starts from a drawn polygon; it starts from data. Write the polytope as an intersection of half-spaces, one primitive outward normal vector and one real constant per facet. For the standard simplex the three inequalities x one at least zero, x two at least zero, and their sum at most one give the normals minus e one, minus e two, and one one.

The Delzant Theorem

Classification

$\left\{\substack{\text{symplectic toric}\\\text{manifolds}}\right\}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ \left\{\substack{\text{Delzant}\\\text{polytopes}}\right\}$

$(M,\omega,T^n,\mu)\ \longmapsto\ \mu(M)$

Left equivalence

Equivariant symplectomorphism.

Right equivalence

Translation in $(\mathfrak{t}^n)^*$.

Printed pp. 175-180The Delzant Theorem
Here is the classification. The map sending a symplectic toric manifold to its moment image is a bijection onto Delzant polytopes, with equivariant symplectomorphisms on the left and lattice-preserving translations on the right. Two toric manifolds are equivalent exactly when their polytopes agree up to translation. Combinatorics fully captures the symplectic geometry.

Surjectivity: Building $M_\Delta$

Step 1

$d$ facets give normals $v_1,\dots,v_d$.

Step 2

Define $\pi:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^n,\ e_i\mapsto v_i$.

Step 3

Kernel $\Rightarrow$ subtorus $N\subset T^d$.

Step 4

Reduce $\mathbb{C}^d$ by $N$ at level $0$.

Printed pp. 175-180From Polytope to Manifold
Surjectivity is proved by an explicit construction, detailed next lecture. The d facet normals define a map pi from R to the d-th power onto R to the n-th, sending each basis vector to a normal. Its kernel exponentiates to a subtorus N of the standard d-torus, and reducing the model space C to the d at level zero builds the manifold with the prescribed image.

The Exact Sequence Behind $N$

Short exact sequence

$0\to\mathfrak{n}\to\mathbb{R}^d\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathbb{R}^n\to 0$

$\pi(e_i)=v_i$; smoothness $\Leftrightarrow\ \pi(\mathbb{Z}^d)=\mathbb{Z}^n$.

Onto + lattice

$\operatorname{rank}\pi=n$, $\dim\mathfrak{n}=d-n$.

Dual sequence

Gives $\iota^*$ to restrict the $\mathbb{C}^d$ moment map.

Printed pp. 175-180The Exact Sequence
The construction is governed by a short exact sequence. The map pi is onto and carries the integer lattice onto the integer lattice, precisely the Delzant smoothness condition. Its kernel n is the Lie algebra of the subtorus we reduce by; the dual sequence supplies the projection used to restrict the standard moment map of C to the d.

Worked Example: $\Delta=[0,a]\Rightarrow \mathbb{CP}^1$

Data

Two normals $v_1{=}-1,\ v_2{=}+1$; $\pi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$.

Subtorus

$\ker\pi=\operatorname{span}(1,1)\Rightarrow N=$ diagonal $S^1$.

Zero level

$|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=2a$

Quotient

Sphere $\big/$ diagonal $S^1=\mathbb{CP}^1$.

Printed pp. 175-180Interval to CP^1
Run the smallest case. The interval from zero to a has two normals, minus one and plus one, so pi from R two to R has kernel the diagonal line. The subtorus is the diagonal circle in the two-torus, the zero level is a sphere of radius root two a in C squared, and quotienting by that circle yields complex projective one.

Printed pp. 175-180A Dictionary of Examples
Collect the dictionary. The standard n-simplex labels complex projective n-space, a unit square labels the product of two projective lines, and shearing or truncating that square produces the Hirzebruch surfaces. Reading off vertices, edges, and lattice lengths recovers fixed points, invariant spheres, and symplectic areas directly from the picture.
