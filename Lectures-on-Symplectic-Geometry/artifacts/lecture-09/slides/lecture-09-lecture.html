Weinstein Tubular Neighborhood Theorem

The symplectic germ near a Lagrangian is determined by $L$ alone

Central claim

A tubular neighborhood of a Lagrangian $L \subset (M,\omega)$ is symplectomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section in $T^*L$.

Consequence

No local symplectic invariants beyond $L$ itself: the model depends only on the diffeomorphism type of $L$.

Printed pp. 49-54Weinstein's Lagrangian Neighborhood
Open by framing the question for printed pages 49 through 54: how much of the symplectic geometry near a Lagrangian is determined by the Lagrangian alone. The answer is essentially all of it. A tubular neighborhood is symplectomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section in the cotangent bundle, so the germ depends only on the diffeomorphism type of the base.

The Linear Observation

Setup

Let $U \subset (V,\Omega)$ be a Lagrangian subspace of a symplectic vector space.

Nondegenerate pairing

$$V/U \times U \to \mathbb{R}, \quad ([v],u)\mapsto \Omega(v,u)$$

Well defined

Because $U$ is Lagrangian, $\Omega(u',u)=0$ for $u'\in U$, so the pairing descends to the quotient.

Printed pp. 49-54The Linear Observation
Stress that the whole theorem rests on one piece of linear algebra. When a subspace is Lagrangian, the symplectic form gives a nondegenerate pairing between the quotient and the subspace itself. That pairing identifies the quotient with the dual space. Verify with students that adding a vector inside the Lagrangian leaves the pairing unchanged, so it descends to the quotient cleanly.

Normal Bundle $\cong$ Cotangent Bundle

Globalize

Apply the pairing fiber by fiber along $L$. Each $V/U$ becomes the dual of the tangent space $T_pL$.

Canonical isomorphism

$$NL \;\cong\; T^*L$$

No choices

The identification uses only $\omega$ — no metric, no complement is selected.

Printed pp. 49-54Normal Bundle Equals Cotangent
Globalize the linear fact fiber by fiber. The symplectic form trivializes the symplectic orthogonal, so the normal bundle of a Lagrangian is canonically the cotangent bundle of that Lagrangian. This is the model space the theorem will match. Emphasize canonical: no choice of metric or complement is needed, the identification comes straight from omega.

The Cotangent Model

Tautological form

On $T^*L$ define $\alpha$ pointwise; in coordinates $\alpha=\sum \xi_i\,dx^i$.

Canonical symplectic form

$$\omega_0 = -d\alpha = \sum_i dx^i\wedge d\xi_i$$

Zero section

$L \hookrightarrow T^*L$ as $\xi=0$ is Lagrangian for $\omega_0$.

Printed pp. 49-54The Cotangent Model
Recall the tautological and canonical forms on the cotangent bundle so the model side is concrete. The zero section is Lagrangian, and its neighborhood carries the standard form. Students should leave this slide knowing exactly what they are mapping into: a neighborhood of the zero section equipped with minus d of the tautological one-form.

Weinstein Lagrangian Neighborhood Theorem

Theorem

There exist neighborhoods $\mathcal{U}\supset L$ in $M$ and $\mathcal{U}_0\supset L$ in $T^*L$, and a symplectomorphism $$\varphi:(\mathcal{U},\omega)\to(\mathcal{U}_0,\omega_0)$$ with $\varphi|_L=\mathrm{id}_L$.

Reading

The map fixes $L$ pointwise and carries $\omega$ to the standard cotangent form.

Upgrade

Refines the ordinary tubular neighborhood theorem: it preserves $\omega$, not only the smooth neighborhood structure.

Uniqueness flavor

Any two symplectic neighborhoods of $L$ are equivalent rel $L$, so $L$ alone fixes the local model.

Printed pp. 49-54Statement of the Theorem
State the Weinstein Lagrangian neighborhood theorem precisely. Given a Lagrangian in any symplectic manifold, there is a symplectomorphism from a neighborhood of it onto a neighborhood of the zero section that restricts to the identity on the Lagrangian. Underline that this upgrades the ordinary tubular neighborhood theorem by preserving the symplectic form, not just the smooth structure.

Proof via Relative Moser

Step 1 — model

Use $NL\cong T^*L$ and the tubular theorem to get a diffeomorphism $\psi$ taking $L$ to the zero section.

Step 2 — two forms

Compare $\omega_1=\psi_*\omega$ with $\omega_0$. They agree along $L$ and on the conormal directions.

Step 3 — interpolate

Set $\omega_t=(1-t)\omega_0+t\,\omega_1$, symplectic near $L$.

Relative Moser

$$\iota_{X_t}\omega_t=-\sigma,\qquad d\sigma=\omega_1-\omega_0$$

Outcome

Since $\omega_1-\omega_0$ vanishes on $L$, choose $\sigma$ vanishing on $L$; the flow $\rho_t$ fixes $L$ and gives $\rho_1^*\omega_1=\omega_0$.

Printed pp. 49-54Proof via Relative Moser
Walk through the proof architecture. Build two forms agreeing on the Lagrangian using the cotangent model, then interpolate. The relative Moser theorem produces an isotopy fixing the Lagrangian and carrying one form to the other. Highlight that agreement along the submanifold, plus matching on the conormal directions, is exactly what relative Moser needs to start.

Nearby Lagrangians as Closed One-Forms

Graph of a one-form

A one-form $\mu$ on $L$ is a section $x\mapsto(x,\mu_x)$ of $T^*L$; its image is a submanifold.

Lagrangian criterion

$$\mathrm{Graph}(\mu)\text{ Lagrangian}\iff d\mu=0$$

Why

Pull back $\alpha$ along the section to get $\mu$; then $\mu^*\omega_0=-d\mu$.

Printed pp. 49-54Nearby Lagrangians as One-Forms
Now harvest the payoff. Inside the cotangent model, a section is a one-form on the base, and its graph is a submanifold. A short computation with the tautological form shows the graph is Lagrangian precisely when the one-form is closed. So Lagrangians near the zero section correspond exactly to closed one-forms on the base.

Intersections = Zeros = Critical Points

Intersection

$\mathrm{Graph}(\mu)\cap L = \{x:\mu_x=0\}$ — the nearby Lagrangian meets $L$ where $\mu$ vanishes.

When $H^1_{dR}(L)=0$

$$d\mu=0 \implies \mu=dh \implies \mu_x=0 \iff dh_x=0$$

Morse bound

Intersections $\ge$ number of critical points of $h$ forced by the topology of $L$.

Printed pp. 49-54Intersections Are Zeros
Connect geometry to analysis. A nearby Lagrangian meets the zero section exactly where its defining one-form vanishes. When the first de Rham cohomology vanishes, closed forms are exact, so the form is the differential of a function and intersection points are critical points of that function. Morse theory then bounds the number of intersections from below.

Symplectomorphisms Near the Diagonal

Product trick

On $M\times M$ use the twisted form $\mathrm{pr}_1^*\omega-\mathrm{pr}_2^*\omega$. The diagonal $\Delta$ is Lagrangian.

Graph criterion

$$\mathrm{Graph}(f)\text{ Lagrangian}\iff f^*\omega=\omega$$

Fixed points

$\mathrm{Graph}(f)\cap\Delta=\mathrm{Fix}(f)$; near $\Delta$ this becomes $\mu=0$.

Printed pp. 49-54Graphs Near the Diagonal
Apply the same machinery to symplectomorphisms. In the product with the twisted form, the diagonal is Lagrangian and so is the graph of any symplectomorphism. A graph close to the diagonal becomes a one-form near the zero section, closed exactly when the map is symplectic. Fixed points are where the graph meets the diagonal, hence zeros of the form.

The Lagrangian Creed

Weinstein's slogan

Everything is a Lagrangian submanifold.

Reframing

Maps, fixed-point problems, and generating functions all become Lagrangians and their intersections.

Dictionary

  • symplectomorphism $\leftrightarrow$ Lagrangian graph in $M\times M$
  • nearby map $\leftrightarrow$ closed one-form $\mu$
  • fixed point $\leftrightarrow$ zero of $\mu$ / critical point

Payoff

One geometric language organizes the constructions of the lectures that follow.

Printed pp. 49-54The Lagrangian Creed
Articulate Weinstein's creed: everything is a Lagrangian submanifold. Symplectomorphisms, fixed-point problems, and generating functions all become statements about Lagrangians and their intersections. This reframing is the organizing philosophy behind much of the field and motivates the constructions of the following lectures.

Toward Arnold and Floer

Near-identity count

A $C^1$-small symplectomorphism of $M$ with $H^1=0$ has at least as many fixed points as a function on $M$ has critical points.

Arnold conjecture

$$\#\mathrm{Fix}(\phi^1_H)\;\ge\;\sum_k \dim H_k(M;\mathbb{R})$$

Orbits

Fixed points of the Hamiltonian time-one map correspond to period-one orbits of the isotopy.

Floer

Floer homology makes the Morse-type lower bound rigorous beyond the near-identity case.

Recap

From a linear pairing to global intersection bounds in one chain.

Printed pp. 49-54Toward Arnold and Floer
Close by pointing forward. The near-identity fixed-point count generalizes: Arnold conjectured that a Hamiltonian time-one map has at least as many fixed points as a function on the manifold has critical points, with period-one orbits playing the role of intersections. Floer homology makes this rigorous. Recap the chain from the linear pairing to these global bounds.
