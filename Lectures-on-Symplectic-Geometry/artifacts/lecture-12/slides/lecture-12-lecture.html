Three Linear Geometries on One Space

Symplectic

A skew, nondegenerate form $\omega(u,v)=-\omega(v,u)$.

Riemannian

A symmetric, positive form $g(u,v)=g(v,u)>0$.

Complex

A map with $J^2=-\mathrm{Id}$, so $i\cdot v := Jv$.

The braiding relation

$$g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv)$$

Thesis

Compatibility ties the three together: pick any two and the third is forced.

Printed pp. 65-71Three Linear Geometries
Open by contrasting three structures on a single vector space: a skew nondegenerate form, a symmetric positive metric, and a square root of minus one. Each is familiar alone. The lecture's thesis is that compatibility braids them so any two determine the third, and that is the picture students should carry forward.

Definition: Almost Complex Structure

Definition

$$J\in\mathrm{End}(TM),\qquad J^2=-\mathrm{Id}_{TM}$$

Pointwise

On each tangent space $T_pM$, the map $J_p$ varies smoothly with $p$.

Complex vector space

Setting $(a+bi)\,v=a\,v+b\,Jv$ makes $T_pM$ a complex vector space.

Consequence

$\dim_{\mathbb R}M$ is even; $J$ exists only on even-dimensional manifolds.

Printed pp. 65-71Definition of J
Define an almost complex structure as a smooth bundle map J on the tangent spaces with J squared equal to minus the identity. Emphasize that this is pointwise linear algebra varying smoothly, and that it makes each tangent space a complex vector space where multiplication by i is the action of J.

$J$ as a Quarter Turn

Read the loop

$J$ turns each vector a quarter circle counterclockwise.

Why $J^2=-\mathrm{Id}$

$J(Jv)=-v$ for every $v$.

Model

On $\mathbb R^2$, $J=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}$.

Printed pp. 65-71J Rotates by 90 Degrees
Use the picture to make J concrete: it sends a tangent vector to a perpendicular one, and applying it twice reverses the vector. Walk the class around the loop v, Jv, minus v, minus Jv. This ninety-degree quarter turn is the geometric heart of the identity J squared equals minus the identity.

Taming and Compatibility

$\omega$-tame

$$\omega(v,Jv)>0\quad\forall\,v\neq 0$$

Taming

$J$ pairs positively with $\omega$; no preservation required yet.

$\omega$-compatible

$$\omega(Jv,Jw)=\omega(v,w)\;\text{ and }\;\omega(v,Jv)>0$$

Compatible

Tame and $J$ preserves $\omega$. Compatible $\Rightarrow$ tame.

Printed pp. 65-71Taming and Compatibility
Introduce the two conditions relating J to omega. Taming asks only that omega of v with Jv be strictly positive on nonzero vectors. Compatibility adds that J preserves omega, meaning omega of Jv and Jw equals omega of v and w. Compatible structures are exactly the tame ones that are also symplectic.

Compatible $J$ Builds a Metric

Riemannian metric

$$g(v,w)=\omega(v,Jw)$$

Symmetric

$g(w,v)=g(v,w)$ follows from $\omega(Jv,Jw)=\omega(v,w)$.

Positive

$g(v,v)=\omega(v,Jv)>0$ by taming.

Printed pp. 65-71The Compatible Metric
Here is the payoff: a compatible J turns omega into a Riemannian metric by g of v and w equals omega of v with Jw. Symmetry comes from compatibility, and positive definiteness is precisely the taming inequality. Show students the closed triangle where omega, g, and J each recover from the other two.

Positivity Becomes Definiteness

Diagonal value

$$g(v,v)=\omega(v,Jv)>0$$

Definite

No nonzero vector is null; $g$ is a true inner product.

Witness

Numerically, $\min\,\mathrm{eig}(g)>0$ certifies positivity.

Printed pp. 65-71Positivity Becomes a Metric
Dwell on why positivity matters. The diagonal value g of v with itself equals omega of v with Jv, which the taming condition forces to be positive. Geometrically the unit balls of g are honest ellipses. Connect this to the notebook, where the minimum eigenvalue of the compatible metric is the computational witness of positivity.

Existence: Polar Decomposition

Theorem

Every symplectic manifold $(M,\omega)$ admits compatible almost complex structures.

Construction

$$\omega(u,v)=g(Au,v),\quad J=(\sqrt{A^{*}A})^{-1}A$$

Printed pp. 65-71Existence via Polar Decomposition
State the existence theorem: every symplectic manifold admits compatible almost complex structures. Sketch the proof. Choose any metric, represent omega by a skew-adjoint operator A, take the positive square root of A times its adjoint, and normalize. Polar decomposition canonically extracts J from that factorization.

The Space of Compatible $J$ is Contractible

Not unique

Many compatible $J$ exist; they form a space $\mathcal{J}(M,\omega)$.

Continuous in $g$

The polar construction is canonical given the chosen metric.

Convex input

Riemannian metrics form a convex set with no obstruction.

Key fact

$$\mathcal{J}(M,\omega)\ \text{is non-empty and contractible}$$

Why it matters

A compatible $J$ behaves like a canonical choice in later constructions.

Printed pp. 65-71The Space of Compatible J
Explain that compatible structures are not unique but form a contractible space. The construction depends continuously on the chosen metric, and the metrics form a convex set, so the compatible J's deform into one another without obstruction. Contractibility is what lets us treat a compatible J as essentially canonical in later proofs.

Recap: The Unitary Intersection

Three groups meet

$$O(2n)\cap Sp(2n)\cap GL(n,\mathbb C)=U(n)$$

Triple

$\omega$, $g$, $J$ form one compatible triple.

Existence

Compatible $J$ always exists on $(M,\omega)$.

Contractible

The choice is essentially unique up to deformation.

Printed pp. 65-71Recap and the U(n) Picture
Close by gathering threads. Compatibility intersects the orthogonal, symplectic, and complex-linear groups, and their common intersection is the unitary group. Remind students that omega, g, and J form one compatible triple, that such triples always exist, and that the choice is contractible. This sets up Kahler geometry in the lectures ahead.
