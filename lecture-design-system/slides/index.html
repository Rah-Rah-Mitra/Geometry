Isometries of the Plane

Definition 3.6
An isometry of ℝ² is a map f : ℝ² → ℝ² satisfying
d(f(P), f(Q)) = d(P, Q) for all points P, Q ∈ ℝ². Equivalently, f preserves the Euclidean distance.
Definition 3.7
The reflection in a line ℓ maps each point P to P′ such that ℓ is the perpendicular bisector of PP′. In coordinates, reflection in the x-axis:
σ(x, y) = (x, −y)

Three Reflections Theorem

Theorem 3.8
Every isometry of ℝ² is a composition of at most three reflections. Specifically:
— 1 reflection → reflection
— 2 reflections in intersecting lines → rotation by 2θ
— 2 reflections in parallel lines → translation by 2d
— 3 reflections → glide reflection
Proof Outline
1
An isometry is determined by the images of three non-collinear points A, B, C
2
If f(A) ≠ A, reflect in the perpendicular bisector of A f(A) to fix A
3
Repeat for B (preserving A), then C. At most 3 reflections needed  ∎
Two reflections = rotation
σℓ₂ ∘ σℓ₁ = RO, 2θ

Example 3.2
Find the reflection of P = (5, 1) in the line ℓ : y = 2x.
Solution

Pitfalls in Coordinate Geometry

Pitfall 1 — Slope of Vertical Lines
m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) is undefined when x₁ = x₂. Never write "slope = ∞" in proofs — use the parametric form (x₁, t) or homogeneous coordinates [a : b : c] instead.
Pitfall 2 — Angle Between Lines
tan θ = |m₁ − m₂| / (1 + m₁m₂) fails when m₁m₂ = −1 (perpendicular lines, denominator zero). Correct approach: use the dot product cos θ = (û · v̂) which works universally.
Pitfall 3 — Isometry ≠ Linear Map
Translations are isometries but not linear: T(0⃗) ≠ 0⃗. Isometries are affine maps of the form f(x) = Ax + b where A ∈ O(2). Confusing "linear" and "affine" breaks arguments about fixed points.
Correct Patterns
Lines via dot product (always works)
n⃗ · (P − P₀) = 0  where  n⃗ = (a, b)
Angle via arctan2 (handles all quadrants)
θ = atan2(det[û, v̂], û · v̂)
Isometry decomposition
f(x) = Ax + b,  A ∈ O(2),  b ∈ ℝ²
Rule of thumb: If a formula has a denominator, ask "when is it zero?" and handle that case separately or choose a formulation that avoids division.
