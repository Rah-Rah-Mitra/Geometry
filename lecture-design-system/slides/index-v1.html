Metric Spaces

Definition 3.1
Metric Space
A metric space is a pair (X, d) where X is a set and d : X × X → ℝ is a function satisfying, for all x, y, z ∈ X:
  • Positivity: d(x, y) ≥ 0, with equality iff x = y
  • Symmetry: d(x, y) = d(y, x)
  • Triangle inequality: d(x, z) ≤ d(x, y) + d(y, z)

Euclidean vs. Hyperbolic Geometry

Euclidean
  • Parallel postulate holds
  • Angle sum of triangle = π
  • Curvature K = 0
  • Similar triangles exist at every scale
Euclidean plane diagram
Hyperbolic
  • Infinitely many parallels through a point
  • Angle sum of triangle < π
  • Curvature K < 0
  • No similar triangles (AAA ⟹ congruence)

Gauss-Bonnet Theorem

Total Curvature of a Surface

∫∫M K dA + ∫∂M κg ds = 2πχ(M)

The total Gaussian curvature of a compact surface M plus the geodesic curvature along its boundary equals 2π times the Euler characteristic.

Common Mistakes

⚠ Common Mistake
Assuming the parallel postulate in hyperbolic space
In hyperbolic geometry, through any point not on a line, there are infinitely many lines that do not intersect the given line. Using the Euclidean parallel postulate here leads to contradictions.
Remark
Always verify which axiom system is in play before applying parallelism arguments. In elliptic geometry, no parallel lines exist at all.
