Actions by Isometries

Definition

$\Gamma$ acts on $X$ so each $g$ is a distance-preserving bijection.

Orbit

$\Gamma\!\cdot\! x = \{g\cdot x : g\in\Gamma\}$ samples the action.

Stabilizer

$\Gamma_x=\{g:g\cdot x=x\}$; free means trivial stabilizers.

Isometric action

$d(g\cdot x,\,g\cdot y)=d(x,y)\quad\forall g\in\Gamma$

Why it matters

An isometric action lets group structure speak in the language of distance.

Printed pp. 131-152Actions by Isometries
A group acts by isometries when every element moves points without changing distances. Stress the orbit and stabilizer language, and that the action descends to a metric on the quotient only under good hypotheses. Free, isometric actions are the bridge between algebra and the metric geometry that follows.

Proper and Cocompact Actions

Proper

Only finitely many $g$ move any bounded set near itself.

Cocompact

A compact set $K$ has $\Gamma\!\cdot\! K = X$: it tiles the space.

Geometric

Proper + cocompact + by isometries = a geometric action.

Printed pp. 131-152Proper and Cocompact Actions
Properness controls how the group sits discretely inside the isometries; cocompactness says a compact piece tiles the whole space under the action. Together they make the action geometric. Emphasise that these two finiteness conditions are exactly what the Svarc-Milnor lemma will consume as hypotheses.

The Word Metric

Generating set

Fix finite $S=S^{-1}$ that generates $\Gamma$.

Word length

$|g|_S$ is the length of a shortest word in $S$ equal to $g$.

Choice vs shape

Changing $S$ rescales numbers, not the coarse geometry.

Left-invariant metric

$d_S(g,h)=|g^{-1}h|_S$

Bi-Lipschitz on changing $S$

$\tfrac1A\,|g|_{S'}\le |g|_S\le A\,|g|_{S'}$

Printed pp. 131-152The Word Metric
Pick a finite generating set, write each element as a shortest word, and the word length becomes a left-invariant metric on the group. Different generating sets change the numbers but not the coarse shape. Make clear that the metric depends on a choice yet its large-scale geometry does not.

The Cayley Graph

Vertices

One vertex per group element $g\in\Gamma$.

Edges

Join $g$ to $gs$ for each generator $s\in S$.

Path metric

Graph distance equals the word metric $d_S$.

Printed pp. 131-152The Cayley Graph
The Cayley graph realises the group as the vertex set of a connected graph with generator edges. Path distance in this graph is exactly the word metric. Show the square grid for the free abelian group of rank two so students see algebra become a concrete metric graph they can walk along.

Quasi-Isometries

Bounded distortion

Stretch by at most $L$, slack by at most $C$.

Coarsely onto

Every point lies within $C$ of the image.

Equivalence

It is symmetric up to constants and transitive.

Quasi-isometric embedding

$\tfrac1L\,d(x,y)-C \le d\!\big(f(x),f(y)\big) \le L\,d(x,y)+C$

Quasi-isometry adds

$\forall y\ \exists x:\ d\!\big(y,f(x)\big)\le C$

Printed pp. 131-152Quasi-Isometries
A quasi-isometry allows multiplicative stretching by L and additive slack by C, plus a coarse surjectivity condition. It is the right notion of sameness for large-scale geometry because it forgets bounded detail. Walk through why the relation is symmetric up to constants and genuinely an equivalence on spaces.

Quasi-Geodesics

Definition

A quasi-isometric embedding of an interval $I\subset\mathbb{R}$.

Efficient travel

Length is comparable to distance up to $(L,C)$.

Stability

In hyperbolic spaces they stay near true geodesics.

Printed pp. 131-152Quasi-Geodesics
A quasi-geodesic is the image of an interval under a quasi-isometric embedding, so it travels efficiently up to fixed constants. These paths are the working tool of coarse geometry. Mention that in hyperbolic spaces quasi-geodesics stay uniformly close to honest geodesics, a stability fact we will need later.

The Svarc-Milnor Lemma

Hypotheses

$\Gamma$ acts geometrically on a length space $X$.

Conclusion

$\Gamma$ is finitely generated and quasi-isometric to $X$.

Slogan

The fundamental theorem of geometric group theory.

Statement

$\Gamma \curvearrowright X \text{ geometric} \;\Rightarrow\; \Gamma \overset{\mathrm{q.i.}}{\sim} X$

Mechanism

The orbit map $g\mapsto g\cdot x_0$ realises the quasi-isometry.

Printed pp. 131-152The Svarc-Milnor Lemma
State the lemma carefully: a group acting geometrically on a length space is finitely generated and quasi-isometric to that space, via the orbit map. This is the fundamental theorem of geometric group theory. Underline that it converts a metric action into an intrinsic statement about the group itself.

The Orbit Map

Definition

$\Phi(g)=g\cdot x_0$ for a fixed basepoint $x_0\in X$.

Quasi-isometry

Under Svarc-Milnor, $\Phi$ has bounded distortion.

Reading it

Word length tracks distance in $X$ up to constants.

Printed pp. 131-152The Orbit Map
Fix a basepoint and send each group element to its image under the action. The Svarc-Milnor hypotheses make this orbit map a quasi-isometry. Use the picture of the integer lattice orbit inside the plane to show generators becoming unit translations and word length tracking Euclidean distance up to constants.

Worked Example: $\mathbb{Z}^2$ and the Plane

The action

$\mathbb{Z}^2$ acts on $\mathbb{E}^2$ by integer translations, geometrically.

Conclusion

The Cayley grid is quasi-isometric to the Euclidean plane.

Distortion

Taxicab vs Euclidean differ by a bounded factor.

Word vs Euclidean

$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,|v|_1 \le |v|_2 \le |v|_1$

Printed pp. 131-152Z-squared and the Plane
The free abelian group of rank two acts on the Euclidean plane by integer translations, geometrically. So its Cayley grid is quasi-isometric to the plane. The taxicab word metric and the Euclidean metric differ by a bounded factor, the cleanest worked instance of the whole machinery in action.

Quasi-Isometry Invariants

Growth

Polynomial, exponential, or intermediate ball-volume growth.

Number of ends

$0,1,2,$ or infinitely many ends, all preserved.

Hyperbolicity

Gromov thin-triangle condition is a q.i. invariant.

Why they are invariants

$X \overset{\mathrm{q.i.}}{\sim} Y \;\Rightarrow\; \mathrm{growth},\,\mathrm{ends},\,\delta\text{-hyp coincide}$

Payoff

Via Svarc-Milnor these separate groups that algebra alone cannot.

Printed pp. 131-152Quasi-Isometry Invariants
Properties preserved by quasi-isometry are the legitimate invariants: growth type, number of ends, and Gromov hyperbolicity among them. Because of Svarc-Milnor these become invariants of the group up to commensurability. Frame invariants as the payoff: they let coarse geometry distinguish groups that algebra alone struggles to separate.
