When a group acts on a CAT(0) space, geometry dictates algebra

The setting

A group $\Gamma$ acting properly, cocompactly, by isometries on a complete CAT(0) space $X$.

The payoff

Solvable subgroups tame, decision problems solvable, distortion controlled.

CAT(0) Groups

Definition

$\Gamma$ is a CAT(0) group if it acts properly cocompactly by isometries on a complete CAT(0) space.

Švarc–Milnor

Such $\Gamma$ is finitely generated and quasi-isometric to $X$.

Quasi-isometry

$\tfrac1\lambda d_X - \varepsilon \le d_\Gamma \le \lambda\, d_X + \varepsilon$

Consequence

The Cayley graph and $X$ share large-scale geometry, so curvature transfers to combinatorics.

Printed pp. 438-518CAT(0) Groups
Define a CAT(0) group precisely: it acts properly, cocompactly, by isometries on a complete CAT(0) space. By the Svarc-Milnor lemma such a group is finitely generated and quasi-isometric to the space. Emphasize that cocompactness is what transfers convexity of the metric into combinatorial control of the Cayley graph.

Semisimple Isometries and Axes

Trichotomy

An isometry is elliptic (fixed point), hyperbolic (axis), or parabolic.

In CAT(0) groups

Every infinite-order element is semisimple: $\min$ displacement is attained.

Translation length

$|g| = \inf_{x\in X} d(x,\,g\!\cdot\! x)$

Printed pp. 438-518Semisimple Isometries and Translation
Recall the trichotomy of isometries and focus on hyperbolic ones with positive translation length achieved on an axis. In a CAT(0) group every infinite-order element acts as a semisimple isometry, so it has an axis. This is the geometric engine behind the Flat Torus theorem on the next slide.

The Flat Torus Theorem

Theorem

If $A \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ acts freely by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space, then the union of its axes contains an $A$-invariant isometric flat $\mathbb{E}^n$, and $\mathbb{E}^n/A$ is a flat $n$-torus.

Slogan

Abelian subgroups are flats: rank $n$ ↔ dimension $n$.

Printed pp. 438-518Flat Torus Theorem
State the Flat Torus theorem: a free abelian subgroup of rank n stabilizes an isometrically embedded flat, a copy of Euclidean n-space, and the quotient contains a flat n-torus. Make the slogan explicit: abelian subgroups are exactly flats. Rank of the abelian subgroup equals dimension of the flat it preserves.

The Solvable Subgroup Theorem

Theorem

Every solvable subgroup of a CAT(0) group is virtually abelian; when the action is cocompact it is finitely generated and virtually $\mathbb{Z}^n$.

Moral

Non-positive curvature collapses solvability down to flat geometry.

Contrast

In general groups solvable subgroups are wild (e.g. infinitely generated, exotic). Curvature forbids this.

Excluded

No Baumslag–Solitar $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$, no Heisenberg-style nilpotent distortion.

Printed pp. 438-518Solvable Subgroup Theorem
State that every solvable subgroup of a CAT(0) group is virtually abelian, in fact finitely generated and virtually free abelian when the action is cocompact. Contrast with general groups, where solvable subgroups are wild. Non-positive curvature collapses solvability down to flat geometry, which is the recurring moral of the chapter.

Centralizers Split as Products

Min set

For hyperbolic $g$, the set $\mathrm{Min}(g)$ of minimal-displacement points splits isometrically.

Product structure

$\mathrm{Min}(g) \cong \mathbb{R} \times Y$

Consequence

The centralizer $C(g)$ acts on $\mathrm{Min}(g)$; it virtually splits with a central $\mathbb{Z}$ factor.

Printed pp. 438-518Centralizers and Splitting
Explain how axes organize centralizers. The centralizer of a hyperbolic isometry splits as a product of a line with a complementary space, so the centralizer of an infinite-order element virtually splits with a central Z factor. This product structure is the tool that proves the Flat Torus and Solvable Subgroup theorems.

A Tits Alternative Flavor

Flat side

A subgroup is virtually abelian — it preserves a flat, no free dynamics.

Free side

Or it contains a rank-one element whose axis is contracting, driving ping-pong on the boundary.

Ping-pong

$\langle a,b\rangle \cong F_2 \;\Leftarrow\; a^{\pm}X^- \subset X^+,\; b^{\pm}X^+\subset X^-$

Printed pp. 438-518A Tits Alternative Flavor
Describe the dichotomy: a subgroup of a CAT(0) group is either virtually abelian or contains a rank-one element giving free, ping-pong dynamics on the boundary. It is not the full Tits alternative in general, but for many CAT(0) groups one gets the clean split between flat behavior and free, hyperbolic-like behavior.

Dehn Functions and Fillings

Isoperimetric function

$\delta(n) = \max_{\ell(w)\le n,\ w=_\Gamma 1}\ \mathrm{Area}(w)$

Meaning

$\mathrm{Area}(w)$ = fewest relator cells needed to fill the loop $w$ by a van Kampen diagram.

Invariance

The growth class of $\delta$ is a quasi-isometry invariant.

Printed pp. 438-518Dehn Functions and van Kampen Diagrams
Define the Dehn or isoperimetric function as the maximal area needed to fill a null-homotopic loop of given length by a van Kampen diagram. Emphasize the picture: a loop in the Cayley two-complex bounds a disc tiled by relator cells, and area counts those cells. The growth rate of this function is a quasi-isometry invariant.

Quadratic Filling for CAT(0)

Theorem

CAT(0) groups satisfy a quadratic isoperimetric inequality:

$\delta(n) \le C\, n^2$

Why

Convexity lets you cone the loop to a basepoint; swept area is bounded by the geodesic homotopy.

Hyperbolic extreme

$\delta(n) \le C\,n$ — thin triangles force linear filling.

Printed pp. 438-518Quadratic Filling for CAT(0)
State the key inequality: CAT(0) groups satisfy a quadratic isoperimetric inequality, area bounded by a constant times length squared. Convexity lets you cone the loop to a basepoint and bound the swept area. Contrast with hyperbolic groups, which improve this to a linear bound, the sharper extreme of non-positive curvature.

Distortion and Quasi-convex Subgroups

Distortion

$\mathrm{Dist}_H^\Gamma(n) = \max\{|h|_H : h\in H,\ |h|_\Gamma \le n\}$

Undistorted

$H$ quasi-convex ⇒ $|h|_H \asymp |h|_\Gamma$: the inclusion is a quasi-isometric embedding.

Distorted

Fibers of mapping tori can be exponentially distorted — short ambient words, long intrinsic ones.

Printed pp. 438-518Distortion and Quasi-convex Subgroups
Define subgroup distortion comparing the intrinsic and ambient word metrics. An undistorted, quasi-convex subgroup embeds without stretching, like a flat or a convex core. Distorted subgroups, such as fibers in certain mapping tori, show that not every subgroup of a non-positively curved group is itself well behaved.
