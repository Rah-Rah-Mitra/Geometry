Gluing Constructions

Reshetnyak's theorem and how curvature survives a seam

The question

When does gluing CAT($k$) pieces give a CAT($k$) space again?

The answer

When the seam is a complete, isometric, convex subspace.

The payoff

Wedges, doubles, books, and trees of spaces.

What Gluing Means

Identify a seam

An isometry $j:A_1 \to A_2$ glues $X_1,X_2$ along their subspaces.

Quotient pseudometric

Distance is the shortest concatenation of paths that may cross the seam.

Glued space

$X = X_1 \sqcup_j X_2,\quad a \sim j(a)$

Crossing distance

$d(x_1,x_2)=\inf_{a\in A}\big(d_1(x_1,a)+d_2(a,x_2)\big)$

Printed pp. 347-366What Gluing Means
Define the gluing of two spaces along an isometry of chosen subspaces, with the induced quotient pseudometric measuring shortest concatenated paths that may cross the seam. Stress that the seam is identified pointwise, so a path can re-enter either piece. The whole chapter studies which seams keep the result a length space of bounded curvature.

Convex and Complete Seams

Convex

Every geodesic between two points of $A$ stays inside $A$.

Complete

$A$ is closed and contains its limits as a metric space.

Examples

A point, a geodesic line, a half-plane boundary, a convex ball.

Printed pp. 347-366Convex Subspaces
Recall that a subset is convex when every geodesic between its points stays inside it, and complete when it is closed as a metric space. These two adjectives are the hypotheses Reshetnyak demands of the seam. Give the half-plane boundary line and a metric ball as contrasting examples so learners feel why convexity is restrictive.

Reshetnyak's Gluing Theorem

Theorem

Let $X_1,X_2$ be CAT($k$) with $k\le 0$, and let $A_i\subset X_i$ be complete, isometric, convex. Then

$X_1 \sqcup_A X_2 \ \text{ is CAT}(k).$

The mechanism

Convexity lets a triangle straddling the seam be rebuilt in the model plane without growing.

Isometric

$A_1$ and $A_2$ are identified by a distance-preserving map.

Convex + complete

The seam is the load-bearing hypothesis; both adjectives are needed.

Scope

The engine behind every construction in this chapter.

Printed pp. 347-366Reshetnyak's Gluing Theorem
State the headline result: gluing two CAT(k) spaces along a complete isometric convex subspace yields a CAT(k) space, for k at most zero. Emphasize that convexity of the seam is exactly what lets a comparison triangle straddling the seam be rebuilt in the model plane without increasing distances. This is the engine for every construction that follows.

Why the Seam Must Be Convex

Decompose at the wall

A triangle crossing the seam splits where a side meets $A$.

Convexity helps

The crossing point lies on a geodesic of $A$, so the wall carries the join.

Reassemble

Glue the two comparison sub-triangles hinge to hinge in the model plane.

Comparison bound

$d_X(p,q)\ \le\ d_{M_k}(\bar p,\bar q)$

If not convex

The crossing geodesic shortcuts outside $A$, fattening the triangle and breaking the bound.

Printed pp. 347-366Why Convexity of the Seam
Walk through the proof idea: a geodesic triangle crossing the seam decomposes at the convex wall, and convexity guarantees the crossing point lies on a geodesic of the wall. Reassembling the comparison triangles hinge-side by hinge-side keeps the configuration thin. Without convexity the crossing geodesic can shortcut outside the wall and fatten the triangle.

A Thin Triangle Across the Seam

One vertex per piece

The side $[p,q]$ pierces the seam at a point of $A$.

CAT($k$) test passes

$d(x,y)\le d(\bar x,\bar y)\ \ \forall x,y$

Inherited

Thinness on every straddling triangle is exactly the curvature bound.

Printed pp. 347-366Thinness Across the Seam
Use the diagram to make the comparison test concrete: a triangle with one vertex in each piece crosses the seam, and the corresponding comparison triangle in the model plane is at least as fat. Distances measured to the comparison points dominate the real ones, so the glued triangle inherits the CAT(k) thinness condition exactly.

Special Case: Gluing at a Point

A point is convex

A single point is trivially complete and convex.

Wedge of CAT($0$)

$X_1 \vee X_2$ at a basepoint is again CAT($0$).

Examples

Bouquets of trees, wedges of disks, branched flat petals.

Printed pp. 347-366Gluing Along a Point
Specialize the seam to a single point, which is trivially complete convex. The wedge of two CAT(0) spaces at a point is again CAT(0); this is how bouquets and wedges of trees or disks acquire non-positive curvature. Note the geodesic between points in different petals must pass through the wedge point, a useful sanity check.

Special Case: Gluing Along a Line

A geodesic is convex

A complete geodesic line or segment is a convex seam.

Two half-planes

Glued along the boundary line they rebuild the flat plane.

Books of pages

Three or more pages sharing one line: a branched flat CAT($0$) space.

Printed pp. 347-366Gluing Along a Geodesic
Take the seam to be a complete geodesic line or segment, which is convex. Gluing two CAT(0) half-planes along their boundary line rebuilds the plane; gluing three along a shared line gives a book of pages, a branched flat space that is still CAT(0). The branch locus is where several convex walls coincide.

The Doubling Construction

Two copies

Take $X$ and a mirror copy $X'$ of a CAT($0$) space.

Glue on a convex set

Identify them along a complete convex $C\subset X$ by the identity.

Result

The double $D_C(X)$ is CAT($0$), with the swap symmetry fixing $C$.

Double

$D_C(X)=X \sqcup_C X',\quad \sigma^2=\mathrm{id},\ \ \mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)=C$

Reading

Reflecting a convex region of the plane across its boundary makes a flat space whose only singular locus sits where convexity could fail.

Printed pp. 347-366The Doubling Construction
Double a CAT(0) space across a complete convex subset by gluing two copies along that subset using the identity. Reshetnyak guarantees the double is CAT(0), and the swap symmetry fixes the seam. Mention that doubling a convex region of the plane across its boundary produces a Riemannian-flat object with a curvature singularity only where convexity could fail.

Trees and Graphs of Spaces

Vertex spaces

CAT($0$) blocks sit at the nodes of a pattern graph.

Edge spaces

Convex walls are the shared seams along the edges.

Trees stay CAT($0$)

Repeated Reshetnyak gluing along a tree preserves the bound.

Cycles need care

Graphs with loops require link conditions, not just convex seams.

Printed pp. 347-366Trees and Graphs of Spaces
Iterate gluing along convex walls following a tree or graph pattern: vertex spaces are CAT(0) blocks, edge spaces are the convex walls they share. A tree of spaces stays CAT(0) by repeated Reshetnyak; a graph of spaces with cycles needs link conditions instead. This is the structural backbone of many non-positively curved complexes.

Failure: A Non-Convex Seam

The kink

A non-convex seam lets a shortest path bend wrongly at the join.

Symptoms

A geodesic bigon appears, or a comparison triangle turns fat.

Lesson

The bound breaks locally; both seam hypotheses are indispensable.

Printed pp. 347-366When the Seam Is Not Convex
Show the failure mode: gluing along a non-convex subset lets a shortest path kink incorrectly at the seam, creating a geodesic bigon or a fat comparison triangle. The curvature bound breaks locally near the kink. This is the negative control in the notebook lab and explains why both hypotheses on the seam are indispensable.

Recap: The Gluing Toolkit

Logic chain

Convex complete seam $\Rightarrow$ Reshetnyak $\Rightarrow$ CAT($k$) preserved.

Corollaries

Wedges, doubles, books, trees of spaces all inherit the bound.

Guardrail

A non-convex seam breaks thinness; check the seam first.

Inspection habit

Name the points. Identify the convex wall $A$. Verify thinness on a triangle that straddles the seam.

Next
