Pseudogroups of Local Isometries

Elements

Isometries $\varphi:V \to W$ between open subsets of $X$.

Closure axioms

Closed under restriction, inverse, partial composition, and gluing of compatible pieces.

Why partial?

Domains differ, so $\psi\circ\varphi$ is defined only where targets meet sources.

Pseudogroup $\mathcal P$

$\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal P \Rightarrow \psi\!\circ\!\varphi\big|_{\varphi^{-1}(\operatorname{dom}\psi)}\in\mathcal P$

Gluing

Maps agreeing on overlaps assemble into one element of $\mathcal P$.

Printed pp. 584-619Pseudogroups of Local Isometries
Start concretely. A pseudogroup collects partial isometries between open subsets of a space, closed under restriction, inverse, composition where defined, and gluing of compatible pieces. Emphasize that elements are only locally defined, so composition is partial. This partiality is exactly what forces us out of groups and into groupoids.

From Pseudogroup to Groupoid

Take germs

An arrow is the germ of a local isometry at a point $x$.

Source and target

$s,t:\mathcal G \to X$ recover the underlying points.

Now total

Germ composition is defined whenever $t(g)=s(h)$.

Groupoid structure

$g:x\to y,\quad h:y\to z,\quad hg:x\to z,\quad g^{-1}:y\to x$

Printed pp. 584-619From Pseudogroup to Groupoid
Promote the pseudogroup to a groupoid by taking germs. An arrow is a germ of a local isometry at a point, with source and target the underlying points. Composition of germs is now total wherever targets match. Stress the source and target maps; they are the structural skeleton every later definition hangs on.

The Germ and Holonomy Groupoid

Germ relation

$\varphi\sim_x\psi$ iff they agree on a neighbourhood of $x$.

Isotropy

Arrows $x\to x$ form the holonomy group $\mathcal G_x$.

Reading

$\mathcal G_x$ measures the local self-symmetry seen at $x$.

Isotropy group

$\mathcal G_x=\{\,g\in\mathcal G : s(g)=t(g)=x\,\}$

Singular points

Nontrivial $\mathcal G_x$ marks an orbifold singularity; trivial holonomy means a manifold point.

Printed pp. 584-619The Germ and Holonomy Groupoid
Two local isometries agreeing near a point share a germ, so germs quotient out irrelevant domain choices. The isotropy group at a point, arrows from that point to itself, is its holonomy. Explain that holonomy measures how the space sees itself locally, the seed of orbifold singular points and of nontrivial local symmetry.

The Etale Viewpoint

Topology on arrows

Germs carry a sheaf-like topology basis $\{\,\text{germ}_x\varphi : x\in\operatorname{dom}\varphi\,\}$.

Etale condition

$s$ and $t$ are local homeomorphisms.

Consequence

Arrow space is locally modelled on the object space.

Printed pp. 584-619The Etale Viewpoint
Give the topology. Germs carry a sheaf-like topology making source and target local homeomorphisms; such a groupoid is etale. Walk through why this is the right setting: the arrow space is locally modelled on the object space, so the groupoid is genuinely low dimensional and behaves like a space with extra symmetry arrows.

Orbifolds as Groupoids

Action groupoid

For $\Gamma\curvearrowright \tilde X$ properly discontinuously by isometries.

Charts

Neighbourhoods modelled as $B/\Gamma_x$ with finite isotropy $\Gamma_x$.

Slogan

An orbifold is the quotient plus its groupoid of local symmetries.

Translation groupoid

$\Gamma\ltimes \tilde X:\quad (\gamma,x):x\to\gamma x,\quad \mathcal G_{[x]}\cong\Gamma_x$

Printed pp. 584-619Orbifolds as Groupoids
Connect to orbifolds. A proper, properly discontinuous action of a group by isometries yields a quotient whose local structure is recorded by the action groupoid. Charts model neighbourhoods as quotients of balls by finite isotropy. The orbifold is not just the quotient space but the quotient together with this groupoid of local symmetries.

Complexes of Groups

Local groups

Assign a group $G_\sigma$ to each cell $\sigma$ of a scwol.

Face maps

Injections $\psi_a:G_\sigma\hookrightarrow G_\tau$ along faces $\tau\le\sigma$.

Groupoid

These assemble into the groupoid of the complex of groups.

Developable case

$G(\mathcal Y)\ \text{developable}\iff\ G(\mathcal Y)=\Gamma\backslash\backslash X,\ X\ \text{simply connected}$

Shadow

A complex of groups is the combinatorial shadow of an orbifold; its groupoid recovers the local isometries.

Printed pp. 584-619Complexes of Groups
Tie to Part III machinery. A complex of groups over a scwol assigns groups to cells and injections to faces, the combinatorial shadow of an orbifold's local groups. The associated groupoid assembles these local groups and gluing maps. Developable complexes of groups are exactly those arising from an actual group action on a simply connected complex.

Equivalence of Groupoids

The real object

The groupoid, not its space of objects, is the invariant.

Morita equivalence

An essential, fully faithful functor relates the two groupoids.

Chart change

Refining a cover gives an equivalent groupoid.

Equivalence functor

$F:\mathcal G\to\mathcal H\quad\text{essentially surjective}\ \&\ \text{fully faithful}$

Invariants

Only equivalence-invariant data is geometrically meaningful, such as the fundamental group up next.

Printed pp. 584-619Equivalence of Groupoids
Insist the groupoid, not its object space, is the real invariant. Two groupoids are equivalent when an essential, fully faithful functor relates them, so refining a cover or changing charts gives an equivalent groupoid. Morita equivalence is the precise notion. Geometric quantities we care about must be equivalence invariants, like the fundamental group below.

The Fundamental Groupoid

Paths

Alternate short geodesic moves inside charts with groupoid arrows.

Homotopy

Loops at a basepoint, modulo homotopy, form $\pi_1(\mathcal G,x_0)$.

Deck group

For a developable orbifold it is the deck group of the universal cover.

Fundamental group

$\pi_1(\mathcal G,x_0)=\{\text{edge-loops at }x_0\}/\!\sim_{\text{htpy}}$

Printed pp. 584-619The Fundamental Groupoid
Define homotopy for the groupoid. A path alternates short geodesic moves with arrows, and the fundamental group collects such loops up to homotopy. For a developable orbifold this recovers the deck group of the universal cover. Emphasize this is the algebraic gatekeeper: it controls whether local pieces can unfold consistently.

Developability

Definition

Local charts assemble into a global development $\tilde X$.

Quotient

$\mathcal G$ acts on $\tilde X$ with quotient the original space.

Obstruction

Holonomy around loops can block consistent unfolding.

Theorem (curvature certificate)

$\text{local model CAT}(0)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal G\ \text{developable},\ \tilde X\ \text{CAT}(0)$

Mechanism

Non-positive curvature kills the holonomy obstruction, so loops lift trivially and the development exists.

Printed pp. 584-619Developability
State the main theme. The space is developable when the local charts assemble into a global development, a simply connected space on which the groupoid acts so the original is the quotient. The obstruction is holonomy around loops. Non-positive curvature of the local model is the hypothesis that kills the obstruction and forces developability.

The Development via Lifted Geodesics

Construction

Lift geodesics from $x_0$, attach a chart along each lifted path.

Gluing

Identify overlaps using the recorded germs.

Guarantee

Local CAT(0) makes lifts unique, so the build is consistent.

Printed pp. 584-619The Development via Lifted Geodesics
Make developability constructive. Build the development by lifting geodesics from a basepoint, attaching charts along each lifted path, and identifying overlaps by the recorded germs. Local CAT(0) geometry guarantees lifts are unique and the construction is consistent, so the development is CAT(0) and the orbifold cover exists. This is the notebook's lifted geodesic lab.
