From Graphs of Groups to Higher Dimensions

Dimension one

A graph of groups always develops to a Bass-Serre tree. Developability is automatic.

Dimension two and up

An arbitrary complex of groups need not develop. The miracle is lost.

Bass-Serre dictionary

$\text{graph of groups} \;\longleftrightarrow\; G \curvearrowright \text{tree}$

New ingredient

Twisting elements correcting composition appear only when faces have dimension $\geq 2$.

Printed pp. 519-572From Graphs of Groups
Stress what survives and what breaks. A graph of groups always develops to a tree; that is the miracle of dimension one. In higher dimensions an arbitrary complex of groups need not develop at all, so developability becomes a theorem to be earned rather than a free gift.

Scwols: Small Categories Without Loops

Graded objects

Each object carries a dimension; every non-identity arrow strictly lowers it.

No loops

Hence no object has a non-trivial automorphism and no oriented cycle exists.

Canonical example

The barycentric subdivision of any polyhedral complex.

Printed pp. 519-572Small Categories Without Loops
Define a scwol carefully. Vertices carry a dimension grading; non-identity arrows strictly decrease it, so there are no non-trivial loops and no non-trivial automorphisms of an object. The barycentric subdivision of any polyhedral complex is the canonical example feeding the whole construction.

A Complex of Groups Over a Scwol

Local groups

An assignment $\sigma \mapsto G_\sigma$ on objects of the scwol $\mathcal{Y}$.

Twisting maps

An injective homomorphism $\psi_a : G_{i(a)} \to G_{t(a)}$ for each arrow $a$.

Correction elements

Elements $g_{a,b} \in G_{t(ab)}$ repairing composition.

Compatibility

$\psi_a\,\psi_b = \mathrm{Ad}(g_{a,b})\,\psi_{ab}$

Reading it

The maps fail to compose strictly; $g_{a,b}$ measures the failure inside the target local group.

Printed pp. 519-572Complex of Groups Over a Scwol
Walk through the three pieces of data: local groups on objects, injective twisting homomorphisms along arrows, and elements correcting composition. Emphasize that the correction elements are not optional decoration; they record the failure of strict functoriality and carry genuine cohomological content.

The Cocycle Condition

Over triples

For composable $a,b,c$ the correction elements must agree two ways around.

Non-abelian 2-cocycle

This is a degree-two condition; abelian local groups land it in $H^2$.

Associativity of twisting

$\psi_a(g_{b,c})\,g_{a,bc} = g_{a,b}\,g_{ab,c}$

Printed pp. 519-572The Cocycle Condition
Explain that the twisting elements must satisfy a compatibility identity over every composable pair of arrows. This is a non-abelian two-cocycle condition. When the local groups are abelian it literally lives in degree-two cohomology, which is the obstruction theory students should keep in the back of their minds.

The Fundamental Group

Generators

Local-group elements together with one edge symbol per arrow of the scwol.

Relations

The twisting maps, the correction elements, and a chosen spanning tree.

Presentation idea

$\pi_1(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{Y}) = \big(\ast_\sigma G_\sigma \ast F(\text{edges})\big)\big/ R$

Sanity check

For a one-dimensional scwol this is exactly the fundamental group of a graph of groups.

Printed pp. 519-572Fundamental Group
Build the fundamental group as a quotient of the free product of local groups with the edge generators, modulo relations from the twisting data and a chosen spanning tree. This generalizes the fundamental group of a graph of groups and reduces to it exactly when the scwol is one-dimensional.

The Universal Development

Assemble

Glue cosets of local groups along the connecting maps to build a scwol $\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}$.

Action

$\pi_1$ acts on $\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}$ with quotient the original $\mathcal{Y}$.

Tree analogy

Mirrors how the Bass-Serre tree sits over a graph of groups.

Printed pp. 519-572The Universal Development
Describe the development as the space the fundamental group acts on, assembled from cosets of local groups glued by the connecting maps. When it exists it is simply connected and its quotient recovers the original scwol, exactly mirroring how the Bass-Serre tree sits over a graph of groups.

Developability

Definition

The complex of groups arises from an actual action on a simply connected scwol.

Equivalent test

Every local group injects into $\pi_1(\mathcal{G})$.

Criterion

$\mathcal{G}\text{ developable} \iff G_\sigma \hookrightarrow \pi_1(\mathcal{G})\ \forall\,\sigma$

It can fail

Local data may collapse globally; obstructions are local link-condition failures, vertex by vertex.

Printed pp. 519-572Developability
Define developable precisely: the complex of groups arises from a genuine action on a simply connected scwol. Equivalently every local group injects into the fundamental group. Warn that this can fail; give the intuition that obstructions are local linking-condition failures detected one vertex at a time.

The Local Development

Model link

At each vertex $\sigma$ the local development $\mathrm{St}(\sigma)$ is the link the universal object must show.

Always defined

It exists even when the global development does not.

Printed pp. 519-572The Local Development
Introduce the local development at a vertex as the model link the universal object must exhibit there. It is always defined even when the global development is not, which is why curvature, a purely local condition, can be checked on it without first knowing developability holds.

Non-Positively Curved Complexes Develop

Haefliger's theorem

If each local development is $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ then $\mathcal{G}$ is developable and its development is $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$.

Mechanism

A combinatorial Cartan-Hadamard: local non-positive curvature forces a simply connected global model.

Link condition

$\mathrm{Lk}(\sigma)\ \text{has girth} \geq 2\pi/\kappa,\ \kappa\leq 0$

Consequence

$\pi_1(\mathcal{G})$ acts properly and cocompactly on a $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ space; local groups are stabilizers.

Printed pp. 519-572Non-Positive Curvature Theorem
State Haefliger's theorem: if every local development is CAT(0), or more generally CAT(kappa) locally with the link condition, then the complex of groups is developable and the development is CAT(0). This is the higher-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard mechanism transplanted into the combinatorial setting.

Building Groups With Prescribed Local Structure

Step 1

Choose shapes whose links are large; prescribe local groups and gluing.

Step 2

Verify the metric link condition combinatorially, one vertex at a time.

Step 3

The theorem returns a group acting on a $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ complex.

Payoff

$\text{local data} \;\Longrightarrow\; G \curvearrowright X,\ X\ \mathrm{CAT}(0)$

Examples

Reflection and Coxeter complexes, right-angled groups, and aspherical complexes with controlled stabilizers.

Printed pp. 519-572Constructing Groups
Show the payoff direction. Prescribe local groups and gluing on a complex whose shapes force large links, verify the metric link condition combinatorially, and the theorem hands back a group acting properly cocompactly on a CAT(0) space, with the local data realized as actual stabilizers.

Recap and Outlook

Four invariants

Scwol, complex of groups, fundamental group, development, in tight correspondence.

The bridge

Local non-positive curvature carries combinatorial data to global simply connected geometry.

One-line summary

$\text{local }\mathrm{CAT}(0) \Rightarrow \text{developable} \Rightarrow \text{global }\mathrm{CAT}(0)$

Looking ahead

Applications to Coxeter groups, right-angled Artin and Coxeter groups, and rigidity of group actions.

Printed pp. 519-572Recap and Outlook
Close by tying the four invariants together: scwol, complex of groups, fundamental group, development.
