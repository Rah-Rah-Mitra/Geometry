Metric Spaces

The axioms

Positivity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality on a set $X$.

Pseudometric

Drop strictness: distinct points may have distance zero.

Metric axioms

$d(x,y)=0 \iff x=y$

$d(x,z) \le d(x,y) + d(y,z)$

Workhorse

Nearly every estimate is the triangle inequality in disguise.

Printed pp. 2-14Metric Spaces
Recall the three axioms a metric must satisfy and contrast a genuine metric with a pseudometric, where distinct points may sit at distance zero. Emphasise that the triangle inequality is the single workhorse axiom: nearly every estimate later in the chapter is one application of it dressed in geometric language.

The Length of a Curve

Polygonal sum

Sample a path $c$ at a partition and sum consecutive distances.

Rectifiable

The curve is rectifiable when the supremum is finite.

Length

$\ell(c) = \sup \sum_{i} d\big(c(t_{i-1}), c(t_i)\big)$

Printed pp. 2-14The Length of a Curve
Define the length of a path as the supremum of polygonal approximations sampled along finer partitions. Point out that length always dominates the endpoint distance, and that a curve is rectifiable exactly when this supremum is finite. The gap between length and distance is what later measures inefficiency of a path.

Geodesics as Isometric Embeddings

Isometry

A map $c:[a,b]\to X$ with distance preserved exactly.

Arc length

Parametrise by arc length, so speed is constant equal to one.

Defining condition

$d\big(c(s), c(t)\big) = |s - t|$

Stronger than smooth

This is a global length-realizing condition, not a differential one.

Printed pp. 2-14Geodesics as Isometric Embeddings
A geodesic is an isometric embedding of a real interval: distance along the image equals distance in the parameter. Stress that this is a global, length-realizing condition, far stronger than the differential-geometry notion. Linear reparametrisation by arc length is the convention, so speed is constant and equal to one.

Segments, Rays, and Lines

Segment

Domain a compact interval $[0,L]$: joins two points.

Ray

Domain a half-line $[0,\infty)$: one endpoint, runs out to infinity.

Line

Domain all of $\mathbb{R}$: a bi-infinite geodesic.

Printed pp. 2-14Segments, Rays, and Lines
Classify geodesics by the domain interval: a compact interval gives a segment joining two points, a half-line gives a ray, and the whole real line gives a bi-infinite geodesic line. Each is an isometric image, so the three differ only in how far the distance-realizing path is allowed to extend.

Geodesic Spaces

Definition

Every pair of points is joined by at least one geodesic segment.

An existence claim

The punctured plane and the open disc both fail it.

Geodesic space

$\forall\, x,y \in X \;\exists\, c:[0,d(x,y)]\to X$

Hopf-Rinow

Completeness with local compactness guarantees segments exist.

Printed pp. 2-14Geodesic Spaces
A space is geodesic when every pair of points is joined by at least one geodesic segment. Note this is genuinely an existence hypothesis: a punctured plane or an open disc fails it. The Hopf-Rinow theorem, met later, gives the standard sufficient conditions of completeness and local compactness.

Uniquely Geodesic Spaces

Uniqueness

Exactly one geodesic segment joins any two points.

Sphere fails

Antipodal points carry infinitely many great-circle arcs.

Printed pp. 2-14Uniquely Geodesic Spaces
Uniqueness is separate from existence. The sphere is geodesic yet antipodal points are joined by infinitely many great-circle arcs. Non-positive curvature, the theme of the book, will force uniqueness. Use the antipodal picture to make vivid that branching of geodesics is exactly the failure curvature controls.

Geodesic vs Non-Geodesic Paths

The minimizer

A geodesic achieves the infimum of length over all joining paths.

The detour

Any other curve is strictly longer between the same endpoints.

Printed pp. 2-14Geodesic vs Non-Geodesic Paths
Contrast a straight distance-realizing segment with a wandering curve sharing the same endpoints. The wandering curve has strictly greater length while the segment achieves the infimum. This picture is the intuitive core of the word geodesic: it is the path that spends no length on detours between its endpoints.

Metric vs Induced Length Metric

Length metric

Redefine distance as the infimum of path lengths within the space.

When equal

The two coincide precisely on geodesic spaces.

Induced length metric

$d_\ell(x,y) = \inf\{\,\ell(c) : c \text{ joins } x,y\,\}$

Printed pp. 2-14Metric versus Induced Length Metric
Given any metric one can define the induced length metric by infimising path length between points. The two agree in geodesic spaces but can differ sharply otherwise. The classic example is two points on a circle in the plane: the chord distance versus the arc distance you must travel within the curve.

Local Geodesics

Local condition

Distance is realized only on each short subinterval.

Helix example

A helix on a cylinder is locally but not globally geodesic.

Local geodesic

$d\big(c(s),c(t)\big)=|s-t| \;\text{ for } |s-t| \text{ small}$

One-way implication

Every geodesic is local; the converse can fail.

Printed pp. 2-14Local Geodesics
A local geodesic realizes distance only on short subintervals, not globally. On a flat cylinder a helix winding around is locally geodesic but not globally minimizing. Emphasise that every geodesic is local but the converse fails, and that this gap is where interesting global geometry lives.

Convex Subsets and Functions

Convex set

Contains a geodesic between any two of its points.

Convex function

Its restriction to every geodesic is convex on the interval.

Convexity along geodesics

$f\big(c(t)\big) \le (1-t)\,f(c(0)) + t\,f(c(1))$

Why it matters

Convex distance functions are the analytic mark of non-positive curvature.

Printed pp. 2-14Convex Subsets and Functions
A subset is convex when it contains a geodesic between any two of its points, and a function is convex when its restriction to every geodesic is convex in the ordinary sense. Convexity of the distance function will be the analytic signature of non-positive curvature throughout the book.
