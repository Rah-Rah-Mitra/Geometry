The Defining Point Symmetry

Geodesic reversal

At each $p$ there is an isometry $s_p$ with $s_p(p)=p$ reversing every geodesic through $p$.

Consequences

Forces homogeneity and completeness; the space is real-analytic.

Local symmetry

$s_p\circ\gamma(t)=\gamma(-t),\quad d\,s_p|_p=-\mathrm{Id}$

Printed pp. 299-341The Point Symmetry
A symmetric space carries at every point an isometry that reverses every geodesic through that point. We emphasise that this single axiom forces homogeneity, completeness, and an analytic Lie-theoretic structure. Stress that the symmetry is local data with global consequences, distinguishing these spaces from arbitrary homogeneous Riemannian manifolds.

Compact, Euclidean, Non-Compact

Compact type

Positive curvature, e.g. spheres. Excluded.

Euclidean type

Flat factors $\mathbb{R}^k$.

Non-compact type

Curvature $\le 0$; Hadamard, hence CAT(0).

De Rham split

$M=M_0\times M_+\times M_-$

Euclidean $\times$ compact $\times$ non-compact.

Our focus

Drop $M_+$; the curvature-non-positive factor is where comparison geometry lives.

Printed pp. 299-341Three Types
De Rham and Cartan split symmetric spaces into compact, Euclidean, and non-compact type. We care about non-compact type because exactly there the sectional curvature is non-positive, giving Hadamard manifolds that are CAT(0). Make clear that compact-type factors carry positive curvature and are excluded from our comparison-geometry programme.

The Model Space $P(n)$

Definition

$P(n)=\{A: A=A^{T},\ A\succ 0\}$, an open convex cone.

Universality

Every non-compact symmetric space embeds totally geodesically in some $P(n)$.

Symmetry at A

$s_A(B)=A B^{-1} A$

At the identity this is inversion $B\mapsto B^{-1}$.

Printed pp. 299-341P(n) of Positive Matrices
Introduce the model space P(n) of positive-definite symmetric matrices as an open convex cone. Explain that it is the universal example: every non-compact symmetric space embeds totally geodesically inside some P(n). The point symmetry at the identity is inversion, and at any A it is conjugated inversion, fixing A.

$P(n)=\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(n)$

Group action

$g\cdot A = gAg^{T}$ for $g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$ is transitive.

Stabiliser

The orthogonal group $O(n)$ fixes the identity $I$.

Homogeneous space

$P(n)\cong \mathrm{GL}(n)/O(n),\quad SP(n)\cong \mathrm{SL}(n)/\mathrm{SO}(n)$

Geometry as algebra

Invariant tensors descend from the Lie algebra of symmetric matrices.

Printed pp. 299-341SL(n,R)/SO(n)
Show that the general linear group acts transitively on P(n) by congruence, with the orthogonal group stabilising the identity. Hence P(n) is the homogeneous space GL(n)/O(n), and its trace-one slice is SL(n,R)/SO(n). Emphasise that geometry is now group theory: invariant tensors descend from the Lie algebra.

Invariant Metric and Geodesics

Metric at $I$

$\langle X,Y\rangle_I=\mathrm{tr}(XY)$ on symmetric $X,Y$.

Geodesics

Through $A$: $t\mapsto A\exp(tX)A^{T}$ via the matrix exponential.

Geodesic from I

$\gamma(t)=\exp(tX),\quad X=X^{T}$

Printed pp. 299-341Invariant Metric and Geodesics
Define the invariant Riemannian metric at the identity by the trace form on symmetric matrices, transported by the group action. Geodesics through A take the form t maps to A exp(tX) A-transpose. Stress that the matrix exponential, not straight lines in the ambient space, parametrises the genuine geodesics of this curved cone.

Distance via Generalised Eigenvalues

Formula

$d(A,B)^2=\sum_i (\log\lambda_i)^2$ where $\lambda_i$ solve $\det(B-\lambda A)=0$.

Equivalently

$d(A,B)=\big\lVert \log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\big\rVert_F$.

Log of spectrum

$d(I,B)=\Big(\textstyle\sum_i (\log\mu_i)^2\Big)^{1/2}$

$\mu_i$ = eigenvalues of $B$.

Reading

Logarithms turn multiplicative spectral data into additive distance.

Printed pp. 299-341Distance and Eigenvalues
The invariant distance between two positive matrices is the root-sum-of-squares of the logarithms of their generalised eigenvalues. We connect this to solving the generalised eigenvalue problem and to the logarithm of the relative matrix. Highlight that logarithms convert multiplicative spectral data into an additive Euclidean-flavoured distance.

Why $P(n)$ Is CAT(0)

Hadamard manifold

Complete, simply connected, sectional curvature $\le 0$.

Cartan–Hadamard

Such a manifold is CAT(0): unique geodesics, convex metric.

Curvature sign

$K(X,Y)=-\tfrac14\,\lVert[X,Y]\rVert^2\le 0$

Printed pp. 299-341Non-Positive Curvature
Explain why P(n) is a Hadamard manifold: it is complete, simply connected, and has non-positive sectional curvature, computed from brackets of symmetric matrices. By Cartan-Hadamard it is therefore CAT(0). Underscore that convexity of the distance function and unique geodesics are the payoff we will reuse repeatedly.

Flats and Rank

Flat

A complete totally geodesic isometric copy of $\mathbb{E}^k$.

In $P(n)$

Maximal abelian subalgebras: simultaneously diagonalisable directions.

Rank

$\mathrm{rank}=\max$ flat dimension; $n-1$ on the trace-one slice.

Printed pp. 299-341Flats and Rank
A flat is a complete totally geodesic isometrically embedded Euclidean subspace. In P(n) flats arise from maximal abelian subalgebras of symmetric matrices, the simultaneously diagonalisable directions. The rank is the maximal flat dimension; for P(n) it equals n minus one on the trace-one slice. Emphasise rank as the obstruction to hyperbolicity.

Cartan Decomposition and Weyl Group

Lie algebra split

$\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}$: antisymmetric $\oplus$ symmetric.

Polar form

$g=k\,\exp(H)$, $k\in\mathrm{SO}(n)$, $H$ diagonal.

Weyl group

$W=N(\mathfrak{a})/Z(\mathfrak{a})\cong S_n$

Permutes the eigenvalues, slicing each flat into chambers.

Concretely

This is diagonalisation $A=Q\Lambda Q^{T}$ read group-theoretically.

Printed pp. 299-341Cartan Decomposition
State the Cartan decomposition of the Lie algebra into symmetric and antisymmetric parts, and the polar form writing any element as orthogonal times exponential of diagonal. The Weyl group permutes eigenvalues, cutting each flat into chambers. Make the link between abstract decomposition and the concrete diagonalisation students already know.

Tits Boundary and Spherical Buildings

Rank one

Boundary is a featureless sphere; the space is hyperbolic-like.

Higher rank

$\partial_T X$ is a spherical building of apartments and chambers.

Apartments

Boundaries of maximal flats, glued along shared chambers.

Printed pp. 299-341Tits Boundary and Buildings
For rank at least two the boundary at infinity, with the Tits angular metric, is a spherical building: apartments are boundaries of flats glued along chambers. We contrast this with the rank-one case where the boundary is a featureless sphere. Stress that combinatorial rigidity emerges precisely in higher rank.
