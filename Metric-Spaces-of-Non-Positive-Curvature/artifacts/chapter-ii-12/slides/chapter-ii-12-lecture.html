A Group Acting on a Complex

The upstairs object

$G$ acts by cellular isometries on a simply connected complex $X$.

The downstairs object

The quotient $Y = G\backslash X$ is small; each cell remembers its stabilizer.

Stabilizer

$G_\sigma = \{\,g\in G : g\cdot\sigma = \sigma\,\}$

Printed pp. 367-396A Group Acting on a Complex
Start from the concrete picture. A group acts by cellular isometries on a simply connected complex, and we slice it into a fundamental domain. Each cell of the quotient remembers the subgroup that fixed it upstairs. The unfolded complex is rich; the quotient with labels is small. Our whole subject is the dictionary between these two.

Stratified Spaces and Strata

Partition by strata

A stratified space splits into pieces indexed by a poset; closures are unions of lower strata.

Action respects strata

$G$ permutes strata, so stabilizers stay constant along each stratum.

Poset condition

$\overline{S_\alpha} = \bigcup_{\beta \le \alpha} S_\beta$

Printed pp. 367-396Stratified Spaces and Strata
Before encoding groups we organise the space. A stratified space is partitioned into strata indexed by a poset, with each stratum's closure a union of lower strata. The action permutes strata, so stabilizers are constant along a stratum. This poset of strata is exactly the scaffold onto which we will hang the local group data.

Strict Fundamental Domain

One copy per orbit

A subcomplex $Y\subset X$ meeting each orbit with $Y \to G\backslash X$ a homeomorphism.

No self-gluing

Strictness: $G$ identifies no two cells of $Y$. The quotient is a clean copy.

Strictness

$Y \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} G\backslash X, \qquad g\sigma=\tau,\ \sigma,\tau\in Y \Rightarrow \sigma=\tau$

Printed pp. 367-396Strict Fundamental Domain
The simple case rests on a strict fundamental domain: a subcomplex meeting each orbit so that the quotient map restricted to it is a homeomorphism onto the quotient. No cell is identified with another by the group. This strictness is what lets stabilizers attach cleanly to quotient cells without choosing extra gluing data.

Simple Complex of Groups

Local groups

Each cell $\sigma$ of $Y$ carries a group $G_\sigma$.

Face inclusions

If $\tau$ is a face of $\sigma$, an injection $\psi_{\tau\sigma}: G_\sigma \hookrightarrow G_\tau$.

Functoriality

$\rho \le \tau \le \sigma \;\Rightarrow\; \psi_{\rho\tau}\circ\psi_{\tau\sigma} = \psi_{\rho\sigma}$

Printed pp. 367-396Simple Complex of Groups
Here is the definition. Over the poset of cells we place a local group at each cell and an injective homomorphism whenever one cell is a face of another, all compatible under composition. That functorial assignment, born from a strict domain, is a simple complex of groups. It is the static fingerprint of a dynamic action.

From Action to Data

Local group = stabilizer

Set $G_\sigma$ to be the stabilizer of a chosen lift of $\sigma$.

Inclusion = containment

Fixing a cell forces fixing its faces, so $G_\sigma \subseteq G_\tau$.

Determined data

$\tau \le \sigma \;\Rightarrow\; \mathrm{Stab}(\sigma) \subseteq \mathrm{Stab}(\tau)$

Commutes for free

All maps are inclusions of subgroups of one ambient $G$.

Printed pp. 367-396From Action to Data
Walk the forward map carefully. Given the action with strict domain, the local group is the stabilizer of a cell and each face inclusion is the literal containment of stabilizers, since fixing a larger cell forces fixing its faces. Everything commutes because these are honest subgroups of one ambient group. The data is determined, not invented.

The Basic Construction

Glue copies of Y

Take $\hat{G}\times Y$ and identify points using the local groups.

Recovers X

When the data came from an action, this rebuilds the original complex.

Basic construction

$D(Y,\hat{G}) = \big(\hat{G}\times Y\big)\big/\!\sim, \quad (g,y)\sim(gh,y)\ \text{for}\ h\in G_{\sigma(y)}$

Printed pp. 367-396The Basic Construction
To reverse the arrow we need a candidate group. From a complex of groups over a domain Y we form the basic construction: glue copies of Y indexed by group elements, factoring out the local group relations. When the data came from an action this rebuilds the original complex. In general it produces our best guess at a development.

Fundamental Group of a Complex of Groups

A colimit

$\hat{G}$ is assembled from the local groups along the face inclusions.

Higher graph of groups

Generalises amalgams and HNN extensions to dimension $\ge 2$.

Universal property

$\hat{G} = \varinjlim_{\sigma}\, G_\sigma, \qquad \iota_\sigma : G_\sigma \to \hat{G}$

Printed pp. 367-396Fundamental Group of a Complex of Groups
The reversing group is the fundamental group of the complex of groups, a colimit assembled from the local groups along the face inclusions, like a graph of groups generalised to higher dimension. Each local group maps into it. Whether those maps stay injective is precisely the subtle question of developability that we confront next.

Developability

Definition

Developable $\iff$ arises from an action on a simply connected complex $\iff$ each $\iota_\sigma$ is injective.

Simple ⇒ developable

Data from an action is always developable, by construction.

Abstract may fail

Local data can be globally inconsistent: $\iota_\sigma$ need not inject.

Printed pp. 367-396Developability
A complex of groups is developable when it actually arises from an action on a simply connected complex, equivalently when every local group injects into the fundamental group. Simple complexes are always developable by construction. Abstract ones need not be: the local data can be globally inconsistent, the higher-dimensional echo of an amalgam that collapses.

The Local Development

Test near a vertex

Data prescribes a link; unfold the vertex into its local development.

Link from cosets

The local link is assembled from cosets of the local groups.

Local criterion

All local developments genuine $\;\Rightarrow\;$ global development exists.

Printed pp. 367-396The Local Development
Developability is tested locally. Around each vertex the data prescribes a link, and the candidate development unfolds that vertex into a local development whose link is built from cosets of the local groups. If every local development is a genuine complex with the right link, the global development exists. Local consistency is the lever.

Non-Positive Curvature ⇒ Developable

Metrize locally

Give $Y$ a metric so each local development is non-positively curved (large links).

Cartan–Hadamard

Locally CAT(0) + simply connected $\Rightarrow$ globally CAT(0).

Theorem

NPC complex of groups $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ developable; development is CAT(0).

Printed pp. 367-396Non-Positive Curvature Implies Developable
Now the geometry pays off. Give the quotient a metric making each local development non-positively curved, so links satisfy the large-link condition. Then the basic construction is locally CAT(0), hence by the Cartan-Hadamard theorem simply connected and CAT(0). A CAT(0) space is contractible, so the action it supports proves developability. Curvature certifies consistency.
