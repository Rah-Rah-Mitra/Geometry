delta-Hyperbolic Spaces

Gromov's coarse negative curvature: triangles that are uniformly thin

One condition

Geodesic triangles are $\delta$-thin for a single uniform constant.

Why it matters

It is a quasi-isometry invariant and defines hyperbolic groups.

This chapter, printed pages 398 to 437, introduces Gromov's coarse negative curvature. The single governing idea is that geodesic triangles are uniformly thin. We motivate it as a large-scale shadow of classical negative curvature, robust enough to survive quasi-isometry and to define a class of groups.

The Thin-Triangles Condition

Definition

$[p,q]\subseteq N_\delta\big([p,r]\cup[r,q]\big)$

and likewise for the other two sides.

Uniform, not local

One $\delta\ge 0$ works for every triangle in the space at once.

Geodesic space

$X$ is geodesic and the bound holds for all $p,q,r$.

Trees: the Limit $\delta = 0$

Tripods

In a tree every triangle is a tripod; each side lies on the union of the others exactly.

Slogan

Hyperbolic spaces look tree-like at large scale, up to the slack $\delta$.

Printed pp. 398-437Trees as the delta = 0 Case
Trees realise the extreme delta equals zero: triangles degenerate to tripods, so each side already lies on the union of the others. Use this limit to anchor intuition, then describe hyperbolic spaces as spaces that look tree-like at large scale up to the slack delta.

The Gromov Product

Definition

$(y\,|\,z)_x=\tfrac12\big(d(x,y)+d(x,z)-d(y,z)\big)$

Meaning

How long geodesics $[x,y]$ and $[x,z]$ stay close after leaving $x$.

Printed pp. 398-437The Gromov Product
Introduce the Gromov product as the overlap length of geodesics from a basepoint, measuring how long two rays travel together. Give the formula and the picture of the internal point of a tripod. This scalar repackages the metric and prepares the four-point reformulation of hyperbolicity.

The Four-Point Condition

Inequality

$(x\,|\,z)_w\ge\min\big((x\,|\,y)_w,(y\,|\,z)_w\big)-\delta$

No geodesics needed

Stated purely in the metric, so it makes sense in any metric space.

Equivalent form

Among the three pairwise sums $d(x,y)+d(z,w)$ etc., the largest two differ by at most $2\delta$.

Robustness

Constants shift between definitions, but the class of hyperbolic spaces is unchanged.

Printed pp. 398-437Four-Point Condition
Present the four-point inequality: the larger two of three pairwise sums differ by at most two delta. Emphasise that it needs no geodesics, only the metric, so it transfers to arbitrary metric spaces. Note the constants change but the class of hyperbolic spaces does not.

Quasi-Geodesics

$(\lambda,\varepsilon)$-quasi-geodesic

$\tfrac1\lambda|s-t|-\varepsilon\le d(c(s),c(t))\le\lambda|s-t|+\varepsilon$

Why coarse

Quasi-isometries only produce quasi-geodesics, so theorems must tolerate detours.

Printed pp. 398-437Quasi-Geodesics
Define quasi-geodesics with multiplicative and additive constants, allowing controlled detours and discontinuity. Contrast with genuine geodesics. Motivate them as what you actually get from a quasi-isometry, so any invariance theorem must tolerate this coarse, lattice-like sampling of a path rather than exact arc length.

Stability: the Morse Lemma

Theorem

In a $\delta$-hyperbolic space, every $(\lambda,\varepsilon)$-quasi-geodesic lies within Hausdorff distance $R=R(\delta,\lambda,\varepsilon)$ of a genuine geodesic with the same endpoints.

Engine

This rigidity drives nearly every later argument about hyperbolic spaces.

Printed pp. 398-437The Morse Lemma
State stability of quasi-geodesics: in a hyperbolic space a quasi-geodesic stays within a bounded Hausdorff distance R of a genuine geodesic joining its endpoints, with R depending only on delta and the quasi-geodesic constants. This rigidity is the technical engine of the whole theory.

Hyperbolicity is QI-Invariant

The argument

A quasi-isometry sends geodesics to quasi-geodesics; the Morse lemma straightens them, carrying thin triangles across.

Consequence

$X \sim_{\mathrm{QI}} Y,\; X\text{ hyperbolic}\;\Rightarrow\; Y\text{ hyperbolic}$

Coarse property

Hyperbolicity depends only on the coarse geometry, not on local structure.

Printed pp. 398-437Quasi-Isometry Invariance
Explain why the Morse lemma forces hyperbolicity to be a quasi-isometry invariant: a quasi-isometry carries geodesics to quasi-geodesics, which the lemma straightens, transporting thin triangles across. Hence hyperbolicity is a property of the coarse space, justifying the notion of a hyperbolic group via its Cayley graph.

Word-Hyperbolic Groups

Definition

$\Gamma$ is hyperbolic if some Cayley graph is $\delta$-hyperbolic; QI-invariance frees this from the generating set.

Isoperimetric criterion

$\mathrm{Area}(w)\le K\,|w|$ for null-homotopic $w$

Decision problems

Word problem solvable by Dehn's algorithm; conjugacy problem solvable via bounded conjugator length.

Finiteness

Hyperbolic groups are finitely presented with only finitely many conjugacy classes of finite subgroups.

Printed pp. 398-437Hyperbolic Groups
A finitely generated group is hyperbolic when some Cayley graph is hyperbolic; invariance makes this independent of the generating set. State the linear isoperimetric inequality characterisation and the consequences: solvable word and conjugacy problems through Dehn's algorithm and bounded conjugator length.

The Boundary at Infinity

Construction

$\partial X = \{\text{geodesic rays}\}/\!\sim$, or sequences $(x_n)$ with $(x_n\,|\,x_m)_o\to\infty$.

Topology

$\partial X$ is compact and metrizable; $\Gamma$ acts by homeomorphisms.

Printed pp. 398-437Boundary at Infinity
Construct the Gromov boundary as equivalence classes of geodesic rays, or sequences with Gromov product diverging. It is compact and metrizable, and the group acts on it by homeomorphisms. This boundary records the asymptotic directions and is itself a quasi-isometry invariant of the space.

The Visual (Gromov) Metric

Visual metric

$d_\varepsilon(\xi,\eta)\asymp e^{-\varepsilon\,(\xi\,|\,\eta)_o}$

Decay

Endpoints sharing a long initial overlap are exponentially close on $\partial X$.

Conformal gauge

Quasi-isometries induce quasi-symmetric boundary maps; the gauge is the invariant.

Printed pp. 398-437Visual Metric
Equip the boundary with a visual metric where distance decays exponentially in the Gromov product of two endpoints. Quasi-isometries induce quasi-symmetric maps of the boundary, so the conformal gauge is the real invariant. Mention the parameter epsilon controlling the exponential and the resulting metric family.

Takeaway

Coarse negative curvature is one constant $\delta$ with far-reaching algebraic consequences.
