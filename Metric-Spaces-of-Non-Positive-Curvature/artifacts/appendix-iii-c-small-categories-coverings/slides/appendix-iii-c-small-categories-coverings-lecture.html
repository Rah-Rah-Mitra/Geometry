Small Categories and scwols

Small category

Sets of objects and morphisms, associative composition, identities.

Without loops

No nonidentity morphism is invertible; no nonidentity endomorphisms.

Consequence

Composition strictly increases a partial order on objects.

scwol axiom

$a\,g\!=\!a\,(gf)\ \Rightarrow\ f=\mathrm{id}$

Why care

The loop-free condition makes the realization a genuine complex with non-degenerate simplices.

Printed pp. 573-583Small Categories and scwols
Recall that a small category has a set of objects and a set of morphisms with associative composition and identities. A scwol is a small category without loops: no nonidentity morphism is invertible and no nonidentity endomorphisms exist, so composable arrows strictly increase a partial order on objects. This loop-free condition keeps the geometric realization a genuine simplicial-type complex.

Geometric Realization

Vertices

One vertex per object of the category.

Simplices

A chain of $k$ composable arrows fills a $k$-simplex.

Metric

For a scwol, $|\mathcal{C}|$ is a piecewise Euclidean complex.

Printed pp. 573-583Geometric Realization
The geometric realization turns the category into a topological space. Objects become vertices, morphisms become edges, and each composable pair becomes a triangle filling in the relation. Chains of length k give k-simplices. For a scwol the realization is a piecewise Euclidean complex, and homotopy questions about the category become honest questions about this space.

Paths in a Category

Edge-path

An alternating word of arrows traversed forward or backward.

Reversal

An arrow may be walked against its direction, $f^{-1}$, even though it is no morphism.

Concatenation

Endpoints must match; this is partial composition of paths.

Edge-path

$c = f_n^{\pm}\cdots f_2^{\pm} f_1^{\pm}$

Loops

A path from $\sigma_0$ back to $\sigma_0$ is the discrete loop we will study.

Printed pp. 573-583Paths in a Category
A combinatorial path is an alternating word of arrows traversed forwards or backwards, with consecutive endpoints matching. Reversing an arrow is allowed even though it is not a morphism, which is exactly how we build groupoid-like inverses. Concatenation of paths is partial composition. These edge-paths are the discrete loops we will divide by homotopy.

Homotopy of Paths

Cancellation

$f f^{-1}$ and $f^{-1} f$ delete to the identity.

Composition move

Replace $g f$ by the pair $g$ then $f$ (and back).

Geometry

Each move slides the path across one triangle of $|\mathcal{C}|$.

Printed pp. 573-583Homotopy of Paths
Two paths are homotopic under the equivalence generated by three moves: cancel an arrow against its formal reverse, and replace a composite arrow by the two factors that compose to it, in either order. Each move corresponds to sliding across a triangle of the realization. Homotopy classes of edge-paths are the morphisms of the fundamental groupoid.

The Fundamental Group $\pi_1$

Base object

Fix $\sigma_0$; take closed edge-paths at $\sigma_0$.

Group law

Concatenation modulo homotopy; reversal is the inverse.

Base independence

For a connected category, $\pi_1$ is well defined up to isomorphism.

Definition

$\pi_1(\mathcal{C},\sigma_0) = \{\text{loops at }\sigma_0\}/\!\simeq$

Groupoid view

$\pi_1$ is the vertex group of the fundamental groupoid $\pi(\mathcal{C})$.

Printed pp. 573-583The Fundamental Group
Fix a base object sigma zero. The fundamental group pi_1 of the category at that base is the group of homotopy classes of closed edge-paths beginning and ending there, with concatenation as product. For a connected category the choice of base only changes pi_1 up to isomorphism, exactly as in the topological setting it generalizes.

Coverings of Small Categories

Covering functor

$p:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{C}$ surjective and a local isomorphism.

Star condition

Over each object $p$ is a bijection on entering and leaving arrows.

Local picture

Each star pulls back to a disjoint union of copies.

Printed pp. 573-583Coverings of Small Categories
A covering is a functor from a category onto our base category that is a local isomorphism: over each object it restricts to a bijection between the arrows entering or leaving the fibers. Locally it looks like a disjoint union of copies of stars. This is the categorical translation of an unramified covering space and it lifts paths uniquely.

Path Lifting and Monodromy

Unique lifting

A path and a chosen start in the fiber determine one lift.

Monodromy

A loop permutes the fiber over $\sigma_0$.

Action

$\pi_1$ acts on $p^{-1}(\sigma_0)$ from this lifting.

Monodromy

$\rho:\pi_1(\mathcal{C},\sigma_0)\to \mathrm{Sym}\big(p^{-1}(\sigma_0)\big)$

Universal case

On the universal cover this action is free and transitive.

Printed pp. 573-583Path Lifting and Monodromy
Unique path lifting holds: given a path downstairs and a start point in the fiber, there is exactly one lift. A closed path therefore permutes the fiber over the base object, giving the monodromy action. The fundamental group acts on the fiber, and for the universal cover that action is simply transitive and free.

The Galois Correspondence

Dictionary

Connected coverings $\leftrightarrow$ conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1$.

Image subgroup

$H = p_*\,\pi_1(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}})$ classifies the cover.

Regular case

$H\trianglelefteq\pi_1$ gives deck group $\pi_1/H$.

Printed pp. 573-583The Galois Correspondence
Connected coverings of a connected category, up to isomorphism, correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of pi_1. The subgroup is the image of the covering's fundamental group; normal subgroups give regular coverings whose deck group is the quotient. The trivial subgroup yields the universal cover, the top of the lattice.

The Universal Cover

Simply connected

Its fundamental group is trivial.

Free action

$\pi_1$ acts freely with quotient $\mathcal{C}$.

Construction

Objects are homotopy classes of paths out of $\sigma_0$.

Quotient

$\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\,/\,\pi_1(\mathcal{C},\sigma_0)\ \cong\ \mathcal{C}$

Geometry

For a scwol the universal cover is the developable, tree-like model.

Printed pp. 573-583The Universal Cover
The universal cover is the simply connected covering on which pi_1 acts freely with quotient the original category. Its objects are homotopy classes of paths out of the base. This is the object that carries the fundamental group's action and makes the cover a tree-like or developable space when the category is a scwol.

Why This Builds Complexes of Groups

Local groups

Stabilizers of the action become the data of a complex of groups.

Development

The universal cover is the development on which $\pi_1$ acts.

Developability

The action recovers the geometry from the local groups.

Slogan

$\text{scwol} + \pi_1 \curvearrowright \widetilde{\mathcal{C}} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{complex of groups}$

Bridge

The covering theory here is exactly the engine III.C feeds into the next chapter.

Printed pp. 573-583Why This Builds Complexes of Groups
A complex of groups is, up to development, a scwol with a fundamental group and an action on its universal cover. The local groups are the stabilizers, the universal cover is the development, and developability means the action recovers the geometry. The covering theory of this appendix is precisely the engine for that construction.
