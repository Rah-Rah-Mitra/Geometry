Definitions and Characterizations of CAT(k) Spaces

Bounding curvature from above by comparison with model triangles

The idea

Compare every geodesic triangle against a model triangle of the same side lengths.

The slogan

A CAT(k) space has triangles no fatter than those in the model space $M_k$.

Why it matters

One inequality yields convexity, unique geodesics, and fixed-point theorems.

Printed pp. 158-168CAT(k) Spaces
This unit opens part two of the course and turns the comparison philosophy into a precise definition. We bound curvature from above by comparing real geodesic triangles against flat or curved model triangles. The slogan to keep in mind is that a CAT(k) space has triangles no fatter than the model.

The Model Spaces $M_k$

Three regimes

$k>0$: scaled sphere. $k=0$: Euclidean plane. $k<0$: scaled hyperbolic plane.

Diameter

$\operatorname{diam} M_k = \pi/\sqrt{k}$ when $k>0$, otherwise infinite.

Constant curvature model

$M_k:\ \text{simply connected, curvature } \equiv k$

Printed pp. 158-168Model Spaces M_k
Before comparing anything we fix the yardsticks. The model spaces are the sphere of curvature k, the Euclidean plane at curvature zero, and the hyperbolic plane scaled by negative k. The diameter of M_k is finite exactly when k is positive, which forces a size restriction on the triangles we may compare.

Geodesic Triangles

Definition

Three vertices $p,q,r$ joined by chosen geodesic segments $[p,q],[q,r],[r,p]$.

The only data

The construction uses just the three side lengths $d(p,q),d(q,r),d(r,p)$.

Perimeter

$L = d(p,q)+d(q,r)+d(r,p)$

Printed pp. 158-168Geodesic Triangles
A geodesic triangle is three points joined by three chosen geodesic segments. We insist the space be geodesic so segments exist, and we record the side lengths. These three numbers are the only data the comparison construction uses, so the triangle is summarised entirely by its perimeter and edge lengths.

The Comparison Triangle

Construction

Build $\bar\triangle \subset M_k$ with the same side lengths as $\triangle(p,q,r)$.

Existence

Unique up to isometry when the perimeter $L < 2\,\operatorname{diam} M_k$.

Comparison points

Each $x\in[p,q]$ maps to $\bar x\in[\bar p,\bar q]$ at equal arc distance.

Printed pp. 158-168Comparison Triangle
Given a triangle in the space we build a triangle in the model with identical side lengths. In Euclidean and hyperbolic models such a triangle always exists and is unique up to isometry; in the spherical model we additionally need the perimeter below twice the model diameter. Each real point gets a comparison point on the matching edge.

The CAT(k) Inequality

Thinness condition

$d(x,y)\ \le\ d_{M_k}(\bar x,\bar y)$

For all points

Holds for every $x,y$ on the sides of every geodesic triangle.

Reading

Real chords are no longer than model chords: the triangle is thin.

Printed pp. 158-168The CAT(k) Inequality
Here is the defining condition. For every pair of points on the real triangle, the distance between them is at most the distance between their comparison points in the model. Triangles in a CAT(k) space are therefore thinner than the model triangles of the same side lengths. This single inequality drives the entire theory.

Thin Triangles versus Fat Triangles

Thin (CAT(0))

Interior chords fall inside the flat comparison chord; non-positive curvature.

Fat (positive)

Chords bulge outward past the model; curvature bounded below, not above.

Printed pp. 158-168Thin versus Fat
The picture contrasts a non-positively curved triangle, whose chord falls inside the flat comparison chord, with a positively curved triangle that bulges outward. Thin means each interior chord is shorter than its model counterpart; fat means longer. CAT(0) captures the thin regime that Alexandrov identified as non-positive curvature.

CAT(0): Non-Positive Curvature

Definition

$X$ geodesic and CAT$(0)$ vs. $\mathbb{E}^2$

Alexandrov

Synthetic curvature $\le 0$: no smoothness or Riemannian metric required.

Examples

Trees, Euclidean buildings, products and convex subsets of CAT(0) spaces.

Printed pp. 158-168CAT(0) = Non-Positive Curvature
Setting k to zero gives the central case. A CAT(0) space is geodesic and satisfies the comparison against the Euclidean plane. This is Alexandrov's synthetic definition of curvature bounded above by zero, requiring no smoothness. Trees, Euclidean buildings, and products of CAT(0) spaces all qualify and inherit strong convexity properties.

Convexity of the Metric

Equivalent face

In CAT(0), $t\mapsto d(\gamma(t),\sigma(t))$ is convex for geodesics $\gamma,\sigma$.

Convexity inequality

$d(\gamma_t,\sigma_t)\le (1-t)\,d(\gamma_0,\sigma_0)+t\,d(\gamma_1,\sigma_1)$

Consequences

Unique geodesics, contractibility, nearest-point projection onto convex sets.

Printed pp. 158-168Convexity of the Metric
An equivalent face of the same condition is convexity. In a CAT(0) space the distance function between two geodesics is a convex function of the parameter, so geodesics between nearby points stay close. Convexity gives uniqueness of geodesics and the contraction onto convex subsets that later powers fixed-point theorems.

The Law of Cosines Bound

Computing the model

The comparison distance comes from the law of cosines in $M_k$.

Euclidean midpoint test

$d(p,m)^2 \le \tfrac12 d(p,q)^2 + \tfrac12 d(p,r)^2 - \tfrac14 d(q,r)^2$

From geometry to arithmetic

Three side lengths and a median give a number the notebook samples.

Printed pp. 158-168The Law of Cosines Bound
The comparison distance can be computed by the model law of cosines. Writing the inequality at the midpoint yields a numerical test on three side lengths and a median, turning a geometric statement into arithmetic. This is exactly the check the notebook samples on a tree-like triangle to confirm the Euclidean comparison numerically.

The CN Inequality (Bruhat-Tits)

Courbure negative

The midpoint case of comparison, stated purely with squared distances.

CN inequality

$d(x,m)^2 \le \tfrac12 d(x,p)^2 + \tfrac12 d(x,q)^2 - \tfrac14 d(p,q)^2$

Equivalence

For complete geodesic spaces, CN $\Longleftrightarrow$ CAT(0).

Printed pp. 158-168The CN Inequality
Bruhat and Tits packaged the midpoint case as the courbure negative inequality. It bounds the squared distance from a point to a midpoint by averaging the squared distances to the endpoints minus a quarter of the squared edge. For complete spaces the CN inequality is equivalent to the full CAT(0) condition.

Equivalent Characterizations

4-point condition

Comparison tested on quadruples of points instead of full triangles.

Reshetnyak gluing

Glue CAT(0) spaces along complete convex subsets and stay CAT(0).

Perimeter condition

Bounds packaged so pieces assemble into a global comparison space.

Printed pp. 158-168Four-Point and Reshetnyak
Several equivalent characterizations exist. The four-point condition tests comparison on quadruples; the Reshetnyak gluing and perimeter conditions let us assemble CAT(0) spaces from pieces. Each repackages thinness in a form convenient for a different proof, and all coincide for complete geodesic spaces.

Local Bounds and Monotonicity in $k$

Curvature $\le k$ locally

Every point has a neighbourhood that is itself a CAT(k) space.

Monotonicity

$\text{CAT}(k)\ \Rightarrow\ \text{CAT}(k')\quad\text{for } k'\ge k$

Looking ahead

Local bounds globalize under completeness and simple connectivity.

Printed pp. 158-168Local Bounds and Monotonicity
Curvature bounded above by k locally means every point has a CAT(k) neighbourhood. Comparison is monotone in the bound: a CAT(k) space is automatically CAT(k') for any larger k', since the model triangles only grow fatter. We close by previewing how local bounds globalize under completeness and simple connectivity.
