The Riemann Curvature Tensor

Definition

$R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$

Meaning

$R$ is the failure of second covariant derivatives to commute, i.e. the holonomy of an infinitesimal loop.

Symmetries

$R$ is antisymmetric in each pair and pair-symmetric, so $\langle R(X,Y)Y,X\rangle$ is the quantity that survives.

Reduction

These symmetries let us collapse $R$ to a single scalar attached to each tangent 2-plane.

Printed pp. 169-174The Riemann Curvature Tensor
Recall the curvature tensor as the failure of second covariant derivatives to commute. Stress that R measures path dependence of parallel transport around an infinitesimal loop, and that all its symmetries let us extract a single number from any tangent two-plane. Keep this tensorial, but anchored to transport intuition.

Sectional Curvature of a 2-Plane

Definition

$K(\sigma)=\dfrac{\langle R(u,v)v,\,u\rangle}{\lVert u\rVert^2\lVert v\rVert^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2}$

Well-defined

$K$ depends only on the plane $\sigma=\mathrm{span}(u,v)$, not the chosen basis.

Printed pp. 169-174Sectional Curvature of a 2-Plane
Define sectional curvature by feeding the curvature tensor an orthonormal pair spanning a tangent plane. Emphasize that K depends only on the plane sigma, not the chosen basis, and that in dimension two it reduces to Gauss curvature. This scalar is the invariant the comparison theory reads.

Curvature as Geodesic Spreading

Positive $K$

Nearby geodesics converge and refocus, as on a sphere.

Negative $K$

Nearby geodesics diverge faster than straight lines, as in hyperbolic space.

Zero $K$

Separation grows linearly, the Euclidean baseline.

Printed pp. 169-174Curvature as Geodesic Spreading
Give the kinematic meaning. Fire a fan of geodesics from a point inside a two-plane and watch nearby rays. Positive sectional curvature focuses them back together, negative curvature drives them apart faster than in flat space. This converging-versus-spreading picture is the visual heart of the appendix.

Jacobi Fields Quantify Spreading

Jacobi equation

$\dfrac{D^2 J}{dt^2} + R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma = 0$

Reading it

$J$ is the infinitesimal separation between neighbouring geodesics; the curvature term is its restoring force.

Scalar form

Along a 2-plane, $j'' + K\,j = 0$: positive $K$ makes $j$ oscillate and refocus, negative $K$ makes it grow exponentially.

Why it is the engine

Bounding $K$ bounds $J$, and bounding $J$ bounds how far apart triangle vertices drift.

Printed pp. 169-174Jacobi Fields Quantify Spreading
Make the spreading precise with Jacobi fields. The Jacobi equation has the sectional curvature as its coefficient, so the sign of K directly governs whether the separation between neighbouring geodesics grows or shrinks. This is the analytic engine behind every comparison estimate that follows.

The Model Spaces $M_k$

Constant curvature models

$M_k = \begin{cases} \mathbb{S}^2_{1/\sqrt{k}} & k>0\\ \mathbb{E}^2 & k=0\\ \mathbb{H}^2_{1/\sqrt{-k}} & k<0 \end{cases}$

Role

Each $M_k$ is the complete, simply connected surface of constant curvature $k$, the yardstick for comparison.

Printed pp. 169-174Model Spaces M_k
Recall the comparison models. For each real k there is a complete simply connected surface of constant curvature k: the sphere when k is positive, the Euclidean plane at zero, and the hyperbolic plane scaled when k is negative. Triangles in the manifold are compared against triangles drawn in M_k.

Comparison Triangles in $M_k$

Construction

For a geodesic triangle $\triangle(p,q,r)$ build $\bar\triangle(\bar p,\bar q,\bar r)$ in $M_k$ with identical side lengths.

CAT(k) inequality

$d(x,y)\;\le\;d_{M_k}(\bar x,\bar y)$

In words

Every chord of the real triangle is no longer than the matching chord of the model.

Printed pp. 169-174Comparison Triangles in M_k
Explain the comparison triangle construction. Given a geodesic triangle in the manifold, build the triangle in M_k with the same three side lengths. CAT(k) compares chord distances inside the real triangle against the corresponding chords of this model triangle. The inequality direction encodes the curvature bound.

Rauch: The Local Engine

Hypothesis

$K(\sigma)\le k \quad\text{for all 2-planes }\sigma$

Rauch comparison

An upper bound on $K$ bounds Jacobi field growth from below, so geodesics spread at least as fast as in $M_k$.

Consequence

Greater spreading means vertices sit closer along chords: triangles become at least as thin as the $M_k$ comparison triangle.

Locality

The estimate holds inside a normal neighbourhood, giving the manifold a local $\mathrm{CAT}(k)$ structure.

Printed pp. 169-174Rauch and the Upper Bound
State the Rauch comparison theorem as the local engine. Bounding sectional curvature above by k controls Jacobi field growth from below, which forces geodesics to spread no slower than in M_k. Locally this makes triangles at least as thin as their comparison triangles, the defining CAT(k) inequality.

The Equivalence: Thin Triangles

Equivalence (local)

$K\le k \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{triangles thinner than } M_k$

Toponogov

Globalizes the local thin-triangle estimate to all triangles when the manifold is complete.

Match

Toponogov's globalization upgrades the local estimate to a global one under completeness, matching the metric CAT(k) definition exactly.

The Reverse: Curvature Bounded Below

Alexandrov bound

$K\ge k \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{triangles fatter than } M_k$

Flip the inequality

Now every chord of the real triangle is at least as long as the model chord.

Two distinct theories

Lower bounds (Alexandrov, $\mathrm{CBB}$) and upper bounds ($\mathrm{CAT}$) are genuinely different worlds, not mirror conveniences.

Our focus

This appendix follows the upper bound, the side that feeds $\mathrm{CAT}(k)$ and nonpositive curvature.

Smooth NPC $=$ Smooth $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$

Headline theorem

$K\le 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{locally } \mathrm{CAT}(0)$

Hadamard manifolds

Complete simply connected $K\le 0$ manifolds are globally $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$.

Embedding

Classical nonpositive curvature sits inside the synthetic theory as its smooth special case.

Payoff

Every $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ theorem specializes to such manifolds while reaching beyond them to singular spaces like trees and complexes.

Printed pp. 169-174Smooth NPC = Smooth CAT(0)
Land the headline. A smooth Riemannian manifold has nonpositive sectional curvature if and only if it is locally CAT(0). This embeds classical Hadamard manifold geometry inside the synthetic metric theory, so every CAT(0) theorem specializes to nonpositively curved manifolds while extending to singular spaces beyond them.
