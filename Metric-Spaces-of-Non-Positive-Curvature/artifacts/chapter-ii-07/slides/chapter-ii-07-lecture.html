Semisimple Isometries and Min Sets

Displacement

$d_\gamma(x)=d(x,\gamma x)$ measures how far $\gamma$ moves a point.

Semisimple

$\gamma$ is semisimple when $d_\gamma$ attains its infimum.

Min set

$\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)=\{x:d_\gamma(x)=|\gamma|\}$.

Translation length

$|\gamma| = \inf_{x\in X} d(x,\gamma x)$

Printed pp. 244-259Semisimple Isometries and Min Sets
Recall that an isometry is semisimple when its displacement function attains its infimum. Define the translation length and the min set as the locus where that infimum is realized. Stress that for a hyperbolic semisimple isometry the min set is a union of parallel axes, the raw material every later construction will exploit.

The Flat Torus Theorem (Statement)

Theorem

Let $A\cong\mathbb{Z}^n$ act properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space $X$. Then $X$ contains an $A$-invariant flat $\mathbb{E}^n$ on which $A$ acts cocompactly by translations.

Hypotheses matter

Proper, semisimple, free abelian — each is load-bearing.

Invariant flat

An isometrically embedded $\mathbb{E}^n\subseteq X$.

Cocompact

$A$ acts as a full-rank lattice of translations.

Quotient

$\mathbb{E}^n/A$ is a flat $n$-torus $T^n$.

Printed pp. 244-259The Flat Torus Theorem
State the theorem precisely: a free abelian group of rank n acting properly by semisimple isometries leaves invariant an isometrically embedded copy of Euclidean n-space, and acts on it cocompactly by translations. Underline each hypothesis, since dropping properness or semisimplicity breaks the conclusion in concrete ways we revisit later.

Building the Flat by Induction

Base case

One generator yields an axis: a flat $\mathbb{E}^1$.

Split the min set

$\mathrm{Min}(\gamma_n)\cong Y_0\times\mathbb{R}$ houses the rest.

Apply hypothesis

$\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$ acts on $Y_0$; get a flat $\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$.

Reassemble

$\mathbb{E}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{E}^n$.

Printed pp. 244-259Building the Flat by Induction
Walk through the induction on rank. A single generator gives an axis; the product splitting of its min set houses the remaining commuting generators, which act on the convex factor. Apply the inductive hypothesis there to obtain a flat of one lower dimension, then reassemble the line factor to lift it to full rank.

The Invariant Flat $\mathbb{E}^n$

Isometric

Euclidean geometry holds exactly on the flat, not merely up to homeomorphism.

Invariant

The whole group $\mathbb{Z}^n$ preserves the flat.

Lattice

Commuting translations generate a rank-$n$ lattice $\Lambda$.

The Flat Torus Quotient

Cocompact action

$\mathbb{Z}^n$ acts by translations with compact quotient.

Quotient

$\mathbb{E}^n / \Lambda \;\cong\; T^n$

Fundamental domain

A parallelepiped spanned by the generating vectors.

Explain how the lattice of translations yields the quotient. Because Z^n acts cocompactly by translations on E^n, the quotient is a compact flat n-torus, the geometric object that names the theorem. The fundamental domain is a parallelepiped whose side vectors are the translation vectors of a generating set.

The Splitting Theorem

Theorem

If a complete CAT(0) space $X$ contains a geodesic line $c$, then the union of all lines parallel to $c$ splits isometrically as $\mathbb{R}\times Y$.

Invariant version

An invariant line gives a $G$-equivariant splitting $X=\mathbb{R}\times Y$.

The line factor

The $\mathbb{R}$ direction carries the translation.

The cross-section

$Y$ collects basepoints of parallel lines.

Engine

This is exactly the inductive step of the Flat Torus Theorem.

Printed pp. 244-259The Splitting Theorem
Introduce the Splitting Theorem: if a CAT(0) space contains a geodesic line whose two endpoint families are preserved, equivalently an invariant line, then the space splits isometrically as a product of the real line with a convex subspace. This is the engine behind the inductive step in the Flat Torus Theorem.

How an Axis Forces a Splitting

Parallel lines

Two complete geodesics at bounded distance bound a flat strip.

Convex union

All lines parallel to $c$ form a convex subspace.

Real factor

That convex set splits off the $\mathbb{R}$ direction of $c$.

The Solvable Subgroup Theorem

Theorem

If $G$ acts properly cocompactly by isometries on a CAT(0) space, then every solvable subgroup of $G$ is virtually abelian and acts cocompactly on a flat.

Sharp limit

Many polycyclic groups cannot be CAT(0).

Virtually abelian

A finite-index subgroup is free abelian $\mathbb{Z}^k$.

Via flats

The abelian part stabilizes a flat by the Flat Torus Theorem.

Excluded

E.g. the Heisenberg and Sol-type groups are ruled out.

Printed pp. 244-259The Solvable Subgroup Theorem
State the Solvable Subgroup Theorem: a solvable subgroup of a group acting properly cocompactly by isometries on a CAT(0) space is virtually abelian, and in fact finitely generated and acting on a flat. Highlight that this sharply limits which solvable groups can be CAT(0), ruling out many polycyclic examples.

Consequences for CAT(0) Groups

Centralizers split

The centralizer of a hyperbolic element acts on a product $\mathbb{R}\times Y$.

No distortion

Abelian subgroups are undistorted: they embed as quasi-flats.

Bounded rank

The rank of $\mathbb{Z}^k\le G$ is bounded by the geometric dimension.

Printed pp. 244-259Consequences for CAT(0) Groups
Survey the structural payoff. Centralizers of hyperbolic elements split, polynomial distortion is forbidden, and abelian subgroups have bounded rank tied to the geometry. These corollaries turn the Flat Torus Theorem into a working toolkit for distinguishing CAT(0) groups from other classes of non-positively curved groups.
