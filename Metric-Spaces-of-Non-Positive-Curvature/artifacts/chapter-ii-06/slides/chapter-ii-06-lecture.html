The Displacement Function

Definition

For an isometry $g$, the displacement at $x$ measures how far $g$ pushes it.

Convexity

In a CAT(0) space $d_g$ is convex along every geodesic — no false minima.

Displacement

$d_g(x) = d(x, gx)$

Convex combination

$d_g(\gamma(t)) \le (1-t)\,d_g(p) + t\,d_g(q)$

Printed pp. 228-243The Displacement Function
Define the displacement function as the distance from a point to its image. Emphasize that convexity of the metric in a CAT(0) space makes this function convex along geodesics, so it has no spurious local minima. That single convexity fact is what lets us reason cleanly about where motion is smallest.

Translation Length

Infimum of motion

The translation length $|g|$ is the global infimum of displacement.

Conjugation invariant

$|hgh^{-1}|=|g|$ — a property of the conjugacy class.

Definition

$|g| = \inf_{x} \, d_g(x) = \inf_{x} d(x, gx)$

Attained or not?

The infimum need not be a minimum — that distinction is the whole story.

Printed pp. 228-243Translation Length
Introduce translation length as the infimum of displacement over the whole space, an isometry invariant under conjugation. Warn that an infimum need not be a minimum: the question of whether it is attained, and where, is exactly what separates the three behavioural types we are about to meet.

Three Types of Isometry

Elliptic

$|g|=0$ attained — a fixed point exists.

Hyperbolic (axial)

$|g|>0$ attained — translates along an axis.

Parabolic

Infimum not attained — no minimizing point.

Printed pp. 228-243Three Types of Isometry
Lay out the trichotomy: elliptic isometries fix a point with zero translation length, hyperbolic ones attain a positive infimum, and parabolic ones never attain their infimum. Tell students this slide is the map for the rest of the lecture, and that semisimple simply means elliptic or hyperbolic.

Elliptic Isometries

Fixed point

$g$ is elliptic iff it fixes some point; then $|g|=0$ is attained.

Bruhat–Tits

A bounded orbit in a complete CAT(0) space has a fixed circumcentre.

Characterization

$\exists\, p:\; gp = p \;\Longleftrightarrow\; |g|=0 \text{ attained}$

Printed pp. 228-243Elliptic Isometries
An elliptic isometry has a fixed point, so its translation length is zero and is attained. Connect this to the Bruhat-Tits fixed-point theorem: a group with a bounded orbit in a complete CAT(0) space fixes the circumcentre. This is the engine behind compactness arguments later in the book.

Hyperbolic Isometries and Axes

Axis

$g$ axial iff a geodesic line is translated: $g\cdot\gamma(t)=\gamma(t+|g|)$.

Off the axis

Displacement rises convexly as you move away from any axis.

Parallel axes

Several parallel axes may coexist; they fill out the min-set.

Printed pp. 228-243Hyperbolic Isometries and Axes
A hyperbolic, or axial, isometry attains a positive translation length along an invariant geodesic, its axis, on which it acts as pure translation. Point out that there may be many parallel axes, and that the displacement grows convexly as you step off any axis. The axis is the geometric heart of the type.

Parabolic Isometries

No minimum

$|g|$ exists as an infimum but is never attained — the min-set is empty.

Boundary behaviour

Orbits accumulate at a single point of the boundary at infinity.

Defining property

$|g|=\inf_x d_g(x)\ \text{not attained};\quad \mathrm{Min}(g)=\varnothing$

Printed pp. 228-243Parabolic Isometries
A parabolic isometry has an infimum of displacement that is never attained; the minimizing set is empty. Use the horoball picture: orbits accumulate at a single boundary point without any invariant geodesic line. Reassure students that parabolics are precisely the obstruction to semisimplicity.

The Min-Set

Definition

$\mathrm{Min}(g)$ is the set where displacement equals translation length.

Closed & convex

It is a closed convex subset, mapped to itself by $g$.

Min-set

$\mathrm{Min}(g) = \{\,x : d_g(x) = |g|\,\}$

By type

Elliptic: fixed-point set. Hyperbolic: union of axes. Parabolic: empty.

Printed pp. 228-243The Min-Set
Define the min-set as the points where displacement equals translation length. It is closed and convex, and the isometry maps it to itself. Stress that for an elliptic isometry the min-set is its fixed-point set, for hyperbolic it is the union of axes, and for parabolic it is empty.

Splitting of the Min-Set

Product structure

For hyperbolic $g$, the min-set splits as $Y\times\mathbb{R}$ isometrically.

The line factor

The $\mathbb{R}$ factor carries the translation; $Y$ is the cross-section of axes.

Consequence

One axis suffices; this feeds the flat torus theorem.

Splitting theorem

$\mathrm{Min}(g) \cong Y \times \mathbb{R},\quad g(y,t)=(y,\,t+|g|)$

Printed pp. 228-243Splitting of the Min-Set
State the structure theorem: for a hyperbolic isometry the min-set splits isometrically as a product of a convex set with the real line, the line factor carrying the translation. Explain that this is why studying one axis suffices, and how the product structure feeds the flat torus theorem.

Semisimple vs Parabolic

Semisimple

Elliptic or hyperbolic: the infimum $|g|$ is attained.

Parabolic

The infimum is not attained — the genuine non-semisimple case.

Dichotomy

$g \text{ semisimple} \iff \mathrm{Min}(g)\neq\varnothing$

When semisimplicity holds

In proper cocompact settings, infinite-order group elements are typically semisimple.

Printed pp. 228-243Semisimple vs Parabolic
Contrast semisimple isometries, elliptic or hyperbolic, with parabolic ones. Note that whether an isometry is semisimple can depend on completeness and properness of the space. Mention that in proper cocompact settings every isometry of an infinite-order element of the group tends to be semisimple, which is highly desirable.

Commuting Isometries and Products

Preserved min-sets

If $gh=hg$, then $h$ leaves $\mathrm{Min}(g)$ invariant, and vice versa.

Shared axes

Commuting hyperbolics share the product splitting of their min-sets.

Product length

$\big| |gh| - |g| \big| \le |h|,\qquad |g^n| = n\,|g|$

Flats appear

Free abelian groups of semisimple isometries produce invariant flats.

Printed pp. 228-243Commuting Isometries and Products
Discuss how commuting isometries preserve each other's min-sets, so a commuting hyperbolic shares invariant axes structure. Explain that the translation length of a product is controlled by the individual lengths, and that abelian subgroups acting freely produce flats through the splitting machinery of the previous slides.

Synthesis and Recap

The invariant

$d_g$ convex; $|g|=\inf d_g$ is conjugation-invariant.

The trichotomy

Elliptic (fixed point), hyperbolic (axis), parabolic (no min).

The structure

$\mathrm{Min}(g)$ convex; for hyperbolic it splits as $Y\times\mathbb{R}$.
