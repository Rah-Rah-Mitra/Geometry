Galilean Space-Time

Affine arena

Events live in a four-dimensional affine space $\mathbb{A}^4$ with no preferred origin.

Absolute time

A linear map $t:\mathbb{A}^4\to\mathbb{R}$ gives every pair of events a well-defined time interval.

Euclidean space

Each simultaneity slice $t=\text{const}$ is a Euclidean $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Structure

$$\mathbb{A}^4 \xrightarrow{\;t\;} \mathbb{R}, \qquad t=c \;\Rightarrow\; \mathbb{R}^3 \text{ Euclidean}$$

No cross-time space

Points at different instants cannot be identified without choosing an observer.

Experimental FactsGalilean Space-Time
Define the affine four-dimensional arena before any group acts on it. Emphasize that absolute time gives a well-defined notion of simultaneity and of time intervals, while space carries a Euclidean metric only within each instant. Warn students that there is no canonical way to identify points of space at different times until an observer is chosen.

The Principle of Relativity

Galileo's claim

The equations of motion take the same form in all inertial frames.

Closed cabin

No mechanical experiment inside reveals uniform translation of the cabin.

Invariance

$$\text{laws}(x) \;=\; \text{laws}(g\cdot x), \quad g \in \text{Gal}$$

Acceleration differs

Uniform motion is undetectable, but accelerated motion is felt at once.

Experimental FactsPrinciple of Relativity
State Galileo's principle plainly: the laws of motion are identical in every inertial frame, so no mechanical experiment performed inside a closed cabin reveals uniform motion. Contrast this with acceleration, which is detectable. This single experimental fact is what forces the transformation group and constrains every admissible force law later in the chapter.

The Galilean Group

Generators

  • Time translation: $t\mapsto t+s$  (1)
  • Space translation: $x\mapsto x+a$  (3)
  • Rotation: $x\mapsto Rx,\; R\in SO(3)$  (3)
  • Boost: $x\mapsto x+vt$  (3)

Ten parameters

Together these give a $10$-dimensional Lie group.

General element

$$\begin{aligned}t' &= t + s\\ x' &= Rx + vt + a\end{aligned}$$

Closure

Composing two Galilean maps yields another: the relativity principle demands exactly this symmetry.

Experimental FactsThe Galilean Group
Walk through the generators one at a time: time translation, space translation, rotation, and the boost to a uniformly moving frame. Count the ten parameters and note that composing any two such maps gives another, so they form a group. Reassure students this is exactly the symmetry that the relativity principle demands of mechanics.

The Galilean Boost

Two inertial frames in relative uniform motion $v$

Boost

$$x' = x - vt, \qquad t' = t$$

Velocities add

Differentiating gives $\dot{x}' = \dot{x} - v$: simple vector addition of velocities.

Simultaneity kept

Because $t'=t$, all observers agree on which events are simultaneous.

Experimental FactsThe Galilean Boost
Focus on the boost, the most distinctive Galilean transformation. Draw the two frames and show how a point's position picks up the term v times t while time itself is untouched. Highlight that velocities add by simple vector addition and that simultaneity is preserved, the feature that classical mechanics later trades away in relativity.

Newton's Principle of Determinacy

The state

For a system of points, the state is the full list of positions and velocities.

The claim

The state at one instant determines the entire motion, future and past.

Initial data

$$\big(x(t_0),\,\dot{x}(t_0)\big) \;\longmapsto\; x(t)\ \text{ for all } t$$

Why second order

Exactly two data per coordinate are needed, so nature may prescribe $\ddot{x}$ from the state.

Experimental FactsPrinciple of Determinacy
Present determinacy as the deepest experimental input: the present positions and velocities of all points fix the entire future and past motion. Make clear this is why second-order equations appear, since exactly two pieces of initial data per coordinate are needed. Frame acceleration as the quantity nature is allowed to prescribe as a function of state.

Configuration Space

Definition

The manifold $M$ of all instantaneous configurations; $\dim M$ is the number of degrees of freedom.

Motion as a curve

A motion is a path $x:\mathbb{R}\to M$; velocity is its tangent vector $\dot{x}\in T_xM$.

State space

$$(x,\dot{x}) \in TM, \qquad \dim TM = 2\dim M$$

One level up

Determinacy is a statement about $TM$: the state, not the position alone, fixes the future.

Experimental FactsConfiguration Space
Introduce configuration space as the manifold of all instantaneous positions, with dimension equal to the number of degrees of freedom. Explain that a motion is a curve in this space and that velocity is its tangent vector. Stress that the true state lives one level up, in position together with velocity, which is what determinacy actually specifies.

Newton's Equation

Form forced by determinacy

Acceleration is a definite function of the state and time.

Force is measured

The right-hand side $F$ is found experimentally for each concrete system.

Newton's law

$$m\,\ddot{x} = F(x,\dot{x},t)$$

Existence & uniqueness

For smooth $F$, each state launches one solution: the mathematical face of determinacy.

Experimental FactsNewton's Equation
Write Newton's equation as the concrete form that determinacy takes: acceleration equals a force function of position, velocity, and time. Emphasize that the function on the right is determined experimentally for each system, and that existence and uniqueness of solutions is exactly the mathematical echo of the physical principle of determinacy.

One State, One Trajectory

Determinism seen in the position-velocity plane

First-order flow

$$\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}x\\ \dot{x}\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\dot{x}\\ F/m\end{pmatrix}$$

No crossings

If two orbits met at a phase point, the future would not be unique, violating determinacy.

Configuration view differs

In $x$ alone curves may cross, since one position can carry many velocities.

Experimental FactsState Determines Trajectory
Use the phase picture to make determinism visual. A single point of the position-velocity plane launches exactly one trajectory, and distinct trajectories never cross there. Contrast this with the configuration line, where curves may cross because the same position can carry different velocities. This is the intuition behind the existence and uniqueness theorem.

Invariance Constrains the Force

Time translation

A closed system has no explicit $t$: $F$ cannot depend on the clock.

Space translation

$F$ depends only on differences $x_i - x_j$, not on absolute position.

Rotation

No preferred direction: $F$ transforms covariantly under $R\in SO(3)$.

Reduced form

$$m_i\,\ddot{x}_i = F_i\big(\{x_j - x_k\},\,\{\dot{x}_j-\dot{x}_k\}\big)$$

Boost invariance

Adding $vt$ to every point leaves the equations unchanged, so only relative velocities may enter.

Experimental FactsGalilean Invariance
Now harvest consequences. Demand that Newton's equation look identical in every Galilean frame and watch the force law lose freedom. Time-translation invariance forbids explicit time dependence in a closed system, space-translation invariance forces dependence on differences of positions, and rotational invariance forbids any preferred direction.

Two-Body Forces Along the Line of Centers

Central pair force

$$F_{12} = f(r)\,\frac{x_1 - x_2}{|x_1 - x_2|} = -F_{21}$$

Examples

Newtonian gravity $f(r)\propto r^{-2}$ and the Coulomb law share this form.

Symmetry first

Magnitude depends only on separation $r$; direction lies along the join.

Experimental FactsTwo-Body Forces
Specialize to two interacting particles. Galilean symmetry plus the action-reaction balance forces the interaction to lie along the line joining them and to depend only on their separation distance. Newtonian gravity and the Coulomb law are the famous instances. Point out how much physics follows from symmetry alone, before any specific dynamics is assumed.

Recap and Outlook

The logical chain

  • Relativity $\Rightarrow$ the Galilean group
  • Determinacy $\Rightarrow$ second-order $m\ddot{x}=F$
  • Invariance $\Rightarrow$ admissible force laws

What we can now do

Read off allowed forces for closed systems and central pair interactions from symmetry alone.

Carry forward

$$\text{symmetry} \;\rightsquigarrow\; \text{conservation laws}$$

Next

The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian pictures recast these facts, and Noether's theorem turns each symmetry into a conserved quantity.
