Introduction to Ergodic Theory

From individual trajectories to long-run averages on the energy surface

The question

What fraction of time does an orbit spend in a region of phase space?

The tool

An invariant measure on the energy level turns dynamics into statistics.

The payoff

Time averages along one orbit equal space averages over the whole surface.

Ergodic TheoryIntroduction
Open by framing ergodic theory as the statistical face of Hamiltonian mechanics. We cannot integrate most systems explicitly, so instead we ask what fraction of time an orbit spends in a region. The energy surface carries an invariant measure, and that measure is the bridge from individual trajectories to long-run averages.

Measure-Preserving Dynamical Systems

The data

A probability space $(M,\mu)$ with $\mu(M)=1$ and a map $T$ or flow $g^t$.

Invariance

Measure is unchanged by the dynamics: sets and their preimages have equal weight.

Definition

$\mu\big(T^{-1}A\big) = \mu(A)\quad\text{for all measurable } A$

Flow version

$\mu\big(g^{t}A\big) = \mu(A)\quad\text{for all } t$

Why probability

Normalizing to total mass one lets us read $\mu(A)$ as a frequency.

Ergodic TheoryMeasure-Preserving Systems
Define the abstract object carefully: a probability space together with a transformation or flow that leaves the measure invariant. Stress that invariance means preimages of a set have the same measure as the set, which is the natural language for both discrete maps and continuous flows on phase space.

The Invariant Measure on an Energy Level

Liouville's theorem

The Hamiltonian flow preserves the phase volume $dp\,dq$.

Restriction

Fix $H=E$. The level set $M_E=\{H=E\}$ inherits a finite invariant measure.

Induced measure

$d\mu_E = \dfrac{dS}{\lVert \nabla H\rVert}$

Ergodic TheoryThe Liouville Measure
Connect the abstraction to mechanics. Liouville's theorem says the Hamiltonian flow preserves phase volume, and restricting to a fixed energy level gives a finite invariant measure once we divide by the gradient of the energy. Emphasize that this is the canonical invariant measure ergodic theory acts upon here.

Poincare's Recurrence Theorem

Theorem

For a measure-preserving $T$ on $(M,\mu)$ with $\mu(M)<\infty$, almost every point of any set $A$ returns to $A$.

Proof idea

If the iterates $A, T^{-1}A, T^{-2}A,\dots$ of a non-returning part were disjoint, their equal positive measures would sum to infinity.

Pigeonhole bound

$\sum_{k\ge 0}\mu\big(T^{-k}B\big)=\infty \;\Rightarrow\; \mu(B)=0$

The paradox

A gas seems to “return” to its initial state. The resolution: recurrence times are astronomically large.

Ergodic TheoryPoincare Recurrence
Walk through the pigeonhole argument. If a positive-measure set never returned, its forward iterates would be disjoint and consume infinite measure, contradicting finiteness. Conclude that almost every point returns arbitrarily near its start. Highlight the apparent paradox with irreversibility and resolve it by recurrence times being astronomically long.

Ergodicity: Indecomposability

Definition

$T$ is ergodic if every invariant set has measure $0$ or $1$:

$T^{-1}A=A \;\Rightarrow\; \mu(A)\in\{0,1\}$

Equivalent form

Every invariant function is constant almost everywhere.

Ergodic TheoryErgodicity
Introduce ergodicity as indecomposability: no invariant set splits the space into two parts of positive measure. Equivalently, invariant functions are constant almost everywhere. Contrast an ergodic system, whose typical orbit explores the whole energy surface, with a decomposable one that stays trapped in a sub-region forever.

Birkhoff's Ergodic Theorem

Time average exists

$\bar f(x)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\!\int_0^T\! f\big(g^t x\big)\,dt$

Convergence

The limit exists for $\mu$-almost every initial point $x$ and any integrable $f$.

If ergodic

$\bar f(x)=\displaystyle\int_M f\,d\mu \quad\text{a.e.}$

Reading

Time average $=$ space average. The orbit “samples” the surface according to $\mu$.

Ergodic TheoryBirkhoff's Theorem
State the central theorem precisely. Time averages along an orbit converge for almost every initial point. When the system is ergodic the limit is constant and equals the space average. This is the rigorous statement of the physicist's hope that time spent equals phase volume occupied.

Time Average Equals Space Average

An ergodic orbit visits each region in proportion to its measure

Fraction of time in A

$\lim_{T\to\infty}\dfrac{\text{time in }A}{T}=\mu(A)$

Indicator function

Take $f=\mathbf 1_A$ in Birkhoff: the time average becomes the visit frequency.

The caveat

Equality holds for almost every $x$; a measure-zero set of orbits may escape it.

Ergodic TheoryTime = Space Average
This is the visual heart of the lecture. Show a single trajectory winding densely over the energy surface and argue that the proportion of time near any region matches that region's measure. Stress almost every: a measure-zero set of exceptional initial conditions may behave differently, and that caveat is essential.

Mixing: A Stronger Property

Definition

$\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu\big(g^tA\cap B\big)=\mu(A)\,\mu(B)$

Hierarchy

Mixing $\Rightarrow$ ergodic, but not conversely.

Ergodic TheoryMixing
Define mixing as asymptotic independence: the measure of the intersection of a moved set with a fixed set factorizes in the limit. Explain that mixing implies ergodicity but is strictly stronger, describing systems that forget their initial conditions and spread any blob uniformly across the whole space.

Examples: Rotation and Torus Translation

Circle rotation

$T_\alpha:\;\theta\mapsto \theta+\alpha \pmod{2\pi}$

Ergodic iff irrational

If $\alpha/2\pi$ is irrational the orbit is dense and equidistributes. It is ergodic but not mixing.

Torus translation

$x\mapsto x+a$ on $\mathbb T^n$ is ergodic when $1,a_1,\dots,a_n$ are rationally independent.

Ergodic TheoryRotations and Tori
Present the simplest examples. An irrational rotation of the circle is ergodic but not mixing; orbits equidistribute yet rigidly preserve spacing. The irrational translation on the torus generalizes this. Use Weyl equidistribution to make the connection between number theory and ergodicity concrete and memorable.

Geodesic Flow on a Negatively Curved Surface

The system

The flow of unit-speed geodesics on a compact surface of curvature $K<0$.

Exponential divergence

Nearby geodesics separate like $e^{\lambda t}$, the seed of sensitive dependence.

Consequence

$\text{geodesic flow is mixing on } M_E$

Ergodic TheoryGeodesic Flow
Contrast the rotations with the geodesic flow on a surface of negative curvature. Here nearby geodesics diverge exponentially, producing genuine mixing and strong chaos. This is the canonical mechanical example where the energy surface is explored thoroughly and sensitive dependence reigns. It motivates the leap from order to chaos.

Recurrence Under a Volume-Preserving Flow

Why it must return

$\mu(g^{t}A)=\mu(A)>0,\;\; \mu(M)<\infty$

Incompressibility

Volume cannot escape to infinity on a compact level, so images must eventually overlap $A$.

Recurrence times

Almost every point returns, but the wait can be enormous.

Ergodic TheoryRecurrence Picture
Return to recurrence with a concrete picture: a droplet of phase points carried by the volume-preserving flow must eventually overlap its starting region. Tie this to the measure-theoretic proof and to the physical intuition that incompressible flow on a finite-volume surface cannot escape to infinity, forcing return.

Synthesis and Outlook

Measure preservation

Gives Poincare recurrence: typical orbits come back.

Ergodicity

Gives equality of time and space averages via Birkhoff.

Mixing

Gives decay of correlations and loss of memory.

The chain

$\text{mixing}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{ergodic}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{recurrent}$

Why it matters

This justifies statistical mechanics: replace intractable trajectories by ensemble averages.

Next

Ergodic TheorySynthesis
Close by assembling the hierarchy: measure preservation gives recurrence, ergodicity gives equality of averages, mixing gives decay of correlations. Remind students that these properties justify statistical mechanics' replacement of intractable trajectories by averages, and point ahead to entropy and the deeper classification of dynamical systems.
