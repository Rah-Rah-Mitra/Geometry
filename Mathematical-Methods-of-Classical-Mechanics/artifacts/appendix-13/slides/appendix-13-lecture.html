Alternating $k$-Forms

Multilinear

Linear in each of its $k$ vector slots.

Antisymmetric

Swapping any two inputs flips the sign.

Consequence

A repeated input gives $0$: forms detect independence.

Defining law

$\omega(\dots,\xi_i,\dots,\xi_j,\dots) = -\,\omega(\dots,\xi_j,\dots,\xi_i,\dots)$

Dimension count

$\dim \Lambda^k(\mathbb{R}^n) = \binom{n}{k},\qquad \Lambda^k = 0 \text{ for } k>n$

Differential FormsAppendix 13
Define an alternating k-form on a vector space as a multilinear map that flips sign under any swap of two arguments. Emphasize the consequence: feeding a repeated vector gives zero, so forms detect linear independence. The space of k-forms has dimension n choose k, vanishing once k exceeds the dimension.

The Wedge Product

Builds degree

$\alpha \wedge \beta$ combines a $p$-form and a $q$-form into a $(p+q)$-form.

Graded sign

$\alpha \wedge \beta = (-1)^{pq}\,\beta \wedge \alpha$.

Self-zero

For a 1-form, $dx \wedge dx = 0$.

Antisymmetrized product

$(dx \wedge dy)(\xi,\eta) = \det\!\begin{pmatrix} dx(\xi) & dx(\eta)\\ dy(\xi) & dy(\eta)\end{pmatrix}$

Toward determinants

The wedge is exactly the determinant rule in disguise.

Differential FormsAppendix 13
Introduce the wedge as the antisymmetrized tensor product producing a higher-degree form. Drill the two rules learners forget: anticommutativity up to a sign from degrees, and that a one-form wedged with itself is zero. Connect to determinants now, since that is the meaning unlocked on the next slide.

Wedge as Oriented Area

Signed area

$\omega(a,b) = \det\!\begin{pmatrix} a_1 & b_1\\ a_2 & b_2 \end{pmatrix} = -\,\omega(b,a)$

Reading

The 2-form returns the signed area of the parallelogram $a,b$ span.

Seed

Every flux and volume integral grows from this picture.

The Exterior Derivative $d$

Raises degree

$d$ turns a $k$-form into a $(k{+}1)$-form.

Graded Leibniz

$d(\alpha\wedge\beta) = d\alpha\wedge\beta + (-1)^{p}\,\alpha\wedge d\beta$.

On functions

$df$ is the ordinary differential, a $1$-form.

Coordinate formula

$d\!\left(\sum a_I\, dx^I\right) = \sum \frac{\partial a_I}{\partial x^j}\, dx^j \wedge dx^I$

In three dimensions

$d_0 \to \nabla,\quad d_1 \to \nabla\times,\quad d_2 \to \nabla\cdot$

Differential FormsAppendix 13
Define d as the unique operator raising degree by one, linear, obeying a graded Leibniz rule, and reducing to the ordinary differential on functions. Give the coordinate formula: differentiate each coefficient and wedge. Preview that in three dimensions d reproduces gradient, curl, and divergence in turn.

The Identity $d^2 = 0$

Always

Applying $d$ twice annihilates any form.

Why

Mixed partials commute; antisymmetric wedge cancels them.

Payoff

Makes closed/exact forms and cohomology possible.

Central identity

$d \circ d = 0$

Vector-calculus echo

$\nabla\times(\nabla f) = 0,\qquad \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times F) = 0$

Differential FormsAppendix 13
State the central identity that applying d twice annihilates any form, rooted in equality of mixed partial derivatives. Translate it to vector calculus: curl of a gradient and divergence of a curl both vanish. This single fact is what makes closed and exact forms, and de Rham cohomology, even possible.

Pullback $f^{*}$

Backward

A map $f:M\to N$ pulls a form on $N$ back to $M$.

By substitution

Replace coordinates and differentials by their images.

Use

Change of variables in integrals, Jacobian free.

Two clean rules

$f^{*}(\alpha\wedge\beta) = f^{*}\alpha \wedge f^{*}\beta,\qquad f^{*}(d\omega) = d(f^{*}\omega)$

Commutes

Pullback respects both the wedge and the exterior derivative.

Differential FormsAppendix 13
Explain that a smooth map pulls forms backward, substituting the map into the coordinates and differentials. Highlight the two clean properties: pullback commutes with the wedge and with the exterior derivative. This is precisely what lets us change variables inside an integral without tracking Jacobians by hand.

Integrating over Oriented Chains

Pull back, integrate

Pull $\omega$ to a parameter domain, integrate the coefficient.

Orientation

Reversing orientation negates the integral.

Chains

Formal sums of oriented $k$-cells; a boundary $\partial c$ is defined.

Definition

$\displaystyle\int_{c}\omega = \int_{D} \phi^{*}\omega,\qquad \phi: D \to M$

Duality preview

The boundary operator $\partial$ is dual to $d$.

Define integrating a k-form over an oriented k-chain by pulling back to a parameter domain and integrating the coefficient. Stress orientation: reversing it negates the integral. Chains are formal sums of oriented cells, and their boundary operator, which we meet next, is dual to the exterior derivative.

The General Stokes Theorem

Master identity

$\displaystyle\int_{c} d\omega = \int_{\partial c} \omega$

Read as duality

$d$ on the form is paired against $\partial$ on the region.

One theorem

Every classical integral theorem is a single instance.

Differential FormsAppendix 13

Three Classical Special Cases

Green

$\displaystyle\oint_{\partial D}\!P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D \!\left(\partial_x Q - \partial_y P\right)dx\,dy$

Gauss (divergence)

$\displaystyle\oiint_{\partial V}\! F\cdot dS = \iiint_V (\nabla\cdot F)\,dV$

Kelvin-Stokes

$\displaystyle\oint_{\partial S}\! F\cdot dr = \iint_S (\nabla\times F)\cdot dS$

Selector

The degree of $\omega$ picks which classical theorem appears.

Closed, Exact, and a Hint of Cohomology

Closed

$d\omega = 0$: the form is killed by $d$.

Exact

$\omega = d\eta$: the form is a derivative.

One way

Since $d^2=0$, every exact form is closed.

de Rham cohomology

$H^k_{dR} = \dfrac{\ker d}{\operatorname{im} d} = \dfrac{\text{closed}}{\text{exact}}$

Meaning

The gap measures the holes of the space, and this language now carries the symplectic form $\omega$.

Differential FormsAppendix 13
Close by defining closed forms as those killed by d and exact forms as those that are themselves a d of something. Since d squared is zero, every exact form is closed; the failure of the converse measures the holes of the space. That quotient is de Rham cohomology, and this language now carries the symplectic form.
