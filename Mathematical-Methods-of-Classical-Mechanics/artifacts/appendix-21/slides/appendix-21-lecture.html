Two Time Scales

Fast clock

The oscillation period $T$ is of order one.

Slow clock

The parameter $\lambda(\varepsilon t)$ changes over a time $\sim 1/\varepsilon$.

Adiabatic

Slow relative to the oscillation: $\varepsilon \ll 1$.

Slow parameter

$$\lambda = \lambda(\tau),\qquad \tau = \varepsilon t$$

Adiabatic InvariantsTwo time scales
Make the small parameter explicit. Let the parameter change on a time of order one over epsilon while the system oscillates on a time of order one. Stress that adiabatic means slow relative to the oscillation, not slow in absolute terms. Everything that follows is an expansion in this ratio epsilon.

The Action of an Oscillator

Definition

For a one-degree-of-freedom system, the action is the loop integral of $p\,dq$.

Geometry

It equals the phase-space area enclosed, divided by $2\pi$.

Action variable

$$I(E,\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{H=E} p\,dq$$

Compare

Energy $E$ changes as $\lambda$ moves; the action $I$ is built to resist.

Adiabatic InvariantsThe action variable
Define the action as the phase-space area enclosed by one closed orbit divided by two pi. Emphasize it is a function of the orbit, hence of energy and the frozen parameter. Contrast it with energy, which is not conserved when the parameter moves; the action is the quantity engineered to be robust.

Freeze $\lambda$

Hold the parameter fixed: motion is exactly periodic with a closed orbit.

Relabel

As the parameter slowly creeps, imagine a slowly relabeled family of frozen orbits, and ask which orbit the true trajectory sits on at each instant.

The Invariance Theorem

Theorem

Under a slow smooth change of $\lambda$ over a time $\sim 1/\varepsilon$, the action returns nearly to its starting value.

Size of the drift

The change in $I$ is bounded by the rate; for a smooth full passage it is exponentially small in $1/\varepsilon$.

Statement

$$\big|I(\varepsilon^{-1})-I(0)\big| \le C\,\varepsilon$$

Reading it

Energy and amplitude may swing widely; the action $I$ barely moves.

Adiabatic InvariantsInvariance theorem
State the central result carefully. Over a slow smooth change of the parameter, the action returns nearly to its starting value; the change is bounded by the rate epsilon and, for smooth changes over a full passage, is exponentially small. Energy and amplitude may swing wildly, but the action barely notices.

Area Is Preserved

Deform the well

As the potential changes shape, the orbit changes shape with it.

Conserved area

The area the loop encloses stays the same — that area is $2\pi I$.

Mechanism

Liouville-type area preservation under slow deformation.

Adiabatic InvariantsArea preservation
Drive the picture home. As the potential well deforms, the orbit changes shape, but the area it encloses is conserved. This is the geometric heart of the theorem: Liouville-type area preservation under slow deformation. Have students trace one loop deforming into another while the enclosed area stays the same.

Pendulum on a Shortening String

The experiment

Pull a swinging pendulum's string slowly through the pivot.

What changes

Length drops, frequency rises, energy rises, angular amplitude grows.

What holds

The ratio $E/\omega = I$ stays fixed.

Adiabatic InvariantsPendulum example
Walk through the canonical example. Pull the string of a swinging pendulum slowly through the pivot. The frequency rises, the energy rises, the amplitude in angle grows, yet the ratio of energy to frequency stays fixed. That fixed ratio is exactly the action, and the example makes the abstract invariant tangible.

Energy Tracks Frequency

Oscillator action

$$I=\frac{E}{\omega}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E=I\,\omega$$

Consequence

With $I$ fixed, energy is proportional to frequency.

Worked number

For a pendulum $\omega\propto \ell^{-1/2}$. Halve the length $\ell$: frequency rises by $\sqrt2$, and so does the energy.

The point

No energy conservation here — the dimensionless action $I$ is what is conserved.

Adiabatic InvariantsEnergy and frequency
Quantify the pendulum. For a slow harmonic-like oscillator the action equals energy over frequency, so energy is proportional to frequency. Halve the string length, the frequency goes up by root two, and the energy follows. Stress that no energy is conserved here; the conserved object is the dimensionless action.

A Charged Particle in a Slow Field

Gyration

A charged particle spirals around magnetic field lines.

Invariant

The magnetic moment $\mu$ — the action of that gyration — is adiabatically invariant.

Magnetic moment

$$\mu=\frac{m v_\perp^2}{2B}=\text{const}$$

Adiabatic InvariantsMagnetic moment
Move to the magnetic example. A charged particle gyrates around field lines; the magnetic moment, the action of that gyration, is adiabatically invariant as the field strength slowly varies along the path. Connect the magnetic moment to the same action integral so students see one theorem behind two physical settings.

Magnetic Mirrors

Into stronger field

As $B$ rises, $\mu$ fixed forces $v_\perp^2 \propto B$ to grow.

Energy budget

Total kinetic energy is fixed, so parallel speed $v_\parallel$ falls to zero.

Reflection

Parallel motion reverses — the particle is mirrored back.

Adiabatic InvariantsMagnetic mirrors
Show the application. As the particle drifts into a stronger field, conservation of the magnetic moment forces perpendicular energy up and parallel energy down until the parallel motion reverses. That reflection is the magnetic mirror, the principle behind mirror confinement and the trapping of particles in planetary radiation belts.
