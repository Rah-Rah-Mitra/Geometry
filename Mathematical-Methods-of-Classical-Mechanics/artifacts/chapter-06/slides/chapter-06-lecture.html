Rigid Bodies

Rotation as motion on a curved configuration space

Rigid constraint

A body whose point-to-point distances are frozen in time.

Two ingredients

Translation of a base point plus rotation about it.

Plan

From $SO(3)$ to Euler's equations and the rolling ellipsoid.

Rigid BodiesConfiguration space and rotation
Open by fixing the picture of a rigid body as a cloud of points whose mutual distances never change. Stress that the whole chapter lives on the rotation group, that motion splits into translation of a point plus rotation about it, and that everything downstream flows from this configuration space.

Configuration Space: $SO(3)$ and $SE(3)$

Orientation = rotation

Fixing one point, a configuration is a rotation matrix $B$.

Add translation

A free body lives in $SE(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes SO(3)$, six dimensions.

Special orthogonal group

$$SO(3)=\{B:\; B^{\!\top}B=I,\ \det B=1\}$$

Curved, not flat

$\dim SO(3)=3$; it is compact and non-commutative.

Rigid BodiesThe rotation group
Explain that orienting a rigid body about a fixed point is exactly choosing a rotation, so the configuration space is the three dimensional group of special orthogonal matrices. With a free base point we get the six dimensional group of rigid motions. Emphasise that these are curved spaces, not vector spaces.

Angular Velocity: Body vs Space

Skew from the constraint

$$\dot B B^{-1}=\widehat{\boldsymbol\omega}_s,\qquad B^{-1}\dot B=\widehat{\boldsymbol\Omega}$$

Two readings

$\boldsymbol\omega_s = B\,\boldsymbol\Omega$ links spatial and body frames.

Point velocities

$\dot{\mathbf r} = \boldsymbol\omega_s \times \mathbf r$.

Rigid BodiesAngular velocity
Derive angular velocity from differentiating the orthogonality constraint, giving a skew matrix and therefore a vector. Distinguish carefully between the body frame angular velocity and the spatial one, related by the current rotation. Point out that every body point moves with the cross product of omega and its position.

The Inertia Tensor

Definition

The symmetric operator $I$ sending body $\boldsymbol\Omega$ to body angular momentum $\mathbf M$.

Spectral theorem

$I$ diagonalises: principal axes $\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3$ and moments $I_1,I_2,I_3>0$.

Mass-weighted integral

$$I_{jk}=\int \big(|\mathbf r|^2\delta_{jk}-r_j r_k\big)\,dm$$

Why it matters

In principal axes every formula becomes diagonal and clean.

Rigid BodiesInertia operator
Introduce the inertia operator as the symmetric positive map sending body angular velocity to angular momentum. Show it is a mass weighted integral, diagonalisable by the spectral theorem. Name its eigenvectors the principal axes and its eigenvalues the principal moments, which organise every later computation.

The Inertia Ellipsoid

Quadratic form

$$\langle I\boldsymbol\Omega,\boldsymbol\Omega\rangle = 1$$

Geometric encoding

A short axis means a large moment; the ellipsoid stores the mass distribution.

Coming up

This ellipsoid will roll on a plane in the Poinsot picture.

Rigid BodiesInertia ellipsoid
Draw the inertia ellipsoid as the level set of the quadratic form of the inertia tensor. Its semi axes run along the principal axes with lengths set by the moments. This single picture encodes the body's mass distribution and will roll on a plane in the Poinsot construction later.

Kinetic Energy as a Quadratic Form

Energy

$$T=\tfrac12\langle I\boldsymbol\Omega,\boldsymbol\Omega\rangle=\tfrac12\langle\mathbf M,\boldsymbol\Omega\rangle$$

Principal axes

$$T=\tfrac12\big(I_1\Omega_1^2+I_2\Omega_2^2+I_3\Omega_3^2\big)$$

Lagrangian

For free rotation this $T$ is the entire Lagrangian.

Conserved

With no external torque, $T$ stays constant along the motion.

Frame note

Best read in the body frame, where $I$ is constant.

Rigid BodiesKinetic energy
Present kinetic energy as one half the inner product of angular velocity with angular momentum, a quadratic form in omega built from the inertia tensor. In principal axes it becomes a clean sum over the three moments. This is the Lagrangian for free rotation and the conserved energy of the motion.

Angular Momentum: Spatial and Body

Spatial vector

$\mathbf m = B\,\mathbf M$ is constant when no torque acts.

Body vector

$\mathbf M = I\boldsymbol\Omega$ moves as the body sees it.

Relation

$$\mathbf m = B\,\mathbf M,\qquad |\mathbf m|=|\mathbf M|$$

Key contrast

The space vector is pinned; the body vector traces a curve on a sphere of radius $|\mathbf M|$.

Rigid BodiesAngular momentum
Separate the two angular momenta: the constant spatial vector and the body frame vector that the body sees. Stress that conservation holds for the spatial vector when no torque acts, while the body vector traces a curve on a sphere. This contrast drives Euler's equations on the next slide.

Euler's Equations of Motion

Body-frame law

$$\dot{\mathbf M}+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf M = 0$$

Components

$$I_1\dot\Omega_1=(I_2-I_3)\Omega_2\Omega_3$$ $$I_2\dot\Omega_2=(I_3-I_1)\Omega_3\Omega_1$$ $$I_3\dot\Omega_3=(I_1-I_2)\Omega_1\Omega_2$$

Gyroscopic term

The cross product couples the three axes nonlinearly.

First integrals

Energy $T$ and $|\mathbf M|^2$ are both conserved.

Closed system

Two invariants confine the body trajectory to a curve.

Rigid BodiesEuler's equations
Write Euler's equations as the body frame statement that the rate of change of angular momentum plus the gyroscopic cross term vanishes. Expand into three coupled scalar equations in the principal components. Note the two conserved quantities, energy and the magnitude of angular momentum, that constrain the trajectory.

Two Surfaces, One Trajectory

Constraint set

$$2T=\sum I_k\Omega_k^2,\quad |\mathbf M|^2=\sum I_k^2\Omega_k^2$$

Intersection

$\mathbf M$ lies on both an energy ellipsoid and a momentum sphere.

Stability

Rotation is stable about the largest and smallest moment, unstable about the middle.

Rigid BodiesConservation and stability
Show that the body angular momentum is pinned to the intersection of an energy ellipsoid and an angular momentum sphere. Walk through how the relative sizes of these surfaces produce closed curves around the extreme axes and saddle structure near the middle axis, foreshadowing the stability discussion.

The Poinsot Construction

Rolling picture

The inertia ellipsoid rolls on a plane fixed perpendicular to $\mathbf m$.

Polhode

The contact track on the body.

Herpolhode

The contact track on the fixed plane.

No slip

$\boldsymbol\omega$ points to the contact point: zero velocity there.

Rigid BodiesPoinsot construction
Give the geometric description of free motion: the inertia ellipsoid rolls without slipping on a fixed plane perpendicular to angular momentum. The contact point traces the polhode on the body and the herpolhode on the plane. This turns an algebraic system into a vivid rolling picture.

Symmetric Free Top & the Heavy Lagrange Top

Symmetric free top

$I_1=I_2\ne I_3$: the body precesses steadily about $\mathbf m$ at a constant rate.

Heavy top integrals

$$E,\quad M_z,\quad M_3$$

Integrability

Three conserved quantities make the Lagrange top solvable.

Rigid BodiesSymmetric and heavy tops
Treat the symmetric free top, where two moments coincide and the body precesses steadily about angular momentum. Then add gravity for the Lagrange top, whose axis both precesses and nutates. Identify the conserved quantities that make this heavy top integrable and produce its looping tip path.

Synthesis: The Chain of Ideas

Configuration

$SO(3)$ and $SE(3)$ host the motion.

Kinematics

Angular velocity in body and space frames.

Dynamics

Inertia tensor, energy, Euler's equations.

Geometry

Poinsot's rolling ellipsoid, polhode and herpolhode.

Invariants drive everything

$$T,\ |\mathbf M|,\ \mathbf m,\ M_z,\ M_3$$

Takeaway

Name the conserved quantity behind each phenomenon.

Rigid BodiesSynthesis
Close by tracing the logical chain from the rotation group through angular velocity, the inertia tensor, energy, and Euler's equations to the Poinsot picture and the heavy top. Ask students to name which conserved quantity controls each phenomenon, reinforcing the geometric unity of rigid body motion.
