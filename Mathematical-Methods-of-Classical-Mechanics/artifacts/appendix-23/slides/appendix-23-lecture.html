Caustics, wave fronts, and the A-D-E classification of simple singularities

The question

Project a Lagrangian or Legendrian manifold to physical space. Where does the projection fold, and what shapes can the folds take?

The answer

Only a short, rigid list of normal forms occurs generically: the elementary catastrophes, organized by the diagrams $A_k$, $D_k$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$.

Why Projections Fold

Lagrangian manifold

A family of rays lifts to a Lagrangian manifold $L^n$ inside phase space $T^\*M$, on which the action is single-valued.

Loss of rank

The projection $\pi:L\to M$ folds wherever its differential drops rank. The set of such points is the caustic.

Critical condition

$$\operatorname{rk}\, d\pi|_x < n \;\Longleftrightarrow\; x \in \text{caustic}$$

SingularitiesLagrangian projection
Rays carry a Lagrangian manifold in phase space. Projecting it down to position space is generically a fold, because the manifold is curved while the projection is flat. Where the tangent plane turns vertical the map loses rank, light piles up, and we see a caustic. The whole appendix studies the local form of that loss of rank.

Caustics Are Bifurcations of a Potential

Generating family

A ray system is encoded by $F(x;q)$ with state $x$ and control $q$. Rays are critical points $\partial_x F=0$.

Caustic = discriminant

The caustic is the set of $q$ where critical points collide, i.e. where $\partial_x F=\partial_x^2 F=0$.

Equilibria and their collisions

$$\partial_x F=0,\qquad \partial_x^2 F=0$$

Degenerate critical points are the seeds of every elementary catastrophe.

Same picture, two readings

Bifurcation of equilibria in mechanics = optical caustic = catastrophe of $F$.

SingularitiesGenerating families
Each ray system comes from a generating family, a function of one state variable and several parameters. Equilibria are critical points in the state; caustics are where critical points collide as parameters move. So caustic geometry is the bifurcation geometry of a potential, and the elementary catastrophes are exactly the normal forms of those collisions.

The Fold $A_2$

Normal form

$$F(x;a)=x^3+a\,x$$

Two roots collide

For $a<0$ there are two equilibria; for $a>0$ none. They annihilate at $a=0$ — a single smooth caustic point.

Universal edge

Every smooth point of a caustic is, locally, an $A_2$ fold.

SingularitiesFold
The simplest collision is the fold. Two critical points of a cubic generating function approach and annihilate as the control parameter crosses zero. On one side there are two equilibria, on the other none, and the boundary is a single smooth caustic point. This A2 fold is the universal building block of every caustic edge.

The Cusp $A_3$

Normal form (two controls)

$$F(x;a,b)=x^4+a\,x^2+b\,x$$

Semicubical cusp

The discriminant in the $(a,b)$ plane satisfies $27\,b^2 + 8\,a^3 = 0$ near the origin — a sharp, $\tfrac32$-power cusp.

Counting rays

Inside the cusp: three rays. Outside: one. The boundary is two $A_2$ folds meeting at the $A_3$ point.

SingularitiesCusp
Raise the generating function to quartic and one control parameter is no longer enough; we need two. The discriminant in that control plane is a semicubical cusp, the famous coffee-cup curve. Inside the cusp there are three rays, outside one, and the two fold edges meet tangentially at the sharp point where three critical points coincide.

The Coffee-Cup Caustic

Everyday optics

Rays reflect off a circular cup wall. Their envelope concentrates light into a bright nephroid with a sharp cusp.

Structural stability

Tilt the lamp slightly and the curve deforms but the cusp survives. The $A_3$ form is robust, not a coincidence of one lighting.

SingularitiesCaustic in a cup
Hold a mug under a lamp and the bright curve on the coffee is a real caustic, a cardioid-like nephroid with a cusp. Parallel-ish rays reflect off the circular wall and their envelope concentrates light. This everyday picture is the A3 cusp made visible, and it is structurally stable: jiggle the lamp and the cusp persists.

The Swallowtail $A_4$

Normal form (three controls)

$$F(x;a,b,c)=x^5+a\,x^3+b\,x^2+c\,x$$

Wave-front singularity

Legendrian projection of a wave front in $\mathbb{R}^3$ produces a self-intersecting folded sheet: the swallowtail.

Features

Two cuspidal edges, one curve of transverse self-intersection, one swallowtail point.

SingularitiesSwallowtail
Wave fronts are Legendrian projections, and their generic singularity in three space is the swallowtail. A quintic generating family produces a self-intersecting folded sheet with two cuspidal edges meeting at a swallowtail point and a curve of transverse self-intersection. It is the A4 member of the same series, one dimension up from the cusp.

The $A_k$ Series

Germ and unfolding

$$f(x)=x^{k+1},\qquad \mu=k$$

Milnor number $\mu=k$; the versal deformation needs $k-1$ control parameters.

Where each appears

$A_1$ point, $A_2$ fold, $A_3$ cusp (plane caustics), $A_4$ swallowtail (space wave fronts), $A_5$ butterfly, …

One ladder, many faces

The same algebraic ladder $x^{k+1}$ generates fold, cusp, swallowtail, and beyond. Higher members surface only when enough parameters are free to unfold them.

Singularities$A_k$ ladder
Folds, cusps and swallowtails are the start of one infinite family. The function x to the k plus one has an A k singularity, and its versal deformation needs k minus one parameters. Caustics in the plane show A2 and A3; wave fronts in space add A4; higher members appear when more parameters are available to unfold them.

The A-D-E Classification

Simple singularities

A germ is simple if no continuous modulus appears under deformation. Arnold proved these split into three families.

The complete list

$$A_k,\quad D_k,\quad E_6,\;E_7,\;E_8$$

Same diagrams, three subjects

These are exactly the Dynkin diagrams of simple Lie algebras and Kleinian surface singularities.

SingularitiesA-D-E
Arnold's theorem says the simple singularities, those with no continuous modulus, fall into exactly three families: A k, D k, and the three exceptionals E6, E7, E8. The same Dynkin diagrams that classify simple Lie algebras and Kleinian surface singularities reappear here. This coincidence is one of the most celebrated patterns in mathematics.

Genericity and Structural Stability

Why so few forms?

A singularity that survives every small perturbation must be finitely determined — and that selects exactly the simple germs.

Transversality

Thom–Arnold arguments show generic ray families meet the degeneracy strata transversally, so only low-codimension forms appear.

Codimension budget

$$\operatorname{codim} A_k = k-1$$

A $k$-parameter family generically realizes $A_1,\dots,A_{k+1}$ and no worse.

SingularitiesStability
Why only this short list? Because a singularity that survives every small perturbation must be one of the simple, finitely determined germs. Thom and Arnold's transversality arguments show that for generic systems only the A-D-E forms occur, and each keeps its shape under deformation. Genericity, not luck, is what makes caustics look the way they do.

Caustics and the Maslov Index

Phase loss at a fold

Each transverse crossing of a caustic shifts the wave phase by $-\tfrac{\pi}{2}$. Counting crossings gives the Maslov index $\mu$.

Quantization

$$\oint p\,dq = 2\pi\hbar\!\left(n+\tfrac{\mu}{4}\right)$$

Geometry into spectrum

The fold count we classified turns classical orbits into quantum levels.

SingularitiesMaslov index
Each time a ray touches a caustic its phase jumps by a quarter period, and counting these crossings gives the Maslov index. That integer corrects the semiclassical phase and enters the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition. So the fold points we classified geometrically carry the analytic information that turns classical orbits into quantum spectra.

Synthesis

The chain of ideas

  • Lagrangian / Legendrian projections fold.
  • Folds are bifurcations of a generating family.
  • Normal forms are the $A$-$D$-$E$ simple singularities.
  • Structural stability fixes the short list.
  • The Maslov index feeds folds back into physics.

Caustic vs wave front

Caustics: $A_2$ folds and $A_3$ cusps in the plane. Wave fronts: add the $A_4$ swallowtail in space.

One ladder, three theories

$$x^{k+1}\;\leadsto\;A_k\;\leadsto\;\{\text{Lie},\,\text{Klein},\,\text{optics}\}$$

Take-home

The shapes you see in light, water and equilibria are governed by one rigid algebraic classification.
