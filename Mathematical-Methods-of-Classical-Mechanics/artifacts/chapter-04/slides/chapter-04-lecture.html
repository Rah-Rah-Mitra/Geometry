The Configuration Manifold $M$

Definition

$M$ = smooth manifold of all configurations; $\dim M = n$ = degrees of freedom.

Generalized coordinates

A chart $q=(q^1,\dots,q^n)$ names points of $M$ locally. The manifold is the coordinate-free object underneath.

Examples

Pendulum: $M=S^1$. Planar double pendulum: $M=T^2$. Rigid body about a point: $M=SO(3)$.

Configuration ManifoldChapter 04
Introduce the configuration manifold M as the set of all positions the system can occupy. The pendulum lives on a circle, a planar double pendulum on a torus. Stress that a chart gives generalized coordinates, but the manifold itself is the coordinate-free object. The dimension of M is the number of degrees of freedom.

The Tangent Bundle $TM$ as State Space

Definition

$$TM=\bigsqcup_{q\in M} T_qM,\qquad \dim TM = 2n.$$

A point of $TM$

A pair $(q,\dot q)$: a position together with a velocity vector attached at that position.

Lifted motion

A motion $q(t)$ in $M$ lifts to $(q(t),\dot q(t))$ in $TM$, recording velocity at every instant.

Tangent BundleChapter 04
Velocities are not points of M but tangent vectors. Assemble all tangent spaces into the tangent bundle TM, whose points are pairs of a position and a velocity. This 2n-dimensional manifold is the true state space of Lagrangian mechanics. Emphasize that a motion is a curve in M, and its lift to TM records velocity at each instant.

The Lagrangian: a Function on $TM$

Definition

$$L:TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad L=L(q,\dot q,t).$$

One function, all dynamics

$L$ assigns a number to each state. Everything in the chapter is derived from this single object.

Action functional

$$S[\gamma]=\int_{t_0}^{t_1} L\big(q(t),\dot q(t),t\big)\,dt.$$

Coordinate free

Because $L$ is a function on the bundle, its value is independent of which chart we used to write $q$.

The LagrangianChapter 04
Define the Lagrangian as a smooth function on the tangent bundle, possibly time dependent. In local coordinates it reads L of q, q-dot, t. Stress that L assigns a number to each state, and that the whole of mechanics will be derived from a single variational principle applied to this one function.

Hamilton's Principle and Euler–Lagrange

Principle of stationary action

$$\delta S=0\ \text{over curves with fixed endpoints.}$$

Euler–Lagrange equations

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^i}=0.$$

Intrinsic

The equations transform correctly between charts, defining a second-order flow on $TM$.

Euler–LagrangeChapter 04
State Hamilton's principle of stationary action and derive the Euler-Lagrange equations. Emphasize that although written in coordinates, the equations transform correctly between charts, so they define an intrinsic second-order system on TM. This coordinate independence is exactly what lets us work on a manifold.

Holonomic Constraints Define a Submanifold

Constraint surface

$$g_a(x)=0,\ a=1,\dots,k\ \Rightarrow\ M=\{x: g_a(x)=0\}.$$

Reduction

Constrained motion is ordinary Lagrangian motion on the submanifold $M$ with the restricted Lagrangian $L|_{TM}$.

Caution

This works for holonomic (position) constraints. Non-holonomic velocity constraints need a different treatment.

Holonomic ConstraintsChapter 04
Explain holonomic constraints as smooth equations on configurations that cut out a submanifold. The constrained configuration space is that submanifold, of lower dimension. Constrained Lagrangian mechanics is just ordinary Lagrangian mechanics on the submanifold, using the restricted Lagrangian. Contrast briefly with non-holonomic constraints, which are not treated this way.

Kinetic Energy is a Riemannian Metric

Kinetic energy

$$T=\tfrac12\, g_{ij}(q)\,\dot q^i\dot q^j,\quad g_{ij}=g_{ji}\succ 0.$$

The mass matrix is the metric

The positive-definite form $g_{ij}(q)$ is a Riemannian metric on $M$. Length is measured by kinetic energy.

Free motion = geodesics

With $U=0$, trajectories are geodesics of $g$ — the manifold's own straightest lines.

Kinetic Energy MetricChapter 04
Show that kinetic energy is a positive-definite quadratic form in the velocities, hence a Riemannian metric on M. The mass matrix is the metric in coordinates. Free motion becomes geodesic motion. Emphasize that geometry is not imposed from outside; the kinetic energy itself supplies the metric of configuration space.

Natural Systems: $L = T - U$

Lagrangian

$$L(q,\dot q)=\tfrac12 g_{ij}\dot q^i\dot q^j - U(q).$$

Equation of motion

$$g_{ij}\ddot q^j+\Gamma_{i,jk}\dot q^j\dot q^k=-\partial_i U.$$

Energy conservation

When $L$ has no explicit time dependence, $E=T+U$ is conserved along motions.

Potential bends paths

The force $-\partial_i U$ deflects the trajectory away from the free geodesic of the metric.

Recovers Newton

On Euclidean $M$ with $g_{ij}=m\,\delta_{ij}$ this is exactly $m\ddot q=-\nabla U$.

Natural SystemsChapter 04
Define a natural mechanical system by L equals kinetic minus potential energy. Write the Euler-Lagrange equations in this case and recover Newton's law with the metric connection. Stress that the potential bends trajectories away from geodesics, and that energy T plus U is conserved when L has no explicit time dependence.

Symmetries: One-Parameter Groups

One-parameter group

$$h^s:M\to M,\quad h^0=\mathrm{id},\quad h^{s+t}=h^s\circ h^t.$$

Generating field

Its infinitesimal generator is the vector field $X(q)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{0} h^s(q)$ on $M$.

Invariance

$$L\big(h^s_*(q,\dot q)\big)=L(q,\dot q)\quad\forall s.$$

SymmetriesChapter 04
Introduce one-parameter groups of diffeomorphisms of M and what it means for the Lagrangian to be invariant under such a group. Build intuition with a rotationally symmetric potential. A symmetry is a continuous transformation that leaves the physics unchanged, and this invariance is about to yield a conserved quantity.

Noether's Theorem

Theorem

If $L$ is invariant under the flow of $X$, then $$I=\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\,X^i$$ is a first integral of the motion.

Reading it off

The conserved quantity is the momentum $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q^i$ paired with the symmetry generator $X$.

Coordinate-free statement

$$I=\langle p,\,X\rangle,\qquad \frac{dI}{dt}=0.$$

Why it holds

Differentiate the invariance $L\circ h^s = L$ in $s$ and use Euler–Lagrange; the total time derivative collapses to zero.

Slogan

Every continuous symmetry of $L$ yields one conservation law.

Noether's TheoremChapter 04
State Noether's theorem precisely: invariance of the Lagrangian under a one-parameter group generated by a vector field X gives a first integral, the pairing of the momentum with X. Stress the construction is coordinate free and that the conserved quantity is read off directly from the generating vector field of the symmetry.

Worked Case: Rotation $\Rightarrow$ Angular Momentum

Symmetry

Rotation about $\hat n$: $X=\hat n\times r$, with $U$ isotropic.

Conserved integral

$$I=\langle p,X\rangle=\hat n\cdot(r\times m\dot r)=\hat n\cdot L_{\text{ang}}.$$

Result

Each rotation axis $\hat n$ gives one conserved component of angular momentum.

Rotation and Angular MomentumChapter 04
Work the canonical example: rotational symmetry of an isotropic system yields conservation of angular momentum. Trace through how the rotation vector field pairs with the kinetic momentum to produce the conserved component. This is the concrete payoff of Noether and connects symmetry directly to the classical conservation laws students already know.

Synthesis and Outlook

The dictionary

  • Configurations $\to$ manifold $M$
  • States $(q,\dot q)\to$ tangent bundle $TM$
  • Dynamics $\to$ a function $L$ on $TM$
  • Motion $\to$ Euler–Lagrange flow

Geometry built in

Kinetic energy supplies a Riemannian metric; symmetry supplies conservation by Noether.

Next: Legendre transform

$$p_i=\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\ \rightsquigarrow\ H(q,p)\ \text{on}\ T^*M.$$

Outlook

Passing to the cotangent bundle $T^*M$ opens Hamiltonian mechanics and symplectic geometry, the subject of the chapters ahead.
