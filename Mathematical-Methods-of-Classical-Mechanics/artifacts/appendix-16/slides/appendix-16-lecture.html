Symplectic Structures

Beyond cotangent bundles: orbits, Kahler manifolds, and reduction

Intrinsic phase spaces

A closed nondegenerate two-form, with no positions or momenta supplied in advance.

Where they come from

Coadjoint orbits of Lie groups, complex projective space, and homogeneous manifolds.

Payoff

Rigid bodies and ideal fluids become Hamiltonian flows on these spaces.

This appendix lifts symplectic geometry off the cotangent bundle. We meet manifolds that are symplectic for intrinsic reasons: coadjoint orbits, complex projective space, and homogeneous spaces. The plan is to fix the definition, flatten it with Darboux, then watch rigid bodies and fluids reappear as flows on orbits.

What Is a Symplectic Manifold

Two conditions

A two-form that is closed and nondegenerate.

Even dimension

Nondegeneracy of an alternating form forces $\dim M = 2n$.

No metric needed

The form alone, not a Riemannian metric, drives the dynamics.

Definition

$$d\omega = 0, \qquad \omega^n \neq 0$$

Volume

The top power $\omega^n$ is a volume form, preserved by every Hamiltonian flow.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
A symplectic manifold carries a closed, nondegenerate two-form. Closedness gives a conserved structure under the flow; nondegeneracy lets every Hamiltonian function name a unique vector field. Stress that nondegeneracy forces even dimension, and that the form, not a metric, governs the dynamics here.

From Energy to Velocity

Musical map

Nondegeneracy gives a bijection between one-forms and vector fields.

The recipe

A function $H$ produces a unique field $X_H$ by contracting $\omega$.

Invariance

Since $d\omega=0$, the flow of $X_H$ preserves $\omega$ exactly.

Defining relation

$$\iota_{X_H}\,\omega = dH$$

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
The form converts a differential of energy into a velocity field. Walk through the musical isomorphism: contract omega with the Hamiltonian vector field, recover dH. Because omega is closed, the flow preserves it, and Liouville volume follows as the top wedge power. This is the engine every later example rides on.

Darboux's Theorem

Theorem

Near any point there are coordinates in which

$$\omega = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dp_i \wedge dq_i$$

No local invariants

All symplectic forms of equal dimension look identical locally.

Contrast with metrics

Riemannian curvature obstructs local flattening; symplectic geometry has no such obstruction.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
Darboux says symplectic manifolds have no local invariants beyond dimension. Any point has a chart where omega becomes the constant sum dp wedge dq. Contrast sharply with Riemannian geometry, where curvature obstructs local flattening. Emphasize that all symplectic interest is therefore global, not local.

The Symplectic Group

Standard form

On $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ the form $\omega_0$ pairs each $q_i$-axis with its $p_i$-axis.

Group and algebra

$$A^\top J A = J, \qquad \mathfrak{sp} = \{ H : JH \text{ symmetric}\}$$

Eigenvalue symmetry

If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue so are $1/\lambda$ and $\bar\lambda$ — reciprocal quadruples.

Stability

This quadruple law controls whether linearized equilibria are stable.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
On a vector space the standard form pairs conjugate planes. The symplectic group consists of linear maps preserving it; its Lie algebra is the Hamiltonian matrices. Mention that the group is noncompact and connected, and that eigenvalues come in reciprocal quadruples, which controls linear stability.

Symplectic Without Momenta

The usual case

Cotangent bundles $T^*Q$ carry the canonical form $d\theta$ globally.

Compact obstruction

A compact symplectic manifold cannot be exact, so it is never a cotangent bundle.

New sources

Algebraic varieties, homogeneous spaces, and orbits supply symplectic forms intrinsically.

Question

Which manifolds admit a symplectic structure at all? The answer needs global topology.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
Not every symplectic manifold is a phase space of positions and momenta. Compact examples like spheres and projective spaces carry symplectic forms yet admit no global Darboux coordinates covering them. This motivates hunting for symplectic structures on algebraic and homogeneous manifolds directly.

Kahler Manifolds and $\mathbb{CP}^n$

Compatible triple

A complex structure $J$, a metric $g$, and a symplectic form $\omega$ that agree.

Fubini-Study

$$\omega = \tfrac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar\partial \log\!\big(1+\lVert z\rVert^2\big)$$

Model example

$\mathbb{CP}^n$ is compact, symplectic, and the prototype Kahler manifold.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
A Kahler manifold marries a complex structure, a metric, and a symplectic form into one compatible triple. Complex projective space is the model: the Fubini-Study form is closed and nondegenerate. Point out that the two-sphere is CP one, our first compact symplectic manifold, with area as its form.

Coadjoint Orbits

The dual algebra

The group acts on $\mathfrak{g}^*$ by the coadjoint representation.

Orbits foliate

$\mathfrak{g}^*$ splits into orbits; each is automatically even dimensional.

Canonical form

Every orbit carries the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau symplectic form, built from the bracket alone.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
Here is the central construction. The dual of a Lie algebra is foliated by coadjoint orbits, and each orbit carries a canonical symplectic form, the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau form. Built purely from the Lie bracket, it needs no extra data. Every orbit is automatically even dimensional.
Mathematical Methods of Classical MechanicsAppendix 16

The KKS Form

Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau

$$\omega_\mu(\xi^\#,\eta^\#) = \langle \mu, [\xi,\eta]\rangle$$

Inputs

Tangent vectors at $\mu$ come from algebra elements $\xi,\eta$ acting by the coadjoint action.

Why nondegenerate

The kernel of this pairing is exactly the stabilizer, so the form descends cleanly to the orbit.

Why orbits

That is precisely why the orbits — not all of $\mathfrak{g}^*$ — are the symplectic leaves.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
Write the form by evaluating the point on the bracket of the two generators. Stress that the kernel of this pairing is exactly the stabilizer, so the form descends to a nondegenerate object on the orbit. This is why orbits, not the whole dual space, are the symplectic leaves.

Rigid Bodies and Fluids on Orbits

$SO(3)$ orbits

Orbits are spheres $\lvert M\rvert = \text{const}$ of angular momentum.

Euler's equations

$$\dot M = M \times \Omega$$

Ideal fluids

Same picture on the diffeomorphism group: flow is geodesic, vorticity is an orbit invariant.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
For SO(3) the orbits are angular-momentum spheres, and Euler's equations are Hamiltonian flow on them with the KKS area form. The same picture, on the diffeomorphism group, makes ideal fluid motion a geodesic and vorticity an orbit invariant. One construction unifies tops and turbulence.

Momentum Maps and Reduction

Momentum map

$$\mu : M \to \mathfrak{g}^*, \qquad \iota_{\xi^\#}\omega = d\langle \mu,\xi\rangle$$

Conservation

A symplectic symmetry makes $\mu$ a conserved quantity — Noether in geometric dress.

Marsden-Weinstein

Quotient a level set $\mu^{-1}(c)$ by the residual symmetry to get a smaller symplectic manifold.

Closing the loop

The coadjoint orbit itself is the reduction of a point — the picture is self-consistent.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
A symmetry acting by symplectomorphisms produces a momentum map valued in the dual algebra; its level sets are conserved. Marsden-Weinstein reduction quotients a level set by the residual symmetry to a smaller symplectic manifold. Note that the coadjoint orbit is itself the reduction of a point.

Recap and Throughline

Local triviality

Closed, nondegenerate, and locally flat by Darboux — all interest is global.

New manifolds

Coadjoint orbits and Kahler spaces give symplectic manifolds with no cotangent parent.

Symmetry

Momentum maps tie symmetry to conservation and to reduction.

Exercise

Compute the area form on the SO(3) momentum sphere and check it is the KKS form.

Symplectic StructuresAppendix 16
Recap the throughline: closed nondegenerate forms, locally trivial by Darboux, globally rich. Coadjoint orbits supply symplectic manifolds with no cotangent parentage, Kahler geometry supplies compact ones, and momentum maps tie symmetry to conserved quantities and reduction. Send students to compute the sphere's area form as the SO(3) example.
