Fast and Slow Variables

The system

One slow action $I$, one fast phase $\varphi$ on the circle.

Separation of scales

$\varepsilon$ is small: the action barely moves per turn of the angle.

Standard form

$$\dot I = \varepsilon\, f(I,\varphi), \qquad \dot\varphi = \omega(I) + \varepsilon\, g(I,\varphi)$$

Geometry

A cylinder: $\varphi$ wraps around, $I$ moves slowly along its length.

Fast and Slow VariablesAppendix 17
Stress the structure of the equations: the action changes at rate epsilon while the angle turns at order one. That separation of timescales is the whole hypothesis. Point out that the angle lives on a circle and the action lives in an open region, so the picture is a cylinder swept slowly sideways as the angle races around.

The Averaging Principle

The replacement

Swap the true right-hand side for its average over $\varphi$.

What we gain

An autonomous equation in the action alone.

Averaged system

$$\dot J = \varepsilon\, \bar f(J), \qquad \bar f(J)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(J,\varphi)\,d\varphi$$

Caveat

This is a recipe, not yet a theorem. When does $J$ track $I$?

The Averaging PrincipleAppendix 17
State the principle as a replacement: substitute the true right-hand side by its average over the fast angle. Emphasize that this is a recipe, not yet a theorem. The averaged system is autonomous in the action alone, vastly simpler, and our task across the next slides is to decide when its solution actually tracks the true one.

The Phase Average

Definition

Integrate $f$ over one full turn of $\varphi$, divide by $2\pi$.

Depends only on $I$

The oscillating part is gone; a clean drift field remains.

Phase AverageAppendix 17
Define the phase average carefully as the integral over one full turn of the angle divided by two pi. Note that it depends only on the action. Give intuition: the fast angle samples every phase nearly uniformly, so over one slow step the action feels only the mean push, and the oscillating remainder integrates away.

How Good Is the Approximation?

The bound

True and averaged actions stay $O(\varepsilon)$ apart.

The horizon

Valid over the long slow time $t\sim 1/\varepsilon$.

Main estimate

$$|I(t)-J(t)| \le C\,\varepsilon \quad \text{for } 0\le t\le \tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$$

Reading it

As $\varepsilon\to 0$ the valid window lengthens, where slow drift is visible.

Error EstimateAppendix 17
Give the headline estimate: the gap between true and averaged actions stays of order epsilon over a time of order one over epsilon. So on the natural slow timescale the approximation is uniformly good. Underline that the time interval grows as epsilon shrinks, which is exactly the regime where the slow drift becomes visible.

Why It Works: A Change of Variables

Add a correction

Shift $I$ by a small periodic $\varepsilon\,h(I,\varphi)$.

Cancel the wiggle

Choose $h$ so its angular derivative kills $f-\bar f$.

Near-identity transform

$$J = I + \varepsilon\, h(I,\varphi), \qquad \omega\,\partial_\varphi h = \bar f - f$$

What remains

Averaged drift plus $O(\varepsilon^2)$, integrated to give the $O(\varepsilon)$ bound.

Justification SketchAppendix 17
Sketch the change of variables that removes the oscillating part. We add a small periodic correction of order epsilon to the action, chosen so its derivative cancels the fluctuating remainder. What survives is the averaged drift plus terms of order epsilon squared, and integrating those over the long time gives the order epsilon bound.

Where Averaging Fails: Resonance

Small denominators

The correction $h$ divides by $\omega(I)$.

The trap

When $\omega\to 0$ the correction blows up; the wiggle no longer averages out.

Divergence

$$h \sim \frac{f-\bar f}{\omega(I)} \;\longrightarrow\; \infty \quad \text{as } \omega(I)\to 0$$

Consequence

In resonant zones the action can be captured or kicked.

Resonance Breaks AveragingAppendix 17
Now the warning. The correction term divides by the frequency, so when the frequency passes through zero or a rational combination vanishes, the small denominator explodes. Inside such resonant zones the oscillation no longer averages out, the trajectory can be captured or kicked, and the simple averaged drift loses its predictive power.

Several Fast Angles

Resonance condition

$$k\cdot\omega(I)=0,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^n\setminus\{0\}$$

Trust region

Average safely off a thin neighborhood of the web.

Many FrequenciesAppendix 17
Generalize to several fast angles. Now resonances form a dense web of planes in frequency space where integer combinations vanish. Averaging is trustworthy on the complement of a thin neighborhood of that web. Mention that controlling the measure of the resonant set is the heart of the deeper Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser and Nekhoroshev theory.

Adiabatic Invariants

Slow parameter

A control $\lambda=\varepsilon t$ drifts slowly through the system.

Action conserved

The averaged drift of the action vanishes, so $I$ is nearly fixed.

Adiabatic theorem

$$I=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p\,dq \;\approx\; \text{const}$$

Example

Pendulum with slowly shortening string: $E/\omega$ stays nearly constant.

Adiabatic InvariantsAppendix 17
Connect to adiabatic theory. When the system has an action–angle structure and a slowly varying parameter, the action is approximately conserved precisely because the averaged drift of the action vanishes. The pendulum with slowly shortening string is the classic example: energy over frequency stays nearly constant through the slow change.

Application: Celestial Mechanics

Fast vs slow

Fast orbital angle; slow elements like eccentricity and perihelion.

Secular motion

Average over the orbit to get long-term precession.

Secular equations

$$\dot e=\varepsilon\,\bar f_e,\qquad \dot\varpi=\varepsilon\,\bar f_\varpi$$

Trouble spot

Period resonances revive the small-divisor problem.

Celestial MechanicsAppendix 17
Bring in the historical engine. Planetary orbits have fast orbital angles and slowly drifting orbital elements like eccentricity and perihelion. Averaging over the fast orbital motion yields the secular equations that predict long-term precession. Resonances between orbital periods are exactly where this averaging program runs into its famous small-divisor trouble.

Summary

Separate

Identify slow actions and fast angles.

Average

Replace the drift by its mean over the angle.

Trust

Good to $O(\varepsilon)$ over time $1/\varepsilon$, away from resonance.

The averaging principle

$$\dot I=\varepsilon f(I,\varphi)\ \rightsquigarrow\ \dot J=\varepsilon\,\bar f(J)$$

The two-sided lesson

A simplification away from resonance; a warning sign where frequencies lock.

SummaryAppendix 17
Recap the arc: separate fast and slow, average the slow drift over the angle, and trust the result for times of order one over epsilon away from resonance. Leave the learner with the two-sided lesson: averaging is a powerful simplification and a precise warning sign wherever frequencies lock together.
