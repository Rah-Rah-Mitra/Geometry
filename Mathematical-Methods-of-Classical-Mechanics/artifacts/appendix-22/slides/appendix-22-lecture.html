The WKB Ansatz

Single mode

Amplitude $a(x)$ varies slowly; the phase $S(x)$ oscillates rapidly at rate $1/h$.

Collect powers of $h$

Substituting into the wave operator orders the equation by powers of the small parameter.

Ansatz

$$u(x)\;\sim\; a(x)\, e^{\,i S(x)/h}$$

Asymptotic, not exact

The series in $h$ need not converge; it captures the leading behaviour only.

Short-Wave AsymptoticsAppendix 22
Explain that we substitute a single oscillating mode with slowly varying amplitude into the wave operator. The large frequency multiplies derivatives of the phase, so collecting powers of the small parameter forces the leading equation to constrain the phase and the next order to constrain the amplitude. Stress this is asymptotic, not convergent.

Eikonal Equation: the Phase

Leading order

The highest power of $1/h$ must vanish, leaving a constraint on $dS$ alone.

It is Hamilton-Jacobi

Set momentum $p = dS$. The phase solves the stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

Eikonal / Hamilton-Jacobi

$$H\!\left(x,\;\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}\right)=0$$

Classical core

Solving for $S$ is exactly the mechanics problem hidden in the wave equation.

Short-Wave AsymptoticsAppendix 22
Highlight that the highest power of frequency must vanish identically, which produces the eikonal equation. Identify it with the time-independent Hamilton-Jacobi equation where the gradient of the phase plays the role of momentum. Emphasise that solving for the phase is exactly the classical mechanics problem hiding inside the wave equation.

Transport Equation: the Amplitude

Next order

The first subleading power of $h$ gives a linear first-order equation for $a(x)$.

Flux conservation

It states that an energy or probability flux is conserved along each ray.

Transport

$$2\,\nabla S\cdot\nabla a + a\,\Delta S = 0$$

Foreshadowing

$a$ grows where rays converge, hinting at the blow-up at caustics.

The amplitude grows where rays converge and shrinks where they spread, which already foreshadows the blow-up at caustics.

Rays as Bicharacteristics

Characteristics

Rays are the characteristic curves of the eikonal equation.

Lifted flow

They are projections of the Hamiltonian's bicharacteristic flow in $(x,p)$.

Shadow

The ray is only the configuration-space shadow of a phase-space trajectory.

Canonical equations

$$\dot{x}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p},\qquad \dot{p}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}$$

Write the canonical equations and stress that position and momentum evolve together in phase space, while the ray is only the shadow of that lifted trajectory.

Wave Fronts and Huygens' Principle

Fronts

Level sets $\{S=\text{const}\}$ are the advancing wave fronts.

Orthogonality

In the isotropic case rays meet every front at a right angle.

Huygens

Each front is the envelope of wavelets emitted by the previous one.

Lifting to a Lagrangian Manifold

Graph of $dS$

The set $\{(x,p): p=\partial S/\partial x\}$ is a half-dimensional surface in phase space.

Lagrangian

The symplectic form restricts to zero on it: $\omega|_{\Lambda}=0$.

Invariant

The bicharacteristic flow maps $\Lambda$ to itself, keeping it smooth.

Lagrangian manifold

$$\Lambda=\{(x,p)\,:\, p=dS(x)\},\quad \dim\Lambda=n$$

Why lift?

Track one smooth surface upstairs instead of a possibly multivalued phase downstairs.

Lift the picture into phase space: the graph of the phase gradient is a Lagrangian manifold, a half-dimensional surface on which the symplectic form vanishes. Explain that the bicharacteristic flow preserves this manifold, so the right object to track is not the multivalued phase downstairs but the single smooth surface upstairs.

Caustics: where Rays Cross

Singular projection

The map $\Lambda\to\{x\}$ degenerates where the ray map's Jacobian vanishes.

Folds and cusps

These singularities of the projection are the caustic; the manifold stays smooth.

Caustic condition

$$\det\frac{\partial x}{\partial(\text{ray label})}=0$$

Now deliver the climax: where neighbouring rays cross, the projection of the Lagrangian manifold to configuration space becomes singular. These fold and cusp points form the caustic, the bright envelope of the ray family. Stress that the manifold itself stays smooth; only its shadow degenerates, which is why a global lift is needed.

Why the Simple Ansatz Breaks

Amplitude blow-up

The transport solution carries an inverse Jacobian that diverges at the fold.

Fold projection

Over a fold, $x$ has two ray preimages; the single-valued ansatz cannot hold.

Divergent amplitude

$$a(x)\;\propto\;\Big|\det\tfrac{\partial x}{\partial q_0}\Big|^{-1/2}\to\infty$$

The cure

Switch to the momentum picture near the fold, where $\Lambda$ projects smoothly.

Short-Wave AsymptoticsAppendix 22
Explain why the simple ansatz fails at a caustic: the amplitude carries an inverse Jacobian of the ray map, and that Jacobian vanishes where rays focus. The naive solution blows up. The cure is to work in the momentum representation near the fold, where the same manifold projects without singularity.

The Maslov Index Correction

Counting caustics

The Maslov index $\mu$ counts signed touches of the ray with the caustic set.

Quarter-period jump

Each fold crossing advances the phase by $\pi/2$.

Global asymptotics

$$u\sim\sum_{j} a_j\, e^{\,i S_j/h \;-\; i\frac{\pi}{2}\mu_j}$$

Quantization

The phase jumps fix Bohr-Sommerfeld conditions and the global solution.

Short-Wave AsymptoticsAppendix 22
Introduce the Maslov index as the integer counting how many times a ray touches the caustic, weighted by the type of fold. Each passage advances the phase by a quarter period, so the global asymptotic solution is a sum over branches of the Lagrangian manifold with these phase jumps inserted. This fixes the quantization conditions.
