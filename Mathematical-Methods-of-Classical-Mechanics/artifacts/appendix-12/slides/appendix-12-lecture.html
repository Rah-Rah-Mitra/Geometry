What is a Lie Group?

Two structures, one object

A set $G$ that is both a smooth manifold and a group, with the two compatible.

Smooth operations

Multiplication and inversion are smooth maps of manifolds, so they may be differentiated.

Dimension

$\dim G$ equals the number of continuous parameters describing an element.

Smoothness axioms

$\mu:G\times G\to G,\;\; (g,h)\mapsto gh$

$\iota:G\to G,\;\; g\mapsto g^{-1}$

Both $C^\infty$. Together they make $G$ a Lie group.

Why it matters

Differentiating these maps at the identity manufactures the Lie algebra.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasDefinition
Stress the double structure here. Multiplication and inversion are not just abstractly defined; they are smooth maps of manifolds. That smoothness is what allows us to differentiate the group operations and extract an algebra. Point out that the dimension of the manifold equals the number of continuous parameters describing a group element.

Examples That Run Mechanics

$SO(3)$ — rotations

$3\times 3$ orthogonal matrices, $\det=1$. Dimension 3: the rigid body's orientation space.

$SU(2)$ — spin

Unit quaternions; a double cover of $SO(3)$, a 3-sphere as a manifold.

$SE(3)$ — rigid motion

Rotations plus translations; dimension 6. The full pose of a body in space.

$GL(n)$ — the ambient group

Invertible $n\times n$ matrices, an open set in $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ containing the rest.

Nesting

$SO(3)\subset SE(3),\quad SO(3)\subset GL(3)$

Lie Groups and AlgebrasExamples
Walk through each example as a manifold and a group at once. SO(3) is the configuration space of a rotating rigid body; SU(2) double covers it and runs the spin story; SE(3) handles full rigid motion in space; GL(n) is the open ambient group inside which the others sit. Emphasize their dimensions out loud.

The Lie Algebra: Tangent at the Identity

Linearize the group

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}=T_eG$ is the tangent space at the identity element.

A vector space

Velocities of curves through $e$; its dimension matches that of $G$.

Example

$\mathfrak{so}(3)=\{A:A^{\top}=-A\}$

Skew-symmetric matrices — the angular velocities.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasLie Algebra
The key move: differentiate the manifold at the identity. The tangent space there is a vector space of the right dimension, and we call it the Lie algebra. Make clear that the identity is special because translation by any group element carries it everywhere, so the whole group looks locally like this one tangent space.

Left-Invariant Vector Fields

Translate the vector

Left multiplication $L_g(h)=gh$ is a diffeomorphism for each fixed $g$.

Spread it everywhere

Push $X\in\mathfrak{g}$ forward by $L_g$ to define $X$ at every point of $G$.

Determined by $e$

A left-invariant field is fixed by its single value at the identity.

Invariance

$(dL_g)\,X(h) = X(gh)$

The field commutes with all left translations.

Identification

$\mathfrak{g}\;\cong\;\{\text{left-invariant fields}\}$

This is what lets the bracket live on $\mathfrak{g}$.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasInvariance
Explain why a single tangent vector at the identity spreads to an entire vector field. Left translation is a diffeomorphism, so pushing the vector forward gives a left-invariant field that is determined everywhere by its value at e. This identification is what turns the tangent space into something with extra algebraic structure.

The Lie Bracket

From space to algebra

The commutator of left-invariant fields gives $\mathfrak{g}$ a bilinear product.

Matrix groups

$[X,Y] = XY - YX$

Concrete

For $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ the bracket reproduces the cross product of vectors.

Axioms of a Lie algebra

$[X,Y] = -[Y,X]$

$[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0$

Antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity.

Meaning

The bracket measures how far two flows fail to commute — the next slide pair makes this visual.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasBracket
Introduce the commutator as the operation that makes the tangent space an algebra rather than just a vector space. Antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity are the two axioms to remember. For matrix groups the bracket is literally XY minus YX, which students can compute by hand, anchoring the abstraction in arithmetic.

One-Parameter Subgroups

A homomorphism from $\mathbb{R}$

A smooth curve $\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G$ with $\gamma(0)=e$ obeying the group law.

Homomorphism property

$\gamma(s+t)=\gamma(s)\,\gamma(t)$

One per generator

Each $X\in\mathfrak{g}$ gives exactly one such subgroup with $\gamma'(0)=X$.

Integral curve

$\gamma(t)$ is the integral curve through $e$ of the left-invariant field $X$.

Generated by $X$

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\gamma(t)=\big(dL_{\gamma(t)}\big)X$

The flow of $X$ from the identity.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasSubgroups
A one-parameter subgroup is a smooth homomorphism from the real line into the group. Each element X of the algebra generates exactly one such curve, the integral curve of its left-invariant field through the identity. Emphasize the homomorphism property: the curve at time s plus t equals the product of its values at s and t.

The Exponential Map

Algebra to group

$\exp(X)=\gamma_X(1)$: ride the one-parameter subgroup of $X$ for unit time.

Matrix groups

$\exp(X)=\sum_{k\ge 0}\dfrac{X^k}{k!}$

Local chart

Near $e$, $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism — canonical coordinates of the first kind.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasExponential
This is the central picture. The exponential map sends a straight line through the origin of the algebra to a one-parameter subgroup threading the identity of the group. For matrices it is the familiar power series. Near the identity it is a local diffeomorphism, giving canonical coordinates of the first kind.

The Bracket as a Non-Closing Parallelogram

Group commutator

$g_X g_Y g_X^{-1} g_Y^{-1}$

$=\, e + t^2[X,Y] + O(t^3)$

with $g_X=\exp(tX)$, $g_Y=\exp(tY)$.

Reading

The leading non-closing term of the flow parallelogram is exactly $[X,Y]$.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasCommutator
Make the parallelogram concrete. Flow along X, then Y, then back along X, then back along Y; in a non-abelian group you do not return to the start. The leading discrepancy is exactly the bracket of X and Y, scaled by the time squared. The bracket measures the failure of the flows to commute.

Adjoint Representation: Ad and ad

Conjugation acts

$g$ acts on $\mathfrak{g}$ by differentiating conjugation $h\mapsto ghg^{-1}$ at $e$.

Adjoint of the group

$\mathrm{Ad}_g\,X = g\,X\,g^{-1}$

A linear action of $G$ on its algebra.

Invariance

Left and right translation interlock through $\mathrm{Ad}$.

Derivative gives the bracket

$\mathrm{ad}_X = \left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp(tX)}$

$\mathrm{ad}_X(Y) = [X,Y]$

One chain

Conjugation → $\mathrm{Ad}$ → $\mathrm{ad}$ → bracket: the same structure at three levels.

Lie Groups and AlgebrasAdjoint
Conjugation by a group element acts on the algebra; its derivative at the identity is the adjoint representation Ad. Differentiating Ad in the group variable produces the lowercase ad, and ad of X is just bracket with X. Tie this chain together slowly: it links group conjugation, algebra automorphisms, and the bracket into one structure.

Coadjoint Orbits: Home of Mechanics

Dualize the action

$\mathrm{Ad}^*$ acts on $\mathfrak{g}^*$, the dual of the algebra, where momenta live.

Coadjoint action

$\langle \mathrm{Ad}^*_g\,\mu,\,X\rangle=\langle \mu,\,\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}X\rangle$

Symplectic orbits

Each orbit carries the natural Kirillov–Kostant symplectic form.

Rigid body

Angular momentum lives on a sphere $\|\mu\|=$ const — a coadjoint orbit of $SO(3)$, where Euler's equations flow.

Fluids

The perfect fluid is geodesic motion on the diffeomorphism group; its Euler equation is coadjoint flow.

Takeaway

$\text{symmetry}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{symplectic dynamics}$

Lie Groups and AlgebrasCoadjoint Orbits
Close on the payoff for mechanics. The coadjoint action lives on the dual of the algebra, and its orbits carry a natural symplectic structure, the Kirillov form. The rigid body's angular momentum sphere and the Euler equations of fluids are coadjoint orbits. This is where symmetry becomes Hamiltonian dynamics.
