One Family, Two Kinds of Curve

Fixed foci

Choose foci at $(\pm c,0)$, equivalently $a^2-b^2=c^2$ fixed.

Slide a parameter

Vary $\lambda$: for $\lambda>b^2$ ellipses, for $a^2>\lambda>b^2$ hyperbolas.

Degenerate cases

The foci themselves and the focal segment close off the pencil.

Confocal pencil

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2-\lambda}+\frac{y^2}{b^2-\lambda}=1$$

A single equation; the sign of each denominator decides ellipse versus hyperbola.

Why "confocal"

Every member shares the same two foci, independent of $\lambda$.

Elliptic CoordinatesAppendix 20
Fix two foci and let a single real parameter slide through the focal interval. Above the larger axis value we get nested ellipses; between the two roots we get hyperbolas. Emphasise that one algebraic family, indexed by lambda, produces both kinds of curve depending on which interval lambda lives in.

Two Roots Give Two Coordinates

Quadratic in λ

$$(a^2-\lambda)(b^2-\lambda)=x^2(b^2-\lambda)+y^2(a^2-\lambda)$$

Two solutions

For a generic point the quadratic has roots $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$.

Elliptic coordinates

The pair $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ labels the point: one ellipse, one hyperbola.

Reading the labels

$\lambda_1$ names which ellipse you sit on; $\lambda_2$ names which hyperbola.

Dimension lift

In space the same idea gives three confocal quadrics through each point: ellipsoidal coordinates.

Inverse map

$x^2,\,y^2$ are rational functions of $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$.

Ellipse Meets Hyperbola at a Right Angle

Theorem

Confocal conics of different type intersect orthogonally everywhere.

Reason

The gradients of two confocal functions are perpendicular at common points.

Diagonal metric

$$ds^2 = g_{11}\,d\lambda_1^2 + g_{22}\,d\lambda_2^2$$

No cross term: that is what separation needs.

Elliptic CoordinatesAppendix 20
The central structural fact: through each point one ellipse and one hyperbola pass, and they meet at a right angle. Sketch why, gradients of the two confocal functions are orthogonal. This orthogonality is what diagonalises the metric and makes separation of variables possible, so dwell on it.

The Elliptic Coordinate Grid

Like a globe

Ellipses act as parallels, hyperbolas as meridians, but bent around the foci.

Degenerate members

Foci are point-ellipses; the focal segment and the outer axis are limit hyperbolas.

Chart validity

The pair $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ is a genuine coordinate chart away from the foci and axis.

Why Hamilton-Jacobi Separates

Hamilton-Jacobi

$$H\!\left(q,\frac{\partial S}{\partial q}\right)=E$$

Separation ansatz

Seek $S=S_1(\lambda_1)+S_2(\lambda_2)$, each term one variable only.

Complete integral

An $n$-parameter solution $S$ delivers the motion by quadratures.

Separated form

$$\Phi_1\!\Big(\lambda_1,\tfrac{dS_1}{d\lambda_1}\Big)=\Phi_2\!\Big(\lambda_2,\tfrac{dS_2}{d\lambda_2}\Big)=\beta$$

Separation constant

The shared value $\beta$ is a second conserved quantity beyond $E$.

Result

Two integrals in involution: the system is Liouville-integrable.

Elliptic CoordinatesAppendix 20
Recall Hamilton-Jacobi from the main text: a complete integral of the partial differential equation gives the full solution by quadratures. In elliptic coordinates the kinetic energy and many natural potentials split into a sum where each variable appears alone, so the equation separates additively. That is the engine of integrability here.

The Liouville Template

Liouville metric

$$ds^2=(f_1-f_2)\big(d\lambda_1^2+d\lambda_2^2\big)$$

Additive potential

Potentials of form $V=\dfrac{V_1(\lambda_1)-V_2(\lambda_2)}{f_1-f_2}$ also separate.

Canonical example

Elliptic coordinates realise exactly this structure.

Second integral

The separation constant $\beta$ is an independent first integral, not a function of energy.

Integrability

Energy plus $\beta$ in involution on a 4D phase space: integrable by Liouville's theorem.

Recipe

Diagonal metric $\Rightarrow$ split HJ $\Rightarrow$ quadratures.

Jacobi: Geodesics on a Triaxial Ellipsoid

Jacobi's theorem

The geodesic flow on $\tfrac{x^2}{a}+\tfrac{y^2}{b}+\tfrac{z^2}{c}=1$ is integrable.

Method

Separate Hamilton-Jacobi in confocal ellipsoidal coordinates.

Joachimsthal integral

A conserved product travels along every geodesic.

The Problem of Two Fixed Centres

Euler's problem

One body attracted by two fixed Newtonian centres.

Potential

$$V=-\frac{\mu_1}{r_1}-\frac{\mu_2}{r_2}$$

Place the foci

Put elliptic-coordinate foci at the two centres.

It separates

In $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ the Hamilton-Jacobi equation splits, giving a second integral.

Why it matters

A solvable model for motion near two attractors; a classical limit of restricted gravitation.

Billiards Inside an Ellipse

The dynamics

A point mass reflects elastically off the ellipse: angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.

The caustic

Every segment touches one confocal conic, an ellipse or a hyperbola, fixed for the whole orbit.

Integrable

The caustic parameter is conserved at each bounce, giving the second integral.

Elliptic CoordinatesAppendix 20
Move to the billiard inside an ellipse. Every trajectory segment stays tangent to one fixed confocal conic, its caustic, an ellipse or a hyperbola. The caustic parameter is conserved at each bounce, so the billiard is integrable. This is the discrete shadow of the ellipsoid geodesic flow and a clean demonstration of the confocal invariant.

The Conserved Confocal Parameter

Reflection law

Bouncing off the ellipse preserves the confocal parameter of the chord's line.

Invariant

$$\lambda_{\text{caustic}}=\text{const along the orbit}$$

Same constant

It is the discrete sibling of the separation constant $\beta$.

One hidden symmetry

Ellipsoid geodesics, two fixed centres, and billiards all guard a confocal invariant.

Caustic dichotomy

Orbits encircling both foci have an elliptic caustic; orbits between the foci have a hyperbolic one.

Equivalent forms

Product of focal distances $r_1 r_2$, or the confocal angle, stays fixed.
