Riemannian Curvature

How geometry decides whether nearby motions stay together or fly apart

The object

Curvature: the local invariant of a metric, measuring deviation from flatness.

The mechanics

Geodesics are free motions; curvature governs how two of them drift relative to one another.

The payoff

Negative curvature of the fluid group $\Rightarrow$ practical unpredictability of weather.

Parallel Transport

The problem

Tangent vectors at different points live in different spaces. A connection prescribes how to carry one to the other.

The defect

Transport around a closed loop returns a rotated vector. The angle measures enclosed curvature.

Parallel TransportAppendix 11
Explain that on a curved surface there is no canonical way to compare vectors at different points; the connection supplies one by prescribing parallel transport. Emphasise the failure of path independence: dragging a vector around a loop rotates it, and that rotation angle is the first fingerprint of curvature we can actually see.

The Covariant Derivative

Differentiation, corrected

$\nabla_v w$ measures the change of $w$ along $v$ after subtracting the part forced by curvilinear coordinates.

Geodesics

A geodesic carries its own velocity parallel to itself: the straightest available path.

Curvature operator

The failure of mixed covariant derivatives to commute defines $R$.

Parallel field

$$\frac{D w}{dt} = \nabla_{\dot\gamma}\, w = 0$$

Geodesic equation

$$\frac{D \dot\gamma}{dt} = 0$$

Curvature tensor

$$R(X,Y) = \nabla_X\nabla_Y - \nabla_Y\nabla_X - \nabla_{[X,Y]}$$

Covariant DerivativeAppendix 11
Introduce the covariant derivative as the rate of change of a field corrected so that genuinely parallel fields read as constant. Note that a geodesic is the curve whose velocity is parallel to itself, the straightest possible path. This sets up the comparison of neighbouring straight paths on the next slides.

Sectional Curvature

Pick a plane

Choose a 2-plane $\sigma=\{\xi,\eta\}$ in the tangent space and sweep out geodesics tangent to it.

Gauss returns

$K(\sigma)$ is the Gaussian curvature of that geodesic surface, a single number per plane.

Sign is everything

$K>0$ focuses, $K<0$ defocuses, $K=0$ keeps geodesics parallel.

Definition

$$K(\sigma)=\frac{\langle R(\xi,\eta)\eta,\,\xi\rangle}{\lvert\xi\rvert^2\lvert\eta\rvert^2-\langle\xi,\eta\rangle^2}$$

Sectional CurvatureAppendix 11
Define sectional curvature as the Gaussian curvature of the surface swept by geodesics tangent to a chosen plane. Underline its operational meaning: it measures, to leading order, how the lengths of nearby parallel geodesics deviate. Positive means they bend toward each other, negative means they peel apart.

Geodesic Deviation: The Jacobi Equation

Setup

Take a geodesic $\gamma(t)$ with velocity $v=\dot\gamma$. Let $J(t)$ be the vector to an infinitesimally near geodesic.

Reading

Curvature acts like a spring constant. Its sign decides whether $J$ oscillates or explodes.

Why it matters

This is the linearised geodesic flow, the exact analogue of stability analysis in mechanics.

Equation of geodesic deviation

$$\frac{D^2 J}{dt^2} + R(J,\,v)\,v = 0$$

Scalar form (constant $K$)

$$\ddot{J} + K\,J = 0$$

$K>0$: harmonic, refocusing. $K<0$: hyperbolic, runaway.

The Jacobi EquationAppendix 11
Present the Jacobi equation as the linearisation of the geodesic flow about one geodesic. The separation field obeys a second-order equation in which curvature plays the role of a spring constant. Positive curvature gives an oscillator that refocuses deviations; negative curvature gives an inverted oscillator with runaway solutions.

On the saddle, two nearby geodesics separate without bound. Have students predict the sign of curvature from the picture before you confirm it.

Negative Curvature ⇒ Exponential Divergence

Solve the Jacobi equation

With $K=-k^2<0$ the equation $\ddot J - k^2 J=0$ has exponential solutions, not oscillations.

Lyapunov, geometrised

The growth rate $k=\sqrt{\lvert K\rvert}$ is a Lyapunov exponent: the geometry sets the clock of chaos.

Consequence

Indistinguishable initial states become macroscopically different in finite time.

Separation growth

$$J(t)=J(0)\,\cosh(k t)+\frac{\dot J(0)}{k}\sinh(k t)$$

Exponential DivergenceAppendix 11
Solve the Jacobi equation with constant negative curvature to expose hyperbolic growth. The separation swells like an exponential whose rate is the square root of the curvature magnitude. This is the geometric birth of sensitive dependence on initial conditions, the Lyapunov exponent written in the language of geometry.

Curvature of Lie Groups

Mechanics as geodesics

A one-sided invariant metric on a group $G$ encodes kinetic energy. Free motion is geodesic flow.

Reduce to the algebra

Invariance lets every geometric quantity be computed on the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}=T_e G$.

Examples

The rigid body ($SO(3)$) and, infinite-dimensionally, the ideal fluid.

Invariant kinetic metric

$$\langle u,v\rangle = \int u\cdot v \quad(\text{at } e),\;\; \text{extended by } G$$

Geometry of Lie GroupsAppendix 11
Shift to Arnold's reformulation of mechanics as geodesic flow on a Lie group carrying a one-sided invariant metric. The kinetic energy becomes the metric; the equations of motion become geodesic equations. Curvature is then computed algebraically from the commutator, turning a dynamical question into Lie-algebra bookkeeping.

Curvature from the Commutator

Algebraic formula

Sectional curvature of an invariant metric is built from the bracket $[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]$ and the inner product.

Simplification

For commuting directions the Riemannian connection and curvature collapse to clean bracket terms.

Output

A computable sign for each plane — the gateway to the fluid result.

Connection on $\mathfrak{g}$

$$2\,\nabla_u v = [u,v]-\mathrm{ad}^*_u v-\mathrm{ad}^*_v u$$

Curvature (special case)

$$\langle R(u,v)v,u\rangle=\tfrac14\lvert[u,v]\rvert^2-\langle\text{sym. terms}\rangle$$

Negative contributions dominate in most directions of the fluid algebra.

Curvature from the BracketAppendix 11
Give the working formula: the right-invariant curvature is assembled from brackets and the inner product on the algebra. Reassure students that the messy terms reduce dramatically for the special directions we care about. The key output is a sign, and for the fluid group that sign will be negative in most sections.

The Ideal Fluid as a Geodesic

Configuration space

The state of an ideal incompressible fluid is a volume-preserving diffeomorphism $g\in S\mathrm{Diff}(M)$.

Euler = geodesic

Inviscid flow is the geodesic of the kinetic-energy metric on this group (Arnold, 1966).

Infinite dimensions

The same Lie-group geometry applies, now to an infinite-dimensional group.

Energy metric

$$E[v]=\tfrac12\int_M \lvert v(x)\rvert^2\,dx,\quad \operatorname{div} v=0$$

The Ideal FluidAppendix 11
Introduce the punchline configuration space: volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of the flow domain. A fluid motion is a path in this infinite-dimensional group, and Euler's equations are exactly its geodesics for the kinetic-energy metric. Arnold's insight reframes hydrodynamics as Riemannian geometry on this group.

Negative Curvature and the Weather

Arnold's theorem

$S\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ has negative sectional curvature in most two-dimensional sections.

Jacobi mechanism

By geodesic deviation, neighbouring fluid motions separate exponentially fast.

Forecasting limit

A small uncertainty grows by orders of magnitude in weeks: detailed long-range weather is unpredictable in principle.

Negative Curvature & WeatherAppendix 11
Connect the threads: Arnold found that this diffeomorphism group has negative curvature in most two-dimensional sections. By the Jacobi mechanism, fluid trajectories diverge exponentially, so a tiny error in the initial state is amplified relentlessly. Cite his estimate that weeks-ahead forecasting is geometrically hopeless.

Summary: From Connection to Chaos

1. Connection

Parallel transport compares vectors; its loop-defect is curvature.

2. Jacobi

$\ddot J + R(J,v)v=0$ turns curvature into focusing or divergence of geodesics.

3. Lie groups

Invariant metrics make mechanics geodesic; curvature is read off the bracket.

4. Fluids

$S\mathrm{Diff}(M)$ curves negatively: weather defies long-range prediction.

One equation to remember

$$\frac{D^2 J}{dt^2}+R(J,v)\,v=0$$

Closing thought

Curvature is the precise bridge between the elegance of geometry and the hard limits of prediction in physics.

SummaryAppendix 11
Recap the logical chain in one breath: connection gives parallel transport, curvature measures its loop-defect, the Jacobi equation turns that into geodesic focusing or divergence, and on the fluid group divergence wins. Leave students with curvature as the bridge from pure geometry to the limits of prediction.
