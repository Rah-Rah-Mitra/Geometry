How to Read an Integrable Model

Symmetry

A group action preserving $H$.

Momentum map

Noether turns symmetry into conserved $P$.

Reduction

Quotient by the symmetry; fewer degrees of freedom.

Liouville-Arnold

$\{F_i,F_j\}=0,\;\; i=1,\dots,n$

$n$ independent integrals in involution on a $2n$-dimensional phase space.

Tori & angles

Compact regular level sets are $\mathbb{T}^n$; motion is linear in angle variables $\dot\varphi_i=\omega_i$.

Additional Mechanical ModelsThe integrable pipeline
Set the common vocabulary first. A symmetry is a group action preserving the Hamiltonian; Noether converts it to a momentum map; level sets of commuting integrals foliate phase space; on compact regular leaves Liouville-Arnold gives tori and angle coordinates. Every example below is one instance of this single pipeline.

The Spherical Pendulum

Configuration

Mass constrained to a sphere of radius $\ell$ under gravity; coordinates polar $\theta$, azimuth $\phi$.

Symmetry

Rotation about the vertical axis. The azimuthal momentum $p_\phi$ is conserved.

Lagrangian

$L=\tfrac{1}{2}m\ell^2(\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\!\theta\,\dot\phi^2)-mg\ell\cos\theta$

Reduce

$p_\phi=m\ell^2\sin^2\!\theta\,\dot\phi$ is cyclic → eliminate $\phi$, leaving a 1-D problem in $\theta$.

Additional Mechanical ModelsMotion on the sphere
The spherical pendulum is the cleanest case. A point mass on a sphere under gravity has rotational symmetry about the vertical, so the azimuthal momentum is conserved. Eliminating the angle leaves a one-dimensional problem in the polar angle governed by an effective potential with a centrifugal barrier.

Effective Potential & Turning Latitudes

Reduced energy

$E=\tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2+U_{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta)$

$U_{\mathrm{eff}}=\dfrac{p_\phi^2}{2m\ell^2\sin^2\!\theta}+mg\ell\cos\theta$

Turning latitudes

$E=U_{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta)$ has two roots $\theta_1<\theta_2$. The bob oscillates between them and precesses in $\phi$.

Additional Mechanical ModelsThe precessing band
Here we read the reduced radial picture. The effective potential combines gravity with the centrifugal term from the conserved azimuthal momentum. Energy is a horizontal line; its two intersections with the potential are the turning latitudes. The mass oscillates in latitude between them while precessing in longitude, tracing a band on the sphere.

The Three Classical Integrable Tops

Euler

Free body, $g=0$. Inertia $I_1,I_2,I_3$ arbitrary. Integrals: energy and $|\mathbf{M}|^2$.

Lagrange

Symmetric $I_1=I_2$, mass on the axis. Integrals: $E,\,p_\phi,\,p_\psi$.

Kovalevskaya

$I_1{:}I_2{:}I_3=2{:}2{:}1$, mass in equator. Extra quartic integral.

Additional Mechanical ModelsTops compared by inertia & symmetry
Rigid body rotation about a fixed point is integrable in exactly three classical cases. Euler: no gravity, motion by inertia alone. Lagrange: a symmetric top with center of mass on the symmetry axis. Kovalevskaya: a special inertia ratio two-to-two-to-one with the center of mass in the equatorial plane. Each adds one subtle integral.

The Heavy Symmetric (Lagrange) Top

Two symmetries

Rotation about the vertical and about the figure axis → conserved $p_\phi$ and $p_\psi$.

Three integrals

$E$, $p_\phi$, $p_\psi$ in involution: integrable by Liouville-Arnold.

Nutation potential

$E'=\tfrac12 I_1\dot\theta^2+V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta)$

$V_{\mathrm{eff}}=\dfrac{(p_\phi-p_\psi\cos\theta)^2}{2I_1\sin^2\!\theta}+mg\ell\cos\theta$

Nutation & precession

The axis nods between two cones while precessing — the same band picture as the pendulum.

Additional Mechanical ModelsGyroscope as reduced 1-D motion
The Lagrange top is the spinning toy gyroscope. Axial symmetry gives two conserved momenta about the vertical and the figure axis, and energy is the third integral. Reduction to the nutation angle yields an effective potential, so the axis nutates between two cones while precessing, exactly mirroring the spherical pendulum structure.

The Kovalevskaya Top

The discovery test

Demand the solution be single-valued (meromorphic) in complex time $t$. Only special parameters pass.

Special inertia

$I_1=I_2=2I_3$, center of mass in the equatorial plane.

The fourth integral (quartic)

$K=\big|(p_1+ip_2)^2 + c\,(\gamma_1+i\gamma_2)\big|^2$

Quartic in the momenta — not a quadratic energy-like quantity.

Why it matters

Integrability here is an analytic property, invisible to physical intuition; found via global structure, not symmetry alone.

Additional Mechanical ModelsA hidden quartic invariant
Kovalevskaya found the last integrable top by demanding the solution be meromorphic in complex time. Her extra integral is quartic in the momenta, not quadratic, and was invisible to physical intuition. It is the historical proof that integrability can be a deep analytic property, discoverable only through careful global structure.

Geodesics & Billiards in an Ellipse

The confocal integral

Every billiard chord stays tangent to one fixed confocal conic — the caustic. That tangency is the second conserved quantity.

Confocal family

$\dfrac{x^2}{a^2-\lambda}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2-\lambda}=1$

Elliptic coordinates $\lambda$ separate the Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

Additional Mechanical ModelsCaustic as conserved quantity
Confocal conics drive this example. A geodesic on an ellipsoid, or a billiard inside an ellipse, conserves a second quantity beyond energy: every segment stays tangent to one fixed confocal conic, the caustic. That caustic is the integral made visible, and elliptic coordinates separate the motion completely.

The Toda Lattice

A chain of particles

Nearest neighbours coupled by exponential springs — an integrable many-body system.

Hamiltonian

$H=\sum_k \tfrac12 p_k^2 + \sum_k e^{\,q_k-q_{k+1}}$

Lax pair

$\dot L=[B,L]$ ⇒ eigenvalues of $L$ are conserved (isospectral flow).

Additional Mechanical ModelsIntegrable many-body chain
The Toda lattice is a chain of particles with exponential nearest-neighbour springs. It is an integrable many-body system: written in Lax pair form, the eigenvalues of the Lax matrix are conserved under the flow. This isospectral structure produced solitons and seeded the modern theory of integrable systems.

Point Vortices in the Plane

Finite Hamiltonian system

$N$ vortices with circulations $\Gamma_k$; positions are the dynamical variables.

Hamiltonian & brackets

$H=-\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j\neq k}\Gamma_j\Gamma_k\ln|r_j-r_k|$

$\Gamma_k\,x_k,\;\; y_k \text{ are conjugate}$

Additional Mechanical ModelsVortices as conjugate variables
Point vortices turn fluid dynamics into a finite Hamiltonian system. Vortex positions are conjugate variables weighted by circulation, and the Hamiltonian is the logarithmic interaction energy. Translational and rotational symmetry give conserved linear and angular impulse, making two and three vortex problems integrable and four generically chaotic.

Ideal Fluid Flow as Geodesics

Arnold's reframing

An incompressible ideal fluid moves as a geodesic on the group $\mathrm{SDiff}(M)$ of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms.

Euler = geodesic equation

$\partial_t v + (v\cdot\nabla)v = -\nabla p,\quad \nabla\!\cdot v=0$

Right-invariant kinetic-energy metric; no force — free motion.

Curvature controls predictability

Negative curvature of $\mathrm{SDiff}$ means geodesics diverge: fluid flows are exponentially sensitive to initial data.

Geometry of the infinite

The same Riemannian language — metric, geodesic, curvature — extends to an infinite-dimensional configuration group.

Additional Mechanical ModelsHydrodynamics as Riemannian geometry
Arnold's deepest reframing closes the gallery. The Euler equations of an incompressible ideal fluid are exactly the geodesic equations on the infinite-dimensional group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms, with the kinetic-energy metric. Fluid motion is free motion, and the curvature of that group controls predictability of the flow.

Synthesis: One Grammar of Integrability

The shared reflex

Find symmetry → momentum map → reduce → look for tori and angles.

The gallery

Pendulum and Lagrange top: effective potentials. Euler & Kovalevskaya: hidden integrals. Ellipse: caustics. Toda: Lax pairs.

From finite to infinite

Vortices compress a fluid to finitely many conjugate pairs; the Euler fluid lifts mechanics to a diffeomorphism group.

Take-away

$\text{symmetry}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{integrals}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{tori}$

Models differ in dimension; the geometry is one.

Additional Mechanical ModelsRecap
Step back and see the single idea. Across pendulum, tops, billiards, lattices, vortices and fluids, the same geometric reflex applies: find the symmetry, build the momentum map, reduce, and look for the tori. The models differ in dimension and difficulty but share one grammar of integrable mechanics.
