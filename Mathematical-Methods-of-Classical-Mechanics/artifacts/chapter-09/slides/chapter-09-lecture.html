Completely Integrable Systems

n integrals

On a $2n$-dimensional symplectic manifold, find $n$ functions $F_1,\dots,F_n$ conserved by the flow.

Independence

The differentials $dF_i$ are linearly independent on the level set.

Involution

All pairwise Poisson brackets vanish, so their flows commute.

Involution condition

$$\{F_i, F_j\} = 0 \qquad i,j = 1,\dots,n$$

Why it matters

Commuting flows act jointly on the common level set, making it an invariant manifold rather than a mere intersection.

Canonical Perturbation TheoryIntegrable systems
State precisely what integrable means: n functionally independent integrals in involution on a 2n-dimensional symplectic manifold. Stress that involution, the vanishing Poisson bracket, is what makes the joint level sets into smooth invariant manifolds carrying commuting flows, not just intersecting hypersurfaces.

The Liouville-Arnold Theorem

Hypothesis

The common level set $M_f = \{F_i = f_i\}$ is compact and connected.

Conclusion

$M_f$ is diffeomorphic to the $n$-torus $\mathbb{T}^n$, and a neighborhood foliates into such tori.

Foliation

$$M_f \cong \mathbb{T}^n = \mathbb{R}^n / 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^n$$

Mechanism

$n$ commuting complete vector fields on a compact manifold generate a free $\mathbb{R}^n$-action; compactness collapses it to a torus.

Walk through the conclusion: a compact connected common level set of the integrals is diffeomorphic to an n-torus, and a neighborhood foliates into such tori. Emphasize that commuting complete vector fields on a compact manifold force the torus topology by the classification of such group actions.

Action-Angle Variables

Actions $I$

Loop integrals of the momentum one-form over the basic cycles $\gamma_i$ of the torus.

Angles $\varphi$

Uniform coordinates running $0$ to $2\pi$ around each cycle.

Action integral

$$I_i = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\gamma_i} p\,dq$$

Trivialized Hamiltonian

$$H = H_0(I), \qquad \dot I = 0, \quad \dot\varphi = \omega(I)$$

Outcome

Hamilton's equations decouple: actions frozen, angles advance uniformly.

Canonical Perturbation TheoryAction-angle variables
Introduce action-angle coordinates as the canonical chart that trivializes integrable dynamics. Actions are adiabatic-style invariants built from loop integrals of the momentum one-form; angles are uniform coordinates. In these variables Hamilton's equations decouple completely and the Hamiltonian depends on actions alone.

Conditionally Periodic Motion

Frequencies

The vector $\omega(I) = \partial H_0/\partial I$ sets the winding speeds on the torus.

Rational ratios

If $\omega$ is rationally dependent the orbit closes into a periodic loop.

Irrational ratios

If $\omega$ is rationally independent the orbit is dense, filling the torus.

Linear flow on the torus

$$\varphi(t) = \varphi_0 + \omega t \pmod{2\pi}$$

Canonical Perturbation TheoryConditionally periodic motion
Define conditionally periodic motion and tie it to the frequency vector, the gradient of the Hamiltonian in actions. Rational frequency ratios close into periodic orbits; irrational ratios fill the torus densely. This dichotomy is exactly what perturbation theory will have to respect torus by torus.

Nearly Integrable Systems

The split

An integrable core plus a small angle-dependent perturbation.

Broken symmetry

The term $\varepsilon H_1$ reintroduces $\varphi$, destroying conservation of the actions.

Perturbed Hamiltonian

$$H(I,\varphi) = H_0(I) + \varepsilon H_1(I,\varphi)$$

Goal

Seek a near-identity canonical transformation $(I,\varphi)\mapsto(J,\psi)$ that removes the angle dependence order by order in $\varepsilon$.

Tool

A generating function $S = I\!\cdot\!\varphi + \varepsilon S_1(I,\varphi) + \cdots$.

Canonical Perturbation TheoryNearly integrable systems
Set up the perturbation problem with the split Hamiltonian. The unperturbed part depends only on actions; the small term reintroduces angle dependence. Our goal is a canonical change of variables removing the angle dependence to higher order, which is where the obstruction will surface immediately.

The Averaging Principle

Fast and slow

Angles spin fast; actions drift slowly under $\varepsilon H_1$.

Average it out

Replace $H_1$ by its angle average to obtain the leading slow drift.

Angle average

$$\bar H_1(I) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^n} H_1(I,\varphi)\,d\varphi$$

Homological equation

$$\omega \cdot \partial_\varphi S_1 = \bar H_1 - H_1$$

Catch

Solving for $S_1$ divides each Fourier mode by $\omega\!\cdot\!k$.

Canonical Perturbation TheoryAveraging principle
Present averaging as the leading approximation: replace the perturbation by its angle average so actions drift slowly while angles spin fast. Explain that this works cleanly away from resonance, and that the formal generating function involves dividing Fourier coefficients by frequency combinations.

The Problem of Small Denominators

Dangerous division

Each Fourier coefficient $H_{1,k}$ is divided by the integer combination $\omega\!\cdot\!k$.

Resonance

When $\omega\!\cdot\!k = 0$ the term blows up; near-resonance makes it merely huge.

Formal series

$$S_1 = \sum_{k\neq 0} \frac{i\,H_{1,k}(I)}{\omega(I)\cdot k}\, e^{i k\cdot\varphi}$$

Canonical Perturbation TheorySmall denominators
Expose the core difficulty. The generating function carries terms divided by integer combinations of the frequencies, and these denominators come arbitrarily close to zero on resonant and near-resonant tori. Even with bounded numerators the series can diverge, so naive perturbation theory fails near resonances.

Diophantine Frequencies

Strong non-resonance

Keep only frequencies whose denominators stay bounded below.

Typical, not rare

The complement of these Diophantine $\omega$ has small measure as $\gamma\to 0$.

Diophantine condition

$$|\omega\cdot k| \ge \frac{\gamma}{|k|^{\tau}} \quad \forall\, k\in\mathbb{Z}^n\setminus\{0\}$$

Trade-off

Larger $\tau$ and smaller $\gamma$ admit more frequencies but weaken control of the series tail.

Payoff

The bound tames every small denominator at once, restoring convergence.

Canonical Perturbation TheoryDiophantine frequencies
Explain the cure conceptually. Restrict to strongly non-resonant frequencies satisfying a Diophantine condition bounding the denominators from below. Such frequencies are typical in the measure sense, so most tori avoid the dangerous resonances even though resonant tori are dense.

The KAM Theorem

Non-degeneracy

Frequencies vary with actions: $\det(\partial\omega/\partial I)\neq 0$, so frequency labels are usable.

Survival

For $\varepsilon$ small enough, Diophantine tori persist as slightly deformed invariant tori with the same $\omega$.

Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser

$$\text{meas}\big(\text{lost tori}\big) \le C\sqrt{\varepsilon}$$

Where order breaks

Only a thin neighborhood of the resonances loses its tori; the bulk of phase space stays quasi-periodic.

Method

A quadratically convergent Newton iteration beats the small denominators.

Canonical Perturbation TheoryKAM theorem
State Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser carefully. For a non-degenerate integrable system and a sufficiently small perturbation, most invariant tori survive, merely deformed, with the same frequencies. The surviving set has large measure, and tori are lost in a thin neighborhood of the resonances.

The KAM Picture and Recap

The chain of ideas

Integrability $\to$ tori $\to$ action-angle $\to$ averaging $\to$ small denominators $\to$ KAM survival.

Growing $\varepsilon$

As the perturbation grows, resonance zones widen, tori dissolve, and chaos fills the gaps.

Take-home

Order is not all-or-nothing: most quasi-periodic motion is robust under small perturbation.
