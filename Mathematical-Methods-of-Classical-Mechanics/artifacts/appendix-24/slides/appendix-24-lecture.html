Poisson Structures

A bracket on functions, more flexible than a symplectic form

The primitive

A bilinear bracket $\{f,g\}$ on smooth functions, not a two-form.

Why loosen

Reduced spaces and dual Lie algebras need a structure that may be degenerate.

Payoff

Rigid bodies and ideal fluids become Hamiltonian in one language.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
This appendix loosens the symplectic framework into the more flexible world of Poisson manifolds. We motivate why a bracket on functions, rather than a nondegenerate two-form, is the right primitive once we want to handle reduced spaces, dual Lie algebras, and rigid-body dynamics inside a single uniform language.

From Symplectic to Poisson

Symplectic origin

A nondegenerate closed $\omega$ inverts to a bracket on functions.

What we keep

The bracket, the flows, and conservation of energy.

What we drop

The demand that the structure be invertible everywhere.

Symplectic bracket

$$\{f,g\} = \omega^{-1}(df, dg)$$

Generalization

Replace $\omega^{-1}$ by any bivector that obeys Jacobi, even a degenerate one.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Recall that every symplectic manifold already carries a Poisson bracket built from the inverse of its symplectic form. The point of this appendix is that the bracket survives even when the form degenerates, so we keep Hamiltonian mechanics while dropping the demand that the structure be invertible everywhere.

Axioms of the Bracket

Bilinear & antisymmetric

$\{f,g\}=-\{g,f\}$, linear in each slot.

Leibniz rule

$\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\}$ — a derivation.

Jacobi identity

The cyclic sum of double brackets vanishes.

Jacobi

$$\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=0$$

Consequence

Functions form a Lie algebra under $\{\cdot,\cdot\}$.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Here we lay out the four defining axioms: bilinearity, antisymmetry, the Leibniz derivation rule, and the Jacobi identity. Emphasize that Leibniz makes the bracket a first-order differential operator in each slot, while Jacobi is the deep compatibility condition that makes Hamiltonian flows close into an algebra.

The Poisson Bivector

Encoded by a bivector

Leibniz forces $\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)$ for a field $\pi$.

Components

$\pi^{ij}$ is antisymmetric; the bracket reads off partial derivatives.

Jacobi as PDE

The Schouten bracket $[\pi,\pi]$ must vanish.

Coordinate form

$$\{f,g\}=\sum_{i,j}\pi^{ij}(x)\,\partial_i f\,\partial_j g$$

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Leibniz forces the bracket to come from a bivector field contracting the two differentials. Walk through how the components form an antisymmetric matrix, and stress that the Jacobi identity becomes a quadratic differential equation on that bivector, the vanishing Schouten bracket, which is far from automatic.

Hamiltonian Vector Fields

From function to flow

$X_H = \pi(dH,\cdot)$ generates the dynamics of $H$.

Bracket as derivative

$\{f,H\}=X_H f$ measures change of $f$ along the flow.

Homomorphism

$X_{\{f,g\}}=[X_f,X_g]$ — exactly because Jacobi holds.

Equation of motion

$$\dot f = \{f,H\}$$

Energy conserved

$\{H,H\}=0$, so $H$ is constant along its own flow.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Every function defines a Hamiltonian vector field by feeding it into the bivector. Explain that the bracket of two functions equals the directional derivative of one along the other's flow, and that the map from functions to vector fields is a Lie algebra homomorphism precisely because Jacobi holds.

Degeneracy and Casimirs

Rank can drop

Unlike symplectic forms, $\pi$ need not be invertible.

Casimir functions

$C$ with $\{C,f\}=0$ for all $f$ — the center.

Always conserved

Every Hamiltonian flow leaves Casimirs fixed.

Casimir condition

$$\{C,f\}=0 \quad \forall f \iff dC \in \ker\pi$$

Role

They label invariant subsets like superselection charges.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Unlike symplectic structures the bivector can drop rank. Functions whose Hamiltonian field vanishes, the Casimirs, bracket to zero with everything and form the center of the algebra. They are conserved by every Hamiltonian flow, so they label invariant subsets and behave like superselection charges of the system.

Symplectic Leaves

Foliation

Hamiltonian fields integrate to leaves through each point.

Genuine symplectic

On each leaf $\pi$ restricts to a nondegenerate form.

Casimirs are constant

Functions constant on leaves are exactly the Casimirs.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
The image of all Hamiltonian fields integrates into a foliation whose leaves carry a genuine symplectic form. Stress that dynamics never leaves the leaf through a starting point, that leaf dimension equals the bivector rank, and that Casimirs are exactly the functions constant along every leaf.

The Lie–Poisson Bracket

On the dual $\mathfrak{g}^*$

Functions on the dual of a Lie algebra.

Differentials are elements

$df,dg \in \mathfrak{g}$ via the natural pairing.

Canonical

No extra choice needed — built from the commutator.

Linear bracket

$$\{f,g\}(\mu)=\big\langle \mu,\,[df,dg]\big\rangle$$

Linearity

$\pi$ is linear in $\mu$ — representation theory becomes geometry.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
The central example lives on the dual of a Lie algebra. The bracket evaluates the point against the commutator of the two differentials, which are themselves Lie algebra elements. This linear structure is canonical, requires no extra choices, and turns representation theory into geometry.

Coadjoint Orbits

Leaves = orbits

Symplectic leaves of $\mathfrak{g}^*$ are coadjoint orbits.

KKS form

Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau symplectic structure.

Even-dimensional

Each orbit is a symplectic homogeneous space.

Coadjoint action

$$\mathcal{O}_\mu=\{\,\mathrm{Ad}^*_g\,\mu : g\in G\,\}$$

Casimirs cut orbits

Invariant polynomials slice $\mathfrak{g}^*$ into orbits.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
For the Lie-Poisson structure the symplectic leaves are precisely the coadjoint orbits of the group acting on the dual. Mention Kirillov, Kostant, and Souriau, and note that the quadratic Casimirs cut the dual into these orbits, each an even-dimensional symplectic homogeneous space.

Rigid Body on $\mathfrak{so}(3)^*$

Momentum space

$\mathfrak{so}(3)^*\cong\mathbb{R}^3$, the angular momentum $\mathbf{m}$.

Casimir

$C=\tfrac12|\mathbf{m}|^2$ — leaves are concentric spheres.

Euler equations

$\dot{\mathbf{m}}=\mathbf{m}\times\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ is the Lie–Poisson flow.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Specialize to the rotation algebra, whose dual is three-dimensional angular-momentum space. The Casimir is the squared length of the momentum, so leaves are concentric spheres. Euler's rigid-body equations are exactly the Lie-Poisson flow, tracing energy-ellipsoid intersections on each fixed-radius sphere.

Reduction from $T^*G$

Cotangent bundle

$T^*G$ carries the canonical symplectic bracket.

Translate to identity

Left/right trivialization sends $T^*G \to \mathfrak{g}^*$.

Bracket descends

The canonical bracket pushes to the Lie–Poisson one.

Reduction map

$$T^*G\;/\;G \;\cong\; \mathfrak{g}^*$$

Why it matters

Rigid bodies and ideal fluids inherit Hamiltonian form after symmetry reduction.

Poisson StructuresAppendix 24
Explain that left or right translation collapses the cotangent bundle of a Lie group onto the dual of its algebra, and the canonical bracket pushes forward to the Lie-Poisson bracket. This is the abstract reason rigid bodies and ideal fluids inherit a Hamiltonian description after symmetry reduction.

Synthesis

Foliated stack

A Poisson manifold is symplectic leaves indexed by Casimirs.

Universal model

The dual $\mathfrak{g}^*$ is the canonical linear example.

Reduction

Symmetry explains why mechanics is Poisson.

One picture

$$\dot f=\{f,H\},\quad \{C,\cdot\}=0,\quad \mathcal{O}_\mu\subset\mathfrak{g}^*$$

Looking ahead

Quantization and integrable systems both rest on this scaffold.
