Variational Principles

Motion as the selection of a stationary path

The shift

From instantaneous forces to whole trajectories scored by a single number.

The action

$S[q]=\int_{t_0}^{t_1} L\,dt$ ranks every admissible curve between fixed ends.

The claim

Nature realises the curve where $S$ is stationary.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Open by reframing mechanics: instead of asking which force pushes a particle at each instant, we ask which whole trajectory a system selects between fixed endpoints. The action functional scores every candidate path, and nature picks the stationary one. This single idea unifies Newton, Lagrange, and Hamilton across the chapter.

Functionals and Admissible Variations

Functional

A map $q(\cdot)\mapsto S[q]\in\mathbb{R}$ that consumes an entire curve, not a point.

Variation

Perturb by $q(t)+\varepsilon\,\eta(t)$ with $\eta(t_0)=\eta(t_1)=0$ so endpoints stay fixed.

One-parameter family

$$\Phi(\varepsilon)=S[q+\varepsilon\eta]$$

Reduction

Searching over curves becomes ordinary calculus in the single variable $\varepsilon$.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Stress that a functional eats an entire curve and returns one number, unlike an ordinary function of finitely many variables. Introduce the admissible variation eta that vanishes at both endpoints, so the perturbed curve still connects the same boundary points. The parameter epsilon turns the search over curves into ordinary calculus.

The First Variation

Differentiate

Stationary means $\left.\dfrac{d\Phi}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}=0$ for every admissible $\eta$.

Integrate by parts

Shift the derivative off $\dot\eta$; the boundary term vanishes since $\eta$ dies at the ends.

First variation

$$\delta S=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\!\Big(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\Big)\eta\,dt$$

Fundamental lemma

If the integral vanishes for all $\eta$, the bracket vanishes pointwise.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Walk through differentiating the action with respect to epsilon at zero. Integration by parts moves the derivative off eta-dot, and the boundary term dies because eta vanishes at the ends. What survives is an integral of eta against a bracket, and arbitrariness of eta forces that bracket to vanish pointwise.

The Euler-Lagrange Equations

Stationarity condition

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^{i}}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{i}}=0$$

Order

Expanding $\tfrac{d}{dt}$ yields a second-order system for $q(t)$.

What it is

The necessary condition that a curve be a stationary point of the action.

Logical hinge

The fundamental lemma converts an integral identity into a differential equation.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
State the Euler-Lagrange equation as the necessary condition for a stationary curve. Emphasize it is generally a second-order differential equation in q once the total time derivative is expanded. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations is the logical hinge that converts an integral identity into a pointwise equation.

Hamilton's Principle of Stationary Action

Lagrangian

$$L=T-U=\tfrac12 m\dot q^2-U(q)$$

Principle

Physical motion makes $S=\int L\,dt$ stationary among neighbouring paths.

Least or stationary?

"Least action" is tradition; on long intervals the true path can be a saddle, not a minimum.

Payoff

The dynamics follow from one scalar $L$ rather than a vector of forces.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Introduce the Lagrangian as kinetic minus potential energy and define the action as its time integral. Hamilton's principle asserts that physical motion makes the action stationary, not necessarily minimal. Caution students that least action is traditional language; stationarity, including saddle behavior on long intervals, is the precise statement.

Equivalence to Newton's Equations

Plug in $L=T-U$

$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}=m\dot q$ and $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q}=-\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial q}$.

Result

$$m\ddot q=-\frac{\partial U}{\partial q}=F$$

Inertia

The momentum term $\tfrac{d}{dt}(m\dot q)$ supplies $m\ddot q$.

Force

The potential term supplies $-\nabla U$, the conservative force.

Conclusion

Variational and Newtonian mechanics describe the same trajectories.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Carry out the computation that the Euler-Lagrange equation for L equals T minus U reproduces mass times acceleration equals minus gradient of U. The momentum derivative gives the inertial term and the potential derivative gives the force. This is the proof that the variational and Newtonian formulations are the same physics.

Picturing the Variation

Reading the gap

Each competitor differs from the true path by the variation $\varepsilon\eta(t)$.

Stationarity

$$S[q+\varepsilon\eta]=S[q]+O(\varepsilon^2)$$

Meaning

First-order path changes cause only second-order action changes.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Use the diagram to make variation tangible: the bold curve is the true motion, the faint curves are competitors sharing both endpoints. The vertical gap between them is epsilon times eta. Action is stationary exactly when first-order changes in the path produce only second-order changes in the action value.

The Legendre Transformation

Conjugate momentum & Hamiltonian

$$p=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q},\quad H(q,p)=p\dot q-L$$

Geometry

Encode the convex curve $L(\dot q)$ by its tangent lines: slope $p$, intercept $-H$.

Invertibility

Convexity of $L$ in $\dot q$ makes the map $\dot q\leftrightarrow p$ a bijection.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Define the conjugate momentum as the derivative of L with respect to velocity, then build the Hamiltonian as the Legendre transform of the Lagrangian. Geometrically the transform encodes a convex function by its tangent lines, trading slope for intercept. Convexity in velocity is what guarantees the transform is invertible.

Hamilton's Canonical Equations

First-order form

$$\dot q=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p},\qquad \dot p=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}$$

Trade-off

One second-order equation becomes two first-order equations on phase space.

Phase space

States are pairs $(q,p)$; the dimension doubles but the flow simplifies.

Antisymmetry

The sign asymmetry between the two equations is the symplectic skeleton.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Show that rewriting the Euler-Lagrange equation in the new variables splits one second-order equation into two first-order equations. Phase space doubles the dimension but linearizes the bookkeeping. Point out the antisymmetric structure: q-dot reads off the p-derivative of H, while p-dot is minus the q-derivative.

Cyclic Coordinates and Conservation

Cyclic coordinate

$L$ depends on $\dot q^{k}$ but not on $q^{k}$ itself.

Conserved momentum

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{k}}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^{k}}=0$$

Examples

Ignorable translation gives conserved linear momentum; ignorable angle gives conserved angular momentum.

Reading

Each missing coordinate hands you a first integral of the motion for free.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
A coordinate is cyclic when the Lagrangian does not depend on it explicitly, only on its velocity. Then the Euler-Lagrange equation says the conjugate momentum has zero time derivative, hence is conserved. Translational and rotational symmetries surface here as ignorable coordinates whose momenta are constants of motion.

A First Look at Noether's Theorem

Symmetry

A one-parameter family $q\mapsto h_s(q)$ leaving $L$ invariant for all $s$.

Conserved quantity

$$I=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\cdot\left.\frac{dh_s}{ds}\right|_{s=0}=\text{const}$$

Proof idea

Differentiate invariance in $s$, then use Euler-Lagrange to spot a total time derivative.

Slogan

Every continuous symmetry of the action yields a conservation law.

Variational PrinciplesChapter 03
Present the first look at Noether: a one-parameter family of transformations leaving the Lagrangian invariant produces a conserved quantity along solutions. The proof differentiates the invariance condition and uses the Euler-Lagrange equation to recognize a total time derivative. Symmetry and conservation become two faces of one statement.
