What is a Symplectic Form?

Closed

$d\omega = 0$ — the integrability condition behind Jacobi.

Nondegenerate

$\omega(v,\cdot)=0 \Rightarrow v=0$ — forces $\dim M$ even.

Isomorphism

$v \mapsto \omega(v,\cdot)$ identifies $T_xM$ with $T_x^*M$.

Definition

$(M,\omega),\quad d\omega=0,\quad \omega^{\wedge n}\neq 0$

Standard form

$\omega = \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} dp_i \wedge dq_i$

An even-dimensional manifold equipped with such a form is a symplectic manifold; the pair is the arena for all that follows.

The Canonical Example: $T^*Q$

Configuration $Q$

Positions $q$ live on the base manifold $Q$.

Momenta as covectors

The fibre over $q$ is $T_q^*Q$, with coordinates $p$.

No choices needed

The form $\omega$ is intrinsic to any cotangent bundle.

Tautological one-form

$\theta = \displaystyle\sum_i p_i\, dq_i$

Canonical two-form

$\omega = -d\theta = \displaystyle\sum_i dp_i \wedge dq_i$

Closed because $\omega = -d\theta$ is exact; nondegenerate by construction. Every mechanical phase space is symplectic for free.

$\omega$ Measures Oriented Area

Area pairing

$\omega(u,v) = \displaystyle\sum_i \big(u_{p_i}v_{q_i} - u_{q_i}v_{p_i}\big)$

Per conjugate plane

Each term $dp_i\wedge dq_i$ reads off the oriented area in the $(q_i,p_i)$ plane.

Orientation matters

Swapping $u$ and $v$ flips the sign — antisymmetry is built in.

From $H$ to the Vector Field $X_H$

Defining relation

$\omega(X_H,\,\cdot\,) = dH$

Invert the form

Nondegeneracy makes $X_H$ unique for each smooth $H$.

Tangent to level sets

$dH(X_H)=\omega(X_H,X_H)=0$, so $H$ is conserved.

Hamilton's Equations as a Flow

Integral curves of $X_H$

$\dot q_i = \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\quad \dot p_i = -\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}$

The flow $g^t$

Following $X_H$ for time $t$ defines the Hamiltonian phase flow.

Built from $\omega$

Because $X_H$ comes from $\omega$, the flow preserves $\omega$ exactly.

Lie derivative vanishes

$\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = d\,\iota_{X_H}\omega = d(dH) = 0$, so the flow is symplectic.

Consequence

$\big(g^t\big)^{*}\omega = \omega$

The Hamiltonian flow carries the symplectic structure along with it — a fact we will cash out as the integral invariants.

The Poisson Bracket

Definition

$\{f,g\} = \omega(X_f, X_g)$

In Darboux coordinates

$\{f,g\} = \displaystyle\sum_i \Big(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial q_i} - \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial q_i}\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial p_i}\Big)$

Evolution

$\dot f = \{f, H\}$ governs every observable.

Antisymmetric

$\{f,g\} = -\{g,f\}$.

Leibniz

$\{f,gh\} = \{f,g\}h + g\{f,h\}$.

Jacobi

$\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=0$ — true because $d\omega=0$.

Introduce the Poisson bracket as the pairing of two Hamiltonian fields through omega. Highlight the three structural facts: antisymmetry, the Leibniz product rule, and the Jacobi identity, which holds precisely because omega is closed. Note that f evolves in time by its bracket with H.

Darboux's Theorem

Theorem

Near any point of a $2n$-dimensional symplectic manifold there exist local coordinates $(q_1,\dots,q_n,p_1,\dots,p_n)$ in which

$\omega = \displaystyle\sum_i dp_i \wedge dq_i.$

No local invariant

Unlike curvature in Riemannian geometry, symplectic manifolds have no local distinguishing data.

All locally standard

Every symplectic manifold looks like the model $(\mathbb{R}^{2n}, \sum dp\wedge dq)$ near a point.

Differences are global

Topology and global geometry, not local shape, separate symplectic manifolds.

Why it matters

It justifies always computing in canonical $(q,p)$ coordinates.

Darboux's TheoremChapter 08
State Darboux's theorem and explain its philosophical weight: unlike Riemannian geometry there is no local curvature invariant. Every symplectic manifold looks identical near a point, so all local symplectic geometry is the standard model. Differences between symplectic manifolds are entirely global, not local.

Canonical Transformations

Definition

$\varphi^{*}\omega = \omega$

Preserve structure

A diffeomorphism is canonical when it preserves the symplectic form.

Form-invariance

Hamilton's equations keep their shape in the new coordinates.

Generating Functions

Implicit construction

A function $S(q,Q)$ of old and new positions defines a canonical map.

Type-one generator

$p_i = \dfrac{\partial S}{\partial q_i},\qquad P_i = -\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial Q_i}$

Why it works

$p\,dq - P\,dQ = dS$ guarantees $\omega$ is preserved.

Mixed variables

Other types use $S(q,P)$, $S(p,Q)$, $S(p,P)$ via Legendre transforms.

Bridge to Hamilton-Jacobi

$\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial t} + H\!\Big(q,\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial q}\Big) = 0$

The action functional itself generates the flow — the heart of Hamilton-Jacobi theory in the next chapter.

Poincaré Integral Invariants

Relative invariant

$\displaystyle\oint_{\gamma} \sum_i p_i\,dq_i = \oint_{g^t\gamma} \sum_i p_i\,dq_i$

Absolute invariant

$\displaystyle\iint_{\sigma}\omega = \iint_{g^t\sigma}\omega$

Liouville volume

$\omega^{\wedge n}/n!$ is the conserved phase-space volume.

One Form, the Whole Theory

Structure

$(M,\omega)$ — closed, nondegenerate, even-dimensional.

Dynamics

$\omega(X_H,\cdot)=dH$ turns energy into motion.

Algebra

$\{f,g\}=\omega(X_f,X_g)$ — a Lie algebra of observables.

Local model

Darboux: all symplectic manifolds are locally $\sum dp\wedge dq$.

Conserved by the flow

$(g^t)^*\omega=\omega \;\Rightarrow\; \oint p\,dq,\;\; \omega^{\wedge n}$
