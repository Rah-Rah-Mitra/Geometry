From Lagrangian to Hamiltonian

Conjugate momentum

Define $p$ as the velocity-gradient of the Lagrangian.

Change of variable

Trade the velocity $\dot q$ for the momentum $p$ as the independent coordinate.

Same physics

No information is lost; only the chart changes.

Momentum

$$p_i = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}(q,\dot q,t)$$

Hamiltonian

$$H(q,p,t) = p_i\,\dot q^i - L(q,\dot q,t)$$

The Legendre Transform

Tangent-line duality

$H$ records, for each slope $p$, how far the tangent of $L$ lies below the curve.

Convexity

If $L$ is convex in $\dot q$, the map $\dot q \mapsto p$ inverts uniquely.

Sign flip

$$dH = \dot q\,dp - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}\,dq - \frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\,dt$$

Point out that the Hamiltonian is the transform's value, and that differentials of L and H carry opposite signs.

Hamilton's Canonical Equations

The system

$$\dot q^i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad \dot p_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}$$

First order

One second-order Euler-Lagrange system becomes two first-order equations.

Antisymmetry

The plus on $\dot q$ and minus on $\dot p$ are the seed of symplectic structure.

Where they come from

Compare $dH$ from the Legendre relation with the Euler-Lagrange equation $\dot p = \partial L/\partial q$. Matching the $dp$ and $dq$ coefficients gives the canonical pair directly.

Reading the field

The right-hand sides define a vector field $X_H = (\partial_p H,\,-\partial_q H)$ on phase space whose integral curves are the motions.

Highlight the antisymmetry: position follows the momentum gradient, momentum follows the negative position gradient. Two first-order equations replace one second-order system, which is the structural heart of the whole formalism.

A complete state

Conservation of Energy

Rate along the flow

$$\frac{dH}{dt} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i}\dot q^i + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\dot p_i + \frac{\partial H}{\partial t}$$

Cancellation

Substituting the canonical equations kills the first two terms by antisymmetry.

Result

$$\frac{dH}{dt} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial t}$$

Time-independent case

If $H$ has no explicit $t$, then $H$ is conserved and every orbit lies in a level set.

Driven case

The partial $\partial_t H$ measures the rate of energy injected by an explicit time dependence.

Energy Level Sets as Orbits

Tangency

$X_H$ is everywhere tangent to $\{H=\text{const}\}$, so motion stays on one surface.

Gradient, rotated

$$X_H = J\,\nabla H,\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$$

Why it cannot leave

$\langle \nabla H, X_H\rangle = \nabla H \cdot J\,\nabla H = 0$.

Cyclic Coordinates

Definition

A coordinate $q^k$ is cyclic if $H$ does not depend on it.

Conserved momentum

$$\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^k}=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \dot p_k = 0$$

Reduction

Each conserved $p_k$ removes one effective degree of freedom.

Canonical example

For a central potential $H=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(p_r^2 + p_\theta^2/r^2\big)+V(r)$, the angle $\theta$ is cyclic, so the angular momentum $p_\theta$ is conserved.

Symmetry link

A cyclic coordinate signals a continuous symmetry; its conserved momentum is the corresponding Noether charge.

Liouville's Theorem

Claim

The Hamiltonian phase flow preserves phase-space volume.

Divergence-free field

$$\nabla\!\cdot X_H = \frac{\partial}{\partial q^i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i} = 0$$

Why

Mixed second partials commute, so the two terms cancel exactly.

Consequence

A region of initial conditions may stretch and fold, but its measure is invariant for all time.

Invariant measure

$$\frac{d}{dt}\!\int_{\Omega_t}\! dq\,dp = 0$$

The Poisson Bracket

Definition

$$\{f,g\} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial q^i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q^i}$$

Canonical relations

$$\{q^i,p_j\}=\delta^i_j,\quad \{q^i,q^j\}=\{p_i,p_j\}=0$$

Algebraic properties

  • Antisymmetry: $\{f,g\}=-\{g,f\}$
  • Bilinearity over constants
  • Leibniz: $\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\}$
  • Jacobi: $\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\text{cyclic}=0$

What it encodes

The bracket packages the symplectic form $J$ into a single operation on observables.

Evolution and First Integrals

Evolution law

$$\frac{df}{dt} = \{f,H\} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}$$

$H$ generates time

Time translation is the flow of the Hamiltonian through the bracket.

First integral

$\{f,H\}=0$ (and no explicit $t$) means $f$ is conserved.

Recovering old facts

$\{H,H\}=0$ gives energy conservation; $\{p_k,H\}=0$ for a cyclic $q^k$ gives the conserved momentum.

Poisson's theorem

By the Jacobi identity, if $f$ and $g$ are integrals then so is $\{f,g\}$ — integrals form a closed algebra.
