Perturbation Theory

When a small force breaks an integrable world

The setup

Integrable $H_0(I)$ perturbed by $\varepsilon H_1(I,\varphi)$.

The obstruction

Small denominators $k\cdot\omega(I)$ wreck the naive series.

The verdicts

Poincare destroys, KAM and Nekhoroshev rescue.

Open by framing the appendix as the place where the dream of integrability meets its limits. We start from an integrable system in action-angle form and ask what survives a small Hamiltonian perturbation. Preview the three landmarks: the homological equation, small denominators, and the verdicts of Poincare, KAM, and Nekhoroshev.

The Integrable Background

Action-angle

$H_0$ depends on actions $I$ alone; angles $\varphi$ are cyclic.

Linear flow

Angles advance uniformly at frequency $\omega(I)=\partial H_0/\partial I$.

Foliation

Phase space is a stack of invariant tori, one per action value.

Unperturbed dynamics

$$\dot I = 0,\qquad \dot\varphi = \omega(I) = \frac{\partial H_0}{\partial I}$$

Key fact

The actions are exact conserved quantities. Everything moves on a fixed torus.

Integrable BackgroundAppendix 18
Remind students that action-angle variables turn an integrable Hamiltonian into a function of the actions alone. The angles wind uniformly with frequencies given by the gradient of H zero. Each torus is invariant, and the whole phase space foliates into these tori. This clean picture is exactly what the perturbation will threaten.

The Perturbed Problem

The new Hamiltonian

A small angle-dependent term spoils conservation of the actions.

The goal

Find canonical coordinates in which the angle dependence is pushed to higher order.

Perturbed system

$$H(I,\varphi) = H_0(I) + \varepsilon\, H_1(I,\varphi) + \dots$$

Strategy

Not to solve, but to transform $H$ so it again looks like a function of actions alone, order by order in $\varepsilon$.

The Perturbed ProblemAppendix 18
Introduce the perturbed Hamiltonian and the central goal: find a canonical change of variables that pushes the angle dependence to higher order in epsilon. Stress that we are not solving the system, only trying to make it look integrable order by order. The generating function is the unknown we expand in powers of epsilon.

The Generating Function Scheme

Near-identity map

A generating function $S(I',\varphi)$ close to the identity defines a canonical change to new variables $(I',\varphi')$.

Expand in $\varepsilon$

Write $S = I'\cdot\varphi + \varepsilon S_1 + \dots$ and match powers.

Canonical transformation

$$I = \frac{\partial S}{\partial \varphi},\qquad \varphi' = \frac{\partial S}{\partial I'}$$

Self-similar

The same linear problem recurs at every order, so order one is the template for all.

Generating Function SchemeAppendix 18
Walk through the near-identity canonical transformation built from a generating function of mixed old and new variables. Expanding everything in epsilon and matching the first order gives a single linear equation for S one. Emphasize that the structure repeats at every order, so understanding order one tells the whole story.

The Homological Equation

Order-one matching

Collecting $\varepsilon^1$ terms gives one linear equation for $S_1$.

Transport form

It is a derivative of $S_1$ along the unperturbed winding $\omega\cdot\partial_\varphi$.

The equation to solve

$$\omega(I)\cdot\frac{\partial S_1}{\partial\varphi} + H_1(I,\varphi) = \overline{H}_1(I)$$

Reading it

Kill the oscillating part of $H_1$; the average $\overline{H}_1$ survives into the new Hamiltonian.

The Homological EquationAppendix 18
Derive the homological equation: the directional derivative of S one along the unperturbed flow must cancel the oscillating part of H one. This is the heart of perturbation theory. It is a transport equation along the linear winding of the torus, and its solvability depends entirely on the arithmetic of the frequencies.
Mathematical Methods of Classical MechanicsAppendix 18

Solving by Fourier Series

Expand on the torus

Write $H_1 = \sum_k h_k(I)\, e^{i k\cdot\varphi}$ over integer vectors $k$.

Divide mode by mode

Each coefficient acquires the factor $1/(i\,k\cdot\omega)$.

Formal solution

$$S_1 = \sum_{k\neq 0} \frac{h_k(I)}{i\,(k\cdot\omega(I))}\, e^{i k\cdot\varphi}$$

The zero mode

$k=0$ cannot be removed: $\overline{H}_1$ corrects the frequencies.

Fourier SolutionAppendix 18
Solve the homological equation by Fourier series on the torus. Each Fourier mode of the perturbation divides by the inner product of the integer vector k with the frequency vector omega. Point out the zero mode constraint: the average of H one shifts the new Hamiltonian and cannot be removed, defining the corrected frequencies.

The Small-Denominator Obstruction

The catastrophe

$k\cdot\omega$ can be made arbitrarily small, exploding the coefficient $h_k/(k\cdot\omega)$.

Resonance

When $\omega$ is rationally related, some $k\cdot\omega$ vanishes exactly.

Small DenominatorsAppendix 18
This is the crisis slide. The denominators k dot omega can be made arbitrarily small by suitable integer vectors, since rationally related frequencies make them vanish exactly. Even tiny Fourier coefficients are amplified without bound. Use the plot to show the denominator dipping toward zero on resonance lines, blowing the series coefficients up.

The Resonance Web

Resonance surfaces

Each condition $k\cdot\omega(I)=0$ is a hypersurface in action space.

Dense failure

The surfaces accumulate densely; near them the series diverges.

Survivors

Non-resonant actions form the complement where transformation may converge.

Resonance WebAppendix 18
Move from a single torus to the whole action space. Each resonance condition k dot omega equals zero is a hypersurface, and together they form a dense web. Near this web the construction fails, while in the non-resonant complement it has a chance. The picture is a fractured plane of resonance lines over surviving regions.

Divergence and Poincare's Theorem

The naive series diverges

Because resonances are dense, no neighborhood escapes small denominators.

Poincare's verdict

A generic perturbation admits no analytic first integral independent of energy.

Nonexistence (Poincare)

$$\nexists\; F\ \text{analytic, independent of}\ H,\ \{F,H\}=0$$

Meaning

Formal integrability fails in the analytic category. The transformation series is asymptotic, not convergent.

Divergence and PoincareAppendix 18
State the bad news plainly. The naive series generally diverges because the resonance web is dense, so no neighborhood is free of small denominators. Connect this to Poincare's theorem: for a generic perturbation there are no analytic first integrals independent of the energy. Integrability is destroyed by the perturbation in the analytic category.

Diophantine Frequencies

Stay far from resonance

Demand a quantitative lower bound on $|k\cdot\omega|$.

Full measure

Almost every $\omega$ is Diophantine, though the bad set is dense.

Diophantine condition

$$|k\cdot\omega| \ \geq\ \frac{\gamma}{|k|^{\tau}}\quad\text{for all } k\neq 0$$

Why it helps

Denominators decay only polynomially, so well-chosen modes keep the series controlled.

Diophantine FrequenciesAppendix 18
Introduce the rescue. Frequencies satisfying a Diophantine condition stay quantitatively far from resonance, so the denominators decay only polynomially in the mode index. This controlled lower bound is precisely what convergence proofs need. Note that almost every frequency vector is Diophantine, even though the bad set is dense.

The KAM Theorem

Tori survive

Under nondegeneracy and small $\varepsilon$, Diophantine tori persist, only deformed.

Large measure

Surviving tori fill most of phase space; resonant gaps host chaos.

Newton scheme

Rapid quadratic convergence outruns the small denominators.

The KAM TheoremAppendix 18
Present KAM as the triumph over small denominators. Under a nondegeneracy condition and sufficiently small epsilon, tori with Diophantine frequencies survive, merely deformed. The invariant tori fill a set of large measure, while resonant gaps host chaos. Mention the rapidly convergent Newton scheme that defeats the divergence of the naive series.

Nekhoroshev Stability & Recap

Exponential stability

Even off the tori, actions barely move for very long times.

Nekhoroshev estimate

$$|I(t)-I(0)| \leq \varepsilon^{a}\quad\text{for}\quad |t|\leq \exp\!\big(c\,\varepsilon^{-b}\big)$$

The arc

Homological equation $\to$ small denominators $\to$ divergence $\to$ KAM and Nekhoroshev.

One lesson

The arithmetic of frequencies decides the fate of the motion: resonance breaks, irrationality protects.

Outlook

These estimates underpin stability of the solar system and of magnetic confinement.

Nekhoroshev & RecapAppendix 18
Close with Nekhoroshev's exponential stability: even where tori break, actions drift only by tiny amounts over times exponentially long in one over epsilon. Recap the arc from homological equation to small denominators to the KAM and Nekhoroshev resolutions, and stress that arithmetic of frequencies governs the fate of motion.
