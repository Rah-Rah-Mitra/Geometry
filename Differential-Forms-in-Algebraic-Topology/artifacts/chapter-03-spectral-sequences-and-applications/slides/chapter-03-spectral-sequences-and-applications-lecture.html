Filtered Complexes

Filtered Complexes Hide Layers

Start With Structure
Definition Lens
A decreasing filtered cochain complex

\(C^\ast = F^0 C^\ast \supset F^1 C^\ast \supset F^2 C^\ast \supset \cdots\), with \(d(F^p C^\ast) \subset F^p C^{\ast+1}\).

Associated Graded

\(\operatorname{gr}^p C^\ast = F^p C^\ast / F^{p+1} C^\ast\)

The first page sees one layer at a time; later pages remember how layers talk to one another.

Filtered complex grammarprinted pages 154-265.
Pause on the word filtered. A filtration is not extra decoration on a complex; it is a way of saying that some information is visible before other information. The differential respects the layers, so the associated graded object can be studied one slice at a time. The spectral sequence is the record of corrections as deeper interactions are allowed to appear.
Page Semantics

Pages Are Successive Ledgers

E_r To E_{r+1}
Page Rule
The next page is a cohomology page

\(E_{r+1}^{p,q} \cong H(E_r^{p,q}, d_r)\)

Read Each Entry As Provisional

  • It has survived all earlier differentials.
  • It may still support or receive a later differential.
  • Its final shadow contributes to associated graded cohomology.

Teaching Warning

An empty target on E_2 does not prove a class is permanent unless later bidegrees are ruled out.

Page ledger: cycles modulo boundaries at stage rSource PDF: Differential Forms in Algebraic Topology.pdf
Frame an E-page as a ledger of provisional classes. The entries are not yet final cohomology classes; they are survivors at a particular resolution. Moving from E_r to E_{r+1} means taking cohomology with respect to d_r. That repeated operation is why the same grid can gradually remove classes that looked legitimate on earlier pages.
Bidegree Rule

The Differential Has A Slope

Cohomological Page
Visual Anchor
For a first-quadrant cohomological spectral sequence

\(d_r: E_r^{p,q} \longrightarrow E_r^{p+r,\ q-r+1}\)

d_0

Tests the internal layer differential.

d_1

Moves to adjacent filtration degree.

d_r, r > 1

Detects delayed interactions between layers.

Slope board inspired by the chapter notebook labprinted pages 154-265.
The bidegree rule is the first visual anchor: d_r: E_r^{p,q} -> E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}. For r equal to zero the arrow is vertical, for r equal to one it is horizontal, and later pages make longer diagonal jumps. Stress that the page number changes which bidegrees can communicate, which is exactly what later makes transgression visible.
Double Complexes

Double Complexes Supply Pages

Two Directions, One Total Complex

Two Useful Filtrations

  • Filter by columns to resolve vertical information first.
  • Filter by rows to resolve horizontal information first.
  • Compare the page story to the total cohomology.
Proof Habit
Do not forget the sign

The signed total differential is the algebraic safety check that lets page computations converge to one total object.

Double complex as page factoryChapter 3 source span: 154-265
Connect the filtered-complex abstraction to double complexes. A total differential combines vertical and horizontal pieces with signs chosen so D squared is zero. Filtering by columns or rows produces different early pages, but both are ways of interrogating the same total cohomology. This is the algebraic pattern behind Cech-de Rham and bundle examples.
Fibrations

Fiber Bundles Give A Geometric Page

Base Times Fiber Data
Serre-Style Lens
A first approximation

\(E_2^{p,q} \approx H^p(B; H^q(F)) \Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(E)\)

What The Page Separates

  • Horizontal direction: cohomology on the base.
  • Vertical direction: cohomology in the fiber.
  • Differentials: twisting and global obstruction data.
Fibration page modelprinted pages 154-265.
A fibration turns the page grid into geometry: one direction records the base and the other records the fiber. In favorable cases the E_2 page resembles H^p(B; H^q(F)), with local-system caveats suppressed for this overview. The important teaching point is that cohomology of the total space is assembled from base and fiber data plus differentials.
Transgression

Transgression Is A Long Jump

Fiber Class To Base Class
Mechanism
A delayed differential crosses the grid

A transgressive class survives the shorter differentials and then maps into base cohomology by a longer \(d_r\).

Why It Matters

Transgression turns information in the fiber direction into a cohomology class that obstructs survival in the base direction.

Path Fibration Clue

\(\Omega X \longrightarrow PX \longrightarrow X\), with \(PX\) contractible.

Transgression as a long page differentialChapter 3 overview
Introduce transgression as a class that survives the short differentials and then travels from the fiber direction into the base direction. The path fibration is perfect because the total path space is contractible. Positive-degree classes cannot survive forever, so a long differential must account for the disappearance and identify a base class with a fiber-originating class.
Path Fibration

Path Fibration For Spheres

Contractible Total Space
Sphere Cartoon
The decisive differential

\(d_n: E_n^{0,n-1} \longrightarrow E_n^{n,0}\)

Reason

\(P S^n\) is contractible, so positive-degree classes on the page cannot remain in the associated graded cohomology of the total space.

Interpretation

The fiber generator is transgressed to the base generator; a disappearing class becomes a structural computation.

Path-fibration transgressionprinted pages 154-265.
In the sphere case, the clean cartoon is d_n from a loop-space generator in bidegree (0,n-1) to the base generator in bidegree (n,0). The formula is not offered as the full loop-space computation. It isolates the mechanism: contractibility of the total space forces a nonzero transgression, and that differential becomes information about the base.
Gysin Sequence

Gysin Sequence As A Page Shadow

Euler Differential
Geometric Identification
The Euler class is a differential

\(d_2(\text{fiber class}) = e\)

Why A Long Exact Sequence Appears

Only two fiber rows remain for a circle fiber, so the page differential compresses into the familiar Gysin exact sequence.

Instructor Check

Ask which cohomology group receives multiplication by \(e\), then read the kernel and cokernel as survivor data.

Gysin sequence from a two-row pageSource PDF metadata included in deck
Now turn the same page logic into a long exact sequence. For an oriented circle bundle over S^2, the only fiber rows are q equal to zero and one, so the decisive differential is d_2. It is multiplication by the Euler class. Varying the Euler number changes the matrix, and the survivors in total cohomology change accordingly.
Euler Number Sweep

Euler Class Controls Survivors

One Differential, Three Outcomes

\(e = 0\)

\(d_2 = [0]\)

The differential vanishes, so product-like base and fiber classes survive.

\(e = 1\)

\(d_2 = [1]\)

The map is an isomorphism over \(Z\); the middle cohomology disappears.

\(e = 2\)

\(d_2 = [2]\)

The cokernel records \(Z/2\), so torsion appears in total cohomology.

Circle bundles over S^2 as an Euler-number labprinted pages 154-265.
Use the three-column sweep to make the Gysin statement concrete. When the Euler number is zero, the differential vanishes and product-like classes remain. When it is one, the map is an isomorphism over the integers and the middle cohomology disappears. When it is two, the cokernel records torsion. One differential explains three different total-space patterns.
Integer Coefficients

Integer Coefficients Reveal Torsion

Beyond Real Forms

Why Leave De Rham Coefficients?

Real differential forms compute real cohomology. Integral spectral sequences can also carry torsion and extension information.

Small Model
A two-cell boundary map can create torsion

\(C_2 \xrightarrow{\times 2} C_1\) gives a \(Z/2\)-type cokernel.

Connection To Pages

The same kernel-cokernel bookkeeping appears when page differentials are integer matrices.

Integral spectral sequence perspectiveChapter 3 source span: 154-265
The chapter does not stay only in de Rham territory because real differential forms cannot see all integral phenomena. A small cellular model, such as the RP^2 boundary map multiplied by two, reminds students that torsion is a cohomological feature, not a visual accident. Spectral sequences with integer coefficients can carry exactly this information through the page machine.
Cohomological Applications

Applications To Homotopy Theory

Spectral Sequences As Bridges

Input

Fibrations organize homotopy-theoretic spaces into page data.

Bridge

Transgression and Hurewicz convert homotopy questions into cohomological constraints.

Output

Towers and Eilenberg-MacLane spaces make obstruction layers computable.

Application map: path fibration to towersprinted pages 154-265.
Read this as a dependency graph rather than a theorem dump. Path fibrations provide transgressions; Hurewicz relates first nontrivial homotopy to homology; Eilenberg-MacLane spaces isolate a single homotopy group; Postnikov and Whitehead towers assemble or remove layers. Spectral sequences provide the cohomological bridge that lets those constructions produce computable restrictions.

Sullivan minimal models are introduced as a compression of rational homotopy information into a differential graded algebra. The odd sphere has one generator with zero differential. The even sphere needs a second generator whose differential kills a square. This mirrors the chapter's larger theme: the differential is not bookkeeping noise; it encodes the topology.
