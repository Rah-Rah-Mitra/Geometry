Open by framing characteristic classes as a routing problem for bundles. The chapter span is short, so the lecture is not a catalog of every construction. It gives students one mental model: local geometric choices produce global cohomology classes, and those classes become computable when we move between curvature, projectivization, roots, and universal examples.

Why characteristic classes exist

The Chapter's Organizing Question

How can a bundle that is locally trivial leave a global fingerprint in cohomology?

DetectFind a global obstruction: orientation, a section, a splitting, or a classifying map.
RepresentUse forms, mod 2 cocycles, tautological classes, or universal generators.
Pull BackNaturality turns universal data or flag-bundle data into classes on M.
ComputeConvert hard bundle geometry into algebra with roots and symmetric polynomials.
Use this slide to slow down the vocabulary. A vector bundle can look locally trivial everywhere, so the question is how to record the failure of one global trivialization. Characteristic classes are the answer, but each family chooses a coefficient system and a geometric test. Keep the four verbs visible: detect, represent, pull back, compute.

The table prevents a common confusion: Euler, Stiefel-Whitney, Chern, and Pontrjagin classes are not interchangeable labels. They live in different coefficient worlds and answer slightly different obstruction questions. Stiefel-Whitney classes are mod 2, Chern classes see complex structure, Pontrjagin classes turn real bundles through complexification, and Euler classes need orientation.

The de Rham compass

Chern-Weil Intuition

Invariant-polynomial recipe

\[ \nabla \leadsto F_\nabla,\qquad P(F_\nabla) \in \Omega^{2k}(M),\qquad [P(F_\nabla)] \in H^{2k}_{dR}(M) \]

  • \(F_\nabla\) is curvature, a matrix of 2-forms.
  • \(P\) is invariant under change of frame.
  • Different connections give cohomologous forms.
Chern-Weil is the intuition layer; the chapter's Chern construction below uses projectivization and splitting.Stage size: 1920x1080
Even though the chapter develops Chern classes through projectivization and splitting, the course is about differential forms, so students need the Chern-Weil picture in the background. A connection has curvature. Invariant polynomials in curvature are closed forms. Changing the connection changes the representative, not the cohomology class. That is the de Rham intuition.

Oriented real bundles

Euler Class: A Top Obstruction

Obstruction statement

If \(E \to M\) is oriented of real rank \(n\), then \(e(E)\) detects the obstruction to a nowhere-zero section.

Geometric modelGeneric section zeros are counted with signs when orientations allow it.
Differential form modelThe Pfaffian of curvature is a de Rham representative in the Chern-Weil setting.
Example to keep nearbyFor a closed oriented surface, the Euler class of the tangent bundle integrates to the Euler characteristic.
Present the Euler class as the most geometric member of the family. For an oriented real rank n bundle, a generic section vanishes in codimension n, and the signed zero locus represents the top obstruction. In de Rham language the Pfaffian curvature form is a representative. The point is not the formula but the obstruction story.

Real bundles over mod 2 coefficients

Stiefel-Whitney Classes: Mod 2 Obstructions

Total class

\[ w(E)=1+w_1(E)+w_2(E)+\cdots,\qquad w_i(E)\in H^i(M;\mathbb Z/2) \]

  • \(w_1=0\) is the orientability test.
  • \(w_2\) is the next lifting obstruction in many geometric problems.
  • Signs disappear, but real-bundle twisting remains visible.
Stiefel-Whitney classes are context for the characteristic-class landscape; they are not de Rham classes.Visible coefficient system: \(\mathbb Z/2\)
Stiefel-Whitney classes are included because they are part of the characteristic-class landscape, even though they do not fit directly into de Rham coefficients. Emphasize the first two tests: w1 detects orientability, while w2 measures the next lifting obstruction in many geometric settings. The mod 2 coefficient system removes signs and preserves real-bundle information.

The chapter's Chern construction

Chern Classes From Projectivization

Defining relation

\[ x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)=0 \]

Leray-Hirsch supplies the basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}\) over \(H^*(M)\).

Why projectivize?Lines in each fiber produce a tautological line bundle whose first Chern class is computable.
What is defined?The coefficients of the unique relation are the Chern classes of the original bundle.
What to remember?The construction is natural, so maps of bases pull the relation back correctly.
Now enter the chapter's main construction. Instead of starting with curvature forms, the text uses the projectivized bundle and its tautological line. The class x equals the first Chern class of the dual tautological line. Leray-Hirsch says powers of x form a basis over the base, so the one relation defines the Chern classes.

Proof transport, not literal splitting

Splitting Principle As Proof Transport

Safe fiction

\[ \pi^*E \cong L_1\oplus\cdots\oplus L_n,\qquad c(E)\ \text{acts like}\ \prod_i (1+x_i) \]

  • Move upstairs to a flag bundle.
  • Prove a symmetric polynomial identity in roots.
  • Use injectivity to descend the identity to \(M\).
Notebook check: the rank 4 flag-basis count equals \(4!\).Use roots only after the pullback-and-injectivity story is on the board.
The splitting principle is the slide where students often need reassurance. It does not claim that the original bundle splits on the original base. It says we may pull back to a flag bundle where the pullback splits, and because cohomology pulls back injectively, any polynomial identity verified upstairs descends downstairs.

Real bundles through complexification

Pontrjagin Classes From Complexification

Definition by even Chern classes

\[ p_i(V)=(-1)^i c_{2i}(V_{\mathbb C}) \]

The notebook check records \(c_1=c_3=0\) for paired roots and \(p_1=u^2+v^2\), \(p_2=u^2v^2\) under the usual convention.

InputA real vector bundle \(V\).
MoveComplexify \(V\) and use Chern classes.
OutputReal-bundle classes in degrees divisible by four.
Pontrjagin classes are the real-bundle family that becomes visible after complexification. The paired roots u and negative u, v and negative v explain why odd Chern classes vanish in the de Rham model. The even Chern classes remain, with the usual sign convention, and those are repackaged as p1, p2, and so on.

This slide is a comparison map, not a proof of every relation. The important teaching move is to ask what structure has been forgotten or added. A complex bundle has an underlying real bundle. An oriented real plane bundle can be treated like a complex line after choosing compatible structure. Reducing integral information mod 2 changes what can be detected.

These examples also prepare the classifying-space slide that follows.

Naturality through classifying spaces

Universal Bundle Lens

Naturality formula

\[ E \cong f^*(\gamma_G),\qquad c(E)=f^*c(\gamma_G) \]

  • Universal classes live once on \(BG\).
  • Every bundle with structure group \(G\) pulls them back.
  • Projective spaces are the first classifying examples for line bundles.
The classifying-space lens explains why characteristic classes are natural. A bundle is pulled back from a universal bundle over BG, and its classes are pulled back from universal cohomology classes. This is the cleanest way to understand why formulas verified for universal examples control all bundles of the same structure group.

What to do before computing

Chapter Checklist

1. Name Structure

Real, oriented real, complex, or complexified real? The structure group decides the class family.

2. Choose Coefficients

De Rham and integral classes remember different data; mod 2 classes deliberately forget signs.

3. Pick A Model

Use curvature for differential-form intuition, projectivization for Chern definitions, or universal bundles for naturality.

4. Justify Roots

Move to a splitting space, prove symmetric identities upstairs, then descend by injectivity.

5. Test Examples

Line bundles, trivial bundles, tangent bundles, and projective spaces reveal sign conventions quickly.

Takeaway

Characteristic classes turn local bundle geometry into stable cohomological data that can be compared, pulled back, and computed.
