The Intermediate Jacobian Fibration

01
1. Fibration of Tori
Intermediate Jacobian Definition

Let $H_{\mathbb{Z}}$ be a polarizable variation of Hodge structure of odd weight $2m-1$ over a complex manifold $S$. For each point $s \in S$, the **intermediate Jacobian** is defined as the complex torus:

\(J^m(H_{\mathbb{Z},s}) = H_{\mathbb{C},s} \,/\, \big(F^m H_{\mathbb{C},s} + H_{\mathbb{Z},s}\big)\)

For a smooth projective family $X \to S$, this corresponds to the intermediate Jacobian $J^m(X_s)$ of the fibers, where $H_{\mathbb{C},s} = H^{2m-1}(X_s, \mathbb{C})$.

The Fibration Structure

These complex tori patch together analytically to form a holomorphic fiber bundle:

\(\mathcal{J}^m \longrightarrow S\)

The bundle of Jacobians is a quotient of the vector bundle $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}} / \mathcal{F}^m \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}$ by the local system of period lattices $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}$:

\(\mathcal{J}^m \cong \big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}} / \mathcal{F}^m \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}\big) \;\Big/\; \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\)

Its stalks are complex Lie groups of complex dimension $\sum_{p < m} h^{2m-1-p, p}$.

We begin by constructing the intermediate Jacobian fibration. Given a polarizable variation of Hodge structure of odd weight $2m-1$, the intermediate Jacobian at each point is a complex torus quotient of the complex Hodge filtration. These tori vary analytically across the base manifold, forming a holomorphic fiber bundle that serves as the natural home for Abel-Jacobi cycle images.

Normal Functions and Griffiths Transversality

02
2. Holomorphic Sections
Definition of a Normal Function

A **normal function** $\nu$ is a holomorphic section of the intermediate Jacobian bundle $\mathcal{J}^m \to S$ that satisfies **Griffiths Transversality**.

Let $\tilde{\nu}$ be a local holomorphic lift of $\nu$ to the vector bundle $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}} / \mathcal{F}^m \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}$. Griffiths Transversality requires the Gauss-Manin connection derivative $\nabla \tilde{\nu}$ to lie in the horizontal subbundle:

\(\nabla \tilde{\nu} \;\in\; \big(\mathcal{F}^{m-1} \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}} \,/\, \mathcal{F}^m \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}\big) \otimes \Omega_S^1\)
Interpretation

This means that while the section $\nu(s)$ moves dynamically, its infinitesimal first-order variations are constrained to the adjacent Hodge filtration stage $\mathcal{F}^{m-1}$. It behaves like a “horizontal” section under the flat Gauss-Manin connection.

Common Traps
  • **Trap 1: Confusion with Scalar Functions**
    Assuming a normal function is a scalar. It is a geometric section of tori, not a complex-valued scalar.
  • **Trap 2: Period Quotient Neglect**
    Forgetting that $\nu(s)$ lives modulo the period lattice $H_{\mathbb{Z}, s}$ in each fiber.
  • **Trap 3: Trivial Derivative Assumptions**
    Assuming transversality forces flatness. The derivative $\nabla\tilde{\nu}$ is generally non-zero modulo $\mathcal{F}^m$.
A normal function is a holomorphic section of the intermediate Jacobian bundle that satisfies Griffiths Transversality. This horizontality constraint means that the covariant Gauss-Manin derivative of any local holomorphic lift must lie within the adjacent step of the Hodge filtration. Normal functions allow us to represent relative homologically trivial cycles algebraically and analytically.

The Abel-Jacobi Map of Cycle Families

03
3. Constructing Sections from Cycles
Cycle Families and Homological Triviality

Let $f: X \to S$ be a smooth proper family of projective varieties. Let $Z \subset X$ be a relative algebraic cycle of codimension $m$.

Assume that on every fiber $X_s$, the cycle $Z_s$ is homologically trivial ($Z_s \equiv 0$ in $H_{2n-2m}(X_s, \mathbb{Z})$). We choose a bounding chain $\Gamma_s$ on $X_s$ such that:

\(\partial \Gamma_s = Z_s\)
The Abel-Jacobi Integration

We evaluate this chain against holomorphic forms in $F^m H^{2m-1}(X_s, \mathbb{C})^*$. The resulting integration yields the Abel-Jacobi image:

\(AJ_{X_s}(Z_s)(\omega) = \int_{\Gamma_s} \omega\)

As $\omega$ varies in $F^m$, this integration is well-defined modulo integration over closed cycles (periods). The resulting assignment defines a holomorphic normal function section:

\(\nu_Z(s) = AJ_{X_s}(Z_s) \;\in\; J^m(X_s)\)
The Abel-Jacobi map links family algebraic cycles to normal functions. For a relative codimension-$m$ cycle that is homologically trivial on every fiber, we integrate holomorphic forms over a bounding chain. The resulting Abel-Jacobi values form a normal function section, whose analytic transitions capture both local period variations and global topological monodromy.

The Infinitesimal Invariant \(\delta\nu\)

04
4. The Derivative Obstruction
Defining the Obstruction Class

A normal function $\nu$ has a local lift $\tilde{\nu}$ in $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathcal{F}^m\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}$. Under the Gauss-Manin connection $\nabla$, the derivative is well-defined modulo periods (which are flat, so $\nabla \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}} = 0$).

This covariant derivative projects to a global coordinate-free **infinitesimal invariant** $\delta\nu$:

\(\delta\nu(s) \;\in\; \mathcal{H}_s^{m-1, m} \otimes \Omega_{S,s}^1 \;\Big/\; \bar{\nabla}\big(\mathcal{H}_s^{m, m-1}\big)\)

where $\bar{\nabla}$ is the Gauss-Manin map $\mathcal{H}^{m, m-1} \to \mathcal{H}^{m-1, m} \otimes \Omega_S^1$.

Interpretation and Vanishing

The infinitesimal invariant $\delta\nu(s)$ acts as the local derivative of the normal function modulo the image of flat deformations.

**Key Invariants:**

  • If $\delta\nu = 0$, the section $\nu$ is locally flat (constant under GM).
  • If $\delta\nu \neq 0$, the normal function carries a genuine algebraic cycle deformation that cannot be locally flattened.
  • The non-vanishing of $\delta\nu$ acts as the infinitesimal obstruction to deforming cycles as algebraic classes.
The infinitesimal invariant is the derivative-level obstruction of a normal function. By differentiating a local lift under the Gauss-Manin connection and projecting to the primitive Hodge quotient modulo the period lattice and the image of the connection map, we obtain a well-defined global class. This invariant measures where a cycle fails to deform as a flat algebraic section.

High-Degree Hypersurfaces & Griffiths' Theorem

06
6. Symmetriser Obstruction
The Symmetriser and Jacobian Ring

Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ be a general hypersurface of high degree $d$. For high degrees, the existence of non-trivial normal functions is bounded by the algebraic properties of the Jacobian ring:

\(R = \mathbb{C}[x_0, \ldots, x_{n+1}] \,/\, J_F\)

The **symmetriser obstruction** evaluates whether the multiplication map of $R$ allows non-trivial kernel forms. For sufficiently large degree $d$, algebraic symmetries force this multiplication map to be injective on the relevant pieces.

Vanishing of the Abel-Jacobi Map

Griffiths' celebrated theorem asserts that if the degree $d$ of the hypersurface family is high enough:

\(d \;\ge\; 2 + \frac{3}{n}\)

then the infinitesimal invariant $\delta\nu$ of any normal function must vanish. This means that all relative algebraic cycles are homologically trivial and carry zero Abel-Jacobi image, showing that no genuine algebraic cycles deform non-trivially.

For algebraic cycles on high-degree hypersurfaces, Griffiths and Voisin proved that the infinitesimal invariant acts as a powerful vanishing tool. The symmetriser obstruction of the Jacobian ring shows that when the degree is large, the infinitesimal invariant must vanish. This severely restricts the Abel-Jacobi map, proving that all algebraic cycles are homologically trivial in high degrees.

Summary: The Normal Function Ledger

08
8. Recap
The Reusable Reading Rule

Always maintain the three-way accounting ledger for Volume II variations of Hodge structure:

  • **Assumptions**: Verify if the variety family is smooth and projective over a complex base. (Deligne's degeneration breaks in general topology!).
  • **Maps**: Track the Abel-Jacobi map $AJ_s$, the Gauss-Manin covariant derivative $\nabla$, and the boundary projection $\delta$.
  • **Surviving Invariants**: Measure the normal function section $\nu(s)$ and its derivative obstruction $\delta\nu$ modulo periods.

Use this ledger to organize proofs and prevent homological calculation traps.

Key Takeaways
  • **Normal Functions**: Moving cycle families map to holomorphic sections of the intermediate Jacobian bundle under Abel-Jacobi.
  • **Griffiths Transversality**: Constrains the first covariant derivative of the lift to the adjacent filtration stage $\mathcal{F}^{m-1}$.
  • **Infinitesimal Invariant**: The derivative class $\delta\nu$ acts as the local obstruction to flat/constant sections, vanishing in high degrees.
Conclusion

By representing cycles as normal functions and evaluating their infinitesimal invariants, algebraic geometry gains a powerful, localized differential method to study algebraic cycle existence and deformation in complex manifolds.

In summary, Chapter 7 establishes normal functions as moving cycle representations and the infinitesimal invariant as their differential obstruction. When studying algebraic cycles in families, always maintain our three-way ledger: verify whether projectivity holds, map the Gauss-Manin derivatives and Abel-Jacobi integrals, and track the surviving invariants that protect algebraic structures against monodromy deformation.
