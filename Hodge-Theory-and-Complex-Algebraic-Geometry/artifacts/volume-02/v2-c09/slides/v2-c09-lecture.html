Rational Equivalence Refines Homology

01
Cycles and Equivalence

Algebraic cycles are formal linear combinations of irreducible subvarieties: \(Z = \sum n_i [V_i]\). We mod out cycles by divisors of rational functions on subvarieties:

\(Z \sim_{\text{rat}} 0 \iff Z = \text{div}(f)\)

for a rational function \(f\) on a codimension \(p-1\) subvariety of \(X\).

Common Pitfall

Do not confuse rational equivalence with ordinary homological equivalence. Rational equivalence is much stronger and highly algebraic, as it tracks deformations parameterized by rational curves \(\mathbb{P}^1\), whereas homological equivalence allows arbitrary topological deforming.

Rational equivalence is the algebraic core of cycle theory. It equates two cycles if they can be parameterized as the zero and polar divisor of a rational function. This provides a much finer and strictly algebraic coordinate system than ordinary topological homology.

Functoriality: Proper Pushforward and Flat Pullback

02
Proper Pushforward

A proper map \(f: X \to Y\) pushes cycles forward, preserving the cycle degree but dropping codimension if the dimension drops:

\(f_* : CH^k(X) \longrightarrow CH^{k+d_Y-d_X}(Y)\)

Flat Pullback

A flat map \(f: X \to Y\) pulls cycles back, preserving the codimension cleanly:

\(f^* : CH^k(Y) \longrightarrow CH^k(X)\)

Flatness guarantees that fibers have constant dimensions, preserving cycle structures.

Chow groups are functorial. Proper maps push cycles forward by taking their geometric image, while flat maps pull them back cleanly by preserving their codimension. This flat pullback is what allows us to define intersection theory later without losing codimension structures.

Localisation and Excision Sequences

03
Excision Sequence

For a closed subvariety \(Y \subset X\) with open complement \(U = X \setminus Y\), we have a fundamental exact localisation sequence of Chow groups:

\(CH^{k-c}(Y) \xrightarrow{i_*} CH^k(X) \xrightarrow{j^*} CH^k(U) \longrightarrow 0\)

where \(c = \text{codim}_X(Y)\), showing how cycles on the complement lift.

Algebraic analogue

This localisation sequence is the algebraic analogue of the excision exact sequence in topology. It describes how cycles restrict to open patches and how global obstructions are localized along the boundary subvariety.

Localisation is a key calculational tool. It shows that any algebraic cycle on the open complement U can be lifted to X, with the kernel of this restriction map consisting exactly of cycles supported along the boundary subvariety Y. This provides a direct analogue to topological excision.

Algebraic Correspondences Act as Operators

04
Correspondences

An algebraic correspondence is a cycle \(\Sigma \subset X \times Y\). It acts as a cycle-theoretic operator between Chow groups via push-pull projections:

\([\Sigma]_*(\alpha) = (p_2)_* (p_1^* \alpha \cdot \Sigma)\)

This generalizes maps of varieties to multi-valued cycle propagation.

Algebraic correspondences act as operators between Chow groups. By embedding an incidence variety Sigma inside the product space X times Y, we can pull back a cycle from X and push it forward to Y. This generalizes geometric maps of varieties to multi-valued cycle operators.

The Intersection Product Requires Smoothness

05
Intersection Ring

Under a smooth projective variety \(X\), Chow groups carry a commutative ring structure under the intersection product:

\(CH^p(X) \times CH^q(X) \longrightarrow CH^{p+q}(X)\)

This product mimics the cup product in cohomology. The algebraic Moving Lemma allows us to replace any two cycles with transverse representatives before intersecting.

Common Trap

Do not use this pullback or intersection structure without smoothness assumptions. On singular varieties, intersecting cycles is obstructed, and Chow groups must be treated as modules over the intersection ring instead.

When X is smooth, Chow groups carry a commutative ring structure. The intersection product acts as the algebraic analogue of the topological cup product. Using the Moving Lemma, we can deform any two cycles to intersect transversely, enabling a well-defined intersection product.

The Cycle-Class Map Connects to Topology

06
Class Homomorphism

The cycle-class map sends rationally equivalent cycles to the same topological cohomology class:

\(\text{cl} : CH^p(X) \longrightarrow H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{Z})\)

This mapping is compatible with intersections and cup products, sending the intersection ring directly into the cohomology ring:

\(\text{cl}(\alpha \cdot \beta) = \text{cl}(\alpha) \cup \text{cl}(\beta)\)

Hodge type

Because algebraic cycles are defined by algebraic equations, their cycle classes always occupy the diagonal Hodge component \(H^{p,p}(X)\), anchoring cycle theory in Hodge structures.

The cycle-class map is our direct hook into ordinary topology. It maps Chow classes onto integral cohomology classes. Because algebraic cycles are defined by polynomials, their topological classes always occupy the diagonal Hodge components, making cycle classes central targets for Hodge conjectures.
