Review of Volume I Foundations

01
The Compact Kahler Engine
  • Elliptic harmonic theory guarantees unique representatives: \(H^k(X, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathcal{H}^k(X)\).
  • Kahler identities align d, partial, and dbar Laplacians.
  • Type splitting splits cohomology: \(H^k(X, \mathbb{C}) = \bigoplus_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(X)\).
  • Hard Lefschetz pairs degrees symmetrically around middle dimension.
Why Projectivity Matters Now

While Volume I worked in the general compact Kahler setting, Volume II heavily relies on algebraic projective varieties. This is necessary because algebraic cycles require an integral polarization and projective embedding to be defined and to guarantee that ample classes are integral.

We must review our Volume I foundations before moving forward. Volume I proved that compact Kahler manifolds carry pure Hodge structures, where cohomology splits cleanly by type. In Volume II, we shift to projective varieties because algebraic cycles and their polarization lattices require integral ample classes.

Mixed Hodge Structures Resolve Non-Kahler Spaces

02
Mixed Hodge Data

Deligne showed that the cohomology of any complex algebraic variety (possibly singular or non-compact) carries a mixed Hodge structure.

This packages two filtrations:

  • Weight filtration \(W_\bullet\) (increasing, rational).
  • Hodge filtration \(F^\bullet\) (decreasing, complex).
Mixed Hodge structures allow us to study varieties that are singular or non-compact. Deligne showed that their cohomology carries both an increasing weight filtration, which measures singular or boundary behaviors, and a decreasing Hodge filtration, which behaves like classical Kahler Hodge structures on the graded pieces.

Variations of Hodge Structure

03
Dynamic Rigidity
  • Smooth proper family \(\pi: \mathcal{X} \to B\) carries moving complex structure.
  • Gauss-Manin connection \(\nabla\) measures filtration movement.
  • Griffiths Transversality enforces the horizontal condition: \(\nabla(F^p) \subseteq F^{p-1} \otimes \Omega^1_B\).
  • This first-order constraint makes period maps highly rigid.
When a variety deforms in a family, its Hodge structure varies. Differentiating this variation using the Gauss-Manin connection reveals a beautiful rigidity: Griffiths transversality.Differentiating the p-th filtration level can only move classes down to the p-minus-one level. This limits the period map's directions.

The Hodge Locus Tracks Extra Symmetry

04
Monodromy and Symmetry

As we move along loops in the parameter space \(B\), flat classes undergo monodromy action: \(\rho: \pi_1(B) \to \text{Aut}(H_\mathbb{Z})\).

Hodge Locus represents points where extra Hodge classes (diagonal classes) appear.

\(HL(B) = \{s \in B \mid H^{p,p}(X_s) \cap H_\mathbb{Z} \ne 0\}\)

Algebraicity

The Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan theorem proves that the Hodge locus is always a union of algebraic subvarieties of \(B\), despite being defined analytically via period integrals. This underscores the deep algebraic nature of Hodge theory.

Monodromy groups record how loops in the base space act on flat classes. The Hodge locus is the set of parameters where new diagonal classes emerge. A massive result by Cattani, Deligne, and Kaplan proves that this analytically defined locus is always algebraic, demonstrating how variations of Hodge structures force algebraicity.

Cycle Classes and Abel-Jacobi Invariants

05
The Cohomology Shadow

An algebraic cycle \(Z\) of codimension \(p\) has a cycle class \([Z] \in H^{p,p}(X) \cap H^{2p}(X, \mathbb{Z})\). The Hodge conjecture asks if all such classes arise from cycles.

The Period shadow

If \([Z] = 0\) homologically, \(Z\) is homologically trivial. We integrate over a bounding chain \(\Gamma\) such that \(\partial \Gamma = Z\). This period integral defines a class in the Intermediate Jacobian torus.

\(AJ(Z)(\omega) = \int_\Gamma \omega \pmod{\text{periods}}\)

Algebraic cycles leave two types of footprints. First, they define a class in cohomology, which must be rational and of pure type (p,p). Second, if this primary class vanishes, they still carry period integrals over a bounding chain. The Abel-Jacobi map packages this secondary period data into intermediate Jacobian tori.

Hyperplanes and Morphisms

06
Lefschetz Themes

Cutting a projective variety with hyperplane sections isolates primitive topology. Restriction acts as an isomorphism below the middle dimension, concentrating new cycles in the middle degree.

Leray Themes

Morphisms \(f: X \to Y\) are organized through Leray filtrations on cohomology. For smooth projective maps, the Leray spectral sequence degenerates at the first page, meaning there are no higher differential obstructions.

Lefschetz and Leray themes act as topological coordinates. Lefschetz hyperplane sections concentrate primitive topology in the middle dimension of the section, restricting topological freedom. Meanwhile, Leray spectral sequences organize how fiber structures contribute to cohomology, showing early degeneration for smooth algebraic maps.
