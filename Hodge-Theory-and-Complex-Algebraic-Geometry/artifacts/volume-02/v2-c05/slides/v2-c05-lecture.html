Welcome to the lecture on Griffiths Transversality and its applications. In this session, we will explore how Griffiths Transversality imposes a fundamental horizontal structure on variations of Hodge structure. We will see how this horizontal constraint can be translated into the algebra of complexes associated to infinitesimal variations, how it shapes the holomorphic Leray spectral sequence, and how we can use these tools to compute the local tangent spaces and equations defining Hodge loci. Let's begin by introducing the de Rham complex of a flat vector bundle.

The de Rham Complex of a Flat Bundle

01
1. Flat Connections & Hodge Filtrations
Flat Connections & de Rham

Let \((H, \nabla)\) be a holomorphic flat vector bundle on a complex manifold \(X\). The connection squares to zero, \(\nabla^2 = 0\). This allows us to form the **holomorphic de Rham complex** with coefficients in \(H\):

\(\mathcal{H} \xrightarrow{\nabla} \mathcal{H} \otimes \Omega_X^1 \xrightarrow{\nabla} \mathcal{H} \otimes \Omega_X^2 \xrightarrow{\nabla} \dots\)

where \(\mathcal{H}\) is the sheaf of holomorphic sections of \(H\). This complex computes the holomorphic de Rham cohomology.

The Hodge Filtration Drop

A Hodge filtration is a decreasing filtration by holomorphic sub-bundles:

\(\mathcal{H} = F^0 \mathcal{H} \supset F^1 \mathcal{H} \supset \dots \supset F^k \mathcal{H} \supset 0\)

**Griffiths Transversality** is the key first-order constraint:

\(\nabla(F^p \mathcal{H}) \subset F^{p-1} \mathcal{H} \otimes \Omega_X^1\)

This ensures that the flat connection drops the filtration degree by **at most one**. A two-step drop (mapping to \(F^{p-2}\)) is strictly forbidden!

Let's begin with a holomorphic flat vector bundle $(H, \nabla)$ over a complex manifold $X$. Since the connection squares to zero, we can form the holomorphic de Rham complex. When we introduce a Hodge filtration $F^p$ on the bundle, Griffiths Transversality specifies that the flat connection $\nabla$ maps $F^p$ into $F^{p-1} \otimes \Omega_X^1$. This is a crucial one-step drop condition. Any two-step drop, where a form in $F^p$ maps into $F^{p-2}$, is strictly forbidden by the horizontal structure of the variation.

Griffiths Transversality & Horizontal Distribution

02
2. Transversality on the Period Domain
Horizontal Tangent Distribution
Horizontal Distribution

The **period domain** \(D\) represents flags \(F^\bullet\) satisfying the Riemann bilinear relations. The compact dual flag manifold \(\check{D}\) has tangent space:

\(T_F \check{D} \cong \bigoplus_p \text{Hom}(F^p, V / F^p)\)

The **horizontal distribution** \(T_D^h\) is the sub-bundle defined by:

\(T_F^h D \cong \bigoplus_p \text{Hom}(F^p, F^{p-1} / F^p)\)

Any Variation of Hodge Structure \(\phi: Y \to D\) is **horizontal**: its derivative must map into this distribution.

Geometric Invariant

The horizontal distribution is generally **non-integrable**. This means horizontal curves cannot fill out the whole flag manifold \(D\), restricting the locus of variations to narrow submanifolds.

Here, we visualize Griffiths Transversality geometrically. The period domain $D$ is a flag manifold representing Hodge filtrations. The tangent space $T_F D$ represents all possible infinitesimal deformations. However, the horizontal distribution $T_F^h D$ is a proper, non-integrable sub-bundle that restricts the allowed directions. A vector $v$ is horizontal if it lies inside $T_F^h D$, which means it satisfies the one-step drop condition. In contrast, a vector $w$ pointing outside this distribution represents a forbidden, non-horizontal direction that violates transversality.

Infinitesimal Variations of Hodge Structure

03
3. Localizing the Variation
Definition of an IVHS

An **Infinitesimal Variation of Hodge Structure (IVHS)** of weight \(k\) is a tuple:

\((V, H_\mathbb{Z}, F^\bullet, Q, T, \delta)\)

where:

  • \(H^{p,q}\) are the Hodge spaces of \(V = H_\mathbb{C}\).
  • \(Q\) is the polarization quadratic form.
  • \(T\) is a complex vector space (tangent space of base).
  • \(\delta : T \to \bigoplus_p \text{Hom}(H^{p,q}, H^{p-1, q+1})\) is the derivative.
Integrability & Commutativity

For any two tangent vectors \(u, v \in T\), the derivative map satisfies the **integrability condition**:

\(\delta(u) \wedge \delta(v) = 0 \quad \text{in } \text{Hom}(H^{p,q}, H^{p-2, q+2})\)

This is the infinitesimal counterpart of flat connection flatness (\(\nabla^2=0\)).

Derivative Block Matrix
Only first sub-diagonal blocks non-zero
An Infinitesimal Variation of Hodge Structure, or IVHS, localizes the geometry of the variation to a single tangent space. It is defined by the Hodge decomposition, a tangent space $T$, and a derivative map $\delta$ which acts as a block-diagonal-lowering matrix. Due to the Griffiths transversality condition, all upper-triangular blocks and blocks lower than one step are identically zero. Furthermore, the integrability condition requires that for any two tangent vectors, their derivatives commute or cup to zero.

Complexes Associated to an IVHS

04
4. The Griffiths Complex
The Griffiths Complex

Using the integrability \(\delta \wedge \delta = 0\), we can build an **infinitesimal de Rham complex** (often called the **Griffiths complex**):

\(0 \to H^{p,q} \xrightarrow{\delta} H^{p-1, q+1} \otimes T^* \xrightarrow{\delta} H^{p-2, q+2} \otimes \Lambda^2 T^* \xrightarrow{\delta} \dots\)

This complex replaces the infinite-dimensional Gauss-Manin de Rham complex with a finite-dimensional algebraic complex at a point.

Homological Invariants

The cohomology of this complex at the first step, denoted by:

\(\mathbb{H}^1(Gr^\bullet H, \delta)\)

controls the **infinitesimal invariants** of the variation. This determines the tangent space to the Hodge locus and local obstructions to extending Hodge classes.

It translates complex geometry directly into local linear algebra checks.

Using the integrability of the derivative map $\delta$, where the wedge product $\delta \wedge \delta$ is zero, we can construct the infinitesimal de Rham complex or Griffiths complex. This complex replaces the global Gauss-Manin connection with local algebraic maps, allowing us to compute variations of Hodge structure and their cohomology using linear algebra. The first cohomology group of this complex controls the local obstructions and is directly related to the infinitesimal invariants and normal functions of the family.

The Holomorphic Leray Spectral Sequence

05
5. Holomorphic Direct Images
Relative Holomorphic Forms

For a smooth projective family \(f: X \to Y\), we filter the relative holomorphic de Rham complex \(\Omega_{X/Y}^\bullet\) by Hodge filtrations. The direct images yield the holomorphic Leray sequence:

\(E_1^{p,q} = R^q f_* \Omega_{X/Y}^p \Longrightarrow R^{p+q} f_* \Omega_{X/Y}^\bullet\)

In contrast to the topological Leray sequence which uses local systems, the holomorphic version works directly with coherent sheaves.

E1 Differentials & Connections

The differential on the \(E_1\) page:

\(d_1 : R^q f_* \Omega_{X/Y}^p \longrightarrow R^q f_* \Omega_{X/Y}^{p+1}\)

coincides exactly with the derivative \(\delta\) of the variation of Hodge structure.

Deligne's celebrated degeneration theorem applies here: this spectral sequence degenerates at the **\(E_1\) page** in the projective case, meaning all \(d_r = 0\) for \(r \ge 1\).

Next, we study the holomorphic Leray spectral sequence for a smooth projective family. By filtering the relative holomorphic de Rham complex, we obtain a spectral sequence whose E1 terms are the direct images of holomorphic form sheaves. In the projective case, the differentials on the E1 page are exactly the Gauss-Manin connections or the derivatives $\delta$. Deligne's theorems imply that this spectral sequence degenerates at the E1 page, which mirrors the topological E2 page degeneration.

Local Study of Hodge Loci & Tangent Spaces

06
6. Geometry of Hodge Loci
Defining the Hodge Locus

Let \(\lambda \in H^k(X_y, \mathbb{Z})\) be a topological cycle. The **Hodge Locus** \(S \subset Y\) is the analytic subvariety where \(\lambda\) remains of type \((p,p)\):

\(S = \{ s \in Y \mid \lambda_s \in H^{p,p}(X_s) \}\)

Infinitesimally, the tangent space \(T_{S, y}\) inside \(T_{Y, y}\) is cut out by the derivative of the variation of Hodge structure:

\(T_{S, y} = \{ v \in T_{Y, y} \mid \delta(v)(\lambda) = 0 \}\)

Thus, the tangent space to the Hodge locus is precisely the kernel of the map \(\sigma_\lambda(v) = \delta(v)(\lambda)\).

Tangent Space Cutout
Tangent vector v preserves cycle type
The Hodge locus is the subset of the parameter space where a given topological cycle $\lambda$ remains of Hodge type $(p,p)$. Infinitesimally, we can determine the tangent space to the Hodge locus using the derivative of the variation. The tangent space is defined as the kernel of the map $\sigma_\lambda$, which means it is cut out by the linear equations where the derivative of the cycle under a tangent vector vanishes. This makes the local study of Hodge loci highly calculable.

Lecture Summary & Course Ledger

08
8. Core Takeaways
Key Theorems Recap

**Griffiths Transversality** is the horizontal bedrock of variations of Hodge structure:

  • **One-Step Drop**: Flat connection \(\nabla\) maps \(F^p\) into \(F^{p-1}\).
  • **Horizontal Distribution**: Tangent map factors through \(T_D^h \subset T_D\).
  • **Infinitesimal Complex**: Wedges of the derivative \(\delta\) vanish, creating the Griffiths complex.
  • **Hodge Loci Tangent**: Cut out by linear vanishing equations \(v \mapsto \delta(v)(\lambda) = 0\).
The Three-Way Reading Ledger

Active Bookkeeping Ledger:

  • **Assumptions**: Smoothness, properness, and projectivity are active (without projectivity, Leray direct image and Hodge locus theorems break).
  • **Maps**: Track the connection \(\nabla\), the local derivative \(\delta\), and the cup-product pairing with direct images.
  • **Surviving Invariants**: The Hodge filtration \(F^p\), the horizontal tangent sub-bundle \(T_D^h\), and the kernel equations cutting out the Hodge locus.
In summary, Griffiths Transversality is the fundamental first-order differential constraint governing variations of Hodge structure. We have studied how it translates geometrically into a horizontal distribution on the period domain, and how it can be analyzed using local complexes, the holomorphic Leray spectral sequence, and tangent equations for Hodge loci.
