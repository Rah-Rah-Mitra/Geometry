Logarithmic Forms and Pole Orders

01
The Logarithmic Complex

For a smooth hypersurface \(X \subset \mathbb{P}^n\), we study differential forms on the open complement \(U = \mathbb{P}^n \setminus X\) having poles of bounded order along \(X\).

\(P_k \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^n}^p(\log X) \subset \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^n}^p(*X)\)

The filtration level \(P_k\) restricts the pole order along the boundary of the complement to at most \(k\).

Common Pitfall

Do not confuse the pole order of a logarithmic differential form with its ordinary cohomological degree. The pole order tracks algebraic singularities on the complement, whereas the degree is topological.

We begin with logarithmic differential forms on the open complement of X in projective space. The pole-order filtration limits the singularity degree along X. Differentiating these forms preserves the pole order, creating an algebraic filtration that matches topological de Rham classes.

Poincaré Residues Map Complement to Hypersurface

02
Poincaré Residue

The Poincaré residue map links logarithmic forms on the complement \(U\) directly to ordinary forms on the hypersurface \(X\).

\(\text{Res} : P_k \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^n}^p(\log X) \longrightarrow \Omega_X^{p-1}\)

Under this projection, forms with pole order at most \(k\) correspond exactly to the Hodge filtration levels on primitive cohomology:

\(\text{Res}(P_k) = F^{n-k} H^n(X)_{\text{prim}}\)

The Poincaré residue is our geometric bridge. It maps a logarithmic form with pole order k on the complement into a smooth form on the hypersurface. In cohomology, this maps the algebraic filtration steps precisely onto the Hodge filtration steps of the primitive cohomology of X.

The Graded Jacobian Algebra of a Hypersurface

03
Jacobian Ideal

For a hypersurface defined by a homogeneous polynomial \(F(x_0, \dots, x_{n+1}) = 0\), the Jacobian ideal is generated by its partial derivatives:

\(J(F) = \left( \frac{\partial F}{\partial x_0}, \dots, \frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{n+1}} \right)\)

Graded Jacobian Ring

The Jacobian ring is the graded polynomial quotient algebra:

\(R = \mathbb{C}[x_0, \dots, x_{n+1}] / J(F)\)

The graded pieces \(R_d\) correspond exactly to the primitive Hodge pieces under residue isomorphism:

\(R_{(k+1)d - n - 2} \cong H^{n-k, k}(X)_{\text{prim}}\)

To compute these Hodge spaces, we use the Jacobian ring. It is the polynomial ring modulo the partial derivatives of F. The graded pieces of this ring correspond exactly to the pure Hodge pieces of X. This isomorphism makes abstract Hodge spaces computable using homogeneous monomials.

Graded Ring Multiplication Models the Infinitesimal Map

04
Infinitesimal Period Map

The derivative of the period map (the Infinitesimal Variation of Hodge Structure) is modeled exactly by graded polynomial multiplication in the Jacobian ring:

\(R_d \times R_{(k+1)d - n - 2} \longrightarrow R_{(k+2)d - n - 2}\)

This multiplication maps the deformation class of the hypersurface to its cohomological shift.

The most beautiful part is that the derivative of the period map is modeled exactly by monomial multiplication in the Jacobian ring. Multiplying a deformation class in R-d with a Hodge class in the graded piece maps it directly into the next Hodge piece, turning algebraic topology into polynomial multiplication.

Macaulay Nondegeneracy and the Symmetriser Lemma

05
Macaulay's Theorem

Macaulay proved that for smooth hypersurfaces, the graded multiplication pairing is completely nondegenerate:

\(R_a \times R_b \longrightarrow R_{a+b} \cong \mathbb{C}\)

where the top dimension occurs at degree \(a+b = (d-2)(n+2)\). This maps to a unique one-dimensional socle, acting as the algebraic Poincaré duality.

Symmetriser Lemma

Griffiths' symmetriser lemma proves that the bilinear cup product pairing and the Jacobian ring pairing are compatible. This ensures that the algebraic multiplication determines the geometric intersection pairing.

Macaulay's theorem provides a crucial algebraic foundation: the graded polynomial pairing is completely nondegenerate, mapping down to a unique one-dimensional socle. Griffiths' symmetriser lemma connects this nondegeneracy to the geometric cup product pairing, aligning algebraic rings with topological Poincaré duality.

Generic Torelli Reconstructs the Hypersurface

06
Reconstruction Map

The Generic Torelli theorem asks whether a hypersurface can be completely reconstructed from its variation of Hodge structure.

Thanks to Macaulay nondegeneracy and the symmetriser lemma, the infinitesimal period map determines the Jacobian ring multiplication table. By reconstruction, this table recovers the original polynomial \(F\) up to projective equivalence.

generic Injectivity

The period map \(\Phi : U / G \to D / \Gamma\) is generically injective for smooth projective hypersurfaces, except for special cases (such as cubic threefolds, which are treated separately).

The Torelli question asks if the Hodge data remembers the variety. For smooth hypersurfaces, the answer is generically yes. Because the infinitesimal period map determines the Jacobian ring's multiplication table, we can algebraise this table and completely reconstruct the original polynomial F, proving generic injectivity of the period map.
