Local systems and representations

The Sheaf-Representation Equivalence

Local System

A local system \(\mathcal{L}\) of \(R\)-modules on \(X\) is a sheaf locally isomorphic to the constant sheaf \(R^r\).

Equivalence Theorem

For a connected path-wise connected space \(X\), there is a category equivalence between local systems of rank \(r\) over \(R\) and representations: $$\rho: \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \mathrm{GL}(r, R)$$

Monodromy representation

Glued constant charts determine parallel transport of stalks along paths. Because transport is invariant under homotopy, loops in the base generate stalk automorphisms depending only on the class in \(\pi_1\):

\([\gamma] \mapsto \rho([\gamma]) \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{L}_{x_0})\)

Section 1: Local Systems & representationsstalks and loops
We begin by defining local systems, which provide the proper language for tracking locally constant geometric data over a base space. By establishing a canonical equivalence between local systems of rank r and representations of the fundamental group into the general linear group, we translate the topological problem of path transport into pure linear algebra. This monodromy representation allows us to represent loops in the base space as concrete matrices acting on a vector space.
Fibrations and Cohomology

Fibrations Induce Cohomology Local Systems

Smooth Fibration

Let \(f: Y \to X\) be a smooth, proper submersion of complex manifolds. By Ehresmann's theorem, \(f\) is a locally trivial differentiable fiber bundle.

Cohomology Local System

For each \(k\), the cohomology groups of the fibers fit together to form a local system of rank \(b_k\) over \(X\): $$\mathcal{H}^k := R^k f_* \mathbb{Z}$$ where the stalk at \(x \in X\) is exactly \(H^k(Y_x, \mathbb{Z})\).

Parallel Transport

Every path \(\gamma: [0,1] \to X\) yields a diffeomorphism between fibers \(Y_{\gamma(0)} \cong Y_{\gamma(1)}\) unique up to homotopy. Tracing this diffeomorphism acts on cohomology, yielding the monodromy representation:

$$\rho: \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \mathrm{Aut}(H^k(Y_{x_0}, \mathbb{Z}))$$

Section 2: Fibrations & Parallel Transportcohomology stalks
Next, we study fibrations and parallel transport. Using Ehresmann's theorem, we know that a smooth, proper submersion behaves locally as a trivial fiber bundle. When we have a path in the base manifold, we can lift it to construct a diffeomorphism between the fibers, which induces an isomorphism on cohomology. Fitting these cohomology groups together over the entire base space produces a local system, whose monodromy tracks the global topological twisting of the family.
Local degeneration and vanishing cycles

Geometry of Monodromy & Vanishing Cycles

Vanishing Cycle \(\delta\)

As the parameter approaches \(s_{crit}\), the cycle \(\delta\) collapses to a point, producing a nodal double point. Inside the smooth fiber, \(\delta\) represents a homology class in \(H_1(Y_{s_0}, \mathbb{Z})\).

Monodromy Orbit

Under the loop \(\gamma(t)\), the transverse cycle \(\beta\) undergoes a topological twist, transforming into \(\beta + \delta\). The cycle \(\delta\) is killed under projection but generates local monodromy.

Local degeneration and transvections

The Picard-Lefschetz Formula

Theorem (Picard-Lefschetz)

Let \(f: Y \to \Delta\) be a family of curves degenerating to a nodal singularity. The local monodromy operator \(T: H^1(Y_t, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^1(Y_t, \mathbb{Z})\) is a transvection:

$$T(\gamma) = \gamma - (\gamma \cdot \delta) \delta$$

where \(\delta \in H_1(Y_t, \mathbb{Z})\) is the vanishing cycle, and \(\cdot\) is the skew-symmetric intersection pairing.

Symplectic Invariant Check

For algebraic curves, the intersection form \(Q(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) = \gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2\) is skew-symmetric. We verify that the transvection \(T\) preserves \(Q\):

\(T(\gamma_1) \cdot T(\gamma_2) = (\gamma_1 - (\gamma_1 \cdot \delta)\delta) \cdot (\gamma_2 - (\gamma_2 \cdot \delta)\delta)\)
\(\phantom{T(\gamma_1) \cdot T(\gamma_2)} = \gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2 - (\gamma_2 \cdot \delta)(\gamma_1 \cdot \delta) - (\gamma_1 \cdot \delta)(\delta \cdot \gamma_2) + 0\)
Since \(\delta \cdot \gamma_2 = -\gamma_2 \cdot \delta\), the middle terms cancel exactly!

residual = 0
Section 4: Picard-Lefschetz formulasymplectic intersection checks
This brings us to the famous Picard-Lefschetz formula, which describes the local monodromy action as a simple transvection. When a cycle travels around an ordinary double point, it is shifted by a multiple of the vanishing cycle, where the multiplier is exactly their intersection pairing. Because this transvection preserves the symplectic intersection form in odd dimensions, we can verify that the monodromy action is a true automorphism of the symplectic lattice, matching our zero-residual checks in the lab.
Topology and complex structures

Monodromy and Variation of Hodge Structure

Variation of Hodge Structure (VHS)

A real Variation of Hodge Structure of weight \(k\) on a complex manifold \(X\) consists of:

  • A local system \(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\) of free \(\mathbb{Z}\)-modules.
  • A filtration by holomorphic bundles \(F^p \mathcal{H} \subset \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}} = \mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X\).
Griffiths Transversality

The flat Gauss-Manin connection \(\nabla\) satisfies: $$\nabla(F^p \mathcal{H}) \subset F^{p-1} \mathcal{H} \otimes \Omega^1_X$$

Interaction with Monodromy

While the local system \(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}\) encodes locally constant topological data, the Hodge filtration subbundles \(F^p \mathcal{H}\) vary holomorphically.

The monodromy representation preserves the flat connection but rotates the filtration step, showing how moving topology restricts algebraic cycles.

Section 3: Monodromy and VHSGriffiths transversality
We now see how this topological monodromy interacts with the complex geometry, formulated through a Variation of Hodge Structure. A Variation of Hodge Structure consists of a flat connection and a filtration by holomorphic subbundles. The crucial Griffiths Transversality theorem states that the flat derivative of a filtration step lands in the adjacent lower step. This tells us that while the flat connection transports the topology, the complex structure shifts in a tightly bounded way.

Global monodromy theorems

Zariski and Noether-Lefschetz Theorems

Zariski Curve Theorem

Let \(C \subset \mathbb{P}^2\) be a nodal curve of degree \(d\). Then the fundamental group of its complement is abelian: $$\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^2 \setminus C, x_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}$$

Noether-Lefschetz Theorem

For a general surface \(S \subset \mathbb{P}^3\) of degree \(d \ge 4\), the Picard number is 1. The only algebraic cycles are multiples of the hyperplane section \(H\).

Role of Irreducibility

Both theorems reflect the global size and irreducibility of the monodromy action.

Because monodromy orbits are large, any invariant Hodge class must be globally constant across the parameter space, matching only the multiples of the hyperplane class.

Section 5 & 6: Zariski & Noether-Lefschetzglobal algebraic constraints
We conclude our study of monodromy with two major global theorems. Zariski's theorem on curve complements shows that the fundamental group of a projective plane minus a nodal curve of degree d is abelian and finite. The Noether-Lefschetz theorem states that on a general surface of degree four or higher, the Picard group is generated solely by the hyperplane section, because the monodromy orbit is so large that any algebraic cycle class must be invariant.

Summary: Navigating Monodromy

Key Takeaways

  1. Stalk Automorphisms: Path transport around loops translates topology into inspectable matrices.
  2. Local Degenerations: Picard-Lefschetz maps local nodes as simple transvections on homology.
  3. Symplectic Invariants: Transvection structures preserve intersection pairings exactly.
  4. Global Constraints: Strong monodromy bounds the dimension of algebraic cycles.

Common Pitfalls

  • Forgetting Loop Orientation: Changing the direction of a loop around a critical value changes the sign of the transvection.
  • Wrong Sign Conventions: The intersection form is skew-symmetric; sign flips break the symplectic invariant checks.
  • Fixed Cycle Confusion: Stalk elements invariant under monodromy do not represent all Hodge classes, only the invariant subspace.
