Surfaces with Geometric Genus Zero

01
The Bloch Conjecture

Let \(X\) be a smooth projective surface over \(\mathbb{C}\). If the geometric genus vanishes, the classical Bloch conjecture asserts that the degree-zero Chow group of zero-cycles is completely representable (which forces it to be zero if the Albanese variety is trivial):

\(p_g = h^{2,0}(X) = 0 \Longrightarrow CH_0(X)_0 \cong Alb(X)(\mathbb{C})\)

This translates a pure transcendental analytic condition into a dramatic simplification of algebraic cycles, where all homologically trivial zero-cycles must be rationally trivial.

Godeaux Surfaces & Classification

Classification of surfaces with \(p_g = 0\) reveals a rich topological variety: Godeaux surfaces (torsion fundamental group \(\mathbb{Z}/5\)), Barlow surfaces, and Enriques surfaces.

The **Godeaux surface** acts as a crucial test case: it has non-trivial fundamental group but no holomorphic two-forms, illustrating how Bloch's conjecture functions even in the presence of torsion topological structures.

The classical Bloch conjecture asserts that if a smooth projective surface has geometric genus zero, then its degree-zero Chow group is representable, forcing it to vanish if the Albanese variety is trivial. We explore the Godeaux surface as a key test case with non-trivial fundamental group, illustrating how Bloch's conjecture acts on surfaces with non-trivial topology.

Filtrations on Chow Groups

02
The Filtration Tower

A conjectural **Bloch-Beilinson filtration** \(F^\bullet\) on the Chow groups \(CH^i(X)_\mathbb{Q}\) organizes cycle classes into nested subspaces:

\(CH^i(X)_\mathbb{Q} = F^0 \supset F^1 \supset F^2 \supset \dots \supset 0\)

where:

  • \(F^1\) consists of cycles homologically trivial.
  • \(F^2\) consists of cycles mapping to zero under the Abel-Jacobi map (locally trivial).
  • Each graded piece \(Gr^j CH^i(X)\) represents cycles that are genuinely new at layer \(j\).
The Saito Filtration

Saito constructed an explicit, unconditional candidate filtration using product varieties and projections to map cycles.

This worked model organizes cycle groups algebraically, ensuring that each step in the filtration tower loses complex information in a controlled way, making the representability assertions editable and falsifiable.

We study filtrations on Chow groups, focusing on the conjectural Bloch-Beilinson filtration and the Saito filtration. These filtrations organize cycles into graded towers, where each successive step loses complex information in a controlled way, allowing us to study algebraic cycles layer by layer.

The Generalised Bloch Conjecture

03
Generalised Vanishing Conjecture

For a smooth projective variety \(X\) of dimension \(n\), the generalized Bloch conjecture asserts that the vanishing of transcendental Hodge cohorts forces corresponding steps of the Chow filtration to collapse.

Specifically, if the holomorphic forms of degree \(q\) up to \(p_g\) vanish, then specific graded pieces of Chow groups must vanish:

\(H^{q,0}(X) = 0 \quad \forall \, q \ge c \Longrightarrow F^c CH_0(X)_\mathbb{Q} = 0\)

This sets up a deep, general duality between transcendental Hodge theory and algebraic cycle support bounds.

Common Traps
  • **Trap 1: Established Assumption**
    Treating conjectural filtrations as established in all cases. They remain conjectures for general varieties.
  • **Trap 2: Trivial Albanese**
    Assuming \(p_g=0\) alone forces \(CH_0(X)_0 = 0\). If \(q=h^{1,0}(X) > 0\), the cycles map to a non-trivial Albanese torus.
The generalised Bloch conjecture extends these ideas to higher-dimensional varieties. It proposes that if specific Hodge cohorts vanish, then specific stages of the Chow filtration must also collapse. This sets up a deep, general duality between transcendental Hodge theory and algebraic cycle groups.

Abelian Varieties and the Pontryagin Product

04
Pontryagin Convolution

Let \(A\) be an abelian variety. The group addition map \(m : A \times A \to A\) defines the **Pontryagin product** on cycles, denoted by \(*\):

\(Z_1 * Z_2 = m_*(Z_1 \times Z_2)\)

Unlike the standard intersection product which acts as a ring multiplication, the Pontryagin product behaves like a group algebra convolution, shifting cycle support along the variety's translation vectors.

The Fourier Transform and Beauville's Decomposition

05
Fourier Transform on Chow

Following Beauville, we define the **Fourier transform** \(\mathcal{F} : CH^*(A)_\mathbb{Q} \to CH^*(A)_\mathbb{Q}\) using the Poincaré bundle \(\mathcal{P}\) on \(A \times \hat{A}\):

\(\mathcal{F}(Z) = p_{2*}\big(p_1^* Z \cdot \text{ch}(\mathcal{P}) \cdot [A]\big)\)

This Fourier operator diagonalizes the Pontryagin product, translating it cleanly into the intersection product:

\(\mathcal{F}(Z_1 * Z_2) = \mathcal{F}(Z_1) \cdot \mathcal{F}(Z_2)\)

Beauville Decomposition

Using the Fourier transform, Beauville proved that the Chow ring of an abelian variety splits into a direct sum of eigenspaces under the scaling map \([k]\):

\(CH^i(A)_\mathbb{Q} = \bigoplus_s CH^i(A)^{(s)}\)

where \(CH^i(A)^{(s)}\) is the eigenspace where \([k]^*\) acts as scaling by \(k^{2i-s}\). This yields an elegant, graded classification of cycle classes.

The Fourier transform on Chow groups, pioneered by Beauville, diagonalizes the Pontryagin product, decomposing the Chow ring of an abelian variety into a direct sum of eigenspaces under the scaling map. This provides an elegant classification of cycles, mirroring classical Fourier analysis.
