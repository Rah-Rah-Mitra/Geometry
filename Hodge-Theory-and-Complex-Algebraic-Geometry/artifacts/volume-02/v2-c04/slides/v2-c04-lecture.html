The Leray Spectral Sequence

Bookkeeping and computing the cohomology of fibered spaces, mapping families, and local systems in algebraic geometry.

Printed pages 94-114 PDF pages 106-126 Notebook: 04-the-leray-spectral-sequence.ipynb Volume II Chapter 04
Welcome to the lecture on the Leray Spectral Sequence, one of the most powerful bookkeeping devices in modern algebraic topology and complex algebraic geometry. Today we will study how the cohomology of a total space $X$ is built from the cohomology of the base space $Y$ and higher direct images representing the cohomology of the fibers. We will examine the algebraic and topological foundations, the bidegrees of differentials on the E2 page, and Deligne's celebrated degeneration and global invariant cycle theorems.

Composed Functors and Spectral Sequences

01
1. Homological Foundations
Composed Functors

If \(\mathcal{A} \xrightarrow{F} \mathcal{B} \xrightarrow{G} \mathcal{C}\) are left exact additive functors between abelian categories, and \(F\) sends injective objects to \(G\)-acyclic objects, there is a Grothendieck spectral sequence:

\(E_2^{p,q} = (R^p G)(R^q F)(A) \Longrightarrow R^{p+q}(G \circ F)(A)\)

For a continuous map \(f: X \to Y\), the pushforward is \(F = f_*\) and the global sections functor is \(G = \Gamma(Y, -)\).

Higher Direct Image Sheaves

The higher direct image sheaves \(R^q f_* \mathcal{F}\) on \(Y\) are the sheaves associated to the presheaf:

\(U \longmapsto H^q(f^{-1}(U), \mathcal{F})\)

Applying the composed functor theorem yields the **Leray Spectral Sequence**:

\(E_2^{p,q} = H^p(Y, R^q f_* \mathcal{F}) \Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \mathcal{F})\)
We begin with the abstract homological machinery. The Leray spectral sequence is a concrete realization of the Grothendieck spectral sequence for composed functors. By considering the pushforward of a sheaf to the base space, and then taking global sections, we obtain a spectral sequence whose E2 page consists of the cohomology of the base space with coefficients in the higher direct images, converging directly to the hypercohomology of the total space. This translates complex geometric structures into manageable algebraic steps.

Base Filtration and Local Systems

02
2. Filtration of Cohomology
The Filtration Steps

We filter the cochains on the total space \(C^k(X)\) by base-degree pullbacks. This induces a decreasing filtration on the total cohomology \(H^k(X, \mathbb{Q})\):

\(H^k(X, \mathbb{Q}) = F^0 H^k \supseteq F^1 H^k \supseteq \cdots \supseteq F^k H^k \supseteq F^{k+1} H^k = 0\)

The graded pieces are isomorphic to the survivors on the \(E_\infty\) page:

\(Gr^p H^{p+q}(X, \mathbb{Q}) = F^p H^{p+q} / F^{p+1} H^{p+q} \cong E_\infty^{p,q}\)
Local System Coefficients

On the \(E_2\) page, the coefficients \(R^q f_* \mathbb{Q}\) are not constant sheaves in general. They are **local systems** (flat vector bundles), whose stalks are the fiber cohomologies: \((R^q f_* \mathbb{Q})_y \cong H^q(F_y, \mathbb{Q})\). Loops in \(Y\) act on these stalks via monodromy.

Common Traps
  • **Trap 1: Constant Coefficients**
    Assuming \(R^q f_* \mathbb{Q}\) is constant. Monodromy often forces non-trivial local systems.
  • **Trap 2: Mismatched Degrees**
    Adding \(p\) and \(q\) incorrectly. The total degree of a class on the grid is always \(p + q\).
  • **Trap 3: Automatic Degeneration**
    Treating degeneration as automatic. It requires specific geometric settings (such as Kähler or projectivity).
To construct the sequence, we filter the cochains of the total space by base degree. The filtration is nested and induces a filtration on the total cohomology. The graded pieces of this filtration correspond to the E-infinity terms of our spectral sequence. The crucial observation is that the E2 terms are computed as base cohomology where the coefficients are local systems, tracking how fiber cohomology varies along loops in the base space. Forgetting these local coefficients is a common trap.

The Leray E2 Page and Differentials

03
3. The Bookkeeping Grid
Here is the E2 page grid of the Leray spectral sequence. The horizontal axis represents the base degree $p$, and the vertical axis represents the fiber degree $q$. Each cell contains a fiber cohomology group. The differentials $d_2$ have bidegree $(2, -1)$, acting as boundary maps that shift two steps right and one step down. They must square to zero, $d_2^2 = 0$. In general topological settings, these differentials can be non-trivial, killing off classes or leaving quotients as survivors.

Multiplicative Structure and Cup Products

04
4. Ring Structures
Multiplicative Compatibility

The cup product on the total space cohomology \(H^*(X, \mathbb{Q})\) is compatible with the spectral sequence structure. On each page \(r \ge 2\), we have a product map:

\(E_r^{p,q} \otimes E_r^{s,t} \longrightarrow E_r^{p+s, q+t}\)

This is a morphism of complexes, compatible with the differential. If \(x \in E_r^{p,q}\) and \(y \in E_r^{s,t}\), the Leibniz rule holds:

\(d_r(x \cdot y) = d_r(x) \cdot y + (-1)^{p+q} x \cdot d_r(y)\)
Products on the E2 Page

On the \(E_2\) page, this product coincides with the cup product on the base space \(Y\) with coefficients in the higher direct image sheaves:

\(H^p(Y, R^q f_* \mathbb{Q}) \otimes H^s(Y, R^t f_* \mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\smile} H^{p+s}(Y, R^{q+t} f_* \mathbb{Q})\)

which is induced by the natural sheaf pairing:

\(R^q f_* \mathbb{Q} \otimes R^t f_* \mathbb{Q} \longrightarrow R^{q+t} f_* \mathbb{Q}\)
The Leray spectral sequence is not just a sequence of vector spaces, but carries a rich multiplicative structure compatible with the cup product on cohomology. This multiplicative structure is a morphism of complexes on each page. The cup product on the E2 page is defined by taking the cup product of base cohomology classes with coefficients in the tensor products of the higher direct image sheaves, and satisfies a Leibniz rule with respect to the differentials.

Smooth Projective Families and Relative Lefschetz

05
5. Algebraic Constraints
Relative Hard Lefschetz

Let \(f: X \to Y\) be a smooth proper family of projective varieties of relative dimension \(d\). Let \(\eta \in H^0(Y, R^2 f_*\mathbb{Q})\) be the relative polarization class.

Cup product with \(\eta^r\) induces a local system isomorphism for all \(r \le d\):

\(\theta^r = \smile \eta^r : R^{d-r} f_* \mathbb{Q} \xrightarrow{\,\cong\,} R^{d+r} f_* \mathbb{Q}\)

This forces a symmetrical rank alignment on higher direct images.

Primitive Decomposition

This allows us to decompose each higher direct image local system into primitive components:

\(R^q f_* \mathbb{Q} \cong \bigoplus_i \eta^i \cdot P^{q-2i} f_* \mathbb{Q}\)

where the primitive local system \(P^k f_* \mathbb{Q}\) is the kernel of \(\smile \eta^{d-k+1}\) on \(R^k f_* \mathbb{Q}\). This decomposability prevents differentials from being non-trivial on the \(E_2\) page.

When we restrict ourselves to smooth projective families of complex algebraic varieties, algebraic geometry imposes extremely powerful constraints. The Relative Hard Lefschetz theorem guarantees that cupping with the relative polarization class defines a sheaf isomorphism between higher direct images of symmetric degrees. This decomposes higher direct images into direct sums of primitive sheaves, which will serve as the key homological engine to control and ultimately vanish the differentials.

Deligne's Degeneration Theorem

06
6. Degeneration
Degeneration at the E2 Page

A fundamental theorem of Deligne states that for a smooth projective family of complex algebraic varieties \(f: X \to Y\), the Leray spectral sequence degenerates at the \(E_2\) page:

\(d_r = 0 \quad \text{for all } r \ge 2\)

Consequently, the \(E_2\) page equals the \(E_\infty\) page, yielding a non-canonical splitting of the cohomology:

\(H^k(X, \mathbb{Q}) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q=k} H^p(Y, R^q f_* \mathbb{Q})\)
Contrast with Topology

In general topology, fiber bundles **do not** degenerate at \(E_2\). Consider the Hopf fibration:

\(S^1 \longrightarrow S^3 \longrightarrow S^2\)

Here, the differential \(d_2 : E_2^{0,1} \to E_2^{2,0}\) must be an isomorphism to kill off the classes and yield \(H^1(S^3) = H^2(S^3) = 0\). Deligne's theorem relies heavily on the Kähler metric and projective structure.

Deligne's degeneration theorem is a crowning achievement in algebraic topology and Hodge theory. It states that for any smooth projective family over a complex manifold, the Leray spectral sequence degenerates at the E2 page. This means that all differentials $d_r$ vanish for $r$ greater than or equal to two. The total cohomology of the variety is therefore isomorphic, as a graded vector space, to the direct sum of the E2 terms, completely avoiding higher boundary obstructions.

The Global Invariant Cycle Theorem

07
7. Monodromy & Invariants
The Invariant Cycle Statement

Let \(f: X \to Y\) be a smooth proper family of projective varieties. Let \(y \in Y\) be a basepoint. Monodromy acts on the fiber cohomology \(H^k(X_y, \mathbb{Q})\) via:

\(\rho : \pi_1(Y, y) \longrightarrow \text{Aut}(H^k(X_y, \mathbb{Q}))\)

The **Global Invariant Cycle Theorem** asserts that the restriction map from the total space is surjective onto the invariant subspace:

\(H^k(X, \mathbb{Q}) \twoheadrightarrow H^k(X_y, \mathbb{Q})^{\pi_1(Y, y)}\)

Any class on the fiber that is invariant under the base loops extends globally to \(X\).

Monodromy Loop
Loops in B deform cycles in fibers
An immediate and spectacular consequence of Deligne's degeneration and the semisimplicity of monodromy is the Global Invariant Cycle Theorem. It asserts that for a smooth projective family, the restriction map from the cohomology of the total space to the cohomology of any fiber has an image that is precisely the subspace of classes invariant under the monodromy action. This means that any fiber class that is stable under parameter loops extends globally to the entire family.
