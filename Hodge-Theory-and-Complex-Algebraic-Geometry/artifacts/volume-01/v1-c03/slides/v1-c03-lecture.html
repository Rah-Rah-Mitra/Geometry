Three Languages, One Object

Metric language

Positive Hermitian matrices measure lengths and angles on each holomorphic tangent space.

Form language

The fundamental form \( \omega \) packages the metric and complex structure as a real \((1,1)\)-form.

Closedness test

The Kahler condition is \( d\omega=0 \), a differential constraint beyond pointwise Hermitian positivity.

Hermitian Metric As Local Linear Algebra

Definition

A Hermitian metric is a smoothly varying positive definite Hermitian form on \(T^{1,0}X\).

\( h=\sum h_{i\bar j}\, dz_i \otimes d\bar z_j,\quad H=H^*,\quad v^*Hv>0. \)

The matrix test is local, but the smooth variation lets these pointwise ellipses glue across coordinate charts.

Hermitian geometry3
Pause on the word positive. Students often understand Hermitian symmetry but forget that the metric is a field of positive forms, not a single matrix. The ellipse makes the local model concrete: changing the metric changes the shape of the unit ball in each holomorphic tangent space.

The Fundamental Form Converts \(g\) And \(J\)

Compatibility identity
\( \omega(u,v)=g(Ju,v) \)

This two form is skew because \(J\) is orthogonal for the Hermitian metric.

In holomorphic coordinates, the same object appears as \( \omega=i\sum h_{i\bar j}\,dz_i\wedge d\bar z_j \), up to the convention used for the real metric normalization.

Kahler forms4
Make the conversion explicit: \(J\) rotates one input, then \(g\) pairs the result with the other input. This is why the metric and form are not two independent decorations. Once the complex structure is fixed, either compatible object determines the other.

The Extra Hypothesis Is Closedness

Common mistake

Hermitian does not automatically mean Kahler. Positivity says \(H\) is a good inner product; closedness says the associated form has no exterior derivative.

\( (X,J,g)\ \text{Kahler} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad d\omega=0. \)

The condition is local to compute but global in consequence: it creates the analytic identities used later by Hodge theory.

Closed (1,1)-forms5
This is the slide where the chapter becomes more than linear algebra. Stress that closedness is not a positivity condition and cannot be checked by looking at eigenvalues alone. It is a first order differential condition on the coefficients of the fundamental form.

Local Potentials Make Closed Positive Forms Computable

Local model

On a coordinate chart, many Kahler forms can be written as

\( \omega=i\,\partial\bar\partial\varphi. \)
What to inspect
  • The complex Hessian gives the Hermitian matrix.
  • Strict plurisubharmonicity gives positivity.
  • The \( \partial\bar\partial \) form is automatically closed.
Local potential calculus6
Present potentials as a computational handle, not as a global promise. Locally they let the instructor turn a function into a Hermitian matrix by differentiating twice. The caution is that the potential may change from chart to chart even when the form is globally defined.

Connections Align In The Kahler Case

The connection viewpoint is the bridge to later identities: parallel complex structure controls how \(d\), \( \partial \), \( \bar\partial \), adjoints, and Laplacians interact.

Connections7
Do not turn this into a full connection theory lecture. The useful point here is alignment: in the Kahler situation, preserving the metric and preserving the complex structure are compatible requirements. That alignment explains why later operator identities look surprisingly rigid.

Fubini-Study Is The Model To Keep In Mind

Affine chart formula
\( \varphi(z)=\log(1+\lVert z\rVert^2),\quad \omega_{FS}=i\,\partial\bar\partial\varphi. \)

Why it matters

  • It is positive on projective space.
  • It is invariant under unitary changes of homogeneous coordinates.
  • It supplies the standard Kahler class for projective examples.
Fubini-Study metric8
This slide should feel like the first serious example. The formula is compact, but its role is large: it gives the canonical metric on projective space and shows how a potential can encode positivity. Keep the focus on why projective geometry naturally produces Kahler forms.

Small Checks That Keep The Geometry Honest

Positive matrix
H = [[2, a],
     [bar a, 3]]
det H = 6 - |a|^2

Check principal minors before calling the metric positive.

Closed form
\( d\omega=0 \)

Differentiate the coefficients, then inspect cancellation of the resulting three form terms.

Potential Hessian
\( \partial\bar\partial \log(1+\lVert z\rVert^2)>0 \)

The model survives because the complex Hessian is positive on tangent directions.

Pointwise positivity \(+\) differential closedness \(+\) compatible type \(=\) Kahler package.
Computational checks9
Tie this slide directly to the notebook. These checks are not proofs of the full theorems, but they prevent category errors. A matrix can be positive without defining a closed fundamental form, and a closed real form can fail to be positive in the required complex directions.

Blowups Warn That Positivity Must Be Managed

Warning

Pulling back a Kahler form across a modification is not the whole story. The exceptional direction must receive controlled positive area.

Teaching Point

Blowups show why Kahler geometry is flexible but not automatic. One adjusts the class while checking positivity against every relevant curve direction.

Blowups10
Use the blowup as a warning rather than as a technical digression. The audience only needs the geometric intuition: a point is replaced by a new projective direction, and any candidate Kahler class must remain positive on that new exceptional curve.

The Kahler Package Feeds Hodge Theory

The moral: the closed fundamental form is not an isolated definition. It is the hypothesis that makes complex differential geometry and elliptic Hodge theory cooperate.

Preparing for Hodge decomposition11
Close the conceptual loop by pointing forward. Later decomposition theorems depend on identities among operators, and those identities trace back to the metric compatibility studied here. Students should see the Kahler condition as infrastructure for Hodge theory, not as a decorative assumption.
