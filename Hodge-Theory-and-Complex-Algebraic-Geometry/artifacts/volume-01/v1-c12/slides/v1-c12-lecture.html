Deligne-Beilinson Cohomology And The Abel-Jacobi Map

The lecture follows one problem: when the ordinary cycle class vanishes, what refined invariant still remembers the geometry of the cycle?

CycleStart with a codimension \(p\) algebraic cycle and its ordinary cohomology class.
VanishingRestrict attention to \(Z_{\mathrm{hom}}^p(X)\), where the primary class is zero.
ChainChoose a bounding topological chain \(\Gamma\) with \(\partial\Gamma=Z\).
PeriodsIntegrate Hodge-filtered forms and quotient by integral periods.
RefinementPackage the result as Abel-Jacobi, then as a Deligne-Beilinson class.
Open by separating primary and secondary information. The ordinary cohomology class is the first test for a cycle, but this lecture begins once that test gives zero. The remaining question is not whether a class exists in singular cohomology, but whether a bounding chain still leaves a period vector after quotienting by the correct lattice.

The Abel-Jacobi Map Starts After The Primary Class

INPUT CONDITION
Homologically Trivial Cycles
For a smooth projective \(X\), the Abel-Jacobi construction is applied to \(Z\in Z^p(X)\) only after \(cl(Z)=0\in H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Z})\).

Common Failure Mode

If \(cl(Z)\neq 0\), the secondary invariant is being asked to repair a primary obstruction. The construction has not reached its natural domain.

Teaching Heuristic

Read \(cl(Z)=0\) as a permission slip for choosing a chain \(\Gamma\) whose boundary is \(Z\), then ask which periods of \(\Gamma\) are independent of that choice.

This slide is the guardrail for the lecture. Students often hear Abel-Jacobi as just another cycle class map, but its domain is already filtered by the vanishing of the usual class. Emphasize that the chain \(\Gamma\) is not extra decoration; it exists because the primary class has disappeared and becomes the source of the secondary period calculation.

Intermediate Jacobian: The Quotient Where Periods Live

DEFINITIONAL MODEL
A Complex Torus From Hodge Data
The \(p\)-th intermediate Jacobian is a quotient of a complex vector space dual to a Hodge-filtered piece by an integral period lattice.

A common form is \(J^p(X)=H^{2p-1}(X,\mathbb{C})/(F^pH^{2p-1}+H^{2p-1}(X,\mathbb{Z}))\).

Interpretation

The Abel-Jacobi invariant is a point of this torus, not a single integral before quotienting.

Treat the intermediate Jacobian as a bookkeeping device for allowable ambiguities. A chain integral first produces a lift in a vector space. Changing the chain by a closed integral cycle shifts that lift by a period. The quotient is precisely what turns this dependence on choices into a well-defined torus point.

The Abel-Jacobi Formula Is A Choice-Independent Period

CONSTRUCTION
Functional On Test Forms
Given \(Z=\partial\Gamma\), define a functional by \(\omega\mapsto\int_\Gamma \omega\) on the appropriate Hodge-filtered forms, then reduce modulo the period lattice.

\(AJ(Z)(\omega)=\int_{\Gamma}\omega\)

\(\Gamma'=\Gamma+C\Rightarrow \int_{\Gamma'}\omega-\int_\Gamma\omega=\int_C\omega\)

Invariant Check

The difference is a period. Therefore the class in \(J^p(X)\) is independent of the bounding chain.

Pause here to say exactly what the formula does and does not claim. The integral depends on the selected chain as a number, so the raw value is not the invariant. The invariant appears only after observing that replacing \(\Gamma\) by another bounding chain changes the value by an integral period, which vanishes in the intermediate Jacobian quotient.

Picard And Albanese Are The Edge Cases

Picard Side

For divisors, the Abel-Jacobi philosophy recovers the passage from line bundles with fixed topological class to the Picard torus.

Albanese Side

For degree-zero zero-cycles, the invariant maps into the Albanese variety, the universal target for holomorphic one-form periods.

UNIFYING POINT
The Middle Is Not An Exception
Intermediate Jacobians extend the same period-quotient story to higher codimension cycles.
Use this slide to anchor the abstract construction in familiar endpoints. Picard varieties and Albanese varieties are not side stories; they are the low and high codimension faces of the same period quotient mechanism. That helps students see intermediate Jacobians as a genuine continuation of classical geometry rather than a new formal object dropped into the chapter.

Correspondences Must Respect The Quotient

FUNCTORIALITY PRINCIPLE
Cycles Act On Period Data
A correspondence should send cycles, chains, cohomology classes, and period lattices compatibly, so Abel-Jacobi classes move to well-defined Abel-Jacobi classes.

What To Check

  • primary class remains controlled;
  • chain boundaries are carried to chain boundaries;
  • period shifts land in the target period lattice.

Failure Signal

If the lattice is not respected, the raw integral may transform but the quotient point is not well-defined.

The correspondence slide is mainly about compatibility, not computation. A correspondence may act cleanly on cycles and on cohomology, but the Abel-Jacobi invariant lives in a quotient. The instructor should ask students to name the three pieces that must survive the pushforward: the boundary relation, the Hodge-filtered test forms, and the integral period lattice.

The Deligne Complex Records Integrality And Forms Together

DEFINITIONAL IDEA
Deligne-Beilinson Cohomology
Deligne cohomology is computed from a complex that places integral data and holomorphic forms in one cohomological object.

Do Not Flatten It

Treating Deligne cohomology as ordinary de Rham cohomology erases the lattice information that makes Abel-Jacobi secondary rather than just differential.

Lecture Emphasis

The complex is a memory device: it remembers which part came from topology and which part came from forms.

Introduce the Deligne complex as a way to hold two ledgers at once. The integral entry remembers topological periods, while the holomorphic-form entries remember differential data. The point is not to make a new cohomology theory for its own sake, but to create the natural home where cycle classes, periods, and secondary invariants can be compared without discarding any one of them.

Differential Characters: Curvature Plus Holonomy

PARALLEL LANGUAGE
Same Secondary Shape
A differential character pairs cycles with circle-valued data while its curvature records a differential form. It is another model for remembering a form and its integral ambiguity together.

cycle input \(C\) → value in \(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}\)

boundary input \(\partial A\) → controlled by \(\int_A \mathrm{curv}\)

Connection To The Chapter

This language makes the Abel-Jacobi invariant feel like holonomy: the boundary is visible, but the surviving value is measured modulo periods.

Differential characters are included because they give a useful mental model for Deligne-Beilinson classes. A character is not just a differential form and not just a homomorphism on cycles. It has a curvature form and a period-sensitive evaluation rule. That is exactly the kind of hybrid object needed when Abel-Jacobi invariants are interpreted as refined cycle classes.

Cycle Class In Deligne Cohomology Refines The Usual Class

REFINEMENT
A Stronger Target
The Deligne-Beilinson cycle class keeps track of the ordinary cohomology class and the secondary Abel-Jacobi data in one structured target.

Projection

Forget the differential refinement and one recovers the ordinary integral cycle class.

Kernel

On homologically trivial cycles, the refined class lands in the part measured by intermediate Jacobians.

This is the synthesis slide. The Deligne-Beilinson cycle class should be presented as a refinement, not a replacement. It maps down to the ordinary topological class, but when that class vanishes the remaining refined information is the Abel-Jacobi invariant. The diagram helps students see why the chapter moves from intermediate Jacobians to Deligne cohomology rather than treating them as separate topics.

Three Pitfalls That Distort The Chapter

Using Abel-Jacobi Too Early

Do not apply the secondary invariant before \(cl(Z)=0\). The first obstruction has to vanish before the period quotient is the right target.

Forgetting The Lattice

The integral \(\int_\Gamma\omega\) is a lift. The Abel-Jacobi point is its class modulo periods, not the unreduced number.

Flattening Deligne Cohomology

Deligne-Beilinson cohomology keeps integral and differential data together. Ordinary de Rham cohomology forgets too much for this task.

Use the pitfall slide as an active diagnostic. For any proposed Abel-Jacobi computation, ask the class the three questions in order: has the ordinary class vanished, has the chain integral been reduced by periods, and is the target remembering both integral and form data? Most incorrect solutions to this material fail one of those checks rather than failing a difficult calculation.

Closing Map: What Survives After Boundaries

Conceptual Outcome

The Abel-Jacobi map measures a secondary obstruction: a period left behind by a homologically trivial algebraic cycle.

Structural Outcome

Deligne-Beilinson cohomology supplies the refined cohomological target where this secondary information naturally belongs.

Next Reading Move

When a later argument mentions a refined cycle class, immediately ask which part is ordinary topology and which part is period-sensitive refinement.

Close by returning to the original question: what remains after a cycle bounds? The answer is not simply nothing. If integration over a bounding chain produces a period vector, and if changes of chain alter that vector only by the allowed lattice, then the residue is a point of an intermediate Jacobian. Deligne-Beilinson cohomology then gives this residue a refined cohomological home.
