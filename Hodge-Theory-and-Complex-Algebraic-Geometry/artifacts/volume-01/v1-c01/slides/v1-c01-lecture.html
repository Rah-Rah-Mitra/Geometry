Definition And Test

Holomorphy Is Vanishing Of The Antilinear Part

Definition

\(df = {\partial f\over \partial z}\,dz + {\partial f\over \partial \bar z}\,d\bar z\), with \(z=x+iy\).

A \(C^1\) function is holomorphic exactly when \({\partial f/\partial\bar z}=0\) at every point.

Geometric Meaning

The real differential \(df_u:T_uU\to C\) is complex-linear precisely when multiplication by \(i\) is respected. The unwanted component is the antilinear response.

Spend time on the decomposition of df because it is the template for the whole course. The notation is analytic, but the content is geometric: a holomorphic function is a real differentiable map whose infinitesimal behavior commutes with the complex structure.
One Variable Mechanism

Stokes Moves The Contour, Cauchy Reads The Center

Cauchy Formula

\(f(z_0)= {1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\partial D}{f(\zeta)\over \zeta-z_0}\,d\zeta\)

  1. For holomorphic \(f\), the form \(f(z)\,dz\) is closed.
  2. Apply Stokes on the punctured disk, with the inner boundary oppositely oriented.
  3. Let the inner circle shrink and extract \(f(z_0)\).
The point is not to reprove every technical detail, but to make the proof architecture visible. Stokes says that a closed form has equal integrals on homologous contours. The singular kernel forces one contour to remember the value at the puncture.
Several Variables

Holomorphy Means Every Coordinate Direction Is Complex-Linear

Definition

For \(U\subset C^n\), a \(C^1\) function is holomorphic when \(df_u:C^n\to C\) is complex-linear, equivalently

\({\partial f\over\partial \bar z_1}=\cdots={\partial f\over\partial \bar z_n}=0.\)

Theorem 1.1 / 1.17 In Use

These differential equations imply local analytic power series expansions, and Cauchy formula supplies the coefficients.

Here the lecture should stress that several-variable holomorphy is stronger than having unrelated one-variable stories. The differential must be complex-linear on the full tangent space, and the vanishing of all dbar derivatives lets Cauchy formula be applied across coordinate circles coherently.
Product Contours

The Polydisc Formula Reconstructs Values From A Torus Boundary

Cauchy In Several Variables

\(f(u)=\left({1\over 2\pi i}\right)^n\int_{\lvert\zeta_i-u_i\rvert=r_i} f(\zeta)\prod_{i=1}^{n}{d\zeta_i\over \zeta_i-u_i}.\)

The contour is a product of circles, so the proof can be read as repeated one-variable Cauchy formula or as a Stokes argument for a product kernel.

Make the boundary torus concrete. In one variable the boundary is a circle; in two variables the Cauchy contour is a product of two circles. This is why values inside a polydisc are controlled by boundary data in every coordinate direction simultaneously.
Consequences Of Cauchy Control

Analyticity, Maximum Principle, And Identity Principle

Power Series Cauchy kernels expand into convergent geometric series on smaller polydiscs, giving local analytic expansions.

\(f(z)=\sum_I a_I z^I\)

Maximum Principle If \(|f|\) has a local maximum, the boundary-average formula forces local constancy.

\(|f(u)|\le \sup_{\partial D}|f|\)

Identity Principle Vanishing on a nonempty open set makes all local coefficients vanish and propagates on connected domains.

open zero set \(\Rightarrow f\equiv0\)

Notebook Check

A toy Cauchy-Riemann residual vanishes for a model holomorphic function, while a controlled non-holomorphic perturbation leaves a nonzero dbar residual.

This slide gathers the immediate consequences before the extension theorems. The important teaching choice is to connect all three results to the same source: once values are boundary averages and coefficients are contour integrals, local behavior becomes rigid.
Removing A Hypersurface Singularity

Riemann Extension Needs Boundedness

Riemann Type Extension

If \(f\) is holomorphic away from \(z_1=0\) and locally bounded near that hypersurface, the Cauchy integral defines the missing values holomorphically.

Failure Mode

Without boundedness, a codimension-one singularity can be genuine. The small contour may carry nonzero residue data instead of disappearing.

The Riemann extension theorem is a useful contrast case. In codimension one, missing data can carry singular behavior, so boundedness is the hypothesis that makes the shrinking contour term vanish. This prepares students to appreciate why Hartogs is stronger in higher codimension.
Higher Codimension Rigidity

Hartogs Extension Removes A Codimension-Two Hole

Hartogs Form Used Here

For \(n\ge2\), a holomorphic function on \(U\setminus\{z_1=z_2=0\}\) extends holomorphically across the missing set.

The proof deforms product contours while avoiding both the exceptional set and the kernel poles \(\zeta_i=z_i\). Stokes then transfers Cauchy formula from safe contours to the desired boundary.

Hartogs is one of the conceptual surprises of several complex variables. The lecture should make clear that the theorem is not merely Riemann extension with a different set. In codimension two, product-contour deformation leaves no room for an isolated hypersurface residue.
Local Dbar Solvability

The Integral Primitive Is The Seed Of Dolbeault Exactness

Theorem 1.28

For a \(C^k\) function \(f\) on an open set in \(C\), locally there is a \(C^k\) function \(g\) satisfying

\({\partial g\over\partial \bar z}=f.\)

Non-Uniqueness

The primitive is determined only up to addition of a holomorphic function, because holomorphic functions are killed by \(\partial/\partial\bar z\).

This theorem should be presented as the bridge to the next chapter. The explicit singular integral is less important here than its role: it proves that locally every dbar-closed-looking datum has a primitive, modulo the holomorphic functions that form the kernel.
