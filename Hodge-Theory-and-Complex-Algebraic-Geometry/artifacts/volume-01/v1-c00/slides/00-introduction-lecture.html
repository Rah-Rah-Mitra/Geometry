The Opening Contract

00
Objects

Compact Kahler manifolds, holomorphic forms, cohomology groups, ample classes, and algebraic cycles.

Maps

Exterior differential, type projection, cup product with the Kahler class, cycle class, and Abel-Jacobi period map.

Invariants

Hodge numbers, Betti ranks, primitive parts, intersection pairings, and period quotients.

Reading Rule

Keep three ledgers open at once: assumptions, maps, and surviving invariants. The same cohomology group will be viewed through several compatible coordinate systems.

The first substantive move is to make the contract explicit. Students should hear that Hodge theory is not one isolated theorem but a bookkeeping system. Assumptions say which geometric world we are in; maps move forms, classes, and cycles; invariants record what survives the movement.

Kahler Geometry Is The Entry Point

02
Definition Lens

A compact Kahler manifold carries compatible complex, Riemannian, and symplectic structures. That compatibility is what permits harmonic representatives to respect complex type.

\(H^k(X,\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}H^{p,q}(X)\)

Use the triangle to separate what is often compressed into one word. Kahler means the complex structure, metric, and closed two-form cooperate. That cooperation is the reason harmonic theory can see complex type. Without it, the later decomposition statements would not be reliable coordinate systems on cohomology.

The Hodge Diamond Is A Ledger

03
Rank Accounting

\(h^{p,q}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{p,q}(X)\)

\(b_k=\sum_{p+q=k} h^{p,q}\)

The diamond stores cohomology by degree and by complex type at the same time.

The diamond should be read as an accounting device, not as a decorative emblem. Every entry is a dimension, conjugation reflects entries across the vertical axis, and diagonal sums recover Betti numbers. This is the first place where topology and complex structure occupy one visible object.

Lefschetz Gives A Second Coordinate System

04
Operator Lens

\(L(\alpha)=\omega\wedge\alpha\)

Hard Lefschetz compares opposite degrees by iterating \(L\). Primitive cohomology records what is not already a raised class.

\(L^{n-k}:H^k(X)\to H^{2n-k}(X)\)

After the Hodge diamond, introduce Lefschetz as a second coordinate system on the same cohomology. The important action is multiplication by the Kahler class. Students should track how degree changes, why the middle dimension matters, and why primitive classes are the pieces from which the rest is generated.

Forms, Representatives, And Classes

05
Distinction

A differential form has a type. A cohomology class has a type decomposition only after passing through the Hodge theorem under the Kahler hypotheses.

Common Trap

Do not identify a raw \((p,q)\)-form with a Hodge component of cohomology until closedness, exactness, and harmonic representatives have been accounted for.

This is a good moment to slow down. Much confusion in the subject comes from moving too quickly from forms to classes. Make students say which object they have at each stage: a form, a closed form modulo exact forms, or a harmonic representative whose type contributes to a Hodge summand.

Projective Is Stronger Than Kahler

06
Compact Kahler
  • Analytic and metric setting.
  • Hodge decomposition and Lefschetz tools apply.
  • Topology is constrained by the Kahler package.
Smooth Projective
  • Comes from algebraic equations in projective space.
  • Has a distinguished integral ample class.
  • Algebraic cycles become central objects.
smooth projective \( \Rightarrow \) compact Kahler, but not conversely
The introduction prepares students for a persistent asymmetry. Projective manifolds provide the algebraic examples and carry ample classes, so they are Kahler. But the Kahler world is larger. This distinction matters later when a statement is analytic for all Kahler manifolds versus algebraic for projective ones.

Cycles Enter Through Their Shadows

07
Two Levels

The cycle class records the first cohomological shadow. When that shadow vanishes in the relevant sense, Abel-Jacobi records finer period information.

\(Z \mapsto [Z]\), then \(Z \mapsto AJ(Z)\)

Introduce cycles as geometric subobjects whose first measurable trace is a cohomology class. The Abel-Jacobi map should be described as a finer invariant, not as a replacement for the cycle class. It becomes meaningful precisely when the primary cohomological shadow is too small to distinguish the cycle.

Takeaways For The Course

09
What To Remember
  • Hodge theory gives compatible decompositions of cohomology.
  • The Kahler class acts, rather than merely labels the geometry.
  • Projective geometry adds algebraic cycles and integral constraints.
How To Read Ahead
  • Name the assumptions before using a theorem.
  • Track whether a statement is about forms, classes, or cycles.
  • Ask which invariant survives each map.
assumptions \( \longrightarrow \) maps \( \longrightarrow \) surviving invariants
Close by giving students a reusable reading strategy. The introduction is successful if they leave with a disciplined set of questions: what assumptions are active, what map is being applied, and what invariant should remain visible. Those questions will organize the later technical chapters.
