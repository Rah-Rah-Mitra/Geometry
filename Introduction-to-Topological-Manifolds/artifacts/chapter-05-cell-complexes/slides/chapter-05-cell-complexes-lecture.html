This chapter spans printed pages 127 through 158 and builds spaces by gluing cells. We treat a CW complex as data that remembers topology: open cells, characteristic maps, attaching maps on boundaries, and a weak topology that lets local closure checks govern global continuity and closedness.

Open and Closed Cells

Open $n$-cell

Homeomorphic to the open ball $\mathring{B}^n$.

Radial proof

Each ray from an interior point hits the boundary once.

Closed cell model

$e^n \cong \overline{B}^n,\qquad \partial e^n \cong S^{n-1}$

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Open and Closed Cells
An open n-cell is homeomorphic to an open n-ball, a closed n-cell to a closed ball. The radial proof shows any compact convex body with interior is a closed cell: from an interior point, each ray meets the boundary exactly once, stretching the ball to fill the body.

Attaching and Characteristic Maps

Attaching map

$\varphi:S^{n-1}\to X^{n-1}$ glues the boundary.

Quotient

Interior stays a fresh open $n$-cell.

Pushout

$X^{n}= X^{n-1}\cup_{\varphi}\overline{B}^n$

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Attaching Maps
Cells are assembled by maps, not by drawing. To attach an n-cell, send the boundary sphere of a closed ball into X by a continuous attaching map, then quotient. The characteristic map extends to the whole disk; its interior becomes a fresh open n-cell while the boundary lands below.

Skeletons and Inductive Gluing

$n$-skeleton

All cells of dimension $\le n$; a subcomplex.

Induction

Define on $X^{n-1}$, extend over new $n$-cells.

Filtration

$X^0\subseteq X^1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X=\bigcup_n X^n$

Coherence

The topology agrees with the skeleton sequence.

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Skeletons and Inductive Gluing
Build upward from the discrete 0-skeleton. The n-skeleton adds all n-cells attached along maps into the previous skeleton, and each skeleton is a subcomplex. Inductive proofs define data on one skeleton, extend across the next layer of cells, and use coherence to recover a global statement.

Why the Letters C and W

(C) Closure finite

Each $\overline{e}$ meets finitely many cells.

(W) Weak topology

Closed iff closed in every $\overline{e}$.

Weak topology test

$A\subseteq X\text{ closed}\iff A\cap\overline{e}\text{ closed }\forall e$

Pathologies

Drop (W): a sequence accumulates globally yet passes every cell test.

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Conditions C and W
The two letters do real work. Closure finiteness C says each closed cell meets only finitely many cells. Weak topology W says a set is closed precisely when its intersection with every closed cell is closed. Without W a sequence can pass every cell test yet still accumulate globally unaccounted.

The Sphere: One Point, One Cell

Two cells

One $0$-cell $p$, one $n$-cell.

Attaching

Crush all of $S^{n-1}$ to $p$.

Quotient

$S^{n}\cong \overline{B}^{n}/\partial \overline{B}^{n}$

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158The Sphere with Two Cells
The simplest interesting structure: take one 0-cell as basepoint and one n-cell. The attaching map crushes the entire boundary sphere to that point. The quotient of a closed n-ball collapsing its boundary is exactly the n-sphere, giving the minimal CW decomposition with two cells total.

The Torus: $1+2+1$ Cells

Cell counts

One $0$-cell, two $1$-cells $a,b$, one $2$-cell.

Boundary word

The square attaches along $a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}$.

Attaching word

$\varphi:\ \partial \overline{B}^2 \mapsto a\,b\,a^{-1}b^{-1}$

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158The Torus CW Structure
The torus needs one 0-cell at the identified corners, two 1-cell loops a and b, and one 2-cell. The 2-cell attaches by the boundary word a b a inverse b inverse, reading the fundamental polygon edge by edge. This single relator encodes the commutator that abelianizes the fundamental group.

Projective Spaces

One cell per dimension

$\mathbb{RP}^n$ has a $k$-cell for each $0\le k\le n$.

Antipodal glue

Attach the $n$-cell via $S^{n-1}\!\to\!\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$.

Cell tower

$\mathbb{RP}^n=\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}\cup_{q}e^{n},\quad q:S^{n-1}\to\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Projective Spaces
Real projective space carries one cell in each dimension from zero up to n, built by attaching an n-cell to projective n-minus-one space via the antipodal quotient of the sphere. This minimal tower makes RP-n a clean computational example whose skeleton filtration matches its dimension exactly.

What the 1-Skeleton Knows

Connectedness

$X$ connected $\iff X^1$ connected.

Path-connected

Connected CW complexes are path-connected.

Compactness, finitely

$K\subseteq X\text{ compact}\Rightarrow K\subseteq\text{ finite subcomplex}$

No secret joins

Higher cells attach below, so they cannot merge components.

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158What the 1-Skeleton Knows
Higher cells cannot secretly join components because their boundary already lands below. So a CW complex is connected exactly when its 1-skeleton is connected, and then it is path-connected. Compactness is finite too: compact subsets are closed and sit inside some finite subcomplex of the whole space.

Classifying 1-Manifolds

Degree two

Boundaryless: each vertex meets two edges.

Boundary

Degree-one vertices are endpoints.

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Classifying 1-Manifolds
With a regular CW structure each edge has two endpoint vertices. A boundaryless 1-manifold forces degree two at every vertex: a finite closed chain is a circle, an infinite chain is the line. Degree-one boundary vertices give a compact interval or, with one infinite direction, a half-line.

Simplicial Complexes

Rigid subcase

Closed simplices, all faces, common-face intersections.

Regular CW

Simplex interiors are the cells.

Boundary of a boundary

$\partial_{1}\partial_{2}=0$

Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Simplicial Complexes
A simplicial complex is a rigid subcase: closed simplices, every face included, intersections that are common faces. Its polyhedron is a regular CW complex with simplex interiors as cells. Boundary matrices satisfy d1 d2 equals zero, and Betti numbers read connectedness and holes combinatorially.

The Cellular Euler Characteristic

Alternating sum

Count cells by dimension with alternating sign.

Invariant

Independent of the decomposition; a homotopy invariant.

Definition

$\chi(X)=\sum_{n\ge 0}(-1)^n\, c_n$

Check the examples

  • $\chi(S^2)=1-0+1=2$
  • $\chi(T^2)=1-2+1=0$
  • $\chi(\mathbb{RP}^2)=1-1+1=1$
Lee, Intro to Topological Manifolds · pp. 127-158Euler Characteristic
For a finite CW complex the Euler characteristic is the alternating sum of cell counts by dimension. It is a homotopy invariant independent of the chosen decomposition. The sphere gives two, the torus gives zero, and projective plane gives one, matching every other valid cell structure.
