Covering Maps

Stacks of sheets, lifted paths, and the algebra of the fundamental group

Local picture

Every base point has a neighborhood evenly covered by disjoint sheets.

Global payoff

Tiny sheets force rigid lifting and encode $\pi_1$ as deck symmetry.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Covering Maps
This chapter, printed pages 277 to 306, treats covering maps as machines with visible parts: stacks of sheets over evenly covered neighborhoods, lifted paths, monodromy permutations, and a universal cover storing path classes as points. We open with the exponential map R to the circle as the guiding model for every later theorem here.

Evenly Covered Neighborhoods

Definition

$p^{-1}(U) = \bigsqcup_{\alpha} V_\alpha,\quad p|_{V_\alpha}\colon V_\alpha \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} U$

Sheets

The $V_\alpha$ are the sheets over $U$; each is a homeomorphic copy of the base patch.

Constant count

Over a connected base the number of sheets is locally constant, hence constant.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Evenly Covered Neighborhoods
A covering map demands that each base point own a neighborhood whose entire preimage splits into disjoint sheets, each mapped homeomorphically down. This is stronger than a local homeomorphism. The pancake stack picture makes the sheet count visible and explains why that count stays constant across a connected base.

The Exponential Cover $\mathbb{R}\to S^1$

Model map

$p(t) = e^{2\pi i t},\qquad p^{-1}(p(t_0)) = t_0 + \mathbb{Z}$

Sheets

Each unit interval maps homeomorphically onto an arc; integers index the fiber.

Local section

Picking one sheet is choosing a branch of the angle, a local inverse of $p$.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306The Exponential Cover
The exponential map sends the real line onto the circle by winding it infinitely, placing the fiber over one point at the integer translates of any lift. Each unit interval becomes a sheet. Choosing one sheet is choosing a local section, a local inverse that picks a single branch of the angle.

A Local Homeomorphism Need Not Cover

Warning example

Restrict $p$ to the open interval $(0,2)$. It is surjective and locally a homeomorphism onto $S^1$.

Failure

$p|_{(0,2)}\colon (0,2)\to S^1\ \text{is not a covering}$

Why

Near the seam the preimage has two endpoint pieces covering only half of a base arc.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Not a Covering
Restricting the exponential map to the open interval gives a surjective local homeomorphism that still fails to be a covering. Near the seam point, two endpoint components cover only one side of a base arc. The defect is not local injectivity; it is the absence of complete sheets over a neighborhood.

Path and Homotopy Lifting

Unique lifting

$\tilde f(0)=e \ \Rightarrow\ \tilde f \text{ exists and is unique}$

Path lifting

Fix the start in the fiber; the whole path lifts deterministically.

Homotopy lifting

A homotopy of base paths lifts holding endpoints fixed, so endpoints are invariants.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Lifting Properties
Fixing a starting point in the fiber, every base path lifts uniquely, and a homotopy of base paths lifts to a homotopy upstairs holding endpoints fixed. Unique lifting forbids a connected source from silently switching sheets, so the endpoint of a lifted path is an invariant of the path-homotopy class.

The Lifting Criterion

Criterion

$\phi \text{ lifts} \iff \phi_*\pi_1(Y) \subseteq p_*\pi_1(E)$

Subgroup containment

The entire decision reduces to comparing two subgroups of $\pi_1(X,x)$.

Why it works

Containment is exactly what makes the endpoint construction of the lift well-defined.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Lifting Criterion
A map from Y lifts through a covering exactly when the fundamental-group image of Y lands inside the subgroup induced by the cover. Subgroup containment is the whole decision procedure. The condition is precisely what stops two different paths in the source from forcing two different endpoint choices for the lift.

Circle Covers: Degree Divisibility

Test

$\deg m \text{ lifts through } p_n \iff m\mathbb{Z}\subseteq n\mathbb{Z} \iff n\mid m$

Induced subgroups

$p_n$ induces $n\mathbb{Z}$; a degree-$m$ map induces $m\mathbb{Z}$.

Worked case

With $n=5,\ m=7$: starting on sheet $0$ the lift ends on sheet $7\bmod 5 = 2$.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Degree Divisibility
For the n-fold circle cover the criterion becomes arithmetic: a degree m self-map of the circle lifts exactly when n divides m, because the cover induces the subgroup nZ and the map induces mZ. A degree seven loop over the five-fold cover ends on sheet two, since seven modulo five equals two.

Monodromy as a Fiber Action

Action

$e\cdot[f] = \tilde f(1),\qquad \pi_1(X,x)\curvearrowright p^{-1}(x)$

Generator

For the $n$-fold circle cover the generator sends sheet $j\mapsto j+1 \bmod n$.

Transitive

Any sheet is reached by a loop of suitable degree; the action has one orbit.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Monodromy
Monodromy packages all lifting from one base point into a right action of the fundamental group on a fiber. Lift a loop starting at a sheet; its endpoint sheet is the action. For the n-fold circle cover the generator cycles sheets by one step, giving a transitive action whose sheet stabilizer is nZ.

The Injection $p_*\colon \pi_1(E)\hookrightarrow \pi_1(X)$

Theorem

$p_*\colon \pi_1(E,e)\to\pi_1(X,x) \text{ is injective}$

Proof idea

If $p\circ\tilde f$ is null-homotopic, lift the null-homotopy; $\tilde f$ was already trivial.

As stabilizer

The image equals the stabilizer of $e$ in the monodromy action.

Image = stabilizer

$p_*\pi_1(E,e) = \mathrm{Stab}_{\pi_1(X,x)}(e)$

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Induced Injection
Pushing forward along a covering map injects the fundamental group of the cover into that of the base. A loop upstairs that projects to a null-homotopic loop lifts a null-homotopy and so was already trivial. The image is the stabilizer of the chosen fiber point under the monodromy action.

Conjugacy and Normal Covers

Base change

$e' = e\cdot[g] \ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Stab}(e') = [g]^{-1}\,\mathrm{Stab}(e)\,[g]$

Conjugacy class

Unbased covers correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups, not single subgroups.

Normal cover

Induced subgroup normal $\iff$ stabilizers agree across the fiber $\iff$ uniform deck symmetry.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Conjugacy & Normality
Changing the chosen point in a fiber conjugates the induced subgroup, so unbased covering isomorphism is naturally stated with conjugacy classes. A covering is normal when its induced subgroup is normal, equivalently when the monodromy stabilizers agree across the fiber, giving uniform deck symmetry rather than a mere conjugacy class.

The Universal Cover

Definition

$\tilde X \text{ universal} \iff \tilde X \text{ simply connected},\ \pi_1(\tilde X)=1$

Universality

It covers every other covering of $X$; any two simply connected covers are isomorphic.

Construction

Points are path-homotopy classes from a base point, unwrapping all of $\pi_1$.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Universal Cover
A universal cover is simply connected. Its induced subgroup is trivial, so it covers every other covering of the same base, and any two simply connected covers are isomorphic. The construction uses path classes from a base point as new points, unwrapping all fundamental-group ambiguity into distinct points upstairs.

Examples: $\mathbb{R}^2\to T^2$ and $S^n\to \mathbb{RP}^n$

Torus

$\mathbb{R}^2\to T^2,\quad (x,y)\mapsto (x,y)\bmod \mathbb{Z}^2$

Lattice fiber

Each fiber is an integer-lattice orbit; deck transformations are integer translations.

Projective space

$S^n\to\mathbb{RP}^n \text{ is a normal double cover}$

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Torus & Projective Space
For the torus the universal cover is the plane tiled by integer translates of one chart, with each fiber an integer-lattice orbit projecting by coordinate-wise modulo one. The sphere double-covers real projective space by identifying antipodes, a normal two-sheeted cover whose deck group is the order-two antipodal involution.

Classification by Subgroups

Galois correspondence

$\{\text{covers}\}/{\cong}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \{\text{subgroups of }\pi_1(X)\}/\text{conj.}$

Based version

Based covers correspond to actual subgroups; sheet number equals the index.

Extremes

Trivial subgroup gives the universal cover; the whole group gives $X$ itself.

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Classification
Connected covers of a nice base, up to isomorphism, correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of the base fundamental group; based covers correspond to actual subgroups. Sheet number equals the subgroup index. The universal cover sits atop the lattice as the trivial subgroup; the base itself sits at the bottom.

Recap: Topology as Subgroup Arithmetic

Local to global

Complete sheets over evenly covered $U$ force rigid, deterministic lifting.

Lifting

Endpoints are homotopy invariants; the criterion is subgroup containment.

Monodromy

Transitive fiber action; stabilizers are the induced subgroups $p_*\pi_1(E)$.

One slogan

$\text{covers of } X\ \leftrightarrow\ \text{subgroups of }\pi_1(X)$

Lee, Introduction to Topological Manifolds · printed pp. 277-306Recap
Covering theory turns topology into subgroup arithmetic: evenly covered sheets force rigid lifting, lifting endpoints are homotopy invariants, the criterion is subgroup containment, monodromy gives transitive fiber actions with induced-subgroup stabilizers, and the universal cover anchors the classification. The circle, torus, and projective spaces supply the running computational examples here.
