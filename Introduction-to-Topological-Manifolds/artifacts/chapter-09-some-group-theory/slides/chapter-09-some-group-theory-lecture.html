Why Group Theory Now

Loops split

A loop in a union breaks into arcs in different open sets.

No forced commuting

The pieces must multiply without collapsing order.

Guiding idea

$\pi_1(U\cup V) \;\leftarrow\; \pi_1(U) * \pi_1(V)$

Goal

Combine generators, impose only deliberate relations.

Printed pp. 233-250Why Group Theory Now
A loop crossing a union splits into arcs living in different open sets, and those pieces must multiply without being forced to commute. This topological pressure is exactly why we need free constructions: they combine generators while imposing only the relations we deliberately choose, never accidental ones.

Free Products

Keep each law

Factors $G$ and $H$ keep their own multiplication.

No cross-relations

Nothing relates a letter of $G$ to a letter of $H$.

Free product

$g_1 h_1 g_2 h_2 \cdots \in G * H$

Nonabelian

$gh \neq hg$ whenever both factors are nontrivial.

Printed pp. 233-250Free Products
The free product stitches two groups together so each factor keeps its own multiplication, yet nothing relates elements from different factors. Words alternate between factors, identity letters vanish, and adjacent letters from one factor collapse using that factor's law. The result is genuinely nonabelian whenever both factors are nontrivial.

Reduced Words and Normal Form

Reduced word

Alternating letters, no identity, no same-factor neighbours.

Uniqueness

One reduced representative per element.

Printed pp. 233-250Reduced Words and Normal Form
Every element of a free product has exactly one reduced representative: an alternating word with no identity letters. The reduction algorithm reads left to right on a stack, multiplying same-factor neighbours and deleting identities. Uniqueness of this normal form is what makes the free product a well-defined group rather than a formal symbol set.

Free Groups

Only one rule

$x x^{-1}$ cancels; nothing else commutes.

One generator

$F(\{a\}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, the integer line.

Free group on $S$

$F(S) = \{\text{reduced words in } S \cup S^{-1}\}$

Topology link

$\pi_1$ of a wedge of circles is free.

Printed pp. 233-250Free Groups
A free group on a set forgets every relation except the cancellation of a generator against its formal inverse. With one generator we recover the infinite cyclic group, an integer line. With two generators the words branch into a tree, and the absence of loops is the visible certificate that no hidden relation exists.

The Cayley Tree of $F(a,b)$

Valence

$\deg(1) = 4, \qquad \deg(w) = 3 \text{ for } w \neq 1$

No loops

A cycle would be a nontrivial word equal to $1$.

Printed pp. 233-250The Cayley Tree of F(a,b)
The Cayley graph of the free group on two generators is the four-valent tree. From the identity there are four moves, and every later vertex has three onward choices because the edge back is forbidden in a reduced word. A loop would encode a nontrivial word equal to the identity, contradicting freeness.

Universal Property of Free Groups

Choose images

Pick where each generator $s \in S$ goes.

Forced extension

Reduction determines every other value.

Universal property

$f: S \to G \;\Longrightarrow\; \exists! \, \tilde f: F(S) \to G$

Use

Defines maps in van Kampen and covering arguments.

Printed pp. 233-250Universal Property of Free Groups
To build a homomorphism out of a free group, simply choose where each generator goes; the reduction rules then force the image of every word. This universal property characterizes the free group up to isomorphism and is the engine behind defining maps in van Kampen and covering-space arguments.

Group Presentations

Generators

Free group on the named symbols $S$.

Relators

Declare chosen words $R$ trivial by quotient.

Presentation

$\langle S \mid R \rangle = F(S) \,/\, \langle\!\langle R \rangle\!\rangle$

Meaning

Generators model loops; relators model identifications.

Printed pp. 233-250Group Presentations
A presentation names generators, takes the free group on them, then declares chosen relators trivial by passing to a quotient. Generators model loops while relators model identifications coming from overlaps or attaching maps. Equality of two words becomes a question about reductions together with inserted relators, exactly the surface-group bookkeeping we will soon meet.

Presentation Examples

Cyclic group

$\langle r \mid r^5 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/5$

Free abelian

$\langle a,b \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1} \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$

Printed pp. 233-250Presentation Examples
The presentation of r with relator r to the fifth folds the integer line into a five-cycle. The presentation of a and b with the commutator relator forces ab to equal ba, abelianizing the group to the integer lattice Z squared. These two patterns, cyclic wrapping and forced commutativity, recur throughout the surface computations ahead.

Free Abelian Groups and Rank

Order forgotten

Elements are finite integer vectors on a basis.

Rank

Number of basis elements; a change-of-basis invariant.

Free abelian group

$\mathbb{Z}\langle S\rangle, \quad \operatorname{rank} \mathbb{Z}^n = n$

Rank-nullity

$\operatorname{rank}(\text{domain}) = \operatorname{rank}(\text{image}) + \operatorname{rank}(\ker)$.

Printed pp. 233-250Free Abelian Groups and Rank
A free abelian group is the additive twin of a free group: order no longer matters, so each element is a finite integer vector on a basis. The number of basis elements is the rank, an invariant unchanged by change of basis. For finite rank this is simply the integer lattice Z to the n.

Abelianization

Abelianization

$w \mapsto \big(\textstyle\sum \exp_a, \sum \exp_b\big) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$

Loss

Order and commutators discarded; rank survives for homology.

Printed pp. 233-250Abelianization
Abelianization sends a reduced word to the vector recording the total exponent of each generator, discarding order and every commutator. Many distinct nonabelian words collapse onto a single lattice point, which is precisely the information loss that makes homological rank computations clean while erasing the finer fundamental-group structure.

Toolkit Recap

Free product $G * H$

Combine with no cross-relations.

Free group $F(S)$

Words with only inverse cancellation.

Presentation $\langle S\mid R\rangle$

Impose chosen relators by quotient.

Free abelian $\mathbb{Z}^n$

Additive rank after forgetting commutators.

Printed pp. 233-250Toolkit Recap
Free products combine without cross-relations, free groups generate words with only inverse cancellation, presentations impose chosen relators by quotient, and free abelian groups record additive rank after commutators are forgotten. Together these four tools supply every algebraic ingredient the Seifert-Van Kampen theorem will assemble in the next chapter.
