The Seifert-Van Kampen Theorem

Computing $\pi_1$ by decomposition: cut a space into overlapping open pieces

From invariant to machine

$\pi_1$ stops being computed one space at a time and becomes a tool for gluing.

The plan

Decompose $X = U \cup V$, read off each piece group, then quotient by overlap loops.

Printed pp. 251-276The Gluing Machine
This chapter turns the fundamental group from a one-space-at-a-time invariant into a gluing machine. When a path-connected space is built from two open pieces with path-connected overlap, its fundamental group is assembled from the piece groups plus exactly the identifications forced by overlap loops.
Introduction to Topological ManifoldsChapter 10

Statement of the Theorem

Hypotheses

$U,V$ open with $X=U\cup V$; $U$, $V$, and $U\cap V$ all path-connected; $p\in U\cap V$.

Watch the conditions

Drop path-connectivity of the overlap and the conclusion fails.

Conclusion

$\pi_1(X,p) \cong \pi_1(U,p) \ast_{\pi_1(U\cap V,p)} \pi_1(V,p)$

Reading

An amalgamated free product: glue the two groups along their shared subgroup.

Printed pp. 251-276Statement of the Theorem
State the hypotheses carefully: U, V, and their intersection must all be open and path-connected, with the base point in the overlap. Under these conditions the fundamental group of the union is the pushout, a free product amalgamated over the intersection group.

The Pushout of Groups

Induced square

Inclusions give $i_*, j_*$ from the overlap group into each piece group.

Universal property

$\pi_1(X) = \dfrac{\pi_1(U)\ast\pi_1(V)}{\langle\langle\, i_*(\gamma)\,j_*(\gamma)^{-1}\,\rangle\rangle}$

Printed pp. 251-276The Pushout of Groups
The induced inclusions give a commuting square of group homomorphisms. The fundamental group of X is the universal object completing that square: the free product of the two piece groups quotiented by the normal closure of all words comparing the two images of each overlap loop.

Why the Map is Surjective

Subdivide the loop

A Lebesgue number splits any loop so each short arc lies in $U$ or $V$.

Rebase the arcs

Connector paths to $p$ turn each arc into a based loop in one piece.

Printed pp. 251-276Why Surjectivity Holds
Any loop in X is subdivided by a Lebesgue number so each short arc lands in U or in V. Connector paths to the base point convert each arc into a based loop in one piece, so alternating letters from the two piece groups generate the whole fundamental group.

The Normal Closure Ledger

Two names, one loop

A loop $\gamma\subset U\cap V$ reads as a letter in $\pi_1(U)$ or in $\pi_1(V)$.

The only relation

These two letters must be identified; nothing else is imposed.

Relator family

$C = \big\{\, i_*(\gamma)\, j_*(\gamma)^{-1} : \gamma \in \pi_1(U\cap V) \,\big\}$

Quotient

$\pi_1(X) = \big(\pi_1(U)\ast\pi_1(V)\big)/\langle\langle C\rangle\rangle$ — the gluing ledger.

Printed pp. 251-276The Normal Closure Ledger
A loop living in the overlap has two names: one through U, one through V. These are different free-product letters but the same loop in X. The theorem says this is the only ambiguity, so we quotient by the normal closure of every comparison word.

The Proof-Square Sweep

Null-homotopy grid

Subdivide the homotopy square so each cell maps into $U$ or $V$.

Row by row

Push the bottom loop word up to the constant top edge.

Printed pp. 251-276The Proof-Square Sweep
For the kernel, take a null-homotopy of a trivial word and subdivide its square so each small cell maps into U or V. Sweep the bottom word upward row by row to the constant top edge; each crossing contributes exactly one overlap relation already in the normal closure.

Two Workhorse Special Cases

Simply connected overlap

$\pi_1(U\cap V)=1 \;\Rightarrow\; \pi_1(X)=\pi_1(U)\ast\pi_1(V)$

No glue

The overlap imposes no relation, so we get a plain free product.

One piece simply connected

$\pi_1(U)=1 \;\Rightarrow\; \pi_1(X)=\pi_1(V)/\langle\langle\, j_*(\gamma)\,\rangle\rangle$

Pure relations

$U$ adds no generators but its overlap loops can kill loops in $V$.

Printed pp. 251-276Two Special Cases
Two reductions do most of the work. If the overlap is simply connected, the union group is just the free product. If one piece is simply connected, it adds no generators but its overlap loops can still impose relations, turning the other piece group into a quotient.

Wedge of Circles → Free Group

Thicken the base

Use the nondegenerate base point to make the overlap contractible.

Result

$\pi_1\!\big(\textstyle\bigvee_{n} S^1\big) \cong F_n$

Printed pp. 251-276Wedges and the Free Group
For a wedge of circles, thicken each summand using the nondegenerate base point so the overlap becomes contractible. The simply connected intersection case then gives a free product, so the figure-eight has fundamental group the free group on two generators, and n circles give the free group of rank n.

Graphs: Tree Core + Chords

Spanning tree

A tree is contractible, so it contributes nothing to $\pi_1$.

Free rank

$\operatorname{rk}\,\pi_1(\Gamma) = E - V + 1$

Printed pp. 251-276Graphs and Spanning Trees
Every finite connected graph has a contractible spanning tree contributing nothing. The edges outside the tree are the loops that cannot collapse. With V vertices and E edges there are E minus V plus one chords, and that cycle rank equals the rank of the free fundamental group.

$\pi_1(S^n)=1$ for $n\ge 2$

Two caps

Cover $S^n$ by upper and lower open caps, each contractible.

Connected band

For $n\ge 2$ the equatorial overlap $\simeq S^{n-1}$ is path-connected.

Conclusion

$\pi_1(U)=\pi_1(V)=1 \;\Rightarrow\; \pi_1(S^n)=1$

Printed pp. 251-276Spheres are Simply Connected
Cover the n-sphere by two open caps overlapping in an equatorial band homotopy equivalent to a smaller sphere. For n at least two that band is path-connected and both caps are contractible, so the simply connected overlap case forces the fundamental group of the sphere to be trivial.

CW Complexes as Presentations

1-skeleton

Generators: free group from the graph after a spanning tree.

2-cells

Each attached disk adds its boundary word as a relator.

$n\ge 3$ cells

Leave $\pi_1$ unchanged: punctured overlap is simply connected.

Torus from a square

$\partial = aba^{-1}b^{-1} \;\Rightarrow\; \pi_1(T^2)=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}\rangle$

Printed pp. 251-276CW Complexes as Presentations
A finite CW complex computes its fundamental group in layers. The one-skeleton gives free generators after choosing a spanning tree, each attached two-cell adds its boundary word as a relator, and cells of dimension at least three leave the group unchanged because their punctured overlap is simply connected.

Compact Surface Groups

Orientable genus $g$

$\big\langle a_i,b_i \;\big|\; \prod_{i=1}^{g}[a_i,b_i] \big\rangle$

Abelianization

Commutators vanish: $\mathbb{Z}^{2g}$, free of even rank.

Nonorientable genus $n$

$\big\langle x_1,\dots,x_n \;\big|\; x_1^2 x_2^2\cdots x_n^2 \big\rangle$

Abelianization

$\mathbb{Z}^{\,n-1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2$ — a visible order-two element.

Printed pp. 251-276Compact Surface Groups
A surface polygon word becomes a group presentation: edges are generators, the single face is the relator. Orientable genus g gives a product of commutators with abelianization the integers to the 2g; nonorientable genus n gives a product of squares with abelianization carrying one order-two factor.

Recap: The Gluing Recipe

The theorem

$\pi_1(X)$ is a free product amalgamated over the overlap group.

Free pieces

Wedges and graphs give free groups of rank $E-V+1$.

Presentations

$1$-cells are generators, $2$-cells are relators, higher cells inert.

Surfaces

Polygon words yield the standard presentations; abelianization sorts the families.

Printed pp. 251-276Recap and Takeaways
The theorem is a controlled recipe: the free product records loops in either set, overlap relations identify the two names of a shared loop. Graphs give free groups, two-cells add relators, and surface polygon words yield the standard presentations whose abelianizations separate the compact surface families.
