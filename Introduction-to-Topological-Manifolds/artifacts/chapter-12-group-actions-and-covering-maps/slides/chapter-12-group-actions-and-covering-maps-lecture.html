Deck Transformations

Definition

A homeomorphism $\varphi$ of $E$ with $q\circ\varphi = q$.

Free action

If $E$ is connected, a deck map fixing one point is the identity.

Group

They form $\operatorname{Aut}_q(E)$ under composition.

Commuting square

$q \circ \varphi = q, \qquad \varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}_q(E)$

Why free

Unique lifting forces equality once one value matches.

Printed pp. 307-338Deck Transformations
A deck transformation is a self-homeomorphism of the covering space that commutes with the projection, so it permutes every fiber while leaving the base fixed. These automorphisms form a group under composition. They act freely whenever the covering space is connected, because a deck map fixing one point must be the identity by the unique lifting property.

Normal (Regular) Coverings

Transitive sheets

The deck group acts transitively on every fiber.

Algebraic test

The image $q_*\pi_1(E)$ is normal in $\pi_1(X)$.

Normality criterion

$q_*\pi_1(E,e)\;\trianglelefteq\;\pi_1(X,x)$

Printed pp. 307-338Normal (Regular) Coverings
A connected covering is normal when its deck group acts transitively on each fiber, so any sheet can be carried to any other by an automorphism. Equivalently the image subgroup of the fundamental group is normal in the base group. The integer translations of the real line over the circle give the cleanest transitive example.

Monodromy: $\pi_1$ Acts on Fibers

Lift a loop

A loop at $x$ lifts to a path between fiber points.

Right action

This permutes the fiber $q^{-1}(x)$.

Stabilizer

Equals the image subgroup $q_*\pi_1(E,e)$.

Monodromy

$\pi_1(X,x)\;\curvearrowright\;q^{-1}(x)$

Transitive

When $E$ is connected, one orbit covers the fiber.

Printed pp. 307-338Monodromy and Fibers
Lifting loops based at a point permutes the fiber over that point, defining a right action of the fundamental group on the fiber. This monodromy action is transitive when the covering space is connected, and the stabilizer of a chosen lift is exactly the image subgroup. Deck transformations realize the part of monodromy that the automorphism group can see.

Properly Discontinuous Free Actions

Local disjointness

Each $x$ has $U$ with $\gamma U \cap U = \varnothing$ for $\gamma \neq e$.

Free

No nontrivial element fixes any point.

Covering space action

$\gamma U \cap U \neq \varnothing \;\Rightarrow\; \gamma = e$

Printed pp. 307-338Covering Space Actions
A covering space action asks each point to have a neighborhood whose nontrivial group translates miss it. Combined with freeness this local disjointness is precisely the collision test that makes the orbit map a covering. Every group element becomes a deck transformation, so the acting group sits inside the automorphism group of the quotient projection.

The Orbit Quotient $E/\Gamma$

Theorem

A covering space action makes $E \to E/\Gamma$ a normal covering.

Deck group

The acting group $\Gamma$ is the full deck group.

Orbit covering

$\operatorname{Aut}_{\pi}(E)\;\cong\;\Gamma, \qquad \pi:E\to E/\Gamma$

Proof idea

Disjoint translates of $U$ become evenly covered sheets; each $\gamma$ is a deck map.

Printed pp. 307-338The Orbit Quotient
When a group acts on a connected, locally path-connected space as a covering space action, the orbit projection onto the quotient is a normal covering whose deck group is exactly the acting group. The proof threads disjoint translates of a neighborhood into evenly covered sheets, then identifies automorphisms with the unique group element relating two lifts.

$\mathbb{Z}$ acts on $\mathbb{R}$ giving $S^1$

Translations

$T_k(x)=x+k$ acts freely and properly.

Quotient

$\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\cong S^1$ via $x\mapsto e^{2\pi i x}$.

Deck group

$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{R}\to S^1)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}$

Printed pp. 307-338The Line over the Circle
Integer translations act on the real line as a free covering space action because a short interval and its integer shifts are pairwise disjoint. The quotient is the circle, the projection is the exponential covering, and the deck group is the integers. Each fiber is an evenly spaced orbit, and translations move any lift to any other lift transitively.

$\mathbb{Z}^2$ acts on $\mathbb{R}^2$ giving $T^2$

Lattice tiling

$\mathbb{Z}^2$ tiles the plane by unit squares.

Fundamental domain

One closed square glues opposite edges to the torus.

Deck group

$\pi_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$ acts by translations.

Printed pp. 307-338The Plane over the Torus
The integer lattice translates the plane, tiling it by unit squares that are pairwise disjoint fundamental domains. The quotient is the torus, the deck group is the rank-two lattice, and a single closed square glues opposite edges to recover the surface. This is the planar picture every learner should keep in mind for the universal cover of the torus.

Antipodal $\mathbb{Z}/2$ on $S^n$ giving $\mathbb{RP}^n$

Antipodal map

$a(p)=-p$ is free: no $p$ equals $-p$.

Quotient

$S^n/\{\pm 1\}\cong\mathbb{RP}^n$, a two-sheeted cover.

Universal cover

$\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^n)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2 \quad (n\ge 2)$

Printed pp. 307-338The Sphere over Projective Space
The antipodal map pairs each point of the sphere with its negative, generating a free two-element action since no point equals its own antipode. The orbit space is real projective space, and the projection is a two-sheeted normal covering. Because the sphere is simply connected for dimension at least two, it serves as the universal cover of projective space.

The Universal Cover and $\Gamma\cong\pi_1$

Simply connected

The universal cover $\widetilde{X}$ has trivial $\pi_1$.

Deck group

So the deck group is all of $\pi_1(X)$.

Recovering the base

$X\;\cong\;\widetilde{X}/\pi_1(X), \qquad \operatorname{Aut}(\widetilde{X})\cong\pi_1(X)$

Action

$\pi_1(X)$ acts freely and properly discontinuously by deck maps.

Printed pp. 307-338The Universal Cover
When the universal cover exists its total space is simply connected, so the image subgroup is trivial and the deck group equals the whole fundamental group of the base. The base is therefore recovered as the universal cover quotiented by its fundamental group acting freely and properly discontinuously by deck transformations.

Classification by Subgroups of $\pi_1$

Based coverings

Match subgroups $H\le\pi_1(X)$ exactly.

Unbased coverings

Match conjugacy classes of subgroups.

Normal coverings

Correspond to normal subgroups; deck group $=\pi_1(X)/H$.

Printed pp. 307-338Classification of Coverings
Connected coverings of a reasonable space correspond to subgroups of the fundamental group through the image of the lift. Based isomorphism classes match subgroups exactly, while unbased classes match conjugacy classes, since moving the basepoint around the fiber conjugates the subgroup. Normal subgroups single out the normal coverings whose deck groups are quotient groups.

Recap and Manifold Quotients

Symmetry view

Deck groups, normal coverings, monodromy, orbit quotients.

Classification

Conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(X)$.

Manifold quotient

free $+$ properly discontinuous $+$ Hausdorff $\Rightarrow$ manifold $E/\Gamma$

Examples

$\mathbb{R}\to S^1$, $\;\mathbb{R}^2\to T^2$, $\;S^n\to\mathbb{RP}^n$.

Printed pp. 307-338Recap
We turned coverings into quotients by symmetry: deck groups, normal coverings, monodromy, orbit constructions, and the subgroup classification. For manifolds, free plus properly discontinuous gives a manifold quotient once the orbit space is Hausdorff, which properness guarantees. The line, the plane, and the sphere illustrated the universal cover quotient throughout the chapter.
