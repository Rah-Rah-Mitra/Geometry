Homotopy of Maps

One map on a product

Time deforms $f$ continuously into $g$.

Equivalence relation

Reflexive, symmetric, transitive on maps.

Definition

$H:X\times I\to Y,\ H(x,0)=f(x),\ H(x,1)=g(x)$

Proof moves

Reverse time for symmetry; glue at $t=\tfrac12$ for transitivity.

Printed pp. 183-216Homotopy of Maps
A homotopy is a single continuous map on the product with the unit interval, not a loose sequence of pictures. The time coordinate sweeps one map continuously into another. We verify reflexivity, symmetry by reversing time, and transitivity by gluing two homotopies along a shared middle slice.

Path Homotopy rel Endpoints

Fixed endpoints

Left edge stays at $p$, right edge at $q$, for all $t$.

Codomain matters

Deleting a point can break an otherwise valid deformation.

Printed pp. 183-216Path Homotopy rel Endpoints
Path homotopy adds two boundary conditions to the homotopy square: the left edge stays at the initial point and the right edge at the terminal point for all time. The codomain matters, since deleting a point can destroy a deformation that was valid in the whole plane, a recurring warning.

Product of Paths

Double speed

Follow $f$ on $[0,\tfrac12]$, then $g$ on $[\tfrac12,1]$.

Gluing lemma

Continuous because $f(1)=g(0)$.

Concatenation

$(f*g)(s)=\begin{cases}f(2s)&s\le\tfrac12\\ g(2s-1)&s\ge\tfrac12\end{cases}$

Caution

Strict at representative level; group laws need classes.

Printed pp. 183-216Product of Paths
Concatenation runs the first path at double speed, then the second, gluing where the terminal point meets the initial point. The gluing lemma supplies continuity. This representative-level product is the raw material whose homotopy classes will satisfy the group axioms after we quotient out reparametrization.

The Fundamental Group

Loops at $x_0$

Paths with $f(0)=f(1)=x_0$.

Classes

Identify path-homotopic loops; multiply by $*$.

Printed pp. 183-216The Fundamental Group
Fixing a base point, we take loops, identify path-homotopic ones, and multiply classes by concatenation. The resulting set of classes under this product is the fundamental group. It records how loops fail to contract, turning continuous topology into a computable algebraic group attached to the pointed space.

Verifying the Group Axioms

Associativity

$(f*g)*h\simeq f*(g*h)$ after reparametrization.

Identity & inverse

Constant loop $c_{x_0}$; reverse path $\bar f$.

Group $\pi_1(X,x_0)$

$[f][\,\bar f\,]=[c_{x_0}],\quad [c_{x_0}][f]=[f]$

Why classes

Lemma 7.9: reparametrizing preserves path class.

Printed pp. 183-216Verifying the Group Axioms
Products of paths are not literally associative because the bracketings spend different fractions of the interval on each piece. Lemma 7.9 says reparametrization preserves path class, so associativity holds after quotienting. The constant loop is the identity and the reversed path is the inverse, completing every group axiom.

Change of Base Point

Travel path

A path $g$ from $p$ to $q$ conjugates loops.

Depends on $g$

The points alone do not fix the isomorphism.

Isomorphism

$\Phi_g:\pi_1(X,p)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\pi_1(X,q),\ [f]\mapsto[\bar g*f*g]$

Pointed maps

Why $\pi_1$ is built on pointed spaces.

Printed pp. 183-216Change of Base Point
In a path-connected space, a chosen path from p to q conjugates loops to give an isomorphism between the two fundamental groups. The isomorphism depends on the chosen path, not merely on the two points, since in nonabelian groups different paths can yield different conjugations of the same loop.

Induced Homomorphism and Functoriality

Compose with $\varphi$

A loop at $p$ maps to a loop at $\varphi(p)$.

Functor laws

$(\mathrm{id})_*=\mathrm{id}$ and $(\psi\circ\varphi)_*=\psi_*\circ\varphi_*$.

Induced map

$\varphi_*:\pi_1(X,p)\to\pi_1(Y,\varphi(p)),\ [f]\mapsto[\varphi\circ f]$

Homomorphism

$\varphi\circ(f*g)=(\varphi\circ f)*(\varphi\circ g)$.

Printed pp. 183-216Induced Homomorphism and Functoriality
A continuous map sends loops to loops by composition, descending to an induced homomorphism on fundamental groups. Identities induce identities and composites induce composites, so pi-one is a covariant functor from pointed spaces to groups. The homomorphism property follows from a literal equality of composed concatenations.

Homotopy Equivalence

Homotopy inverse

$\psi\circ\varphi\simeq\mathrm{id}_X$ and $\varphi\circ\psi\simeq\mathrm{id}_Y$.

Invariance

Equivalent spaces share $\pi_1$ up to isomorphism.

Theorem

$X\simeq Y\ \Rightarrow\ \pi_1(X,x_0)\cong\pi_1(Y,\varphi(x_0))$

Base-point fix

The homotopy traces a path correcting base points.

Printed pp. 183-216Homotopy Equivalence
Two spaces are homotopy equivalent when continuous maps compose, in both orders, to maps homotopic to the identities. Homotopy invariance then forces the induced maps on fundamental groups to be isomorphisms, with base-point corrections supplied by the path that the homotopy traces between the two image base points.

Deformation Retraction of the Punctured Plane

Radial slide

Push each $x\ne0$ straight onto the unit circle.

Strong

Circle points stay fixed; nothing crosses $0$.

Printed pp. 183-216Deformation Retraction of the Punctured Plane
The radial homotopy slides each nonzero point straight to the unit circle while fixing points already on the circle and never crossing the origin. This strong deformation retraction exhibits the inclusion of the circle and the radial map as homotopy inverses, so the punctured plane and the circle share a fundamental group.

Simply Connected and Contractible Spaces

Simply connected

Path-connected with $\pi_1(X)\cong\{1\}$.

Contractible

$\mathrm{id}_X$ homotopic to a constant map.

Spheres

$\pi_1(S^n)\cong\{1\}\quad(n\ge 2)$

Proof idea

Subdivide, nudge off a point, project stereographically.

Printed pp. 183-216Simply Connected and Contractible Spaces
A space is simply connected when it is path-connected with trivial fundamental group, and contractible when its identity map is homotopic to a constant. Spheres of dimension at least two are simply connected, proved by subdividing a loop finely, nudging it off a point, and projecting stereographically.
