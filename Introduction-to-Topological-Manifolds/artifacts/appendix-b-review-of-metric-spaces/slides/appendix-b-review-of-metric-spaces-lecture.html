The distance-driven warm-up for point-set topology

Why review this

Topology abstracts the open-set behavior of metric spaces. Manifolds are tested by continuity, compactness, and local Euclidean structure.

The plan

Axioms → balls → open sets → convergence → continuity → completeness → compactness.

The Metric Axioms

Positivity

$d(x,y)\ge 0$, with equality exactly when $x=y$.

Symmetry

$d(x,y)=d(y,x)$; distance does not care about direction.

Triangle inequality

$d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$; no detour is shorter.

Definition

$d:M\times M\to[0,\infty)$

A metric space is a set $M$ with such a $d$. Coordinates, addition, and dot products may all be absent.

What survives

These three axioms alone build balls, open and closed sets, limits, and continuity.

Printed pp. 395-400The Metric Axioms
A metric keeps only the distance part of geometry, discarding addition and coordinates. We state positivity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality, stressing that these three conditions alone are strong enough to generate the entire local language of balls, open sets, limits, and continuity used throughout the book.

Four Metrics, Four Balls

The same center and radius, four different neighborhoods

Open ball

$B_r(x)=\{y\in M: d(x,y)<r\}$

Closed ball

$\bar B_r(x)=\{y: d(x,y)\le r\}$

The lesson

The invariant is not the shape but the radius rule. Different metrics give different neighborhoods, so topology keeps the sets, not the formula.

Printed pp. 395-400Four Metrics, Four Balls
The unit ball changes shape with the metric even on the same plane: round under Euclidean, diamond under the taxicab norm, square under the sup metric, and a lone point under the discrete metric. This is the cleanest motivation for abstracting topology away from any single distance formula.

The Triangle Inequality at Work

Key inequality

$d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$

Why balls are open

If $y\in B_r(x)$, set $s=r-d(x,y)>0$. Any $z\in B_s(y)$ satisfies $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)<r$.

So

Every point of an open ball owns a smaller ball inside it — openness for free.

Printed pp. 395-400The Triangle Inequality at Work
The triangle inequality is the workhorse that makes open balls open. We trace the estimate for a point y inside a ball: any z near enough to y stays inside because its distance to the center is bounded by the detour through y. This one inequality powers openness, limits, and continuity.

Open Sets and the Metric Topology

Local test

$U$ is open if every $x\in U$ has some $B_r(x)\subseteq U$.

Closure properties

Arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets are open; $\varnothing$ and $M$ are open.

Metric topology

$\mathcal{T}=\{U\subseteq M: U \text{ open}\}$

Printed pp. 395-400Open Sets and the Metric Topology
Openness is a purely local containment test: every point owns a ball that fits inside the set. Arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets stay open, so the open balls generate a topology. This collection is exactly the structure topology later abstracts, discarding the metric itself.

Closed Sets and Sequence Limits

Definition

$F$ is closed when $M\setminus F$ is open.

Sequential test

In a metric space, $F$ is closed iff every convergent sequence in $F$ keeps its limit in $F$.

Boundedness

A set is bounded if its diameter $\sup d(x,y)$ is finite; equivalently it fits inside some ball.

Closed via limits

$x_n\in F,\ x_n\to x \;\Rightarrow\; x\in F$

Why it holds

If the limit landed in the open complement, a ball there would trap the tail outside $F$ — a contradiction.

Printed pp. 395-400Closed Sets and Sequence Limits
A set is closed when its complement is open, but in metric spaces there is a second reliable test: a closed set contains the limit of every convergent sequence drawn from it. This sequential characterization is the tool analysts and topologists reach for constantly when reasoning about limiting constructions.

Convergence and Cauchy Tails

Convergence

$\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall n\ge N:\ d(x_n,x)<\varepsilon$

Cauchy

$\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall m,n\ge N:\ d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon$

One-way implication

Convergent $\Rightarrow$ Cauchy, by the triangle inequality. The converse needs completeness.

Printed pp. 395-400Convergence and Cauchy Tails
Convergence names a destination: every epsilon-ball about the limit eventually swallows the tail. The Cauchy condition mentions no destination, only that the tail tightens in diameter. Every convergent sequence is Cauchy, but the converse can fail, which is precisely the gap completeness is invented to close.

Continuity: Two Equivalent Faces

Epsilon-delta

$\forall\varepsilon\,\exists\delta:\ d_M(x',x)<\delta\Rightarrow d_N(f(x'),f(x))<\varepsilon$

Open-preimage

$U\subseteq N \text{ open} \;\Rightarrow\; f^{-1}(U) \text{ open}$

Same argument

One radius statement read in two directions: target balls pull back to domain balls.

Printed pp. 395-400Continuity: Two Equivalent Faces
Metric continuity has a pointwise epsilon-delta form and a global open-preimage form, and they are the same radius argument read in opposite directions. We sketch how continuity converts target balls into domain balls, and how that conversion makes preimages of open sets open, bridging analysis to topology.

Uniform Continuity

Pointwise

For continuity, $\delta$ may depend on both $\varepsilon$ and the point $x$.

Uniform

For uniform continuity, one $\delta$ must work for every $x$ at once.

Compact domains

A continuous map on a compact metric space is automatically uniformly continuous.

Uniform continuity

$\forall\varepsilon\,\exists\delta\,\forall x,x':\ d_M(x,x')<\delta\Rightarrow d_N(f(x),f(x'))<\varepsilon$

Quantifier order

The single moved quantifier — $\delta$ chosen before $x$ — is the whole difference.

Printed pp. 395-400Uniform Continuity
Uniform continuity strengthens ordinary continuity by demanding a single delta that works at every point for a given epsilon. We contrast pointwise continuity, where delta may shrink as the point moves, with the uniform version, and recall that continuous maps on compact metric spaces are automatically uniformly continuous.

Completeness

Definition

every Cauchy $(x_n)$ converges in $M$

Complete

$\mathbb{R}^n$ with the Euclidean metric: every Cauchy sequence has a limit.

Not complete

$(0,1)$ omits the endpoint; $\mathbb{Q}$ omits irrational limits like $\sqrt{2}$.

Inheritance

A closed subset of a complete metric space is itself complete.

Printed pp. 395-400Completeness
A metric space is complete when every Cauchy sequence actually converges inside it. Euclidean space is complete, while the open interval and the rationals are not, since they omit limits their own Cauchy data demand. Closed subsets of complete spaces inherit completeness, a fact used repeatedly later.

Compactness as Finite Control

Sequential

Every sequence has a subsequence converging to a point of the space.

Total boundedness

For every $\varepsilon$, finitely many $\varepsilon$-balls cover the space — a finite $\varepsilon$-net.

Metric equivalence

Compact $\iff$ complete $+$ totally bounded $\iff$ sequentially compact.

Printed pp. 395-400Compactness as Finite Control
In metric spaces compactness fuses sequential compactness with total boundedness and completeness: every sequence has a convergent subsequence, and finite epsilon-nets cover the space at every scale. Closed bounded subsets of Euclidean space are the model examples; the open interval leaks a limit and so fails.

Recap and Bridge to Topology

The chain

Axioms build balls; balls build open sets; open sets define convergence and continuity.

Limit behavior

Cauchy tails plus finite $\varepsilon$-nets govern completeness and compactness.

The abstraction

Topology keeps the open sets and discards the metric — the same neighborhoods, no distance.

Printed pp. 395-400Recap and Bridge to Topology
We recap the dependency chain: axioms generate balls, balls generate open sets, open sets define convergence and continuity, and Cauchy behavior plus finite nets govern completeness and compactness. The next chapters keep the open-set behavior and discard the metric, which is exactly the abstraction topology performs.
