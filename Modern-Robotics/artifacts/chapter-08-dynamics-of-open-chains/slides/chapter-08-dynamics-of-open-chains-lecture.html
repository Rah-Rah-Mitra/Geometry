Inverse vs Forward Dynamics

Inverse dynamics

Given motion $\theta,\dot\theta,\ddot\theta$, find the torques $\tau$. Drives control and tracking.

Forward dynamics

Given torques $\tau$, predict the acceleration $\ddot\theta$. Drives simulation.

Two queries, same ingredients

$\tau = \mathrm{ID}(\theta,\dot\theta,\ddot\theta)$

$\ddot\theta = \mathrm{FD}(\theta,\dot\theta,\tau)$

Shared parts

Mass matrix, velocity-product term, and gravity loading appear in both.

Printed pp. 271-324Inverse vs Forward Dynamics
Open-chain dynamics answers two complementary queries built from the same ingredients. Inverse dynamics is given the motion and returns the torques that produce it, while forward dynamics is given the torques and predicts acceleration. Inverse drives control and trajectory tracking; forward drives simulation, and each reuses the same mass, velocity, and gravity terms.

The Manipulator Equation

Equations of motion

$\tau = M(\theta)\,\ddot\theta + c(\theta,\dot\theta) + g(\theta)$

Inertial term

$M(\theta)\ddot\theta$ resists angular acceleration.

Velocity-product

$c(\theta,\dot\theta)$ collects Coriolis and centripetal forces, quadratic in $\dot\theta$.

Gravity

$g(\theta)$ is the static torque needed to hold the pose.

Printed pp. 271-324The Manipulator Equation
The manipulator equation collects every effect into three labelled terms. The mass matrix multiplies acceleration, the velocity-product vector gathers Coriolis and centripetal coupling, and the gravity vector loads the joints at rest. Reading torque as the sum of these parts makes the structure of robot dynamics inspectable rather than a wall of symbols.

The Mass Matrix is a Metric

Kinetic energy

$\mathcal{K} = \tfrac12\,\dot\theta^\top M(\theta)\,\dot\theta > 0$

Positive definite

$M(\theta)=M(\theta)^\top \succ 0$: energy never collapses along a velocity direction.

Configuration dependence

Folding links moves masses toward or away from joints, changing $M$.

Printed pp. 271-324Mass Matrix as a Metric
Kinetic energy is a quadratic form in joint velocity, and the symmetric positive-definite mass matrix is its metric. Its smallest eigenvalue measuring how cheaply the lightest motion direction accelerates must stay positive, confirming energy never collapses along any joint-velocity direction. The matrix changes as links fold because masses move nearer or farther from joints.

Christoffel Symbols and the Coriolis Term

Velocity-product coefficients

$c_i = \sum_{j,k}\Gamma_{ijk}(\theta)\,\dot\theta_j\,\dot\theta_k$

Christoffel symbol of $M$

$\Gamma_{ijk}=\tfrac12\!\left(\tfrac{\partial M_{ij}}{\partial\theta_k}+\tfrac{\partial M_{ik}}{\partial\theta_j}-\tfrac{\partial M_{jk}}{\partial\theta_i}\right)$

Geometric origin

Coriolis and centripetal forces come directly from how inertia varies with $\theta$.

Constant inertia

If $M$ is constant, all $\Gamma_{ijk}=0$ and the velocity term vanishes.

Quadratic in rate

Centripetal $\propto\dot\theta_j^2$; Coriolis $\propto\dot\theta_j\dot\theta_k$ for $j\neq k$.

Printed pp. 271-324Christoffel Symbols and Coriolis
The velocity-product term is not arbitrary; its coefficients are the Christoffel symbols of the mass matrix, built from partial derivatives of its entries. This ties Coriolis and centripetal forces directly to how inertia varies with configuration. When the mass matrix is constant the symbols vanish and these velocity-coupling forces disappear entirely from the equation.

Spatial Inertia, Twists, and Momentum

Spatial inertia (6x6)

$\mathcal{G}_b=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{I}_b & 0\\ 0 & m\,I\end{bmatrix}$

Spatial momentum

$\mathcal{P}_b=\mathcal{G}_b\,\mathcal{V}_b$

Printed pp. 271-324Spatial Inertia and Twists
The recursive formulation lives in SE(3) using twists and wrenches. Each link carries a six-by-six spatial inertia matrix combining rotational inertia and mass. A twist packages angular and linear velocity into one screw, and the spatial momentum is the spatial inertia applied to that twist, giving a single covariant momentum object per link.

Recursive Newton-Euler: Forward Pass

Propagate motion outward

From the fixed base to the tip, compute each link's twist and acceleration.

Twist recursion

$\mathcal{V}_i=\mathrm{Ad}_{T_{i,i-1}}\mathcal{V}_{i-1}+\mathcal{A}_i\,\dot\theta_i$

Acceleration

Differentiate, including the velocity-product bracket $[\mathcal{V}_i,\mathcal{A}_i\dot\theta_i]$.

Printed pp. 271-324Newton-Euler Forward Pass
The forward pass propagates motion outward from the base to the tip. Starting from the fixed base twist and acceleration, each joint adds its own contribution, and the adjoint map transports the parent twist into the child frame. By the end every link knows its twist and acceleration, ready for the force computation that follows.

Recursive Newton-Euler: Backward Pass

Wrench balance at link i

$\mathcal{F}_i=\mathrm{Ad}^\top_{T_{i+1,i}}\mathcal{F}_{i+1}+\mathcal{G}_i\dot{\mathcal{V}}_i-[\mathrm{ad}_{\mathcal{V}_i}]^\top\mathcal{G}_i\mathcal{V}_i$

Project onto the screw axis

$\tau_i=\mathcal{F}_i^\top\,\mathcal{A}_i$

Printed pp. 271-324Newton-Euler Backward Pass
The backward pass propagates wrenches inward from the tip to the base. At each link Newton-Euler equates the net wrench to spatial inertia times acceleration plus a velocity-product term, and adds the wrench transmitted from the child. Projecting each joint wrench onto its screw axis yields exactly the joint torque sought.

The Power-Pairing Invariant

Power is frame-invariant

$\mathcal{F}^\top\mathcal{V}=\big(\mathrm{Ad}_T^{-\top}\mathcal{F}\big)^\top\big(\mathrm{Ad}_T\,\mathcal{V}\big)$

Why it matters

Joint torques do work through rates; wrenches do work through twists. Transforms must preserve $\mathcal{F}\!\cdot\!\mathcal{V}$.

Twist transforms by $\mathrm{Ad}_T$

Velocity is pushed forward by the adjoint.

Wrench transforms by $\mathrm{Ad}_T^{-\top}$

Force is dual, so it uses the inverse transpose.

Diagnostic

A nonzero pairing error flags torque assigned to the wrong frame.

Printed pp. 271-324Power Pairing Invariant
Torques do work through joint rates and spatial wrenches do work through twists, and a correct frame transform must preserve that scalar power. The adjoint maps the twist while its inverse transpose maps the wrench, so their pairing is unchanged. Checking this invariant catches inverse-dynamics code that assigns torque to the wrong frame.

Forward Dynamics by Inverting the Equation

Solve for acceleration

$\ddot\theta=M(\theta)^{-1}\big(\tau-c(\theta,\dot\theta)-g(\theta)\big)$

Bias forces for free

Run inverse dynamics with $\ddot\theta=0$ to get $c+g$ in one pass.

Composite rigid-body

Builds $M(\theta)$ column by column from the recursion.

Articulated-body

Computes $\ddot\theta$ directly in linear time without forming $M^{-1}$.

Then integrate

Step $\ddot\theta\to\dot\theta\to\theta$ to simulate the robot forward.

Printed pp. 271-324Forward Dynamics by Inversion
Forward dynamics inverts the manipulator equation. Run inverse dynamics with zero acceleration to obtain the bias forces, subtract them from applied torque, then solve the mass-matrix linear system for acceleration. The composite rigid-body and articulated-body algorithms compute the mass matrix and its inverse action efficiently using the same recursive chain structure.

Task-Space (Operational-Space) Inertia

Map dynamics through the Jacobian

$\Lambda(\theta)=J^{-\top}M(\theta)\,J^{-1}$

Near singularities

$J$ loses rank, $\Lambda$ becomes ill-conditioned, and apparent endpoint mass blows up.

Printed pp. 271-324Task-Space Inertia
Mapping dynamics through the Jacobian gives the operational-space formulation, where the task-space inertia is the Jacobian-inverse-transpose times mass times Jacobian-inverse. Near singularities the Jacobian loses directions and this inertia becomes ill-conditioned, so manipulability warns when the apparent endpoint mass blows up along lost directions of motion.

Actuators, Motor Inertia, and Gearing

Reflected rotor inertia

$M_{\text{eff}} = M(\theta) + G^2 I_{\text{rotor}}$

Squared scaling

A gear ratio $G$ scales reflected inertia by $G^2$; high ratios let the motor dominate.

Apparent inertia reshaped

Gearing makes the link feel heavier or lighter at the actuator.

Pitfall

Ignoring rotor inertia makes fast motions look unrealistically cheap.

Friction too

Joint friction and damping add further torque the controller must supply.

Printed pp. 271-324Actuators and Gearing
Real joints carry motor inertia and gearing that reshape apparent dynamics. A gear ratio scales reflected rotor inertia by its square, so high ratios make the actuator dominate the link inertia. Ignoring this term makes aggressive motions look unrealistically cheap, hiding the true torque and energy cost of fast acceleration.

Recap and Takeaways

Dynamics adds a metric

Kinematics says where; the mass matrix $M(\theta)$ adds how much effort each motion costs.

Algorithms are structured passes

Newton-Euler: outward for motion, inward for force, linear in the number of joints.

One equation to remember

$\tau = M(\theta)\ddot\theta + c(\theta,\dot\theta) + g(\theta)$

Coordinates vs physics

The same energy looks different in different coordinates; the invariant power is what is real.

Printed pp. 271-324Recap and Takeaways
Two ideas carry the chapter. Dynamics adds a configuration-dependent metric, the mass matrix, on top of kinematics, and the efficient algorithms are simply structured passes through the chain. Inverse and forward dynamics share ingredients, Newton-Euler recursion computes them in linear time, and coordinate choice changes appearance but never the underlying physics.
