The Forward Kinematics Map

Inputs

An open chain of $n$ joints with values $\theta=(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_n)$.

Output

One rigid configuration of the end-effector frame in the fixed frame.

Why it matters

Every plan, simulation, and controller starts from this map.

Configuration map

$$T:\ \mathbb{R}^n \to SE(3),\quad \theta \mapsto T(\theta)$$

Group structure

$T(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}R & p\\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$, with $R\in SO(3)$, $p\in\mathbb{R}^3$.

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188The Forward Kinematics Map
We define the map from joint coordinates to the end-effector frame as an element of the special Euclidean group. Each joint contributes a constrained one-parameter motion, and forward kinematics asks how those individual motions stack to produce the observable pose of the gripper frame in the fixed base frame.

Screw Axes and Twists

Twist

A screw axis pairs angular $\omega$ with linear $v$: $\mathcal{S}=(\omega,v)$.

Revolute

Rotation about a line: $\|\omega\|=1$, $v=-\omega\times q$.

Prismatic

Translation along a direction: $\omega=0$, $\|v\|=1$.

Bracket in se(3)

$$[\mathcal{S}]=\begin{bmatrix}[\omega] & v\\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\in se(3)$$

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Screw Axes and Twists
A screw axis packages an angular and a linear part into a unit twist, and its matrix bracket lives in the Lie algebra se(3). Revolute joints rotate about a line; prismatic joints translate along a direction. Exponentiating the scaled twist then yields the joint's finite rigid displacement.

The Matrix Exponential

One joint, one motion

Scaling a twist by $\theta$ and exponentiating gives a transform.

Rotation block

Rodrigues' formula builds $R(\theta)$ from $[\omega]$.

Translation block

The off-axis sweep gives $p(\theta)$.

Exponential of a twist

$$e^{[\mathcal{S}]\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}e^{[\omega]\theta} & * \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\in SE(3)$$

Rodrigues (rotation)

$$e^{[\omega]\theta}=I+\sin\theta\,[\omega]+(1-\cos\theta)[\omega]^2$$

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188The Matrix Exponential
The matrix exponential sends a twist times its joint value to a homogeneous transform. For a revolute axis the rotation block uses Rodrigues' formula and the translation block captures the off-axis sweep. This single map converts every joint's instantaneous screw into a finite, composable rigid motion.

Home Configuration $M$

Definition

$M=T(0)$: the end-effector frame when all joint values are zero.

Anchor

Every product of exponentials is built around $M$.

Read off the drawing

Choose $M$ so screw axes are easy to extract.

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Home Configuration M
The home configuration M records the end-effector frame relative to the fixed frame when all joint values are zero. Every product of exponentials is anchored to M; choosing it carefully removes bookkeeping and makes the screw axes easy to read straight off the mechanism drawing rather than computing them.

Space-Frame Product of Exponentials

Fixed-frame screws

Each $\mathcal{S}_i$ is the joint axis expressed in the base frame.

Order

Multiply from the first joint outward, on the left of $M$.

Constant data

Only the space screws and $M$ are needed.

Space form

$$T(\theta)=e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}e^{[\mathcal{S}_2]\theta_2}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_n]\theta_n}\,M$$

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Space-Frame PoE
The space-frame product of exponentials multiplies exponentials of the fixed-frame screw axes on the left of the home pose, ordered from the first joint outward. Each axis is expressed once in the base frame, so the formula needs only those constant space screws and the home configuration M.
Modern RoboticsChapter 4

Body-Frame Product of Exponentials

Body screws

Each $\mathcal{B}_i$ is the joint axis in the end-effector frame.

Order

$M$ comes first; multiply factors on its right.

Equivalence

$\mathcal{B}_i=[\mathrm{Ad}_{M^{-1}}]\mathcal{S}_i$; same pose, opposite order.

Body form

$$T(\theta)=M\,e^{[\mathcal{B}_1]\theta_1}e^{[\mathcal{B}_2]\theta_2}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{B}_n]\theta_n}$$

When to prefer it

Convenient when axes are naturally described in the moving gripper frame.

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Body-Frame PoE
The body-frame formula places the home pose first and multiplies exponentials of end-effector-frame screw axes on its right. It is equivalent to the space form but composes in the opposite order, which is convenient when the joint axes are most naturally described in the moving gripper frame.

Worked Example: Planar 3R Arm

Setup

Three revolute joints, axes out of plane: $\omega_i=\hat z$.

Home pose

$M$ places the tip at $x=L_1+L_2+L_3$ along the base axis.

Closed-form tip

$$x=\textstyle\sum_k L_k\cos\!\big(\textstyle\sum_{i\le k}\theta_i\big)$$

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Planar 3R Worked Example
For a planar three-revolute arm every screw axis points out of the plane and the home pose places the tip at the summed link lengths. We assemble the three exponentials and read off the closed-form tip position, matching the notebook's numerical endpoint check for lengths one, three-quarters, and forty-five hundredths.

Spatial Arm: UR5 / PUMA

Six axes

Tabulate six space screws $\mathcal{S}_1,\dots,\mathcal{S}_6$ from the geometry.

Home pose

One $M$ for the gripper frame at $\theta=0$.

Reuse

The same screws become Jacobian columns next chapter.

Six-factor product

$$T(\theta)=\Big(\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{6} e^{[\mathcal{S}_i]\theta_i}\Big)\,M$$

Bookkeeping

Consistent screw definitions make the spatial case no harder than planar.

A six-joint spatial arm such as the UR5 or PUMA uses six space screw axes plus a home pose. We tabulate each axis from the geometry, then form the six-factor product. The same data later drives the Jacobian, so consistent screw bookkeeping pays off twice across the kinematics chapters.

PoE vs. Denavit-Hartenberg

Denavit-Hartenberg

Four parameters per link, tied to per-link intermediate frames.

Cost

Fragile frame assignment; different rules for prismatic joints.

Product of exponentials

Screw axes plus one home pose $M$; no intermediate frames.

Benefit

Uniform for revolute and prismatic; carries full screw vectors.

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Comparison with Denavit-Hartenberg
Denavit-Hartenberg parametrizes each link with four numbers tied to per-link frames, while product of exponentials needs only screw axes and one home pose. PoE avoids fragile intermediate-frame assignment and represents revolute and prismatic joints uniformly, at the cost of carrying full six-vector screws for each joint.

Order, Pitfalls, and the URDF

Order matters

Swapping factors changes the pose: $SE(3)$ is non-commutative.

Drawing is not data

A link-frame sketch cannot replace numeric screw-axis values.

URDF

A robot description is reliable only when geometry and axes agree.

Non-commutativity

$$e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}e^{[\mathcal{S}_2]\theta_2}\ \neq\ e^{[\mathcal{S}_2]\theta_2}e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}$$

Habit

Name the representation and its invariant at the same time.

Printed pp. 137-170 / PDF pp. 155-188Order, Pitfalls, and the URDF
Joint order matters: swapping two factors changes the pose because SE(3) is non-commutative. A link-frame drawing is not a substitute for screw-axis data, and a robot description such as a URDF is reliable only when its declared geometry and joint axes genuinely agree with each other.
