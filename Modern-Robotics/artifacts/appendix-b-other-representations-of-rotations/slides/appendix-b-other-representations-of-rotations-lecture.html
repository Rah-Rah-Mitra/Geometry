The Invariant Object: SO(3)

Rotation group

Orthonormal columns, determinant one. A length- and angle-preserving linear map.

Curved manifold

SO(3) is three dimensional but not flat, so no three numbers chart it without a singularity.

Definition

$R^{\top}R = I,\qquad \det R = +1$

Why many charts

Topology forbids a single global minimal chart, forcing the tradeoffs ahead.

Printed pp. 575-584The Invariant Object: SO(3)
Every representation in this appendix encodes one object: a rotation in SO(3), a real three by three matrix with orthonormal columns and determinant one. The group is a curved three dimensional manifold, so no single set of three numbers can chart it smoothly everywhere without introducing a coordinate singularity somewhere.

Euler Angles (ZYX)

Three turns

Factor a rotation into elementary turns about coordinate axes.

Body-fixed ZYX

$R = R_z(\alpha)\,R_y(\beta)\,R_x(\gamma)$

Convention bound

Meaning depends on axis order and on body vs. space frame.

Printed pp. 575-584Euler Angles (ZYX)
Euler angles factor a rotation into three elementary turns about coordinate axes. The ZYX convention rotates by yaw about z, then pitch about y, then roll about x. Three numbers give a minimal, human readable chart, but the meaning depends entirely on the chosen axis order and reference frame.

Roll-Pitch-Yaw

Space-fixed

Compose rotations about the fixed world axes, not the moving body axes.

World-frame order

$R = R_x(\text{roll})\,R_y(\text{pitch})\,R_z(\text{yaw})$

Intuitive

Natural for vehicles, aircraft, and cameras.

Printed pp. 575-584Roll-Pitch-Yaw
Roll-pitch-yaw is the space fixed cousin of Euler angles, composing rotations about the fixed world axes rather than the moving body axes. It is intuitive for vehicles and aircraft, yet it inherits the same three parameter structure and therefore the same unavoidable coordinate singularity at certain pitch values.

Gimbal Lock: The Three-Angle Singularity

Axes align

When the middle angle aligns two gimbals, the outer two rotations act about one line.

Degeneracy

$\beta = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\;\Rightarrow\;\partial_\alpha R \parallel \partial_\gamma R$

Not physical

The body still rotates freely; only the chart loses a degree of freedom.

Printed pp. 575-584Gimbal Lock
When the middle rotation aligns two of the gimbal axes, the first and third rotations turn about the same line and one degree of freedom collapses in the coordinates. This gimbal lock is a chart failure, not a physical one: the rigid body can still rotate freely in every direction.

Exponential Coordinates (Axis-Angle)

Axis and angle

One three vector $\hat\omega\theta$ stores rotation direction and amount.

Rodrigues' formula

$R = e^{[\hat\omega]\theta} = I + \sin\theta\,[\hat\omega] + (1-\cos\theta)[\hat\omega]^2$

Minimal & natural

Singular only at $\theta = 2\pi k$, where the axis is undefined.

Printed pp. 575-584Exponential Coordinates
Exponential coordinates pack the rotation axis and angle into a single three vector omega-hat times theta. The matrix exponential recovers the rotation through Rodrigues' formula. This chart is minimal and geometrically natural, singular only at angle multiples of two pi where the direction of the axis becomes undefined.

Unit Quaternions

Four numbers, one constraint

A point on the unit sphere $S^3$ in four dimensions.

Half-angle form

$q = \big(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2},\ \hat\omega\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\big),\quad \|q\|=1$

No gimbal lock

Smooth global container for attitude.

Printed pp. 575-584Unit Quaternions
A unit quaternion stores a rotation as four numbers constrained to the unit sphere in four dimensions. The scalar part is cosine of half the angle and the vector part is the axis scaled by sine of half the angle, giving a smooth global container that has no gimbal lock.
Modern RoboticsAppendix B

The Double Cover: $S^3 \to SO(3)$

Two to one

The unit 3-sphere covers SO(3) twice: $q$ and $-q$ are the same rotation.

Identification

$q \sim -q \;\Longrightarrow\; R(q) = R(-q)$

Watch interpolation

Sign flips create artificial jumps in plotted or interpolated signals.

Printed pp. 575-584The Double Cover
The unit three sphere double covers SO(3): the quaternions q and minus q name the exact same rotation. This two to one map explains why four constrained numbers represent a three dimensional group, and why naive interpolation must watch for sign flips along a trajectory.

Composition and Conversion

Compose by multiply

$q_2 \otimes q_1$ composes rotations cheaply and stably.

Quaternion to matrix

$R = I + 2q_0[\hat q] + 2[\hat q]^2$

Renormalize

Drift off $S^3$ is fixed by a single division.

Printed pp. 575-584Composition and Conversion
Quaternion multiplication composes rotations directly, costing far fewer operations than multiplying matrices and staying numerically stable under repeated products. A fixed bilinear formula converts any unit quaternion into its rotation matrix, so attitude can move freely between the two representations whenever a computation demands it.

Cayley-Rodrigues Parameters

Rational chart

Scale the axis by $\tan(\theta/2)$; build $R$ with the Cayley transform.

Cayley transform

$R = (I+[a])(I-[a])^{-1},\quad a = \tan\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\hat\omega$

Excludes $\pi$

$\|a\|\to\infty$ as $\theta\to\pi$: the chart warns at its boundary.

Printed pp. 575-584Cayley-Rodrigues Parameters
The Cayley-Rodrigues parameters scale the rotation axis by the tangent of half the angle, building the rotation from a rational Cayley transform without trigonometric functions. The chart is compact and algebraically clean, but its norm blows up at a rotation by pi, which the chart excludes entirely.

We close by comparing the charts on parameter count, singularities, and composition cost. Three angle conventions are minimal but lock; exponential coordinates are minimal and natural; quaternions are smooth and cheap to compose; Cayley parameters are rational yet exclude pi. The invariant is always the rotation, never the chart.
