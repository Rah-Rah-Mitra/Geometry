Wheels as Velocity Constraints

Roll

One contact direction is free to roll.

No slip

Another direction must not slide.

Printed pp. 513-564Wheels as Velocity Constraints
A wheel is not just an actuator; it declares which contact component rolls, which must not slip, and which becomes wheel angular speed. Changing only the wheel modules turns one rectangular chassis into several different robots, so we always read wheels as constraints on the chassis twist.

The Unicycle Model

Two controls

Forward speed $v$ and turning rate $\omega$.

No side-slip

Body lateral velocity is pinned to zero.

Kinematics

$\dot q = (v\cos\phi,\; v\sin\phi,\; \omega)$

Printed pp. 513-564The Unicycle Model
The unicycle is the canonical nonholonomic base. It commands forward speed and turning rate, while its body-sideways velocity is pinned to zero at every instant. Integrating these controls rotates forward motion through the heading, so curved paths still accumulate world-frame lateral displacement.

Differential Drive and the Car

Diff drive

Two wheel speeds give $v$ and $\omega$.

Car-like

Steering angle and wheelbase bound curvature.

Differential drive

$v=\tfrac{r}{2}(\dot\theta_R+\dot\theta_L),\;\; \omega=\tfrac{r}{d}(\dot\theta_R-\dot\theta_L)$

Car curvature

$\kappa = \dfrac{\tan\psi}{L}$

Printed pp. 513-564Differential Drive and the Car
Differential drive sets forward speed and yaw rate from two wheel speeds, recovering the unicycle twist. The car-like robot adds a steering angle and a wheelbase, so curvature is bounded. Both share the no-side-slip rolling constraint that defines nonholonomic motion.

The Rolling Constraint Is Pfaffian

Linear in velocity

A one-form annihilates side-slip.

Not integrable

It does not reduce configuration freedom.

Pfaffian constraint

$-\dot x\,\sin\phi + \dot y\,\cos\phi = 0$

Consequence

Config space stays 3D; only 2 velocity directions are free.

Printed pp. 513-564The Rolling Constraint Is Pfaffian
The no-side-slip condition is a Pfaffian constraint: linear in the velocities but not integrable to a position constraint. That non-integrability is exactly why the configuration space stays three dimensional while only two velocity directions are available at any instant.

Four wheel speeds reach all three twists.

Mecanum and omni wheels add passive rollers that let four wheel speeds span all three planar twist components. The wheel map then has rank three, so a least-squares inverse recovers a commanded chassis twist. No planar direction is missing in the ideal instantaneous kinematic model.

The Mecanum Wheel Map

Wheel map

$H$ sends body twist to four wheel rates.

Rank three

Wheels overconstrain planar motion.

Forward / inverse

$u = H\,\mathcal{V}_b, \qquad \mathcal{V}_b = H^{+}u$

Check

$\mathrm{rank}\,H = 3$; pseudo-inverse rebuilds compatible twists.

Printed pp. 513-564The Mecanum Wheel Map
The mecanum matrix H sends the body twist to four wheel rates and depends on length, width, and wheel radius. With rank three the four wheels overconstrain rather than underconstrain motion, and the pseudo-inverse reconstructs compatible twists to floating-point accuracy, which the notebook verifies numerically.

Odometry and Dead Reckoning

Integrate twists

Rotate body motion into the world frame.

Drift

Bad radii or signs amplify into pose error.

Update

$q_{k+1} = q_k + \Delta t\, R(\phi_k)\,\mathcal{V}_b$

Sanity

Unicycle rollout yields a nonzero path and matched heading.

Printed pp. 513-564Odometry and Dead Reckoning
Odometry integrates the running account of small twist decisions. Each step rotates body-frame motion into the world frame and accumulates heading. Wrong wheel radii, roller angles, or encoder signs quietly amplify into pose drift, so dead reckoning must be checked against both algebra and motion.

Controllability and Planning

Lie brackets

Combining moves generates the missing direction.

Parallel parking

Sideways poses need a maneuver.

Printed pp. 513-564Controllability and Planning
Even with no sideways velocity, a nonholonomic robot is controllable: Lie brackets of forward and turning motions generate the missing direction. That is why parallel parking needs a maneuver of short alternating moves rather than a single straight slide into the gap.

Feedback Control to a Pose

Error

Range, bearing, and heading to the target.

Command

Choose $v$ and $\omega$, not lateral velocity.

Polar control law

$v=k_\rho\,\rho, \qquad \omega=k_\alpha\,\alpha + k_\beta\,\beta$

Resolve laterally

Curvature and heading erase side error over time.

Printed pp. 513-564Feedback Control to a Pose
Feedback control steers a nonholonomic base toward a target pose or trajectory using errors in position and heading. Because instantaneous sideways motion is impossible, the controller commands forward speed and yaw rate, letting curvature and heading change resolve the lateral error over time.

Base plus arm
