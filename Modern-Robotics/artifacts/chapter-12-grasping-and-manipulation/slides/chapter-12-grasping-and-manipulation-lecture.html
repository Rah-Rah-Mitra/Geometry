The Chapter Question

What can contacts prevent, permit, or command?

Motion side

Each contact admits only certain relative twists: roll, slide, or break.

Force side

Each contact transmits only wrenches inside its friction-bounded cone.

Two dual views

$\text{twist } \mathcal{V} \;\;\perp\;\; \text{wrench } \mathcal{F}$

Closure

The unifying thread is to separate what a contact says about motion from what it says about force. Rolling, sliding, and breaking are motion modes; friction cones and wrench cones are force objects. Closure asks whether contacts together span every motion or every wrench the world might demand of the grasp.

Contact Kinematics

Unilateral normal

The contact resists penetration but cannot pull: a push-only constraint.

Three modes

Rolling, sliding, and breaking partition the local contact behavior.

Compatibility

$\hat n^{\mathsf T}\!\left(v_{\text{obj}} - v_{\text{hand}}\right) \ge 0$

Reading

Equality keeps contact; strict inequality opens it; negative is forbidden.

Printed pp. 461-512Contact Kinematics
A nonadhesive point contact forbids interpenetration along the normal but may open if bodies separate. The first-order condition signs the normal component of relative velocity, classifying each contact instant as rolling, sliding, or breaking. These local rules decide which relative twists between hand and object are kinematically admissible at that instant.

Roll, Slide, Break: First-Order Conditions

Mode by normal speed

$\dot d = \hat n^{\mathsf T}\Delta v$

  • Rolling: $\dot d = 0$, matched tangents.
  • Sliding: $\dot d = 0$, tangential slip.
  • Breaking: $\dot d > 0$ (separation).
  • Penetration: $\dot d < 0$ forbidden.
Printed pp. 461-512Roll, Slide, Break
Let the contact normal point into the object. The signed normal speed of approach determines the mode: zero with matched tangents means rolling, zero with relative tangential motion means sliding, and positive separation means the contact breaks. Penetration, a negative value, is physically forbidden by the unilateral nonpenetration constraint at the point.

The Coulomb Friction Cone

Coulomb law

$\lVert f_t\rVert \le \mu\, f_n, \qquad \alpha = \tan^{-1}\mu$

Inside vs boundary

Interior forces stick; boundary forces are at the verge of slipping.

Geometry

Friction turns a unilateral inequality into an admissible cone.

Printed pp. 461-512The Coulomb Friction Cone
Coulomb friction bounds the tangential force by the coefficient times the normal force, so admissible contact forces fill a cone about the surface normal. The half-angle is the friction angle, the arctangent of the coefficient. Any force inside the cone sticks; a force on the boundary is on the verge of sliding.

Linearizing the Friction Cone

Why linearize

A polyhedral cone makes closure a linear feasibility problem.

Inner approximation

Edge rays are chosen so their positive span lies inside the true cone.

Polyhedral cone

$f = \sum_{k} \lambda_k\, e_k,\qquad \lambda_k \ge 0$

Refinement

More edge rays approximate the curved cone more tightly.

Printed pp. 461-512Linearized Friction Cone
The curved friction cone is awkward for linear algebra, so we replace it with a polyhedral inner approximation: a finite fan of edge rays whose nonnegative span lies inside the true cone. More rays approximate the cone better. This conversion turns force-closure questions into linear feasibility checks and small linear programs.

From Forces to Wrenches

Force becomes wrench

A force at a point produces a moment plus that force on the object.

Wrench cone

Mapping every friction-cone ray gives the wrenches one contact can transmit.

Planar contact wrench

$\mathcal F = \begin{bmatrix}\tau_z\\ f_x\\ f_y\end{bmatrix},\quad \tau_z = p_x f_y - p_y f_x$

Combining

The grasp's reachable wrenches are the sum of the contact cones.

Printed pp. 461-512Contact Wrench Cone
Each admissible contact force at a point produces a wrench on the object, a moment paired with a force. Mapping every ray of the friction cone through this point gives the contact wrench cone, the set of wrenches one contact can transmit. The grasp combines these cones additively in wrench space across all contacts.

The Grasp Map and Wrench Space

Grasp map

$\mathcal F_{\text{obj}} = G\, f_c, \qquad G = [\,\mathcal F_1\ \cdots\ \mathcal F_m\,]$

Rank alarm

If columns miss a direction, no actuation can directly resist it.

Printed pp. 461-512The Grasp Map
The grasp map collects each contact's wrench generators as columns acting on contact-force coordinates. In the plane a force at a point contributes a moment equal to the cross product plus the two force components. The image of this map is exactly the set of wrenches the grasp can apply to the object.

Form Closure

Frictionless immobilization

Contact normals alone block every infinitesimal object motion.

Spanning test

Normals must positively span the whole wrench space.

Positive span

$\Big\{\textstyle\sum_i k_i\, \mathcal F_i : k_i \ge 0\Big\} = \mathbb{R}^n$

Contact count

First-order form closure: $\ge 4$ contacts in the plane, $\ge 7$ in space.

Printed pp. 461-512Form Closure
Form closure immobilizes an object with frictionless contacts alone: the contact normals must positively span the wrench space so that no infinitesimal twist escapes. In the plane first-order form closure needs at least four contacts, in space at least seven. Geometry alone, not friction, blocks every motion here.

Force Closure

Friction allowed

Now the wrench cones, not just normals, do the spanning.

Fewer contacts

Friction lets two or three contacts secure a planar object.

Cone span condition

$\operatorname{cone}\{\mathcal F_k\} = \mathbb{R}^n \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \text{force closure}$

Meaning

The grasp can resist any small external disturbance wrench.

Printed pp. 461-512Force Closure
Force closure allows friction, so the contact wrench cones, not just the normals, must positively span every wrench direction. With friction far fewer contacts suffice: two soft-finger or three point contacts can secure a planar object. Force closure means the grasp can resist any external disturbance wrench applied to the part.

Worked Example: A Rank-Deficient Grasp

Missing torque

$p_i \parallel f_i \;\Rightarrow\; \tau_z^{(i)} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \operatorname{rank} G = 2$

Lesson

The picture looks secure; the rank exposes a missing wrench direction.

Fix

Offset a contact or add friction tangents to restore torque authority.

Printed pp. 461-512A Grasp That Fails
Three contacts pushing symmetrically through a disk's center balance forces but generate no torque about the center, so the grasp map has rank two, not three. The picture looks secure while the algebra exposes a missing wrench direction. Offsetting a contact or adding friction tangents restores the torque authority.

Manipulation: Pushing and Pivoting

Pushing

Controlled sliding moves a part; friction predicts which way it rotates.

Pivoting

Tipping about an edge uses gravity and a single contact edge.

Same toolkit

$\text{friction cone} + \text{wrench cone} \Rightarrow \text{motion prediction}$

Closure vs motion

Closure is the secure limit; manipulation exploits the unsecure modes.

Printed pp. 461-512Manipulation Modes
Beyond static holding, contacts let a robot push, pivot, and topple parts using controlled sliding and tipping about an edge. The same friction-cone and wrench reasoning predicts whether a pushed object rotates which way or whether it tips. Closure is the secure limit; manipulation deliberately uses the unsecure contact modes to advantage.

Recap and Synthesis

Contacts are dual

Constraints on motion and generators of force at once.

Cones lift to wrenches

Friction cones map to per-contact wrench cones on the object.

Closure is spanning

Form and force closure are positive-spanning tests in wrench space.

One habit

$\text{picture of contacts} \;\;\&\;\; \operatorname{rank} G$

Takeaway

Read the contact geometry and the grasp-map rank together, never apart.

Printed pp. 461-512Recap and Synthesis
Contacts are dual objects: constraints on motion and generators of force. Friction cones make unilateral contact geometric, wrench cones lift that to the object, and closure becomes a positive-spanning test in wrench space. Read the contact picture and the grasp-map rank together, never as two separate facts about the grasp.
