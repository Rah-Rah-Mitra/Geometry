From composition to constraint solving in parallel mechanisms

Open chain

Pose is a forward product of joint transforms.

Closed chain

Pose must satisfy loop-closure equations.

Chapter question

How do loops turn kinematics into constraint solving?

Printed pp. 245-270Kinematics of Closed Chains
This chapter spans printed pages 245 through 270 and shifts the mental model from composition to constraint. A closed chain forces several kinematic paths to agree on one body pose, so the configuration must live on the zero set of a loop equation rather than at the end of a single product of transforms.

From Open to Closed Chains

Open chain

Joint vector freely chooses the end-effector pose.

Closing a loop

Adds equations the joint values must jointly obey.

Joint roles

Actuated joints are commanded; passive joints settle.

Mobility drop

$\dim \mathcal{C} = n_{\text{joints}} - \#\,\text{constraints}$

Consequence

Fewer degrees of freedom than the joint count alone suggests.

Printed pp. 245-270From Open to Closed
Open chains compose transforms forward and the joint vector freely chooses the pose. Closing the loop adds equations the joint values must jointly satisfy, dropping the mobility below the joint count. We name actuated and passive joints because only the actuated ones are commanded while the passive ones settle into an assembly.

Loop-Closure Constraints

Two paths, one frame

Both kinematic routes must reach the same pose.

One equation per loop

Independent loops stack into a constraint system.

Loop equation

$\Phi(q_a, q_p) = 0$

Configuration space

$\mathcal{C} = \{\, q : \Phi(q) = 0 \,\}$

Geometry

The solution set is a curved implicit surface.

Printed pp. 245-270Loop-Closure Constraints
Loop closure writes the requirement that two paths reach the same frame as a vector equation in the joint coordinates. Stacking one equation per independent loop gives the constraint system whose solution set is the mechanism's configuration space, generally a curved implicit surface rather than a flat coordinate box.

Mobility by Counting

Gruebler's formula

Estimate DoF from links and joints minus constraints.

Just a budget

Special geometry can break the count.

Planar / spatial mobility

$\mathrm{dof} = m(N - 1 - J) + \sum_{i} f_i$

Caveats

Overconstrained and paradoxically mobile linkages defy the estimate.

Check

Confirm against the constraint Jacobian rank.

Printed pp. 245-270Mobility by Counting
Gruebler's formula estimates degrees of freedom from link and joint counts minus the constraints each joint imposes. It is a quick budget, not a guarantee: special geometries create overconstrained or paradoxically mobile linkages where the actual mobility differs from the count, so the formula is a first estimate to be checked against the constraint Jacobian.

Forward vs Inverse Kinematics

Inverse (easy)

Pose → leg lengths by direct geometry.

Forward (hard)

Actuators → pose via coupled polynomials.

Multiplicity

Several real assembly modes per actuator vector.

Inverse map

$\ell_i = \lVert\, p_i - b_i \,\rVert$

Forward map

$\text{solve } \Phi(x_{\text{platform}}; \ell) = 0$

Printed pp. 245-270Forward vs Inverse
Parallel mechanisms invert the usual difficulty. Given a platform pose, each leg's required length or angle often follows from a direct geometric solve, making inverse kinematics easy. Going the other way, from actuator values to platform pose, can demand solving coupled polynomials with several real assembly modes, so forward kinematics is the harder problem.

Worked Example: The Planar Five-Bar

Two actuators

Grounded revolute joints $\theta_1,\theta_2$.

Two degrees of freedom

$\mathrm{dof} = 5 - 1 \cdot 2 - 0 = 2$

Assembly modes

One actuator pair, distinct elbow configurations.

Printed pp. 245-270The Planar Five-Bar
The planar five-bar grounds two actuated revolute joints and joins them through three moving links to a shared end point. With two actuators and one loop it has two degrees of freedom. The same actuator pair can place the coupler point in distinct elbow configurations, the simplest example of multiple assembly modes.

The Stewart-Gough Platform (6-UPS)

Inverse easy

Each leg length is a distance from pose.

Forward kinematics

$\ell_i^2 = \lVert R\,a_i + p - b_i \rVert^2$

Up to 40 solutions

General 6-6 forward kinematics is famously hard.

Printed pp. 245-270The Stewart-Gough Platform
The Stewart-Gough platform connects a fixed base to a moving platform through six legs whose lengths are the actuators. Inverse kinematics gives each length directly from the platform pose by a distance computation, while forward kinematics is famously hard, admitting up to forty assembly solutions for a general six-six geometry.

The Constraint Jacobian

Differentiate the loop

Time-derivative of $\Phi=0$ is linear in rates.

Split the columns

Actuated block $H_a$, passive block $H_p$.

Velocity constraint

$J_c(q)\,\dot q = 0$

Block form

$H_a\,\dot q_a + H_p\,\dot q_p = 0$

Allowed motion

Instantaneous velocities span the null space of $J_c$.

Printed pp. 245-270The Constraint Jacobian
Differentiating the loop equation in time gives a linear relation between joint velocities through the constraint Jacobian. Splitting columns into actuated and passive parts yields two blocks. Allowed instantaneous motions are the null space of the full Jacobian, and the actuated block must be invertible to map commanded rates into platform velocity.

Three Kinds of Singularity

Actuator

$H_a$ drops rank; commands cannot drive the platform.

End-effector

Platform gains a freedom or resists a wrench for free.

Configuration-space

Assembly branches meet; surface stops being smooth.

Rank test

$\det H_a = 0 \;\;\text{or}\;\; \det H_p = 0$

Diagnostic

Each type is a distinct rank loss of a Jacobian block.

Printed pp. 245-270Three Kinds of Singularity
Closed chains fail in three distinct ways. An actuator singularity makes the actuated Jacobian block drop rank, so commanded velocity cannot control the platform. An end-effector singularity lets the platform lose a freedom or resist a wrench for free. A configuration-space singularity is where assembly branches meet and the constraint surface stops being smooth.

Singular Geometry of the Loop

Singular curve

$\det J_c(q) = 0$

Fold

Fully stretched or folded elbow loses independent motion.

Conditioning

Numerical fragility grows near the surface.

Printed pp. 245-270Singular Geometry of the Loop
Singularities have a geometric signature in the planar loop. When the elbow stretches fully or folds back, small changes in one coordinate stop producing independent endpoint motion and the Jacobian determinant crosses zero. The reach boundary and the fold curve mark where branches pinch together and conditioning degrades sharply near the surface.

Actuation and Assembly Branches

Actuators underspecify

The actuator vector is not the full configuration.

Branch completes it

Passive variables and branch choice fix the assembly.

Multiplicity

$q_a \mapsto \{\, q^{(1)}, q^{(2)}, \dots \,\}$

Planning rule

Track the occupied branch along a trajectory.

Avoid

Configuration-space singularities risk a branch switch.

Printed pp. 245-270Actuation and Branches
An actuator vector alone does not pin down the configuration. The passive variables and the chosen branch complete it, and one actuator command can correspond to several physically distinct assemblies. Trajectory planning must therefore track which branch the mechanism occupies and avoid configuration-space singularities where an unintended branch switch could occur.

Recap and Takeaways

Implicit geometry

Configurations satisfy $\Phi(q)=0$, not a forward product.

Inverse easy, forward hard

The parallel-mechanism trade is the rule.

Jacobian is the diagnostic

Rank loss flags every singularity type.

Three singularities

$\text{actuator} \;/\; \text{config-space} \;/\; \text{end-effector}$

Printed pp. 245-270Recap and Takeaways
Closed-chain kinematics is implicit geometry: configurations satisfy loop equations rather than a forward product. Inverse kinematics is usually easy and forward kinematics hard for parallel robots. The constraint Jacobian is the local diagnostic, and its rank loss flags the actuator, configuration-space, and end-effector singularities that bound safe operation.
