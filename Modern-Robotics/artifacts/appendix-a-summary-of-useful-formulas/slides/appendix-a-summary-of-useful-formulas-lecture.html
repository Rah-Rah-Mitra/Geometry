Rotations: $SO(3)$ and the Skew Map

The group

$SO(3)=\{R\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}: R^{\top}R=I,\ \det R=1\}$

Hat / skew map

$[\omega]=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\omega_3&\omega_2\\\omega_3&0&-\omega_1\\-\omega_2&\omega_1&0\end{bmatrix}$

First diagnostic

Check $\det R = 1$ and orthonormal columns before trusting any rotation.

Printed pp. 565-574Rotations: SO(3) and the Skew Map
Rotation matrices form the group SO(3): orthonormal columns with determinant one. Angular velocity enters through the skew-symmetric bracket that turns a three-vector into a matrix. This hat map is the gateway to the exponential, so checking that a matrix truly lies in SO(3) is the first diagnostic of the appendix.

Exponential Coordinates and Rodrigues

Rodrigues formula

$R=e^{[\hat\omega]\theta}=I+\sin\theta\,[\hat\omega]+(1-\cos\theta)[\hat\omega]^2$

Matrix log

$[\hat\omega]\theta=\log R,\qquad \theta=\cos^{-1}\!\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}R-1}{2}$

Axis-angle

A unit axis $\hat\omega$ and angle $\theta$ are the exponential coordinates of $R$.

Failure mode

Near $\theta=\pi$ the log sits on a branch boundary; the axis is ambiguous.

Check

The notebook tracks the $\log(\exp(\cdot))$ round-trip residual.

Printed pp. 565-574Exponential Coordinates and Rodrigues
Rotating by angle theta about a unit axis is the matrix exponential of the skew axis, expanded in closed form by Rodrigues. The matrix logarithm inverts it to recover axis and angle, but near a rotation of pi the log sits at a branch boundary. Watching that failure mode protects the round-trip residual the notebook tests.

Rigid Motions: $SE(3)$ and Twists

Homogeneous transform

$T=\begin{bmatrix}R&p\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\in SE(3)$

Twist (velocity of a frame)

$\mathcal{V}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\v\end{bmatrix},\quad [\mathcal{V}]=\begin{bmatrix}[\omega]&v\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$

Whose velocity?

A twist is the velocity of a moving frame; its frame must be named.

Printed pp. 565-574Rigid Motions: SE(3) and Twists
Homogeneous transforms in SE(3) bundle a rotation and a translation into one frame. Their tangent objects are twists, stacking an angular velocity with a linear velocity, written as a four-by-four matrix through the same hat construction. The twist is the velocity of a moving frame, and which frame owns it determines every later computation.

The Screw Axis and $\exp$ on $se(3)$

Exponential on $SE(3)$

$T=e^{[\mathcal{S}]\theta},\qquad \mathcal{S}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\v\end{bmatrix}$ a screw axis

Matrix log

$[\mathcal{S}]\theta=\log T \;\Rightarrow\; (\hat{\mathcal{S}},\theta)$

Unifies

Pure rotation and pure translation are limiting cases of one helical motion.

Printed pp. 565-574The Screw Axis and exp on se(3)
A twist is a scaled screw axis: rotation about a line combined with translation along it. Exponentiating the screw matrix produces the rigid transform, and the matrix logarithm of SE(3) recovers the screw coordinates. This unifies pure rotation and pure translation as limiting cases of one helical motion the diagram makes visible.

The Adjoint Map: Changing Frames

Adjoint of a transform

$[\mathrm{Ad}_T]=\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\ [p]R&R\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$

Change a twist's frame

$\mathcal{V}_a=[\mathrm{Ad}_{T_{ab}}]\,\mathcal{V}_b$

Common bug

A missing or inverted adjoint. Name which $\mathrm{Ad}$ moved the twist.

Printed pp. 565-574The Adjoint Map: Changing Frames
A twist expressed in one frame becomes another column vector in a second frame. The adjoint of a transform performs that change of coordinates, built from the rotation and the skew of the translation. Most frame bugs in robotics are a missing or inverted adjoint, so naming which adjoint moved a twist is essential debugging discipline.

Forward Kinematics: Product of Exponentials

Space form

$T=e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_n]\theta_n}\,M$

Body form

$T=M\,e^{[\mathcal{B}_1]\theta_1}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{B}_n]\theta_n}$

Same arm

$M$ is the home pose; $\mathcal{S}_i$ are fixed-frame screws, $\mathcal{B}_i$ body-frame.

Printed pp. 565-574Forward Kinematics by Product of Exponentials
The product of exponentials writes the end-effector pose as a chain of screw exponentials applied to a home configuration. The space form lists screw axes in the fixed frame; the body form lists them in the end-effector frame. Both describe the same arm, and choosing one fixes the frame of every Jacobian column downstream.

The Space and Body Jacobians

End-effector twist

$\mathcal{V}=J(\theta)\,\dot\theta,\qquad J=[\,J_1\ \cdots\ J_n\,]$

Space and body forms

$J_b(\theta)=[\mathrm{Ad}_{T_{bs}}]\,J_s(\theta)$

Singularity

Rank loss in $J$ is a lost instantaneous direction of motion.

Printed pp. 565-574The Space and Body Jacobians
The Jacobian assembles the end-effector twist from joint rates, with one screw column per joint. The space Jacobian lives in the fixed frame and the body Jacobian in the end-effector frame, related by an adjoint. Rank loss in this matrix is exactly a singularity, where the arm loses an instantaneous direction of motion.

Wrenches and the Kineto-Statics Duality

Wrench (moment, force)

$\mathcal{F}=\begin{bmatrix}m\\f\end{bmatrix},\qquad \text{power}=\mathcal{F}^{\top}\mathcal{V}$

Kineto-statics

$\tau=J^{\top}(\theta)\,\mathcal{F}$

Same matrix

$J$ governs both motion and force; the notebook checks power is preserved.

Printed pp. 565-574Wrenches and the Kineto-Statics Duality
A wrench stacks a moment and a force, dual to a twist because their product is power. Setting the end-effector twist to zero, joint torques balance an applied wrench through the transpose of the Jacobian. This kineto-statics relation lets the same Jacobian govern both motion and force, and the notebook checks power is preserved across frames.

Manipulator Dynamics

Equations of motion

$\tau=M(\theta)\ddot\theta+c(\theta,\dot\theta)+g(\theta)$

Spatial inertia

$\mathcal{G}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{I}&0\\0&m\,I\end{bmatrix},\quad M(\theta)=M^{\top}\succ0$

Three terms

Inertial $M\ddot\theta$, velocity-dependent Coriolis/centripetal $c$, and gravity $g$.

Printed pp. 565-574Manipulator Dynamics
The equations of motion split joint torque into an inertial term, a velocity-dependent Coriolis and centripetal term, and gravity. The mass matrix is symmetric positive definite, guaranteeing positive kinetic energy, and spatial inertia carries this structure on each link. Perturbing one configuration and watching the smallest eigenvalue stay positive is the appendix's closing sanity check.

The Appendix as a Checklist

Four steps

State the domain, name the frame, compute an invariant, then perturb one value.

Witnesses

$\det R$, log branch, $\mathrm{Ad}$ placement, $\operatorname{rank}J$, $\lambda_{\min}(M)$.

Pitfall

A summary page is dangerous if it omits domains and conventions.

Takeaway

Every reusable formula is paired with an invariant.

Printed pp. 565-574The Appendix as a Checklist
Read together, these formulas form a debugging checklist: state the domain, name the frame, compute an invariant, then perturb one value and confirm the expected quantity changes. SO(3) determinants, log branch boundaries, adjoint placement, Jacobian rank, and mass-matrix eigenvalues are the witnesses that catch implementation mistakes early.
