Serial Chains and Link Frames

Why frames

Forward kinematics needs a coordinate frame fixed to every link of the chain.

The savings

A general relative pose has six free numbers. D-H uses a frame rule to cut that to four.

Per-link frame

$\{i\}\ \text{attached to link } i,\quad T_{i-1,i}\in SE(3)$

Single-DOF joints

Each joint is revolute (rotates) or prismatic (slides), contributing exactly one variable.

Printed pp. 585-596Serial Chains and Link Frames
A serial manipulator is a chain of rigid links joined by single-degree-of-freedom joints. Forward kinematics needs a frame on every link. D-H supplies a minimal, reproducible recipe for placing those frames so that only four parameters relate each neighbor, instead of the six a general rigid transform would demand.

The Common Normal Rule

z along the axis

Each frame's $z$-axis points along its joint's axis of motion.

x along the normal

The $x$-axis runs along the common normal between consecutive joint axes.

Result

This shared line fixes origin and orientation, dropping six numbers to four.

Printed pp. 585-596The Common Normal Rule
The heart of the convention is geometric. Point each frame's z-axis along its joint axis, then choose the x-axis along the common normal between consecutive joint axes. This single shared line fixes the origin and orientation enough that the relative pose collapses from six free numbers down to four.

The Four D-H Parameters

Link length $a$

Distance along the common normal between the two joint axes.

Link twist $\alpha$

Rotation about $x$ that carries one $z$-axis onto the next.

Link offset $d$

Slide along the joint axis $z$; the variable for a prismatic joint.

Joint angle $\theta$

Rotation about $z$; the variable for a revolute joint.

Printed pp. 585-596The Four D-H Parameters
Each link row carries four numbers: link length a measured along the common normal, link twist alpha rotating one z-axis onto the next, link offset d sliding along the joint axis, and joint angle theta. Revolute joints vary theta; prismatic joints vary d. The other two stay fixed by the mechanism.

The Link Transform: Two Screws

Screw about $x$

Twist $\alpha$ then translate length $a$ along $x$.

Screw about $z$

Rotate $\theta$ then translate offset $d$ along $z$.

Order is rigid

$SE(3)$ is noncommutative, so swapping factors gives a different frame.

Link transform

$T_{i-1,i} = \mathrm{Rot}_x(\alpha)\,\mathrm{Trans}_x(a)\,\mathrm{Rot}_z(\theta)\,\mathrm{Trans}_z(d)$

As elementary motions

$= e^{[\hat{x}]\alpha}\,e^{a\,\hat{x}}\,e^{[\hat{z}]\theta}\,e^{d\,\hat{z}}$

Printed pp. 585-596The Link Transform
The relative pose between neighboring link frames factors as a fixed sequence of two screw motions: a screw about x carrying twist alpha and length a, then a screw about z carrying angle theta and offset d. Because SE(3) is noncommutative this order is rigid, and swapping factors describes a different frame entirely.

The Homogeneous Matrix

Rotation block

Mixes $\theta$ and $\alpha$ through their sines and cosines.

Translation column

Records $a$ along $x$ and $d$ along $z$ in the previous frame.

Closed form

$T_{i-1,i}=\begin{bmatrix} c\theta & -s\theta\, c\alpha & s\theta\, s\alpha & a\,c\theta \\ s\theta & c\theta\, c\alpha & -c\theta\, s\alpha & a\,s\theta \\ 0 & s\alpha & c\alpha & d \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Printed pp. 585-596Transform as a Matrix
Writing each elementary rotation and translation as a homogeneous matrix and multiplying gives the closed form of the link transform. The rotation block mixes theta and alpha through sines and cosines, while the translation column records a along x and d along z, expressed in the previous frame's coordinates.

Forward Kinematics by Composition

Chain the factors

Multiply per-link transforms in order from base to tip.

One variable each

Each factor depends on a single joint coordinate.

End-effector pose

$T_{0,n}(\theta)=T_{0,1}\,T_{1,2}\cdots T_{n-1,n}$

Toward the Jacobian

Differentiating this ordered product yields the manipulator Jacobian.

Printed pp. 585-596Forward Kinematics by Composition
Forward kinematics is then pure matrix bookkeeping: chain the per-link transforms in order from base to tip. Each factor depends on exactly one joint variable, so the end-effector pose is an ordered product, and the chain rule on this product later yields the manipulator Jacobian cleanly.

For a planar two-link arm both twists vanish and both offsets are zero, so each row reduces to length a and angle theta. Multiplying the two transforms reproduces the familiar tip position with summed angles, a check the notebook computes numerically to confirm endpoint agreement with a screw-axis model.

D-H versus Product of Exponentials

D-H strengths

Compact four-column table; minimal parameters; easy hand calculation and consistency checks.

PoE strengths

Each joint screw stays explicit in one shared frame, so geometric axes are visible.

PoE form

$T(\theta)=e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_n]\theta_n}\,M$

Same kinematics

When conventions and home pose agree, both give identical forward kinematics.

Printed pp. 585-596DH versus Product of Exponentials
D-H compresses a chain into a compact four-column table, ideal for hand work and consistency checks. Product-of-exponentials keeps each joint screw explicit in one shared frame, exposing axes directly. When conventions and home pose agree the two give identical kinematics; disagreements are convention mismatches, not deep failures.

Ambiguities and Singular Cases

Intersecting axes

The common normal has zero length, so $a=0$ and its direction is undetermined.

Parallel axes

A whole family of common normals exists; the placement must be stated explicitly.

Printed pp. 585-596Ambiguities and Singular Cases
The convention has sharp edges worth naming. When neighboring axes intersect the common normal has zero length, and when they are parallel a whole family of normals exists, so frame placement must be stated explicitly. Sign conventions can also migrate between parameters, which is why a table without a diagram is dangerous.

Takeaways

Use D-H when

You want a compact serial-chain table and quick hand kinematics.

Use PoE when

You want geometric screw axes exposed directly in one frame.

Always pair table with diagram

A D-H table without a frame drawing is easy to misread and reproduce wrongly.

Name the invariant

State the representation and what should stay fixed together.

Printed pp. 585-596Takeaways
D-H stays valuable for compact serial-chain tables and quick hand kinematics, while product-of-exponentials often reveals geometric screw axes more directly. The reliable habit is to name the representation and its invariant together, always pairing a D-H table with a frame diagram so another reader can reproduce the same chain unambiguously.
