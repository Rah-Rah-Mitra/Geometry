From Pose to Rate

Configuration map

Forward kinematics sends joint angles $\theta$ to an end-effector pose.

Differentiate

Time-differentiating the map produces a linear, configuration-dependent relation.

Velocity relation

$\mathcal{V} = J(\theta)\,\dot{\theta}$

Local only

$J(\theta)$ is the slope of a nonlinear map, recomputed at every posture.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236From Pose to Rate
Forward kinematics gives a configuration map from joint angles to end-effector pose. Differentiating that map in time produces velocity kinematics, a linear relation valid only at the current configuration. The Jacobian is the slope of this nonlinear map, recomputed afresh at every posture the manipulator visits.

The Manipulator Jacobian

Definition

Partial derivatives of the pose map assembled into a matrix.

Columns

Column $i$ is the twist from moving joint $i$ alone at unit rate.

Column structure

$J = \big[\,J_1\ J_2\ \cdots\ J_n\,\big]$

Twist

$\mathcal{V}=(\omega,\,v)$ stacks angular and linear velocity.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236The Manipulator Jacobian
The Jacobian assembles partial derivatives of the configuration map into a matrix that sends joint-rate vectors to an end-effector twist. Each column is the twist generated by moving one joint alone at unit rate while every other joint is frozen, so columns carry direct physical meaning.

The Space Jacobian

Fixed frame

Twist expressed in the base frame: $\mathcal{V}_s = J_s(\theta)\,\dot{\theta}$.

Columns

Screw axes pushed forward through the product of exponentials.

i-th column

$J_{s,i} = \mathrm{Ad}_{e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_{i-1}]\theta_{i-1}}}\,\mathcal{S}_i$

Meaning

The screw joint $i$ contributes as the world frame sees it.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236The Space Jacobian
The space Jacobian expresses the end-effector twist in the fixed base frame. Its columns are joint screw axes pushed forward through the product of exponentials up to that joint, so each column is the instantaneous screw the joint contributes as seen by an observer standing in the world frame.

The Body Jacobian

Tip frame

Twist expressed in the end-effector frame: $\mathcal{V}_b = J_b(\theta)\,\dot{\theta}$.

Same map

Two coordinate views of one rate-to-motion relation.

Adjoint link

$J_s = \mathrm{Ad}_{T_{sb}}\,J_b, \qquad J_b = \mathrm{Ad}_{T_{bs}}\,J_s$

Columns

Screw axes pulled back from the tip toward each joint.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236The Body Jacobian
The body Jacobian expresses the same twist in the end-effector frame. Its columns are joint screw axes pulled back from the tip, and the body and space Jacobians are related by the adjoint of the end-effector pose, two coordinate views of one underlying linear map between rates and motion.
Modern RoboticsChapter 5

Columns Are Screw Axes

Six-vectors

Each column carries an angular part $\omega$ and linear part $v$.

Span

Column span = all twists the joints can instantaneously produce.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Columns Are Screw Axes
Reading the Jacobian column by column makes geometry visible. Each column is a six-vector with angular and linear parts, the screw axis of one joint expressed in the chosen frame. The span of these columns is exactly the set of twists the manipulator can produce at this instant from its joints.

Kinematic Singularities

Rank loss

Columns become dependent; $J$ drops below full rank.

Lost motion

Some twist direction is unreachable for any joint rates.

Singular condition

$\operatorname{rank} J(\theta) < \min(6,\,n) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \det\!\big(J J^{\top}\big)=0$

Geometry

A rank event, not a numerical accident; it bounds the workspace.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Kinematic Singularities
A singularity is a configuration where the Jacobian loses rank, so its columns become linearly dependent. The arm instantaneously cannot generate motion in some twist direction, no matter how the joints move. Singularities are rank events tied to geometry, not numerical accidents, and they bound the usable workspace.

The Manipulability Ellipsoid

Image of a sphere

Map the unit joint-velocity sphere through $J$ to get achievable twists.

Axes and lengths

$J J^{\top} u_i = \sigma_i^2\, u_i$

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236The Manipulability Ellipsoid
Mapping the unit sphere of joint velocities through the Jacobian yields an ellipsoid of achievable end-effector twists. Its axis directions are eigenvectors of J J transpose and its semi-axis lengths are singular values, so a long, round ellipsoid means dexterity and a flat one warns of an approaching singularity.

Dexterity Measures

Volume

The product of singular values vanishes exactly at singularities.

Conditioning

The condition number $\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ blows up there.

Manipulability

$\mu = \sqrt{\det\!\big(J J^{\top}\big)} = \sigma_1\sigma_2\cdots\sigma_m$

Use

Steer planning away from poorly conditioned postures.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Dexterity Measures
Scalar summaries of the ellipsoid quantify dexterity. The product of singular values, proportional to the ellipsoid volume, vanishes exactly at singularities. The condition number, the ratio of largest to smallest singular value, blows up there. These numbers let a planner steer the arm away from poorly conditioned postures.

Statics and Virtual Work

Hold still

The arm resists an external wrench $\mathcal{F}$ at the tip.

Power balance

Joint power equals end-effector power for any virtual motion.

Virtual work

$\tau^{\top}\dot{\theta} = \mathcal{F}^{\top}\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{F}^{\top} J\,\dot{\theta}\quad\forall\,\dot{\theta}$

Consequence

Holding for every $\dot\theta$ forces a matrix identity.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Statics and Virtual Work
Hold the arm still and let it resist an external wrench at the tip. The principle of virtual work equates joint power with end-effector power for any virtual displacement, and because this holds for every joint rate the wrench and torque relation must hold as a matrix identity independent of motion.

Force from the Transpose

Kineto-statics duality

$\tau = J^{\top}(\theta)\,\mathcal{F}$

One matrix

$J$ maps rates to twists; $J^{\top}$ maps tip force to torque.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Force from the Transpose
Virtual work forces the joint torques that balance a tip wrench to equal the Jacobian transpose times that wrench. The same matrix that maps rates to twists maps forces backward to torques. This kineto-statics duality means velocity and force live in dual spaces joined by one transpose.

Force Ellipsoid and Duality

Reciprocal axes

The force ellipsoid stretches where the velocity ellipsoid flattens.

Near singularity

Easy to resist force in the very direction motion is lost.

Force ellipsoid

$\big(J J^{\top}\big)^{-1}:\ \text{semi-axes } 1/\sigma_i$

Duality

Directions of easy motion and easy force are reciprocal.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Force Ellipsoid and Duality
At a singularity the velocity ellipsoid flattens, but the force ellipsoid, governed by the inverse Jacobian transpose, stretches along the same lost direction. The arm cannot move there yet resists force effortlessly. Directions of easy motion and directions of easy force are reciprocal throughout the workspace.

Recap and Invariants

Motion

$\mathcal{V}=J\dot\theta$; rank loss marks singularities.

Force

$\tau=J^{\top}\mathcal{F}$; the dual map through power.

Pitfalls

Big joint speeds near singularity do not buy endpoint freedom; manipulability needs a stated metric.

Invariant check

Twist-wrench power pairing is frame-invariant; a changed value flags a bad adjoint.

Printed pp. 171-218 · PDF 189-236Recap and Invariants
We unified instantaneous motion and static force through one matrix. The Jacobian maps joint rates to twists, its rank loss marks singularities, its singular values shape the manipulability ellipsoid, and its transpose converts tip wrenches into joint torques. The notebook checks an invariant power pairing to guard against frame and adjoint mistakes.
