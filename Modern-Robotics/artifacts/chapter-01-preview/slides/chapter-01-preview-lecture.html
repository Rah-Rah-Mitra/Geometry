Mechanisms as Collections of Rigid Bodies

Links and joints

A mechanism is rigid links connected by joints. Each joint constrains relative motion and contributes degrees of freedom.

End-effector

The task cares about one frame: where the gripper or tool is, in position and orientation.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Mechanisms
A robot mechanism is a chain of rigid links joined by joints. We model it as a kinematic structure, not a drawing. Each joint, revolute or prismatic, contributes one or more degrees of freedom, and the end-effector frame is what a task actually cares about when reaching and grasping objects.

Configuration Space and Degrees of Freedom

Configuration space

The set of all attainable poses. For an open chain its dimension equals the number of joint variables.

Gruebler count

$\mathrm{dof} = m\,(N-1-J) + \sum_{i} f_i$

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Configuration
The configuration space collects every pose the mechanism can attain. For an open chain its dimension equals the number of joint variables, the degrees of freedom. Gruebler's formula counts them from links, joints, and constraints, and the space is often curved, like a torus, rather than flat Euclidean.

The Lie Groups SO(3) and SE(3)

Rotations

$SO(3) = \{R \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3} : R^\top R = I,\ \det R = 1\}$

Rigid displacements

$SE(3) = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} R & p \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}$

Why groups

Orientation and pose live in matrix Lie groups, not raw angle triples. Composition is matrix product; no gimbal lock.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Lie groups
Rotations form the Lie group SO(3) and rigid-body displacements form SE(3). These matrix groups, not raw angle triples, are the honest home of orientation and pose. Working in them avoids chart singularities like gimbal lock and gives a clean algebra for composing successive motions by matrix multiplication.

Twists: Velocity as a Screw

Twist (se(3))

$\mathcal{V} = \begin{bmatrix} \omega \\ v \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^6$

Chasles' theorem

Every rigid motion is a screw: rotation about an axis plus translation along it.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Twists
A spatial velocity is a twist, pairing angular and linear parts into one six-vector that lives in the Lie algebra se(3). By Chasles' theorem any rigid motion is a screw: rotation about an axis together with translation along it. Twists make velocity geometric rather than coordinate bound.

Wrenches: Force as the Dual of Motion

Wrench

$\mathcal{F} = \begin{bmatrix} m \\ f \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^6$

Power pairing (invariant)

$P = \mathcal{F}^\top \mathcal{V}$

Duality

Twists describe motion; wrenches describe force and moment. Their pairing is power, unchanged by a change of frame.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Wrenches
Forces and moments combine into a wrench, the dual object to a twist. The pairing of wrench with twist yields power, an invariant under change of frame. This duality between motion and force runs through statics, dynamics, and contact analysis across the entire book.

Product of Exponentials: Forward Kinematics

PoE formula

$T(\theta) = e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]\theta_1}\, e^{[\mathcal{S}_2]\theta_2}\cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_n]\theta_n}\, M$

No link frames

Only screw axes $\mathcal{S}_i$ and the home pose $M$ are needed — geometric and easy to write.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Forward kinematics
Forward kinematics maps joint angles to end-effector pose. The product of exponentials formula builds that pose by exponentiating each joint twist in turn and composing with the home configuration. It needs no intermediate link frames, only screw axes, making it geometric and remarkably easy to write down.

The Jacobian: Hinge of the Pipeline

Velocity

$\mathcal{V} = J(\theta)\,\dot{\theta}$

Statics (transpose)

$\tau = J(\theta)^\top \mathcal{F}$

Singularities

Where $J$ loses rank, instantaneous task motion collapses to a lower-dimensional set.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Jacobian
The Jacobian maps joint velocities to end-effector twists and, by its transpose, maps wrenches to joint torques. It is the hinge between kinematics, statics, and dynamics. Where it loses rank we meet singularities, configurations where instantaneous task motion collapses to a lower-dimensional set of directions.

Recap and Outlook

Takeaways

  • Robotics is organized around spaces, maps, velocities, forces, and feedback.
  • SO(3) and SE(3) unify orientation and pose.

Pitfalls

  • Task space is not configuration space.
  • Chapters are not isolated formulas; the geometry is shared.

Habit

Name the representation and its invariant together. Coordinates matter only once we know the geometric object they encode.

Printed pp. 1–10 · PDF pp. 19–28Recap
Robotics is organized around spaces, maps, velocities, forces, and feedback. The Lie groups SO(3) and SE(3), with twists and wrenches, supply one vocabulary reused in every later chapter. Name the representation and its invariant together, and the geometry stays inspectable throughout the rest of the course.
