Rotation Matrices and SO(3)

Orthonormal frame

Columns are unit, mutually orthogonal axes.

Three freedoms

Nine entries minus six constraints leave three.

Not a vector space

Sum of rotations is rarely a rotation.

Special orthogonal group

$$SO(3)=\{R\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}\mid R^{\top}R=I,\ \det R=1\}$$

Action

$R$ rotates frames and changes vector coordinates.

Printed pp. 59-136Rotation Matrices and SO(3)
A rotation matrix stores an orthonormal right-handed frame in its columns. The constraints of orthogonality and unit determinant cut the nine entries down to three degrees of freedom, defining the special orthogonal group SO(3), a curved manifold rather than a flat vector space of matrices.

Angular Velocity and $\mathfrak{so}(3)$

Differentiate the constraint

$\dot R R^{\top}$ is skew-symmetric.

Lie algebra

Skew matrices form $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, the tangent space at $I$.

Bracket notation

$[\omega]$ encodes $\omega$ as a skew matrix.

Skew form

$$[\omega]=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\omega_3&\omega_2\\\omega_3&0&-\omega_1\\-\omega_2&\omega_1&0\end{bmatrix}=-[\omega]^{\top}$$

Velocity

$\dot R = [\omega]R$ in the body frame.

Printed pp. 59-136Angular Velocity and so(3)
Differentiating the orthogonality constraint shows that the velocity of a rotation is a skew-symmetric matrix times the rotation. Skew-symmetric matrices form the Lie algebra so(3), the tangent space at the identity, and the bracket notation packages an angular velocity vector into that matrix.

Exponential Coordinates of Rotation

Exponential map

$$R=e^{[\omega]\theta},\qquad \|\omega\|=1$$

Three coordinates

The vector $\omega\theta\in\mathbb{R}^3$ charts any rotation.

Flat to curved

$\exp$ maps $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ onto $SO(3)$.

Printed pp. 59-136Exponential Coordinates of Rotation
Rotating about a unit axis omega by angle theta is the matrix exponential of the skew matrix scaled by theta. This exponential map sends the flat Lie algebra onto the curved group, so axis and angle become a chart of three exponential coordinates for any rotation.

Rodrigues' Formula

Closed form

Powers of $[\omega]$ cycle, collapsing the series.

Cheap and stable

Two trig evaluations replace a matrix exponential.

Geometry

$[\omega]$ rotates in-plane; $[\omega]^2$ projects.

Rodrigues

$$e^{[\omega]\theta}=I+\sin\theta\,[\omega]+(1-\cos\theta)\,[\omega]^2$$

Identity used

$[\omega]^3=-[\omega]$ for unit $\omega$.

Printed pp. 59-136Rodrigues' Formula
The matrix exponential closes into a finite formula because powers of a unit skew matrix cycle. Rodrigues' formula expresses the rotation using sine and cosine of the angle, giving a cheap, numerically stable way to convert axis-angle coordinates into a concrete rotation matrix without an infinite series.

Matrix Logarithm of Rotation

Inverse problem

Recover axis $\omega$ and angle $\theta$ from $R$.

Trace gives angle

$\operatorname{tr}R=1+2\cos\theta$.

Chart edges

Degenerates at $\theta=0$ and near $\theta=\pi$.

Logarithm

$$[\omega]\theta=\log R,\quad [\omega]=\tfrac{R-R^{\top}}{2\sin\theta}$$

Implementation

Branch carefully where $\sin\theta\to0$.

Printed pp. 59-136Matrix Logarithm of Rotation
The inverse problem recovers an axis and angle from a rotation matrix using the matrix logarithm. The trace fixes the angle and the antisymmetric part fixes the axis, but the chart degenerates near angle pi and at the identity, exactly where careful implementations must branch.

Homogeneous Transforms and SE(3)

One matrix

Rotation block plus translation column.

Composition

Chaining motions is matrix multiplication.

Cheap inverse

Built from $R^{\top}$ and $-R^{\top}p$.

Special Euclidean group

$$T=\begin{bmatrix}R&p\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\in SE(3),\ R\in SO(3)$$

Inverse

$T^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}R^{\top}&-R^{\top}p\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$

Printed pp. 59-136Homogeneous Transforms and SE(3)
Stacking a rotation block and a translation into a four-by-four matrix represents a full rigid displacement. These homogeneous transforms form the special Euclidean group SE(3); composing motions becomes matrix multiplication, and the inverse is built cheaply from the transpose of the rotation block.

Twists and Spatial Velocity

Six-vector

Angular $\omega$ stacked over linear $v$.

Tangent to SE(3)

Matrix form lives in $\mathfrak{se}(3)$.

Frame choice

Space twist or body twist, never confuse them.

Twist

$$\mathcal{V}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\v\end{bmatrix},\quad [\mathcal{V}]=\begin{bmatrix}[\omega]&v\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$$

Velocity

$\dot T = [\mathcal{V}]\,T$ in the space frame.

Printed pp. 59-136Twists and Spatial Velocity
The velocity of a rigid body packs an angular part and a linear part into a six-vector twist, the tangent object of SE(3). Its matrix form lives in the Lie algebra se(3), and choosing the space frame or the body frame changes which twist we read.

Screw Motion: Chasles–Mozzi

Chasles–Mozzi

$$\text{any }T = \text{screw}(\hat s, h, \theta)$$

Pitch

$h=$ translation per radian along the axis.

Limits

$h=0$ pure rotation; $h\to\infty$ pure translation.

Printed pp. 59-136Screw Motion: Chasles-Mozzi
The Chasles-Mozzi theorem states that every rigid displacement is a screw: a rotation about an axis combined with a translation along that same axis. The pitch h measures translation per radian, unifying pure rotations, pure translations, and the general helical motion into one geometric primitive.

Exponential Coordinates of $SE(3)$

Screw axis

$\mathcal{S}=(\omega,v)$ with $\|\omega\|=1$.

Finite motion

Exponentiate the twist scaled by $\theta$.

Logarithm

Recovers the screw from any $T$.

se(3) exponential

$$T=e^{[\mathcal{S}]\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}e^{[\omega]\theta}&G(\theta)v\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$$

Coordinates

The six numbers $\mathcal{S}\theta$ chart all of $SE(3)$.

Printed pp. 59-136Exponential Coordinates of SE(3)
A finite rigid motion is the matrix exponential of a twist times the displacement angle, generalizing Rodrigues' formula to se(3). The six exponential coordinates are a screw axis scaled by distance traveled, and the matrix logarithm recovers that screw from any homogeneous transform.

Wrenches: Force and Moment

Six-vector dual

Moment $m$ stacked over force $f$.

Power pairing

Inner product with a twist gives power.

Covector

Transforms by the dual rule, not the twist rule.

Wrench and power

$$\mathcal{F}=\begin{bmatrix}m\\f\end{bmatrix},\qquad P=\mathcal{F}^{\top}\mathcal{V}$$

Invariance

Power $P$ is the same in every frame.

Printed pp. 59-136Wrenches: Force and Moment
A wrench pairs a moment and a force into a six-vector dual to the twist. The instantaneous power is their inner product, a frame-independent scalar. Because power must be invariant, wrenches transform by the dual rule, marking them as covectors rather than ordinary vectors.

The Adjoint Representation

Change of frame

$[\mathrm{Ad}_T]$ moves twists between frames.

Dual for wrenches

$[\mathrm{Ad}_T]^{\top}$ carries wrenches the other way.

Power preserved

The twist/wrench pairing stays invariant.

Adjoint

$$[\mathrm{Ad}_T]=\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\ [p]R&R\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$$

Check

$\mathcal{F}'^{\top}\mathcal{V}'=\mathcal{F}^{\top}\mathcal{V}$.

Printed pp. 59-136The Adjoint Representation
The adjoint map built from a transform changes the frame in which a twist is expressed while preserving the physical motion. Its inverse transpose carries wrenches the dual way, so the power pairing between a twist and a wrench stays constant across any change of frame.
