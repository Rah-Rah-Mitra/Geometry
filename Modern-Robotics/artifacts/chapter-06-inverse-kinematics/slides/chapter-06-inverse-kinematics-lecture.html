Inverse Kinematics

Solving for joint values that achieve a desired end-effector pose

The goal

Find joints $\theta$ with $T(\theta)=T_{sd}$, the target pose.

Two roads

Closed-form geometry for special arms; numerical iteration in general.

The catch

The forward map is nonlinear, periodic, and often many-to-one.

The Inverse Problem

Root finding

Solve $T(\theta)=T_{sd}$ by driving the pose residual to zero.

Three outcomes

A target may have several solutions, none, or one hard to reach.

Forward map

$x = f(\theta), \qquad \text{seek } \theta = f^{-1}(x_d)$

Local view

Given the error we see now, which joint direction shrinks it next?

Printed pp. 219-244The Inverse Problem
Forward kinematics sends joint vectors to a pose; inversion seeks a preimage of a target. Because the map is nonlinear and periodic, a reachable target may admit several joint solutions, none, or one hard to approach. The practical question is local: which tangent direction in joint space reduces the current pose error.

Analytic IK: Planar 2R Arm

Law of cosines

The target distance fixes the elbow angle up to a sign.

Closed form

An exact algebraic recipe, not an iterative search.

Elbow angle

$\cos\theta_2 = \dfrac{x^2+y^2-L_1^2-L_2^2}{2L_1L_2}$

Shoulder angle

$\theta_1 = \operatorname{atan2}(y,x) - \operatorname{atan2}(L_2 s_2,\, L_1+L_2 c_2)$

Printed pp. 219-244Analytic IK: Planar 2R Arm
For special geometries we solve in closed form. A planar two-link arm reaches a point by the law of cosines, which fixes the elbow angle up to sign. The wrapped first angle then aligns the shoulder. This gives an exact algebraic recipe rather than an iterative search over configurations.

Two postures

Same endpoint

Different regions of configuration space, identical reach.

This branch multiplicity is generic; analytic solvers enumerate branches while numerical solvers land on whichever basin the initial guess selects.

Newton-Raphson on Pose Error

Linearize

Approximate the forward map at the current $\theta$ by its Jacobian.

Iterate

Correct, recompute, repeat until the residual is below tolerance.

Newton step

$\theta \leftarrow \theta + J_b^{\dagger}(\theta)\, \mathcal{V}_b$

Convergence

Quadratic near a root, but only inside a basin of the guess.

Printed pp. 219-244Newton-Raphson on Pose Error
When no closed form exists we linearize. Reading the configuration error as a body twist from the matrix logarithm of T inverse times the target, Newton-Raphson repeatedly corrects the joint vector using the body Jacobian. Each step trusts the first-order model of the forward map near the current configuration.

The Body Twist Error

Matrix log

The error frame becomes a single six-vector twist.

Match conventions

The residual must agree with the Jacobian it feeds.

Configuration error

$[\mathcal{V}_b] = \log\!\big(T^{-1}(\theta)\, T_{sd}\big)$

Reads

Rotation and translation discrepancy captured together.

Printed pp. 219-244The Body Twist Error
The residual must match the Jacobian convention. We express the error as a body twist whose matrix log captures both rotation and translation discrepancy between current and target frames in a single six-vector. Mixing body, space, and coordinate errors can look plausible while pushing the iteration in the wrong direction.

The Iteration Step

Exact vs least-norm

Square full-rank $J$ gives the true Newton step.

Otherwise

The pseudoinverse selects the least-norm joint update.

Printed pp. 219-244The Iteration Step
The core update multiplies the Jacobian pseudoinverse by the body twist and adds it to the joints. When the Jacobian is square and full rank this is the exact Newton step; otherwise the pseudoinverse selects the least-norm joint update. Convergence is quadratic near a solution but only inside a basin around the guess.

The Pseudoinverse

Redundant arm

Returns the minimum-norm joint velocity for the task twist.

Overdetermined

Returns the least-squares fit when no exact solution exists.

Moore-Penrose

$J^{\dagger} = J^{\mathsf{T}}\big(J J^{\mathsf{T}}\big)^{-1}$

Workhorse

Turns rectangular or rank-poor systems into a defined update.

Printed pp. 219-244The Pseudoinverse
The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse handles non-square and rank-deficient Jacobians. For redundant arms it returns the minimum-norm joint velocity achieving the task twist; for overdetermined tasks it returns the least-squares fit. It is the workhorse that turns a singular or rectangular linear system into a well-defined joint correction.

Damping Near Singularity

The blow-up

Lost rank means huge joint motion for tiny task corrections.

Trade-off

Stronger damping shrinks peak steps but adds iterations.

Damped least squares

$\Delta\theta = J^{\mathsf{T}}\big(J J^{\mathsf{T}} + \lambda^2 I\big)^{-1} \mathcal{V}_b$

Notebook sweep

The damped peak update stays below the undamped one.

Printed pp. 219-244Damping Near Singularity
Near a singularity the Jacobian loses rank and the raw pseudoinverse demands enormous joint motion for tiny task corrections. Damped least squares adds a regularizer, trading exact local correction for bounded, robust steps. The notebook sweep confirms stronger damping shrinks the peak update while usually costing extra iterations to reach tolerance.

Redundancy and Null Space

Extra freedom

More joints than task dimensions leaves a null space.

Secondary goals

Avoid joint limits or obstacles without disturbing the task.

Null-space motion

$\dot\theta = J^{\dagger}\mathcal{V}_b + \big(I - J^{\dagger}J\big)\, z$

Projector

$I - J^{\dagger}J$ moves posture, not the end-effector.

Printed pp. 219-244Redundancy and Null Space
A redundant arm has more joints than task dimensions, so its Jacobian has a nontrivial null space. Adding any null-space velocity changes posture without altering first-order end-effector motion. This freedom lets secondary objectives, like avoiding joint limits or obstacles, ride along with the primary task through the projector I minus J-dagger J.

Recap and Pitfalls

What we built

  • Analytic IK enumerates branches for special arms.
  • Newton-Raphson with $J_b$ inverts general poses.
  • Pseudoinverse and damping tame redundancy and singularity.

Pitfalls to watch

  • A local method can converge to the wrong branch.
  • Small task error can demand large joint motion.
  • Residual conventions must match the Jacobian.

Habit

Report numerical margins, not just a final joint vector.

Printed pp. 219-244Recap and Pitfalls
We recap: analytic IK enumerates branches for special geometries; Newton-Raphson with the body Jacobian inverts general poses; the pseudoinverse and damping handle redundancy and singularity; null-space motion resolves extra freedom. Pitfalls include wrong-branch convergence, large motion near singularities, and residual conventions that quietly mismatch the Jacobian.
