The Control Objective

Joint space

Track a reference $\theta_d(t)$ in configuration coordinates.

Task space

Track an end-effector path filtered through the Jacobian $J(\theta)$.

Tracking error

$\theta_e(t) = \theta_d(t) - \theta(t)$

Goal

Drive $\theta_e \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$, with good transients.

Printed pp. 403-460The Control Objective
Motion control demands that the configuration track a reference trajectory, expressed either in joint coordinates or in task space through the Jacobian. We define the error as desired minus actual, and the entire chapter studies how to shape the differential equation that this error obeys.

Error Dynamics and Stability

Chosen equation

A controller imposes a differential equation on $\theta_e$.

Eigenvalues

Roots in the open left half plane give decay.

Linear error dynamics

$\ddot{\theta}_e + K_d\,\dot{\theta}_e + K_p\,\theta_e = 0$

Stability

$K_p, K_d \succ 0$ makes the origin asymptotically stable.

Printed pp. 403-460Error Dynamics and Stability
A controller is judged by the differential equation it imposes on the tracking error. If that equation is linear and its characteristic roots sit in the open left half plane, the error decays to zero. Stability is therefore a statement about eigenvalues, not about any single torque value.

PID Joint Control

Proportional

Restoring torque proportional to error.

Derivative

Damps oscillation, opposes error rate.

Integral

Erases steady offset from gravity, friction.

PID law

$\tau = K_p\,\theta_e + K_i\!\!\int\!\theta_e\,dt + K_d\,\dot{\theta}_e$

Steady state

Without $K_i$, a constant load leaves residual error.

Printed pp. 403-460PID Joint Control
Proportional, integral, and derivative terms act on the joint error directly. The proportional gain pulls toward the target, derivative damps oscillation, and integral erases steady offsets from gravity or friction. Without integral action a constant disturbance leaves a permanent steady-state error.

Second-Order Error and Damping

Standard form

$\ddot{\theta}_e + 2\zeta\omega_n\,\dot{\theta}_e + \omega_n^2\,\theta_e = 0$

Damping ratio $\zeta$

Sets overshoot, ringing, and settling time.

Printed pp. 403-460Second-Order Error and Damping
With proportional and derivative feedback the error obeys a mass-spring-damper equation. The damping ratio decides the transient: under-damped responses overshoot and ring, over-damped responses crawl, and critical damping gives the fastest settling without overshoot. Gains are chosen to place that ratio at one.
Modern RoboticsChapter 11

Choosing Gains for Critical Damping

Critical damping

$\zeta = 1: \quad K_p = \omega_n^2, \quad K_d = 2\omega_n$

Pick $\omega_n$

Natural frequency sets response speed.

Trade-off

Larger $\omega_n$ is faster but needs more torque.

Caution

High gains amplify unmodeled dynamics and noise.

Printed pp. 403-460Choosing Gains for Critical Damping
Specifying a natural frequency and a unit damping ratio fixes both gains: the proportional gain equals the natural frequency squared and the derivative gain equals twice the natural frequency. Larger natural frequency means faster response but demands more torque and amplifies any unmodeled dynamics.

Feedforward Control

Model-predicted torque

Inject the dynamics expected along the plan.

Residual only

Feedback corrects what the model misses.

Feedforward + PD

$\tau = M(\theta_d)\ddot{\theta}_d + h(\theta_d,\dot{\theta}_d) + K_p\theta_e + K_d\dot{\theta}_e$

Limit

Model error reappears as tracking error.

Printed pp. 403-460Feedforward Control
Feedforward injects the torque predicted by the model along the planned trajectory, so feedback only has to correct the residual. When the model is accurate the feedback effort shrinks toward zero, but every modeling error reappears as a tracking error that feedback alone must absorb.

Computed-Torque Control

Control law

$\tau = M(\theta)\big(\ddot{\theta}_d + K_p\theta_e + K_d\dot{\theta}_e\big) + c(\theta,\dot{\theta}) + g(\theta)$

Feedback linearization

Cancels $M$, $c$, $g$ to decouple the joints.

Printed pp. 403-460Computed-Torque Control
Computed torque uses the mass matrix, Coriolis vector, and gravity to cancel the nonlinear dynamics exactly, leaving a clean linear error equation in every joint. This feedback linearization decouples the joints so that a single pair of gains delivers identical second-order behavior throughout the workspace.

Linearized Error Dynamics

After cancellation

Commanded acceleration sets the loop.

Decoupled joints

One gain pair governs every joint identically.

Closed loop

$\ddot{\theta}_e + K_d\,\dot{\theta}_e + K_p\,\theta_e = 0$

Global validity

Second-order shaping holds across the workspace.

Printed pp. 403-460Linearized Error Dynamics
After cancellation the commanded acceleration equals the desired acceleration plus a proportional and derivative correction on the error. The closed-loop error then satisfies a decoupled linear system whose decay rate we set directly, which is exactly the second-order shaping seen earlier, now valid globally.

Force Control

Stiff contact

Position commands become ill defined.

Regulate wrench

Track desired end-effector force/torque.

Force law

$\tau = J^{\!\top}(\theta)\big(F_d + K_{fp}(F_d - F)\big) + \tilde g(\theta)$

Stability

Add damping against environment stiffness.

Printed pp. 403-460Force Control
When the end-effector presses on a stiff environment, position commands become meaningless and we regulate the contact wrench instead. A force controller compares measured to desired wrench and adjusts torque through the Jacobian transpose, often adding damping so the loop stays stable against the environment stiffness.
Modern RoboticsChapter 11

Hybrid Motion-Force Control

Selection split

$\tau = P\,\tau_{\text{motion}} + (I - P)\,\tau_{\text{force}}$

Projection $P$

Routes feedback into free vs. constrained directions.

Printed pp. 403-460Hybrid Motion-Force Control
A contact task splits Cartesian directions into a motion subspace and an orthogonal force subspace. Selection or projection matrices route position feedback into free directions and force feedback into constrained ones, so the robot slides along a surface while pressing into it with a commanded normal force.

Impedance and Admittance Control

Impedance

Render a mass-spring-damper at the tip.

Admittance

Measured force drives a reference motion.

Rendered dynamics

$M_d\,\ddot{x}_e + B_d\,\dot{x}_e + K_d\,x_e = F_{\text{ext}}$

Tuning

Virtual stiffness trades accuracy for gentle contact.

Printed pp. 403-460Impedance and Admittance Control
Impedance control renders a chosen mass-spring-damper between displacement and contact force, letting the end-effector feel compliant rather than rigid. Admittance is the dual: measured force drives a reference motion. Tuning the virtual stiffness and damping trades tracking accuracy against gentle, stable interaction.

Synthesis and Pitfalls

Unifying idea

Every controller is a chosen error dynamics.

Projections

Hybrid and impedance are task intent projected.

Pitfall

High gains hide model error until contact or saturation.

Pitfall

Force control without a contact model is ill posed.

Printed pp. 403-460Synthesis and Pitfalls
Every controller here is a chosen error dynamics layered on the robot's geometry and dynamics. High gains can mask model error until contact or saturation exposes it, and force control without a contact model is ill posed. Hybrid and impedance laws are projections of task intent onto motion and force.
