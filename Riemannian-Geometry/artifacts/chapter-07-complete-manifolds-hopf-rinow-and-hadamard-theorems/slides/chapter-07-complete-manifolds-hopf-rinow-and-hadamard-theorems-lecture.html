Geodesic Completeness

Definition

$M$ is geodesically complete if every geodesic $\gamma$ extends to a map on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

Exponential view

Equivalently $\exp_p$ is defined on the whole tangent space $T_pM$ for one (hence every) point $p$.

Geodesic equation

$\dfrac{D}{dt}\dot{\gamma}(t)=0,\qquad \gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M$

Why it matters

No geodesic escapes the manifold in finite time, so global constructions are well posed.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Geodesic Completeness
A manifold is geodesically complete when every geodesic can be reparametrized to run over the entire real line. Equivalently the exponential map at each point is defined on the whole tangent space, so no geodesic ever falls off the edge of the manifold in finite parameter time.

An Incomplete Manifold: the Punctured Plane

Failure

The straight geodesic aimed at $0$ exits $M$ at finite $t^\*$; it cannot be continued.

Not defined

$\exp_p(t^\*\,v)\notin M,\quad |v|=1$

Lesson

Completeness is a real hypothesis: removing a point already breaks it.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154An Incomplete Manifold
The punctured plane shows incompleteness concretely. A straight geodesic aimed at the missing origin reaches the puncture in finite arc length and cannot continue. The exponential map fails to be defined for the full tangent vector, so completeness genuinely constrains the geometry rather than coming for free.

The Metric Space of a Manifold

Distance

$d(p,q)$ is the infimum of lengths of piecewise smooth curves joining $p$ to $q$.

Metric completeness

$(M,d)$ is complete: every Cauchy sequence converges in $M$.

Riemannian distance

$d(p,q)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_0^1 |\dot{\gamma}(t)|\,dt$

The bridge

Hopf-Rinow shows this analytic notion equals geodesic completeness exactly.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Metric Completeness
Every connected Riemannian manifold becomes a metric space under the infimum of lengths of joining curves. Metric completeness asks that every Cauchy sequence converge. The deep content of Hopf-Rinow is that this analytic notion coincides exactly with the geometric notion of geodesic completeness.

The Hopf-Rinow Theorem

Theorem

For a connected manifold $M$ and a point $p$, the following are equivalent.

  • (a) $\exp_p$ is defined on all of $T_pM$.
  • (b) Closed and bounded subsets of $M$ are compact.
  • (c) $(M,d)$ is a complete metric space.
  • (d) $M$ is geodesically complete.
do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154The Hopf-Rinow Theorem
Hopf-Rinow gathers four equivalent statements: the exponential map at one point is defined everywhere, closed bounded sets are compact, the manifold is metrically complete, and it is geodesically complete. Any single condition forces all the others on a connected manifold, which is remarkable.

Corollary: Minimizing Geodesics

Corollary

If $M$ is complete, any $p,q\in M$ are joined by a minimizing geodesic $\gamma$.

Length equals distance

$\ell(\gamma)=d(p,q)$: the geodesic realizes the shortest path.

From local to global

Local existence of short geodesics becomes a global existence statement.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Minimizing Geodesics Exist
A powerful corollary of Hopf-Rinow guarantees that any two points of a complete connected manifold are joined by a minimizing geodesic whose length equals their distance. Completeness thus upgrades the local existence of short geodesics into a genuinely global existence statement valid everywhere.

Proof Roadmap of Hopf-Rinow

Step 1

Take a small geodesic sphere about $p$; pick the point $x_0$ on it nearest to $q$.

Step 2

Shoot the unit geodesic $\gamma$ from $p$ through $x_0$ and define $A=\{s: d(\gamma(s),q)=d(p,q)-s\}$.

Step 3

Show $A$ is closed and open in $[0,d(p,q)]$; by connectedness it is the whole interval.

Conclusion

$\gamma\big(d(p,q)\big)=q,\quad \ell(\gamma)=d(p,q)$

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Proof Strategy of Hopf-Rinow
The proof fixes a point and follows the unit geodesic toward the target, tracking the largest radius on which a minimizing geodesic reaches every sphere. A continuity and compactness argument shows this radius cannot stop short, so the geodesic arrives exactly at the target distance.

Conjugate Points and Jacobi Fields

Jacobi equation

$\dfrac{D^2 J}{dt^2}+R(J,\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma}=0$

Key fact

If $K\le 0$, no conjugate points occur along any geodesic of $M$.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Conjugate Points and Curvature
Conjugate points mark where nearby geodesics from a common origin refocus, signalling that the exponential map becomes singular. Jacobi fields measure this spreading, and nonpositive sectional curvature forces them to grow without bound, so no refocusing and hence no conjugate points can occur.

The Cartan-Hadamard Theorem

Theorem

Let $M^n$ be complete, simply connected, with sectional curvature $K\le 0$. Then $\exp_p:T_pM\to M$ is a diffeomorphism.

  • Consequently $M$ is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$.
  • $M$ has no conjugate points anywhere.
  • Any two points are joined by a unique geodesic.

Hypotheses do work

Completeness gives surjectivity; $K\le 0$ gives a local diffeomorphism; simple connectivity makes it global.

Covering view

$\exp_p$ is a covering map; simple connectivity forces it to be one-sheeted.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154The Cartan-Hadamard Theorem
Cartan-Hadamard states that a complete simply connected manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature has its exponential map a diffeomorphism from the whole tangent space. Such a manifold is therefore diffeomorphic to Euclidean space and carries no conjugate points anywhere in it.

$\exp_p$ as a Global Diffeomorphism

Diffeomorphism

$\exp_p: T_pM \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } M,\quad K\le 0$

No folding

The absence of conjugate points keeps $d\exp_p$ nonsingular at every vector.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Exponential Map as Global Diffeomorphism
Under the Cartan-Hadamard hypotheses the exponential map carries straight lines through the origin of the tangent space onto complete geodesics, with no folding or overlap. The flat tangent space becomes a faithful global chart for the curved manifold lying below it.

Consequences and Examples

Hyperbolic space

$\mathbb{H}^n$ with $K=-1$ is the model Hadamard manifold: unique geodesics, exponential volume growth.

Topology fixed

No closed, simply connected manifold can carry $K\le 0$; the global type is $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Comparison geometry

The theorem anchors triangle and volume comparison against constant-curvature models.

Universal covers

Nonpositively curved $M$ has a Hadamard universal cover, central to geometric group theory.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Consequences and Examples
Hyperbolic space realizes Cartan-Hadamard with strictly negative curvature, exhibiting unique geodesics and exponential volume growth. The theorem rules out closed nonpositively curved simply connected manifolds and underlies comparison geometry, covering space arguments, and the study of nonpositively curved groups.

Recap

Completeness

Geodesic and metric completeness coincide; closed bounded sets are compact.

Hopf-Rinow

Complete manifolds carry a minimizing geodesic between any two points.

Cartan-Hadamard

Simple connectivity plus $K\le 0$ makes $\exp_p$ a diffeomorphism onto $M$.

One line

$M$ complete, simply conn., $K\le 0\ \Rightarrow\ M\cong\mathbb{R}^n$

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry · pp. 144-154Recap: Completeness Organizes Global Geometry
To summarize, geodesic and metric completeness coincide by Hopf-Rinow, which also yields minimizing geodesics between all points. Adding simple connectivity and nonpositive curvature gives Cartan-Hadamard, making the exponential map a diffeomorphism and fixing the global topology as Euclidean space.
