The Cartan-Hadamard Universal Cover

Diffeomorphism

The exponential map is a global diffeomorphism onto the cover.

No topology

$\widetilde M$ is contractible, so $\pi_1(\widetilde M)=0$.

Cartan-Hadamard

$K\le 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \exp_p:T_pM \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \widetilde M \cong \mathbb{R}^n$

Consequence

Loops in $M$ become paths between deck-translated lifts.

Printed pp. 253-264Cartan-Hadamard Cover
When a complete manifold has nonpositive curvature its universal cover is diffeomorphic to Euclidean space by the exponential map at any point. This removes all topology from the cover, so every loop in the base lifts to a path between distinct deck-translated copies of a chosen lift.

Deck Transformations Act as Isometries

The action

$\pi_1(M)$ acts on $\widetilde M$ by covering isometries.

No fixed points

Nontrivial elements move every point.

Printed pp. 253-264Deck Transformations as Isometries
The fundamental group acts on the universal cover by deck transformations that are isometries of the lifted metric. Because the action is free and properly discontinuous, no nontrivial element fixes a point, which is the geometric source of every rigidity result in the chapter.

Free Homotopy Meets Conjugacy

Dictionary

Free homotopy classes $\leftrightarrow$ conjugacy classes in $\pi_1$.

Basepoint

Moving the basepoint conjugates the loop's class.

Correspondence

$[\gamma]_{\text{free}} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \{\, h g h^{-1} : h \in \pi_1 \,\}$

Why it matters

Closed geodesics are sought one conjugacy class at a time.

Printed pp. 253-264Free Homotopy and Conjugacy
Free homotopy classes of loops in the base correspond to conjugacy classes in the fundamental group. Choosing a basepoint trades free homotopy for based homotopy, and the conjugacy ambiguity exactly matches the freedom to move the basepoint of a closed curve around the manifold.

One Closed Geodesic per Class

Existence

Minimize length within a nontrivial free homotopy class.

Uniqueness

Negative curvature kills competing minimizers.

Theorem

$M$ compact, $K<0 \Rightarrow$ each $[\gamma]\neq 1$ has a unique closed geodesic

Mechanism

The lift realizes the minimal translation distance of $g$.

Printed pp. 253-264Closed Geodesics Exist
On a compact manifold of negative curvature each nontrivial free homotopy class contains a closed geodesic. One minimizes length within the class; negative curvature forbids competing minimizers, so the closed geodesic representing the class is unique up to its natural parametrization.

Every Isometry Has an Axis

Axis

A nontrivial isometry $g$ translates one geodesic into itself.

Length

Translation length $=$ length of the closed geodesic.

Axis

$\exists\, \text{geodesic } \alpha :\; g\cdot\alpha=\alpha,\quad d(x,gx)=\ell(g)\;\forall x\in\alpha$

Reading

Algebra of $g$ becomes a measurable geometric length.

Printed pp. 253-264The Axis of an Isometry
A deck transformation that is a nontrivial isometry of the Cartan-Hadamard cover translates points along a unique geodesic, its axis. The translation length equals the length of the closed geodesic downstairs, tying the algebra of the group element to a measurable geometric quantity.

Convexity Forces Uniqueness

Convex distance

$t\mapsto d(\alpha(t),\beta(t))$ is convex when $K\le 0$.

No bounded strips

Two distinct geodesics cannot stay close both ways.

Printed pp. 253-264Uniqueness from Convexity
In nonpositive curvature the distance between two geodesics is a convex function of the parameter. Convexity forbids two distinct geodesics from staying a bounded distance apart in both directions, which is precisely why each conjugacy class owns exactly one axis and one closed geodesic.

Commuting Elements Share One Axis

Preservation

If $gh=hg$ then $h$ maps $g$'s axis to an axis of $g$.

Collapse

Uniqueness of the axis forces the two to coincide.

Shared axis lemma

$gh=hg \;\Rightarrow\; \mathrm{axis}(g)=\mathrm{axis}(h)=\alpha$

Reduction

Both act as translations of the single line $\alpha\cong\mathbb{R}$.

Printed pp. 253-264Commuting Isometries Share an Axis
If two axial isometries commute, each preserves the other's axis, and convexity forces those axes to coincide. Commuting elements therefore translate along a single common geodesic, reducing an abelian relation to translations of the real line by fixed amounts.

Preissmann's Theorem

Statement

Every nontrivial abelian subgroup of $\pi_1(M)$ is infinite cyclic.

Hypotheses

$M$ compact and $K<0$ throughout.

Theorem (Preissmann)

$A\le\pi_1(M)$ abelian, $A\neq 1 \;\Rightarrow\; A\cong\mathbb{Z}$

Proof core

Commuting translations of $\alpha\cong\mathbb{R}$ form a discrete, hence cyclic, group.

Printed pp. 253-264Preissmann's Theorem
Preissmann proved that in a compact manifold of strictly negative curvature every nontrivial abelian subgroup of the fundamental group is infinite cyclic. The proof packages the shared-axis lemma: commuting translations of one geodesic form a discrete subgroup of the real line, hence are cyclic.

Corollary: No Flat Tori Inside

Torus group

$\pi_1(T^n)=\mathbb{Z}^n$ is abelian of rank $n$.

Obstruction

Rank $n\ge 2$ cannot embed in $\pi_1(M)$.

Printed pp. 253-264No Flat Tori
Since the fundamental group of an n-torus is the free abelian group of rank n, Preissmann's theorem forbids it from sitting inside the fundamental group of a compact negatively curved manifold whenever n exceeds one. No flat torus can immerse as a totally geodesic subspace.

Worked Picture: Lifting a Saddle Loop

Downstairs

A loop on the saddle tightens to a closed geodesic.

Upstairs

Its lift runs along the axis of the deck element $g$.

Invariant

Read off $\ell(g)=d(x,gx)$ along the axis.

Printed pp. 253-264Worked Picture: A Saddle Loop
On a saddle surface a noncontractible loop tightens to a closed geodesic threading the negatively curved region. We trace its lift to the universal cover, identify the deck translation that closes it, and read off its translation length as the geodesic's length.

Synthesis and Outlook

Chain of ideas

Contractible cover $\to$ unique geodesics $\to$ axes $\to$ cyclic abelian subgroups.

Rigidity

Curvature sign dictates the algebra of $\pi_1(M)$.

Take-away

$K<0,\ M\text{ compact} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{abelian } A\le\pi_1(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$

Onward

Gateway to Mostow rigidity and curvature-group dictionaries.

Printed pp. 253-264Synthesis and Outlook
Negative curvature converts a topological group into rigid geometry: contractible cover, unique closed geodesics, axial isometries, and cyclic abelian subgroups. These tools open onto the Mostow rigidity and the broader program relating curvature bounds to the algebra of the fundamental group.
