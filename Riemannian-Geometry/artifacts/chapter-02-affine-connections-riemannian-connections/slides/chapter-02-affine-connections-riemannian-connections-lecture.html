Why Differentiation Needs a Connection

Naive derivative

Differentiating components of $Y$ along $X$ gives a vector that points off the tangent plane.

Tangential projection

The covariant derivative keeps only the part of the change that lives in $T_pM$.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
On a surface the ordinary derivative of a tangent field leaves the tangent plane, so it is not intrinsic. A connection supplies the missing rule that projects the change back into the tangent space, making directional differentiation a well defined manifold operation.

Definition of an Affine Connection

Map

$\nabla:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)$, written $\nabla_X Y$.

Three axioms

Function-linear in $X$; additive in $Y$; Leibniz rule in $Y$.

Defining properties

$\nabla_{fX+gZ}Y = f\nabla_X Y + g\nabla_Z Y$

$\nabla_X(fY) = f\,\nabla_X Y + (Xf)\,Y$

Reading

Tensorial in the direction $X$, a derivation on the field $Y$.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
An affine connection assigns to each pair of fields a new field, linear over functions in the first slot and a derivation obeying the Leibniz rule in the second. These three axioms are exactly what is needed for a coordinate-free notion of directional derivative.

Christoffel Symbols in Coordinates

Definition

The functions $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ record how basis fields differentiate one another.

Not a tensor

They depend on the chart; their failure to transform tensorially carries the connection.

Coordinate connection

$\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j = \sum_k \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\partial_k$

General fields

$\nabla_X Y = \sum_k\Big(X^i\partial_i Y^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}X^iY^j\Big)\partial_k$

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
In a chart the connection is encoded by the Christoffel symbols, the components of the covariant derivative of one basis field along another. Everything about the connection in that chart is recovered from these functions, which need not be tensor components.

Covariant Derivative Along a Curve

Induced operator

For a field $V$ along $c(t)$, $\nabla$ gives $\frac{DV}{dt}$.

Linearity & Leibniz

It is $\mathbb{R}$-linear and satisfies $\frac{D(fV)}{dt}=f'V+f\frac{DV}{dt}$.

Coordinate formula

$\dfrac{DV^k}{dt} = \dfrac{dV^k}{dt} + \sum_{i,j}\Gamma^k_{ij}\,\dfrac{dc^i}{dt}\,V^j$

Two pieces

An ordinary derivative plus a connection correction along the velocity.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
Restricting a field to a curve, the connection induces a derivative of that field with respect to the curve parameter. Its coordinate expression couples the ordinary derivative of the components with the Christoffel symbols evaluated along the velocity.

Parallel Transport

Parallel field

$V$ is parallel along $c$ exactly when $\frac{DV}{dt}\equiv 0$.

Existence & uniqueness

A linear ODE gives one parallel field for each initial vector.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
A field is parallel along a curve when its covariant derivative vanishes identically. This linear differential equation has a unique solution for each initial vector, defining parallel transport, the connection-dependent way to carry tangent vectors between points along a path.

Compatibility with the Metric

Definition

$\nabla$ is compatible with $g$ when transport preserves inner products.

Equivalent forms

$\nabla g=0$, or the product rule below holds for all fields.

Metric product rule

$X\,\langle Y,Z\rangle = \langle \nabla_X Y, Z\rangle + \langle Y, \nabla_X Z\rangle$

Consequence

Lengths and angles are invariant under parallel transport.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
A connection is compatible with the Riemannian metric when parallel transport preserves inner products, equivalently when the metric is covariantly constant. This makes lengths and angles invariant under transport and ties the connection to the geometry of the manifold.

Symmetry and Torsion

Symmetric connection

The torsion vanishes: covariant derivatives commute up to the bracket.

In coordinates

Symmetry of the lower indices: $\Gamma^k_{ij}=\Gamma^k_{ji}$.

Torsion-free condition

$\nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X = [X,Y]$

Meaning

No intrinsic twisting; the connection respects the bracket of fields.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
A connection is symmetric, or torsion-free, when the covariant derivative is symmetric in its two arguments up to the Lie bracket. In coordinates this says the lower Christoffel indices are symmetric, ruling out any antisymmetric twisting term.

Fundamental Theorem of Riemannian Geometry

Theorem (Levi-Civita)

On a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ there is a unique affine connection that is symmetric and compatible with $g$.

Name

This $\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita, or Riemannian, connection.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
Levi-Civita's theorem states that on any Riemannian manifold there is exactly one connection that is both symmetric and compatible with the metric. Existence and uniqueness together single out the Riemannian connection as the canonical derivative of the geometry.

The Koszul Formula

Idea of proof

Add the metric product rule cyclically and use symmetry to isolate $\nabla_X Y$.

Why unique

The right side uses only $g$ and brackets, so $\nabla$ is forced.

Koszul identity

$2\langle\nabla_X Y,Z\rangle = X\langle Y,Z\rangle + Y\langle Z,X\rangle - Z\langle X,Y\rangle$

$\;+\,\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle - \langle[Y,Z],X\rangle + \langle[Z,X],Y\rangle$

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
The Koszul formula expresses the metric inner product of the covariant derivative purely in terms of the metric, bracket derivatives, and Lie brackets of the three fields. Because the right side is fixed, the connection it defines is forced to be unique.

Christoffel Symbols from the Metric

Coordinate Koszul

In a chart the brackets $[\partial_i,\partial_j]$ vanish, simplifying Koszul.

Working recipe

First derivatives of $g_{ij}$ determine every $\Gamma^k_{ij}$.

Levi-Civita symbols

$\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac12\sum_{m} g^{km}\big(\partial_i g_{jm} + \partial_j g_{im} - \partial_m g_{ij}\big)$

Use

This is what one computes for geodesics and curvature later.

Riemannian Geometry — do CarmoPrinted pp. 48-59
Specializing Koszul to a coordinate basis yields the classical formula giving the Christoffel symbols as combinations of first derivatives of the metric components. This is the working recipe by which the Levi-Civita connection is computed in practice.
