The Index Form $I(V,W)$

Second variation

$I$ is the Hessian of the energy at the geodesic.

Curvature enters

Positive curvature drives $I$ toward negative values.

Index form

$I(V,W)=\displaystyle\int_0^a\!\Big(\langle \tfrac{DV}{dt},\tfrac{DW}{dt}\rangle - \langle R(V,\gamma')\gamma',W\rangle\Big)\,dt$

Symmetry

$I$ is symmetric and bilinear on $\mathcal{V}$.

Printed pp. 242-252The Index Form I(V,W)
The index form is the symmetric bilinear form arising from the second variation of energy. It pairs covariant derivatives against a curvature term, so positive curvature pushes the form negative. Understanding when this form fails to stay positive is the entire problem of the chapter.

Jacobi Fields and the Index Form

Integrate by parts

$I(V,W)=-\int_0^a\langle V, J(W)\rangle\,dt$ on smooth fields.

Null directions

$I(V,\cdot)=0 \iff V$ is a Jacobi field.

Jacobi equation

$\dfrac{D^2 J}{dt^2} + R(J,\gamma')\gamma' = 0$

Linear, 2nd order

A $2n$-dimensional solution space along $\gamma$.

Printed pp. 242-252Jacobi Fields and the Index Form
Jacobi fields are exactly the null directions of the index form among fixed-endpoint fields, satisfying the Jacobi equation. Integrating the index form by parts isolates the Jacobi operator, revealing that degeneracy of the form is governed by these solutions of a linear second-order differential equation.

Conjugate Points

Definition

$\gamma(t_0)$ is conjugate to $\gamma(0)$ if some nonzero Jacobi field vanishes at both.

Multiplicity

$\dim$ of the space of such vanishing Jacobi fields.

Printed pp. 242-252Conjugate Points
Two parameter values are conjugate when a nontrivial Jacobi field vanishes at both. Conjugate points are where neighbouring geodesics refocus and where the geodesic stops being a local energy minimizer. Their multiplicity is the dimension of the space of such vanishing Jacobi fields.

The Index of a Geodesic

Definition

$\operatorname{ind}(\gamma)$ is the max dimension of a subspace where $I$ is negative definite.

Meaning

Independent ways to shorten $\gamma$ to second order.

Index

$\operatorname{ind}(\gamma)=\max\{\dim W : I|_{W}\prec 0\}$

Finite?

A priori possibly infinite — the theorem settles it.

Printed pp. 242-252The Index of a Geodesic
The index is the dimension of a maximal subspace of admissible fields on which the index form is negative definite. It counts independent directions in which the geodesic can be shortened to second order. A priori this number could be infinite; the theorem shows it is not.

Monotonicity of the Index

Endpoint moves

Let the index $i(t)$ depend on the right endpoint $\gamma(t)$.

Step function

$i(t)$ is nondecreasing and constant off conjugate points.

Jump rule

$i(t^{+}) - i(t^{-}) = \operatorname{mult}\big(\gamma(t)\big)$

Printed pp. 242-252Monotonicity of the Index
As the endpoint slides outward along the geodesic, the index is a nondecreasing step function of the parameter. It is constant on intervals free of conjugate points and jumps by the multiplicity each time the moving endpoint crosses a conjugate point to the start.

Synthesis and Consequences

One integer, three ideas

Variational stability, curvature, and Jacobi fields converge in the index.

Minimality fails

Past the first conjugate point $\gamma$ is no longer minimizing.

Morse theory

Indices of geodesics build the homology of the path space.

Takeaway

$\operatorname{ind}(\gamma)$ is finite and equals the conjugate-point count with multiplicity.

Printed pp. 242-252Synthesis and Consequences
The theorem ties variational stability, curvature, and Jacobi fields into one finite integer. It implies geodesics past their first conjugate point are not minimizing and underlies Morse theory on path spaces, where indices assemble into homology of the loop space.
