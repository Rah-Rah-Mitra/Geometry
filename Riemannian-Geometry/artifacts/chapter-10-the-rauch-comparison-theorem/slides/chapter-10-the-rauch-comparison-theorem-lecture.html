Jacobi Fields Encode Spreading

Variation field

Separation of nearby geodesics from $p$ along $\gamma$.

Curvature term

The growth is driven by $R$ through the geodesic.

Jacobi equation

$$\tfrac{D^2 J}{dt^2} + R(J,\gamma')\gamma' = 0$$

Reading

$\lvert J(t)\rvert$ measures the local spread.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Jacobi Fields Encode Spreading
A Jacobi field measures the separation between infinitesimally close geodesics emanating from a point. Its norm grows or contracts according to curvature through the Jacobi equation. Comparison theory turns this differential equation into inequalities by bracketing the curvature term between two constants.

Constant Curvature Models

Scalar Jacobi equation

$$f'' + K f = 0,\quad f(0)=0,\ f'(0)=1$$

Three yardsticks

$K>0$: sine. $K=0$: linear. $K<0$: hyperbolic sine.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Constant Curvature Models
On spaces of constant curvature the Jacobi equation decouples into a scalar equation whose solutions are sine, linear, and hyperbolic sine. These three model functions are the yardsticks: every Jacobi field on a variable curvature manifold is compared against one of them according to its curvature bound.

The Index Form

Second variation

Energy hessian on fields along a geodesic.

Detects

Conjugate points where the index form degenerates.

Index form

$$I(V,V)=\!\int_0^a\!\big(\lvert V'\rvert^2 - \langle R(V,\gamma')\gamma',V\rangle\big)\,dt$$

Key property

Jacobi fields minimize $I$ among fields with fixed ends.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241The Index Form
The index form is the second variation of energy restricted to vector fields along a geodesic. It is the bilinear quantity that detects conjugate points, and the Rauch theorem is proved entirely by comparing index forms of two manifolds, so we record its definition and sign conventions carefully here.

Statement of the Rauch Theorem

Setup

Jacobi fields $J,\tilde J$ on $M,\tilde M$ with $J(0)=\tilde J(0)=0$ and equal speeds.

Hypothesis

Curvature dominates: $K_M \ge \tilde K_{\tilde M}$ along the geodesics.

Conclusion

$$K_M \ge \tilde K \ \Longrightarrow\ \lvert J(t)\rvert \le \lvert \tilde J(t)\rvert$$

Meaning

More curvature shortens Jacobi fields, suppressing spread.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Statement of the Rauch Theorem
The Rauch comparison theorem states that if the sectional curvature of one manifold dominates that of another along corresponding geodesics, then its Jacobi fields are shorter. Greater curvature suppresses spreading; smaller curvature lets geodesics fan out and delays the first conjugate point.

The Index Comparison Argument

Normalize

Rescale so $\lvert J(t_0)\rvert=\lvert\tilde J(t_0)\rvert$ at a test point.

Minimize

Compare index forms using the minimizing property.

Monotone ratio

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\lvert J(t)\rvert^2}{\lvert \tilde J(t)\rvert^2} \le 0$$

Conclusion

The squared-norm ratio is non-increasing, giving the bound.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241The Index Comparison Argument
The proof compares the index forms of the two Jacobi fields after normalizing their initial data. The minimizing property of Jacobi fields among fields with the same boundary values, together with the curvature inequality, forces the ratio of squared norms to be monotone, yielding the comparison.

Curvature Bounds the Spread

$K \le K_1$

Geodesics spread at least as fast as the $K_1$ model.

$K \ge K_2$

Geodesics spread at most as fast as the $K_2$ model.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Curvature Bounds the Spread
Picture two geodesic pencils from a point. Under an upper curvature bound the rays separate at least as fast as the lower constant-curvature model; under a lower bound they separate at most as fast. This is the geometric content read straight off the Jacobi field magnitudes.

Conjugate Points Comparison

Zeros of $J$

A conjugate point is where a nontrivial $J$ vanishes again.

Corollary

Curvature bounds bracket the first conjugate distance.

Bound

$$K \le \delta \ \Longrightarrow\ t_{\mathrm{conj}} \ge \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\delta}}$$

Reading

Smaller curvature postpones the first conjugate point.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Conjugate Points Comparison
Because curvature controls Jacobi field zeros, an upper curvature bound pushes the first conjugate point no nearer than the model distance, while a lower bound brings it no farther. Smaller curvature therefore postpones conjugate points, a corollary used to bound injectivity and prove manifold theorems.

Bounding Jacobi Field Norms

Two-sided squeeze

Norm trapped between model solutions.

Estimate

$$\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\delta}\,t)}{\sqrt{\delta}} \le \lvert J(t)\rvert \le \frac{\sinh(\sqrt{\delta}\,t)}{\sqrt{\delta}}$$

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Bounding Jacobi Field Norms
Integrating the monotone ratio yields explicit two-sided bounds: the Jacobi field norm is squeezed between the sine, linear, and hyperbolic-sine model solutions chosen by the curvature bracket. These inequalities are the quantitative heart of every comparison estimate that follows in the chapter.

Comparison of Metric Balls

Exp differential

$d(\exp_p)$ is built from Jacobi fields.

Volume

Bounding $\lvert J\rvert$ bounds how $\exp_p$ distorts volume.

Distortion

$$\big(d\exp_p\big)_v(w) = \tfrac{1}{\lvert v\rvert}\,J(\lvert v\rvert)$$

Reading

Lower curvature gives larger balls and faster volume growth.

do Carmo, Riemannian Geometry — pp. 210-241Comparison of Metric Balls
Since the exponential map differential is governed by Jacobi fields, bounding their norms bounds how the exponential map distorts volume. Lower curvature yields larger metric balls and faster volume growth; upper curvature yields smaller balls, giving the Bishop-type volume comparison estimates.
