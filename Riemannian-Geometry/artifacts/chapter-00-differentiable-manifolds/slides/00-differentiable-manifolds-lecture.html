Riemannian GeometryChapter 0

Charts and Atlases

Chart

A homeomorphism $\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ from an open set of $M$.

Atlas

A family of charts whose domains cover all of $M$.

Dimension

The integer $n$ is constant on each connected component.

Coordinate map

$\varphi(q)=\big(x^1(q),\dots,x^n(q)\big)$

Reading

Coordinates are local labels; in general no single chart covers $M$.

Printed pp. 1-34Charts and Atlases
A manifold is glued from coordinate patches. Emphasize that a chart is a homeomorphism onto an open set of Euclidean space, and an atlas is a covering family of such charts. The number n is the dimension, fixed and constant on each connected piece. Coordinates are local, never global in general.

Smooth Transition Maps

Overlap

On $U\cap V$ two charts give two coordinate descriptions of the same point.

Change of coordinates

$\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:\varphi(U\cap V)\to\psi(U\cap V)$

Differentiable structure

Charts are compatible when every such transition map is $C^\infty$.

Printed pp. 1-34Smooth Transition Maps
On overlaps two charts disagree about coordinates, and the change-of-coordinate map records the disagreement. The differentiable structure lives entirely in the demand that every such transition map be smooth. Walk through the composition explicitly so students see it is a map between open subsets of Euclidean space.

Smooth Maps Between Manifolds

Read through charts

$f:M\to N$ is smooth if its coordinate expression is smooth.

Local representative

$\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}^n$

Well defined

Independent of compatible charts, since transitions are smooth.

Diffeomorphism

A smooth bijection with smooth inverse: the right notion of sameness.

Printed pp. 1-34Smooth Maps Between Manifolds
Smoothness of a map between manifolds is defined by reading it through charts on both sides. Stress that the definition is independent of which compatible charts you pick, because transition maps are smooth. A diffeomorphism is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse, the natural notion of sameness for manifolds.

Curves and Velocities

Smooth curve

$\alpha:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M$ with $\alpha(0)=p$.

Velocity in coordinates

$\dot{\alpha}(0)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0}\big(\varphi\circ\alpha\big)(t)$

Equivalence

Curves agreeing to first order carry the same tangent vector.

Printed pp. 1-34Curves and Velocities
Before defining tangent vectors abstractly, ground them in curves. A smooth curve through a point has a velocity, and in coordinates that velocity is just the derivative of the coordinate expression. Two curves with the same coordinate velocity should be regarded as carrying the same tangent vector at the point.

The Tangent Space

Definition

$T_pM$ is the set of velocities $\dot{\alpha}(0)$ of curves through $p$.

Vector space

Real, with $\dim T_pM=\dim M=n$.

Coordinate basis

$T_pM=\operatorname{span}\Big\{\partial_1\big|_p,\dots,\partial_n\big|_p\Big\}$

Intrinsic

Tangent vectors live on $M$, not as arrows in any ambient space.

Printed pp. 1-34The Tangent Space
Here is the central construction. The tangent space is the set of velocities of curves through the point, and it is a real vector space of the same dimension as the manifold. The coordinate basis consists of the partial derivative operators. Insist that tangent vectors are intrinsic, not arrows in any ambient space.

Tangent Vectors as Derivations

Algebraic view

A vector acts on smooth functions as a linear operator.

Leibniz rule

$v(fg)=f(p)\,v(g)+g(p)\,v(f)$

Expansion

$v=\sum_i v^i\,\partial_i\big|_p,\qquad v(f)=\sum_i v^i\,\partial_i f(p)$

Equivalence

Derivations and curve-velocities describe the same $T_pM$.

Printed pp. 1-34Tangent Vectors as Derivations
The derivation viewpoint makes tangent vectors algebraic. A tangent vector is a linear operator on smooth functions obeying the Leibniz product rule. Show that this matches the velocity picture exactly, and that the partials form a basis. This abstraction is what lets us do calculus with no ambient Euclidean space.

The Differential of a Map

Pushforward

$df_p:T_pM\to T_{f(p)}N$ is linear.

On velocities

$df_p\big(\dot{\alpha}(0)\big)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_0(f\circ\alpha)(t)$

Matrix = Jacobian

$\big[df_p\big]^j_{\,i}=\dfrac{\partial f^j}{\partial x^i}(p)$

Chain rule

Functorial: $d(g\circ f)=dg\circ df$.

Printed pp. 1-34The Differential of a Map
A smooth map pushes tangent vectors forward. The differential is the linear map on tangent spaces obtained by differentiating coordinate expressions, with the Jacobian as its matrix. Emphasize the chain rule reading: differentiate the curve, then the map, to recover the velocity of the image curve. This is functorial.

Immersions and Embeddings

Immersion

$df_p$ injective for every $p$: rank equals $\dim M$.

Embedding

Injective immersion that is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Caution

Injective immersion alone need not embed.

Printed pp. 1-34Immersions and Embeddings
Rank of the differential classifies maps. An immersion has injective differential everywhere; an embedding is an injective immersion that is also a homeomorphism onto its image. Use the figure-eight to show why topological injectivity matters: an injective immersion can still fail to be an embedding when the image self-approaches.
Riemannian GeometryChapter 0

Submanifolds

Definition

The image of an embedding; locally a coordinate slice.

Slice chart

$S\cap U=\{x^{k+1}=\dots=x^n=0\}$

Regular value theorem

$F^{-1}(c)$ is a submanifold when $c$ is a regular value

Example

$S^{n}=\{\,|x|^2=1\,\}$ as a level set in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.

Printed pp. 1-34Submanifolds
Embeddings produce submanifolds, the geometric objects we actually study. Stress that a regular submanifold inherits charts in which it looks like a coordinate slice. The regular value theorem is the practical engine: preimages of regular values are submanifolds, which is how spheres and level sets enter the picture so naturally.

Vector Fields and the Lie Bracket

Vector field

A smooth assignment $p\mapsto X_p\in T_pM$, acting on functions.

Lie bracket

$[X,Y]f=X(Yf)-Y(Xf)$

Closure

Second-order terms cancel; $[X,Y]$ is again a vector field.

Printed pp. 1-34Vector Fields and the Lie Bracket
A vector field smoothly assigns a tangent vector to each point and acts as a first-order operator on functions. The Lie bracket measures the failure of two such operators to commute, and remarkably it is again a vector field. Verify in coordinates that the second-order terms cancel, leaving a genuine first-order object.

Orientation and Recap

Orientability

Choose an atlas with all transition Jacobians of positive determinant.

Consistency condition

$\det D(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1})>0$ on every overlap

Obstruction

The Mobius band admits no such atlas.

Dependency chain

  • Charts $\Rightarrow$ smooth structure
  • Smoothness $\Rightarrow$ tangent spaces, differentials
  • Differentials $\Rightarrow$ immersions, embeddings, submanifolds
  • Fields $\Rightarrow$ Lie bracket; atlases $\Rightarrow$ orientation

Next

Add a metric: lengths, angles, and curvature.

Printed pp. 1-34Orientation and Recap
Orientation asks whether charts can be chosen so all transition Jacobians have positive determinant. Not every manifold admits this, the Mobius band being the classic obstruction. Close by tracing the dependency chain: charts give smoothness, smoothness gives tangent spaces and differentials, and those give immersions, fields, and orientation.
