The Geodesic Equation

Zero acceleration

The velocity is parallel along the curve, so it does not accelerate.

In coordinates

A second order ODE coupled by the Christoffel symbols.

Geodesic definition

$\dfrac{D}{dt}\dfrac{d\gamma}{dt} = 0$

Local form

$\ddot{x}^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\dot{x}^i\dot{x}^j = 0$

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87The Geodesic Equation
A geodesic is a curve whose velocity is parallel along itself, so its covariant acceleration vanishes identically. In a coordinate chart this becomes a second order system coupling the coordinates through the Christoffel symbols, and existence theory for ODEs gives a unique geodesic from each prescribed initial position and velocity.

The Geodesic Flow

Lift to $TM$

Pair each point with its velocity to get a first order field.

Geodesic field $G$

Its integral curves project to geodesics on $M$.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87The Geodesic Flow
Lifting the second order geodesic equation to the tangent bundle produces a first order vector field whose integral curves are exactly the geodesics. This geodesic field generates a flow on the tangent bundle, and smooth dependence on initial data ensures geodesics vary smoothly with both their starting point and their starting velocity.

Homogeneity of Geodesics

Rescale velocity

Doubling speed halves the time to cover the same image.

Why it matters

Any velocity can be shrunk until time one is reached.

Homogeneity lemma

$\gamma(t,\,p,\,a v) = \gamma(a t,\,p,\,v)$

Consequence

Small enough $v$ gives a geodesic defined on all of $[0,1]$.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87Homogeneity of Geodesics
Rescaling the initial velocity rescales the geodesic in time by the same factor, so a geodesic traversed twice as fast covers the same image curve in half the time. This homogeneity property lets us shrink any tangent vector until the corresponding geodesic is defined on the full unit interval, which the exponential map needs.

The Exponential Map

Definition

Follow the geodesic with velocity $v$ for unit time.

Domain

An open neighborhood of the origin in $T_pM$.

Exponential map

$\exp_p(v) = \gamma(1,\,p,\,v) = \gamma\!\big(|v|,\,p,\,v/|v|\big)$

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87The Exponential Map
The exponential map sends a tangent vector to the point reached at time one along the geodesic leaving the base point in that direction. It is defined on an open neighborhood of the origin of the tangent space, depends smoothly on its argument, and packages the entire geodesic spray into a single map from velocities to points.

A Local Diffeomorphism

Derivative at zero

$d(\exp_p)_0$ is the identity on $T_pM$.

Inverse function theorem

So $\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism near the origin.

Normal neighborhood

$\exp_p : B_\varepsilon(0) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} U \subset M$

Reading

$U$ is a normal neighborhood; $B_\varepsilon(p)=\exp_p(B_\varepsilon(0))$ is a normal ball.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87Local Diffeomorphism
The derivative of the exponential map at the origin is the identity, so by the inverse function theorem it restricts to a diffeomorphism from a small ball in the tangent space onto a normal neighborhood of the base point. This is the precise sense in which geodesics deliver honest local coordinates around every point of the manifold.

Normal Coordinates

Definition

Pull coordinates back through $\exp_p^{-1}$.

Radial geodesics

Lines through the origin map to geodesics from $p$.

At the center $p$

$g_{ij}(p) = \delta_{ij}, \qquad \Gamma^k_{ij}(p) = 0$

Use

Tensor computations simplify dramatically at the center point.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87Normal Coordinates
Pulling coordinates back through the inverse exponential map gives normal coordinates centered at the point. In these coordinates the metric agrees with the identity at the center, all Christoffel symbols vanish there, and radial straight lines through the origin become the geodesics emanating from that point, which simplifies many local computations.

The Gauss Lemma

Statement

Geodesic spheres are orthogonal to the radial geodesics.

Key tool

This orthogonality drives the minimizing proof.

Gauss lemma

$\big\langle d(\exp_p)_v(v),\; d(\exp_p)_v(w)\big\rangle = \langle v, w\rangle$

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87The Gauss Lemma
The Gauss lemma states that the image of a sphere in the tangent space meets the radial geodesics orthogonally. Equivalently, geodesics leaving a point stay perpendicular to the geodesic spheres surrounding it, which is the geometric key that lets us discard angular motion in the minimizing argument coming next.

Geodesics Minimize Locally

Claim

Inside a normal ball the radial geodesic is the shortest curve.

Strategy

Split a competitor into radial and angular parts.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87Geodesics Minimize Locally
Inside a normal ball the radial geodesic from the center to a point is strictly shorter than every other piecewise smooth curve joining them. The proof splits a competing curve into radial and angular components and uses the Gauss lemma to discard the angular contribution as length wasted off the direct path.

The Minimizing Estimate

Polar geodesic form

Write the metric as radial plus angular pieces.

Drop the angle

The angular term only adds length.

Length bound

$\ell(c) \ge \int_a^b \left|\tfrac{dr}{dt}\right| dt \ge r(b)-r(a)$

Equality

Holds only when the angle is fixed and $r$ increases: the radial geodesic.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87The Minimizing Estimate
Writing a competing curve in polar geodesic coordinates, its length integral dominates the integral of the radial speed, which already equals the geodesic distance between the endpoints. Equality forces the angular part to vanish and the radius to increase monotonically, so only the radial geodesic actually achieves the shortest possible length.

Totally Normal Neighborhoods

Definition

Contained in a normal ball of each of its own points.

Existence

One surrounds every point of the manifold.

Uniform property

$\forall\, q \in W:\; W \subset B_\delta(q) \;=\; \exp_q(B_\delta(0))$

Payoff

Any two points of $W$ join by a unique minimizing geodesic inside $W$.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87Totally Normal Neighborhoods
A totally normal neighborhood is one contained in a normal ball of each of its own points, so any two points inside it are joined by a unique minimizing geodesic that stays within. Such neighborhoods exist around every point of the manifold, established by a uniform continuity argument applied to the exponential map jointly in position and velocity.
Riemannian GeometryChapter 3

Convex Neighborhoods

Definition

The connecting geodesic between any two points stays inside.

Mechanism

Squared distance is strictly convex along geodesics.

do Carmo, printed pp. 60-87Convex Neighborhoods
A neighborhood is convex when the connecting geodesic between any two of its points lies entirely within it. Bounding the second derivative of squared distance along geodesics shows that sufficiently small geodesic balls are strictly convex, which completes the local picture of geodesics as uniquely determined, well behaved shortest paths.
