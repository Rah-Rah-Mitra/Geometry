Variations of Geodesics

Family

A smooth map $f(s,t)$ with each $t\mapsto f(s,t)$ a geodesic.

Variation field

$J(t)=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial s}(0,t)$ is the transverse derivative.

Geodesic variation

$f:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times[0,a]\to M,\quad \tfrac{D}{\partial t}\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}=0$

Idea

The field $J$ encodes how the geodesic deforms as $s$ varies.

Printed pp. 110-123Variations of Geodesics
We begin with a smooth family of curves sharing a base geodesic. The variation field is the transverse derivative of this family at the base parameter. When every curve in the family is itself a geodesic, the variation field is forced to satisfy a distinguished differential equation derived below.
Riemannian GeometryChapter 5

The Jacobi Equation

Statement

Every geodesic variation field $J$ obeys this curvature equation.

Why curvature

Swapping covariant derivatives introduces the Riemann tensor.

Jacobi equation

$\dfrac{D^2 J}{dt^2} + R\!\left(J,\gamma'\right)\gamma' = 0$

Origin

$\tfrac{D}{\partial s}\tfrac{D}{\partial t}\gamma' - \tfrac{D}{\partial t}\tfrac{D}{\partial s}\gamma' = R(\partial_s,\partial_t)\gamma'$.

Printed pp. 110-123The Jacobi Equation
Differentiating the geodesic equation across the family and applying the symmetry of mixed covariant derivatives produces the Jacobi equation. The curvature term appears because the order of covariant differentiation in the transverse and tangential directions differs precisely by the Riemann tensor acting on the variation field.

Linearization of the Geodesic Flow

Linearized flow

The Jacobi equation is the geodesic equation differentiated about $\gamma$.

Decomposition

Split $J = J^{\top} + J^{\perp}$ along and across $\gamma'$.

Tangential part

$J^{\top}(t) = (a + bt)\,\gamma'(t)$

Normal part

$J^{\perp}$ carries every contribution of curvature.

Printed pp. 110-123Linearization of the Geodesic Flow
The Jacobi equation is exactly the linearization of the nonlinear geodesic equation about a fixed geodesic. Solutions live in the tangent space along the curve, so the field decomposes into a tangential part that is affine in the parameter and a normal part that carries all the curvature information.
Riemannian GeometryChapter 5

Existence and Initial Data

Linear ODE

Second order and linear, so solutions exist for all $t$.

Dimension

The Jacobi fields form a $2n$-dimensional vector space.

Initial data

$J(0)=v,\qquad \dfrac{DJ}{dt}(0)=w$

Uniqueness

$(v,w)\in T_pM\times T_pM$ determines $J$ uniquely.

Printed pp. 110-123Existence and Initial Data
Because the Jacobi equation is linear of second order, a Jacobi field along a geodesic is uniquely determined by its initial value and the initial value of its covariant derivative. The space of Jacobi fields therefore has dimension twice that of the manifold, a fact used repeatedly in later arguments.

Jacobi Fields from the Exponential Map

Radial family

Fix $p$, vary the launch velocity $v(s)=v + s w$.

Vanishing at $p$

The resulting field satisfies $J(0)=0$.

Derivative of $\exp_p$

$J(t) = \big(d\,\exp_p\big)_{tv}\!\left(t\,w\right)$

Reading

Jacobi fields are the concrete differential of $\exp_p$.

Printed pp. 110-123Jacobi Fields from the Exponential Map
Fixing the base point and varying only the initial velocity yields a Jacobi field vanishing at the origin. Its value at parameter one equals the differential of the exponential map applied to a tangent vector, so Jacobi fields are the concrete derivative of the exponential map along radial geodesics.

Conjugate Points

Definition

$q=\gamma(t_0)$ is conjugate to $p$ if some nonzero $J$ vanishes at both ends.

Singular $\exp_p$

$\big(d\exp_p\big)_{t_0 v}$ fails to be injective at $q$.

Printed pp. 110-123Conjugate Points
A point is conjugate to the base point along a geodesic when a nontrivial Jacobi field vanishes at both endpoints. Equivalently the differential of the exponential map becomes singular there, so radial geodesics that started apart are refocused, signalling the failure of the exponential map to be a local diffeomorphism.
Riemannian GeometryChapter 5

Multiplicity of Conjugate Points

Definition

Multiplicity is the dimension of the space of Jacobi fields vanishing at both ends.

Kernel view

It equals $\dim\ker\big(d\exp_p\big)_{t_0 v}$.

Multiplicity

$\mu(q) = \dim\{\,J : J(0)=J(t_0)=0\,\} \le n-1$

Bound

Tangential fields never vanish twice, so $\mu \le n-1$.

Printed pp. 110-123Multiplicity of Conjugate Points
The multiplicity of a conjugate point is the dimension of the space of Jacobi fields vanishing at both endpoints, equivalently the dimension of the kernel of the exponential differential. This integer is finite and counts how many independent directions of geodesics refocus simultaneously at the conjugate point.

Curvature Controls Spreading

Constant curvature

For a unit normal field, $\|J\|$ solves $j'' + Kj = 0$.

Sign of $K$

$K<0$ spreads geodesics; $K>0$ refocuses them.

Printed pp. 110-123Curvature Controls Spreading
For a normal Jacobi field its second covariant derivative equals minus curvature times the field. Negative curvature forces convex growth so geodesics diverge exponentially, while positive curvature acts like a restoring force, bending neighbours back together and producing conjugate points within bounded distance.

Conjugate Points on the Sphere

Curvature one

On $S^n$ the field $|J|=\sin t$ along a great circle.

Refocus at $\pi$

$|J|$ vanishes again at $t=\pi$, the antipode.

Spherical Jacobi field

$J(t) = \sin(t)\,E(t),\qquad J(0)=J(\pi)=0$

Multiplicity

The antipode is conjugate with multiplicity $n-1$.

Printed pp. 110-123Conjugate Points on the Sphere
On the unit sphere every great circle leaving a pole refocuses exactly at the antipode. The Jacobi field grows like the sine of the parameter, vanishing again after distance pi, so the antipode is conjugate to the pole with multiplicity equal to the dimension of the sphere minus one.
