Basic Object

Open Chains And Closed Chains

Definition

A polygonal chain is an ordered list of vertices p0, p1, ..., pn joined by fixed segments. Its link lengths are li = |pi - pi-1|.

Configuration

Once translations and rotations are factored out, a planar open chain with n links is controlled by its turn angles.

fixed lengths, variable joints3 / 16
Emphasize the distinction between the data and the drawing. The list of lengths is not the same as a configuration. A configuration is one realized placement of those links, usually considered up to rigid motion so the interesting variables are internal.
Cauchy's Arm Lemma

Opening Angles Cannot Bring The Endpoint Closer

Cauchy Arm Lemma, Informal Form

For two planar arms with the same link lengths, increasing the relevant joint angles cannot decrease the distance between the endpoints.

|p_n - p_0| <= |q_n - q_0|

The inequality compares complete arms, not a single hinge in isolation.

monotonicity under opening4 / 16
State this as a monotonicity principle. The proof idea is geometric: opening angles accumulates outward. The important classroom warning is that the conclusion is about the final endpoint distance, not about every vertex moving monotonically in one coordinate direction.
Proof Roadmap

Why The Arm Lemma Is A Global Statement

Fix The Links

Both arms have the same sequence of segment lengths.

Compare Angles

Each corresponding turn is no smaller in the opened arm.

Triangulate The Fan

Introduce diagonals from the initial vertex and compare triangles.

Propagate Distances

Induct along the chain so the terminal diagonal cannot shrink.

Common Misread

The lemma does not say every deformation that looks more extended is certified. The angle inequalities are the certificate.

Computational Use

Distance sweeps over angle families reveal monotone regions and expose where a proposed comparison lacks hypotheses.

proof as propagation5 / 16
Use this slide to avoid presenting the arm lemma as a black box. The proof strategy is a controlled induction through auxiliary triangles. Each step turns an angle comparison into a diagonal comparison, so the endpoint inequality is accumulated rather than guessed.
Configuration Space

Angles Are Coordinates, But Geometry Supplies The Constraints

Planar Open Chain

After pinning the first vertex and first link, a configuration can be encoded by (theta1, ..., theta n-1) on a torus of angle choices.

Feasible Versus Legal

Fixed lengths are automatic in angle coordinates. Avoiding collisions, staying inside a region, or closing a loop are extra constraints.

continuous space from finite data6 / 16
The angle coordinate picture is useful because it separates constraints. The bar lengths are built in; geometric legality is not. This is the right moment to connect the chapter with algorithmic search, because algorithms often move through this space while testing constraints.
Reachability

The Endpoint Lives In An Annulus

Reachable Radii

For link lengths l1, ..., ln, the terminal distance r satisfies an upper bound by total length and a lower bound by the excess of the longest link, if any.

max(0, Lmax - (L - Lmax)) <= r <= L

Rotating a realized endpoint fills the corresponding circle of radius r.

triangle inequality as a region7 / 16
The annulus is the triangle inequality made visual. The outer radius is the fully aligned chain. The inner radius appears only when the longest link is too long to be cancelled by the rest. This picture is a good place to ask students to predict examples before naming the formula.
From Radii To Paths

Reachability Is More Than A Static Set

Path Question

If two endpoints are reachable, can a collision-free motion carry the chain from one placement to another?

Algorithmic Lens

The endpoint annulus is a projection of the configuration space. It answers where the terminal point can be, but not which route the whole chain takes.

projection loses information8 / 16
This slide prevents an overclaim. The annulus describes possible endpoints, but it collapses many configurations to the same point. Motion planning needs the lifted path in configuration space, where self-intersection and obstacles can matter. Ask students what information about the middle joints has disappeared in the projection.
Folding Verification

Local Turn Signs Are Fast, But They Are Not The Whole Story

Orientation Test

The sign of a cross product detects whether three consecutive vertices turn left, turn right, or become collinear.

sign((b - a) x (c - b))
Verification Gap

A sequence of legal local turns may still create a global crossing elsewhere in the chain.

local tests versus global geometry9 / 16
Students often trust local predicates too much because they are exact and cheap. The point is to respect them without overusing them: orientation signs are excellent local witnesses, but global simplicity requires segment intersection checks or stronger structural invariants.
Certificates

A Useful Folding Audit Has Three Layers

Layer 1: Lengths

Every proposed move preserves each bar length within numerical tolerance or exactly by construction.

Layer 2: Turns

The sign pattern and angle bounds match the intended fold or comparison theorem.

Layer 3: Intersections

Nonadjacent segment pairs are tested so global simplicity is not inferred from local behavior.

Layer 4: Continuity

A path should change configurations continuously; endpoint snapshots alone do not certify a motion.

Instructor Prompt

Which layer would catch a chain that keeps all lengths but passes through itself halfway through the motion?

building reliable evidence10 / 16
Frame verification as layered evidence. Length preservation is necessary but too weak. Turn signs add local structure. Intersection tests catch the global failure mode. Continuity reminds students that a pair of legal drawings does not automatically produce a legal deformation between them.
Straightening

In The Plane, Open Chains Can Be Untangled

Planar Straightening Principle

A simple open polygonal chain in the plane admits a continuous motion to a straight configuration while preserving lengths and avoiding self-intersection.

Intuition

The motion increases global separation while maintaining simplicity, much like carefully opening an articulated ruler.

planar chains are not locked11 / 16
Keep the theorem at a lecture-ready level rather than proving the full result. The main conceptual contrast is that planarity plus openness prevents permanent locking. The proof machinery is deep, but the geometric message is accessible: a simple planar arm can be unfolded without tearing or crossing.
Spatial Warning

Three Dimensions Add Room, But Also Add Locks

Locking Phenomenon

A spatial chain may have more ambient room and still be trapped by its own three-dimensional arrangement.

Projection Trap

A planar drawing of a spatial linkage omits over-under information, so it can make a locked configuration look harmless.

ambient dimension changes the problem12 / 16
This slide is deliberately cautionary. More dimension does not simply mean easier motion. Spatial chains carry knot-like information in their over-under relationships, and a projection can erase exactly the feature that blocks a motion. Emphasize that a correct certificate must remember depth ordering, not just the planar shadow.
Discrete Curve Shortening

Move Vertices To Reduce Excess Turning

Process

A discrete shortening step replaces local bends by shorter or straighter alternatives while preserving the endpoints or other required boundary data.

Why It Helps

The flow turns qualitative untangling into measurable descent: perimeter, total turn, or another energy should decrease.

descent as geometry13 / 16
Present curve shortening as a computational habit of mind. Instead of directly guessing a final straightening, define a local operation that reduces an energy. The mathematical task is then to prove the operation preserves legality and cannot stall at a bad shape.
Worked Micro-Example

A Three-Link Arm: Bounds, Angles, And A Sanity Check

Lengths

Let the links have lengths 4, 3, 2. Then L = 9 and the longest link is 4.

0 <= r <= 9

There is no forced inner gap because the shorter links can cancel the longest one.

Questions To Ask Students

  • Which configuration reaches r = 9?
  • How can the chain fold to bring the endpoint to the start?
  • What changes if the lengths are 7, 3, 2?
Sanity Check

Endpoint bounds are necessary for a target point, but a motion plan still needs collision and continuity checks.

small numbers, full concept stack14 / 16
This example is intentionally small enough to do aloud. For 4, 3, and 2 the reachable radii start at zero. Switching to 7, 3, and 2 creates an inner radius of two, which makes the annulus formula memorable and testable.
Synthesis

What To Remember About Polygonal Chains

Geometric Principle

Fixed-length chains turn metric constraints into motion questions. The right invariant depends on the question: distance, angle, crossing, or energy.

Computational Principle

Most algorithms alternate between generating candidate configurations and certifying that required invariants have survived.

invariants organize motion15 / 16
Tie the chapter together by naming the invariants. Lengths define the linkage, angles support comparison lemmas, crossings protect simplicity, and energies guide deformation. The same chain can look very different depending on which invariant is currently doing the work.
Closing Exercise

Design A Certificate For A Proposed Motion

Task

Given two drawings of the same simple open chain, propose a sequence of intermediate configurations and list what must be checked at every step.

Deliverable

A short certificate with preserved lengths, angle or distance claims, nonintersection tests, and a reason the sequence approximates a continuous motion.

discussion prompt16 / 16
End with a certificate-design exercise rather than a computation. The goal is for students to articulate what evidence would make a motion trustworthy: bar lengths, legal local turns, absence of crossings, and continuity. This also previews why computational geometry needs both construction and verification.
