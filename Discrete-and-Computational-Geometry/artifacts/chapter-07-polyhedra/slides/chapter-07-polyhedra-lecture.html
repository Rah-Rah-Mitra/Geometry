Three Ledgers For One Object

Incidence Ledger

\(V-E+F\)

Records how faces meet along edges and vertices. It is insensitive to the exact coordinates of the polyhedron.

Angle Ledger

\(2\pi-\sum\theta_i\)

Records curvature as missing angle at vertices. It turns a flat-faced surface into a finite curvature table.

Cut/Gluing Ledger

trees + matches

Records how a surface can be opened into the plane or assembled from polygons.

Regular Vertex Inequality

Convex Corner Test

If \(q\) regular \(p\)-gons meet at a convex vertex, then \(q\alpha_p<2\pi\), where \(\alpha_p=\frac{(p-2)\pi}{p}\).

Why Strict?

Equality makes a flat tiling around the vertex. Convexity needs a positive angular gap.

Finite Consequence

The inequality becomes \((p-2)(q-2)<4\), leaving exactly five regular convex cases.

Local inequality produces global scarcityDefect \(>0\) is the curvature seed
Use the regular vertex inequality as the first example of local geometry controlling a global classification. If q regular p-gons meet at a convex vertex, the total angle must be less than a full turn. This single strict inequality leaves only a small finite menu.

Euler Characteristic

Euler Formula

For a convex polyhedron, \(\chi=V-E+F=2\).

What It Sees

Euler characteristic sees the topological type of the boundary, not the exact face shapes.

What It Ignores

A cube and a wildly skewed convex hexahedron have the same incidence ledger when their face structure agrees.

Topology from finite incidence countsNext: what happens when the topology is not a sphere?
Move from local angle arithmetic to global incidence arithmetic. For convex polyhedra the boundary has the topology of a sphere, so vertices minus edges plus faces equals two. The point is not the number alone but the fact that many different decompositions preserve it.

Genus Changes The Ledger

Orientable Surface Ledger

\(\chi=2-2g\).

Common Mistake

\(V-E+F=2\) is not a universal polyhedral-surface formula. It is the genus-zero convex boundary row.

Computational Habit

Record the surface type before using an Euler check. Otherwise a correct mesh can look arithmetically wrong.

Euler characteristic is topologicalConvex polyhedra occupy the sphere-like case
Contrast the sphere ledger with surfaces that have handles. The orientable formula two minus twice the genus is a reminder that Euler characteristic is topological data, while convex polyhedra live in the genus zero row. This slide prevents students from overextending V minus E plus F equals two.

Curvature Lives At Vertices

Vertex Defect

\(\kappa(v)=2\pi-\sum_{\text{faces at }v}\theta_f(v)\).

Faces

Each face is Euclidean. Inside a face there is no intrinsic curvature.

Edges

Across an edge, two adjacent faces can be unfolded into one plane locally.

Vertices

The angle gap cannot be unfolded away. That gap is the curvature assigned to the vertex.

Curvature is a finite vertex tableDefect is positive for convex vertices
Introduce polyhedral curvature as concentrated at vertices. Each face is flat and each edge can be unfolded locally, so the only place curvature remains is the angular gap at a vertex. The diagram asks students to see curvature as missing angle rather than as smooth bending.

sphere-like convex surface

Polyhedral Gauss-Bonnet

Finite Curvature Sum

\(\sum_v \kappa(v)=4\pi\) for a convex polyhedron.

Executable Check

Add every vertex defect. The answer is independent of how the curvature is distributed.

Interpretation

Euler characteristic and total curvature are speaking about the same sphere-like topology through different ledgers.

Angle defects form a finite Gauss-Bonnet proof objectNotebook check: total defect equals \(4\pi\)
State the executable version of Gauss-Bonnet for a convex sphere-like polyhedron. Add the vertex defects and the total is four pi, regardless of whether the surface is a tetrahedron, cube, or icosahedron. The global invariant is recovered by a finite sum.

Rigidity Is A Different Question

Cauchy Rigidity, Informal Form

A convex polyhedron is rigid when corresponding faces remain congruent.

Not Euler

Rigidity is not determined by \(V-E+F\). It asks whether a continuous flex exists.

Proof Signal

Angle comparisons around a vertex lead to sign patterns; impossible sign-change behavior rules out flexing.

Same faces, same edge lengths, no convex flexThe certificate is a contradiction pattern
Separate incidence and curvature checks from rigidity. Cauchy rigidity says a convex polyhedron with matching corresponding faces cannot flex into a noncongruent convex polyhedron. The proof idea follows sign changes around a cycle, so the visual focuses on a graph certificate rather than a 3D animation.

Flexibility And Volume

Convex Hypothesis Matters

Cauchy rigidity is a convex theorem. Dropping convexity changes the problem qualitatively.

Flexible Examples

Nonconvex polyhedra can admit a continuous flex while preserving face shapes.

Bellows Contrast

For flexible polyhedra, volume can remain constant during the flex. Shape motion and volume change are different questions.

Rigidity, flexibility, and volume preservation are distinct claimsUse hypotheses carefully
Give students room for the important contrast: nonconvex polyhedra can be flexible even though convex ones are rigid under Cauchy's hypotheses. Bellows-type results add that volume can remain fixed during a flex. The lesson is that face congruence, rigidity, convexity, and volume are distinct statements.

Cut Trees And Unfoldings

Dual Tree Condition

For \(F\) faces, an uncut adjacency tree has \(F-1\) dual edges and connects all faces.

Why A Tree?

Cycles keep material locked around a loop; disconnected choices separate the development.

Important Limitation

A spanning tree is a necessary combinatorial certificate for a net, not a guarantee that the planar layout avoids overlap.

Durer-style unfolding questions become graph questions firstGeometry returns when checking overlap
Turn to planar developments. A cut set that opens a polyhedron without disconnecting the faces corresponds to a spanning tree in the dual graph. For a cube this means five dual edges are chosen, but the spanning-tree condition alone does not guarantee a nonoverlapping net.

Shortest Paths Become Straight

Development Principle

Unfold the crossed faces; a shortest path segment becomes a straight segment in the plane.

Local Reason

Crossing an edge can be made flat by rotating one face into the plane of its neighbor.

Computational Payoff

Candidate paths can be tested in a planar development before being folded back onto the surface.

Geodesic reasoning becomes planar geometry after the right cutsCut choices encode which paths are visible
Explain why unfolding is useful for geodesics. When a shortest path crosses a sequence of faces, unfolding those faces into one plane turns the path into a straight segment. The apparent 3D problem becomes a planar straightness test after the right cuts are made.

Cut Loci And Source Unfolding

Source Point

Choose a point \(s\) on the surface and organize shortest paths that start at \(s\).

Cut Locus

The branching set marks where shortest-path choices stop being single-valued.

Source Unfolding

Cutting along that locus produces a planar layout where paths from \(s\) are straight radial segments.

Shortest-path uniqueness fails along the branching setCut along it to flatten from a source
Describe the cut locus as the branching set that prevents a global one-piece flattening from a source point. Cutting along that locus gives a source unfolding where shortest paths from the source are represented by straight radial segments. The emphasis is organization, not a full algorithmic proof.

Alexandrov Gluing Ledger

Gluing Conditions, Informal Ledger

edge lengths match, angle totals at glued vertices are \(\le 2\pi\), and total curvature is \(4\pi\).

Metric Compatibility

Edges identified to each other must have the same length.

Curvature Compatibility

Each glued vertex receives nonnegative defect; all defects sum to the sphere total.

Existence, Not Recipe

The theorem certifies a convex polyhedron exists; it does not hand us a simple folding sequence.

Cutting asks how to open a known surfaceGluing asks whether pieces determine one
Reverse the logic: instead of cutting a polyhedron into polygons, glue polygons and ask whether they form a convex polyhedron. The ledger conditions are metric edge matching, angle totals at most two pi at glued vertices, and total positive curvature four pi. These are existence conditions, not construction steps.

What To Carry Forward

Classification

Regular convex solids come from a strict local angle inequality.

Topology

Euler characteristic is the incidence ledger for the surface type.

Curvature

Vertex defects add to \(4\pi\) on convex sphere-like polyhedra.

Rigidity

Convex rigidity is a motion question, not an Euler or curvature check.

Unfolding

Cut choices start as dual spanning-tree certificates and then face planar overlap tests.

Gluing

Alexandrov-style ledgers turn polygon identifications into existence data.
