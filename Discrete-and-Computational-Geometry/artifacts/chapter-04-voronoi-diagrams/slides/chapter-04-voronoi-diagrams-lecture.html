Definition

Voronoi Cells Are Winner Sets

Definition For a finite site set S in the plane and a site p in S,
Vor(p) = { x : ||x - p|| <= ||x - q|| for every q in S }.
  • The cell includes ties, so shared boundaries belong to two or more cells.
  • The whole diagram is the partition made by all these winner sets.
  • Computation starts by replacing distances with algebraic inequalities.
Emphasize the inclusive inequality in the definition. Boundaries are not gaps between cells; they are exactly the places where at least two nearest-site certificates are simultaneously true. After naming the sites, ask students what happens to a query point as it crosses a black edge in the diagram. The answer should be that the winning site changes while distances to the two adjacent sites tie on the edge.
Algebra

Distance Comparisons Become Lines

Linearization Squaring the inequality ||x-p|| <= ||x-q|| cancels the quadratic term x dot x:
2(q-p) dot x <= ||q||^2 - ||p||^2.

Thus each competitor contributes one half-plane. A Voronoi cell is convex because it is an intersection of convex half-planes.

Walk through the cancellation rather than expanding every coordinate in full. The key move is that comparing squared distances removes the x dot x term and leaves a first-degree inequality in the query point. This is the first major simplification of the chapter: a nonlinear-sounding proximity condition becomes a half-plane bounded by a perpendicular bisector.
Construction

One Cell As An Intersection

  • Choose a focus site p.
  • For every other site q, keep the side of the bisector containing p.
  • The boundary pieces that survive clipping form the polygonal cell.
Inspection Target Every edge of the cell should be traceable to exactly one competing site. Vertices appear where two such constraints are tight at once.
This is the computational heart of a single Voronoi cell. The final polygon is not guessed from a picture; it is the feasible region of several simple linear inequalities, one for each competing site. Point out that each side of the blue polygon has an owner, meaning a specific competing site whose bisector became tight along that boundary segment.
Geometry

Hull Sites Own Unbounded Cells

Common Misread A cell is not unbounded because its site is far from the center of the drawing. It is unbounded exactly when some outward direction has that site as the permanent nearest candidate.

For a site on the convex hull, move farther in an outward normal direction. All other sites remain behind a supporting line, so the hull site keeps winning.

Connect unbounded cells to supporting lines of the site set. For a hull site, there is an outward direction in which every other site stays behind a supporting line, so the hull site keeps winning arbitrarily far away. For an interior site, competing sites surround it, and the half-plane intersection closes up into a bounded polygon.
Dual Structure

Delaunay Edges Record Neighboring Cells

Voronoi-Delaunay Duality In general position, connect two sites when their Voronoi cells share an edge. The straight-line graph is the Delaunay triangulation.
  • Voronoi edges are perpendicular to their dual site-site segments.
  • Voronoi vertices correspond to triples of sites.
  • The dual graph is local: adjacency is certified by one empty circle.
Ask students to track one black Voronoi edge and then locate the red segment joining the two sites on either side of it. That pairing is the operational meaning of the dual. The red Delaunay edge is not the same geometric object as the black Voronoi edge; it records which two regions touch and usually crosses the corresponding Voronoi edge at right angles.
Certificate

Voronoi Vertices Are Empty-Circle Centers

Local Witness If a point c is a Voronoi vertex, then at least three sites tie for nearest distance at c. The circle centered at c through those sites contains no site in its interior.

This certificate is why Delaunay triangles prefer large angles: a skinny illegal triangle often exposes a point inside a neighbor's circumcircle.

The empty circle is the shortest bridge from Voronoi vertices to Delaunay triangles. The center is not arbitrary: it is the point where three nearest-site inequalities become equalities. Stress the word empty as a certificate. If a fourth site were inside the circle, then the center would be closer to that fourth site and could not be a Voronoi vertex for the displayed triple.
Counting

Linear Complexity From Euler Counting

Planar Bound For n >= 3 sites in general position, the planar Voronoi diagram has at most
V <= 2n - 5,    E <= 3n - 6.

The apparent n(n-1)/2 family of bisectors collapses because only a linear number of bisector pieces survive as cell boundaries.

The important surprise is linearity, not the exact constants alone. Pairwise bisectors form a quadratic family, but the planar subdivision cannot keep all of their pieces visible. Explain the auxiliary vertex at infinity as a counting device for unbounded rays, then connect Euler counting and degree constraints to the bounds on vertices and edges.
Algorithmic Update

Insert A Site By Finding The Cavity

Update Rule Delete triangles whose circumcircles contain the new site, then connect the new site to the boundary of the resulting cavity.
Present insertion through the Delaunay dual because the changing region is easy to certify. The marked triangles are precisely those whose empty-circle promise is broken by the new site. Once those triangles are removed, the boundary of the cavity is the stable part of the old triangulation, and connecting the new site to that boundary produces the updated local star.
Algorithmic Caveat

Local Pictures Do Not Guarantee Small Total Work

Caution A single insertion often looks local, but an unlucky insertion order can make the sum of all changed cavities quadratic.
  • Randomized insertion controls the expected number of structural changes.
  • The certificate view explains what changes, not automatically how much changes.
  • Robust implementations must also handle nearly cocircular events.
Use this slide to keep the algorithmic story honest. The update is local in the sense that only the cavity changes, but the cavity itself can be large under an adversarial insertion order. This distinction matters for algorithm analysis: a clean geometric certificate explains what must be changed, while randomized ordering or additional data structures control the expected amount of work.
3D Translation

Lifting Turns Delaunay Into A Lower Hull

Lifting Principle Lift each site to the paraboloid z = x^2 + y^2. The projection of the lower convex hull is the Delaunay triangulation.

A supporting plane below lifted points encodes an empty circle in the original plane. The same certificate has become convexity.

The purpose of lifting is conceptual compression. Empty-circle tests in the plane become lower-supporting-plane tests in three dimensions, so a proximity statement is turned into a convex hull statement. Point to the lifted points first, then to the projected lower hull chain, and make clear that the red planar triangulation is a shadow of lower hull incidence.
Unification

Voronoi Is The Dual Shadow Of The Same Hull

  • Lower hull facets project to Delaunay triangles.
  • Normals to those facets encode circumcenters.
  • Adjacent lower facets produce adjacent Voronoi vertices.
Mental Model Delaunay sees which sites form empty circles; Voronoi sees where the centers of those circles live.
Treat this as a synthesis slide. Delaunay and Voronoi are not two unrelated diagrams placed on top of the same sites; they are two projections of one incidence structure. Delaunay focuses on which sites share empty circles, while Voronoi focuses on where the centers of those empty circles live and how adjacent centers connect.
Duality

Point-Line Duality Converts Envelopes To Hulls

One Standard Map A line ell: y = mx + b maps to the point ell* = (m,-b), and a point p=(a,b) maps to the line p*: y = ax - b.
  • Incidence is preserved.
  • Vertical order is reversed.
  • Lower envelopes become upper hull chains.
Use point-line duality as the chapter closing move from proximity diagrams to a broader computational geometry principle. Incidence is preserved, vertical order is reversed, and lower envelopes become upper hull chains. The exact formula is less important than the habit it teaches: transform the problem until a reliable primitive, such as convex hull, becomes the natural engine.
Applied Lab

Move One Site, Watch Certificates Change

What To Measure Track the fraction of query points whose nearest site changes and the symmetric difference of Delaunay edge sets.

The diagram changes continuously between events, but the combinatorial structure changes discretely when a tie or cocircular certificate is crossed.

Tie the applied lab back to certificates. Continuous motion changes the coordinates of boundaries continuously, but the combinatorial structure changes only at discrete events such as a tie crossing or a cocircular configuration. Ask students what measurements would reveal the event: nearest-site labels change on a raster sample, and Delaunay edges change by symmetric difference.
Checks

Three Misconceptions To Catch Early

Mistake 1Thinking every pairwise bisector appears in the final diagram. Most bisectors are completely hidden by nearer sites.
Mistake 2Assuming the Delaunay edge is the same segment as the Voronoi edge. They are dual, usually crossing at right angles.
Mistake 3Treating degeneracy as harmless. Four cocircular sites make a Voronoi vertex with degree four and a non-unique triangulation unless a convention is chosen.
Quick Diagnostic Given four sites at the corners of a square, predict the Voronoi diagram first, then list the possible Delaunay diagonals.
Use this as an interactive pause before the summary. The square example is deliberately degenerate and checks three distinctions at once: a bisector need not survive, a Voronoi edge is not a Delaunay edge, and a cocircular set may admit more than one valid triangulation. Let students predict first, then use the slide to name the convention problem.
Takeaways

What The Chapter Adds To The Toolkit

  • Voronoi cells are intersections of half-planes, so cell geometry is convex and algebraic.
  • Delaunay triangulations are the straight-line duals of Voronoi adjacency.
  • Empty circles are the local certificates behind vertices, triangles, and insertions.
  • Planar counting gives linear diagram complexity.
  • Paraboloid lifting turns Delaunay into a lower convex hull.
  • Point-line duality turns envelopes and half-plane questions into hull questions.
  • Moving sites changes continuous geometry through discrete certificate events.
Next Mental MoveWhen a proximity problem looks hard, ask which dual representation makes the certificate easiest to inspect.
Close by naming the durable pattern from the chapter. Computational geometry often succeeds by changing representation until the same facts become easier to certify. Voronoi cells, Delaunay edges, empty circles, lower hull facets, and dual line envelopes are not separate tricks; they are a toolkit for converting geometric proximity and intersection questions into finite, checkable structures.
