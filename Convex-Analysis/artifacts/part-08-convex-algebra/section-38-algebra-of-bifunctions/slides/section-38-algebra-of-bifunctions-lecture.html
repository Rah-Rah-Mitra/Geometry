Linear Model
indicator bifunctions

A Linear Map Becomes A Graph Indicator

Model Object

\(F_A(u,x)=0\) when \(x=Au\), and \(+\infty\) otherwise.

Why It Matters

This graph indicator is the calibration case: every bifunction operation should reduce to a familiar operation on matrices.

Adjoint Check

The adjoint graph records \(u^*=A^*x^*\), so dual arrows point in the reverse direction.

Pairing Invariant

The same scalar is read either as \(\langle Au,x^*\rangle\) or as \(\langle u,A^*x^*\rangle\).

Linear transformations are the sanity checkPairing: \(\langle Fu,x^*\rangle=\langle u,F^*x^*\rangle\)
Use the linear case to fix intuition before the notation gets heavier. A graph indicator says that all allowed pairs lie on the graph of a map. Its adjoint is the graph indicator of the transpose map, with the arrow reversed. Every later identity should look like this once specialized to matrices.
Addition
infimal convolution

Adding Bifunctions Means Splitting The Output

Bifunction Sum

\[((F_1\Box F_2)u)(x)=\inf_y\{(F_1u)(x-y)+(F_2u)(y)\}.\]

Linear Shadow

If \(F_1,F_2\) come from matrices \(A_1,A_2\), this operation is the graph indicator of \(A_1+A_2\).

Dual Reading

The same output slope \(x^*\) tests both summands, so inner-product values add.

Operation is commutative and associative where definedOutput slope \(x^*\) is shared by both summands
Present addition as a resource split. The output x is decomposed into two pieces, one paid for by F1 and the other by F2, and the cheapest split defines the new bifunction. This is exactly why the dual value against x-star becomes a sum of two dual readings.
Adjoint Of Addition
regularity gate

The Adjoint Turns Infimal Addition Into Dual Addition

Identity Pattern

\[(F_1\Box F_2)^*=F_1^*\Box F_2^*\]

Hypothesis To Watch

The relative interiors of the parameter domains must overlap for the clean identity to hold without extra closure.

Closed Version

For closed proper inputs, the dual-side operation may need closure when the regularity condition is weakened.

The slogan is: add first, then adjoint; or adjoint first, then add, provided the domains really meet.

Relative interior condition is the algebraic traffic lightClosure protects identities at boundaries
Slow down here because this is the first place where formal algebra meets regularity. The theorem is not just a symbolic rule. It says that infimal convolution and adjoint commute when the two parameter domains overlap in a robust relative-interior sense. If that robustness fails, closure is the repair mechanism.
Scaling
positive scalar action

Scaling Preserves The Bifunction Structure

Positive Scaling

\[((F_\lambda)u)(x)=\lambda(Fu)(\lambda^{-1}x),\quad \lambda>0.\]

Adjoint Rule

\[(F_\lambda)^*=(F^*)_\lambda,\qquad ((F_\lambda)u,x^*)=\lambda(Fu,x^*).\]

Linear Shadow

If \(F\) represents \(A\), then \(F_\lambda\) represents the linear map \(\lambda A\).

Scaling is the easiest operation: no middle variable is introducedPositive scalars preserve the convex orientation
Treat scaling as the easy control case. The graph function is moved by a one-to-one linear transformation, so convexity, closedness, and properness move with it. The dual equation is also clean: scaling the bifunction scales the inner-product reading and gives the same scalar action after taking the adjoint.
Images
functions through arrows

A Bifunction Can Push A Convex Function Forward

Image Formula

\[(Ff)(x)=\inf_u\{f(u)+(Fu)(x)\}.\]

Interpretation

The input cost \(f(u)\) and the transport cost through \(F\) compete over all possible sources \(u\).

Dual Image

After conjugacy, the adjoint bifunction carries the dual function in the opposite direction.

Attainment

Relative-interior overlap is again the condition that prevents the minimizing source from escaping.

Projection of a joint graph functionDuality turns image problems into adjoint image problems
This operation connects bifunction algebra with ordinary convex functions. Think of f as a cost assigned to the source space and F as a generalized transport rule. The image function asks for the cheapest source and transport certificate that reaches x. The adjoint formula is the dual version of that same routing problem.
Composition
product of bifunctions

Composition Eliminates A Hidden Middle Variable

Product

\[((GF)u)(y)=\inf_x\{(Fu)(x)+(Gx)(y)\}.\]

Linear Shadow

If \(F\) represents \(A:X\to Y\) and \(G\) represents \(B:Y\to Z\), then \(GF\) represents \(BA\).

Semigroup View

Products are associative where defined; endomorphism bifunctions form a non-commutative algebraic system.

Composition is not commutativeThe hidden variable is the matching space between two bifunctions
Define product as the operation that makes bifunctions act like arrows. F moves from the source space to a middle space, G moves from the middle space to the target, and the product minimizes over the hidden middle variable. In the linear case this is exactly matrix multiplication, with the order carefully preserved.
Adjoint Of Composition
the central identity

The Adjoint Of A Product Reverses The Arrows

Adjoint-Of-Composition

\[(GF)^*=F^*G^*.\]

Matrix Check

With \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(B=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\), the identity is \((BA)^T=A^TB^T\).

Regularity

The clean theorem uses overlap between \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F^*)\) and \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}G)\), plus closure in the corollary.

This is the slide to keep in mind: compose forward, adjoint backward.

Core invariant: residual \(\|(BA)^T-A^TB^T\|=0\)Source pages 401-412 emphasize composition and adjoints
Make this the conceptual center of the lecture. Product moves forward through F and then G. The adjoint product moves backward through G-star and then F-star, which is written as F-star G-star because function composition is read from right to left. The small matrix residual in the notebook is the finite-dimensional audit of this rule.
Domain Gate
where identities become exact

Relative-Interior Overlap Is The Algebraic Compatibility Test

For Addition

Overlap of \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F_1)\) and \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F_2)\) supports \((F_1\Box F_2)^*=F_1^*\Box F_2^*\).

For Composition

Overlap of \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F^*)\) and \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}G)\) supports \((GF)^*=F^*G^*\).

For Images

Overlap of \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f)\) and \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F)\) keeps the optimizing source attainable.

Relative interior is the course's recurring replacement for naive nonempty intersection.

Domains are projected graph domainsBoundary-only contact is where closure issues appear
Use this slide as a unifying diagnostic rather than a separate technical aside. Each theorem asks whether the spaces where the two objects can actually communicate overlap robustly. Relative interior is the language for robust overlap inside affine hulls, and it is what keeps infima, suprema, and adjoints from losing boundary information.
Inner Product
convex meets concave

The Section Extends Pairing Beyond Vectors

Function Pairing

\[\langle f,g\rangle=\sup_x\{g^*(x)-f(x)\}=\inf_y\{f^*(y)-g(y)\}\]

Existence

The pairing is defined when the two displayed extrema agree; Fenchel duality supplies common sufficient conditions.

Vector Case

If \(f\) and \(g\) are point indicators, this reduces to the ordinary dot product.

Dual Flip

When the pairing exists, conjugating both sides changes the sign: \(\langle f^*,g^*\rangle=-\langle f,g\rangle\).

Function pairing generalizes vector pairingFenchel duality explains equality of the two extrema
Introduce the generalized inner product as the scalar that survives when a convex function and a concave function are paired through Fenchel duality. It is not automatically defined; the max-side and min-side expressions must agree. Once they do, conjugating both functions reverses the sign, which is exactly the behavior needed for adjoint transport.
Transport Identity
pair through the adjoint

Adjoints Move Pairings Across A Bifunction

Inner-Product Transport

\[\langle Ff,g^*\rangle=\langle f,F^*g^*\rangle,\qquad \langle f^*,F^*g\rangle=-\langle f,F^*g^*\rangle.\]

Meaning

Push \(f\) forward through \(F\), then pair with \(g^*\); or pull \(g^*\) back through \(F^*\), then pair with \(f\).

Hypothesis

A relative-interior condition ensures the image and pairing operations all exist in the same scalar chain.

This is the function-level version of \(\langle Au,x^*\rangle=\langle u,A^*x^*\rangle\).

Pairing is transported, not merely rewrittenSigns come from convex-concave conjugacy
This theorem is the payoff for defining the generalized inner product. It says that pairing after pushing a function forward through F gives the same scalar as pairing before the push, after pulling the dual object back through F-star. The sign changes in the other equalities come from conjugating convex and concave partners.
Co-Finite Case
clean algebra

Co-Finite Bifunctions Remove Many Boundary Warnings

Total Domains

For a closed co-finite bifunction, both the primal and adjoint domains fill their ambient spaces.

Saddle Connection

Co-finite bifunctions correspond to finite saddle functions, so every dual pairing is finite and inspectable.

Algebra

Under addition, scaling, and composition, co-finite bifunctions preserve the clean adjoint rules.

Clean Product Rule

\[F,G\ \text{co-finite}\quad\Rightarrow\quad (GF)^*=F^*G^*.\]

Linear graph indicators are co-finite examplesEndomorphism bifunctions form a non-commutative semigroup under product
Co-finiteness is where the algebra begins to feel like ordinary linear algebra again. The domains are full, adjoints remain co-finite, and the main addition and product identities no longer carry the same boundary anxiety. This special case is useful because it tells students what the general theorems are trying to approximate.

The Matrix Lab Audits The Composition Identity

Notebook Matrices

\[A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.\]

Residual

\[\|(BA)^T-A^TB^T\|=0.\]

Forward

Compose the two graph indicators to get the graph of \(BA\).

Dual

Transpose after composing, or compose the transposes in reverse.

Check

A nonzero residual would reveal a sign, order, or pairing mistake.

Artifact invariant: displayed matrices satisfy the adjoint-of-composition identityThe lab is a finite audit, not a replacement for the theorem
Tie the lecture back to the assigned notebook. The matrices are deliberately small so students can multiply them by hand or inspect the residual numerically. The point is not that matrices prove the full bifunction theorem. The point is that the finite model makes the order reversal and the pairing convention impossible to ignore.

Good Experiments Break One Hypothesis At A Time

Experiment A

Swap the order of the matrices and observe that \(AB\) and \(BA\) usually represent different products.

Experiment B

Replace one graph indicator by a convex cost and track the minimizing middle variable in the product.

Experiment C

Restrict a domain to force boundary-only contact, then decide whether a closure appears in the dual identity.

A useful report names the operation, the hidden variable or pairing, and the residual that certifies the identity.

Change one ingredient, then recheck the identityOrder, domain, and closure are the main failure modes
Frame the lab as controlled hypothesis testing. Students should avoid changing the whole example at once. Changing order tests non-commutativity, changing the middle cost tests the product definition, and changing domains tests the relative-interior assumptions. Each experiment should end with a visible residual or closure diagnosis.
