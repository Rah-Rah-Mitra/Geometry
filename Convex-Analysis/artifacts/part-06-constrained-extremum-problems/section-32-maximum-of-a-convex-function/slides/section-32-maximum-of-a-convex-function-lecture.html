The Maximum Of A Convex Function

Convex maximization reverses the instincts from minimization: high values live on boundary structure, and extreme points become the natural search certificates.

Different Character
local information is weak

Convex Maxima Do Not Behave Like Convex Minima

Minimization Instinct

For a convex objective, a local minimum is global, and first-order conditions can certify it.

Maximization Reality

Local maxima may occur at separate boundary pieces. Finding one gives little guidance about whether it is the largest one.

Search Philosophy

local peak \(\not\Rightarrow\) global peak

Convex minimization: local is enoughConvex maximization: compare boundary candidates
Emphasize the change in computational character. A convex function can have many local maxima on a convex feasible set, especially at sharp boundary points. After finding one local maximum, nearby comparisons do not tell us whether another face or vertex has a higher value.
Theorem 32.1
relative interior

An Interior Maximizer Forces Constancy

Maximum Principle

If \(z\in \operatorname{ri} C\) and \(f(z)=\sup_C f\), then \(f\) is constant on \(C\).

Proof Move

For any \(x\in C\), pass beyond \(z\) along the line through \(x\). Relative interior gives a point \(y\in C\) with \(z=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\).

Contradiction

If \(f(x)\) were lower than the maximum, convexity would force \(f(z)\) to be lower than its own maximum.

Relative interior supplies the beyond pointConvexity turns one strict drop into a contradiction
The first theorem is the core warning. If a convex function reaches its supremum at a relative interior point of a convex set, the value cannot drop anywhere else in the set. The segment-through-the-interior argument turns any strict drop into a convexity contradiction.
Corollary 32.1.1
union of faces

The Maximizer Set Comes In Whole Faces

Face Logic

x\in W,\quad x\in \operatorname{ri} F_x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F_x\subset W.

Why It Works

Every point of \(C\) belongs to the relative interior of a unique face. Apply the interior maximum principle inside that face.

How To Read It

A maximum set can be a vertex, an edge, a higher-dimensional face, or a union of such pieces.

Maximizers are not arbitrary dustThey respect the face lattice of C
Use this as a structural picture of all maximizers, not just one point. Each maximizer lies in the relative interior of a unique face. The interior maximum principle then forces the function to be constant on that whole face, so maximizers arrive in face-sized pieces.
Theorem 32.2
generators suffice

The Supremum Over A Hull Is Already On The Generators

Hull Reduction

C=\operatorname{conv}S \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sup_{x\in C} f(x)=\sup_{s\in S} f(s).

Sublevel Argument

The set \(\{x:f(x)\le \alpha\}\) is convex. If it contains \(S\), it contains \(\operatorname{conv}S\).

Attainment Clause

A maximum on the hull can be attained only when the same supremum is attained by the generating set.

Convexity of f gives convex sublevel setsConvex hulls do not create larger function values
Here the convex hull becomes a search-reduction tool. Since sublevel sets of a convex function are convex, controlling the function on the generating set controls it on the entire hull. Therefore the supremum over the hull is already visible on the generators.
Boundary Principle
relative boundary

For Many Closed Convex Sets, Search The Boundary First

Boundary Reduction

C=\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{relbd}C) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sup_C f=\sup_{\operatorname{relbd}C} f.

Scope

Closed convex sets that are not essentially affine have enough boundary to generate the whole set.

What It Does Not Say

The boundary may still be infinite and curved; this is a reduction in geometry, not yet an algorithm.

The convex hull theorem powers the boundary statementInterior search collapses to relative boundary search
This slide converts the hull theorem into a boundary principle. For many closed convex sets, the set is generated by its relative boundary, so a convex maximum does not need an interior search. The boundary is still large, but it is the first major dimension reduction.
Theorem 32.3
lineality and extreme points

Under No Upward Escape, Extreme Points Carry The Supremum

Extreme Point Reduction

No half-line in \(C\) lets \(f\) go to \(+\infty\). Then after removing lineality, \(\sup_C f\) equals the supremum over the extreme points of \(C\cap L^\perp\).

Lineality

Along true lines in \(C\), bounded-above convex behavior forces constancy, so those directions can be factored out.

Extreme Directions

Rays that do not improve indefinitely can be pushed back to endpoints, leaving the convex hull of extreme points.

Line-free closed C: an attained supremum appears at an extreme pointBounded polyhedra: the list is finite
Now explain why vertices enter. Lineality directions carry constant values under the bounded-above hypothesis, and upward extreme directions cannot improve the value indefinitely. After quotienting out lines and pushing along rays, the supremum reduces to extreme points.
Corollaries 32.3.2-32.3.4
finite search conditions

Compactness Gives Attainment; Polyhedra Give A Vertex List

Compact Domain Route

If \(C\) is nonempty, closed, bounded, convex, and \(C\subset \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f)\), then \(f\) is continuous on \(C\), so the supremum is finite and attained.

Extreme Point Location

When a line-free closed convex set has an attained supremum, at least one maximizer can be chosen from its extreme points.

Polyhedral Payoff

For a line-free polyhedron with \(f\) bounded above, the extreme points are finite, so maximizing reduces to comparing finitely many values.

HypothesesOutput
compact \(C\subset \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f)\)finite attained supremum
closed, no lines, attainedsome extreme point maximizes
polyhedral, no lines, bounded abovefinite vertex comparison
Continuity plus compactness gives existencePolyhedral extreme points make the comparison finite
This is the practical theorem students will remember. On a compact convex set inside the relative interior of the domain, continuity gives a finite attained maximum, and the preceding principle places some maximizer at an extreme point. For polyhedra, this becomes a finite vertex scan.
Why The Domain Hypothesis Matters
not just closed and bounded

Touching The Domain Boundary Can Break Attainment Or Boundedness

Failure Mode

C\subset \operatorname{dom}f \quad\text{does not replace}\quad C\subset \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f).

Approach Without Attainment

High values may converge to a boundary point where the limiting value is not available to the feasible problem.

Unbounded Rise

Even a closed bounded feasible set can touch a domain edge where a closed convex function rises without a finite supremum.

Relative-interior containment supplies continuityBoundary singularities can defeat compact-set intuition
The compact corollary has a real domain hypothesis. If the feasible set only sits in the domain and brushes a singular boundary, the supremum can be approached without being attained, or values can blow up. The examples in the source span are warning signs about boundary regularity.
Theorem 32.4
necessary condition

At A Nonconstant Maximum, Every Subgradient Points Normal To C

Subgradient Normality

x\in \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f),\ f(x)=\sup_C f,\ f\text{ nonconstant on }C \Rightarrow g\in\partial f(x)\setminus\{0\},\ \langle y-x,g\rangle\le 0.

Interpretation

The supporting affine lower model at \(x\) has a nonzero slope, and that slope exposes \(x\) as a maximizer for the linear function.

Unit Ball Case

Normals at a boundary point of the Euclidean ball are positive multiples of \(x\), giving an eigen-type condition \(\lambda x\in\partial f(x)\).

This is necessary, not a standalone global maximum algorithmFor general S, the same g exposes x over conv S
Subgradients do not provide a global maximum test, but they still describe any attained nonconstant maximum in the relative interior of the domain. Every subgradient must be a nonzero outward normal to the feasible set, so the supporting linear function is also maximized there.

Convex Maximization Is A Boundary And Extreme-Point Story

Interior

An attained relative-interior maximum forces constancy on the convex set.

Faces

The set of maximizers is a union of faces of the feasible region.

Hull

For \(C=\operatorname{conv}S\), the supremum over \(C\) equals the supremum over \(S\).

Extreme Points

With no upward escape, extreme points carry the supremum after lineality is removed.

For compact polyhedral domains in the good part of the domain of \(f\), compare vertex values and then interpret any maximizing face.
