Program Data
ordinary convex program

An Ordinary Program Remembers Its Constraint Functions

Problem Form

Minimize \(f_0(x)\) over \(x\in C\), subject to \(f_i(x)\le 0\) for \(i\le r\), and \(f_i(x)=0\) for \(i>r\).

Convex Data

\(C\) is convex; \(f_0,\ldots,f_r\) are convex on \(C\); equality constraints are represented by affine functions.

Feasible Solutions

The feasible set is \(C_0=C\cap C_1\cap\cdots\cap C_m\), with \(C_i=\{x:f_i(x)\le 0\}\) for inequalities.

Why Representation Matters

Multipliers attach to the named \(f_i\), so equivalent feasible regions may carry different multiplier certificates.

Convex inequalities, affine equalitiesThe program data determines the multiplier language
Emphasize that an ordinary convex program is more than an objective plus a feasible set. The separate functions f_i matter because multipliers attach to those functions. Two programs can describe the same feasible points but have different multiplier behavior if the constraint representation changes.
Feasible Geometry
active set

Only Active Boundaries Push Back At The Optimum

Inactive Inequality

If \(f_i(x^*)<0\), the constraint has slack. Its multiplier must be zero in a KKT certificate.

Active Inequality

If \(f_i(x^*)=0\), the boundary can contribute an outward normal direction with nonnegative weight.

Equality Constraint

Affine equalities remain active and their multipliers are free in sign, because motion is restricted on both sides.

Activity turns a constraint into a normal; complementarity decides whether its price is allowed to be nonzero.

Active set: constraints tight at \(x^*\)Complementarity: \(\lambda_i f_i(x^*)=0\)
Use the picture to distinguish inactive constraints from active constraints. Inactive inequalities shape the feasible set globally but do not press on the optimum locally. Active constraints contribute normal directions, and equality constraints are always active because they must hold exactly.
Multiplier Certificate
Kuhn-Tucker vector

A Multiplier Vector Prices The Constraints

Weighted Objective

\(h_\lambda(x)=f_0(x)+\lambda_1 f_1(x)+\cdots+\lambda_m f_m(x)\), with \(\lambda_i\ge 0\) for inequality constraints.

Lower Certificate

On feasible points, inequality terms are nonpositive. Nonnegative prices force \(h_\lambda(x)\le f_0(x)\).

Kuhn-Tucker Vector

The vector \(\lambda\) is a certificate when \(\inf h_\lambda\) matches the optimal value of the constrained program.

Reduction Principle

Minimize \(h_\lambda\) first; then keep the minimizers that are feasible and satisfy complementarity.

Theorem 28.1 routeReplace constraints by a priced convex minimization, then filter
Introduce the weighted objective h_lambda as the algebraic device that trades constraints for prices. For feasible points, nonnegative weights on inequalities make h_lambda a lower certificate. A Kuhn-Tucker vector is special because the lower certificate reaches the primal optimum rather than merely sitting below it.
Constraint Qualification
relative interior room

Multiplier Existence Needs Room Inside The Constraints

Existence Pattern

If the value is not \(-\infty\) and there is a feasible point in \(\operatorname{ri} C\) satisfying the nonlinear inequalities strictly, then a Kuhn-Tucker vector exists.

Strict Feasibility

For pure inequality programs, a point with \(f_i(x)<0\) for all inequalities gives the familiar Slater-type condition.

Linear Constraints

When constraints are linear, relative-interior feasibility is enough; strictness is not required for affine equalities.

Failure Mode

If feasibility lives only on a sharp boundary, the separating argument may not normalize into legal multiplier prices.

Theorem 28.2 and corollariesExistence of prices is a geometric regularity statement
The relative interior condition is a constraint qualification. It rules out the kind of boundary-only feasible geometry where a separating hyperplane would have the wrong normalization. The slide should make existence feel like a separation theorem with enough room to separate the impossible strict system from the feasible one.
KKT Conditions
certificate checklist

The KKT Certificate Has Four Interlocking Tests

1. Primal feasibility

\(x\in C\), inequalities hold, and affine equalities hold exactly.

2. Dual feasibility

\(\lambda_i\ge0\) for inequality constraints; equality multipliers are unrestricted.

3. Complementarity

\(\lambda_i f_i(x)=0\) for each inequality; inactive constraints carry zero price.

4. Stationarity

\(0\in\partial f_0(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i\partial f_i(x)\), omitting zero-price terms.

Kuhn-Tucker Theorem

Under the existence hypotheses, \(x\) is optimal exactly when some multiplier vector \(\lambda\) satisfies the KKT conditions with \(x\).

Theorem 28.3 and Corollary 28.3.1KKT is necessary and sufficient when multipliers exist
Frame the KKT conditions as four tests that must be read together. Primal feasibility puts the point in the program, dual feasibility gives legal prices, complementary slackness deletes inactive inequality prices, and stationarity says the priced objective has no remaining first-order descent.
Differentiable Case
normal balance

Stationarity Says The Boundary Can Balance Descent

Smooth Stationarity

\[\nabla f_0(x^*)+\lambda_1\nabla f_1(x^*)+\cdots+\lambda_m\nabla f_m(x^*)=0.\]

Inequality Normals

Only active inequalities can appear, and their coefficients are nonnegative.

Equality Normals

Affine equality normals can be added with either sign because the feasible set lies on both sides of the affine direction.

Nonsmooth Version

Replace gradients by subgradients and read the same balance as \(0\in\partial h_\lambda(x^*)\).

Normal cone intuitionThe active constraints supply exactly the missing first-order directions
For differentiable data, stationarity becomes a vector balance. The negative objective gradient must lie in the cone generated by active inequality normals plus the span of equality normals. This is the geometric heart of the lecture: the optimum is where the feasible boundary can exactly push back.
Saddle Form
min in x, max in lambda

The Lagrangian Makes Optimality A Saddle Point

Lagrangian

\(L(\lambda,x)=f_0(x)+\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i f_i(x)\), with \(\lambda_i\ge0\) for inequality constraints and \(x\in C\).

Saddle Certificate

\[L(\lambda,x^*)\le L(\lambda^*,x^*)\le L(\lambda^*,x)\]

for all legal \(\lambda\) and all \(x\in C\).

Reading The Inequalities

Multipliers cannot improve at fixed \(x^*\), and \(x^*\) minimizes the priced objective at fixed \(\lambda^*\).

Theorem 28.3Optimal solution plus Kuhn-Tucker vector iff Lagrangian saddle point
Translate the conditions into a saddle statement. Holding x fixed lets the multipliers punish violations; holding lambda fixed turns the problem into minimizing the priced objective. At a saddle point, neither player can improve by moving alone, so the primal point and multiplier vector certify each other.
Dual Value
prices and lower bounds

Multipliers Are Supporting Prices For Perturbations

Perturbation Function

\(p(u)\) is the optimal value after replacing each constraint limit by the component \(u_i\).

Dual Function

\(g(\lambda)=\inf_{x\in C} L(\lambda,x)\), a concave lower-bound function of the multiplier vector.

Value Recovery

When \(\lambda^*\) is Kuhn-Tucker, \(g(\lambda^*)\) equals the primal optimal value.

Price Interpretation

At equilibrium prices, no allowed perturbation is worth buying after its price is charged.

Theorem 28.4 and Corollary 28.4.1Kuhn-Tucker vectors maximize the dual lower-bound function
Connect multipliers to sensitivity. The perturbation function records the best value after relaxing or tightening the constraint limits. A multiplier vector acts like a supporting price at the unperturbed problem, while the dual function g(lambda) gives lower bounds whose maximizers are exactly the Kuhn-Tucker vectors when they exist.
Decomposition
large problem to priced subproblems

A Shared Multiplier Splits Coupled Blocks

Separable Form

Minimize \(\sum_k f_{0k}(x_k)\) subject to block constraints \(x_k\in C_k\) and a small coupling equation \(\sum_k A_k x_k=a\).

With A Multiplier

For a candidate \(u^*\), each block solves a local problem \(f_{0k}(x_k)+\langle x_k,A_k^Tu^*\rangle\).

Global Coordination

The only remaining coupled task is to find a price vector making the local minimizers satisfy the shared equation.

Why It Matters

A high-dimensional constrained problem becomes many smaller minimizations plus a lower-dimensional dual search.

Decomposition principle near the end of Section 28Multipliers coordinate separable convex subproblems
The decomposition principle is the computational payoff. If the objective and constraints split into blocks except for a small coupling equation, a multiplier vector turns one coupled problem into independent priced subproblems. The global work moves to finding the shared price vector.
Worked Check
stationarity and slackness residuals

A One-Constraint Quadratic Makes The Certificate Numeric

Example

Minimize \(\frac12\|x-a\|^2\) subject to \(g(x)=x_1+x_2-1\le0\), with \(a=(1.2,1.0)\).

Candidate

The projection onto the active line is \(x^*=(0.6,0.4)\), so \(g(x^*)=0\).

Multiplier

\(\nabla f(x^*)=(-0.6,-0.6)\) and \(\nabla g=(1,1)\), so \(\lambda=0.6\) gives \(\nabla f+\lambda\nabla g=0\).

Residuals

Dual feasibility holds, complementarity gives \(\lambda g(x^*)=0\), and stationarity residual is exactly zero in this model.

Notebook check: stationarity and complementary slackness residualsA compact numerical model for active normals
Use the small quadratic example as a numerical anchor for the notebook invariant. The unconstrained point lies beyond the halfspace, so the optimizer is its projection onto the active line. The multiplier equals the amount of normal correction, and both stationarity and complementary slackness reduce to visible zero residuals.
