Continuity And Differentiability Of Saddle-functions

Finite saddle-functions inherit compact continuity, convergence, directional derivative, and differentiability rules from one-variable convex analysis, but with opposite signs in the two variables.

Sign Convention

\(K(u,v)\) is concave in \(u\) and convex in \(v\). The \(u\)-certificate is upper supporting; the \(v\)-certificate is lower supporting.

Finite On A Relatively Open Product
Theorem 35.1

Compact Continuity Transfer

Compact-Core Regularity

If \(K\) is finite, concave in \(u\), and convex in \(v\) on \(C\times D\), then \(K\) is continuous there and Lipschitz on every closed bounded \(S\times T\subset C\times D\).

Two One-Variable Bounds

\[ |K(u',v)-K(u,v)|\le L_u|u'-u| \]

\[ |K(u,v')-K(u,v)|\le L_v|v'-v| \]

Product Bound

\[ |K(u',v')-K(u,v)|\le L\|(u',v')-(u,v)\|. \]

Continuity is local to finite interior productsThe estimate is built by adding two partial estimates
Present the first theorem as a transfer principle rather than a surprise. A finite convex function is locally Lipschitz on compact cores of its domain, and the saddle case repeats that argument twice: once with v fixed and once with u fixed. The visual box reminds students that the constants are only promised on an interior compact rectangle.
Pointwise Bounds Propagate
Theorem 35.2

Families Stay Equi-Lipschitz

Uniform Family Control

For a family \(\{K_i\}\), pointwise boundedness on a product sample \(C'\times D'\) whose closed convex hull covers \(C\times D\) gives uniform boundedness and equi-Lipschitz behavior on compact subsets.

Why The Sample Matters

The sample set need not be the whole domain, but it must be rich enough that convex hull closure reaches the product interior.

What The Result Buys

A single compact box gets one bound and one Lipschitz constant that work for every \(K_i\).

Use Later

This is the compactness engine behind dense-set convergence and subsequence extraction.

Family control is local on compact boxesUniform constants replace function-by-function constants
The family theorem is the workhorse behind the later convergence statements. Pointwise boundedness on a sufficiently spanning sample set is not merely a weak data condition; through convexity in one direction and concavity in the other, it propagates to uniform bounds and a common Lipschitz constant on every compact box.
Continuity, Limits, Compactness
35.3-35.5

Dense Data Determines Limits

Parameter Continuity

If \(t\mapsto K(u,v,t)\) is continuous on dense product data, the saddle regularity theorem upgrades this to joint continuity in \((u,v,t)\).

Pointwise Dense Limits

If \(K_i\) has finite limits on a dense product set, the limit extends to every point and convergence is uniform on compact boxes.

Subsequence Compactness

If a sequence is merely bounded on dense product data, some subsequence converges compact-uniformly to a finite saddle-function.

Dense data plus equi-Lipschitz control gives full-domain conclusionsUsed again for differentiability proofs
This slide bundles the continuity in a parameter, dense-set convergence, and subsequence compactness results. The message is operational: if a sequence of finite saddle-functions is controlled on dense product data, the compact Lipschitz theorem prevents hidden oscillation between sample points. Uniform convergence on compact sets is what lets later derivative statements pass to limits.
Interior First-Order Motion
Theorem 35.6

Directional Derivative Splits

Split Formula

At an interior finite point, the directional derivative exists and

\[ K'(u,v;a,b)=K'(u,v;a,0)+K'(u,v;0,b). \]

Shape Of The Derivative

The derivative is positively homogeneous, concave in the \(a\) direction, and convex in the \(b\) direction.

Proof Roadmap

Compare the diagonal quotient with a two-leg quotient, then use compact continuity to control the moving base point.

Partial derivative existence is automatic; joint existence is the theoremInterior finite point only
The joint directional derivative is the first genuinely saddle-specific moment. The theorem says that at an interior finite point, moving simultaneously in u and v has the same first-order effect as adding the u-only and v-only directional effects. The proof idea is a two-leg path plus continuity, and the diagram gives that path a geometric form.
Two Supporting Inequalities
partial subgradients

Partial Subgradient Certificates

Concave Side

\(u^*\in \partial_uK(u,v)\) when

\[ K(u',v)\le K(u,v)+\langle u^*,u'-u\rangle. \]

Convex Side

\(v^*\in \partial_vK(u,v)\) when

\[ K(u,v')\ge K(u,v)+\langle v^*,v'-v\rangle. \]

Saddle Mapping

\[ \partial K(u,v)=\partial_uK(u,v)\times\partial_vK(u,v). \]

The notation is a product of one-variable certificatesEach value is a closed convex set when nonempty
Now translate derivatives into certificates. On the concave u side, the affine plane lies above the slice, so the displayed inequality has the reverse direction from the usual convex subgradient inequality. On the convex v side, the affine plane lies below. The product of these two certificate sets is the saddle subdifferential.
Interior Subgradient Geometry
support functions

Derivative As A Support Test

Interior Nonemptiness

At an interior finite point of a proper saddle-function, both partial subgradient sets are nonempty, closed, bounded, and convex.

Directional Readout

\[ K'(u,v;a,0)=\inf_{u^*\in\partial_uK(u,v)}\langle u^*,a\rangle \]

\[ K'(u,v;0,b)=\sup_{v^*\in\partial_vK(u,v)}\langle v^*,b\rangle. \]

Minimax View

The joint directional derivative evaluates the linear form \(\langle u^*,a\rangle+\langle v^*,b\rangle\) over the product \(\partial K(u,v)\), with opposite extremal directions.

Subgradient sets turn first-order motion into extreme pairingsConcave side uses inf; convex side uses sup
At an interior point of a proper saddle-function, the partial subgradient sets are nonempty, closed, bounded, and convex. Directional derivatives can then be read from the extreme pairings with these sets: an infimum on the concave side and a supremum on the convex side. The rectangle is a synthetic model of that product set.
Nearby Graphs Cannot Escape
Theorem 35.7 and corollary

Outer Continuity Of The Saddle Mapping

Set-Valued Containment

For each interior finite \((u,v)\) and each \(\epsilon>0\), nearby points \((x,y)\) satisfy

\[ \partial K(x,y)\subset \partial K(u,v)+\epsilon B. \]

Scalar Shadows

\(K'(u,v;a,0)\) is lower semicontinuous in the base point, while \(K'(u,v;0,b)\) is upper semicontinuous.

Convergence Version

If \(K_i\to K\) and \((u_i,v_i)\to(u,v)\), one-sided bounds on the partial directional derivatives survive in the limit.

Set-valued continuity is one-sided and localEnough for stable differentiability arguments
The continuity theorem for the subdifferential should be heard as an outer-continuity statement. Nearby saddle subgradients cannot suddenly escape far outside the old subgradient set plus a small ball. The one-sided semicontinuity of the directional derivatives is the scalar shadow of this set-valued containment.
Collapse Of The Product Set
Theorem 35.8

Differentiability Means Singleton Mapping

Equivalence

\(K\) is differentiable at \((u,v)\) exactly when \(\partial K(u,v)\) contains one vector pair.

\[ \partial K(u,v)=\{(\nabla_uK(u,v),\nabla_vK(u,v))\}. \]

Why Joint Differentiability Follows

Unique partial certificates give separate differentiability. Rescaled difference quotients form saddle-functions converging to zero on each fixed direction; compact convergence upgrades this to uniform first-order error on bounded directions.

First-Order Error Test

\[ \frac{K(u+a,v+b)-K(u,v)-\langle u^*,a\rangle-\langle v^*,b\rangle}{\|(a,b)\|}\to0. \]

Singleton product set is the saddle analogue of differentiabilitySeparate differentiability becomes joint differentiability
The differentiability theorem has two directions. If K is differentiable, the gradient is the only possible saddle subgradient. Conversely, if the product subdifferential contains exactly one vector pair, separate differentiability follows from the one-variable theory, and the saddle convergence theorem upgrades that to joint differentiability.
Fast Differentiability Diagnostic
Corollary 35.8.1

Linearity Is The Directional Test

Equivalent Criterion

At a finite point with a neighborhood of finiteness, \(K\) is differentiable iff the map

\[ (a,b)\mapsto K'(u,v;a,b) \]

is linear.

Partial Shortcut

Finite two-sided partial derivatives in all \(m+n\) coordinate directions are enough to force the linear directional derivative test.

What Fails At A Kink

A directional derivative can be positively homogeneous without being linear. The missing additivity records a nonsingleton partial subgradient set.

The test uses the directional derivative as the local modelCoordinate two-sided partials suffice
The corollary gives a fast diagnostic: near a finite point, differentiability is equivalent to the directional derivative being a linear function of the direction. It is enough to have all two-sided partial derivatives finite, because the split directional derivative then cannot keep a kink in any coordinate direction.
Thin Nonsmooth Set
Theorem 35.9

Smooth Points Are Almost Everywhere

Almost-Everywhere Differentiability

For finite \(K\) on an open convex product \(C\times D\), the differentiability set \(E\) is dense and its complement has measure zero.

Continuity On \(E\)

The gradient mapping \(\nabla K:E\to\mathbb R^{m+n}\) is continuous when restricted to differentiability points.

Line-Slice Idea

For each coordinate direction, nonsmoothness is detected by jumps of a one-dimensional monotone derivative. Only countably many large-jump layers are needed.

The theorem combines one-dimensional jump control with finitely many coordinatesDense smooth set, not necessarily open
The full-measure theorem says nonsmoothness is thin even for saddle-functions. Along each coordinate line, a one-dimensional convex derivative can only have finitely many large jumps on a bounded interval. Taking countable jump thresholds and finitely many coordinates leaves a dense differentiability set whose gradient map is continuous on that set.
Smooth Limit Stability
Theorem 35.10

Gradients Converge With The Functions

Gradient Convergence

If differentiable finite \(K_i\) converge pointwise on \(C\times D\) to differentiable finite \(K\), then

\[ \nabla K_i(u,v)\to \nabla K(u,v) \]

uniformly on every closed bounded subset.

Why Uniform?

Pointwise convergence becomes compact-uniform convergence of functions. Differentiability of the limit and the compact derivative theorem convert value convergence into gradient convergence.

Dense Data Variant

It is enough to know convergence on a dense product subset; the earlier compact convergence theorem fills in the rest.

Function convergence plus differentiability controls gradient convergenceUniform on compact subsets
The last theorem is a stability result. When differentiable finite saddle-functions converge pointwise to a differentiable finite saddle-function, compact convergence of the functions and continuity of the limiting gradient force the gradients to converge uniformly on compact sets. This is the saddle analogue of the smooth convex convergence theorem from the differential theory.
