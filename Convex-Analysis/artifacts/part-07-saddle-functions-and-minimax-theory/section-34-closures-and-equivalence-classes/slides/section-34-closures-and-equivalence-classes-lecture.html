Two Closure Directions

Saddle Setting

\(K(u,x^*)\) is concave in \(u\) and convex in \(x^*\).

Ordered Closures

\(\underline K=\operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1 K,\qquad \overline K=\operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2 K.\)

The first is the lower closure; the second is the upper closure.

Why Order Matters

One order repairs convex lower semicontinuity after concave closure; the other order repairs concave upper semicontinuity after convex closure.

For convex-concave functions the lower/upper terminology reversesClosure is a slice operation before it is a global operation
Start with a concave-convex saddle-function and name the two directions separately. Closing in the first variable repairs concave slices, while closing in the second variable repairs convex slices. The important point is that a two-step closure has an order, and Section 34 studies what that order remembers.
Theorem 34.1
closure does not drift

The First Stabilization

Stability

\(\operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1(\operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1 K)=\operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1 K\)

\(\operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2(\operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2 K)=\operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2 K\)

Proof Route

Represent a convex slice closure by a closed convex bifunction \(F\), then use the concave adjoint \(F^*\) to certify the other closure.

Teaching Interpretation

The lower and upper operations may disagree with each other, but each operation has its own fixed-point output.

The theorem gives endpoints, not equality between endpointsLater equivalence explains the gap
Theorem 34.1 is a reassurance theorem. Once the lower closure is formed, applying the same lower-closure operation again does not keep changing the object; the same holds for the upper closure. The proof works by translating the saddle data through the associated bifunction and its adjoint.
Boundary Conflict
the \(u^v\) model

A Corner Can Refuse A Value

Open-Square Test

On \((0,1)\times(0,1)\), the model \(K(u,v)=u^v\) is concave in \(u\) and convex in \(v\).

Corner Disagreement

Approaching the origin with \(v\) fixed pushes values toward \(0\). Approaching with \(u\) fixed while \(v\to 0\) pushes values toward \(1\).

Lesson

No value at \((0,0)\) makes both closure directions simultaneously natural. The gap is structural, not a notation accident.

Lower endpoint chooses the lower limiting boundaryUpper endpoint chooses the upper limiting boundary
Use the corner picture as the first real warning. A boundary point may be approached in one order by fixing the convex variable and in the other order by fixing the concave variable. If those limiting stories disagree, no single corner value can satisfy both closure directions at once.
Domains
finite core, possible outliers

Effective Domain Is Product Data

Concave-Convex Domain

\(\operatorname{dom}_1K=\{u:K(u,v)>-\infty\ \hbox{for all }v\}\)

\(\operatorname{dom}_2K=\{v:K(u,v)<+\infty\ \hbox{for all }u\}\)

Effective Domain

\(\operatorname{dom}K=\operatorname{dom}_1K\times\operatorname{dom}_2K\) is convex and \(K\) is finite on this product.

Important Caution

A saddle-function may still be finite at some points outside the product core. The product is the stable domain, not the whole finite set.

dom1 is a convex set in the first spacedom2 is a convex set in the second space
The effective domain definition is stricter than asking where a displayed formula is finite. The first domain keeps all second-variable slices above minus infinity; the second domain keeps all first-variable slices below plus infinity. Their product is the stable finite core used by the equivalence theory.

Equivalence And Closedness

Equivalence

\(K\sim L \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{cl}_1K=\operatorname{cl}_1L,\ \operatorname{cl}_2K=\operatorname{cl}_2L.\)

Closed Saddle-Function

\(K\) is closed when its one-direction closures are equivalent to \(K\).

\(\operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2K=\operatorname{cl}_1K,\qquad \operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1K=\operatorname{cl}_2K.\)

Near Coincidence

Equivalent closed representatives agree on the relative interior of their effective domain product.

Closedness is weaker than being both lower and upper closedEquivalence is stable under changing boundary representatives
Equivalence means the two one-direction closures agree, not necessarily that the original functions agree at every boundary point. A closed saddle-function is one whose closures are already equivalent to it. This lets the theory keep boundary ambiguity while still having well-defined lower and upper representatives.
Theorem 34.2
closed classes from bifunctions

The Omega Of \(F\) Sandwich

Class From \(F\)

For a closed convex bifunction \(F\), define

\(\underline K(u,x^*)=(Fu,x^*),\qquad \overline K(u,x^*)=(u,F^*x^*).\)

\(\Omega(F)=\{K:\underline K\le K\le \overline K\}.\)

What Theorem 34.2 Adds

\(\Omega(F)\) is one equivalence class of closed concave-convex functions, and every closed class appears this way from a unique \(F\).

Domain And Recovery

\(\operatorname{dom}K=\operatorname{dom}F\times\operatorname{dom}F^*\), and \(F,F^*\) are recovered from any representative by sup and inf transforms.

For \(K\in\Omega(F)\): \(\operatorname{cl}_2K=\underline K\), \(\operatorname{cl}_1K=\overline K\)Unique \(F\) is the class certificate
Theorem 34.2 is the organizing theorem. A closed convex bifunction supplies a lower member and an upper member, and every closed saddle-function in the class lies between them. The class is recovered from the sandwich, and the bifunction is recovered from any member by conjugate-type formulas.
Corollaries 34.2.1-34.2.4
class facts to use

Consequences Of The Sandwich

Common Domain

Equivalent closed saddle-functions have the same effective domain, and they agree whenever \(u\in\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}_1K)\) or \(v\in\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}_2K)\).

Endpoints

Each closed class has a unique least member that is lower closed and a unique greatest member that is upper closed.

Improper Edge

The only improper closed saddle-functions are the constant functions \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\), and they are not equivalent.

Continuous finite data on closed \(C\times D\) gives a proper closed classThe lower and upper simple extensions are the endpoint examples
Pause here to extract the practical conclusions before going into slice structure. Every proper class has one least lower closed member and one greatest upper closed member. If the bifunction is improper, the only closed saddle-functions left are the two constant infinities, which are not equivalent.

Slice Structure Of A Closed Class

Let \(C=\operatorname{dom}_1K,\ D=\operatorname{dom}_2K\)

For a proper closed \(K\), interior, boundary, and exterior slices have different closure behavior.

relative interiorclosed proper, domain \(D\)closed proper, domain \(C\)
relative boundaryproper, domain between \(D\) and \(\operatorname{cl}D\)proper, domain between \(C\) and \(\operatorname{cl}C\)
outsideimproper; forced \(-\infty\) on \(\operatorname{ri}D\)improper; forced \(+\infty\) on \(\operatorname{ri}C\)
The theorem is both necessary and sufficientInterior slices are the clean case; boundary slices carry the ambiguity
Theorem 34.3 is a diagnostic table. Interior slices are the clean case: closed proper convex or concave functions with the expected domain. Boundary slices can have domains between the original domain and its closure. Outside slices become improper, with infinity signs forced on the relative interior.
Theorem 34.4
same kernel iff equivalent

Kernel Determines The Class

Kernel

\(\operatorname{ker}K=K\big|_{\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}_1K)\times\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}_2K)}.\)

Equivalence Criterion

Two closed proper concave-convex saddle-functions are equivalent if and only if they have the same kernel.

Why This Works

Closed convex or concave functions are determined by their values on the relative interior of their effective domains.

Each proper closed class has exactly one kernelBoundary representatives are classified by their shared interior data
Now make the kernel the main invariant. Once the values are known on the relative interiors of the two domain factors, closedness forces the closures of all relevant slices. Theorem 34.4 says this finite interior saddle-function determines the entire proper closed equivalence class.
Theorem 34.5
existence from simple data

Simple Kernels Generate Classes

Simple Saddle-Function

For interior \(u\)-slices and \(v\)-slices, the effective domains stay inside the closures of the intended domain factors.

Generation Rule

If \(K\) is proper, concave-convex, and simple, then

\(\operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1K\le \operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2K\)

and the functions between them form the proper closed class with the same kernel.

Corollary 34.5.1

Any finite concave-convex function on \(C\times D\) yields exactly one proper closed equivalence class for its relative-interior kernel.

Lower and upper simple extensions are the model examplesSimple proper data gives an existence theorem for kernels
The simple condition prevents slice domains from wandering beyond the closures of the intended factors. Theorem 34.5 then says the lower and upper closures of a proper simple saddle-function are equivalent, ordered, and form the endpoints of the class with the same kernel.

Recap

Board Prompt

Given a concave-convex \(K\), locate its kernel and decide which boundary values are forced by the lower and upper closures.

Five Takeaways

  • Ordered closures stabilize, but the two orders can disagree.
  • Closed equivalence classes are closure fingerprints.
  • A closed convex bifunction \(F\) gives the class sandwich.
  • The kernel on relative interiors determines a proper closed class.
  • Simple finite kernels generate unique proper closed classes.
