Open by naming the shift in viewpoint. Earlier convex analysis mostly watched one function move toward minima or supporting hyperplanes. Section 33 asks students to track two directions at once: one argument is optimized like a concave variable, the other like a convex variable, and the saddle point is where both one-sided stories agree.
Definition
concave-convex convention

A Saddle-Function Has Opposite One-Variable Geometry

Concave-Convex

\(K(u,x^*)\) is concave in \(u\) for fixed \(x^*\), and convex in \(x^*\) for fixed \(u\).

First Coordinate

Concavity means chords lie below the graph, so the natural optimization direction is upward.

Second Coordinate

Convexity means chords lie above the graph, so the natural optimization direction is downward.

Name Discipline

Convex-concave reverses the roles. The lecture keeps the concave-convex orientation used by the source span.

Saddle means one variable wants max-like geometryThe other variable wants min-like geometry
Use the sign convention slowly. A concave-convex saddle-function bends downward in the first variable and upward in the second. The diagram is deliberately asymmetric: moving along the concave coordinate feels like maximizing, while moving along the convex coordinate feels like minimizing. That sign split is the grammar for the rest of Part VII.
Domains
restricted C x D data

Restricted Saddle Data Has Two Natural Extensions

Lower Simple Extension

Keep \(K\) on \(C\times D\); use \(+\infty\) outside \(D\) and \(-\infty\) outside \(C\).

Upper Simple Extension

The same slice rules are enforced in the opposite order, so the double-outside region changes.

Ambiguity

If \(u\notin C\) and \(x^*\notin D\), the convex and concave extension rules compete.

Simple extensions preserve one-variable convexity and concavityThe next section studies the discrepancies explicitly
The first technical nuisance is domain extension. If the original function is defined only on C times D, the natural infinity outside D is plus infinity for convexity in the second variable, while the natural infinity outside C is minus infinity for concavity in the first variable. When both variables leave their domains, there is no single forced value, which is why lower and upper simple extensions appear.
Closure Operations
cl_1 and cl_2

Closure Is Applied One Slice At A Time

Convex Closure

\(\operatorname{cl}_2 K\): close \(K(u,\cdot)\) as a convex function for each fixed \(u\).

Concave Closure

\(\operatorname{cl}_1 K\): close \(K(\cdot,x^*)\) as a concave function for each fixed \(x^*\).

Preservation

These operations keep the saddle sign pattern, so they are safe regularizers for later duality arguments.

Closure is coordinate-sensitiveClosedness is not a single global adjective in this section
Present closure as coordinate-wise regularization rather than a global smoothing operation. Closing in the convex variable repairs lower semicontinuity slice by slice; closing in the concave variable repairs upper semicontinuity slice by slice. The important habit is to ask which coordinate is being closed and which inequality direction that closure is supposed to protect.
Theorem 33.1
from bifunction to saddle

Partial Conjugacy Turns A Convex Bifunction Into A Saddle

Construction

\(K_F(u,x^*)=(Fu,x^*)=\sup_x\{\langle x,x^*\rangle-(Fu)(x)\}\).

Convex Slice

For fixed \(u\), \(K_F(u,\cdot)\) is the conjugate of the convex function \(Fu\), hence closed convex.

Concave Slice

For fixed \(x^*\), the supremum formula induces concavity in \(u\), by projecting a convex function.

Full conjugacy is not usedOnly the image variable x is conjugated
This is the central construction. Start with a convex bifunction F, freeze u, and take the ordinary conjugate of F u with respect to x. The resulting expression is convex in the dual variable x star, but Theorem 33.1 says it is also concave in u. That extra concavity is what turns a family of convex functions into a saddle-function.
Correspondence
convex-closed slices

The Saddle Slice Recovers The Image Function

Reverse Formula

\((Fu)(x)=\sup_{x^*}\{\langle x,x^*\rangle-K(u,x^*)\}\).

Image-Closed Match

Convex-closed concave-convex saddle-functions correspond to image-closed convex bifunctions.

Involution Habit

Each fixed \(u\) slice uses ordinary conjugacy twice, but the full object remembers the parameter \(u\).

One-to-one after the right closure hypothesisPartial conjugates are convex functions on R^{m+n} seen through slices
Now reverse the construction. If a saddle-function is convex-closed in the second variable, each slice can be conjugated back to recover F u. This is the image-closed correspondence: no hidden generator is needed once the closed convex slices are known. Students should recognize the same conjugate involution idea from earlier sections, now applied only in one coordinate.
Theorem 33.2
adjoint closure relation

The Adjoint Pairing Agrees After The Right Closure

Closure Identity

\((u,F^*x^*)=\operatorname{cl}_u(Fu,x^*)\).

Second Identity

\(\operatorname{cl}_{x^*}(u,F^*x^*)=((\operatorname{cl}F)u,x^*)\).

Linear Analogy

If \(F\) is the indicator bifunction of a linear map \(A\), this becomes \(\langle Au,x^*\rangle=\langle u,A^*x^*\rangle\).

The adjoint identity is closure-sensitiveIt is exact on the right relative interiors
The adjoint theorem is best read as a generalized inner product identity. In linear algebra, the pairing Au with x star equals the pairing u with A star x star. For bifunctions, the two expressions may need closure before they match. The closure relation explains both the analogy and the boundary cases where the analogy is too optimistic.
Relative Interiors
agreement and exceptions

Equality Is A Domain Statement, Not A Decoration

Typical Agreement

If \(u\in\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F)\), then \((Fu,x^*)=(u,F^*x^*)\) for every \(x^*\).

Closed Adjoint Side

If \(F\) is closed and \(x^*\in\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}F^*)\), the same equality holds for every \(u\).

Boundary Risk

Outside both domains, one pairing can become \(+\infty\) while the other becomes \(-\infty\).

Program Meaning

The equality condition matches exact value agreement for a shifted primal-dual pair of convex programs.

Relative interior is the safe operating zonePolyhedral cases sharpen the statement
Emphasize that equality is not promised everywhere. It holds on relative interiors of the appropriate domains and in the polyhedral case except for the clear double-outside exception. Near the boundary, the two partial conjugate views can disagree, become infinite in opposite directions, or reflect abnormal dual behavior. That is the bridge to the next section.
Closedness
fully, lower, upper

Lower And Upper Closedness Are Two-Step Conditions

Convex-Closed

\(\operatorname{cl}_2 K=K\). The convex variable has closed slices.

Concave-Closed

\(\operatorname{cl}_1 K=K\). The concave variable has closed slices.

Fully Closed

Both one-coordinate closures already hold. Finite everywhere saddle-functions are a safe example.

Lower / Upper

Lower: \(\operatorname{cl}_2\operatorname{cl}_1K=K\). Upper: \(\operatorname{cl}_1\operatorname{cl}_2K=K\).

Lower closed concave-convex functions correspond to closed convex bifunctionsUpper closed functions correspond to closed concave bifunctions
Organize the many closedness words into a small ladder. Convex-closed and concave-closed are one-coordinate properties. Fully closed means both are present. Lower closed and upper closed are weaker two-step properties that remember which coordinate should be semicontinuous in the natural direction. This ladder prevents the section from sounding like a list of nearly identical labels.
Corollary 33.3.3
finite data on C x D

Continuous Restricted Saddles Produce A Unique Closed Bifunction

Restricted Case

Finite continuous \(K\) on closed convex \(C\times D\) gives lower and upper closed simple extensions.

Domain Record

The associated closed convex bifunction has \(\operatorname{dom}F=C\), and its adjoint has \(\operatorname{dom}F^*=D\).

Why It Matters

The abstract all-space theory can still remember the original feasible sets exactly.

Closed convex sets C and D are retained as domainsSimple extensions become canonical representatives
Use the continuous-on-C-times-D case as the sanity check. For closed convex C and D and finite continuous saddle data, the lower and upper simple extensions give the two closed representatives that the theory wants. A unique closed convex bifunction sits behind them, with domain C and adjoint domain D, so the abstract closure language returns to ordinary restricted saddle data.
Notebook Lab
zero partial derivatives

A Quadratic Saddle Makes The Inequalities Inspectable

Model Saddle

\(K(u,x^*)=-\frac12u^2+ux^*+\frac12(x^*)^2\).

Stationarity

\(\partial_u K=-u+x^*\), and \(\partial_{x^*}K=u+x^*\), so both vanish at \((0,0)\).

Saddle Inequality

\(K(u,0)\le K(0,0)\le K(0,x^*)\), so the center is max-like in \(u\) and min-like in \(x^*\).

Source notebook check: zero partials and local saddle inequalitiesThe example is synthetic course material
Tie the lecture back to the notebook artifact. The quadratic K of u and x star equals minus one half u squared plus u x star plus one half x star squared. It is concave in u and convex in x star, the partial derivatives vanish at the origin, and the saddle inequality reads K of u zero is at most K of zero zero, which is at most K of zero x star.
Executable Invariant
residual dashboard

The Lab Checks Stationarity And Both One-Sided Inequalities

Partial Residual

\(|\partial_uK|+|\partial_{x^*}K|\) should vanish at the candidate.

Concave Side

Sampled values should satisfy \(K(u,x^*_0)\le K(u_0,x^*_0)\).

Convex Side

Sampled values should satisfy \(K(u_0,x^*_0)\le K(u_0,x^*)\).

A useful learner report states which residual failed, then names the coordinate whose saddle behavior was broken.

Change one coefficient at a timeKeep the sign split visible while interpreting residuals
Frame the lab as a residual dashboard. The stationarity residual checks the two partial derivatives at the candidate. The concave-side inequality checks that moving u alone cannot raise the value above the center, and the convex-side inequality checks that moving x star alone cannot lower it below the center. When a parameter change breaks one residual, students should identify which side of the saddle has failed.
