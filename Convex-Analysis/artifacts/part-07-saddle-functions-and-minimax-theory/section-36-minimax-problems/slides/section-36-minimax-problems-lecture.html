Minimax Problems

Minimax theory asks when the order of optimization can be exchanged. Saddle points give the certificate: one variable is minimized, the other is maximized, and both see the same value.

Two Orders

Minimax And Maximin Ask Opposite Sequential Questions

Minimax

\(\inf_x\sup_y K(x,y)\).

Interpretation

The \(x\)-player chooses a position while anticipating the worst response in \(y\).

Maximin

\(\sup_y\inf_x K(x,y)\).

Interpretation

The \(y\)-player chooses a position while anticipating the best counter-response in \(x\).

The order changes the information structureEquality is a theorem, not notation
Spend time on the order of quantifiers. Minimax and maximin are not two spellings of the same expression. One lets the maximizing variable respond after x, the other lets the minimizing variable respond after y. Equality requires geometric structure or a saddle certificate.
Always True
weak duality shape

The Maximin Value Cannot Exceed The Minimax Value

Order Inequality

\(\sup_y\inf_x K(x,y)\le \inf_x\sup_y K(x,y)\).

Why

For every pair \(x,y\), the lower envelope in \(x\) is no larger than the upper envelope in \(y\).

Gap

The difference between the two sides is the minimax gap. A positive gap means the order of play matters.

The inequality is automaticThe section's work is about equality and attainment
This slide is the baseline. The lower value can never exceed the upper value, regardless of convexity. The interesting minimax theorem begins when the gap is zero and the two orders agree. That is the analog of strong duality in this saddle-function language.
Saddle Certificate
one point, two inequalities

A Saddle Point Certifies Equality Of Values

Saddle Inequality

\(K(x^*,y)\le K(x^*,y^*)\le K(x,y^*)\).

Left Inequality

Holding \(x^*\) fixed, the point \(y^*\) maximizes the payoff.

Right Inequality

Holding \(y^*\) fixed, the point \(x^*\) minimizes the payoff.

Consequence

The common value \(K(x^*,y^*)\) equals both the minmax and the maximin value.

Saddle point equals value certificateOne point proves both orders
Give students the exact certificate they should try to verify. A saddle point satisfies two one-sided optimality statements. It is a maximum in y along one slice and a minimum in x along the other. Those two inequalities squeeze the lower and upper values together.
Hypotheses
convex-concave plus compactness

Convex-Concave Geometry Supports Minimax Equality

Convex In \(x\)

For fixed \(y\), the minimizing side sees a convex optimization problem.

Concave In \(y\)

For fixed \(x\), the maximizing side sees a concave optimization problem.

Compact Or Closed-Level Control

Existence of optimizers requires a mechanism that prevents escaping sequences.

Shape hypotheses support equalityExistence hypotheses support attainment
Separate shape from attainment. Convexity in the minimizing variable and concavity in the maximizing variable are what make the saddle geometry plausible. Compactness, closedness, or level-set control are different assumptions: they keep the relevant extrema from escaping.
Worked Example
box game

A Convex-Concave Payoff On A Box Shows Equality

Payoff

\(K(x,y)=\frac12x^2+xy-\frac12y^2\), with \((x,y)\in[-1,1]^2\).

Saddle Candidate

The stationary equations \(x+y=0\) and \(x-y=0\) meet at \((0,0)\).

Value

At the candidate, \(K(0,0)=0\), and the slice shapes certify that neither player can improve by moving alone.

The example is synthetic and inspectableThe notebook invariant compares computed values
Use the example as a laboratory rather than a theorem substitute. The payoff is convex in x and concave in y, and the stationary equations meet at the center of the box. The slice shapes make it plausible that the two orders agree, while the numerical check can confirm the common value.
Failure Modes
why gaps appear

Minimax Equality Can Fail In Predictable Ways

No Attainment

The lower and upper values may approach each other only along escaping sequences.

Wrong Shape

If a slice is not convex or concave in the required direction, a saddle certificate may not exist.

Boundary Gap

Even with good local behavior, incompatible boundary constraints can leave a positive minimax gap.

Failures identify which hypothesis was doing workThe gap is a diagnostic quantity
Use this slide to avoid turning minimax equality into a wish. Equality can fail because extrema escape, because the payoff lacks the right convex-concave shape, or because boundary restrictions produce a genuine gap. Each failure corresponds to a different theorem hypothesis.
Executable Invariant
order residual

The Notebook Check Compares The Two Orders

Residual

\(r=\left|\inf_x\sup_y K(x,y)-\sup_y\inf_x K(x,y)\right|.\)

Pass Condition

The displayed saddle example has a small order residual and a candidate point satisfying the two saddle inequalities.

Fail Condition

A positive residual says the two optimization orders are seeing different values, even if each one is internally optimized.

Tie the slide directly to the notebook check. The computation should not merely find a plausible saddle point; it should compare both optimization orders. A small residual supports the equality claim, while a positive residual means the sequential games have different values.

Experiment A

Change the coupling coefficient \(xy\). Track how the saddle point and common value move.

Experiment B

Move a box boundary so the stationary saddle is no longer feasible. Identify the boundary saddle or gap.

Experiment C

Break concavity in \(y\). Compare the new minmax and maximin values and explain the residual.

A useful report names the changed parameter, the candidate saddle, both order values, and the residual.

The explanation should identify whether equality survived, moved to the boundary, or failed because a hypothesis broke.
