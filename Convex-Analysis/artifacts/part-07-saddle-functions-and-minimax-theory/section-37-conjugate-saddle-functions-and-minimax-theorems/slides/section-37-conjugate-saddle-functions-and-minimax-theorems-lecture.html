Saddle Pairing
two variables, two signs

Saddle Conjugacy Uses A Signed Pairing

Ordinary Pattern

\(f^*(u)=\sup_x\{\langle x,u\rangle-f(x)\}\).

Saddle Pattern

Pair \(x\) with one slope and \(y\) with the opposite optimization sign.

Reason

The \(x\)-side behaves like convex minimization, while the \(y\)-side behaves like concave maximization.

Conjugacy follows the optimization sign of each variableThe pairing must respect convex-concave structure
Do not rush the sign convention. In a saddle function, x and y have opposite geometric roles. The conjugate transform has to remember which variable is minimized and which one is maximized. That is why a naive two-variable convex conjugate would lose the saddle interpretation.
Inverse Bifunction
reverse the roles

The Inverse Bifunction Reverses The Saddle View

Role Reversal

The inverse exchanges the two sides and flips the sign convention needed to preserve convex-concave structure.

Why It Helps

Minimax statements often compare a saddle problem with its reversed or adjoint problem.

Check

After reversal, the variable that was convex should still be treated as the minimizing side in the new representation.

Inverse operations are bookkeeping for paired programsSign errors create false minimax statements
Use this slide to make the inverse bifunction feel like a controlled coordinate change. It is not just swapping two letters. The reversal must preserve which direction is convex and which direction is concave, because the later minimax conclusions depend on the orientation of the two optimization variables.

Why Quadratic

Introduce the quadratic as the section's laboratory. It is not meant to replace the theorem, but it exposes the moving pieces. Students can evaluate the saddle, transform it, transform it back, and compare sampled values. That makes biconjugacy a visible reconstruction property.
Biconjugate
recover the closed saddle

Biconjugacy Recovers The Proper Closed Saddle Object

Reconstruction Theme

\(K^{**}=K\) when the saddle function has the right closed convex-concave regularity.

What Is Recovered

The transform recovers the closed saddle object, just as ordinary biconjugacy recovers closed proper convex functions.

Closure Warning

If boundary values are missing, the biconjugate can repair the closure rather than returning the raw expression.

Biconjugacy is a reconstruction theoremClosure determines what exactly returns
Connect this theorem with earlier convex biconjugacy. The second transform does not magically preserve every poorly behaved expression. It recovers the correctly closed saddle object. That closure warning is central because minimax equalities can fail when boundary or existence information is lost.

Conjugacy Packages The Minimax Equality Proof

1. Encode

Represent the program as a saddle function.

2. Transform

Use saddle conjugacy to read the paired program.

3. Close

Check the closure or continuity condition that preserves values.

4. Attain

Verify an existence condition for the saddle or value.

Minimax equality is a value statement; saddle existence is the stronger certificate that the value is attained.

Conjugacy is the proof architectureExistence is a separate certificate layer
Use this slide as the theorem roadmap. The program is encoded as a saddle object, transformed through conjugacy, and compared after closure. Equality of values is one conclusion, while the actual existence of a saddle point is a stronger statement that needs attainment hypotheses.
Existence Conditions
attainment versus value

Existence Conditions Prevent Value-Only Conclusions

Compactness

Compact feasible domains keep optimizing sequences from escaping.

Level Control

Closed level sets or coercive terms can substitute for compact domains in finite-dimensional examples.

Closure

Closedness of the saddle object keeps limiting boundary certificates from disappearing.

Value equality and saddle existence are related but distinctAttainment hypotheses identify actual optimizers
Clarify the difference between proving equality of values and proving that a saddle point exists. Compactness, level control, and closure are all ways of preventing limiting certificates from drifting out of the problem. Students should name which existence mechanism is being used.

The Notebook Check Audits Biconjugate Reconstruction

Residual

\(r=\max_{(x,y)\in G}|K^{**}(x,y)-K(x,y)|\).

Pass Condition

The sampled grid residual is small, so the conjugate transform and its inverse reconstruct the quadratic saddle on inspected points.

Fail Condition

A large residual indicates a sign convention error, a missing closure step, or a transform that no longer matches the original saddle object.
