Relative Interiors Of Convex Sets

The right topology for convex analysis is often not the topology of the ambient room. It is the topology of the smallest affine world containing the set.

Ordinary Interior Fails For Thin Convex Sets

Pitfall

If \(C\subset\mathbb{R}^n\) has smaller affine dimension, then \(\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb{R}^n} C=\varnothing\).

Segment

A line segment in the plane has no two-dimensional ball around any point, but its relative interior is the open segment.

Singleton

A one-point convex set has affine hull equal to that point, so the point is relatively interior to itself.

Takeaway

Dimension is not a defect. The topology must be restricted to the affine hull before asking interior questions.

Ordinary interior asks for all ambient directionsRelative interior asks only for legal affine-hull directions
Use the three examples to break the habit of measuring every object in the ambient space. A square has ordinary interior, an edge does not, and a singleton looks impossible until its affine hull is remembered. The goal is to make nonempty relative interior feel normal.
Definition
interior inside aff C

Affine Hull Topology

Relative Interior

\[ \operatorname{ri} C=\{x\in\operatorname{aff}C:\exists \epsilon>0,\ (x+\epsilon B)\cap\operatorname{aff}C\subset C\}. \]

What Changes

The ball is still Euclidean, but only the slice through \(\operatorname{aff}C\) is tested.

Why It Is Stable

Translations and one-to-one affine coordinate changes preserve affine hulls, closure, and relative interior.

Full Dimension

If \(\operatorname{aff}C=\mathbb{R}^n\), then relative interior is ordinary interior.

Define the affine hull as the smallest flat world containing the set, then define relative neighborhoods as ordinary balls sliced by that world. Students should see that no directions are missing by accident; directions outside the affine hull were never legal local directions.
Theorem 6.4 Lens
one-dimensional certificates

Segment Extension Test

Criterion

For nonempty convex \(C\),

\[ z\in\operatorname{ri}C \iff \forall x\in C,\ \exists \mu>1:\ (1-\mu)x+\mu z\in C. \]

Geometric Meaning

Every segment with endpoint \(z\) has a small legal extension beyond \(z\).

Check Asset

assets/segment-extension-test.json records a one-dimensional witness list for a closed segment.

Boundary Failure

At an endpoint of a segment, choosing the opposite endpoint gives no room to extend beyond the endpoint inside \(C\).

Segment certificates replace epsilon-neighborhood intuitionThe test is relative because all tested chords live in aff C
Treat the extension test as a practical certificate. If z is in the relative interior, every chord ending at z can be pushed a bit further through z and remain in C. Conversely, if all those one-dimensional tests pass, z cannot be sitting on the relative boundary.
Closure Laws
same affine hull, same closure

\(\operatorname{ri}\) And Closure

Fundamental Segment Rule

If \(x\in\operatorname{ri}C\) and \(y\in\operatorname{cl}C\), then \((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\in\operatorname{ri}C\) for \(0\le\lambda<1\).

Complementary Laws

\[ \operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{ri}C)=\operatorname{cl}C,\qquad \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{cl}C)=\operatorname{ri}C. \]

Relative Boundary

The relative boundary is \(\operatorname{cl}C\setminus\operatorname{ri}C\), measured in the same affine hull.

Artifact: assets/ri-closure-ledger.svgBoundary behavior becomes controlled for convex sets
The closure laws are the backbone of the section. A convex set, its closure, and its relative interior share the same affine hull and closure; the boundary is the part left over. Theorem 6.1 is the geometric reason: moving from a relative-interior point toward a closure point stays inside until the endpoint.
Boundary Geometry
ri is not monotone under inclusion

Faces And Relative Boundary

Monotonicity Trap

\(C_1\subset C_2\) gives \(\operatorname{cl}C_1\subset\operatorname{cl}C_2\), but it does not force \(\operatorname{ri}C_1\subset\operatorname{ri}C_2\).

Face As Its Own World

An edge of a square is boundary for the square and still has a nonempty relative interior as a one-dimensional set.

Dimension Signal

A convex piece trapped in the relative boundary of a nonempty convex set has smaller affine dimension.

Teaching Cue

Do not ask whether the edge contains a two-dimensional ball. Ask whether it contains a one-dimensional ball inside its own line.

Faces explain why inclusion and relative interior must be handled carefullyBoundary pieces carry their own affine hulls
Use the face example to warn against a tempting monotonicity rule. A face can be contained in a larger convex set while its own relative interior is disjoint from the larger set's relative interior. Boundary pieces have lower affine dimension and need their own topology.

Polytope Examples

Triangle

\(\operatorname{aff}C=\mathbb{R}^2\). Relative interior equals ordinary interior.

Edge

One equality defines the affine hull; only endpoint inequalities act as boundary.

Singleton

The affine hull has no nonzero directions; \(\operatorname{ri}\{p\}=\{p\}\).

Same ambient room, different affine dimensionsRelative interior is computed after deciding which inequalities define aff C
For polytopes, relative interior can be read from active inequalities. In the square, strict inequalities give the usual interior. On an edge, one equality is structural because it defines the affine hull, while the two endpoint inequalities must be slack. For a singleton, every allowed direction is already zero.
Operation Rules
relative-open calculus

Products And Sums

Products

\[ \operatorname{ri}(C_1\times C_2)=\operatorname{ri}C_1\times\operatorname{ri}C_2. \]

Minkowski Sums

\[ \operatorname{ri}(C_1+C_2)=\operatorname{ri}C_1+\operatorname{ri}C_2. \]

Closure Caution

The guaranteed closure relation is \(\operatorname{cl}(C_1+C_2)\supset \operatorname{cl}C_1+\operatorname{cl}C_2\). Equality needs extra hypotheses later.

Products keep coordinates separateSums are linear images of products
Present products and sums as calculus rules, not memorized identities. In a product, each coordinate has its own relative topology. For a Minkowski sum, choose interior representatives in each summand and add them. The closure statement is only an inclusion here, which is a useful caution.
Linear Maps
projections preserve ri exactly

Linear Images

Image Rule

\[ \operatorname{ri}(A C)=A(\operatorname{ri}C). \]

Proof Move

Map the segment-extension certificate forward. Convexity and linearity preserve the same \(\mu>1\).

Closure Is Weaker

Continuity gives \(\operatorname{cl}(AC)\supset A(\operatorname{cl}C)\), but equality is not automatic.

Scalar Case

\(\operatorname{ri}(\lambda C)=\lambda\,\operatorname{ri}C\) for every real scalar \(\lambda\).

Images are exact for riClosures require more care under projections
The linear image rule is especially important for projections. Relative interior commutes with a linear map because segment-extension certificates can be mapped forward. Closure need not commute as equality, so the slide separates the exact relative-interior rule from the weaker closure inclusion.
Intersections
qualification matters

Intersections And Qualification

Finite Intersection Rule

If \(\bigcap_i \operatorname{ri}C_i\neq\varnothing\), then

\[ \operatorname{ri}\left(\bigcap_i C_i\right)=\bigcap_i\operatorname{ri}C_i. \]

Closure Rule

\[ \operatorname{cl}\left(\bigcap_i C_i\right)=\bigcap_i\operatorname{cl}C_i \]

under the same common-relative-interior hypothesis.

Failure Mode

Boundary-only contact can leave a nonempty intersection whose relative interior is not detected by intersecting the input relative interiors.

Artifact: assets/intersection-qualification.svgQualification prevents boundary-only algebra from lying
State the qualification before the formula. If the input relative interiors overlap, finite intersections behave cleanly: the relative interior of the intersection is the intersection of relative interiors. The boundary-only contact panel shows why the condition is not decorative; without it the right side can be too small.

Degeneracy Is Data
0D, 1D, and boundary worlds

Degenerate Examples

Singleton

\(\operatorname{aff}\{p\}=\{p\}\). The only relative neighborhood is the point itself.

Ray

The relative interior of a ray is the ray without its endpoint, measured along its line.

Face

A face is boundary for the parent set but can be relatively open in its own affine hull.

Affine Set

An affine set is relatively open and closed at the same time.

Nonempty convex sets have nonempty relative interiorDegenerate examples are normal in convex analysis
Degenerate examples are not exceptions to hide. They are the reason relative interior exists. A singleton, a ray, and a face all have empty ordinary interior in a larger ambient space, but each has a meaningful relative interior once the affine hull has been selected.

Recap: The Right Topology Is Relative

1. Choose The World

Replace the ambient room by \(\operatorname{aff}C\).

2. Test A Point

Use relative neighborhoods or the segment-extension criterion.

3. Stabilize Boundary

Use \(\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{ri}C)=\operatorname{cl}C\) and \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{cl}C)=\operatorname{ri}C\).

4. Apply Calculus

Carry \(\operatorname{ri}\) through images, sums, products, and qualified intersections.
