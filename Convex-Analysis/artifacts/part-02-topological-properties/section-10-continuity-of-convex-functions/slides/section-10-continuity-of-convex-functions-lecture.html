Continuity Of Convex Functions

Why convex functions are forced to behave well on relative interiors, why boundaries remain delicate, and how compact-core bounds turn pointwise convex information into uniform control.

Theorem 10.1 Thread
relative-open continuity

Interior Regularity

Core Statement

If \(f\) is convex and \(C\) is a relatively open convex subset of \(\operatorname{dom} f\), then \(f\) is continuous relative to \(C\).

Why It Feels Inevitable

Inside \(C\), a small neighborhood is still available in the affine hull. Convexity then compares each value with values on nearby chords rather than allowing a free vertical jump.

Finite Everywhere Case

A convex function finite on all of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) has no effective-domain boundary, so the regularity becomes ordinary continuity everywhere.

Source orientation: Theorem 10.1Continuity relative to the set, not necessarily from outside it
Make Theorem 10.1 feel like a local theorem rather than a global miracle. The proof route can be summarized as lower semicontinuity from closure facts plus upper semicontinuity from the geometry of the epigraph. Students should hear that the relative-open hypothesis is doing real work by keeping a small convex testing zone around the point.
Domain Geometry
ri(dom f)

Relative Interior Domain

Relative Continuity

At \(x\in S\), only paths \(y\to x\) with \(y\in S\) count. Ambient approaches from outside \(S\) are not part of the test.

Low-Dimensional Domains

A convex function finite on a segment, plane, or affine slice can still be continuous relative to that slice even when the ambient space has empty interior.

Boundary Signal

The moment \(x\notin \operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom} f)\), some approach directions disappear. Continuity may still hold, but it no longer follows for free.

Relative topology turns thin domains into legitimate interiorsKeep ambient and relative tests separate
Spend time on the word relative. Many functions in convex analysis live naturally on faces, affine images, or lower-dimensional feasible sets. The ambient ball may be useless there, but a relative ball inside the affine hull is enough. This is why the theorem is stated for relatively open convex sets rather than ordinary open sets only.
Boundedness Becomes Continuity
the hidden engine

Local Boundedness

Operational Heuristic

For a convex function, local two-sided boundedness on a small convex neighborhood is almost the same as local continuity.

1. Upper bound

A chord through nearby points blocks upward spikes.

2. Lower bound

Closure or properness prevents a hidden drop to negative infinity.

3. Compactness

Bounds on a buffer become uniform constants on the core.

Boundary Caveat

At a relative boundary point, the needed buffer may not exist. The available chords can all point the wrong way.

Local boundedness is the quantitative ingredient behind upper semicontinuityThe boundary caveat will return
Local boundedness is the quiet mechanism behind the polished continuity statements. In ordinary analysis, boundedness alone would be far too weak. Convexity changes the game because values at the sides of a small convex neighborhood control values between them. Emphasize that this mechanism needs a genuine neighborhood in the relevant affine hull.

One-Dimensional Test

On a line segment, convexity makes the difference quotients ordered. Nearby values are trapped by secants through fixed surrounding points.

Inspection Target

Fix a small core around \(x_0\). Endpoint values provide upper control, while convexity and closure facts block downward escape.

Use the chord picture as the main intuition of the lecture. If a convex graph tried to jump at an interior point, chords from fixed surrounding points would have to bend in incompatible ways. The monotone secant-slope check is not a full proof, but it is the finite picture students should keep in mind when reading the theorem.
Theorem 10.4 Thread
closed bounded core

Lipschitz On Compact Cores

Quantitative Regularity

If \(f\) is proper convex and \(S\) is closed and bounded inside \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom} f)\), then \(f\) is Lipschitz relative to \(S\).

Proof Mechanism

Find \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(S+\varepsilon B\) still inside the relative interior. Bounded values on that buffer give a uniform secant constant on \(S\).

Teaching Translation

Compact cores have a moat. The moat turns qualitative continuity into a usable modulus.

Asset: assets/compact-core-lipschitz.svgUniform continuity is automatic on compact cores
Present Theorem 10.4 as the quantitative version of interior regularity. Once a compact set sits strictly inside the relative domain, there is room to extend a secant past one point and compare it to a bounded value on a buffer. The Lipschitz constant is therefore a geometric consequence of distance from the boundary.
Boundary Delicacy
approach paths matter

Boundary Blow-Up

Boundary Warning

A closed convex function can be well behaved along every segment from an interior point and still fail to be continuous at a relative boundary point.

What Breaks

Tangent approaches may stay inside the domain while losing the fixed simplex or buffer that controlled the interior proof.

What Survives

One-sided limits along a fixed simplex can still exist and agree with the boundary value when closedness gives lower semicontinuity.

Asset: assets/boundary-blowup.svgBoundary behavior is a domain-geometry question
The boundary example should feel fair, not pathological for its own sake. Convexity still controls straight approaches from inside a fixed simplex, but it cannot compare every tangent approach to the same interior data. This is the moment to say that closedness gives lower semicontinuity, while upper semicontinuity at the boundary needs extra geometry.
Lower Semicontinuity Is Not Enough
closed epigraph

Closed Versus Continuous

Closed Function

For a proper convex function, closedness means the epigraph is closed. It guarantees lower semicontinuity, not automatic boundary continuity.

Interior

On \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom} f)\), convexity supplies upper semicontinuity, so closed plus convex becomes continuous.

Boundary

At the boundary, lower semicontinuity only prevents a downward gap. Values may still rise sharply along admissible tangent paths.

Closedness speaks through epigraphsContinuity needs both lower and upper semicontinuity
Separate two properties that students often fuse together. A closed convex function has a closed epigraph, so it cannot suddenly fall below its limiting values. Continuity also requires it not to jump upward. Interior convexity supplies the missing upper control, but boundary points require additional hypotheses such as local simplicial structure or bounded extension data.
Operations With Qualification
sum and envelope rules

Continuity Under Sums

Qualified Sum Rule

When two convex functions are finite on a shared relative-interior region, \(f+g\) is continuous on compact cores inside that region.

Upper Envelopes

A pointwise supremum of convex functions is convex. If the supremum stays finite on the working region, interior continuity applies.

Infimal Translates

Functions like \(h(x)=\inf_{z\in C} f(x-z)\) inherit convexity from the epigraph operation. The continuity conclusion starts once \(h\) is finite on a relative-open set.

Operation rule: convexity first, finiteness and relative-interior qualification secondBoundary-only intersections are warnings
Use sums as a first operation where qualification matters. Convexity of f plus g is easy, but continuity requires a place where both functions have real neighborhoods in their effective domains. The same pattern explains supremums and infimal projections: once the operation has produced a finite convex function on a relative-open region, the continuity theorem takes over.
Do Not Overgeneralize
composition and parameters

Composition Warnings

Convexity Is Not Automatic

A composition \(g\circ F\) needs monotonicity, affine structure, or a known convex-composition rule. Continuity cannot rescue a nonconvex expression.

Safe Pattern

Affine precomposition \(x\mapsto f(Ax+b)\) preserves convexity and transfers relative-interior continuity on qualified preimage regions.

Parameter Pattern

If \(f(x,t)\) is convex in \(x\) and continuous in \(t\), compact parameter windows plus equi-Lipschitz estimates upgrade to joint continuity.

Warning Checklist

  • Is the output still convex?
  • Is the effective domain finite on a relative-open set?
  • Does a compact core avoid the boundary?
  • Are parameter bounds uniform on a neighborhood?
Theorem 10.7: compact parameter windows produce uniform controlComposition requires convexity before continuity can be invoked
Composition is where students often overgeneralize the section. The continuity theorems apply after convexity and finite-domain hypotheses are secured; they do not make arbitrary compositions convex. Emphasize the safe affine case and then connect parameter continuity to the family theorem, where compactness in the parameter supplies the uniform bounds needed for joint continuity.
Epigraph Language
open slices and projections

Epigraph View

Proof Roadmap
  1. Work inside a relatively open effective domain.
  2. Use closure facts for lower semicontinuity.
  3. Show strict sublevel sets are relatively open.
  4. Combine both semicontinuities.

Projection Move

Intersections of the interior of the epigraph with height halfspaces project to open sublevel regions in the base space.

Boundary Question

At boundary points the epigraph may still be closed, but the projected slice can lose the local symmetry that gave upper control.

Epigraph language links Sections 7 and 10Sublevel openness is the upper semicontinuity signal
Re-enter epigraph language to connect this section with the previous closure material. The point is not to reproduce the printed proof line by line, but to name the moves: lower semicontinuity comes from closed epigraph behavior, and upper semicontinuity comes from open strict sublevel sets obtained by slicing and projecting the epigraph over an interior domain.

Section 10 Recap
relative interiors, boundaries, operations

Recap

Main Sentence

Convexity turns local domain thickness into continuity; compact thickness becomes a Lipschitz modulus.

Three Takeaways

  • Continuity is automatic on relatively open convex subsets of the effective domain.
  • Boundary continuity is delicate and depends on closedness plus approach geometry.
  • Operations preserve continuity only after convexity, finiteness, and qualification are checked.
