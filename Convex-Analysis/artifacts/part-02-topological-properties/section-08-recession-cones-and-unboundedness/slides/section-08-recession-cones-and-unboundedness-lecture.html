Recession Cones And Unboundedness

Recession directions are asymptotic certificates: they say which rays stay feasible, which functions stop growing, and which optimization problems can escape to infinity.

Ray Test
direction that never leaves

Direction That Never Leaves

Ray Certificate

\(x+t d\in C\quad\hbox{for every }x\in C,\ t\ge 0.\)

What Is Being Certified?

The vector \(d\) is not just a long step from one point. It is a direction that every point of the convex set can follow forever.

Why Convexity Helps

If one forward shift is always safe, convexity fills the intermediate segment and repeated shifts build the whole half-line.

Inspection Prompt

Move the base point. If the same direction works from every point, the direction belongs to the recession cone.

Artifact: assets/recession-direction.svgNotebook invariant: adding a recession direction preserves feasibility
Start with the operational picture before the notation. A recession direction is stronger than seeing one faraway point; it asks for an entire half-line from every point of the set. This universal base-point condition is what later makes the cone independent of where the ray starts.
Definition
0+ C

Recession Cone Definition

Definition

\(0^+C=\{d: C+t d\subseteq C\ \hbox{for all }t\ge 0\}\).

Core Shape

\(0^+C\) is a convex cone containing \(0\). If \(C\) is closed, its horizon limits recover the same cone.

Base-Point Form

\(d\in 0^+C\iff x+t d\in C\) for every \(x\in C\) and \(t\ge 0\).

Three Equivalent Probes

  • Translate the whole set: \(C+t d\subseteq C\).
  • Trace one ray from each base point.
  • For closed \(C\), take limits of normalized faraway points.

What The Cone Forgets

The cone records directions at infinity, not where the set begins. Two translated copies of \(C\) have the same recession cone.

Closed convex sets have stable horizon behaviorTranslation affects location, not recession
Now attach the notation to the picture. Emphasize that the definition shifts the entire set rather than only a selected point. The cone language follows from positive scaling and convex averaging, while closedness is the condition that lets horizon limits agree cleanly with recession directions.
Reversible Escape
lineality space

Lineality Space

Definition

\(L(C)=0^+C\cap(-0^+C)\).

Meaning

A direction in \(L(C)\) can be followed forever forward and backward. Through every point of \(C\), the full line parallel to that direction remains in \(C\).

Decomposition Lens

Lineality is the flat part at infinity. The remaining quotient geometry is the pointed part where escape has a preferred sign.

Optimization Warning

Objective functions that are flat along lineality directions can have whole affine families of minimizers or ambiguous search paths.

Artifact: assets/lineality-vs-pointed.svgLineality is the largest subspace contained in the recession cone
Lineality is the reversible part of recession. The strip example has both a direction and its negative, so the asymptotic behavior is a genuine line. The pointed cone example has escape, but not in both signs, which is why lineality separates flatness from one-way unboundedness.
Boundedness Test
closed convex sets

Boundedness Certificate

Theorem

For a nonempty closed convex set \(C\subset\mathbb{R}^n\), \(C\) is bounded if and only if \(0^+C=\{0\}\).

How To Use It

To prove boundedness, rule out every nonzero ray. To prove unboundedness, exhibit one nonzero recession direction.

Proof Roadmap

An unbounded sequence has normalized directions with a convergent subsequence; closed convexity turns the limiting direction into a ray certificate.

Hypothesis Watch

Closedness is not cosmetic. Without it, limiting directions can appear in the closure before they are usable from the original set.

Boundedness becomes a cone equality checkOne nonzero ray is enough to certify unboundedness
This is the first major payoff. For closed convex sets, unboundedness cannot be wild; it produces a nonzero direction at infinity. Explain the compactness idea behind the proof without drowning in details: normalize faraway points, take a limiting direction, and use closed convexity to promote it to a ray.
Polyhedral Diagnostic
A d <= 0

Polyhedron Inequality Test

Homogeneous Test

P=\{x:Ax\le b\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0^+P=\{d:Ad\le 0\}.

Derivation

If \(x+t d\) must satisfy \(A(x+t d)\le b\) for all \(t\ge 0\), any positive component of \(A d\) eventually breaks an inequality.

Example Half-Strip

\(P=\{(x_1,x_2):x_1\ge0,\ 0\le x_2\le2\}\) has recession cone \(\{(s,0):s\ge0\}\).

Lineality Test

For the same polyhedron, \(L(P)=\{d:Ad=0\}\). The half-strip has no nonzero lineality.

For polyhedra the recession cone is wonderfully concrete. Substitute x plus t d into every inequality and look at the coefficient of t. If any row has positive drift, a large enough t violates the constraint; if every drift is nonpositive, every forward step remains feasible.
Cone Calculus
sum / product / intersection

Cone Of A Sum/Product

Reliable Rules

\(0^+(C\times D)=0^+C\times 0^+D\)

\(0^+(\cap_i C_i)=\cap_i 0^+C_i\quad\hbox{for closed convex }C_i.\)

Sum Direction

If \(d_C\in0^+C\) and \(d_D\in0^+D\), then \(d_C+d_D\in0^+(C+D)\). Equality is safe in polyhedral and standard closed settings, but should be checked when closure is delicate.

Preimage Rule

For linear \(A\), \(0^+(A^{-1}C)=\{d:Ad\in0^+C\}\) whenever the preimage is nonempty and closed.

Cone arithmetic is a hypothesis-sensitive bookkeeping toolIntersection and product rules are the safest daily diagnostics
Use this slide as a practical calculus, not as a license to forget hypotheses. Products are coordinatewise and intersections retain the directions common to every set. Sums at least contain sums of recession directions; in polyhedral work that is usually the computed rule, while closure-sensitive cases deserve inspection.
Function Horizon
f^{0+}

Recession Of Functions

Definition Through Epigraphs

\(\operatorname{epi} f^{0+}=0^+(\operatorname{epi} f)\).

Closed Convex Formula

\(f^{0+}(d)=\sup_{t>0}\frac{f(x+t d)-f(x)}{t}\), independent of \(x\in\operatorname{dom} f\).

Interpretation

\(f^{0+}(d)\) is the asymptotic slope in direction \(d\). Negative values drive descent; zero values mark flat escape; positive or infinite values resist escape.

Domain Link

If \(d\) is impossible in the domain at infinity, the recession value becomes \(+\infty\) through the epigraph view.

Artifact: assets/function-recession-epigraph.svgFunction recession is set recession in one higher dimension
Move from sets to functions by lifting the graph into its epigraph. The recession function is not an extra definition floating in space; it is the vertical boundary of the epigraph recession cone. The slope formula gives students a calculational version when the function is closed and convex.
Horizon Behavior
exp example

Epigraph Horizon

Example

f(x)=e^x:\quad f^{0+}(d)=\begin{cases}0,&d\le0,\\ +\infty,&d>0.\end{cases}

Read The Picture

Moving left makes the curve flatten toward height zero, so a horizontal epigraph ray survives. Moving right makes every finite vertical slope fail.

Horizon Does Not Mean Minimum

The horizontal escape direction predicts a finite limiting value, but the function never actually reaches that value.

Contrast

For \(f(x)=x^2\), every nonzero direction has infinite recession value, so lower levels cannot drift away.

Horizon cone stores asymptotic slopes, not just feasible directionsFlat escape will reappear as nonattainment
The exponential example is the cleanest way to separate flat escape from actual descent. Along negative directions, the graph gets flatter and flatter, so the horizon slope is zero. Along positive directions, growth overwhelms every finite slope, so the recession value is infinite.
Lower Level Sets
shared recession cone

Level Boundedness

Level-Set Rule

For closed proper convex \(f\), all nonempty sets \(\{x:f(x)\le\alpha\}\) have recession cone \(\{d:f^{0+}(d)\le0\}\).

Boundedness Criterion

If one nonempty lower level set is bounded, then every lower level set is bounded. Equivalently, the cone \(\{d:f^{0+}(d)\le0\}\) is only \(\{0\}\).

Coercive Behavior

The quadratic has no nonzero nonincreasing recession direction, so its lower levels remain compact intervals.

Escape Behavior

The exponential has \(d=-1\) with \(f^{0+}(d)=0\), so every positive lower level stretches without bound to the left.

Artifact: assets/level-boundedness.svgLevel boundedness is recession-cone triviality for the function
This slide connects horizon slopes to compactness of optimization search regions. The main message is that lower level sets all share the same directions of escape. Once one nonempty level is bounded, the shared cone must be trivial, and boundedness propagates to the rest.
Optimization Consequence
descent at infinity

Objective Descent Directions

Unbounded-Below Certificate

If \(d\in0^+C\) and \(f^{0+}(d)<0\), then minimizing \(f\) over \(C\) has value \(-\infty\).

Linear Objective

For \(f(x)=c^T x\), the recession value is \(c^T d\). A feasible direction with negative dot product is an exact certificate of escape.

Half-Strip Check

On \(P=\{x_1\ge0,\ 0\le x_2\le2\}\), the direction \(d=(1,0)\) is feasible. With \(c=(-1,0.25)\), \(c^T d=-1\).

Failure Mode
finite infimum, no minimizer

No-Minimum Failure

Different From -Infinity

f(x)=e^x,\quad f^{0+}(-1)=0,\quad \inf f=0,\quad \operatorname{argmin} f=\emptyset.

What Fails

The direction does not decrease at a negative linear rate. It lets the function approach a limiting height without ever touching it.

Geometric Reading

The epigraph has a horizontal recession ray, but the boundary value at height zero is missing from the graph.

Existence Lesson

Ruling out negative recession directions prevents \(-\infty\), but a nonzero zero-slope direction can still destroy attainment.

Flat escape explains nonattainmentNegative recession and zero recession are different failure modes
Students often compress all unbounded behavior into one failure. Separate the two cases. A negative recession value sends the objective to minus infinity, while a zero recession value can create a finite limiting value that is not attained. The exponential function is the canonical one-dimensional picture.

Recap
asymptotic certificates

Recap

1. Rays Certify

A direction belongs to \(0^+C\) only when every point can follow it forever.

2. Lineality Reverses

The lineality space is the part of recession that works in both signs.

3. Epigraphs Lift

The recession function is the horizon boundary of the epigraph.

4. Optimization Escapes

Negative recession gives \(-\infty\); zero recession can give nonattainment.
