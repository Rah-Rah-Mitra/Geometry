Extreme Points And Faces Of Convex Sets

Faces are decomposition cells: segment tests identify indivisible points, support vectors expose boundary pieces, and recession structure records the same story at infinity.

Extreme Point Test
no nontrivial split

Segment Test For Extreme Points

Definition

\(x\in C\) is extreme when \(x=(1-\lambda)y+\lambda z,\ 0\lt\lambda\lt1,\ y,z\in C\) forces \(y=z=x\).

Interpretation

An extreme point is not a strict interior point of any feasible chord. It cannot be decomposed into two distinct feasible endpoints.

Face Link

A zero-dimensional face is a singleton \(\{x\}\), and those singleton faces are exactly the extreme points.

Teaching Check

Move the chord, not the point. If one nontrivial chord passes through \(x\), the point is not extreme.

Artifact: assets/extreme-point-segment-test.svgSegment test is intrinsic; no support vector is required
Use the chord picture slowly. A point is extreme only when every attempt to write it as a strict convex average collapses to the same point. The edge point fails because the segment endpoints are different feasible points.
Definition
endpoint forcing

Face Definition

Face Of \(C\)

\(F\subset C\) is a face if \(F\) is convex and \( (1-\lambda)y+\lambda z\in F,\ 0\lt\lambda\lt1,\ y,z\in C \Rightarrow y,z\in F.\)

Boundary Cell

The face contains every endpoint of every segment whose relative interior it captures. This is stronger than being a convex subset of \(C\).

Immediate Examples

  • \(\emptyset\) and \(C\) are always faces.
  • Vertices, edges, and facets of a polytope are faces.
  • A whole exposed edge is one face, not a pile of separate points.

Dimension Drop

Any proper nonempty face sits in the relative boundary, so its dimension is strictly smaller than \(\dim C\).

Proper faces lie in the relative boundaryFace tests are segment tests upgraded from points to subsets
The definition is stronger than convex subset. A face inherits every endpoint of a segment whose relative interior it captures. This endpoint-forcing property is why faces behave like honest boundary pieces instead of arbitrary convex slices.
Support Certificate
linear maximizers

Exposed Face By Support Vector

Exposed Face

\(F_C(c)=\{x\in C: c\cdot x=h_C(c)\},\quad h_C(c)=\sup_{y\in C}c\cdot y.\)

Why It Is A Face

If a strict average reaches the maximum score, linearity forces both endpoints to have the same maximum score.

Support Geometry

For nonzero \(c\), the hyperplane \(c\cdot x=h_C(c)\) supports \(C\) and its contact set is exactly \(F_C(c)\).

Degenerate Direction

The zero vector exposes all of \(C\). Nontrivial exposed faces come from nonzero support vectors.

Connect this slide back to support functions. A vector c scores every point of C, and the maximizers form an exposed face. Generic directions usually expose points; special normals can expose higher-dimensional faces. Ask learners to identify the exposed set before naming it, then point out that the certificate is the normal vector plus the attained support value.

Polyhedron Form

\(C=\{x:Ax\le b\},\quad F_i=\{x\in C:a_i\cdot x=b_i\}\) is an exposed face when nonempty.

Not Every Convex Subset

A diagonal segment inside a square is convex, but it is not a face because captured interior points do not force endpoints to remain in the segment.

Polytopes make facial structure finite and inspectableActive inequalities are computational support certificates
For polytopes, faces become active-constraint patterns. One active supporting inequality may select an edge, two independent active inequalities may select a vertex, and the zero support vector leaves the whole set active. Use the examples to make the rank of active normals visible: geometry, algebra, and the solver's active-set report are the same object.
Warning
extreme need not be exposed

Non-Exposed Warning

Implication Only One Way

\(\hbox{exposed point}\Rightarrow\hbox{extreme point},\qquad \hbox{not conversely in general.}\)

Face-Of-Face Trap

An exposed point of an exposed face need not be exposed in the ambient set. The supporting certificate may isolate it only after restricting to the face.

Density Rescue

For closed convex sets, exposed points approximate extreme points in the Straszewicz sense. That is an approximation theorem, not a pointwise exposure theorem.

Teaching Rule

Use support vectors to certify exposure. Use segment tests to certify extremality. Do not substitute one test for the other.

Artifact: assets/nonexposed-warning.svgOriginal schematic based on the section's warning theme
Pause on the warning because it prevents a common overreach. Exposure is a powerful certificate, but not every face or extreme point has such a certificate in the original set. Later density results recover exposed approximations, not literal equality.
Facial Cells
unique relative-interior cell

Minimal Face Containing A Point

Partition Principle

Relative interiors of nonempty faces partition \(C\). Each \(x\in C\) lies in the relative interior of a unique minimal face.

Minimal Face

\(\operatorname{face}_C(x)\) is the intersection of all faces of \(C\) containing \(x\). In a polyhedron, it is usually read from the active inequalities at \(x\).

Lattice Rules
intersections and inheritance

Faces Under Intersections

Intersection Rule

The intersection of any family of faces of \(C\) is again a face of \(C\). Therefore the faces form a complete lattice under inclusion.

meet

Greatest lower bound is literal intersection.

join

Least upper bound is the smallest face containing the union.

inherit

A face of a face is a face of the original set.

restrict

If \(F\subset D\subset C\), then \(F\) is a face of \(D\).

Exposure Caveat

The same inheritance statement can fail for exposedness. A face may be exposed after restriction but not exposed in the ambient set.

Computational Reading

Intersections of active equality sets move down the lattice. Adding active constraints reduces dimension until a relative-interior cell is reached.

Artifact: assets/face-lattice.svgStrictly decreasing chains terminate by dimension drop
Use this as the algebra of facial structure. Intersections move downward in the lattice, faces of faces remain faces of the original set, and the smallest face above a collection is obtained as a lattice join.
At Infinity
directions as faces

Recession And Lineality Cautions

Recession And Lineality

\(0^+C=\{d:C+td\subset C\ \forall t\ge0\},\quad L(C)=0^+C\cap(-0^+C).\)

Extreme Direction

A half-line face contributes a direction at infinity. For cones, these are the usual extreme rays.

Converse Warning

An extreme ray of the recession cone need not be realized as a half-line face of \(C\). Parabolic sets make this caution visible.

Lineality Reduction

If \(L(C)\ne\{0\}\), faces correspond to faces of a lineality-free slice \(C_0=C\cap L(C)^\perp\), then lift by adding \(L(C)\).

Asset ledger: assets/extreme-ray-ledger.jsonRecession structure is facial structure at infinity
Separate finite boundary pieces from behavior at infinity. Half-line faces give extreme directions, while lineality means every candidate can move both forward and backward, destroying isolated extreme structure unless we quotient it away. Emphasize that recession data is not a side case for unbounded sets; it is the part of the facial story that records how the set escapes to infinity.
Representation
Krein-Milman intuition

Decomposition Into Extremes And Rays

Finite-Dimensional Spine

C\hbox{ closed, no lines}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C=\operatorname{conv}\{\hbox{extreme points and extreme directions}\}.

Bounded Case

C\hbox{ compact convex}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C=\operatorname{conv} E(C).

Krein-Milman Intuition

Compact convex bodies are generated by their indivisible boundary points. In finite dimensions, unbounded closed sets add extreme directions as points at infinity.

Exposed Version

Replacing extreme data by exposed data generally requires closure: \(C=\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{conv} S_{\mathrm{exposed}})\) under the no-lines hypothesis.

Asset: assets/extreme-ray-ledger.jsonClosed bounded case is the compact convex hull theorem
State the finite-dimensional Krein-Milman intuition in practical language. Closed convex sets with no lines are built from extreme points and extreme directions; bounded sets need only extreme points, while exposed data may require closure. Keep the message computational: if a representation has too many generators, the theorem tells us which generators are structurally meaningful and which ones can be compressed away.

Numerical Face Certificate

Polyhedral Face Candidate

C=\{x:Ax\le b\},\quad F_I=\{x\in C:A_Ix=b_I\}.

Certificate Steps

  1. Check \(F_I\ne\emptyset\).
  2. Find \(p\in F_I\) with strict slack on inactive inequalities.
  3. Use a nonnegative multiplier on active rows to expose \(F_I\).
  4. For a point \(x\), set \(I=\{i:a_i\cdot x=b_i\}\) to test its minimal face.

Numerical Tolerance

Classify active rows by residuals, but report the tolerance. Near-degenerate faces can swap dimension when residuals are comparable to solver error.

Recap: Certificates For Decomposition

Main Thread

Faces identify the pieces of \(C\) that behave as indivisible convex cells; exposed faces are the cells seen by support vectors.

Five Takeaways

  • Extreme points are singleton faces.
  • Faces force segment endpoints.
  • Exposed faces are support maximizer sets.
  • Minimal faces partition \(C\) by relative interiors.
  • Unbounded closed sets need extreme directions and lineality checks.
