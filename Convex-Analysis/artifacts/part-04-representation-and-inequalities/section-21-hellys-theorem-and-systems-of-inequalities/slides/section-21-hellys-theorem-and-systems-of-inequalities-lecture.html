Helly's Theorem Turns Intersection Search Into A Small Certificate

The section's central move is compression: in finite-dimensional convex geometry, a large infeasible intersection can be explained by only \(d+1\) sets or inequalities.

Lecture Thesis

Feasibility is witnessed by a point; infeasibility is witnessed by a small convex subfamily or by nonnegative multipliers.

Geometry

\(\cap_i C_i\neq\emptyset\) is an intersection question.

Dimension

Only \(d+1\) constraints are needed for finite empty certificates.

Algorithm

Search small subsystems, then verify the witness directly.

This prepares them to read systems of inequalities through the same lens.
Helly's Theorem
finite convex families

Every Finite Convex Intersection Is Controlled By \(d+1\) Tests

Finite Helly In \(R^d\)

Let \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\subset R^d\) be convex. If every subcollection of at most \(d+1\) sets has nonempty intersection, then \(\bigcap_{i=1}^m C_i\neq\emptyset\).

Contrapositive

If \(\bigcap_i C_i=\emptyset\), then some \(J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}\) with \(|J|\le d+1\) already has \(\bigcap_{j\in J}C_j=\emptyset\).

Why The Contrapositive Teaches Better

The certificate is finite and inspectable. One does not need to display every set in the family; one displays the small obstruction.

Working Translation

Convex feasibility is an intersection problem; Helly is the rule that bounds how much of the family an infeasibility proof must remember.

Use this slide to separate the two verification modes. A feasible point is easy to audit because every constraint can be checked against the same vector. Systems Of Inequalities
convex level sets

A Convex Inequality System Is A Convex Intersection In Disguise

Section 21 Setup

Systems are written as \(f_i(x)\le\alpha_i\) and \(f_i(x)<\alpha_i\), with each \(f_i\) convex. Their solution set is an intersection of convex level sets.

Equations Fit Too

An affine equation \(\langle b,x\rangle=\beta\) is represented by the two weak inequalities \(\langle b,x\rangle\le\beta\) and \(-\langle b,x\rangle\le-\beta\).

Systems become setsSet certificates become inequality certificates
Connect the theorem language to the algebra students will actually manipulate. A convex inequality defines a convex level set, so solving a system is just intersecting those sets. The strict and weak versions matter, and affine equalities can be folded in as paired inequalities. This translation makes Helly immediately relevant to feasibility problems.
Infeasible Subsystem Certificate
nonnegative multipliers

Multipliers Turn An Empty Strict System Into A Checkable Inequality

Theorem 21.1 Pattern

Either some \(x\in C\) satisfies \(f_i(x)<0\) for all \(i\), or there are \(\lambda_i\ge0\), not all zero, with \(\sum_i\lambda_i f_i(x)\ge0\) for every \(x\in C\).

Why It Certifies Failure

If all \(f_i(x)<0\), any nonzero nonnegative weighted sum is negative. A universal nonnegative lower bound contradicts that possibility.

Alternative theorem as certificate theoremWeights must be nonnegative
Present multipliers as proof objects. If the proposed feasible point existed, every strict inequality would make the weighted sum negative. The theorem supplies the opposite inequality everywhere on C, so both alternatives cannot hold. The drawing shows the finite half-plane version where the contradiction is simply f1 plus f2 plus f3 equals one.
Separation / Farkas Preview
orthants and alternatives

The Orthant Separation Proof Foreshadows Farkas Alternatives

Separation Move

Map each candidate \(x\in C\) to a vector of upper residuals in \(R^m\). If no residual vector lies strictly below zero, separate that convex image from the nonpositive orthant.

Normal Vector

The separating normal has nonnegative coordinates because it must support the nonpositive orthant from the correct side.

Multiplier Meaning

Those coordinates are the multipliers \(\lambda_i\) in the infeasibility certificate.

Preview

Farkas-type theorems refine this same alternative: either a primal feasible point exists, or a dual multiplier proves that it cannot.

Section 21 uses separation as an engineSection 22 refines the linear case
This is the bridge to the next section. The proof of the multiplier alternative separates a convex residual set from the nonpositive orthant. The separating normal becomes the vector of multipliers. In the linear case this pattern sharpens into Farkas-style alternatives and elementary-vector representation theory, which is why the section ends by pointing forward.
Example In R2

A Planar Infeasibility Certificate Can Be A Triple

Half-Plane System

\(-x\le0,\quad -y\le0,\quad x+y+1\le0.\)

Omitted inequalityFeasible witness for the remaining pair
\(-x\le0\)\((-2,0)\) satisfies \(y\ge0\) and \(x+y\le-1\)
\(-y\le0\)\((0,-2)\) satisfies \(x\ge0\) and \(x+y\le-1\)
\(x+y+1\le0\)\((0,0)\) satisfies \(x\ge0\) and \(y\ge0\)

Triple Contradiction

Adding all three inequalities gives \(1\le0\). Each pair is feasible, so the minimal obstruction really has size \(2+1\).

R2 certificate size: 3The contradiction is arithmetic and geometric
Make the example concrete. The three inequalities say x is nonnegative, y is nonnegative, and their sum is at most negative one. Any pair can be satisfied, so pairwise tests are misleading. The three together sum to the contradiction one less than or equal to zero, exactly matching the planar Helly threshold.
Example In R3
four constraints are enough

In Three Dimensions, The Sharp Empty Certificate Has Size Four

Four Half-Spaces

\(x_1\le0,\ x_2\le0,\ x_3\le0,\ -x_1-x_2-x_3+1\le0.\)

Summed Certificate

The four left sides add to \(1\). If all were nonpositive at the same point, their sum would be nonpositive, which is impossible.

Sharpness Check

Omitting any one constraint leaves a feasible point: \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), or \((0,0,0)\).

Geometric Reading

The first three half-spaces force the sum \(x_1+x_2+x_3\le0\); the fourth forces it to be at least \(1\).

R3 certificate size: 4Dimension controls the active obstruction count
Use the three-dimensional example to show that d plus one is not cosmetic. The first three inequalities put the point in the nonpositive orthant, while the fourth demands a positive total sum. The four together are impossible, but dropping any one of them restores feasibility. This is the same certificate grammar in one higher dimension.

Failure Outside Convexity
convexity does real work

Without Convexity, Small Intersection Tests Can Lie

Nonconvex Counterexample

Let \(A=\{a,b\}\), \(B=\{b,c\}\), and \(C=\{a,c\}\). Every pair intersects, but \(A\cap B\cap C=\emptyset\).

Why Helly Does Not Apply

The sets are not convex. There is no segment-filling structure forcing the pairwise overlaps to organize around a common point.

Teaching Point

The theorem is about convex families, not about arbitrary incidence patterns.

Ledger Entry

If the sets are not convex, the Helly threshold is no longer a reliable certificate rule.

Failure modeConvexity is the structural hypothesis
This slide protects against a common overgeneralization. Pairwise intersection data can be arranged in a cycle with no common point when the sets are arbitrary. Convexity rules out that kind of disconnected bookkeeping by forcing segments between points to remain in the set. The theorem's power is exactly in that geometric structure.
