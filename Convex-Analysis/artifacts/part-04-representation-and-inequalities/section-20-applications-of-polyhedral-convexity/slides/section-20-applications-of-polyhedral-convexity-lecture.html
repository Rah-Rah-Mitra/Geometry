Applications Of Polyhedral Convexity

Polyhedral structure turns convex arguments into finite certificates: active inequalities, vertex optima, multiplier cones, and closedness statements that can be checked by small systems of linear equations.

Lecture Goal

Use linear programs as the laboratory for polyhedral convexity: identify active constraints, certify an optimum, and explain why finitely many faces make stronger conclusions possible.

Core Invariant

The chosen optimum satisfies every inequality and ties exactly the displayed active constraints.

Main Caution

Polyhedral conclusions are finite, but not automatic. Degenerate active sets and unbounded directions still need explicit checks.

Finite Faces Turn Geometry Into Checklists

Optimization

Linear objectives over polyhedra achieve optima on faces when the value is finite and the feasible region has no improving recession direction.

Representation

Active inequalities and vertex descriptions convert local geometry into finite matrices and small certificates.

Closedness

Images, sums, and projections that can be delicate for general convex sets often behave better in the polyhedral category.

Finite face structure is the hidden engineApplications reduce to active finite data
Position the section as a bridge from representation theory to optimization practice. The common mechanism is finite face structure. Once the class sees a polyhedron as finitely many faces, the later applications to optima, multipliers, and closed images become different readings of the same finite combinatorics.
Active Constraints
equalities at a point

Active Constraints Name The Local Face

Polyhedron

\(P=\{x:Ax\le b\}\), with active set \(I(x)=\{i:a_i^Tx=b_i\}\).

Face From Equalities

Holding the active inequalities as equalities cuts out the smallest visible face containing the point.

Certificate Role

The active set is a finite record of which constraints are responsible for local geometry at the point.

Active inequalities are finite local dataFaces are equality patterns inside Ax <= b
Make active constraints the first concrete certificate. At a point of a polyhedron, most inequalities are slack and irrelevant to the local face. The constraints that tie as equalities define the face, and at a nondegenerate planar vertex two independent active lines are enough to pin down the point.
Linear Program
objective direction

A Linear Objective Is Certified By An Active Face

Problem

\(\max\{c^Tx:x\in P\}\).

Finite Candidate Set

If the optimum is finite and \(P\) has vertices, the objective can be checked on faces exposed by supporting level sets.

Active Certificate

At the selected vertex, the active constraints tie, all other inequalities remain satisfied, and the objective cannot improve along feasible directions.

Unbounded Exception

A recession direction \(r\) with \(Ar\le0\) and \(c^Tr>0\) destroys existence of a finite optimum.

Objective level sets expose a faceUnbounded recession directions must be ruled out
Use the moving level lines to show why an optimum sits on an exposed face. For a polygon the face is often a vertex, but the certificate is not just the picture. It includes feasibility of all inequalities, equality for the active set, and absence of a feasible recession direction that improves the objective.
Finite Certificates
small linear systems

Polyhedral Claims Come With Small Certificates

Vertex Certificate

A candidate \(x\) is a vertex when active normals span the ambient space of feasible directions.

Feasibility Certificate

Check \(Ax\le b\) row by row. The active rows identify the equality pattern.

Optimality Certificate

Express the objective normal as a nonnegative combination of active outward normals.

Certificates are row-based and finiteActive normals explain local and optimal behavior
Translate polyhedral applications into a certificate ledger. Feasibility is a finite row check. The active set is a finite equality pattern. Optimality is a finite cone-membership statement for the objective normal. This is the section's practical theme: the geometry is convex, but the certificate is linear algebra.
Normal Cone
active multipliers

Normal Cones Package Active Multipliers

Polyhedral Normal Cone

\(N_P(x)=\operatorname{cone}\{a_i:i\in I(x)\}\) for active outward normals.

Optimality Condition

For maximizing \(c^Tx\), the objective normal must lie in the normal cone of the exposed optimal face.

Degeneracy

More active constraints than dimension can occur. The multiplier certificate may not be unique.

Normal cones convert active constraints into multipliersOptimal directions live in exposed normal cones
Introduce the normal cone as the compact language for active multipliers. At a polyhedral point, the cone is generated by finitely many active normals. A linear objective is optimized at that point when its normal belongs to this cone, which is exactly the multiplier certificate in geometric form.
Closedness
polyhedral stability

Polyhedra Behave Well Under Linear Operations

Stability Theme

Linear images and projections of polyhedra are polyhedral, hence closed in finite dimension.

Why This Helps

General convex projections can lose closedness. Polyhedral projections remain describable by finitely many linear inequalities.

Application

Existence and duality statements often simplify once the feasible set or epigraph is polyhedral.

Finite inequality descriptions survive projectionThis is a key polyhedral refinement over general convexity
Use this slide to explain why polyhedral hypotheses strengthen conclusions. Projection is a common source of closure trouble for general convex sets, but a polyhedron projects to another polyhedron in finite dimension. That finite description keeps the resulting set closed and makes existence or duality arguments cleaner.
Piecewise Linear Convexity
finite maxima

Polyhedral Epigraphs Are Piecewise Linear Convex Models

Model

\(f(x)=\max_{1\le i\le m}\{a_i^Tx+\beta_i\}\).

Polyhedral Epigraph

The epigraph is the intersection of finitely many halfspaces \(t\ge a_i^Tx+\beta_i\).

Subgradient Certificate

At a point, active affine pieces generate the subdifferential as a convex hull of active slopes.

Optimization Link

Breakpoints and faces are the functional version of active constraints in a polyhedron.

Polyhedral convex functions have finite affine piecesActive pieces generate subgradients
Connect sets and functions by moving to epigraphs. A piecewise linear convex function has a polyhedral epigraph because finitely many affine lower bounds define it. The active pieces at a point play the same role as active inequalities, and their slopes generate the local subgradient information.
Sensitivity
normal fan

Changing The Objective Moves Through The Normal Fan

Stable Regions

All objective normals in the same normal cone select the same exposed face.

Transitions

Crossing a normal-fan boundary changes the active face and can change the optimal vertex.

Degenerate Case

If the objective lies on a cone boundary, a whole edge or higher-dimensional face may be optimal.

Normal fans organize linear program sensitivityActive faces change only across finitely many boundaries
Use sensitivity as another application of finite face structure. The objective normal does not choose a new optimizer continuously at every angle. Instead it moves through finitely many normal cones. Inside one cone the optimizer is stable, and crossing a boundary explains exactly when the active face changes.
Executable Invariant
active residuals

The Notebook Check Audits Feasibility And Activity

Residuals

\(s=b-Ax,\quad s_i=0 \text{ for active } i,\quad s_i\ge0 \text{ otherwise}.\)

Feasibility

Every slack value must be nonnegative within tolerance.

Activity

The selected active rows must tie as equalities at the candidate optimum.

Optimality

The objective should align with the active normal cone or fail with an explicit improving direction.

Source notebook invariant: optimum satisfies all inequalities and ties active constraintsResiduals identify the failed hypothesis
Tie the slide to the notebook check. The selected optimum must pass three finite tests: all slacks nonnegative, active slacks zero, and objective normal compatible with the active cone. If a test fails, the failure is informative because it says whether feasibility, activity, or optimality was incorrectly claimed.

Lab: Change The Objective Or A Constraint

Experiment A

Rotate the objective vector. Track which normal cone contains it and when the optimal face changes.

Experiment B

Move one inequality boundary. Watch the active set change and recompute the vertex certificate.

Experiment C

Add a recession direction. Check whether the objective becomes unbounded or stays finite.

A useful report names the changed parameter, the new active set, and the residual that confirms or rejects the certificate.

Change one ingredient at a timeEvery result should have a finite certificate
Frame the lab as active-set debugging. Students should perturb one ingredient and then explain the new face structure before trusting the numerical answer. The certificate should say which constraints are active, whether all slacks have the right sign, and whether the objective is bounded in the displayed direction.
