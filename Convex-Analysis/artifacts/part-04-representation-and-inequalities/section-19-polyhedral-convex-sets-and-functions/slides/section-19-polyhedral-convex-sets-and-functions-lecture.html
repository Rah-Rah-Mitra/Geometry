H-representation
membership by residuals

Halfspace Intersection

Polyhedral Convex Set

\(P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n: a_i\cdot x\le \beta_i,\ i=1,\ldots,m\}\).

Finite Certificate

A point \(x\) is feasible exactly when every residual \(a_i\cdot x-\beta_i\) is nonpositive.

Equations Fit

The equality \(a\cdot x=\beta\) is the pair \(a\cdot x\le\beta\) and \(-a\cdot x\le-\beta\).

Cone Test

If every boundary hyperplane passes through the origin, the inequalities are homogeneous and the set is a polyhedral cone.

Start with the H-representation because it is the most checkable form. A point belongs to the polyhedron exactly when every affine inequality has nonpositive residual. Equations are not a separate species here, since each equality is just two weak inequalities.
V-representation
vertices and rays

Generator Representation

Finite Generation

\(P=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}+\operatorname{cone}\{d_1,\ldots,d_r\}\).

Coefficient Ledger

\(x=\sum_i \lambda_i v_i+\sum_j \mu_j d_j,\quad \lambda_i,\mu_j\ge0,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i=1.\)

Bounded Case

If there are no recession rays, the object is a polytope: a convex hull of finitely many points.

Unbounded Case

Rays are not optional decoration. They are the data that records movement to infinity while staying in the set.

Displayed example: conv{(0,0),(0,2),(3,5)} + cone{(1,0)}The same set also satisfies four inequalities
Switch to the internal representation. Vertices describe bounded choices, while directions describe how the set can recede without leaving itself. In the unbounded example, a triangle is swept along one horizontal recession ray, which is why the V-representation uses both a convex hull and a cone.

Active Constraints

Active Set

\(I(x)=\{i:a_i\cdot x=\beta_i\}\).

Relative Interior

Inside the relative interior of a face, the active inequalities stay fixed.

Boundary Refinement

Moving to a smaller face adds active constraints. A vertex usually has more equalities than an edge.

Residual Audit

Numerically, a face claim is a residual claim: active rows are zero, inactive rows are strictly negative.

Finite face structure
from patterns to geometry

Faces Of Polyhedra

Face Consequence

Every face of a polyhedral convex set is again polyhedral, and there are only finitely many faces.

Facet

A maximal proper face, usually selected by one active inequality.

Vertex

A zero-dimensional face, selected by enough independent active equalities.

Extreme Direction

A one-dimensional face of the recession cone, visible as a generator ray.

Face language connects geometry to the H/V equivalenceExtreme points and extreme directions are special faces
Connect active patterns to the finiteness of faces. A finite inequality list gives only finitely many possible equality patterns, and the relative interiors of faces do not overlap. Vertices, facets, and recession rays are therefore not isolated facts; they are the visible pieces of one finite face structure.

Polyhedral Cones

Cone H-side

\(K=\{x:a_i\cdot x\le0,\ i=1,\ldots,m\}\).

Cone V-side

\(K=\operatorname{cone}\{r_1,\ldots,r_s\}\).

Recession Cone

For \(P=\{x:Ax\le b\}\), directions \(d\) that keep \(x+\lambda d\) feasible satisfy \(Ad\le0\).

No Convex Weights

Cones scale freely, so coefficients are nonnegative but do not sum to one.

Asset: assets/polyhedral-cone.svgRecession data is the unbounded part of the V-representation
Specialize the dictionary to cones. On the H-side, every boundary hyperplane passes through the origin, so the inequalities are homogeneous. On the V-side, there are no vertices to convex-average; the whole cone is built from nonnegative combinations of finitely many rays.

Piecewise-Linear Convex Functions

Polyhedral Convex Function

A convex function \(f\) is polyhedral when \(\operatorname{epi} f\) is a polyhedral convex set.

Standard Form

\(f(x)=\max_i(a_i\cdot x-\beta_i)+\delta_C(x)\), with \(C\) polyhedral.

Affine Pieces

Each affine piece contributes one lifted halfspace \(t\ge a_i\cdot x-\beta_i\).

Domain Term

The indicator \(\delta_C\) keeps the function finite only on the polyhedral set \(C\).

Asset: assets/piecewise-linear-epigraph.svgThe notebook invariant checks max of affine pieces
Move from sets to functions by looking at epigraphs. A proper polyhedral convex function can be read as a finite maximum of affine pieces, possibly restricted to a polyhedral domain by an indicator term. This is the function version of finite inequalities.
Lifted halfspaces
R^{n+1}

Epigraph As Polyhedron

Lift

\(\operatorname{epi} f=\{(x,t):t\ge f(x)\}\).

Max Rule

If \(f=\max_i \ell_i\), then \(t\ge f(x)\) is equivalent to \(t\ge \ell_i(x)\) for every \(i\).

Domain Rule

If \(x\in C=\{x:Bx\le c\}\), append the vertical halfspaces \(Bx\le c\) in the lifted space.

Function Check

Polyhedral convexity of \(f\) becomes ordinary polyhedral convexity of \(\operatorname{epi} f\).

Epigraphs turn function questions into set questionsFinite affine pieces become finite lifted inequalities
Translate the maximum formula into epigraph inequalities. The condition t greater than or equal to every affine piece is a finite system of halfspaces in the lifted space. The lower envelope of those halfspaces is the graph, and the region above it is the epigraph.

Face Analogy

An active affine piece in an epigraph is like an active inequality on a polyhedron.

Read the piecewise-linear graph through active affine functions. Away from a breakpoint one affine piece wins uniquely. At a breakpoint two pieces tie, and the subgradient becomes the convex hull of the active slopes. This is the function analogue of active constraints on a face.

Computational Caution

Conversion may grow the number of faces or generators. The theorem says a finite certificate exists, not that every conversion is small.

Conversion is a proof technique and an algorithmic taskUse the representation that exposes the desired certificate
Explain conversion as a certificate-changing task rather than a cosmetic rewrite. H to V asks for vertices and recession rays; V to H asks for supporting inequalities. Linear images, inverse images, sums, finite maxima, and epigraph constructions preserve polyhedrality because they preserve finite certificates.
