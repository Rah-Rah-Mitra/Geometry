Linear Inequalities

Alternative theorems turn linear feasibility into certificates. Either a target lies in the cone generated by the system, or a separating functional proves exactly why it cannot.

cone generators
target vector
separating normal
alternative

Lecture Goal

Read systems of linear inequalities as cone-membership questions and learn what a Farkas certificate must check.

Core Invariant

The certificate is nonnegative on every cone generator and strictly negative on the rejected target.

Main Caution

Weak, strict, homogeneous, and inhomogeneous systems have different certificate forms. The inequality sign matters.

Alternative Logic
exactly one certificate

An Alternative Theorem Gives Two Mutually Exclusive Certificates

Cone Membership

Either \(b=A\lambda\) for some \(\lambda\ge0\), or there is \(y\) with \(A^Ty\ge0\) and \(\langle b,y\rangle<0\).

Primal Certificate

The nonnegative vector \(\lambda\) constructs the target from the generators.

Dual Certificate

The vector \(y\) separates the target from every nonnegative combination of generators.

Alternative statements are certificate pairsThe two systems cannot both hold
State the alternative in a form that students can test. The primal side constructs b from the columns of A using nonnegative weights. The dual side gives a vector y that all columns accept but b rejects. The signs make mutual exclusion immediate by taking inner products.
Cone Membership
nonnegative combinations

Cone Membership Is The Feasible Side

Generated Cone

\(\operatorname{cone}\{a_1,\dots,a_m\}=\{\sum_i\lambda_i a_i:\lambda_i\ge0\}\).

Certificate

A vector of nonnegative weights is enough. Multiplying the generators and summing reconstructs the target.

Boundary Case

Zero weights identify unused generators. A boundary target may need fewer rays than the full cone description.

Feasibility is a nonnegative coefficient problemZero weights reveal smaller certificates
Show the easy side first. When b lies in the cone, the certificate is constructive: list the nonnegative coefficients. This is the same finite-witness mentality as the previous sections, now applied to rays rather than convex combinations with weights summing to one.
Separation
rejected target

A Separating Normal Certifies Infeasibility

Certificate Inequalities

\(\langle a_i,y\rangle\ge0\ \forall i,\qquad \langle b,y\rangle<0.\)

Why It Works

Every conic combination of generators has nonnegative pairing with \(y\), so a target with negative pairing cannot be in the cone.

Sign Discipline

Flipping the normal reverses the signs. The certificate must match the chosen inequality convention.

Residual Check

The notebook invariant is exactly this sign pattern: nonnegative on generators, negative on the target.

The dual certificate is a separating functionalSign conventions must be checked, not guessed
Now focus on the rejected target. The certificate normal gives a scalar test that every generator passes and b fails. Because nonnegative combinations preserve nonnegative pairing, the target cannot be generated. This is the cleanest way to make infeasibility visible and checkable.
Farkas Lemma
matrix form

Farkas Lemma Is Linear Algebra With A Sign Certificate

One Common Form

Exactly one holds: \(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\), or \(y^TA\ge0,\ y^Tb<0\).

Construction Side

The vector \(x\) gives the nonnegative combination that produces \(b\).

Exclusion Side

The vector \(y\) proves no such nonnegative combination exists.

Farkas certificates are finite matrix checksExactly one side should pass
Give the matrix statement after the geometry so the symbols have a job. The primal vector x is a construction certificate. The dual vector y is an exclusion certificate. Students should verify mutual exclusion by multiplying y transpose into Ax equals b and comparing the two sign conclusions.
Strict / Weak
boundary behavior

Changing The Inequality Sign Changes The Certificate

Weak Systems

Closed halfspaces and closed cones support non-strict separation statements.

Strict Systems

Open constraints require margin information; a boundary certificate may no longer settle feasibility.

Homogeneous Systems

Scaling can erase constants. Normalize the certificate before comparing signs.

Do not swap alternative forms without checking signs and closureStrict feasibility requires margin language
Use this slide to slow down around inequality signs. Weak systems interact with closed cones and boundary certificates. Strict systems require positive margin or a different alternative. Homogeneous systems add scaling conventions. The lesson is practical: the certificate must match the exact system being claimed.
Slack Variables
convert inequalities

Slack Variables Convert Inequalities Into Equalities Plus Nonnegativity

Slack Form

\(Ax\le b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Ax+s=b,\ s\ge0.\)

Why It Helps

Farkas-style alternatives often become cone-membership statements once slacks turn inequalities into nonnegative variables.

Inspection Target

At a feasible point, positive slack means inactive, and zero slack means active.

Slack signs record activityMany alternatives become cone statements after adding slacks
Present slack variables as translation, not as bookkeeping clutter. They turn row inequalities into an equality with nonnegative variables, exactly the setting where cone alternatives apply. The value of a slack variable also records activity, so the same device connects feasibility algebra with face geometry.
Executable Invariant
sign residuals

The Notebook Check Audits The Certificate Signs

Certificate Residuals

\(r_A=\min_i\langle a_i,y\rangle,\quad r_b=\langle b,y\rangle.\)

Pass Condition

The generator residual satisfies \(r_A\ge0\) and the target residual satisfies \(r_b<0\).

Fail Condition

If a generator has negative pairing, the proposed separator also rejects part of the cone, so it is not a valid certificate.

Source notebook invariant: nonnegative on generators, negative on targetResiduals expose invalid certificates immediately
Tie this slide directly to the notebook invariant. A Farkas-style certificate is a sign audit. Every generator must pair nonnegatively with the normal, while the rejected target pairs negatively. The minimum generator pairing and the target pairing are compact residuals that reveal whether the proposed certificate actually proves the alternative.
Learner Lab
move target or cone

Lab: Move The Target And Watch The Alternative Flip

Experiment A

Move the target into the cone and compute nonnegative generator coefficients.

Experiment B

Move the target outside the cone and compute a separating normal with the correct signs.

Experiment C

Add a generator that crosses the separating line. Watch the old certificate fail its generator residual.

A useful report states which side of the alternative holds, gives the certificate vector, and lists the sign residuals.

Change one ingredient at a timeExactly one side of the alternative should survive
Use the lab to make alternatives feel testable. When the target moves inside the cone, the certificate should be nonnegative coefficients. When it moves outside, the certificate should be a separating normal. Adding a generator is a good stress test because it can invalidate a separator that previously worked.

Linear Inequalities Are Certificate Problems
