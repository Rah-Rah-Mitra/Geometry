Caratheodory's Theorem

A point in a finite-dimensional convex hull never needs every point in the set. It can be certified by at most dimension plus one witnesses and nonnegative coefficients summing to one.

many points
affine dependence
dimension + 1
simplex witness

Lecture Goal

Turn the theorem into an inspectable reduction algorithm: start with too many points, find an affine dependence, lower one coefficient to zero, and repeat.

Certificate

The final witness is a small convex combination whose coefficients are all nonnegative and sum exactly to one.

Dimension Count

The number of required points is controlled by affine dimension, not by the number of sampled points.

Theorem
small convex representation

The Convex Hull Has Small Witnesses

Caratheodory

If \(x\in \operatorname{conv} S\subset \mathbb R^d\), then \(x\) is a convex combination of at most \(d+1\) points of \(S\).

Affine Version

Replace \(d\) by the affine dimension of the set. A flat object in a high-dimensional ambient space uses the lower count.

Certificate Form

\(x=\sum_{i=0}^m \lambda_i s_i,\quad \lambda_i\ge 0,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i=1,\quad m\le d.\)

The theorem is a certificate compression resultAmbient dimension can overestimate the true affine count
State the theorem in the coefficient language because that is the form students will inspect in computations. Stress the affine-dimension refinement immediately. If a set lies in a plane inside a much larger space, the representation count follows the plane, not the surrounding coordinate system.
Dimension Count
affine span

The Count Comes From Affine Dimension

Affine Dimension

\(\dim\operatorname{aff} S=\dim\operatorname{span}\{s-s_0:s\in S\}\).

Interpretation

Choose one anchor point, subtract it, and count independent directions. That count controls the theorem.

Why It Matters

More ambient coordinates do not create more independent directions if the data lie on a lower-dimensional flat.

Anchor, subtract, count directionsAffine dimension is the correct theorem input
Make the dimension count tactile. Pick an anchor point and draw the difference vectors. The theorem is controlled by how many independent directions these differences span. This is why a planar polygon embedded in three-dimensional space still needs at most three points, not four, to represent a point in its convex hull.
Proof Mechanism
remove one point

The Proof Is A Coefficient Reduction Move

Affine Dependence

\(\sum_i \alpha_i s_i=0,\quad \sum_i\alpha_i=0,\quad \alpha\ne 0.\)

Coefficient Update

\(\lambda_i(t)=\lambda_i-t\alpha_i\), choose \(t\) until one positive coefficient becomes zero.

Invariant

The represented point and the sum of coefficients stay unchanged along the affine-dependence direction.

Stopping Rule

Choose the largest allowed step that keeps every coefficient nonnegative. At least one point leaves the representation.

Affine dependence exists whenever there are too many witnessesThe update preserves the same convex combination
Explain the proof as an algorithm rather than a contradiction. If more than dimension plus one points appear, their differences are affinely dependent. Moving the coefficient vector along that dependence preserves the represented point and the total weight, while the step size is chosen so that one coefficient reaches zero without making any coefficient negative.
Planar Example
three witnesses

A Planar Hull Needs Only A Triangle Witness

Starting Data

Many sampled points define a polygonal convex hull. A chosen interior point may initially be written using many positive weights.

Compressed Witness

Caratheodory reduces that representation to three vertices because the affine dimension is two.

Barycentric Form

\(x=\lambda_1a+\lambda_2b+\lambda_3c,\quad \lambda_i\ge 0,\quad \lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1.\)

The witness triangle can be smaller than the full hullThe coefficients are the visible certificate
Use the planar picture to match the notebook target. The full hull may involve many sample points, but the representation of one point can be certified by a single triangle. This does not mean the triangle equals the hull; it only needs to contain the particular point being represented.
Coefficient Certificate
nonnegative and sums to one

Barycentric Coefficients Are The Audit Trail

Matrix Check

\([a\ b\ c]\lambda=x,\quad \mathbf 1^T\lambda=1,\quad \lambda\ge 0.\)

Inside The Simplex

All coefficients are nonnegative. A zero coefficient means the point lies on a face and fewer witnesses suffice.

Outside The Simplex

A negative coefficient is not a convex certificate. It signals that the chosen simplex does not contain the point.

Coefficients are the certificate, not an afterthoughtResiduals check equality and normalization
Focus on the coefficients as the object to audit. A visual triangle is persuasive, but the certificate is the vector lambda. It must reconstruct the point, sum to one, and have no negative entries. Zero entries should be celebrated because they reveal a lower-dimensional face representation.
Conic Variant
dimension witnesses

Conic Representations Drop The Sum-To-One Constraint

Cone Form

If \(x\in\operatorname{cone} S\subset\mathbb R^d\), then \(x\) is a nonnegative combination of at most \(d\) points of \(S\).

Difference From Convex Hulls

Convex combinations have a normalization equation. Conic combinations only require nonnegative coefficients.

Same Reduction Logic

Linear dependence among too many rays lets one coefficient be driven to zero while preserving the vector.

Convex: d+1 pointsConic: d rays under the usual finite-dimensional count
Introduce the conic form as the sibling theorem. Removing the normalization equation changes the count and the algebra, but the proof instinct is the same: too many rays create a linear dependence, and moving along that dependence preserves the represented vector while eliminating a redundant coefficient.
Simplex Support
minimal active set

The Final Witness Lives In A Simplex

Simplex Support

The active witnesses form a simplex when they are affinely independent. The coefficients are then unique within that simplex.

Faces Are Cheaper

If the point lies on a face, one or more coefficients vanish and the active set can be smaller than the maximum bound.

Minimality Signal

\(\lambda_i>0\) for all active vertices means the point is in the relative interior of that witness simplex.

Teaching Cue

Ask whether a displayed point really needs the full triangle, or whether a line segment already certifies it.

The theorem gives an upper bound, not a promise of minimalityZero coefficients reveal smaller supports
Clarify that the theorem gives an upper bound on witness size. A point on an edge in the plane needs only two vertices, even though the theorem allows three. Once students watch zero coefficients identify faces, they can read the geometry and the algebra as the same certificate.
Algorithm
compress the support

Reduction Algorithm For A Convex Combination

1. Start

List active points with positive coefficients.

2. Test

If the active count is at most dimension plus one, stop.

3. Depend

Find a nonzero affine dependence among active points.

4. Step

Move coefficients until one active weight becomes zero.

The represented point never moves. Only the witness list becomes shorter.

The algorithm is the constructive proofEvery step preserves equality and normalization
Convert the proof into a checklist students can execute by hand or in code. The important invariants are simple: the represented point is unchanged, coefficient sum remains one, and nonnegativity is preserved by the chosen step. Repeating the move must eventually stop because at least one active coefficient disappears each time.
Boundary Cases
lower counts appear naturally

Boundary Points Usually Need Fewer Witnesses

Interior

A point in the relative interior of a full simplex usually uses all vertices of that simplex.

Edge

A point on an edge has one zero barycentric coefficient, so two vertices are enough.

Vertex

A vertex is already represented by one point with coefficient one.

Maximum witness count is not always achievedFaces are detected by zero coefficients
Use boundary cases to prevent overreading the theorem. Caratheodory gives a maximum number of witnesses, but the geometry may be cheaper. Interior, edge, and vertex positions correspond exactly to three, two, and one positive coefficient in a planar simplex, so the coefficient vector reveals the face structure.
Executable Invariant
residual and weights

The Notebook Check Audits The Reduced Witness

Residuals

\(r_x=\left\|x-\sum_i\lambda_i s_i\right\|,\quad r_1=\left|\sum_i\lambda_i-1\right|.\)

Pass Conditions

The reconstruction residual is small, the normalization residual is small, and every retained coefficient is nonnegative within tolerance.

Fail Conditions

A negative coefficient, a large residual, or a support larger than dimension plus one means the displayed certificate has not been compressed correctly.

Source notebook invariant: reduced coefficients are nonnegative and sum to oneResiduals make the theorem inspectable
Tie this slide directly to the notebook checks. A Caratheodory certificate is easy to audit because it has three visible tests: equality, normalization, and nonnegativity. The computation is not the proof, but it disciplines the diagram by requiring the displayed small witness to reconstruct the point numerically.
