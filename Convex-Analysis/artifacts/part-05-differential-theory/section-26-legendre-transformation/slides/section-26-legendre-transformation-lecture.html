The Legendre Transformation

Smooth conjugacy becomes a change of coordinates: replace a primal point \(x\) by its slope \(p=\nabla f(x)\), then read the conjugate value from the tangent line.

Slope-Coordinate Definition

For a differentiable convex function on an open convex set \(C\), set \(D=\nabla f(C)\) and

\[g(p)=\langle x,p\rangle-f(x),\quad p=\nabla f(x).\]

Lecture Route

  • Use tangent intercepts to compute \(f^*(p)\).
  • Turn \(\nabla f\) and \(\nabla f^*\) into inverse coordinates.
  • Test the reciprocity on quadratics, entropy, barriers, and Hessians.

Open by naming the central move: a point x on a smooth convex graph is replaced by its slope p. The Legendre transform is not a new curve drawn from nowhere; it is the same supporting-tangent information written in slope coordinates. Keep the tangent line visible because it carries both the value of the conjugate and the equality case in Fenchel's inequality.
Change The Coordinate, Not The Certificate
x -> p

Slope Coordinates

Primary Coordinate Map

\[x\in C=\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom} f)\quad\longmapsto\quad p=\nabla f(x).\]

The slope labels the supporting tangent to the graph at \(x\).

Why Convexity Matters

  • Monotonicity makes slopes ordered rather than arbitrary.
  • Strict convexity removes flat segments with repeated slopes.
  • Essential smoothness keeps boundary slopes from behaving like missing coordinates.

In this section, the subdifferential map \(\partial f\) becomes the gradient map exactly when the function is essentially smooth.

Key idea: the dual variable is a slope coordinate.Section 26
The first technical habit is to stop treating p as a mysterious dual variable. For a differentiable convex function, p is the gradient at x. When strict convexity removes flat segments, the slope changes injectively with x; when essential smoothness controls the boundary, the slope map behaves like a coordinate chart rather than a partial label.
Tangent Encodes The Conjugate
Fenchel equality

Tangent Line Intercept

At A Matched Slope

If \(p=\nabla f(x)\), then the tangent line is

\[t(z)=f(x)+\langle p,z-x\rangle=\langle p,z\rangle-f^*(p).\]

Intercept Ledger

\[f^*(p)=\langle p,x\rangle-f(x).\]

The intercept of the supporting affine function is \(-f^*(p)\).

Equality Certificate

\[f(x)+f^*(p)-\langle p,x\rangle=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\in\partial f(x).\]

Tangent support turns a supremum into an attained value.Legendre tangent map
Use the tangent line as the visual definition. At a paired point, the tangent has equation t(z)=p z minus f star of p. Rearranging the tangent formula gives f star of p equals p x minus f of x. This is exactly the affine gap that the conjugate maximizes, but here it is attained by the slope-matched point.
Graph Transformation
x-parametrized dual graph

Conjugate As Transformed Graph

Parametric Recipe

  1. Choose \(x\in C\).
  2. Compute \(p=\nabla f(x)\).
  3. Place the dual point \((p,\langle p,x\rangle-f(x))\).
Ordinary Conjugate Agreement

For a closed proper convex \(f\) differentiable on \(C\), the Legendre conjugate is the restriction of \(f^*\) to the gradient image \(D=\nabla f(C)\).

When the inverse \((\nabla f)^{-1}\) is hard to write, \(x\mapsto(\nabla f(x), f^*(\nabla f(x)))\) still parameterizes the dual graph.

Transformed graph: \((x,f(x))\) becomes \((p,f^*(p))\).Section 26.4
This slide turns the graph into data. Starting with x, compute p as the gradient and then compute the intercept ledger value f star of p. The point on the dual graph is therefore produced by a parametrized transformation from the primal graph. Fenchel equality is the checksum that the pair has not drifted apart.
Essential Smoothness
single-valued subdifferential

Gradient Map

Section 26.1, Operational Form

For closed proper convex \(f\), the mapping \(\partial f\) is single-valued exactly when \(f\) is essentially smooth.

Then \(\partial f\) Reduces To

\[ \partial f(x)= \begin{cases} \{\nabla f(x)\}, & x\in \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom} f),\\ \varnothing, & x\notin \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom} f). \end{cases} \]

Boundary Test

As \(x_i\) approaches a boundary point from inside, \(\|\nabla f(x_i)\|\to\infty\). Boundary subgradients disappear.

Essential smoothness removes boundary subgradients.Theorem 26.1
Connect the picture to the source-span theorem language. A closed proper convex function has a single-valued subdifferential exactly in the essentially smooth case. On the interior of the effective domain that single value is the gradient. The gradient map is therefore the ordinary-function version of the subdifferential map.
Legendre Type Symmetry
grad f* = (grad f)^(-1)

Inverse Gradient

Legendre Type

When \(f\) and \(f^*\) have the matching smooth and strict-convex behavior,

\[\nabla f:C\to C^*,\qquad \nabla f^*:C^*\to C\]

are continuous inverse maps.

Round Trip

\[\nabla f^*(\nabla f(x))=x,\qquad \nabla f(\nabla f^*(p))=p.\]

This is the smooth-conjugacy version of \(\partial f^*=(\partial f)^{-1}\).

The transform is involutory on the correct open domains.Theorem 26.5
Now add the symmetry. If both sides have the right smooth and strict-convex behavior, the conjugate gradient sends p back to the original x. Students should say this aloud as a coordinate statement: primal position maps to slope, and slope maps back to primal position. The conjugate is smooth because the inverse map is well behaved on its natural domain.
Calibration Example
curvature reciprocal

Quadratic Example

Take \(f(x)=x^2\)

\[ p=f'(x)=2x,\qquad x=\frac{p}{2},\qquad f^*(p)=px-f(x)=\frac{p^2}{4}. \]

Fenchel Check

\[x^2+\frac{p^2}{4}-px=\left(x-\frac p2\right)^2\ge 0.\]

Equality Slope

Equality holds exactly when \(p=2x\), so the tangent and the conjugate ledger agree.

Entropy And Barrier Examples

Entropy \(x\log x\)

\[ f(x)=x\log x,\quad x>0 \]

\[ p=\log x+1,\quad x=e^{p-1} \]

\[ f^*(p)=e^{p-1},\quad p\in\mathbb R. \]

Log Barrier \(-\log x\)

\[ f(x)=-\log x,\quad x>0 \]

\[ p=-1/x,\quad x=-1/p \]

\[ f^*(p)=-1-\log(-p),\quad p<0. \]

What Changes?

  • The same Legendre recipe works on the interior.
  • The gradient image \(D\) differs: all real slopes for entropy, negative slopes for the barrier.
  • The ordinary conjugate extends by \(+\infty\) off its domain.
Boundary blow-up keeps the open-domain chart honest.Examples for slope ranges
Use these one-dimensional examples to show why domains matter. Entropy sends positive x to every real slope, while the log barrier sends positive x only to negative slopes. Both are smooth inside their domains and both have gradients that blow up at the boundary, but their slope ranges lead to different conjugate domains.

Hessian Reciprocity

Differentiate The Round Trip

If \(p=\nabla f(x)\) and \(x=\nabla f^*(p)\), then

\[\nabla^2 f^*(p)=\big(\nabla^2 f(x)\big)^{-1}.\]

One Dimension

\[f''(x)\,(f^*)''(p)=1.\]

A steep primal graph produces a shallow conjugate graph at the paired slope.

Where The Transform Lives
D = grad f(C)

Domain And Range Caveats

Do Not Assume Full Space

The Legendre conjugate first lives on \(D=\nabla f(C)\). The ordinary conjugate \(f^*\) may be larger: it includes boundary values and \(+\infty\) outside its effective domain.

Almost Convex Range

For essentially smooth \(f\), the gradient range satisfies

\[\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom} f^*)\subset D\subset \operatorname{dom} f^*.\]

Without the hypotheses, the gradient image can fail to be the clean open convex coordinate domain that a classical Legendre transform expects.

Legendre chart first, closed conjugate second.Corollary 26.4.1
Pause on the warning because it prevents overgeneralization. The Legendre conjugate first lives on the image of the gradient map, not automatically on all of dual space. Without the boundary blow-up and strictness hypotheses, the image may fail to be an open convex coordinate domain, so the ordinary conjugate needs a closure-and-extension step.

Rule Ledger

Single-Valued

\(\partial f\) is single-valued exactly when \(f\) is essentially smooth.

Inside the domain this means \(\partial f(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\}\).

Dual Smoothness

\(f\) is essentially strictly convex exactly when \(f^*\) is essentially smooth.

Strictness on the primal side becomes differentiability on the dual side.

Legendre Type

If both interiors satisfy the hypotheses, \(\nabla f\) and \(\nabla f^*\) are inverse homeomorphisms.

The transform is symmetric between \((C,f)\) and \((C^*,f^*)\).

Finite Full-Space Case

For differentiable finite convex \(f\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), \(\nabla f\) maps onto all of \(\mathbb R^n\) exactly under strict convexity plus co-finiteness.

Fenchel Equality

\[f(x)+f^*(p)=\langle p,x\rangle\]

is the paired-coordinate certificate.

Hessian Rule

\[\nabla^2 f^*(p)=\big(\nabla^2 f(x)\big)^{-1}\]

at \(p=\nabla f(x)\), when second derivatives exist and are invertible.

The rules are about when slope coordinates are legitimate coordinates.Sections 26.1-26.6
This ledger is a memory aid for the section. Essential smoothness makes the subdifferential single-valued, essential strict convexity is dual to essential smoothness, Legendre type makes the transform symmetric between interiors, and strict plus co-finite finite functions give the full-space version where the classical transformation and Fenchel conjugacy coincide.
After The Interior Chart

Boundary And Closure

Interior Formula Is Not The Whole Story

Inverting \(p=\nabla f(x)\) gives \(f^*(p)\) on the gradient range. Boundary values of the closed conjugate may require limits.

Closure Principle

Once \(g=f^*|_D\) is known on the slope range \(D\), closed convexity determines boundary values by limiting along line segments from the relative interior.

Outside The Range

Off the closure of the effective slope domain, the ordinary conjugate takes value \(+\infty\).

The Legendre chart is local to the smooth interior; conjugacy is closed and global.Closure caveat
After computing the Legendre conjugate on the slope range, the ordinary conjugate may still require boundary values and infinity outside the closure of the range. This is a source of many wrong answers: a formula obtained by inverting the gradient is only the interior chart unless the hypotheses guarantee that the chart already covers the full relevant domain.
Recap
slope-coordinate conjugacy

Legendre Transformation: The Loop

Compute

\[x\ \xrightarrow{\nabla f}\ p,\qquad f^*(p)=\langle p,x\rangle-f(x).\]

Verify

\[f(x)+f^*(p)-\langle p,x\rangle=0.\]

Zero Fenchel gap confirms the tangent support certificate.

Invert

\[p\ \xrightarrow{\nabla f^*}\ x,\qquad \nabla^2 f^*(p)=\big(\nabla^2 f(x)\big)^{-1}.\]

Goal met: Legendre transformation as smooth conjugacy through slope coordinates.End Section 26
Close by asking students to narrate the whole loop. Start from x, compute p by the gradient, write the tangent line, record f star of p, verify Fenchel equality, invert by grad f star, and compare Hessians. That loop is the Legendre transformation as smooth conjugacy through slope coordinates.
