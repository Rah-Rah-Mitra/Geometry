Gradient Map Picture

Smooth Convex Case

\(p(x)=\nabla f(x)\)

If \(f\) is convex and differentiable, the gradient map is monotone.

What The Picture Tests

  • Choose two points \(x_0,x_1\).
  • Draw \(p_0=\nabla f(x_0)\) and \(p_1=\nabla f(x_1)\).
  • Check whether \(x_1-x_0\) points against \(p_1-p_0\).

Positive Curvature Signal

For a quadratic \(f(x)=\frac12 x^T A x\), the check becomes \(d^T A d \ge 0\), the visible trace of positive semidefinite curvature.

The panel on the right makes the map itself visible: the difference of gradients is not a random vector, but the image of the displacement through a positive curvature field.
Two Supporting Lines
pairwise graph test

Monotone Inequality

Subgradient Monotonicity

If \(p\in\partial f(x)\) and \(q\in\partial f(y)\), then

\[\langle x-y,\ p-q\rangle \ge 0.\]

Proof In One Move

From the supporting inequalities, \(f(y)\ge f(x)+\langle p,y-x\rangle\) and \(f(x)\ge f(y)+\langle q,x-y\rangle\). Add them and cancel \(f(x),f(y)\).

Graph Reading

Two graph points \((x,p)\) and \((y,q)\) cannot reverse direction in the domain and range at the same time.

Pairwise monotonicity is the m=1 shadow of cyclic monotonicityNo differentiability hypothesis is used
Derive the inequality by writing the two supporting inequalities and adding them. The cancellation is the whole proof. Students should notice that no differentiability is needed: the same line works for any selected subgradients p and q, so monotonicity belongs to the set-valued graph rather than to a smooth formula.
One-Dimensional Warmup
left and right derivatives

Secant Slopes

Theorem 24.1 Thread

\(f'_-(x)\le f'_+(x)\), and both one-sided slope functions are nondecreasing.

Secant Bracketing

For \(u<x<v\), convexity places the local slopes between the left and right secant slopes. Moving the window right cannot lower the slope.

Subgradient Interval

In one dimension, \(\partial f(x)\) is the interval of slopes between the left and right derivative values.

Endpoint Convention

At domain ends, infinite one-sided values keep the graph complete rather than pretending the boundary has ordinary differentiability.

Asset: subgradient-graph.svgOne-dimensional slope monotonicity motivates the full graph theorem
In one dimension, monotonicity can be seen through secant slopes. As the secant window moves to the right, convexity prevents the slope from decreasing. The left and right derivatives are therefore monotone functions, and the jump between them is exactly where the subdifferential becomes an interval instead of a point.

One-Dimensional Maximality

Maximal Monotone Intuition

Totally Ordered Graph

For \(n=1\), monotone means any two graph points are ordered coordinatewise.

Complete Nondecreasing Curve

A maximal ordered graph must include every vertical interval at a jump; otherwise a missing ordered point could be inserted.

Why Closedness Matters

Limit points of graph samples are not optional. Theorem 24.4 turns this visual requirement into a closed graph theorem in \(R^n\times R^n\).

Higher-Dimensional Warning

In dimensions above one, pairwise monotonicity is weaker than the cyclic condition needed to be a subgradient map.

One-dimensional maximal monotone = complete nondecreasing curveGeneral maximal monotone is subtler and reappears later
The one-dimensional theorem says that subgradient graphs are complete nondecreasing curves. A missing vertical interval would still look monotone locally, but it would fail completeness because another ordered point could be inserted. This is the first geometric hint of maximality, before the higher-dimensional cyclic statement arrives.
Closed Graph Plus Singleton Values
smooth region

Continuity On Smooth Regions

Teaching Corollary

If \(f\) is convex and differentiable on an open region, then \(\nabla f\) varies continuously there.

Closed Graph

Limits of \((x_i,p_i)\) stay in \(\operatorname{graph}\partial f\).

Local Bound

Compact interior windows keep subgradients bounded.

Singleton

At smooth \(x\), \(\partial f(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\}\).

Consequence

Every convergent subsequence of \(\nabla f(x_i)\) has the same limit, so the whole gradient sequence converges.

Limit Of The Claim

At a nonsmooth point, the target is a set. The right continuity language is closed graph and outer containment, not ordinary vector continuity.

Uses Theorems 24.4, 24.5.1, and 24.7 as a proof routeContinuity is recovered when the subgradient set collapses to one vector
Explain continuity of the gradient as a consequence of three ingredients: the graph of the subdifferential is closed, subgradients are locally bounded on compact sets inside the domain, and at a smooth point the subdifferential has only one vector. Any convergent subsequence of gradients must land on that one vector, so the whole gradient sequence converges.
Nonsmooth Point
vertical interval

Jumps At Corners

Corner Model

For \(f(x)=|x|\), \(\partial f(0)=[-1,1]\).

What Discontinues

The ordinary derivative jumps from \(-1\) to \(+1\). There is no single vector value that makes the gradient continuous through the corner.

What Remains Continuous

The set-valued graph is closed: approaching from either side lands inside the interval \([-1,1]\).

Graphical Rule

Do not leave the jump hollow. The vertical segment is exactly the family of supporting slopes at the nonsmooth point.

Asset: corner-jump.svgJumps are handled by filling the subgradient set
The absolute value example prevents students from overgeneralizing smooth behavior. The derivative jumps from minus one to plus one, but the subdifferential at the corner is the whole interval between them. The map is not continuous as a single-valued gradient, yet it is closed and outer-continuous as a set-valued graph.
From Pairs To Loops
potential test

Cyclic Monotonicity Preview

Deck Sign Convention

For graph pairs \((x_i,p_i)\), set \(x_{m+1}=x_0\). A convex subgradient cycle satisfies

\[\sum_i\langle x_i-x_{i+1},p_i\rangle\ge 0.\]

Why It Is Stronger

Pairwise monotonicity sees two points at a time. Cyclic monotonicity tests whether all arrows can come from one convex potential.

Section 24 Characterization

A multivalued map is contained in a subdifferential exactly when it is cyclically monotone; maximal cyclically monotone maps are subdifferential maps.

Counterexample To Remember

The rotation \(P(x,y)=(-y,x)\) has pairwise residual \(d\cdot Pd=0\), but its loop residual in the check file is \(-4\).

Check file: assets/cyclic-monotonicity-check.json

Cycle residuals detect whether arrows are potential-generatedTheorems 24.8 and 24.9
Pairwise monotonicity checks every two-point comparison. Cyclic monotonicity asks for consistency around every finite loop, which is the correct test for being generated by a convex potential. The rotation example is useful because it passes pairwise monotonicity with equality but still creates negative work around a loop.

Matrix Test

\(P(x)=Qx\) is monotone iff \(S=(Q+Q^T)/2\) is positive semidefinite.

It is cyclically monotone iff \(Q\) is symmetric positive semidefinite.

Skew parts may pass pairwise tests but fail cyclesFor gradients, the matrix must be symmetric PSD
For a linear map, the pairwise test collapses to a matrix test on the symmetric part. A skew component disappears from dot(d,Qd), so it does not harm monotonicity. But cyclic monotonicity is stricter: a linear subgradient map must be symmetric positive semidefinite, matching the Hessian of a convex quadratic.

Failure Examples

Not Closed

A convex function with a missing boundary value can break the one-sided derivative limit formulas. The closed proper hypothesis prevents this boundary leak.

right limit can miss \(f'_+(x)\)

Hollow Jump

If a corner graph omits part of its vertical interval, it may look ordered but fails closedness and fails the complete-curve characterization.

missing \(p\in[f'_-,f'_+]\)

Rotation Field

The map \(P(x,y)=(-y,x)\) is monotone because \(d\cdot Pd=0\), but it is not cyclically monotone and cannot be a convex subgradient map.

cycle residual \(=-4\)

Section 24 Message

Each failure is a missing condition: closedness, complete vertical data, or cyclic consistency. Pairwise monotonicity alone is not enough to recover a convex potential in higher dimensions.

Failure examples are synthetic and source-alignedUse them to locate the missing theorem hypothesis
Collect the traps in one place. A nonclosed convex function can lose derivative limit behavior at the boundary. A graph with a missing jump interval fails closedness and maximality. A rotation field can be monotone without being cyclically monotone. Each failure marks a hypothesis that Section 24 deliberately keeps visible.

Graphical Checks

Increasing Order

Pick two graph points. Domain movement and slope movement should have nonnegative pairing.

Filled Jumps

At a corner, the vertical interval of supporting slopes must be present, not implied.

Closed Limits

Sequences of plotted graph points should not converge to a missing point.

Compact Bounds

On an interior window, displayed slopes should stay inside a finite range.

Cycle Residuals

Finite loops should have nonnegative residual under the chosen sign convention.

Matrix Split

For linear maps, inspect the symmetric part before interpreting arrows as gradients.

Graphical checks connect the visuals to executable JSON assetsAssets: corner-jump.svg, cyclic-monotonicity-check.json
Move from theorem statements to inspection habits. A good graph should reveal increasing order, filled vertical jumps, bounded slope range on interior windows, and no negative cycle residual. When one of those features is absent, the visual should tell students which theorem or qualification has stopped applying.
Recap

Recap

Monotone Graphs

Subgradient maps obey \(\langle x-y,p-q\rangle\ge0\). In one dimension, complete nondecreasing curves are exactly subgradient graphs.

Continuity Logic

The graph is closed, subgradients are bounded on compact interior sets, and smooth points turn set-valued convergence into gradient continuity.

Cyclic Potential Test

Cyclic monotonicity is the finite-loop condition that characterizes maps contained in, or maximal as, convex subdifferentials.

Takeaway

A subgradient graph is a generalized derivative object because every visual feature is backed by an inequality: support inequalities, monotone pairings, closed graph limits, compact slope bounds, and cyclic loop residuals.
