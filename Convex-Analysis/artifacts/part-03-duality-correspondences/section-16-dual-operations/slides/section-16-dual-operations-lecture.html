Dual Operations

Duality turns one operation into another: sums become infimal convolutions, linear images become adjoint pulls, and closure records the hypotheses that kept the geometry honest.

operation
conjugate
dual operation
closure

Lecture Goal

Read dual operations as geometry on epigraphs: combine functions in primal coordinates, then inspect which dual certificates survive.

Core Model

For convex functions \(f\) and \(g\), the conjugate of \(f+g\) is governed by splitting the dual vector into two witness slopes.

Main Caution

The elegant algebra is often exact only after a closure step or a regularity condition. Missing boundary points are not a cosmetic issue.

Primal / Dual Dictionary

Primal Operations Acquire Dual Names

Conjugacy Lens

\(f^*(y)=\sup_x\{\langle x,y\rangle-f(x)\}\).

Dual Operation

An operation is dualized by applying it to the object, taking the conjugate or polar, and rewriting the result as an operation on the dual objects.

Reading Rule

Every dual formula is a statement about which affine certificates can support the transformed epigraph.

Conjugacy translates geometry into inequalitiesOperation pairs are certificates seen from opposite sides
Build the dictionary slowly. The left column describes operations on primal variables or primal epigraphs; the right column describes how the supporting slopes must be arranged after conjugacy. Emphasize that a dual formula is not a mnemonic table but a feasibility statement for affine lower bounds.
Core Identity
split the slope

The Conjugate Of A Sum Splits A Certificate

Expected Dual Form

\((f+g)^*(y)=\operatorname{cl}\inf_{u+v=y}\{f^*(u)+g^*(v)\}\).

Geometric Meaning

A supporting slope \(y\) for \(f+g\) can be assembled from a slope \(u\) supporting \(f\) and a slope \(v\) supporting \(g\).

Closure Reminder

The raw infimum may approach the right value along a limiting sequence without attaining it at the boundary.

The operation is infimal convolution of conjugatesClosure distinguishes limit value from attained value
Present the sum rule as a certificate splitting problem. To support f plus g with slope y, imagine assigning part of that slope to f and the remainder to g. The dual side minimizes over all such splits, and the closure term warns that the best split may only appear as a limit.
Infimal Convolution
split the point

Infimal Convolution Splits A Primal Point

Definition

\((f\square g)(x)=\inf_z\{f(z)+g(x-z)\}\).

Dual Pairing

\((f\square g)^* = f^*+g^*\) under the standard properness and closure hypotheses.

Operational Reading

A point \(x\) is assembled from two primal pieces. The optimizer chooses the split that makes the total cost smallest.

Domain Check

If no feasible split has finite cost, the infimal convolution is infinite at that point.

Sum and infimal convolution are dual operation partnersPoint splitting mirrors slope splitting
Now reverse the earlier point of view. Infimal convolution splits the primal point instead of the dual slope. It is the natural partner to addition because conjugacy swaps the role of choosing an x in a supremum with choosing compatible pieces whose sum or difference gives the displayed variable.
Linear Operations
adjoint bookkeeping

Linear Images Become Adjoint Constraints

Pullback Form

\(h(x)=f(Ax)\).

Dual Constraint

\(h^*(y)=\inf\{f^*(u):A^Tu=y\}\), with closure when the range boundary is active.

Why The Adjoint Appears

The pairing obeys \(\langle Ax,u\rangle=\langle x,A^Tu\rangle\), so dual slopes must pass through \(A^T\).

Linear maps push primal variables forwardAdjoints pull dual certificates back
Use this slide to connect dual operations with linear algebra students already know. The whole formula is forced by the pairing identity. A slope u on the target space becomes a slope A transpose u on the source space, and the infimum records all target slopes that pull back to the requested source slope.
Set Function Pairs
support and gauge

Support And Gauge Operations Keep The Same Logic

Support Addition

\(\sigma_{C+D}(y)=\sigma_C(y)+\sigma_D(y)\).

Gauge Intersections

\(\gamma_{C\cap D}(x)=\max\{\gamma_C(x),\gamma_D(x)\}\) for absorbing convex sets.

Same Pattern

Set operations are reflected by functional operations because gauges and supports encode feasible dilations and valid linear bounds.

Inspection Target

Ask which operation combines feasible points, and which operation combines all inequalities that certify the set.

Support functions convert Minkowski addition into algebraic additionGauges convert intersections into maxima
Keep the support and gauge cases connected to the previous formulas. They are not separate tricks; they are the same certificate translation applied to functions that encode sets. Support functions remember all linear inequalities of a set, while gauges remember how much scaling is needed to make a point feasible.
Monotonicity
larger primal, smaller dual

Duality Reverses The Order

Conjugate Order

\(f\le g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g^*\le f^*\).

Reason

Subtracting a larger primal function leaves a smaller supremum, so the conjugate inequality points the other way.

Operation Consequence

When a primal operation relaxes a feasible set or decreases cost, the dual certificate set tightens in the opposite direction.

Order reversal is a diagnostic for dual formulasWrong inequality direction usually signals a mistaken operation
Treat order reversal as a quick error detector. If the primal function is raised, every affine payoff loses value, so the conjugate must go down. Many mistakes in dual operation algebra reveal themselves immediately because they preserve the inequality direction when conjugacy should have reversed it.
Regularity
where equalities live

Closure Qualifications Decide Whether The Formula Is Exact

Generic Shape

\(\text{dual operation}=\operatorname{cl}(\text{formal operation})\).

Boundary Failure

A minimizing split may drift to the edge of the domain. The formal operation has the right limiting value but not the closed epigraph.

Qualification Idea

Relative interior overlap, continuity at a shared point, or a suitable range condition often removes the closure term.

Regularity is geometry, not administrative detailMissing limits change the epigraph
Pause here because closure terms are easy to rush past. The point is not that authors enjoy adding technical qualifiers. The closed epigraph is the object that conjugacy naturally sees. If a formal operation misses a boundary limit, a clean algebraic expression must be closed before it matches the dual object.
Finite-Dimensional Check
quadratic lab

A Quadratic Example Makes The Operation Visible

Two Quadratics

\(f(x)=\frac12 x^TQx,\quad g(x)=\frac12 x^TRx\).

Conjugates

\(f^*(y)=\frac12 y^TQ^{-1}y,\quad (f+g)^*(y)=\frac12 y^T(Q+R)^{-1}y.\)

Split Problem

The infimal convolution of \(f^*\) and \(g^*\) asks for the best \(u+v=y\). The optimizer balances the two inverse quadratic costs.

Positive definite quadratics avoid closure surprisesThe split can be checked by linear equations
Use quadratics as the clean computational laboratory. Every conjugate is explicit, the split problem is a small least squares calculation, and there is no domain boundary hiding the minimizer. This example helps students see why the infimal convolution formula is plausible before confronting singular or boundary-sensitive cases.
Epigraph Geometry
projection after intersection

Dual Operations Are Epigraph Constructions

Intersection View

Adding functions intersects vertical constraints at the same primal point. Infimal convolution projects a product epigraph along a split-variable map.

Projection View

Projection of a closed convex set need not preserve every boundary value unless the right closedness condition is present.

Visual Rule

\(\operatorname{epi}(f\square g)\) is the closure of a projected feasible-cost set.

Practical Check

Whenever the operation uses a projection or an infimum, inspect whether the projected epigraph remains closed.

Epigraph projections explain why infima and closures appear togetherConjugacy reads supporting hyperplanes of the final set
Bring the class back to geometry rather than symbolic manipulation. Infimal convolution is produced by putting two epigraphs together, adding their costs, enforcing the split, and projecting away the auxiliary variable. Projection is exactly where closedness can be lost, so the closure term has a visible geometric source.
Executable Invariant
grid sanity check

A Numerical Checker Tests The Dual Operation

Grid Infimum

\(q(x_i)=\min_j\{f(z_j)+g(x_i-z_j)\}\).

Check 1

The computed value should be no larger than the two unshifted choices \(f(x_i)+g(0)\) and \(f(0)+g(x_i)\).

Check 2

Midpoint convexity should hold up to the grid error expected from sampling and interpolation.

Check 3

If the grid minimizer runs to the edge, enlarge the grid before trusting the operation.

Students should identify the chosen split, compare it with obvious feasible splits, and notice whether convexity residuals stay small or expose a failed hypothesis or too-small grid.
Failure Modes
what breaks first

Most Mistakes Are Visible Before The Formula Fails

Missing Domain Overlap

If the functions cannot be evaluated on compatible points, the proposed split has no finite witness.

Wrong Inequality Direction

Conjugacy reverses order. A dual operation that preserves every inequality direction is probably written on the wrong side.

Escaping Minimizer

The infimum may be approached by splits that drift toward infinity or toward a missing boundary face.

Nonclosed Projection

A projected epigraph can lose a limiting point even if the original product epigraph was closed.

Adjoint Mismatch

Using \(A\) where \(A^T\) belongs breaks the pairing identity and sends dual variables to the wrong space.

Hidden Normalization

Gauge, support, polar, and conjugate formulas use different constants. Mixing them changes the certificate.

Failure diagnosis should mention the violated hypothesisDiagrams identify whether the problem is domain, boundary, or pairing
Use this as a diagnostic slide. Instead of saying that a theorem simply does not apply, ask which geometric component failed. Did the domains miss each other, did the minimizing split escape, did a projection lose a boundary point, or did the linear map send a dual variable into the wrong space?

Lab: Change One Ingredient And Track The Dual Operation

Experiment A

Replace one quadratic by an absolute-value penalty. Watch the infimal convolution develop a piecewise-linear region.

Experiment B

Restrict one domain to an interval. Check whether the best split lands at the boundary and whether closure becomes visible.

Experiment C

Insert a non-square matrix \(A\). Compare the primal pullback with the adjoint constraint \(A^Tu=y\).

A good lab report names the changed parameter, the visible geometric consequence, and the residual that confirms or refutes the proposed dual operation.

Frame the lab as controlled experimentation. Students should not randomly edit every parameter at once. Recap
dual operations in one pass

Dual Operations Are Certificate Algebra

1. Translate

Take the operation to the epigraph or conjugate side.

2. Split

Name the primal point split or the dual slope split.

3. Optimize

Minimize over compatible witnesses or maximize valid payoffs.

4. Close

Add closure unless a stated qualification removes it.

The most reliable memory aid is geometric: sums share a point, infimal convolutions split a point, and conjugates split a slope.
