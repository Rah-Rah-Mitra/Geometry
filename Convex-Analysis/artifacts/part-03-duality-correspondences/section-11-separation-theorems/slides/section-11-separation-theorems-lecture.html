Certificate View

Hyperplane Certificates

Data

\(c\neq 0,\quad H=\{x:\langle c,x\rangle=\alpha\}\).

Certificate Inequality

\(\sup_{x\in C}\langle c,x\rangle \le \alpha \le \inf_{y\in D}\langle c,y\rangle.\)

Left Set

All sampled or exact support values lie at or below \(\alpha\).

Right Set

All values lie at or above \(\alpha\).

Normal

The sign of \(c\) decides which side is called left.

Certificate object: \(c,\alpha\)Weak separation allows equality at the boundary
Treat a separating hyperplane as a compact certificate rather than just a drawing. To verify it, one does not inspect every point visually; one checks the ordered support values induced by the same normal. Flipping the normal only swaps the two sides, so the mathematical content is the signed ordering.
Proper Separation
nondegenerate separator

Proper Separation

Definition

Convex sets \(C,D\) are properly separated if a separating \(c\neq 0\) exists and the two sets are not both trapped in the same equality hyperplane.

One Useful Test

\(\sup_C\langle c,x\rangle \le \inf_D\langle c,x\rangle,\quad \inf_{C\cup D}\langle c,z\rangle < \sup_{C\cup D}\langle c,z\rangle.\)

What Proper Excludes

A separator is not informative if every point of both sets already lies in the separating hyperplane.

Geometric Meaning

Proper separation may have zero gap. It still records a genuine side preference for at least one of the sets.

Duality Meaning

Properness prevents the certificate from becoming a tautology; the normal extracts a real scalar distinction.

Weakordered sides
Propernot all equality
Strongpositive gap
Proper separation is a qualitative nondegeneracy conditionRelative interiors give the sharp qualification later
Pause on the word proper because it is easy to overread it as positive margin. Proper separation still permits equality of the two support numbers. Its job is to rule out a useless certificate where both convex sets are entirely inside the same hyperplane and the normal has detected no real side information.
Margins
strict versus strong

Strong Separation With Distance

Strong Form

\(\sup_{x\in C}\langle c,x\rangle + \delta \le \inf_{y\in D}\langle c,y\rangle\quad(\delta>0).\)

Distance Route

If a nearest pair \(p\in C,\ q\in D\) exists with \(p\neq q\), then \(c=q-p\) points across the gap and the midpoint threshold separates.

Hypothesis Watch

Closedness and compactness are often what make the nearest pair exist. Without them, the infimum distance can be zero.

Strong separation = visible gap in support valuesDistance arguments turn geometry into a normal vector
Separate strict language from strong language. Strict inequalities say the points are on open opposite sides, while strong separation records a positive numerical gap in the support values. The nearest-pair argument is the most memorable construction because the vector between closest points becomes the certificate normal.
Boundary Certificates
supporting half-space

Supporting Hyperplanes

Support At \(x_0\)

\(C\subseteq\{x:\langle c,x\rangle\le \langle c,x_0\rangle\},\quad x_0\in C,\ c\neq 0.\)

Support Function View

\(h_C(c)=\sup_{x\in C}\langle c,x\rangle=\langle c,x_0\rangle.\)

Why It Is Separation

The hyperplane separates the set from any point just beyond the supporting side.

Boundary Caveat

Interior points cannot usually support the whole set; the half-space would cut through the neighborhood around the point.

Artifact: assets/support-hyperplane.svgSupporting hyperplanes are separation with one set collapsed to a boundary witness
Explain support as one-sided contact. A supporting hyperplane is allowed to touch a face, an edge, or a single exposed point, but it must contain the whole set in one closed half-space. The support-function equation is a useful bridge to the next sections on conjugacy and dual representation.
Point Separation
closed convex set

Point Outside A Closed Convex Set

Projection Separator

If \(C\) is closed and convex, \(y\notin C\), \(p=P_C(y)\), and \(c=y-p\), then

\(\langle c,x\rangle\le \langle c,p\rangle < \langle c,y\rangle\quad(x\in C).\)

Certificate Strength

The dot-scale gap is \(\langle c,y-p\rangle=\|y-p\|^2>0\), so the separation is strong.

What Closedness Buys

The nearest point \(p\) is attained, so the proof has an actual boundary witness rather than a limiting sequence.

Artifact: assets/projection-proof.svgProjection proof: nearest point condition becomes a normal inequality
This is the most concrete theorem in the lecture. Closedness gives a nearest point, convexity turns the nearest-point condition into an obtuse-angle inequality, and that inequality is exactly the separating hyperplane. The positive distance from y to C becomes the positive margin in the certificate.

Disjoint Convex Sets

Basic Pattern

Convert \(C\cap D=\emptyset\) into a statement about \(C-D\) missing the origin, then separate the origin from that convex difference set.

Difference Set

\(C-D=\{x-y:x\in C,\ y\in D\}\).

Certificate Pullback

A separator for \(0\) and \(C-D\) is the same normal \(c\) that orders \(C\) and \(D\).

Qualification
relative interiors

Relative Interior Qualification

Sharp Proper-Separation Test

\(C,D\) nonempty convex:

\(\operatorname{ri}C\cap\operatorname{ri}D=\emptyset\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C,D\text{ can be properly separated}.\)

Why Relative?

Lower-dimensional convex sets have their own interior inside their affine hull. Ordinary ambient interior would erase them.

Diagnostic

If a proposed separator cuts through a shared relative-interior point, convexity forces points on both sides nearby.

Artifact: assets/ri-separation-qualification.svgRelative interior is the right language for faces and lower-dimensional sets
This is the conceptual centerpiece. The theorem says that proper separation is exactly controlled by relative interiors, not by ambient interiors. Stress why this matters for lines, faces, and affine slices: a lower-dimensional convex set can have empty ordinary interior while still having a rich relative interior.

Farkas Preview

Separation As Either-Or

For a cone image \(AK\), either \(b\) belongs to the image or a separating normal proves it does not.

LP-Shaped Version

\(\text{either }Ax=b,\ x\ge0\text{ is feasible,}\)

\(\text{or }\exists y:\ A^Ty\ge0,\ b^Ty<0.\)

No Overlap

The primal solution and the separating certificate cannot both exist because they would give \(b^Ty=y^TAx\ge0\) and \(b^Ty<0\).

Reading Later Duality

Multipliers are not magic variables. They are the normal vectors of separating hyperplanes written in algebraic coordinates.

Certificate Habit

When a feasibility claim fails, ask which hyperplane would witness the failure and how to verify its signs.

Do not try to prove Farkas fully here; use it as a preview of the duality pattern. The important lesson is the alternative structure: either a primal representation exists, or a hyperplane normal certifies impossibility. That normal will later be called a multiplier, a dual variable, or a subgradient.
Proof Picture
nearest point argument

Projection Proof Picture

Step 1

Choose \(p=P_C(y)\), the nearest point in the closed convex set.

Step 2

For every \(x\in C\), the segment from \(p\) toward \(x\) cannot initially reduce the distance to \(y\).

Step 3

\(\langle y-p,x-p\rangle\le0\) becomes the half-space containing \(C\).

Step 4

The exterior point satisfies the strict reverse inequality by \(\|y-p\|^2>0\).

Artifact: assets/projection-proof.svgProof move: variational inequality to supporting hyperplane
Walk through the proof from the picture instead of from formalism alone. The nearest point creates a right-angle condition against every feasible direction inside the convex set. Expanding the squared distance along a segment gives the variational inequality, and the inequality is already the separating half-space.

Numerical Separator Solver

Nearest-Pair Program

\(\min_{x\in C,\ y\in D}\frac12\|x-y\|^2.\)

Recipe

solve for x*, y*
c = y* - x*
alpha = (c.x* + c.y*) / 2
verify max_C c.x <= alpha <= min_D c.y

Solver Discipline

The final verification is separate from optimization. A numerical candidate becomes a certificate only after the support inequalities check out.

Failure Mode
zero distance

Failure Without Closedness

Counterexample Shape

Let \(C=\{(u,v):v>0\}\) and \(b=(0,0)\). They are disjoint and convex, but \(\operatorname{dist}(b,C)=0\).

What Fails

No positive slab can fit between \(b\) and \(C\), because points of \(C\) approach \(b\) arbitrarily closely.

What Survives

The boundary line still gives a weak support relation for the closure. The missing margin is exactly the missing closedness signal.

Artifact: assets/closedness-failure.svgClosedness is not decoration; it creates attained distance
Use this example to keep students from upgrading every separation statement to a strong one. The open half-space and the boundary point are disjoint, but the distance is zero because the set approaches the point. A weak supporting line exists for the closure, yet no positive margin certificate can be valid.
