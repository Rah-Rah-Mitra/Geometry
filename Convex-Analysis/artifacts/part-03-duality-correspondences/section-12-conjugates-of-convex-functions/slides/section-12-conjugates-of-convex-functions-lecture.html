Conjugates Of Convex Functions

Fenchel conjugacy turns a convex function into a slope-indexed price sheet: each dual slope records the best affine gap it can certify.

Definition

For \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[-\infty,+\infty]\), the Fenchel conjugate is

\[ f^*(p)=\sup_x\{\langle p,x\rangle-f(x)\}. \]

Lecture Goal

  • Read slopes as prices and support lines as certificates.
  • See examples: quadratics, indicators, norms, and domains.
  • Use Fenchel gaps to check equality and subgradients.

Slope Price View

Economic Reading

Choose a price vector \(p\). Producing \(x\) earns \(\langle p,x\rangle\) and costs \(f(x)\). The conjugate is the best net surplus.

Price Sheet

\[p\mapsto f^*(p)=\sup_x(\hbox{revenue}-\hbox{cost}).\]

The optimizing \(x\), when it exists, tells which primal point that price exposes.

Low priceleft support
Neutral priceflat support

Concepts: duality pairing, slope parameter\(p\) is a functional, not a new primal point
Ask students to imagine choosing a price p before choosing x. The expression p x - f(x) is net profit after paying the cost f(x). The conjugate f*(p) is the best possible profit at that price, so changing p changes which primal point is worth producing.
Definition As A Measurement
vertical gap

Supremum Of Affine Gaps

Fixed Slope

\[ f^*(p)=\sup_x \{\langle p,x\rangle-f(x)\}. \]

Inspection Target

  1. Draw the affine reward \(x\mapsto \langle p,x\rangle\).
  2. Measure the gap above \(f(x)\) at each \(x\).
  3. Keep the largest gap; that number is \(f^*(p)\).

If the gaps are unbounded above, the conjugate value is \(+\infty\). This is common, not a malfunction.

Conjugacy converts points in \(x\)-space into affine certificates indexed by \(p\).Original visual asset: conjugate-support-lines.svg
This slide converts the picture into the definition. For each fixed p, graph the vertical gaps between the affine reward p x and the cost f(x). The supremum of those gaps is f*(p). For a convex cost, the winning x is usually where the supporting line has slope p.
Affine Minorants
one slope, one intercept

Support Lines

Certificate Form

For every \(p\), the conjugate value builds an affine lower bound

\[ h_p(x)=\langle p,x\rangle-f^*(p)\le f(x). \]

Why It Is Below

Since \(f^*(p)\) dominates \(\langle p,x\rangle-f(x)\) for every \(x\), rearranging gives the support inequality.

Touching Means Equality

If \(h_p(\bar x)=f(\bar x)\), then \(p\) is a slope supporting \(f\) at \(\bar x\).

Affine minorants are the data carried by the epigraph dual encoding.Equality prepares the subgradient graph.
Emphasize that the pair (p, f*(p)) is not abstract decoration: it writes the affine lower bound h_p(x)=p x - f*(p). When the line touches f, it certifies both Fenchel equality and a subgradient relation. When it does not touch, it still remains below f.
Worked Example
closed, smooth, proper

Quadratic Example

Computation

\[ f(x)=\frac{q}{2}x^2,\quad q>0. \]

\[ p x-\frac{q}{2}x^2 \quad\hbox{maximized at}\quad x=\frac{p}{q}. \]

\[ f^*(p)=\frac{1}{2q}p^2. \]

Gradient matching is the equality certificate: \(p=\nabla f(x)=qx\).

Original visual asset: quadratic-conjugate.svgCurvature \(q\) becomes dual curvature \(1/q\).
Use the quadratic as the calibration example because every object is explicit. The maximizing x satisfies p=q x, and substituting that x gives f*(p)=p squared over two q. The key visual message is reciprocal curvature: steep primal bowls produce shallow dual bowls.

Domain By Polar Bound
norm -> indicator

Norm / Dual Norm Pair

Norm Conjugate

\[ (\|\cdot\|)^*(p)= \begin{cases} 0,&\|p\|_*\le 1,\\ +\infty,&\|p\|_*>1. \end{cases} \]

The dual unit ball is the set of slopes that never overpay the norm: \(\langle p,x\rangle\le \|x\|\) for all \(x\).

Outside the dual ball, scale the offending \(x\). The affine gap grows without bound.

Original visual asset: dual-norm-visual.svgFor \(\|\cdot\|_1\), the dual ball is \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\le 1\).
For a norm, the conjugate is an indicator rather than another finite norm. If p stays inside the dual unit ball, p dot x never beats the norm by a positive unbounded amount. If p leaves that ball, scaling x in the exposing direction makes p dot x minus the norm diverge.
Dualize Twice
only affine evidence remains

Biconjugate Picture

Biconjugate

\[ f^{**}(x)=\sup_p\{\langle p,x\rangle-f^*(p)\}. \]

Every \(p\) contributes one affine lower certificate. Their supremum is automatically convex and closed.

Features invisible to lower affine support are not recovered: upward spikes, open boundary values, and nonconvex dents are replaced by the envelope.

Original visual asset: biconjugate-closure.svgThe operation returns evidence, not memory.
The biconjugate keeps only information visible to affine lower certificates. Any nonconvex bump, missing boundary value, or upward discontinuity that cannot be certified from below disappears. What remains is the closed convex envelope recovered by taking all support lines back to the primal side.
Recovery Theorem
proper + closed + convex

Closed Convex Recovery

Operational Statement

If \(f\) is closed, proper, and convex, then \(f^{**}=f\).

For a general \(f\), \(f^{**}\) is the closed convex function generated by all affine minorants of \(f\).

What Closedness Adds

Boundary limiting values are included, so no missing endpoint can evade the affine-envelope reconstruction.

What Convexity Adds

The function already equals the supremum of its supporting affine functions.

Conjugacy reverses inequalities; biconjugacy restores the lower envelope.Closed convex recovery is the slide deck's central invariant.
State the recovery theorem in operational terms. For a closed proper convex f, conjugating twice returns f. For a general function, the same operation returns the closed convex minorant generated by the affine lower bounds. Closedness is doing real work here, not cosmetic work.
Universal Gap Certificate
always nonnegative

Fenchel Inequality

\[ \langle p,x\rangle \le f(x)+f^*(p) \]

One-Line Proof

Because \(f^*(p)=\sup_z\{\langle p,z\rangle-f(z)\}\), the candidate \(z=x\) gives \(f^*(p)\ge \langle p,x\rangle-f(x)\).

Gap

\[ G_f(x,p)=f(x)+f^*(p)-\langle p,x\rangle \ge 0. \]

This inequality is the reusable certificate behind weak duality.Gap zero is the equality case.
Fenchel inequality is the one-line certificate that every later duality argument will reuse. Since f*(p) is the supremum of p x minus f(x), every particular x gives a lower candidate. Rearranging gives p x less than or equal to f(x)+f*(p).
Tight Support
gap equals zero

Subgradient Equality Case

Equality Criterion

\[ f(x)+f^*(p)=\langle p,x\rangle \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\in \partial f(x). \]

The subgradient inequality \(f(z)\ge f(x)+\langle p,z-x\rangle\) is the same affine support line written at the contact point.

For a differentiable \(f\), this collapses to \(p=\nabla f(x)\).

Subgradients are equality witnesses for conjugacy.In later sections, this graph becomes a central monotone mapping.
Equality is the diagnostic condition. If p is a subgradient at x, the affine function with slope p supports f at x, so the Fenchel gap is zero. Conversely, zero gap means the supporting inequality is tight at x, which is exactly the subgradient certificate.

Diagnostics
what can go wrong

Failure Modes

Infinite Values

A slope outside the natural dual domain may make \(\langle p,x\rangle-f(x)\) unbounded above.

Missed Suprema

A finite grid can underestimate \(f^*(p)\) when the best witness sits between samples or near a boundary.

Not Closed

Conjugating twice recovers the closed convex envelope, so open endpoint data can change.

Order Reversal

If \(f\le g\), then \(f^*\ge g^*\). Costs ordered one way become price ledgers ordered the other way.

Audit Questions

  • Is the primal function proper?
  • Is the epigraph closed and convex?
  • Can the claimed equality point be checked by a Fenchel gap?

Repair Moves

  • Add domain restrictions explicitly.
  • Track witnesses for the supremum.
  • Use biconjugacy only with the right closure expectation.
The conjugate is robust, but interpretation requires domain and closure checks.Failure modes are teaching opportunities.
Close the technical body by naming the common traps. A conjugate can be infinite outside its natural domain, finite sampling can miss the true supremum, nonclosed functions may not return under biconjugacy, and reversing inequalities under conjugacy is easy to forget.

Recap

What To Produce

  1. A slope \(p\) and a candidate witness \(x\).
  2. The affine support \(h_p(x)=\langle p,x\rangle-f^*(p)\).
  3. The Fenchel gap \(G_f(x,p)\).
  4. A closure decision: \(f^{**}=f\) or envelope recovery.
Core Formulas

\[ f^*(p)=\sup_x\{\langle p,x\rangle-f(x)\},\qquad f^{**}(x)=\sup_p\{\langle p,x\rangle-f^*(p)\}. \]

Examples To Remember

  • Quadratic -> reciprocal quadratic.
  • Indicator of a set -> support function.
  • Norm -> indicator of the dual unit ball.
