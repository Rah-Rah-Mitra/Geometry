Surfaces And Riemannian Geometry

A parametrized surface becomes geometric when tangent vectors can be measured, areas can be integrated, and coordinate changes can be separated from intrinsic data.

surface patch tangent plane first fundamental form area density isometry

Lecture Question

Which measurements are created by the parametrization, and which survive when the coordinates are changed?

Open by shifting the course from moving points to moving coordinate patches. The source span covers printed pages 87 through 103, where the central question is how a parametrized surface inherits measurements. Keep attention on what can be tested: tangent vectors, metric coefficients, area density, and isometry.
Parametrization
Coordinates Are Inputs

A Surface Patch Is A Map

Definition Pattern

$$X:U\subset\mathbb{R}^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^3,\qquad (u,v)\mapsto X(u,v)$$

The surface is inspected through a two-parameter map, not merely through its rendered image.

What The Coordinates Do

  • Grid curves in \(U\) become coordinate curves on the surface.
  • Small domain vectors become tangent vectors by differentiation.
  • The same geometric patch may admit many coordinate systems.
Present the parametrization as a machine that takes two coordinates and returns a point in space. The picture should not be treated as the definition by itself. The useful data are the map, the coordinate directions, and the way small rectangles in the domain are pushed into the ambient space.
Regularity
Rank Condition

Regular Means Rank Two

Regular patch: \(dX_{(u,v)}:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^3\) has rank \(2\) at every point under discussion.

Emphasize that a smooth image can still fail as a surface patch if the two partial derivative vectors become dependent. The cross product is the local alarm bell in three dimensions. When it vanishes, the coordinate net has collapsed and metric or area formulas no longer describe a genuine tangent plane.
Tangent Space
Pushforward Construction

The Tangent Plane Comes From Pushforwards

From Domain To Surface

$$\partial_u\mapsto X_u,\qquad \partial_v\mapsto X_v$$

The tangent plane is the linear approximation carried by the parametrization.

Why This Matters

Every tangent vector is represented as \(aX_u+bX_v\). Lengths, angles, and areas are computed after this pushforward, not from the flat coordinate drawing alone.

Use this slide to make the tangent plane feel constructed rather than guessed. The coordinate basis vectors in the parameter domain are pushed forward to X_u and X_v, and their span is the first-order approximation to the patch. Every later measurement is made inside this moving plane.
First Fundamental Form
Induced Metric

The First Fundamental Form Measures Vectors

Metric Matrix

$$G=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G_0\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}X_u\cdot X_u&X_u\cdot X_v\\X_v\cdot X_u&X_v\cdot X_v\end{pmatrix}$$

Vector Measurement

$$\lVert aX_u+bX_v\rVert^2= \begin{pmatrix}a&b\end{pmatrix}G\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}$$

The entries are coordinate data, but the measured length is geometric.

Introduce the metric matrix as a compact record of dot products. A coordinate vector a du plus b dv has squared length obtained by multiplying through G. This is the moment where the surface becomes Riemannian: the patch now carries its own ruler for tangent vectors.
Length And Angle
Metric Ellipse

Metric Ellipses Reveal Distortion

Reading The Ellipse

  • Round means the coordinate directions act orthonormally.
  • Tilt records a nonzero cross term \(F\).
  • Axes record preferred tangent directions for the metric.

Angle Formula

$$\cos\angle(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\xi^T G\eta}{\sqrt{\xi^TG\xi}\sqrt{\eta^TG\eta}}$$

Compare the ordinary unit circle in coordinates with the unit vectors measured by the induced metric. If the ellipse is stretched or tilted, coordinates are not orthonormal. Students should read the ellipse as a diagnostic for lengths and angles, not as decoration on the surface.
Area Form
Coordinate Area To Surface Area

Area Is The Metric Determinant

Formula

$$dA=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv$$

Equivalent Test

$$\sqrt{\det G}=\lVert X_u\times X_v\rVert$$

The notebook checks this identity numerically on a graph surface.

Move from lengths to area by drawing the parallelogram spanned by X_u and X_v. Its area is the square root of EG minus F squared, which also equals the norm of the cross product. This formula is the local conversion rate from coordinate area to surface area.
Metric Rotation
Oriented Tangent Plane

Metric Rotation Encodes Orientation

Orientation Operator

The operator \(J\) rotates tangent vectors by a positive quarter turn with respect to the metric.

$$J^2=-I,\qquad J^TGJ=G$$

Inspection Target

Check that \(J\) preserves metric lengths while reversing a vector after two applications.

Explain J as a ninety-degree rotation that belongs to the metric, not necessarily to the drawn coordinate grid. The checks J squared equals minus the identity and J preserves G are the algebraic signature of an oriented Riemannian tangent plane. The diagram separates coordinate arrows from measured rotation.
Reparametrization
Coordinate Change

Change Coordinates, Preserve Geometry

If \((u,v)=\phi(s,t)\), then \(\widetilde G=(D\phi)^T\,G\,D\phi\).

Coordinate Signal

Grid spacing, slopes, and sample density may change under \(\phi\).

Geometric Signal

Length, angle, and area agree when the metric is transformed correctly.

Common Failure

Comparing raw entries of \(G\) before converting coordinates confuses notation with geometry.

A reparametrization may shear or stretch the visible grid, but it should not change intrinsic measurements when handled correctly. The Jacobian of the coordinate change transforms the metric matrix. This slide prepares students to distrust raw coordinate spacing while trusting transformed metric data.
Isometry
Metric-Preserving Correspondence

Isometry Means The Same Metric Data

Definition Test

A map is an isometry when it preserves inner products of tangent vectors.

Teaching Lens

Look for equality of metric data, not equality of ambient appearance.

Define isometry through preservation of the first fundamental form rather than through visual similarity. Two surfaces may sit very differently in space, yet tangent lengths and angles agree after the right correspondence. The central test is whether pullback metric coefficients match, not whether drawings look alike.
Calibration Example
Flat Sheet To Cylinder

Plane And Cylinder Calibrate Isometry

Unit Cylinder Map

$$X(u,v)=(\cos u,\sin u,v)$$

$$X_u\cdot X_u=1,\quad X_v\cdot X_v=1,\quad X_u\cdot X_v=0$$

Conclusion

The plane rectangle and cylinder share local metric data even though one is visibly bent in \(\mathbb{R}^3\).

Use the plane-to-cylinder example as the clean calibration case. Rolling a flat sheet into a unit cylinder bends the embedding, but the local metric in the unrolled coordinates remains du squared plus dv squared. This helps students separate bending in space from intrinsic stretching.
Coordinate Metric
Sphere As A Ruler Test

Sphere Coordinates Change The Ruler

Unit Sphere Coordinates

$$X(\theta,\phi)=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)$$

Metric And Area

$$ds^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2$$

$$dA=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$$

The sphere shows why coordinate formulas matter before curvature is introduced. Longitude lines crowd together near the poles, and the metric coefficient for the angular direction carries a sine squared factor. Area density also changes with latitude, so equal coordinate rectangles need not have equal surface area.

Pitfall
Drawing Versus Measurement

Pitfall: Trusting The Drawing First

Warning

A sampled plot can look regular while the area density is close to zero.

Better Habit

Before interpreting area, angle, or isometry, inspect the rank condition and \(\det G\).

Warn that a surface plot can look smooth while the determinant of the metric becomes dangerously small. The drawing is a hypothesis generator; the metric determinant is the test. Students should learn to ask where the coordinate grid is nearly singular before believing any area or angle measurement.
Applied Lab
Parameter Debugging

Applied Lab: Perturb And Recompute

1. choose parameter

Change one amplitude, frequency, or coordinate choice.

2. recompute partials

Build \(X_u\), \(X_v\), and the normal again.

3. rebuild metric

Calculate \(E\), \(F\), \(G_0\), and \(\det G\).

4. compare residuals

Check area, \(J\), and matched metric identities.

5. inspect artifact

Only trust the figure after the measurements agree.

Frame the lab as a controlled debugging exercise. Change one parameter, recompute the partial derivatives, rebuild the metric, and inspect residuals before interpreting the picture. The valuable outcome is not only a passing check; a failing residual identifies which hypothesis of the geometric statement has been broken.
