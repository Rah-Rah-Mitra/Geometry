Energy Vocabulary

Three curvature-squared energies differ by what they count

\( W(\Sigma)=\int_\Sigma H^2\,dA \)
\( \int_\Sigma |A^\circ|^2\,dA \)
\( \int_\Sigma |A|^2\,dA \)
Mean part
Penalizes average bending in the two principal directions.
Trace-free part
Detects deviation from being locally umbilic.
Full second form
Counts all normal bending, including the topological offset.
Gauss equation
Converts between packages through \(K\).
Curvature packagesame geometry, different accounting
Use this slide to sort the energy vocabulary before doing any variation. Mean curvature, the trace-free part, and the full second fundamental form carry different pointwise information, but on a closed surface their integrals differ by a Gauss-Bonnet term.
Topology Offset

Gauss-Bonnet turns energy comparisons into fixed-genus comparisons

Closed surface identity

\(\int_\Sigma K\,dA = 2\pi\chi(\Sigma)\) turns curvature algebra into topology.

Practical reading

Hold topology fixed, compare the variable part, then use the offset to translate between energy conventions.

Gauss-Bonnetconstant offset, same minimizer
Emphasize that the offset is topological, not numerical noise. Once the Euler characteristic is fixed, minimizing one curvature-squared package is equivalent to minimizing another package shifted by a constant. This is why the notebook compares energies through invariant differences.
Pointwise Density

The integrand is local, but the question is global

\(dW = H^2\,dA\)

Inspection target

Look for small patches where \(H^2\) is high enough to overcome their small area.

Common trap

A visually dramatic bend is not automatically the main contributor unless the area weight agrees.

Density viewlocal integrand, global functional
This is the pointwise object to watch in computations. A surface patch contributes area times squared mean curvature, so a small region with large bending can dominate the visual heat map even when the patch is tiny.
Stationarity

A Willmore surface makes the normal residual vanish

Residual form

\(R_W=\Delta_\Sigma H + 2H(H^2-K)\)

A stationary surface satisfies \(R_W=0\) wherever the variation is allowed.

Euler-Lagrangestationarity as a residual plot
Present the equation as a residual, not as a formula to memorize. A Willmore surface balances the Laplacian of mean curvature against cubic curvature terms. In a numerical lab, the residual tells the learner where the surface still wants to move.

First Variation Roadmap

The proof is an integration-by-parts pipeline

1. Vary area

Record how the area form changes under normal speed.

2. Vary curvature

Differentiate mean curvature and expose second derivatives of the speed.

3. Move derivatives

Integrate by parts until the arbitrary speed multiplies the residual.

\( \delta W = \int_\Sigma R_W\,\phi\,dA \quad \Rightarrow \quad R_W=0 \)

Boundary caution

Closed surfaces and compactly supported variations hide the boundary terms; open surfaces must account for them.

Proof scaffoldvariation, curvature, integration by parts
The proof route has three rungs: vary the area element, vary the curvature, then integrate by parts until the normal speed is arbitrary. Stress that boundary terms vanish only under the closed-surface or compact-support assumptions.

Sanity Checks

Each visualization needs a numerical contract

Checks to run

  • Area element remains positive on the sampled grid.
  • \(\int K\,dA\) agrees with the expected topology.
  • Inversion comparison preserves \(W\) within tolerance.
  • Residual heat map matches scalar residual norm changes.
Diagnosticstrust the invariant, then the picture
Close the technical part with checks. Area elements should stay positive, the Gauss-Bonnet estimate should match the topology, inversion comparisons should agree within tolerance, and residual plots should identify the same regions highlighted by curvature.
