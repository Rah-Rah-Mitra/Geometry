Open the lecture by naming the object of study as a moving point rather than a drawing. The source span is short, but it establishes the vocabulary that the whole course will reuse: parametrized curve, speed, length, arclength, tangent, and bending energy.
Regular Parametrized Curves
Definition Lens

A Curve Is A Regular Map

Working Definition

A chapter curve is a smooth map

$$\gamma:[a,b]\to \mathbb{R}^n$$

with nonzero velocity everywhere:

$$\gamma'(t)\ne 0.$$

The same geometric point may be visited more than once; regularity is about motion, not injectivity.

Emphasize the word regular before writing formulas. The map may pass through the same point twice, so the image alone cannot be the definition. The derivative is the diagnostic: if the velocity vector vanishes, the moving-particle description has stalled and the curve is not regular there.
Regularity Diagnostics
Image Versus Parametrization

Self-Intersection Is Not The Problem

Regular Self-Intersection

At a crossing, two times can have distinct nonzero velocities. The map is still regular.

Singular Cusp

A cusp fails the regularity test at the stalled parameter value, even if the trace is easy to draw.

Use the figure eight as a useful surprise. At the crossing there are two different parameter values and two different velocity directions, so the local motion can still be perfectly regular. Contrast that with the cusp, where the picture may look like a curve but the speed collapses.
Orientation-Preserving Reparametrization
Same Trace, New Time

Reparametrization Changes The Clock

Allowed Clock Change

$$\phi:[c,d]\to[a,b],\qquad \phi'(u)>0$$

Then \(\tilde{\gamma}(u)=\gamma(\phi(u))\).

Chain Rule Signal

$$\tilde{\gamma}'(u)=\gamma'(\phi(u))\,\phi'(u).$$

The trace and direction are preserved; the speed profile can change.

Describe reparametrization as replacing the clock while keeping the oriented trace. The increasing map phi preserves the order of traversal, and the chain rule explains how speeds change. This prepares students to distinguish a coordinate signal from a geometric quantity.
Length
First Integral Invariant

Length Adds Speed Along The Clock

Definition

The speed is \(v(t)=\lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert\).

$$L(\gamma)=\int_a^b \lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert\,dt$$

Under an orientation-preserving reparametrization, substitution cancels the changed clock speed.

$$L(\gamma\circ\phi)=L(\gamma).$$

Here the integral should be read as an accumulator. Fast portions contribute more per unit parameter time, but after a valid reparametrization the substitution rule gives the same total. The lower plot is the diagnostic: different speed profiles can produce the same length.
Ambient Euclidean Symmetry
Congruence Check

Rigid Motion Does Not Alter Length

Rigid Motion

A rigid motion has the form

$$g(y)=Ay+b,\qquad A\in O(n).$$

Differentiating \(g\circ\gamma\) removes \(b\):

$$ (g\circ\gamma)' = A\gamma'.$$

Since \(A\) is orthogonal, \(\lVert A\gamma'\rVert=\lVert\gamma'\rVert\), so \(L(g\circ\gamma)=L(\gamma)\).

Connect the ambient geometry to the integral formula. A translation disappears after differentiation, and an orthogonal matrix preserves vector norms. This is the first instance of a recurring pattern: identify the quantity being measured, then check which transformations leave it unchanged.
Arclength
Replacing Clock Time By Distance

Arclength Is The Intrinsic Coordinate

Cumulative Distance

$$s(t)=\int_a^t \lVert\gamma'(\tau)\rVert\,d\tau.$$

Because regularity gives \(s'(t)=v(t)>0\), the function \(s\) is invertible onto \([0,L]\).

Arclength is the distance already traveled, not merely another decorative label on the parameter interval.

Present s of t as a new ruler laid along the curve. Because regularity keeps speed positive, the arclength function increases strictly and can be inverted. The diagram shows why dense parameter samples do not mean equal geometric spacing until arclength is used.
Arclength Reparametrization
Unit-Speed Representative

Unit Speed Is A Useful Representative

Rebuild The Curve By Distance

If \(s:[a,b]\to[0,L]\) is the arclength coordinate, define

$$\tilde{\gamma}(s)=\gamma(t(s))=\gamma\circ s^{-1}.$$

Then \(\lVert\tilde{\gamma}'(s)\rVert=1\): one unit of parameter equals one unit of distance.

Unit speed is a convenient representative, but the course keeps formulas valid for arbitrary regular parametrizations.
The arclength reparametrization produces a representative that moves one unit of distance per unit of parameter. Stress that the course will often keep arbitrary parametrizations instead, because families of curves and later surface formulas are easier to handle with invariant derivatives.
Operators Along A Curve
Invariant Calculus

Differentiate With Respect To Arclength

Derivative Along Distance

$$\frac{dg}{ds}=\frac{g'}{v},\qquad v=\lVert\gamma'\rVert.$$

This asks for change per unit distance along the curve, not per unit clock time.

Integral Over Arclength

$$\int_\gamma g\,ds=\int_a^b g(t)\,v(t)\,dt.$$

The \(ds\) notation remembers that distance weights the parameter interval.

This slide turns arclength into an operator rather than a new picture. Dividing by speed asks how a quantity changes per unit distance traveled along the curve. The same idea also defines integration against ds, which weights parameter time by the speed of the curve.
Unit Tangent Field
Velocity Without Speed

The Unit Tangent Records Direction Only

Definition

$$T=\frac{d\gamma}{ds}=\frac{\gamma'}{\lVert\gamma'\rVert}.$$

The unit tangent is a map \(T:[a,b]\to S^{n-1}\).

It discards how fast the parameter runs and keeps the direction of travel.

The unit tangent forgets speed and keeps direction. On a plane curve it can be drawn as arrows along the trace, while abstractly it is a map into the unit sphere. Students should inspect where the arrows rotate quickly, because that is where bending will concentrate.
From Tangent To Curvature
Local Turning Rate

Curvature Is Tangent Turning Per Length

Normalize

$$T=\gamma'/\lVert\gamma'\rVert$$

Differentiate By Distance

$$\frac{dT}{ds}=\frac{T'}{v}$$

Measure Turning

$$\kappa=\left\lVert\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rVert$$

Move from velocity to tangent variation. For unit-speed curves, curvature is the size of dT/ds; for arbitrary parametrizations the same formula is recovered by dividing by speed. The comparison panels make clear that the same trace direction can turn slowly or sharply.
Bending Energy
Global Cost Of Local Turning

Bending Energy Squares The Turning

Energy Functional

$$B(\gamma)=\frac12\int_\gamma \left\lVert\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rVert^2\,ds.$$

The quantity is unchanged by orientation-preserving reparametrization because every ingredient is measured per arclength.

Large localized turning is expensive: the square amplifies sharp bends.

Introduce bending energy as a global cost built from local tangent turning. Squaring matters because sharp turns are penalized disproportionately. This is the bridge from elementary curve geometry into the variational questions of elastic curves in the next chapter.
Examples To Calibrate The Formula
Line, Circle, Helix

Three Calibration Examples

Line Segment

$$\kappa=0,\qquad B=0.$$

Circle Of Radius r

$$\kappa=1/r,\qquad B=\frac{L}{2r^2}.$$

Helix

$$\kappa=\frac{a}{a^2+c^2}$$

Increasing pitch lowers tangent turning per length.

Use the examples as calibration instruments, not as a list to memorize. A straight line has constant tangent and zero bending. A circle of radius r has constant curvature one over r. Stretching a helix changes how quickly its tangent turns per arclength.
Common Pitfall
Coordinates Are Not Geometry

Parameter Signals Versus Geometric Signals

Clock-Dependent Signals

  • Point density in a plot.
  • The raw speed \(v(t)\) as a function of parameter time.
  • Uneven sample spacing from a chosen grid.
  • Orientation reversal when the clock runs backward.

Geometric Signals

  • Length under valid reparametrization.
  • The arclength derivative \(d/ds\).
  • The unit tangent field \(T\).
  • Bending energy measured over arclength.
This is the conceptual checkpoint. Sampling density, parameter speed, and plotted point spacing belong to the chosen clock, while length, arclength differentiation, tangent direction, and bending energy belong to the oriented curve. The notebook checks are designed to expose exactly this distinction.
