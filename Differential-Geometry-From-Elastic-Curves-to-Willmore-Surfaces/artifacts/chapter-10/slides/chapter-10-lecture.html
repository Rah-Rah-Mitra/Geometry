Total Gaussian Curvature

Curves on surfaces turn locally, regions accumulate Gaussian curvature, and parallel transport remembers the curvature enclosed by a loop.

Chapter Question

$$\int_\Omega K\,dA+\int_{\partial\Omega}k_g\,ds+\sum_i\alpha_i=2\pi\chi(\Omega)$$

Curves On Surfaces
Intrinsic Versus Ambient Bending

Surface Curves Have Two Curvatures

Surface Connection

The intrinsic derivative keeps only the tangent-plane change of the velocity.

$$\nabla_TT=k_g\,J T$$

Normal Curvature

The surface normal records how the same curve bends in ambient space.

$$\gamma''=\nabla_TT+k_nN$$

Boundary Term

Gauss-Bonnet integrates the geodesic curvature \(k_g\), not the full space curvature.

Pause on the decomposition of acceleration. A curve on a surface can bend because the surface itself bends in space, and it can also turn within the surface. The first contribution is normal curvature and depends on the embedding direction. The second is geodesic curvature and is the boundary term that Gauss-Bonnet will integrate.
Boundary Turning
Smooth Arcs Plus Corners

Boundary Turning Lives In The Tangent Plane

Continuous Turning

Along each smooth boundary arc, \(k_g\,ds\) measures how the tangent steers inside the surface.

Corner Turning

At a corner, the tangent jumps by an exterior angle \(\alpha_i\), so the ledger needs a discrete term.

$$\hbox{boundary turn}=\int_{\partial\Omega}k_g\,ds+\sum_i\alpha_i$$
This slide reframes geodesic curvature as a steering rate. The tangent vector is constrained to remain in the tangent plane of the surface, and the Levi-Civita connection tells how much of its change is intrinsic turning. Corners are not swept under the rug; their exterior angles are added as discrete turning events.
Gauss-Bonnet
Interior Plus Boundary Plus Corners

Gauss-Bonnet Is A Ledger

Interior

$$\int_\Omega K\,dA$$
+

Boundary

$$\int_{\partial\Omega}k_g\,ds+\sum\alpha_i$$
=

Topology

$$2\pi\chi(\Omega)$$
Present the formula as bookkeeping. The interior term measures Gaussian curvature across the region, the boundary term measures intrinsic turning along the boundary, and the corner sum records jumps in tangent direction. Their total is not arbitrary; it lands on two pi times the Euler characteristic of the region.
Examples
Flat Disk And Spherical Triangle

Two Calibration Regions

Flat Disk

Interior curvature is zero. The whole total comes from boundary turning.

$$0+\int_{\partial D}k_g\,ds=2\pi$$

Spherical Geodesic Triangle

Geodesic sides have \(k_g=0\). Curvature appears as angle excess.

$$\int_\Omega K\,dA=(A+B+C)-\pi$$
Use the flat disk and the spherical triangle as calibration examples. In the Euclidean disk the curvature term vanishes, so all of the two pi comes from boundary turning. On the sphere, a geodesic triangle has zero geodesic curvature along the sides, so the angle excess is paid for by the curved interior.
Calibration
Unit Hemisphere

Hemisphere Check

Boundary

The equator is a geodesic on the sphere, so \(k_g=0\) along the boundary.

Interior

For the unit sphere, \(K=1\), and the hemisphere area is \(2\pi\).

$$\int_{\Omega}K\,dA=2\pi$$
The hemisphere is the cleanest mental test. The boundary equator is a geodesic, so its geodesic curvature term vanishes. The region is a disk, so the right side is two pi. Therefore the total Gaussian curvature over the unit hemisphere must supply exactly two pi.
Orientation
Flat Annulus

Annulus Cancellation

Orientation Is Part Of The Data

The inner component is traversed in the opposite direction from the outer component.

Flat Interior

Since \(K=0\), the only possible contribution comes from the two boundary curves.

$$0+(2\pi-2\pi)=2\pi\cdot0$$
The annulus teaches orientation discipline. In the flat plane the Gaussian curvature is zero, and the Euler characteristic of an annulus is zero. The outer boundary and the inner boundary are oriented in opposite ways, so their geodesic curvature contributions cancel rather than add.
Parallel Transport
No Intrinsic Spin

Parallel Transport Around A Loop

Transport Equation

$$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V=0$$

A tangent vector field is parallel when its covariant derivative along the curve vanishes.

Preserved Quantities

Length and inner product are preserved, so transport is an isometry between tangent planes.

Returned Angle

A closed loop can still return the vector rotated by a holonomy angle.

Move from boundary turning to transport. A tangent vector is carried along the curve by asking its covariant derivative in the curve direction to vanish. The equation preserves vector length and inner products, but after a closed loop the returned vector may be rotated relative to the initial one.
Holonomy
Curvature As Returned Angle

Holonomy Detects Enclosed Curvature

Small-Loop Principle

A tiny positively oriented loop returns a vector with angle defect approximately equal to the enclosed total curvature.

$$\Delta\theta\approx\int_\Omega K\,dA$$

Sign Comes From Orientation

Reverse the loop orientation and the holonomy angle changes sign.

This slide makes the local meaning of holonomy explicit. For a small loop, the returned angle is approximately the integral of Gaussian curvature over the enclosed patch, with sign determined by orientation. Positive curvature behaves like a tiny rotation source, while flat regions produce no first-order angle defect.
Topology From Cancellation
Triangulated Region

Why Topology Appears

Internal Edges Cancel

Each internal edge is traversed twice with opposite orientations, so its boundary-turn contribution cancels.

Angle Defect Remains

The uncancelled accounting combines vertex, edge, and face counts into \(\chi=V-E+F\).

$$\sum_{\hbox{triangles}}\hbox{local ledger}=2\pi(V-E+F)$$
Explain the topological term through cancellation. When a region is triangulated, edge contributions appear twice with opposite orientations and disappear in pairs. What remains is the angle bookkeeping at vertices and the count of faces, edges, and vertices, which is exactly where the Euler characteristic enters.

Guardrails
Where The Ledger Breaks

Common Misreads

Mistake Correction Ledger Term Affected
Use full space curvature of a boundary curve. Use geodesic curvature, the tangent-plane steering measured by the surface connection. \(\int_{\partial\Omega} k_g\,ds\)
Ignore the inner boundary orientation. Orient every boundary component so the region stays on the left. sign of boundary integrals
Drop corner angles. Add exterior angle jumps whenever the boundary is piecewise smooth. \(\sum_i\alpha_i\)
Treat holonomy as arbitrary frame drift. Parallel transport preserves lengths; the residual rotation is forced by enclosed curvature. \(\Delta\theta\)
$$\hbox{wrong sign or missing term}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\hbox{the Gauss-Bonnet total no longer closes}$$
Use this slide as a guardrail before the recap. Students often mix normal curvature with geodesic curvature, forget that boundary orientation changes signs, ignore corner angles, or treat holonomy as an arbitrary rotation. Each mistake breaks a different term in the Gauss-Bonnet accounting identity.
