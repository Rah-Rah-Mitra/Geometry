Chapter 08 - Curvature

Surface Curvature Is How The Normal Turns

A surface bends by rotating its unit normal. Chapter 08 turns that rotation into a tangent-linear operator, two principal numbers, and a signed area distortion on the sphere of normals.

Normal Field

\(N(u,v)\) is unit and perpendicular to the coordinate tangent plane.

Shape Operator

\(S=-dN\) records how the normal changes along tangent directions.

Curvature Scalars

\(k_1,k_2,H,K\) compress the directional bending data.

The unit normal is the first actor: once the surface chooses a consistent normal, bending is read from how that normal changes as the point moves. The Normal Starts With Regular Coordinates

Unit normal

Orientation Choice

\(\displaystyle N=\frac{X_u\times X_v}{\|X_u\times X_v\|}\)

Changing the order or reversing orientation changes the sign of \(N\).

Immediate Tests

  • \(\|N\|=1\)
  • \(N\cdot X_u=0\)
  • \(N\cdot X_v=0\)

Failure Mode

If \(\|X_u\times X_v\|\) is tiny, a smooth-looking picture may hide a singular sample.

Normal orientation is data, not decorationAll later signs depend on it
Slow down on orientation. A parametrized surface supplies two tangent directions, and their cross product gives a candidate normal only when the parametrization is regular. The important check is not that the arrow looks plausible, but that it has unit length and is perpendicular to both partial derivatives.

The Shape Operator Is Hidden Inside \(dN\)

Weingarten map

Definition

For a tangent vector \(V\in T_pM\), differentiate the normal field along \(V\):

\(\displaystyle S_p(V)=-\,dN_p(V).\)

Why The Result Is Tangent

Since \(N\cdot N=1\), differentiating gives \(dN(V)\cdot N=0\). So \(dN(V)\) lies in the tangent plane.

Reading It

Large \(\|S(V)\|\) means the normal rotates rapidly when the surface is traversed in direction \(V\).

The derivative of \(N\) carries bending dataThe tangent plane receives that data as \(S\)
Introduce the shape operator as the tangent part of normal change. If the normal field turns while we move in a tangent direction, the negative derivative records bending in that direction. The minus sign is a convention that makes outward spheres have positive principal curvatures in this deck.

From Fundamental Forms To A Matrix

Coordinate computation

Metric Matrix

\(\displaystyle I=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix}\)

It measures lengths and angles in the tangent coordinates.

Second Form Matrix

\(\displaystyle II=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\f&g\end{pmatrix}\)

It measures normal acceleration of the parametrization.

Shape Matrix

\(\displaystyle [S]=I^{-1}II\)
Metric first, bending secondEigenvalues come only after this conversion
This slide turns the geometric definition into a matrix computation. The first fundamental form measures tangent vectors, the second fundamental form measures normal acceleration, and solving the metric matrix against the second-form matrix gives the linear map whose eigenvalues are principal curvatures.

Normal Curvature Varies With Direction

Euler formula

Directional Curvature

\(\displaystyle k_n(V)=\langle S(V),V\rangle\)

Here \(V\) is a unit tangent vector measured by the surface metric.

Euler Formula

\(\displaystyle k_n(\theta)=k_1\cos^2\theta+k_2\sin^2\theta\)

Extrema

The maximum and minimum directional curvatures are exactly the principal curvatures.

Normal curvature is a directional signalPrincipal values are the extrema of that signal
Emphasize that normal curvature depends on a direction, not just on the point. The quadratic expression on the unit tangent circle is Euler's formula: the value in direction angle theta is a weighted average of the two principal curvatures. The polar plot makes maxima and minima visible.

Principal Directions Diagonalize The Bending

Eigenvectors of \(S\)

Eigenvalue Equations

\(\displaystyle S(e_1)=k_1e_1,\qquad S(e_2)=k_2e_2\)

Self-Adjointness

\(\langle S(V),W\rangle=\langle V,S(W)\rangle\) using the surface metric.

Geometric Consequence

Orthogonal eigendirections organize curvature-line fields where \(k_1\ne k_2\).

Diagonalizing \(S\) separates the two bending axesUmbilics are the exception, not the rule
Principal directions are the tangent directions in which the shape operator does not shear the direction into another one. Because the operator is self-adjoint for the surface metric, these directions can be chosen orthogonal away from repeated eigenvalues. That is the linear algebra reason curvature lines exist locally.

Two Scalars From The Same Pair Of Numbers

Trace and determinant

Mean Curvature

\(\displaystyle H=\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}S\)

The average of the two principal bends.

Gaussian Curvature

\(\displaystyle K=k_1k_2=\det S\)

The signed product of the two principal bends.

Different Jobs

\(H\) sees average bending; \(K\) sees whether the surface bends the same way or opposite ways.

\(H\) is trace information\(K\) is determinant information
Mean and Gaussian curvature compress the same two numbers in different ways. Mean curvature is the average bending and is the quantity that appears in first variation of area. Gaussian curvature is the product and, through the Gauss map, belongs to the intrinsic story as well as the extrinsic one.

Calibrate The Signs With Standard Surfaces

Four local models

Plane

No normal change, so \(H=K=0\).

Sphere

Equal positive bends in all directions.

Cylinder

One principal direction is straight.

Saddle

Opposite signs give negative Gaussian curvature.

\(\displaystyle \text{plane: }(0,0)\quad \text{sphere: }(a,a)\quad \text{cylinder: }(a,0)\quad \text{saddle: }(a,-b)\)
Examples are sign testsThey also expose degeneracies
Use the example grid to calibrate signs and degeneracies. A plane has no normal change. A sphere bends equally in every direction. A cylinder bends only around its circular direction. A saddle has opposite signs, so its Gaussian curvature is negative even when the surface looks smooth.

Umbilics Are Points With No Favorite Direction

Repeated principal curvature

Definition

\(\displaystyle S_p=\lambda I\)

Equivalently \(k_1(p)=k_2(p)\).

Meaning

Every unit tangent direction has the same normal curvature at \(p\).

Diagnostic

Direction fields become ambiguous at umbilics because there is no unique principal axis.

Spheres are entirely umbilicGeneric surfaces have isolated or constrained umbilics
Umbilic points are where the shape operator is a scalar multiple of the identity. Every tangent direction is principal there, so a direction field cannot choose a favorite axis. On a sphere every point is umbilic; on a general surface the condition is rare and diagnostically important.

Gaussian Curvature Is Signed Normal-Area Distortion

Gauss map

Area Scaling

\(\displaystyle dA_{S^2}=K\,dA_M\)

Positive \(K\)

The normal image preserves local orientation and expands or contracts with one sign.

Negative \(K\)

The normal image reverses orientation because the two principal bends have opposite signs.

The determinant of \(S\) is also a JacobianGauss map area is the visual meaning of \(K\)
Present the Gauss map as a map from the surface to the unit sphere of normals. A small patch on the surface is carried to a patch of normal directions, and Gaussian curvature is the signed area scaling in the limiting sense. Positive, zero, and negative curvature correspond to different local behaviors of that normal image.

Curvature Pictures Need Guardrails

Pitfalls

Near-Singular Grid

A patch can look smooth while \(\det I\) is close to zero, making normals unstable.

Sign Convention Drift

Flipping \(N\) flips the shape operator and \(H\), while \(K\) remains unchanged.

Pretty But Unchecked

A color plot without the trace, determinant, and self-adjointness checks is not evidence.

\(\displaystyle \text{trust the picture only after } \|N\|,\; N\cdot X_i,\; I S-II,\; H-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}S,\; K-\det S \text{ are checked.}\)
The failure modes are mathematical, not merely numericalChecks are part of the lecture content
Warn students that a curvature display is only trustworthy after the regularity checks pass. A grid can look fine while the metric determinant is nearly zero, or a sign convention can quietly flip the Gauss map area. Residual panels and ledgers are part of the mathematical explanation, not a programming afterthought.
