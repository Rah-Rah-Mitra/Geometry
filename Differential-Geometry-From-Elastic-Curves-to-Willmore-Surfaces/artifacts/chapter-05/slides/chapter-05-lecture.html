Unit-Speed Curves: Tangent Change Is Normal Data

Start with \(s\)

Unit-speed identity

\(\langle T,T\rangle=1 \Rightarrow \langle T',T\rangle=0\)

Curvature magnitude

\(\kappa=\lVert T'\rVert=\lVert\gamma''\rVert\)

New issue in R3

The normal plane is two-dimensional, so direction inside that plane must be tracked or chosen.

Source grounding: printed pages 59-61; PDF pages 67-69.

Begin from the cleanest setting, a unit-speed curve. The tangent has unit length, so its derivative is automatically normal to the tangent. Curvature measures the size of that normal change, while the normal direction itself is now a moving choice rather than a fixed rotation by ninety degrees.

The Frenet Frame Packages Curvature And Torsion

When \(\kappa>0\)

Frame definitions

\(T=\gamma',\quad N=\frac{T'}{\lVert T'\rVert},\quad B=T\times N\)

\[ T'=\kappa N,\qquad N'=-\kappa T+\tau B,\qquad B'=-\tau N \]

The osculating plane is spanned by \(T\) and \(N\). Torsion measures how that plane turns through space.

Frenet data is an original teaching visualization for the assigned chapter span.

Present the Frenet frame as a convenient moving coordinate system when curvature is nonzero. The equations show that curvature couples tangent to normal, and torsion couples normal to binormal. The picture should help students see torsion as rotation of the osculating plane along the curve.

Frenet Normals Are Informative But Fragile

Low curvature hazard

Failure mode

If \(\kappa\) is small, \(N=T'/\lVert T'\rVert\) divides by a tiny number. The direction can become numerically unstable.

Geometry lesson

Curvature magnitude can be meaningful even when the Frenet normal direction is not a reliable coordinate.

Chapter bridge

Parallel normal fields and rotation-minimizing frames reduce avoidable spin in the normal plane.

Source grounding: Frenet-normal limitations in printed pages 59-85; PDF pages 67-92.

The Frenet frame is powerful but fragile. If curvature becomes very small, the principal normal is poorly conditioned, and a numerical trace can flip directions dramatically. This is why the previous chapter's parallel normal fields matter: they separate geometric bending from avoidable frame spin.

Torsion Measures Rotation Of The Binormal

Local signal
\(\tau=-\langle B',N\rangle\)

Geometric read

Curvature bends the tangent. Torsion turns the osculating plane around the tangent direction.

Helix diagnostic

For a circular helix, both \(\kappa\) and \(\tau\) are constant, so the frame rotates steadily.

Source grounding: total torsion and Frenet-frame measurements in printed pages 59-85; PDF pages 67-92.

Define torsion as the rate at which the binormal rotates toward the normal direction. A helix makes the signal visible because both curvature and torsion are constant. The warning is that torsion belongs to the chosen Frenet frame, so its interpretation depends on the frame remaining well defined.

Total Torsion Is Accumulated Normal-Plane Rotation

Holonomy meter
\(\Theta=\int_{s_0}^{s_1}\tau(s)\,ds\)

Holonomy viewpoint

A normal vector carried along the curve can return rotated even when the centerline nearly closes.

Numerical issue

Angles should be unwrapped before integration, otherwise jumps near \(\pi\) create false torsion spikes.

Notebook anchor: torsion holonomy meter and unwrapped angle check.

Move from local torsion to total torsion by integrating the signal along arclength. The holonomy meter emphasizes accumulation: small rotations of the normal plane can add up to a detectable angle. For closed or nearly closed framed data, the accumulated angle is often more important than a pointwise spike.

The Tangent Indicatrix Moves On The Unit Sphere

Elasticity lens

Map into \(S^2\)

\(s\mapsto T(s)\) records the direction history of the original curve.

\(\left\lVert\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rVert=\kappa(s)\)

Elastic reading

Bending energy becomes the squared speed of this spherical tangent path.

Torsion is not the speed on the sphere. It is the rotation of the normal frame along this path.

Notebook anchor: tangent path on the sphere with frame-orthogonality diagnostics.

The tangent map sends a space curve to the unit sphere. This converts bending of the original curve into a spherical trajectory, and it is a useful way to read elastic space curves. Curvature controls the speed of the tangent path, while torsion describes how the accompanying normal frame turns around it.

Elastic Space Curves Separate Direction From Gauge

Variational thread
\(E(\gamma)=\frac12\int \kappa(s)^2\,ds\)

Same energy, larger room

The penalty is still bending, but the curve now has a two-dimensional normal plane for storing direction changes.

Useful mental model

Study \(T(s)\) on \(S^2\), then remember that a normal frame can still twist around the tangent.

Source grounding: elastic space curves in printed pages 59-85; PDF pages 67-92.

Connect the chapter back to the variational theme of the course. Bending energy still penalizes curvature squared, but the curve can now store geometry in torsion and frame rotation. The tangent-sphere view helps separate the energy of changing direction from the choice of normal-plane gauge.

Binormal Flow Moves A Curve By Its Own Curvature Frame

Vortex filament law
\(\partial_t\gamma=\kappa B=T\times T_s\)

Why binormal?

The displacement is perpendicular to the tangent and normal directions determined by the curve.

Notebook diagnostic

After one short step, compare arclength before and after to catch an unstable discretization.

The flow is geometric only after the curve is sampled and differentiated consistently.

Notebook anchor: short binormal-flow diagnostic and small arclength-drift check.

Introduce binormal flow as a geometric evolution law rather than a fluid-mechanics detour. The velocity is perpendicular to both tangent and normal, so the curve moves through its own binormal direction. The notebook check about small arclength drift is exactly the right diagnostic for a short numerical step.

A Framed Curve Stores A Normal Director

Centerline plus marking

Data

A framed curve is a pair \((\gamma,U)\) with \(U\perp T\) and \(\lVert U\rVert=1\).

Companion vector

\(V=T\times U\) completes an oriented frame \((T,U,V)\).

Why it matters

Material rods, ribbons, and tubes need a centerline and a way to remember rotation around it.

Source grounding: framed curves and twist in printed pages 59-85; PDF pages 67-92.

A framed curve carries a chosen normal director in addition to the centerline. This extra director is not redundant; it records twist that the centerline alone cannot see. The key distinction is between bending of the centerline and rotation of material markings around that centerline.

Twist Energy Penalizes Rotation Of The Chosen Frame

Frame strain
\(\omega(s)=\langle U'(s),V(s)\rangle,\qquad E_{\mathrm{tw}}=\frac12\int \omega(s)^2\,ds\)

Bending components

How \(T\) changes inside the normal plane.

Twist component

How the chosen normal director rotates around \(T\).

Nonnegative energy does not mean frame-independent energy. It depends on the chosen director.

Notebook anchor: framed tube with twist stripe and nonnegative twist-energy check.

Frame strain decomposes into bending components and twist components. The twist energy is nonnegative because it is a squared rotational rate, but it can change when the chosen director changes. This slide should make students inspect which quantity is intrinsic to the curve and which belongs to the framing.
