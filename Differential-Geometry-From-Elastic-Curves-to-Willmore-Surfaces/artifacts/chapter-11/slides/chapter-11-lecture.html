Definition

Closed By Identification

Closed Surface

A compact two-dimensional surface with no boundary after all prescribed boundary identifications have been made.

Polygon Model

Start with a disk-like region, label boundary arcs in pairs, and form the quotient by gluing matched arcs.

What Must Be Checked?

After gluing, every former boundary point needs a disk neighborhood, not a half-disk neighborhood.

Construction kit: polygon plus pairingsObject of study: the quotient surface
Begin with the word closed in its topological sense, not as a drawing convention. A closed surface has no boundary after all identifications have been made. The polygon is only a construction kit; the mathematical object is the quotient surface after matched edges have been glued.

Why A Ledger?

It separates combinatorics from drawing style: relabeling the picture should not change the computed surface.

Pair each boundary arc exactly onceThen count vertex orbits
Treat the edge labels as data rather than ornament. Each boundary side must have exactly one partner, and the pairing must be an involution. The orientation sign records whether the arrows are opposed or aligned, while vertex orbits tell us which corners become the same point.
Orientable Model

Torus Gluing Keeps Orientation Consistent

Polygon Word

\(a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b^{-1}\)

Opposite edge pairs are traversed in opposite directions around the boundary.

Cell Count

One face, two edge pairs, one vertex orbit: \(\chi=1-2+1=0\).

Classification Label

For orientable closed surfaces, \(\chi=2-2g\), hence this model has genus \(g=1\).

Torus: orientable genus 1Total curvature target: 0
Use the torus as the clean model of orientable gluing. Opposite edges are paired with compatible reversed traversal around the polygon boundary, so a consistently chosen normal direction survives after gluing. The word remembers the same information as the edge-arrow picture.
Non-Orientable Contrast

One Gluing Sign Changes The Surface

Diagnostic Loop

Carry a local orientation once around the \(b\)-loop. If it returns reversed, no global orientation exists.

Cell Count Still Works

The Euler characteristic is still read from \(V-E+F\); orientability is an additional piece of data.

Klein model: \(a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b\)
Local patches do not reveal every global obstructionFollow orientation around loops
Now change one pairing sign and keep the rest of the mental model fixed. The Klein bottle contrast is useful because the local square still looks harmless, but transporting an orientation around one loop returns the opposite choice. This is the diagnostic failure, not a drawing defect.
Orientation Criterion

Orientation Is A Consistency Test

Propagation Rule

Pick a local orientation on one face. Gluing tells how that choice must be transported to adjacent faces.

Failure Mode

A closed propagation path that returns with the opposite local orientation proves non-orientability.

Computational Reading

It is a signed graph problem: edge signs encode whether local choices agree or flip.

Orientable means global consistencyNon-orientable means an unavoidable sign contradiction
Explain orientation as a propagation problem on small triangles. Choose a local clockwise rule, carry it across a glued edge, and continue. If the process returns to a triangle with the opposite rule, the surface is non-orientable; if it never contradicts itself, the surface is orientable.
Covering Construction

The Orientation Double Cover

Fiber Over A Point

Each point has two possible local orientations. The cover keeps them separate as actual points upstairs.

Loop Test

Orientation-preserving loops stay on one sheet; orientation-reversing loops swap sheets.

\(\widetilde M=\{(p,o): o \text{ is a local orientation at }p\}\)
Covering viewpoint turns sign failure into topology upstairsUseful for non-orientable surfaces
The orientation cover replaces each point by the two possible local orientation choices. On an orientable surface the two layers separate globally; on a non-orientable surface, loops that reverse orientation switch layers. This lets the class study non-orientable geometry using an orientable cover.
Cell Count

Euler Characteristic From The Gluing Ledger

One Polygon Model

Start with \(F=1\). Edge pairings produce \(E\). Vertex identifications produce \(V\).

\(\chi(M)=V-E+F\)

Example Pattern

A square model for a torus has \(V=1\), \(E=2\), \(F=1\), so \(\chi=0\).

Cell counts are quotient countsNot counts in the unglued drawing
Move from pictures to counting. A polygon model supplies one face, edge pairs after gluing, and vertex orbits after corner identifications. The Euler characteristic is the signed cell count, so a purely combinatorial ledger already predicts a topological invariant of the closed surface.
Classification Number

For Orientable Surfaces, Genus Counts Handles

\(\chi=2-2g,\qquad g=\frac{2-\chi}{2}\)

Reading The Formula

Every added handle lowers Euler characteristic by two.

From Ledger To Name

Once orientability is known, \(\chi\) determines the genus for connected closed surfaces.

Orientable classification coordinate: genus gHandles lower chi by 2
Define genus as the number of handles for connected orientable closed surfaces. The formula chi equals two minus two genus is the bridge from a gluing ledger to a classification label. Sphere, torus, and double torus give the numerical rhythm before any proof details.
Crosscap Count

For Non-Orientable Surfaces, Count Crosscaps

Non-Orientable Genus

The integer \(k\) counts projective-plane summands, often pictured as crosscaps.

\(\chi=2-k\)

Orientation Cover

The two-sheeted orientation cover has \(2\chi=4-2k\), hence orientable genus \(k-1\).

Non-orientable classification coordinate: crosscap count kCover genus: k - 1
Contrast the handle count with the crosscap count. A connected non-orientable closed surface has Euler characteristic two minus k, where k is the number of projective-plane summands. Its orientation double cover has twice the Euler characteristic and orientable genus k minus one.
Global Curvature

Gauss-Bonnet Closes The Ledger

\(\displaystyle \int_M K\,dA = 2\pi\,\chi(M)\)

Orientable Rewrite

For genus \(g\), total curvature is \(4\pi(1-g)\).

What This Means

Local curvature can vary dramatically, but the signed total is locked to the gluing invariant.

Gauss-Bonnet turns topology into a curvature integralClosed means no boundary term remains
Bring the chapter back to differential geometry. Curvature is measured locally by the surface metric, but for a closed surface the total Gaussian curvature is forced by Euler characteristic. This is where local bending and global gluing data meet in one formula.
Invariant Versus Distribution

Curvature Can Move, Total Cannot

Local Freedom

Changing the embedding or smoothing a region can move positive and negative curvature around the surface.

Global Constraint

For the same closed topology, the signed area under \(K\) remains \(2\pi\chi\).

Inspection target: distinguish the heatmap of \(K\) from the single total-curvature number.
Metric data creates KTopology fixes the signed total
Emphasize the difference between local curvature distribution and total curvature budget. A surface can be smoothed, dented, or parametrized differently, and the positive and negative patches may move. For a fixed closed topology, the integral remains tied to Euler characteristic.
Applied Lab

Notebook Diagnostics

Change One Parameter

Modify a pairing sign, a genus count, or a local curvature distribution, then rerun the ledger.

Useful Failure

A failed check should point to a precise misunderstanding: bad pairing, orientation contradiction, or wrong quotient count.

Successful Reading

The visible surface changes while the intended invariant remains stable within the stated tolerance.

Trust comes from invariant checksPictures and ledgers should agree
Connect the lecture to the assigned notebook checks. The computational habit is to verify the boundary pairing involution, confirm the orientation signs, compute genus from the ledger, and compare the Euler characteristic with the total-curvature target. The pictures earn trust through these tests.
Summary

A Reading Pipeline For Closed Surfaces

1. Gluing
Write the boundary pairing map and vertex orbits.
2. Orientation
Propagate local choices and watch for sign contradictions.
3. Genus
Use \(\chi=V-E+F\) and orientability to name the surface.
4. Curvature
Apply \(\int K\,dA=2\pi\chi\) as the global budget.

Seminar Prompt

Design two different gluing diagrams with the same \(\chi\). Decide whether they represent the same surface only after testing orientability.
