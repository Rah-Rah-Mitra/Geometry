Curvature, Area, Elastica, And Winding In The Plane

The plane gives every curve a preferred positive normal. That single choice turns bending into a signed signal, area into an oriented integral, and closed regular homotopy into an integer classification.

Local Signal

\(T'=\kappa J T\) records whether the curve turns with or against the chosen orientation.

Functionals

Sector area and bending energy turn a traced path into quantities that can be varied and tested.

Global Integer

\(\int \kappa\,ds=2\pi n\) becomes the tangent winding number for closed curves.

The Plane Has A Built-In Positive Normal

The operator \(J\)

\(J(x,y)=(-y,x),\quad J^2=-I\)

\(\langle JX,JY\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle\)

\(\langle JX,Y\rangle=\det(X,Y)\)

Orientation becomes a scalar measurement.

Source grounding: Section 3.1, printed pp. 29-31; PDF pp. 38-40.

Before defining curvature, slow down on the operator J. It is not decoration; it is the reason plane curves have signed curvature instead of only a curvature magnitude. The determinant identity tells students that rotating the first vector by ninety degrees converts orientation into an inner product.

Signed Curvature Measures How \(T\) Turns Toward \(J T\)

Definition 3.1

Unit-Speed Definition

\(\dfrac{dT}{ds}=\kappa J T\)

One scalar \(\kappa\) says how strongly and in which oriented direction the tangent changes.

Computable Formula

\(\kappa=\dfrac{\det(\gamma',\gamma'')}{|\gamma'|^3}\)

Invariance Check

Orientation-preserving reparametrization changes derivatives and speed together, so the ratio stays the same.

Notebook check: determinant curvature agrees with angle derivative.

The main teaching point is that the sign of kappa is a comparison between the actual change in tangent and the positive normal J T. A left turn has one sign and a right turn has the other. The determinant formula then gives a parametrization-independent way to compute the same signed quantity.

Curvature Functions Reconstruct Unit-Speed Plane Curves

Theorem 3.2

Integrate The Curvature

\(\alpha(x)=\alpha_0+\int_0^x \kappa(u)\,du\)

Recover Tangent And Curve

\(T=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha),\quad \gamma(x)=p_0+\int_0^x T(u)\,du\)

Uniqueness

Same \(\kappa\) means same curve up to a translation and orientation-preserving rotation.

Source grounding: Fundamental theorem of plane curves, printed pp. 31-32; PDF pp. 40-41.

Here the curvature function becomes a construction recipe rather than just a measurement. Integrate kappa to obtain the tangent angle, turn that angle into a unit tangent, and integrate once more to get the curve. Congruence freedom remains because the initial point and initial direction were choices.

Sector Area Is Signed Swept Area

Definition 3.3

\(A(\gamma)=\frac12\int_a^b \det(\gamma,\gamma')\,dx\)

When the position ray rotates counterclockwise, the contribution is positive; clockwise pieces subtract.

For open curves, translating the origin can change \(A(\gamma)\). For closed curves, or differences with common endpoints, the translation term cancels.

Source grounding: Section 3.2, printed pp. 32-35; PDF pp. 41-44.

Emphasize that sector area is signed swept area, not simply the visible bounded region. The origin ray can sweep the same region more than once, and clockwise pieces subtract. This makes the formula powerful for variations but dangerous if students read it as ordinary unsigned area.

Sector Area Supplies A Variational Constraint

Theorems 3.5-3.6

First Variation

\(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}A(\gamma_t)=-\int_a^b \langle Y,J\gamma'\rangle\,dx\)

Length With Fixed Sector Area

Critical curves have constant curvature, hence their images are straight lines or circular arcs.

Why This Matters

The area constraint turns a geometric optimization problem into a curvature condition.

Source grounding: area variation and fixed-area length criticality, printed pp. 34-35; PDF pp. 43-44.

This slide ties sector area back to variational calculus from Chapter 2. For compactly supported variations, the derivative of area pairs the variational field with J gamma prime. When length is constrained by fixed sector area, the Euler-Lagrange condition forces constant curvature, so the critical images are lines or circles.

Planar Elastica Reduce Bending To A Scalar Equation

Section 3.3

Total Squared Curvature

\(B(\gamma)=\frac12\int_0^L \kappa^2\,ds\)

Elastic Equation With Tension

\(\kappa''+\frac12\kappa^3+\lambda\kappa=0\)

Energy Picture

\(E=\frac12(\kappa')^2+\frac18\kappa^4+\frac{\lambda}{2}\kappa^2\)

Source grounding: planar elastic curves and curvature potential, printed pp. 36-37; PDF pp. 45-46.

The reduction to a scalar ODE is the reward for working in the plane. Total squared curvature becomes one half the integral of kappa squared, and the elastic equation is now an equation for kappa alone. The potential picture helps students remember which curvature values are dynamically allowed.

Adding Fixed Sector Area Adds A Constant Force

Length + area + bending

Two Constraints

Constrain length and sector area while varying total squared curvature.

Curvature Equation

\(\kappa''+\frac12\kappa^3+\lambda\kappa+\mu=0\)

Interpretation

\(\lambda\) records tension; \(\mu\) records the signed-area constraint.

Source grounding: area-constrained elastic curves, printed p. 37; PDF p. 46.

The extra sector-area constraint adds a constant forcing term to the elastica equation. Interpret mu as the term that keeps the signed area from drifting while the curve tries to lower bending energy. This is where length, area, and bending energy become a coupled variational system.

Total Curvature Turns Angle Change Into An Integer

Theorem 3.8

Total Curvature

\(\int_a^b \kappa\,ds\)

Angle Lift

\(T=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha),\quad \alpha(b)-\alpha(a)=\int_a^b\kappa\,ds\)

Closed Curve

\(\int_a^b\kappa\,ds=2\pi n,\quad n\in\mathbb Z\)

Source grounding: tangent winding number, printed pp. 38-39; PDF pp. 47-48.

Total curvature is the integral form of angle change. For a closed curve, the tangent returns to its starting direction, so the lifted tangent angle can only change by an integer multiple of two pi. That integer is the first global invariant in the course.

Tangent Winding Gallery

All integers occur

\(n=1\)

\(n=0\)

\(n=-1\)

The integer belongs to the tangent path \(T:[a,b]\to S^1\), not just to the visible trace of \(\gamma\).

Source grounding: representatives of tangent winding classes, printed p. 39; PDF p. 48.

Use the gallery to separate the curve in the plane from the path traced by its tangent on the unit circle. A curve can self-intersect, bend back, or be traversed in different ways, but the tangent winding records the net number of turns made by the tangent direction.

Open Plane Curves Are Regularly Homotopic

Theorem 3.10

Normalize Length

Scale until the length matches the parameter interval.

Use Arclength

Reparametrize so the speed is one.

Fix Initial Data

Translate start point and rotate starting tangent.

Interpolate Kappa

Set \(\kappa_t=(1-t)\kappa+t\tilde\kappa\), then reconstruct.

Source grounding: regular homotopy of open plane curves, printed pp. 39-41; PDF pp. 48-50.

For open plane curves, regular homotopy is flexible. The proof strategy normalizes length, arclength parametrization, starting point, and starting tangent, then interpolates curvature functions and reconstructs curves. The keyword regular means the velocity never vanishes during the deformation.

Closed Regular Homotopy Is Classified By Winding

Whitney-Graustein

Invariant Direction

\(n_t=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_a^b \kappa_t\,ds_t\)

If \(\gamma_t\) is a closed regular homotopy, \(n_t\) is continuous and integer valued. Therefore it is constant.

Classification Direction

Equal tangent winding is enough. After normalizing and interpolating curvature, one repairs the intermediate curves so they close without losing regularity.

Source grounding: Theorem 3.12, printed pp. 42-44; PDF pp. 51-53.

Closed curves introduce a real obstruction. During a closed regular homotopy, the winding number is both continuous in the homotopy parameter and integer valued, so it cannot jump. Whitney-Graustein says this obstruction is complete: equal winding is exactly the condition for closed regular homotopy.

The Closure Repair Keeps Velocity Nonzero

Proof mechanism

Interpolation May Drift

Curvature interpolation gives regular curves, but intermediate endpoints may not meet.

Subtract Average Drift

\(\tilde\gamma_t(x)=\gamma_t(x)-\frac{x-a}{b-a}\int_a^b T_t\,ds\)

Regularity Check

If the corrected velocity vanished, equality in the triangle inequality would force a constant tangent, impossible for a closed curve in this setup.

Source grounding: proof repair in Whitney-Graustein theorem, printed pp. 43-44; PDF pp. 52-53.

The proof of the closed classification has a subtle repair step. Curvature interpolation gives a regular path of curves, but the intermediate curves may fail to close. Subtracting the average tangent drift closes the curve, and the key estimate proves that the modified velocity is still nonzero.

Common Traps In Plane Curve Geometry

Before assigning problems

Sign Drift

Changing the positive rotation convention changes the sign of \(\kappa\), sector contributions, and winding.

Origin Drift

Sector area is not origin independent for open curves. Use closed curves or compare curves with common endpoints.

Regularity Drift

A homotopy that lets \(\gamma'\) vanish is not regular and can destroy the tangent winding argument.

Trace Drift

The visible trace of a self-intersecting curve does not determine the tangent path or the traversal count.

Pitfalls synthesized from the chapter source span and notebook sanity checks.

These are the mistakes to call out before the lecture ends. Sign conventions depend on the chosen positive rotation, sector area depends on the origin unless the setup cancels that dependence, and ordinary homotopy is weaker than regular homotopy because it allows the derivative to vanish.
