Open the chapter as a repair to a tempting but wrong operation. Differentiating a tangent vector field in ambient space usually leaves the tangent plane, so the lecture asks what part of that derivative belongs intrinsically to the surface.
Lecture Route

What The Connection Must Do

Split A Derivative

The ambient derivative \(d_X(df(Y))\) becomes one tangent vector plus one normal scalar.

$$d_X(df(Y)) = df(\nabla_XY) + II(X,Y)n$$

Forget The Embedding

The same coefficients are recovered from the metric entries \(g_{ij}\) and their derivatives.

$$\Gamma^k_{ij} = \frac12 g^{k\ell}(\partial_i g_{j\ell}+\partial_j g_{i\ell}-\partial_\ell g_{ij})$$

Measure Curvature

Connection curvature, Codazzi, and Gauss equations test whether metric and shape data fit one surface.

$$R \longrightarrow K,\qquad \nabla A \longrightarrow \hbox{Codazzi}$$

Definition By Projection

Tangential Differentiation

$$d_X(df(Y)) = df(\nabla_XY) + II(X,Y)n$$

Definition

\(\nabla_XY\) is the unique tangent vector field whose embedded image is the tangent projection of the ambient derivative.

What Gets Discarded?

The discarded normal part is not noise. It is the second fundamental form, the part that later defines the shape operator.

DefinitionAmbient derivative = tangent derivative + curvature normal
Stress that the ambient derivative is not discarded or magically tangent. It is decomposed into a tangent projection and a normal remainder. The tangent projection is the Levi-Civita derivative, while the normal coefficient is the second fundamental form.
Coordinate Coefficients

Christoffel Symbols: Two Routes, One Answer

Extrinsic Projection

Resolve the tangent part of \(f_{ij}\) in the basis \(f_u,f_v\).

$$f_{ij}=\Gamma^1_{ij}f_u+\Gamma^2_{ij}f_v+h_{ij}n$$

Metric Formula

Differentiate \(g_{ij}=\langle f_i,f_j\rangle\) and raise an index with \(g^{-1}\).

$$\Gamma^k_{ij}=\frac12 g^{k\ell}(\partial_i g_{j\ell}+\partial_jg_{i\ell}-\partial_\ell g_{ij})$$
Christoffel symbolsProjection and metric derivatives agree
This is the main computational bridge. One route projects second derivatives of the surface into the tangent basis. The other route differentiates the metric. Their agreement is the concrete sign that the same connection has both extrinsic and intrinsic descriptions.
Levi-Civita Characterization

Metric Compatibility And No Torsion

Uniqueness Principle

There is one connection on a surface that preserves the metric and has no torsion. The ambient projection construction realizes it.

$$X\langle Y,Z\rangle = \langle\nabla_XY,Z\rangle+\langle Y,\nabla_XZ\rangle$$
$$\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX=[X,Y]$$
CharacterizationMetric compatibility plus torsion-free determines Gamma
Present the characterization as a uniqueness test rather than a memory exercise. Metric compatibility says inner products differentiate by the product rule, and torsion-free says coordinate directions commute through the connection. Together these two constraints are enough to solve for the Christoffel symbols from the metric, so there is no hidden embedding data in the derivative.
Operational Formula

Covariant Derivative Of A Tangent Field

$$Y=Y^k\partial_k,\qquad X=X^i\partial_i$$
$$\nabla_XY=X^i\left(\partial_iY^k+\Gamma^k_{ij}Y^j\right)\partial_k$$

Interpretation

The term \(\partial_iY^k\) changes coefficients. The term \(\Gamma^k_{ij}Y^j\) corrects for the turning coordinate basis.

Derivative formulaCoefficient change plus basis change
Now move from basis vectors to arbitrary tangent fields. The derivative of the coefficient functions is the visible change in coordinates, and the Gamma term corrects for the fact that the coordinate basis itself turns across the surface.
Codazzi Equation

The Shape Operator Enters

Shape Operator

The normal part of the derivative gives \(h_{ij}\). Raising an index gives \(A=g^{-1}h\).

Codazzi Symmetry

For coordinate directions, the covariant derivative of \(A\) is symmetric in the two directions.

$$ (\nabla_u A)(\partial_v) = (\nabla_v A)(\partial_u) $$

Diagnostic Meaning

A large Codazzi residual says the shape data and connection data cannot be from the same smooth surface.

CodazziThe connection differentiates shape data coherently
The connection also differentiates endomorphisms such as the shape operator. Codazzi says that the covariant derivative of A has the symmetry forced by being the second derivative data of an actual embedded surface. In computations, it is a consistency test: the first and second fundamental forms must belong to the same parametrized surface.
Connection Curvature

Gauss Equation: Curvature From Noncommuting Derivatives

$$R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$$

Gauss Equation

For a surface, the curvature operator of the connection is determined by Gaussian curvature and the metric area form.

$$R(X,Y)Z = K(\langle Y,Z\rangle X-\langle X,Z\rangle Y)$$

Notebook Check

The residual compares connection curvature with the \(K\)-weighted rotation convention used in the chapter.

Gauss equationConnection curvature equals metric curvature data
Connection curvature measures the noncommutativity of covariant differentiation. On a surface, that operator is completely controlled by Gaussian curvature and the metric area rotation. This is the hinge between extrinsic shape and intrinsic curvature, because the determinant of the shape operator can be recovered from connection curvature.
Intrinsic Curvature

Theorema Egregium Dependency Chain

Metric Determines Gamma

The connection coefficients can be computed from \(g\) alone.

Gamma Determines R

Connection curvature is built from Gamma and its derivatives.

R Determines K

On a surface, the Gauss equation extracts Gaussian curvature.

Theorema EgregiumGaussian curvature is invariant under isometry
Keep the dependency chain explicit. If the metric determines the connection and the connection curvature determines K through the Gauss equation, then any isometry carries the same K. The surprise is not that K can be computed from shape, but that it does not need shape.

Debugging The Chapter

Common Sign And Index Traps

Normal Orientation

Changing \(n\) changes signs in \(h\) and \(A\). Curvature identities must change coherently.

Area Rotation \(J\)

The Gauss residual depends on the chosen orientation convention for the tangent plane.

Missing Gamma Term

Coordinate derivatives alone do not give a covariant derivative on a curved surface.

Mixed Data

Using \(g\) from one surface and \(h\) from another breaks Gauss and Codazzi immediately.

DebuggingMost failures are convention or data-consistency failures
This slide is a preemptive repair station. Most failed computations in this chapter come from changing the normal orientation, using the wrong area rotation sign, forgetting the Gamma correction, or mixing metric and shape data from different surfaces.
