Intrinsic Operators
the parameter square is only a coordinate device

Surface Calculus Is Metric Calculus

Metric data

For \(f(u,v)\), the induced metric is \(g_{ij}=\langle f_i,f_j\rangle\). It controls length, angle, area, and the coordinate expression of all differential operators.

\(dA=\sqrt{\det g}\,du\,dv\)
Gradient
\( \langle \operatorname{grad} h,Y\rangle = dh(Y) \)
Divergence
\( \operatorname{div}Y={1\over \sqrt{\det g}}\partial_i(\sqrt{\det g}\,Y^i) \)
Rotation
\(J:T_pM\to T_pM,\quad J^2=-I\)
Emphasize that the parameter rectangle is not the geometry. The induced metric converts a differential into a gradient, weights divergence by the area density, and defines the quarter-turn J in tangent planes. These choices make the formulas invariant under reparametrization.
A Calibration Identity
the notebook checks \( \operatorname{div}(J\operatorname{grad} g)=0 \)

Rotate A Gradient, Lose The Divergence

Interior identity
\( \operatorname{div}(J\operatorname{grad} g)=0 \)

The rotated gradient runs along level curves; locally it has no source or sink.

Boundary warning

The integral of a divergence can still be nonzero on a patch because the boundary records flux.

\( \int_U \operatorname{div}Y\,dA=\int_{\partial U}\langle Y,\eta\rangle\,ds \)
The rotated-gradient identity is a useful calibration before variations begin. Locally, J grad g follows level curves of g, so its flow does not create or destroy area density in the interior. Boundary flux is still visible, which prepares the tangential part of the area formula.
Variation Field
normal speed plus tangent relabeling

Every Velocity Splits

Normal component
\( \phi=\langle \dot f,N\rangle \)

Changes the embedded surface in the normal direction.

Tangential component
\( df(Z)=\dot f-\phi N \)

Moves labels along the surface unless boundary flux is present.

The geometry of area sees these two jobs differently.
Present the decomposition as a measurement tool. The scalar phi is the signed normal speed that pushes the surface through space. The tangent field Z moves parameter labels along the surface. Only the normal part changes the embedded shape in the interior.
Boundary Flux
why \(df(Z)\) still appears in the formula

Tangential Motion Is Relabeling Until The Boundary

Interior-supported tangent field

A tangent velocity with no boundary interaction changes the coordinate names of nearby points, but not the measured surface patch.

\( \int_U \operatorname{div}Z\,dA=0 \)
Boundary-crossing tangent field

When the field points through the boundary, the patch being measured gains or loses a strip of area.

\( \int_U \operatorname{div}Z\,dA=\int_{\partial U}\langle Z,\eta\rangle\,ds \)
Students often expect every visible arrow to change area. The important exception is that a tangential field with support in the interior is a reparametrization effect, while a tangential field crossing the boundary changes which patch is being measured through its boundary flux.
Hinge Formula
local before global

Area Density Has A Local Derivative

Local identity
\( \dot{dA}=(2\phi H+\operatorname{div}Z)\,dA \)
Interpretation

\(2\phi H\) is normal expansion or contraction. \( \operatorname{div}Z \) is tangent transport of area density.

Sign convention

For an outward round sphere, \(H=1/R\); outward normal motion increases area.

This is the hinge formula for the chapter. The term 2 phi H is the normal response of area density, and div Z is the tangential transport term. Because the formula is local, integration can later convert the divergence term into a boundary contribution.
Integrated Area Formula
normal gradient plus boundary flux

First Variation Of Area

General patch
\( {d\over dt}\operatorname{Area}(f_t)\big|_{0}=\int_U 2\phi H\,dA+\int_U \operatorname{div}Z\,dA \)

The second term becomes a boundary integral by the divergence theorem.

Interior-supported normal test
\( \delta A(\phi N)=\int_U 2\phi H\,dA \)

Thus the normal gradient of area is \(2H\,N\).

Move from the density identity to the integrated statement. With compact support away from the boundary, tangential divergence contributes no net area change, so the normal gradient of area is 2H N in this sign convention. With a boundary, the flux term must be kept.
Round Sphere Calibration
outward normal means \(H=1/R\)

Mean Curvature Sign Check

Convention
\(H={1\over R}\)

for the outward unit normal on a round sphere.

Area derivative
\( A(t)=4\pi(R+tc)^2,\quad A'(0)=8\pi Rc \)

and \( \int 2cH\,dA=2cR^{-1}4\pi R^2 \).

Curvature derivative
\( H(t)={1\over R+tc},\quad \dot H=-{c\over R^2} \)
The round sphere fixes the sign convention. With outward normal, the chapter uses H equal to one over R. If the sphere expands outward at constant speed, its area increases and its mean curvature decreases, giving a clean test for both first variation and curvature variation signs.
Mean Curvature Variation
transport, Laplacian, and curvature reaction

Curvature Also Varies

Normal and tangential effects
\( \dot H=dH(Z)+{1\over 2}\Delta\phi-\phi(2H^2-K) \)

For constant \(\phi=c\) on a round sphere, this gives \( \dot H=-c/R^2 \).

The formula explains why mean curvature is not merely transported: normal bumps diffuse and interact with the existing shape operator.
Do not try to derive the full curvature-variation formula on the slide. Instead, identify the three kinds of terms: tangential transport of H, a Laplacian response to uneven normal speed, and a curvature-squared reaction. The sphere test checks the constant-speed case.
Unconstrained Criticality
area tests every compactly supported normal speed

Minimal Surfaces

Criticality condition
\(0=\delta A(\phi N)=\int_U 2\phi H\,dA\)

for every compactly supported test speed \( \phi \).

Conclusion
\(H=0\)

Mean curvature vanishes pointwise.

Language check

Minimal means first-order stationary for area; global minimization is a stronger question.

Minimal means critical for area under compactly supported normal variations, not necessarily smallest among all competitors. Since arbitrary phi can probe every point, the first variation can vanish for all such tests only when H is zero everywhere on the surface.
Fixed Volume
the CMC equation appears by one multiplier

Volume Adds One Constraint

Area gradient
\( \delta A(\phi N)=\int 2H\phi\,dA \)
Cone-volume gradient
\( \delta V(\phi N)=\int \phi\,dA \)
Lagrange multiplier
\(2H=\lambda\)

so \(H\) must be constant.

Here the cone-volume derivative supplies the constraint gradient. Its normal density is phi, while area has density 2H phi. The Lagrange multiplier equation can use a single constant multiplier only when H is constant on the surface.

Compare the models by what the Euler-Lagrange equation sees. Planes and Enneper-type patches represent H equal to zero. Round spheres represent positive constant H. A generic graph patch usually has H varying across the surface, so it fails the fixed-volume criticality test.
