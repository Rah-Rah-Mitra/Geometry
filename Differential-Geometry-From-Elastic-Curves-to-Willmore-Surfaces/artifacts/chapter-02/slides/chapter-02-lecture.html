One-parameter families

A Variation Is a Smooth Surface of Curves

Definition

A smooth family \(t\mapsto g_t\) means the two-variable map \((x,t)\mapsto g_t(x)\) is smooth.

$$\dot{g}(x)=\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}\right|_{\tau=t} g_\tau(x),\qquad g'(x)=\frac{\partial g_t}{\partial x}(x)$$
First Rule

Before arclength enters, dot and prime are ordinary partial derivatives, so \((\dot{g})'=\dot{(g')}\).

The family is one object in two variables; a single curve is a slice at fixed \(t\).

smoothness in \((x,t)\)Schwarz theorem supplies \(\dot{g}'=(g')^{\cdot}\)
Describe a family as a smooth surface traced by the parameters x and t, even when every t-slice is the curve of interest. The important computational point is that dot and prime are partial derivatives in two independent directions, so they commute before arclength enters.
Variation fields

The Velocity Through Curve Space Lives Along the Curve

Variation of \(\gamma\)

A family \(t\mapsto\gamma_t\) with \(\gamma_0=\gamma\).

Variational Vector Field

\(Y=\dot{\gamma}_0:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^n\) records the first-order displacement at every parameter value.

Support in the Interior

For compactly supported variations, \(Y\) is zero near \(a\) and \(b\). Boundary terms then disappear from first-variation formulas.

variation field \(Y\)interior support is the endpoint-safe testing class
Read the arrows along the base curve as the velocity of the curve family at t equals zero. Stress that a variation field is not a tangent vector in ambient space at one point; it is a whole vector-valued function along the entire curve.
First local computation

How the Arclength Element Changes

Variation of \(ds\)

For speed \(v=|\gamma'|\) and unit tangent \(T\),

$$\dot{ds}=\left\langle\frac{dY}{ds},T\right\rangle ds.$$

Reading the Formula

  • \(Y\) itself is not enough; the derivative of \(Y\) along the curve matters.
  • Only the component of \(dY/ds\) in the tangent direction changes speed to first order.
local density firstglobal length comes by integrating
Pause on the formula for the variation of ds because it is the local engine for everything that follows. Tangential stretching changes the speed immediately, while a purely normal displacement only affects length through how its derivative tilts relative to the tangent.
Moving arclength coordinate

Time Variation Does Not Commute With \(d/ds\)

Commutator Formula
$$\left(\frac{dg}{ds}\right)^{\cdot}=\frac{d\dot{g}}{ds}-\left\langle\frac{dY}{ds},T\right\rangle\frac{dg}{ds}.$$

Why the Extra Term Appears

The operator \(d/ds\) is \(v^{-1}d/dx\). Since \(v\) varies with \(t\), the arclength derivative itself varies.

partial derivatives commutegeometric derivatives carry moving normalization
Emphasize the distinction between x-derivatives and arclength derivatives. Smooth partials commute, but d by ds contains the speed in its denominator, and the speed changes under variation. The commutator term is the bookkeeping cost of using moving geometric coordinates.
First variation of length

Critical Length Means Zero Curvature Vector

$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}L(\gamma_t)=\langle Y,T\rangle\big|_a^b-\int_a^b\left\langle Y,\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rangle ds.$$
Interior-Supported Variations

The endpoint term is zero, so all compactly supported tests see only \(-dT/ds\).

Conclusion

A length-critical curve has \(dT/ds=0\), hence it parametrizes a straight line segment.

boundary term plus interior termcompact support turns variation into a gradient test
Use the integration by parts line to separate boundary behavior from interior geometry. When the variation is compactly supported, the endpoint term vanishes and the only possible obstruction to criticality is the curvature vector dT by ds in the interior.
Variation of bending energy

Bending Energy Measures Motion of the Tangent

Bending Energy
$$B(\gamma)=\frac12\int_a^b\left\langle\frac{dT}{ds},\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rangle ds.$$

Why the Variation Is Heavier

  • \(T\) changes because the curve changes.
  • \(d/ds\) changes because the speed changes.
  • The density and the measure both contribute.
bending = squared speed of \(T(s)\)the tangent sphere organizes the geometry
Frame bending energy as the length of the velocity of the tangent map on the sphere. The variation formula looks heavier because changing the curve changes both the tangent derivative and the arclength measure. The slide separates those roles before the integrations by parts.
After integration by parts

Bending Variation Splits Into Boundary Data and a Bulk Gradient

Boundary Layer

Endpoint terms involve \(Y\) and \(dY/ds\). They vanish for variations supported in the interior.

$$\left[\left\langle\frac{dY}{ds},\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rangle-\left\langle Y,\frac{d^2T}{ds^2}+\frac32\left|\frac{dT}{ds}\right|^2T\right\rangle\right]_a^b$$
Bulk Gradient

The interior term has the form \(\int\langle Y,G_B\rangle ds\), where

$$G_B=\frac{d^3T}{ds^3}+3\left\langle\frac{dT}{ds},\frac{d^2T}{ds^2}\right\rangle T+\frac32\left|\frac{dT}{ds}\right|^2\frac{dT}{ds}.$$
endpoint clamping lives in boundary termsfree elastica use the bulk term
Move slowly through the boundary and bulk split. The boundary terms record what would have to be clamped for endpoint-sensitive variations. The bulk term is the gradient that remains visible to all compactly supported tests and therefore becomes the free elastica equation.
From integral tests to pointwise equations

Compactly Supported Bumps Force the Gradient to Vanish

Fundamental Lemma

If \(\int_a^b\langle Y,G\rangle ds=0\) for every \(Y\in C_0^\infty((a,b),\mathbb{R}^n)\), then \(G=0\).

Criticality Pipeline

  • Write the first variation as a pairing with \(Y\).
  • Choose \(Y\) with interior support.
  • Apply the lemma to get a pointwise ODE.
no functional-analysis topology needed hereonly vector space pairing and bump tests
Explain compact support as a microscope. If a smooth vector field pairs to zero against every small bump, then it cannot hide a nonzero value at any interior point. This is why variational identities turn into pointwise differential equations.
Unconstrained critical points

Length Gives Lines; Bending Gives Free Elastica

Length Functional

$$\delta L(Y)=-\int_a^b\left\langle Y,\frac{dT}{ds}\right\rangle ds$$

Critical for all compactly supported \(Y\) means

$$\frac{dT}{ds}=0.$$

The tangent is constant, so the image is a straight segment.

Bending Functional

$$G_B=\frac{d^3T}{ds^3}+3\langle T_s,T_{ss}\rangle T+\frac32\langle T_s,T_s\rangle T_s$$

A free elastic curve satisfies

$$G_B=0.$$

Equivalently, a fourth-order equation for \(\gamma(s)\).

the fundamental lemma supplies the pointwise conditionfree means no length constraint
Connect the two Euler equations to geometric intuition. Length has a first-order-looking gradient and forces zero curvature, hence a straight segment. Bending has a third derivative of the tangent field, so its free critical curves are governed by a higher-order balance.
Optimization under a geometric constraint

Fixed Length Turns Criticality Into Gradient Alignment

Constrained Critical Point

If \(\delta \widetilde{E}(Y)=0\) implies \(\delta E(Y)=0\) for every compactly supported variation, then \(\gamma\) is critical for \(E\) under fixed \(\widetilde{E}\).

Lagrange Multiplier
$$G_E=\lambda G_{\widetilde{E}}.$$

The proof is linear algebra in the vector space of fields along the curve.

constraint functional: lengthenergy functional: bending
Present the Lagrange multiplier theorem as orthogonality, not as a mysterious optimization trick. If all length-preserving first variations also preserve bending to first order, then the bending gradient must lie in the one-dimensional span of the length gradient.
Fixed-length bending criticality

Torsion-Free Elastica Carry a Constant Tension

Elastic Equation for \(T\)
$$\frac{d^3T}{ds^3}+3\left\langle T_s,T_{ss}\right\rangle T+\frac32\left\langle T_s,T_s\right\rangle T_s-\lambda T_s=0.$$
Meaning of \(\lambda\)

The multiplier is the tension associated with preserving total length while varying bending energy.

torsion-free elastic curve = bending critical under fixed length\(\lambda\) is constant
The fixed-length wire problem adds the tension term to the free bending equation. Point out that the parameter lambda is global rather than a function of s; it records the single scalar constraint, and changing its value selects different elastic shapes.
Tangent dynamics

Torsion-Free Elastica Are Spherical Pendulum Motions in \(T(s)\)

Unit-Speed Characterization
$$T''-\langle T'',T\rangle T=a-\langle a,T\rangle T.$$
Energy Equals Tension
$$\lambda=\frac12\langle T',T'\rangle-\langle a,T\rangle.$$

The tangent moves on \(S^{n-1}\); the curve follows by \(\gamma(s)=\gamma(0)+\int_0^sT(u)\,du\).

pendulum on \(S^{n-1}\)arclength \(s\) is the time variable
Translate the elastica equation into motion of the unit tangent on the sphere. The curve itself is recovered by integrating T, so the pendulum path controls the direction of travel while the arclength parameter supplies the clock.
Planar elastica preview

On \(S^1\), the Pendulum Path Becomes a Tangent-Angle Signal

How to Read a Planar Elastic Curve

  • The tangent is \(T(s)=(\cos\theta(s),\sin\theta(s))\).
  • Pendulum dynamics control \(\theta(s)\).
  • Integrating \(T\) reconstructs the curve shape.
Inspection Prompt

Where does the tangent angle slow down, and how does that appear in the reconstructed curve?
