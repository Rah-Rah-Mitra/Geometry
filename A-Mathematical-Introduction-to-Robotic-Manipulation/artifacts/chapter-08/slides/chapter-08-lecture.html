Nonholonomic Motion Planning

How can Lie-bracket controllability be converted into actual steering commands?

Constraint Form

Admissible velocities satisfy \( \omega_i(q)\dot q = 0 \), so not every visible path is executable.

Constructive Goal

Use loops, harmonics, or words so allowed directions generate hidden endpoint motion.

Chapter Artifacts

Brockett steering, chained-form harmonics, and rolling-contact geometric phase.

Source span: printed pages 355-394; PDF pages 373-412. Notebook: A-Mathematical-Introduction-to-Robotic-Manipulation/part-03-nonholonomic-systems/chapter-08-nonholonomic-motion-planning/08-nonholonomic-motion-planning.ipynb

Open by separating ordinary obstacle-avoidance planning from planning under velocity constraints. In this chapter the planner cannot draw an arbitrary curve in configuration space and then ask the robot to follow it. The constructive question is sharper: given a bracket-generating distribution, what input history actually realizes the hidden displacement? Point to the source span and notebook path as the grounded reading route.

Three Planning Strategies

Chapter roadmap
1. Optimal control

Attach a cost to each admissible trajectory and solve for inputs minimizing time, path length, or control effort.

\( \min \int_0^1 \|u(t)\|^2\,dt \)

2. Constant-input words

Concatenate flows so the first-order motion cancels while bracket terms survive.

\( e^{\epsilon g_1}e^{\epsilon g_2}e^{-\epsilon g_1}e^{-\epsilon g_2} \)

3. Canonical paths

Choose a reusable family of maneuvers tailored to a mechanism, such as car turns or rolling-contact loops.

maneuver library \( \Rightarrow \) desired displacement

Grounding: Chapter introduction, printed pp. 355-357; PDF pp. 373-375.

Use the three columns as a taxonomy of methods, not as mutually exclusive camps. Optimal control gives principled costs but can be hard to solve. Piecewise-constant words expose Lie brackets and are especially clean for nilpotent systems. Canonical paths package known maneuvers into reusable primitives. The rest of the lecture moves among these strategies as the system class changes.

Brockett System As The First Test Case

Growth vector (2, 3)

Model System

\( \dot q_1=u_1,\quad \dot q_2=u_2,\quad \dot q_3=q_1u_2-q_2u_1 \)

Bracket Direction

The fields \(g_1=(1,0,-q_2)^T\), \(g_2=(0,1,q_1)^T\) have a bracket in the missing vertical direction.

\( [g_1,g_2]=(0,0,2)^T \)

Notebook Check

Endpoint \( (0,0,1.005303) \) agrees with the saved control-loop line integral to numerical precision.

Grounding: Brockett first-order system, printed pp. 358-360; PDF pp. 376-378. Artifact: ../figures/brockett-sinusoidal-steering.png. Check: ../checks/final-sanity.json.

Brockett's three-state system is the smallest serious demonstration of constructive nonholonomy. The first two states follow the inputs directly, but the third state records the signed area swept by those direct coordinates. Ask students to inspect the figure as a control-space loop: returning to the origin in q1 and q2 is compatible with a nonzero endpoint in q3.

Why The Optimal Input Is A Sinusoid

Skew matrix

Variational Endpoint Problem

Minimize control effort while enforcing the bracket-coordinate constraint.

\( L=(\dot q_1^2+\dot q_2^2)+\lambda(\dot q_3-q_1\dot q_2+q_2\dot q_1) \)

Euler-Lagrange Consequence

\( \dot u=\Lambda u,\quad \Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\lambda\\ \lambda&0\end{bmatrix},\quad \dot\lambda=0 \)

Closure Selects Frequency

For steering \( (0,0,0) \) to \( (0,0,a) \) over one unit of time, the least-cost closed loop uses frequency \(2\pi\).

Grounding: Brockett optimal input derivation, printed pp. 358-360; PDF pp. 376-378.

The variational calculation should be read as a geometry generator. The multiplier for the bracket constraint is constant, so the input derivative is obtained by multiplying by a fixed skew matrix. A fixed skew matrix rotates vectors without changing their norm, which is why the optimal input is sinusoidal. The frequency is then selected by the endpoint closure condition.

First-Order Canonical Systems

Direct then bracket

m-Input Model

\( \dot q_i=u_i,\qquad \dot q_{ij}=q_i u_j-q_j u_i,\quad i<j \)

Matrix Reading

\( \dot q=u,\qquad \dot Y=q u^T-u q^T,\quad Y\in\mathfrak{so}(m) \)

Algorithm 1

  1. Move the directly controlled \(q_i\) coordinates.
  2. Use integrally related sinusoids to set \(q_{ij}\) while \(q_i\) closes.

Grounding: first-order canonical systems and Algorithm 1, printed pp. 360-361; PDF pp. 378-379.

Move from the three-state example to the m-input canonical system by naming the state split. The q_i coordinates are directly actuated, while the q_ij coordinates are first bracket coordinates. The steering algorithm is deliberately staged: first place the directly controlled coordinates, then use closed sinusoidal loops to write the desired skew matrix without disturbing the direct coordinates.

Second-Order Bracket Directions

Nested brackets

Model Coordinates

\( \dot q_i=u_i,\quad \dot q_{ij}=q_i u_j,\quad \dot q_{ijk}=q_{ij}u_k \quad \text{mod Jacobi} \)

Frequency Isolation

Choose frequencies so low-order coordinates close, while exactly one product chain contains a zero-frequency term.

Two-Input Pattern

\( u_1=a\sin 2\pi t,\quad u_2=b\cos 4\pi t \Rightarrow q_{121} \text{ changes} \)

Grounding: second-order model systems and Algorithm 2, printed pp. 361-363; PDF pp. 379-381.

This slide explains why the first-order recipe does not simply scale up. At second order, Jacobi relations and bracket nesting matter, and optimal inputs can leave the elementary sinusoid class. The constructive workaround is staged rectification: steer direct coordinates, correct first brackets, then choose frequencies whose products leave a zero-frequency term only in the desired second-order coordinate.

One-Chain Systems

Goursat-style model

Definition

\( \dot q_1=u_1,\quad \dot q_2=u_2,\quad \dot q_3=q_2u_1,\quad \dot q_4=q_3u_1,\ldots,\dot q_n=q_{n-1}u_1 \)

Bracket Ladder

The iterated brackets \(g_1, g_2, \operatorname{ad}_{g_1}^k g_2\) hit independent coordinate directions.

\( \operatorname{ad}_{g_1}^k g_2=[g_1,\operatorname{ad}_{g_1}^{k-1}g_2] \)

Grounding: one-chain system and Proposition 8.2, printed pp. 363-365; PDF pp. 381-383.

Present the one-chain form as the chapter's clean higher-order model. The vector field g1 shifts information down the chain while g2 injects the second coordinate. Repeated brackets ad_g1^k g2 pick off successive coordinates, so the proof of controllability is nearly a basis count. The diagram is meant to make that bracket ladder visible.

Chained-Form Harmonics

Algorithm 3

Target Coordinate

For \(k\ge 1\), steer \(q_{k+2}\) using the kth harmonic.

\( u_1=a\sin 2\pi t,\qquad u_2=b\cos 2\pi kt \)

Endpoint Increment

\( q_{k+2}(1)-q_{k+2}(0)=\left(\frac{a}{4\pi}\right)^k\frac{b}{k!} \)

Why It Works

Earlier coordinates are periodic after one cycle. The desired coordinate inherits one constant term after repeated products.

Artifact: ../figures/chained-form-harmonics.png. Grounding: Proposition 8.3, printed pp. 365-366; PDF pp. 383-384.

The harmonic artifact is the operational memory for Algorithm 3. The input u1 oscillates at the base frequency while u2 is chosen at the kth harmonic. As products are integrated down the chain, all nonmatching harmonics average away and a constant term remains in q_{k+2}. That is why the endpoint formula contains a factorial and powers of the amplitude.

Fourier Corrections In Real Systems

Model plus remainder

Local Model

A coordinate change can reveal a chained-form leading term, with nonlinear terms kept as a correction.

\( \dot \alpha = f(l)u_1,\qquad f(l)=c_1 l+O(l^2) \)

Fourier Selection

\( f\!\left(\frac{a_2}{2\pi}\sin 2\pi t\right)=\beta_1\sin 2\pi t+\beta_2\sin 4\pi t+\cdots \)

Only harmonics paired with the input survive integration over a period.

Grounding: Fourier techniques for hopping robot and kinematic car, printed pp. 366-369; PDF pp. 384-387.

Emphasize that real models rarely arrive already in chained form. The chapter's hopping-robot and car examples use coordinate changes and then treat nonlinear leftovers by Fourier coefficients. The pedagogical point is not that nonlinear terms vanish, but that the target displacement can be tied to a specific harmonic coefficient. Students should see the Fourier coefficient as a correction, not a decorative analysis step.

Conversion To Chained Form

Chart and input transform

Question

\( \dot q=g_1(q)u_1+\cdots+g_m(q)u_m,\qquad v=\beta(q)u,\quad z=\phi(q) \)

Can the transformed system be written as a one-chain?

Sufficient Two-Input Tests

  • Relevant distributions have constant rank.
  • The nested distributions are involutive.
  • Functions \(h_1,h_2\) satisfy the differential constraints.

Car Example

\( z_1=x,\quad z_2=\ell^{-1}\sec^2\theta\tan\phi,\quad z_3=\tan\theta,\quad z_4=y \)

Grounding: conversion to chained form and kinematic car example, printed pp. 369-371; PDF pp. 387-389.

This is the coordinate-hygiene slide. A nonlinear system can be steered with chained-form tools only after a legitimate state diffeomorphism and input transformation have been found. The sufficient conditions use constant-rank involutive distributions and functions h1 and h2 supplied by Frobenius-style reasoning. The car example is useful because the abstract conditions become a concrete change of variables.

Optimal Steering Beyond Model Systems

Necessary conditions

Driftless System

\( \dot q=\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(q)u_i,\qquad \min \frac12\int_0^1\|u(t)\|^2\,dt \)

Optimal Control Law

\( u_i=-p^Tg_i(q),\qquad \dot u=\Omega(q,p)u \)

The entries of \(\Omega\) are bracket pairings such as \(p^T[g_i,g_j]\).

Invariant

\( \Omega^T=-\Omega \Rightarrow \|u(t)\|=\|u(0)\| \)

Grounding: optimal steering and Ritz approximation, printed pp. 371-375; PDF pp. 389-393.

For a general driftless nonholonomic system, the closed-form sinusoid story becomes a necessary-condition story. The Hamiltonian calculation says the optimal controls equal minus the costate paired with each input vector field. Differentiating produces a skew-symmetric Omega matrix built from Lie brackets, so the control norm remains constant even when the actual time dependence is no longer a simple fixed-frequency sinusoid.

Philip Hall Coordinates

Nilpotent bookkeeping

Basis For Independent Words

For a nilpotent controllability Lie algebra, choose a Philip Hall basis \(B_1,\ldots,B_s\) of bracket words up to finite order.

Flow Coordinates

\( S_t(q)=e^{h_s(t)B_s}\cdots e^{h_2(t)B_2}e^{h_1(t)B_1}(q) \)

Canonical Equation

\( \dot h=Q(h)v,\qquad h(0)=0 \)

Only the first \(m\) inputs are physical; bracket inputs are synthesized by words.

Grounding: Philip Hall coordinates and Chen-Fliess-Sussmann equation, printed pp. 375-379; PDF pp. 393-397.

Philip Hall coordinates are a way to make bracket bookkeeping finite and ordered for nilpotent systems. The Hall basis records only independent Lie words after skew-symmetry and Jacobi identities have been handled. In those coordinates every flow can be represented as a product of exponentials along Hall basis elements, while fictitious bracket inputs are introduced temporarily and later synthesized from real controls.

BCH Words Generate Brackets

Words to motion

Composition Formula

\( \exp X\,\exp Y=\exp\!\left(X+Y+\frac12[X,Y]+\frac1{12}([X,[X,Y]]-[Y,[X,Y]])+\cdots\right) \)

Bracket Word

\( \exp(\epsilon X)\exp(\epsilon Y)\exp(-\epsilon X)\exp(-\epsilon Y)=\exp(\epsilon^2[X,Y]+O(\epsilon^3)) \)

Nilpotent Advantage

If the Lie algebra terminates after finite order, the word calculus terminates too.

Grounding: Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula and piecewise-constant inputs, printed pp. 379-381; PDF pp. 397-399.

The Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula is the algebraic reason a word of flows can generate a bracket direction. The simple commutator loop cancels the first-order motion and leaves a second-order bracket term. Higher-order words work similarly, but nilpotency is the feature that keeps the expansion finite. Without nilpotency the same idea becomes an approximation scheme rather than an exact finite recipe.

Dynamic Finger Repositioning

Rolling contact

Grasp Kinematic Constraint

\( J_h(\theta,x)\dot\theta=G^T(\theta,x)\dot x \)

Control Form

\( \dot x=u_1,\qquad \dot\theta=J_h^+G^T u_1+Ku_2 \)

Columns of \(K\) span the null space of the hand Jacobian.

Two Cases

  • No redundancy: contact changes require closed object loops.
  • Redundancy: internal motions reposition contacts with \( \dot x=0 \).

Grounding: dynamic finger repositioning problem, printed pp. 381-384; PDF pp. 399-402.

Return to manipulation by interpreting rolling contact as a nonholonomic planning problem. The grasp constraint relates finger velocities and object velocity. If there is a null space in the hand Jacobian, internal motions can move contacts while keeping the object fixed. This gives a clean reason to study a single rolling fingertip: the redundant degrees decouple when object motion is held at zero.

Geometric Phase From Rolling Loops

Area becomes angle

Rolling Lift

\( \dot\alpha_f=u,\quad \dot\alpha_o=M_o^{-1}R_\psi M_f u,\quad \dot\psi=(T_f+T_oR_\psi)M_f u \)

Spherical Finger On Plane

\( \dot\psi=-\sin u_f\,\dot v_f \)

Gauss-Bonnet Phase

\( \Delta\psi=-\operatorname{Area}(\Omega) \)

Artifact: ../figures/geometric-phase-fingertip.png. Grounding: geometric phase algorithm, printed pp. 384-387; PDF pp. 402-405.

The geometric-phase figure connects the chapter's area idea back to rolling contact. A closed path of the spherical fingertip can return the contact coordinates while changing the contact angle. The Gauss-Bonnet relation says that this phase is the negative area enclosed on the sphere. Stress that the accumulated phase is history-dependent; the loop is not wasted motion.
