Chapter 06: Hand Dynamics And Control

Constrained Hand Dynamics: Motion, Contact, And Internal Force

A multifingered hand is not several robot arms in parallel. It is one constrained dynamical system whose useful controls split into object motion, contact consistency, and squeeze.

Source grounding: printed pages 265-316; PDF pages 283-334Notebook: 06-hand-dynamics-and-control.ipynb
Open by naming the split that will organize the lecture: the hand must move the object, satisfy rolling or contact constraints, and regulate forces that do not show up as object motion. Students should hear that dynamics and control are being built on the constraint geometry from earlier grasping chapters, not replacing it.
1. Constraints As Velocity Geometry

From Constraints To Allowed Velocities

Pfaffian form

A contact model first removes instantaneous directions. The admissible velocity space is the nullspace of the constraint rows.

Allowed velocities
$$A(q)\dot q = 0,\qquad \dot q \in \ker A(q)$$
  • Holonomic constraints also come from position equations.
  • Nonholonomic constraints must remain velocity constraints.
  • The rank of A counts forbidden instantaneous directions.

Then connect the drawing to A(q) dot q equals zero: each row is a covector that tests velocity, and the hand is allowed to use only the velocities that pass all tests simultaneously.
2. Multipliers Become Physical Forces

Lagrange Multipliers As Constraint Forces

Constrained dynamics
$$M(q)\ddot q + C(q,\dot q)\dot q + N(q) = \tau + A(q)^T\lambda$$
Interpretation

The multiplier vector is not a numerical trick. It is the contact or joint reaction needed to keep acceleration consistent with the constraints.

Constraint consistency
$$A(q)\ddot q + \dot A(q,\dot q)\dot q = 0$$
Lagrange multipliers carry the reaction wrench informationSlide 3 / 16
This slide is the bridge from kinematic restrictions to dynamics. Emphasize that lambda is determined by enforcing the acceleration-level constraint, while A transpose lambda lies in generalized force coordinates. The plane picture also prepares d'Alembert's principle: these reaction forces are orthogonal to every allowed virtual displacement.
3. Remove Forces That Cannot Do Virtual Work

d'Alembert Projection

Virtual displacement test

Choose any virtual displacement that respects the instantaneous constraints. Constraint reactions disappear from the virtual-work equation.

d'Alembert statement
$$\delta q^T\!\left(M\ddot q + C\dot q + N - \tau\right)=0,\quad A(q)\delta q=0$$
Projection by a nullspace basis
$$N_A^T\!\left(M\ddot q + C\dot q + N\right)=N_A^T\tau,\qquad A N_A=0$$
Projection keeps dynamics on the constraint distributionSlide 4 / 16
Do not rush the notation here. First say the geometric sentence: allowed virtual motions cannot detect ideal constraint forces. Then show how the nullspace basis N_A turns that sentence into equations. This is why projection is a modeling step, not merely algebraic cleanup after solving for multipliers.
4. A Contact Model In Miniature

Rolling Disk Split

Two kinds of restriction
  • No lateral velocity at the contact point.
  • Forward translation couples to wheel spin.
  • Heading changes rotate the allowed direction field.
Canonical velocity split
$$-\sin\theta\,\dot x+\cos\theta\,\dot y=0,\qquad \cos\theta\,\dot x+\sin\theta\,\dot y-r\dot\phi=0$$

The disk is a compact warning label for hand contacts: local rolling equations are simple, but their allowed directions vary with configuration.

Rolling contact separates allowed motion from forbidden slipSlide 5 / 16
Use the disk because it is less visually crowded than a hand and still contains the important issue. The first equation forbids sideways motion, while the second couples rolling distance to spin. Once theta changes, the allowed direction in the plane changes, which is the seed of the nonholonomic behavior on the next slide.
5. The Common Modeling Error

The Nonholonomic Failure Mode

Failure mode

Replacing a velocity constraint by an invented position equation can remove real motions or add impossible ones.

What must stay differential
$$A(q)\dot q=0\;\;\not\Rightarrow\;\;h(q)=\mathrm{constant}$$
  • Admissible loops can create net displacement.
  • Constraint rows define a distribution, not always level sets.
  • Projection must use virtual velocities, not guessed coordinates.
Nonholonomic constraints are distributions, not automatic surfacesSlide 6 / 16
Name the mistake explicitly because it is attractive in calculations. Students may want to integrate every row of A into a lost coordinate. The loop diagram shows why that is dangerous: legal small motions can compose into a displacement that no single position constraint would predict. This matters for rolling fingers as much as for wheels.
6. One System, Many Coordinates

Composite Hand-Object Dynamics

Stacked state
$$q=\begin{bmatrix}q_h\\x_o\end{bmatrix},\qquad M_c(q)\ddot q+C_c(q,\dot q)\dot q+N_c(q)=B\tau+A(q)^T\lambda$$
Meaning of the blocks
  • Finger dynamics supply joint inertia and actuator torques.
  • Object dynamics supply mass, inertia, and external wrench response.
  • Contact constraints identify compatible finger and object velocities.
The hand and object are coupled before control is designedSlide 7 / 16
The essential teaching point is that contact coupling is not an afterthought. If the fingers and object are modeled separately and patched together later, the constraint forces become mysterious. The composite equation says where every coordinate lives and where lambda enters, so later control laws can be checked for dimensional and physical consistency.
7. Why Control Can Reuse Robot Dynamics

Structural Lemma For Control

Lemma

After ideal constraints are imposed and independent coordinates are selected, the reduced hand-object dynamics retain the manipulator form: symmetric positive inertia, velocity-product terms, potential or bias terms, and projected inputs.

Reduced coordinates
$$\bar M(z)\ddot z+\bar C(z,\dot z)\dot z+\bar N(z)=\bar B(z)\tau+\bar J(z)^T f_{\mathrm{ext}}$$
  1. Select coordinates or a nullspace basis for admissible motion.
  2. Project the full equation onto that admissible subspace.
  3. Use the retained structure to design tracking and force regulation.
Projected systems keep the structure needed for feedbackSlide 8 / 16
Present this as a structural lemma rather than a new modeling assumption. The payoff is that the controller can use familiar manipulator tools after constraints are handled correctly. The caveat is equally important: the reduced coordinates and projected inputs carry the contact geometry, so hiding the projection hides the assumptions that make the controller valid.
8. Forces Invisible To Object Acceleration

Internal Forces Are Nullspace Forces

Grasp map split
$$w_o=Gf_c,\qquad f_c=G^\dagger w_o+N_G\eta,\qquad G N_G=0$$
Control meaning

The vector eta changes contact loading without changing the net object wrench. That is how a grasp squeezes harder while tracking the same object motion.

Internal force is useful only if it remains compatible with unilateral contact and friction limits.

Generated chapter artifact: squeeze forces in the nullspace of the grasp map.

Artifact: internal-force-nullspace.pngSlide 9 / 16
Keep the algebra tied to the picture. The grasp map sends contact forces to an object wrench, so its nullspace contains contact force patterns the object cannot feel as a net wrench. Those patterns are not wasted effort: they maintain grip margin, preload rolling contacts, and prepare the hand to reject disturbances without changing the commanded motion.
9. Same Algebra Beyond Hands

Other Constrained Robot Systems

Coordinated Lifting

Multiple manipulators share one object acceleration and must agree on the same constraint wrench.

Workspace Dynamics

Task-space equations inherit inertia through the Jacobian, for example \(\widetilde M=M_o+J^{-T}M_fJ^{-1}\).

Hybrid Force-Position

Position is controlled in admissible tangent directions while normal reaction forces are regulated separately.

10. Nullspaces Are Not Just For Forces

Redundancy: Motions Invisible To The Task

Task split
$$\dot x = J(q)\dot q,\qquad \dot q=J^\dagger \dot x+\left(I-J^\dagger J\right)\zeta$$
Hand interpretation

Extra finger mobility can improve posture, avoid limits, or regulate contacts while leaving the object task velocity unchanged.

A redundant direction is useful only after it has survived the contact constraints. Task nullspace and constraint nullspace must be reconciled.

Velocity nullspaces complement force nullspacesSlide 11 / 16
Connect this to internal forces without conflating them. Internal force nullspaces live in contact force coordinates; redundancy nullspaces live in motion coordinates. A dexterous hand often has both, and a controller has to decide how to spend them: keep the object tracking, improve the grasp geometry, and avoid joint or contact limits.
11. When Contact Geometry Shrinks The Task

Nonmanipulable Grasps Restrict Object Motion

Diagnostic

A grasp can hold an object yet fail to command arbitrary local object motion. The missing motions appear as rank loss in the grasp or hand Jacobian structure.

Motion feasibility
$$V_o \in \operatorname{im} J_{\mathrm{hand-object}},\qquad \operatorname{rank}J_{\mathrm{hand-object}}<6\Rightarrow\text{restricted object twists}$$
  • Some contacts resist disturbance but cannot produce a desired twist.
  • Manipulability is local and configuration dependent.
  • Force closure does not automatically imply motion dexterity.
Stable grasping and dexterous manipulation are different testsSlide 12 / 16
Slow down on the distinction between holding and manipulating. A nonmanipulable grasp may resist some external wrenches and still lack enough independent motion directions to steer the object locally. This is where rank is not abstract linear algebra; it is the difference between a controller target that is reachable and one that asks for motion the contacts cannot provide.
12. Actuation Geometry

Tendon Extension Becomes Torque Coupling

Virtual work identity
$$\delta \ell = R(q)\delta q,\qquad \tau = R(q)^T f_t$$
Coupling

A tendon route maps joint motion into tendon extension. Transposing that map converts tendon tension into joint torques.

Because tendons pull but do not push, feasible torque directions form a cone rather than a full vector space.

Actuator geometry changes the control input spaceSlide 13 / 16
Treat tendon actuation as another map between spaces. The same virtual-work logic used for contact forces now explains why R transpose maps tension into generalized torque. The asymmetry is crucial: motors at joints can often be idealized as signed torques, while tendons impose pull-only feasibility and couple joints through their routing.
13. Feasibility With Pull-Only Inputs

Elastic Tendons And Force Closure

Pull-only feasible torques
$$f_t\ge 0,\qquad \tau=R(q)^T f_t,\qquad \mathcal C_\tau=\{R(q)^T f_t: f_t\ge 0\}$$
Elastic preload

Elastic tendon stretch can maintain positive tension, giving the hand a passive route toward contact preload and force-closure margins.

The controller must ask whether a desired internal force lies inside both the contact-force cone and the tendon-generated torque cone.

Use the artifact to make feasibility visible. The tendon matrix may span useful torque directions, but the nonnegative tension requirement clips that span to a cone. Elasticity helps by keeping tension positive and by storing preload, yet it does not erase the cone test. Force closure must be checked against the actual actuation model.
14. Two Objectives, One Torque Command

Tracking Plus Internal Force Regulation

Composite control shape
$$\tau=\tau_{\mathrm{track}}+J_c(q)^T\!\left(G^\dagger w_{\mathrm{cmd}}+N_G\eta_{\mathrm{cmd}}\right)+\tau_{\mathrm{posture}}$$
Division of labor
  • Tracking terms shape object motion and contact-compatible acceleration.
  • Internal-force terms adjust squeeze without changing the object wrench.
  • Posture terms spend remaining redundancy on limits and conditioning.
Control law terms must live in compatible projected spacesSlide 15 / 16
Frame the displayed law as a shape rather than a prescription for one implementation. The important point is compositional: object tracking, commanded wrench, internal squeeze, and posture objectives enter through maps that respect contact constraints. If any term is added in the wrong coordinate space, the controller may fight the constraints instead of using them.
15. Closing Synthesis

Hierarchical Control As Geometry-Aware Composition

Lecture takeaways
  • Pfaffian constraints define the velocities a hand may use.
  • d'Alembert projection removes ideal reactions from motion equations.
  • Internal forces are nullspace commands, not object accelerations.
  • Tendons and force closure add feasibility cones to the hierarchy.
Geometric control contract
$$\text{track motion}\;\oplus\;\text{regulate internal force}\;\oplus\;\text{respect actuation cones}$$

A good hand controller composes maps between spaces and checks each nullspace, rank, and cone before asking for torque.

Chapter 06 completes the constrained dynamics path for multifingered manipulationSlide 16 / 16
Close by returning to the hierarchy rather than a single formula. The chapter's control story is geometry-aware composition: constraints first, projected dynamics second, task and internal-force objectives third, and actuator feasibility last. That order is not bookkeeping. It is what prevents the hand from commanding beautiful object motion that violates contact, friction, or tendon limits.
