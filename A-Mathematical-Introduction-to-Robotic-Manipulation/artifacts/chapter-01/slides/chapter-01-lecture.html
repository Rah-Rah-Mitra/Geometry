Chapter 01: Introduction

What Geometry Must Manipulation Make Operational?

A robot course begins when pictures of machines become maps between configuration, task, contact, and control spaces.

Guiding test: can the geometric object be measured, composed, differentiated, or constrained by a machine?

Source grounding: printed pages 1-18; PDF pages 19-36Notebook: 01-introduction.ipynb
Open the lecture by making the title a genuine question rather than a slogan. Manipulation is not introduced as a catalog of hardware. It is introduced as the problem of turning motions, frames, contacts, forces, and task descriptions into objects that can be computed with and checked.
From Robot Images To Math Objects

Every Visible Machine Feature Asks For A Coordinate Model

Translation rule
  • Arm links -> rigid bodies with attached frames.
  • Joints -> one-parameter subgroups or constraints.
  • Tool tips -> task frames in workspace.
  • Contacts -> local frames, normals, cones, and wrenches.
Source grounding: objects named in printed pp. 1-18Visible artifact family: original SVG diagram
Use this slide to convert intuition into vocabulary. A student may recognize the arm, but the course needs the frame on the base, the joint variable, the end-effector frame, and contact information. The important move is from visual familiarity to operational representation.
History Matters Mathematically

Robotics History Is A Sequence Of Modeling Demands

Read the timeline as mathematics
  • Teleoperation asks for remote frames and human-command mappings.
  • CNC and industrial arms ask for repeatable paths and tolerances.
  • Dexterous systems ask for contact geometry and controllable internal forces.
  • Mobile manipulation asks for constraints that cannot be integrated away.
Artifact: figures/manipulation-timeline.pngSource span: printed pp. 1-18; PDF pp. 19-36
Do not treat the timeline as a museum panel. Each historical shift exposes a new mathematical burden. The arrival of programmable motion changes geometry from descriptive measurement into a specification language, while dexterity and mobility expose constraints that simple point-to-point motion cannot explain.
Teleoperation, CNC, Programmability

Commanding A Robot Means Encoding A Motion Specification

Teleoperation

operator input -> remote pose -> force feedback

Geometry must preserve the meaning of a motion when the hand and tool live in different frames.

CNC / Programmability

program path -> actuator commands -> realized curve

Geometry must turn a desired path into repeatable coordinates with tolerances and error checks.

Grounding: introductory motivation, printed pp. 1-18Operational question: what must be encoded?
This is the first place to emphasize programmability as a mathematical event. Once a machine follows a command, the command has to be represented in a frame, converted to joint or actuator coordinates, and checked against the actual motion. That is why geometry becomes operational.
Open Chains, Workspace, Error

An Open Chain Trades Joint Coordinates For Task Coordinates

Forward map

\(q \in Q \mapsto g(q) \in SE(3)\)

  • Configuration space counts independently actuated choices.
  • Workspace records poses a tool can reach.
  • Error appears when small joint changes are amplified at the task frame.
Notebook focus: links, joints, task frames, degrees of freedomSource span: pp. 1-18 / PDF 19-36
Keep the map q to g(q) at the center of the discussion. Students should see that a manipulator is not just a shape but a function from joint variables to a rigid motion. Workspace and accuracy are consequences of that function, not separate vocabulary lists.
Manipulator Taxonomy

Machine Families Are Different Geometric Commitments

Inspection target
  • Serial chains expose composition of joint motions.
  • Parallel mechanisms expose loop closure and constraints.
  • Hands expose multiple contacts and internal forces.
  • Mobile systems expose velocity constraints and planning geometry.
Artifact: figures/manipulator-taxonomy.pngOriginal generated figure, not a textbook figure
Use the taxonomy to prevent the course from narrowing too early to one industrial arm picture. The chapter introduces a family of geometric problems. Serial, parallel, hand, and mobile systems share the need for frames and constraints, but they stress different pieces of the mathematical toolkit.
Six DOF: Promise And Limits

Six Parameters Describe A Free Rigid Body, Not Every Robot Capability

Promise

pose = 3 translations + 3 rotations

  • A general tool pose lives in \(SE(3)\).
  • A six-joint serial arm may locally command six task directions.
Limits
  • Singularities can remove task directions.
  • Joint limits can cut the workspace.
  • Contact tasks may require fewer pose freedoms and more force control.
Check payload: sample_serial_chain_dof = 6Artifact evidence: checks/final-sanity.json
Frame six degrees of freedom as a useful promise that must be tested rather than assumed. A free rigid body has six pose coordinates, and a six-joint serial chain is the standard aspiration. But local rank, limits, collisions, and contacts decide what can actually be controlled.
End-Effectors Versus Hands

The Tool Frame Is Simple; Dexterity Begins When Contact Multiplies

Chapter objects: end-effectors, hands, contacts, task framesSource grounding: printed pp. 1-18
Contrast a single gripper with a hand because the mathematical shift is not cosmetic. A tool frame can often be modeled as one pose objective. A hand introduces several contact frames, object equilibrium, friction cones, and internal forces that do not appear in the object's net wrench.
Dexterity And Contact Frames

Dexterity Is Local Geometry Plus Feasible Force Direction

Contact model

normal + tangent plane + friction cone

  • The contact frame is a moving coordinate chart on the object.
  • The cone marks forces compatible with non-slipping contact.
  • Dexterity asks whether contact choices span useful motions and wrenches.
Later dependency: grasping and hand control reuse this local modelSource span: PDF pp. 19-36
This slide previews why contact geometry deserves its own chapters later. A contact point is not only a point. It carries a normal direction, tangential directions, and a cone of feasible forces. Dexterity is the ability to assemble these local pieces into controlled object motion.

Rigid Motion Bottleneck

Almost Every Later Topic Passes Through Rigid Motion

Bottleneck

frames + transformations + composition

Rigid motion is the shared grammar for tool poses, coordinate changes, visual servoing, screw axes, and contact frames.

Bridge to Chapter 02: Rigid Body MotionSource grounding: introduction dependency framing
Present rigid motion as the bottleneck idea, not merely as the next chapter title. Every time the course compares two frames, transports a velocity, composes a screw motion, or writes a contact wrench, it needs a reliable model of rigid displacement and frame convention.
Kinematics / POE Roadmap

Kinematics Turns Joint Motion Into A Product Of Rigid Motions

Roadmap formula

\(g(q)=e^{\hat{\xi}_1 q_1} e^{\hat{\xi}_2 q_2}\cdots e^{\hat{\xi}_n q_n}g_0\)

Chapter 01 only names the need; later chapters make this composition explicit and differentiable.

Dependency: rigid motion -> manipulator kinematicsSource span: pp. 1-18
The product-of-exponentials formula is a preview, not a derivation. Its role here is to show what the introduction is preparing students to understand. The serial arm becomes an ordered product of small geometric generators, followed by the home pose of the tool.
Dynamics / Control Roadmap

Geometry Enters Dynamics Through Velocities, Forces, And Energy

Later chapters ask
  • How does joint velocity map to tool velocity?
  • How do inertial and gravitational effects enter equations of motion?
  • Which feedback law stabilizes the desired trajectory?
Dependency: kinematics -> dynamics and controlGrounding: course dependency map
Make clear that dynamics is not a departure from geometry. Velocities come from differentiating kinematics, forces and torques must be expressed in frames, and control closes a loop around geometric error. The dependency graph is therefore a mathematical prerequisite graph, not merely a reading order.
Nonholonomic Behavior Preview

Some Constraints Govern Velocities Without Becoming Position Equations

Preview idea

allowable velocities form a distribution

Motion can be possible through brackets and maneuvers even when direct sideways motion is forbidden.

Dependency: rigid motion -> nonholonomic systems -> motion planningSource grounding: introductory roadmap
This preview helps students understand why nonholonomic systems belong in a manipulation text. A wheel cannot usually move sideways at an instant, but sequences of allowed motions can change position in surprising ways. The future mathematics studies velocity constraints and the motion they generate.

Operational Geometry Recap

What Geometry Must Manipulation Make Operational?

Frames

Coordinates for bodies, tools, cameras, contacts, and tasks.

Maps

Functions from joint choices to workspace poses and velocities.

Constraints

Joint limits, contact cones, closure equations, and nonholonomic distributions.

Checks

Ranks, graph acyclicity, degree counts, artifact sanity, and lab invariants.

A useful robotics abstraction must remain visible in hardware examples and testable in code.

Artifacts used: timeline, taxonomy, dependency map, checks, labSource span: printed pages 1-18; PDF pages 19-36
Close by answering the opening question with four nouns: frames, maps, constraints, and checks. The chapter has not yet developed the full algebra, but it has identified what the algebra must do. It must connect hardware examples to computable geometric objects without hiding the assumptions.
