Chapter 07

Forbidden Now, Possible Later

Nonholonomic behavior begins with a sharp asymmetry: the instantaneous velocity constraint forbids a direction, but carefully ordered admissible motions can still generate net displacement in that direction.

Instantaneous Rule

A wheel, car, or rolling contact may have a side direction that is not allowed in the current tangent space.

Motion Sequence

Composed flows do not simply add; their second-order term can expose a bracket direction.

Reachability Test

The chapter asks whether the Lie algebra generated by allowed fields spans every direction needed for motion planning.

Source grounding: printed pages 317-354; PDF pages 335-372Notebook: 07-nonholonomic-behavior-in-robotic-systems.ipynb

Open with the paradox, because it is the cleanest way to motivate the whole chapter. A car cannot move sideways at an instant, and a rolling contact cannot slip in its forbidden direction. Yet the driver can parallel park, and a rolling contact can reorient through sequences. Tell students that the lecture is about the mathematical object that reconciles those facts: a velocity distribution whose brackets may create effective directions not present at first order.
Velocity Constraints As One-Forms

Pfaffian Constraints

Definition

\(A(q)\dot q=0,\qquad \omega^i_q(\dot q)=0\)

Each row of \(A(q)\) is a one-form; its kernel is the set of velocities the mechanism may actually take.

\(\Delta_q=\ker A(q)\subset T_qQ,\qquad \dim \Delta_q=n-\operatorname{rank} A(q)\)

Engineering reading: a constraint does not remove configurations first. It removes tangent directions first.

Source grounding: Pfaffian constraints, printed pp. 317-354Visual: original SVG nullspace diagram

Spend time on the row interpretation. A matrix row is not just an algebraic object; it is a covector that tests a candidate velocity. The distribution Delta is what remains after all tests return zero. That distinction matters later, because integrability asks whether these allowed tangent planes fit together into configuration-space surfaces, while nonholonomy asks what motion sequences can do when they do not.

What To Inspect

At each sample point, the one-form rejects velocities transverse to the drawn distribution and keeps only the local allowed directions.

What The Picture Is Not

The drawn directions are not paths yet. They are local tangent instructions that still need flows and compositions.

Common Mistake

A visually smooth field of allowed directions can still fail to be tangent to any family of leaves.

Use the image as a bridge between covectors and motion. The one-form is easier to compute with, but the distribution is easier to reason about geometrically. Ask the class to read a single point first: which drawn direction survives the Pfaffian test? Then zoom out and ask the integrability question: do these directions assemble into sheets, or do their brackets force motion out of any candidate sheet?
Constraint Taxonomy

Holonomic, Partially Holonomic, Completely Nonholonomic

Holonomic

\(h(q)=c,\qquad dh(\dot q)=0\)

Allowed velocities are tangent to level sets. The constraint can be integrated into position equations.

Partially Holonomic

\(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{L}(\Delta)\lt n\)

Some first integrals survive, but bracket-generated directions enlarge the original distribution.

Completely Nonholonomic

\(\mathcal{L}_q(\Delta)=T_qQ\)

Allowed fields and their iterated brackets span the full tangent space at the points under study.

Source grounding: integrability and nonholonomy classification, printed pp. 317-354Visual: original SVG taxonomy

The goal is to prevent students from treating nonholonomic as a synonym for difficult. Holonomic constraints have enough first integrals to define leaves. Partially holonomic systems sit between the extremes: brackets add directions, but not all directions. Completely nonholonomic systems are the dramatic case for planning, because the Lie closure reaches the full tangent space. Keep the terms tied to rank and leaves, not to intuition alone.
Dynamics Of Allowed Directions

Vector Fields And Flows

Flow Definition

\(\dot q=g(q),\qquad q(t)=\varphi_t^g(q_0)\)

A vector field turns an allowed direction rule into a local motion map.

Control systems compose short pieces of flows: move along \(g_1\), then \(g_2\), then reverse \(g_1\), then reverse \(g_2\).

The order of these pieces matters because flows generally do not commute.

Source grounding: vector fields and flows, PDF pages 335-372Visual: original SVG flow field

This slide sets up the bracket square by making flows concrete. A vector field is an assignment of tangent vectors, but a flow is the result of following that assignment for time t. In control language, each admissible input selects a vector field or combination of vector fields. The later bracket formula is not an abstract algebra trick; it is the leading error term produced when four short flow segments are composed in a loop.
Second-Order Motion From A Loop

Lie Bracket Flow Square

Bracket Loop

\(q(4\epsilon)=q_0+\epsilon^2[g_1,g_2](q_0)+O(\epsilon^3)\)

First-order displacements cancel around the square; the commutator records the second-order drift that remains.

The sign depends on the convention for the order of the four flows, so always inspect the chosen loop orientation.

Source grounding: Lie bracket flow computation, printed pp. 317-354Artifact: ../figures/lie-bracket-flow-square.png

Make the cancellation explicit. Four small moves appear to return the system to its starting point if students only add first-order arrows. The image and formula show why that reasoning is incomplete: the flows are evaluated at shifted points, so a second-order term survives. Connect this directly to parking and rolling examples. The forbidden direction is not violated instantaneously; it is accumulated through a loop of legal motions.
Algebra Of Effective Directions

Bracket Algebra Rules

Bilinearity

\([aX+bY,Z]=a[X,Z]+b[Y,Z]\)

Antisymmetry

\([X,Y]=-[Y,X],\qquad [X,X]=0\)

Jacobi Identity

\([X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0\)

Function Coefficients

\([fX,Y]=f[X,Y]-Y(f)X\)

Source grounding: bracket algebra and generated distributions, printed pp. 317-354Visual: original SVG bracket expansion

Treat the rules as a computational grammar. Bilinearity lets students expand candidate fields, antisymmetry prevents double counting, Jacobi identifies dependencies among nested brackets, and the function-coefficient rule explains why brackets are not merely formal symbols with scalar labels. Emphasize that the algebra is used to build a rank test, so simplification is not cosmetic. It decides whether a proposed new direction is genuinely independent.
The Integrability Boundary

Frobenius: Leaves Versus Brackets

Frobenius Criterion

A constant-rank distribution \(\Delta\) is tangent to a foliation exactly when it is involutive.

\(X,Y\in\Delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad [X,Y]\in\Delta\)

If one bracket escapes the distribution, the local planes cannot be the tangent planes of configuration-space leaves.

Source grounding: Frobenius theorem, PDF pages 335-372Visual: original SVG leaf/bracket contrast

This is the conceptual hinge of the lecture. Frobenius turns a geometric question about leaves into a bracket calculation. If all brackets of tangent vector fields remain inside the distribution, the allowed planes fit together as tangent planes to submanifolds. If a bracket points outside, then loops of allowed motion detect a direction that no leaf could contain. That is precisely the phenomenon students saw in the flow square.
Chapter Integrability Route

Pfaffian Integrability Test

Single Constraint

\(\alpha(q)\,\omega(q)=dh(q)\)

An integrating factor turns one Pfaffian equation into a holonomic position constraint.

Multiple Constraints

\(\Omega=\operatorname{span}\{\omega_i\},\qquad \Delta=\Omega^\perp\)

Build the allowed vector fields, then test whether their brackets remain in \(\Delta\).

If \([X,Y]\notin\Delta\), legal velocity directions do not assemble into configuration leaves.

Source grounding: integrating factors, annihilating distributions, and FrobeniusVisual: original SVG test workflow

Give students the chapter's actual test route. First ask whether a single Pfaffian equation has an integrating factor that turns it into an exact differential. For multiple one-forms, build the annihilating distribution Delta equals Omega perpendicular, then use Frobenius to test bracket closure. The point is not to introduce a new exterior-calculus toolbox; it is to show how the one-form view and the vector-field view meet.
From Local Brackets To Reachability

Chow And The Rank Condition

Chow-Rashevskii Message

If a connected system has a bracket-generating distribution, then horizontal paths can connect points within the relevant component.

\(\operatorname{span}\{g_i,[g_i,g_j],[g_i,[g_j,g_k]],\ldots\}_q=T_qQ\)

For control, the rank condition is a reachability certificate, not a recipe for the shortest motion.

Source grounding: Chow theorem and Lie algebra rank condition, PDF pages 335-372Visual: original SVG rank ladder

After Frobenius says when leaves exist, Chow says why the opposite case can be powerful. A bracket-generating distribution gives a reachability statement: even though controls are restricted to horizontal directions, enough short loops can synthesize the missing directions. Be careful with the practical caveat. The theorem guarantees connectivity under its hypotheses, but it does not hand over an efficient trajectory, account for actuator bounds, or solve obstacle avoidance.
Physical Sources Of Velocity Constraints

Where Nonholonomy Comes From

Rolling

No slip couples translation and rotation at the contact point.

Steering

Wheels choose directions through orientation, not through arbitrary lateral velocity.

Angular Momentum

Hoppers and space robots gain velocity constraints from conserved momentum, not from ground contact.

A nonholonomic constraint is not simply an awkward coordinate choice. It can come from rolling contact, steering geometry, or angular-momentum conservation while leaving positions apparently unconstrained.

Source grounding: robotic examples in printed pages 317-354Visual: original SVG rolling-contact diagram

Use examples to anchor the formalism without replacing it. Rolling and steering give contact-level no-slip equations, while angular-momentum conservation gives velocity constraints even when there is no wheel on the ground. Hoppers and space robots are important because they show that nonholonomy is not only a rolling phenomenon. It can also appear when internal shape change is coupled to conserved momentum.
Canonical Mobile Robot Example

Kinematic Car

\(\dot x=v\cos\theta,\qquad \dot y=v\sin\theta,\qquad \dot\theta=\frac{v}{L}\tan\phi\)

\(-\sin\theta\,dx+\cos\theta\,dy=0\)

The Pfaffian form says the car has no sideways velocity in its body frame.

Parallel parking is therefore a bracket story: small legal arcs synthesize lateral displacement over time.

Source grounding: kinematic car model, printed pp. 317-354Visual: original SVG car model

Use the car to make Pfaffian notation feel physical. The form minus sine theta dx plus cosine theta dy measures sideways body-frame motion, and the constraint sets that measurement to zero. The car can drive forward and change orientation, but not translate sideways at an instant. The parking maneuver is therefore not a violation of the constraint; it is a sequence that changes orientation and accumulates a lateral net effect.
Growth Vectors In Contact Systems

Rolling Disk And Fingertip

Rolling Disk

Configuration includes position, heading, and spin. No-slip constraints tie translational motion to angular motion.

Rolling Fingertip

Contact frames on the fingertip and object evolve together; admissible motions depend on local surface geometry.

Dashboard Reading

The growth vector records how many independent directions appear after brackets of each depth.

Source grounding: rolling systems and growth vectors, PDF pages 335-372Artifact: ../figures/growth-vector-dashboard.png

The dashboard should be read as a rank story, not just as a plot. For a rolling disk, the no-slip rules are velocity constraints at the ground contact. For a rolling fingertip, the same logic is lifted into two moving surface frames. In both cases, first-order allowed directions may be too few, and the growth vector tells how quickly bracket operations add missing directions.
How Many Bracket Layers Are Needed?

Filtration And Degree Of Nonholonomy

\(\Delta^1=\Delta,\qquad \Delta^{s+1}=\Delta^s+[\Delta,\Delta^s]\)

The growth vector is \((\dim\Delta^1,\dim\Delta^2,\ldots)\). It stops when the filtration stabilizes.

The degree of nonholonomy is the first step \(r\) with \(\Delta^r=TQ\), when such a step exists.

Source grounding: filtration and growth vector material, printed pages 317-354Visual: original SVG filtration staircase

Connect this definition back to computation. A rank condition is not checked by listing all possible brackets forever. The filtration organizes the search by depth. At each step, add brackets between original allowed directions and the directions already discovered. The degree of nonholonomy measures how many layers of this process are needed before the system sees the full tangent space.
Avoiding Duplicate Bracket Words

Philip Hall Basis

A Hall basis gives an ordered, nonredundant list of bracket words in the free Lie algebra generated by the control fields.

\(X_1,\ X_2,\ [X_1,X_2],\ [X_1,[X_1,X_2]],\ [X_2,[X_1,X_2]],\ldots\)

The point is bookkeeping: antisymmetry and Jacobi create many formal duplicates that should not be recomputed in a rank test.

Source grounding: Hall bases and bracket bookkeeping, PDF pages 335-372Visual: original SVG Hall bracket tree

Present the Hall basis as an algorithmic convenience with mathematical depth. When there are several control fields, the number of formal nested brackets grows rapidly. Antisymmetry tells us some words only differ by sign, and Jacobi tells us some combinations are dependent. A Hall ordering supplies a disciplined list of representatives, which is why it appears naturally when implementing rank-growth checks.
