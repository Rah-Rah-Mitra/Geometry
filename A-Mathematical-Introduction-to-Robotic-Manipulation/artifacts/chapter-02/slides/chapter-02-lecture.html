Rigid Motion Invariance

Rigid Motion Keeps The Body Rigid

Invariant

For body points \(p_i,p_j\), a rigid motion preserves \(\|p_i-p_j\|\) and dot products.

Coordinate Change

The same invariant can appear through different coordinate triples in different frames.

Emphasize that a rigid body motion is recognized by what it refuses to change. Pairwise distances and angles in the body remain fixed even while every coordinate listed by an observer may change. This distinction prepares students for why frames are state variables rather than merely labels on a drawing.
Rigid Body Transformation

Rigid Body Transformation

\(p_s = R_{sb}p_b + q_{sb}\)

Rotation Block

\(R_{sb}\) tells how body axes are expressed in the space frame.

Translation Block

\(q_{sb}\) locates the body origin in space coordinates.

Introduce the familiar formula p_s equals R p_b plus q as a coordinate statement, not as the definition of rigidity. The rotation tells how the body axes are seen in the space frame, while q gives the origin offset. Ask the room to identify which part can change a distance and which part cannot.

Rotation Group

SO(3): Rotations As A Group

\(SO(3)=\{R\in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}: R^T R=I,\ \det R=1\}\)

Orthogonal

Columns are unit axes and stay mutually perpendicular.

Orientation Preserving

Determinant one keeps a right-handed frame right-handed.

Group Law

Products and inverses remain rotations, but order matters.

State the two algebraic tests for a rotation matrix and make students connect each test to a geometric meaning. Orthogonality preserves dot products, and determinant one keeps handedness. The multiplication rule is the first reminder that rotations form a group, not just a convenient file format for orientations.

Exponential Coordinates

Exponential Coordinates For Rotation

\(R = e^{\hat{\omega}\theta}\)

Axis-Angle

\(\omega\) gives the unit axis, while \(\theta\) gives the signed amount of rotation.

Hat Map

\(\hat{\omega}v = \omega \times v\), so cross products become matrix multiplication.

Present exponential coordinates as a compact local description: an axis and an angle combined into one vector. The hat map is the bridge from vectors to skew matrices. The important teaching point is that the exponential map turns an infinitesimal generator into a finite rotation while respecting the group structure locally.
Coordinate Caution

Local Coordinates Are Fragile

Chart Singularities

Minimal coordinates can become ambiguous or ill-conditioned near special rotations.

Composition Is Curved

Usually \(e^{\hat{a}}e^{\hat{b}}\neq e^{\widehat{a+b}}\).

Object First

Keep the rotation as the geometric object; use coordinates as local instruments.

coordinates can fail locally; the group element remains the pose
Warn students that minimal coordinates are useful but not neutral. Near special angles, charts can become ambiguous or ill-conditioned, and composition is not simple vector addition. The purpose of this warning is not to discourage exponential coordinates, but to keep the geometric object separate from a particular parameter chart.
Homogeneous Transforms

Homogeneous Transforms And SE(3)

\(T=\begin{bmatrix}R&q\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\in SE(3),\qquad \bar{p}_s=T\bar{p}_b\)

Pose

One matrix records orientation and origin location.

Inverse

\(T^{-1}\) changes the observer from space back to body.

Round Trip

Notebook se3_roundtrip_error = 3.93e-17.

Return to full rigid motion with the block matrix T. The upper-left block rotates, the upper-right block translates, and the bottom row makes affine transformations act linearly on homogeneous coordinates. Stress that SE(3) packages pose into a group element, so inverse and composition have geometric meanings.
Noncommutative Composition

Composition Order Matters

Route A

\(T_A = T_{\mathrm{translate}}T_{\mathrm{rotate}}\)

Rotate the body, then move the rotated frame.

Route B

\(T_B = T_{\mathrm{rotate}}T_{\mathrm{translate}}\)

Move first, then rotate the displacement as part of the frame.

in general \(T_A \neq T_B\), so frame labels and multiplication order carry meaning
Use the two routes to make noncommutativity visible. Rotate then translate is usually not the same pose as translate then rotate, even if the same symbols appear. This is the first place where students should stop reading products left to right as ordinary scalar multiplication and start reading them as frame-sensitive actions.

Screw Motion

Axis + Pitch

Rotation about an axis and translation along it combine into one rigid displacement.

Instantaneous Version

A twist is the velocity-level description of this screw idea.

Rigid Velocity

Twists

\(V=\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\v\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^6,\qquad \hat{V}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\omega}&v\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\)

Angular Part

\(\omega\) gives the instantaneous axis direction and spin rate.

Linear Part

\(v\) gives the velocity contribution at the chosen frame origin.

Frame Dependent

The motion is physical; the six coordinates depend on the observer.

Define a twist as the velocity object for a rigid body. The angular part records instantaneous rotation, while the linear part records the velocity of the chosen reference point. The same physical motion will have different six numbers in different frames, so the next slide gives the transformation law rather than a new concept.
Frame Change For Twists

Adjoint Frame Changes

\(V_s = \operatorname{Ad}_T V_b,\quad \operatorname{Ad}_T=\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\\hat{q}R&R\end{bmatrix}\)

Origin Matters

The \(\hat{q}R\) block is the correction from moving the point where velocity is observed.

The adjoint is the rule for moving twist coordinates between frames. Point out the cross-product block: translation changes the linear velocity part because a rotating body gives different point velocities at different origins. The adjoint is therefore not decorative; it is exactly the correction needed when the observer frame moves.
Worked Notebook Check

Worked Invariant Check

Transform The Velocity

\(V'=\operatorname{Ad}_T V\)

The twist coordinates change with the frame.

Transform The Force

\(F'=\operatorname{Ad}_T^{-T}F\)

The wrench uses the matching dual rule.

\(F'^T V' = F^T V\), recorded power_invariance_error = 0.0
Walk through the saved computation as a compact unit test for the chapter. A twist is pushed through the adjoint, a wrench is pushed through the inverse-transpose rule, and the scalar power pairing is unchanged. The zero power-invariance error is the numerical witness that the two transformation laws match.
Dual Coordinates

Wrenches And The Coadjoint Rule

Wrench

A force-moment pair \(F\) is dual to a twist because their pairing is power.

Coadjoint

If \(V'=\operatorname{Ad}_T V\), then \(F'=\operatorname{Ad}_T^{-T}F\).

Generated notebook artifact: artifacts/chapter-02/figures/wrench-twist-duality.png

Use the duality figure to distinguish forces from velocities without separating them completely. A wrench pairs with a twist to produce instantaneous power. Because twists move by the adjoint, wrenches must move by the coadjoint rule so the pairing does not depend on which frame was used for the calculation.

Inspection Lab And Takeaways

Rigid motion preserves the body's distances, angles, and handedness.

Coordinates

Students should move one twist through three frames, transform the matching wrench correctly, and check which quantities change. The takeaways are the deck's spine: rigid geometry is invariant, coordinates are frame-dependent, and power is the dual consistency test.
