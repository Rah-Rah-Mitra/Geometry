Coordinate Conversion

Hat And Vee: Vectors Become Generators

\(\omega=(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3)\quad \longleftrightarrow \quad \hat{\omega}\in so(3)\)

\[ \hat{\omega} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\omega_3 & \omega_2\\ \omega_3 & 0 & -\omega_1\\ -\omega_2 & \omega_1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

Vee is the reverse readout: recover the three independent coordinates from the skew-symmetric matrix.

Key invariant: skew symmetry is structural, not cosmeticSource grounding: Appendix B, printed pp. 435-440
The hat operator is the bridge from coordinates to an infinitesimal generator. Stress that the matrix is not an arbitrary storage format. Its skew symmetry makes cross products appear as matrix multiplication, and that is why the same conversion sits under rotation exponentials, twist exponentials, and Jacobian columns. Vee is the sanity check that no independent coordinate was lost.
Group Engine

Exponential Maps Are The API's Mathematical Engine

\(R(\theta)=e^{\hat{\omega}\theta}\)

A rotation generator integrates to a rotation matrix.

\(g(\theta)=e^{\hat{\xi}\theta}\)

A twist generator integrates to a rigid displacement.

API calls are trustworthy only when the output still lies in the intended group.

Checks to expect: rotation membership and homogeneous bottom rowSource span: PDF pages 453-458
The appendix is short, so the exponential map does a lot of work. It turns an angular velocity generator into a finite rotation and a twist generator into a finite rigid motion. Explain that the API is organized around this engine: hat builds the generator, exponential moves to the group, and membership checks catch numerical or convention errors.
Homogeneous Coordinates

Homogeneous Coordinates: Points Translate, Vectors Do Not

Point

\(p^h=\begin{bmatrix}p\\1\end{bmatrix}\)

Translation changes the represented location.

Free vector

\(v^h=\begin{bmatrix}v\\0\end{bmatrix}\)

Translation cancels; only the rotational part acts.

\(g=\begin{bmatrix}R&p\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\)

Visual family: framesSource grounding: printed pages 435-440
This is the slide for the most common hidden mistake in homogeneous coordinates. A point uses a trailing one because translating the frame changes the point's coordinates. A free vector uses a trailing zero because translation must not change direction or magnitude. Ask students to predict the effect before looking at the artifact; the bottom coordinate tells the story.
Screw Geometry

Twist Coordinates Encode Screw Geometry

\(\xi=\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\v\end{bmatrix}\)

A six-vector records angular and linear velocity data for one screw motion.

revolute: \(v=-\omega\times q\)

prismatic: \(\omega=0,\ v=u\)

Conventionaxis direction and sign
Dimensionsix coordinates per joint
Invariantsame physical screw after frame conversion
Appendix focus: twist exponentials and screw axesPDF pages 453-458
A twist is where the appendix becomes geometric rather than merely algebraic. The angular part names the screw axis direction. The linear part records the velocity induced at the origin, which is why the sign in v equals minus omega cross q matters. The class should leave this slide knowing what data a RobotLinks-style axis record must preserve.
Frame Conversion

Adjoint: Moving Twist Coordinates Between Frames

For \(\xi=(\omega,v)\):

\[ \mathrm{Ad}_g = \begin{bmatrix} R & 0\\ \hat{p}R & R \end{bmatrix} \]

\(\xi_s=\mathrm{Ad}_g\,\xi_b\)

Same instantaneous motion, new coordinate frame.

Power and rank claims only make sense after twists and wrenches are expressed in compatible frames.

Frame invariant: physical velocity is unchanged by coordinate transportSource span: printed pages 435-440
Use the adjoint as a coordinate transport rule, not as a mysterious six-by-six block matrix. The same physical motion can be described in the body frame or in the space frame, but the numbers are different. This is the moment to connect API design with mathematical hygiene: a function name that omits the frame is not enough information for reliable robotics code.
RobotLinks

RobotLinks: From Joint Axes To POE Kinematics

\(g(\theta)=e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}e^{\hat{\xi}_2\theta_2}\cdots g_0\)

Pipeline
  1. Choose joint axes and home pose.
  2. Convert each axis into a twist.
  3. Compose exponentials in order.
  4. Differentiate to obtain \(J_s(\theta)\).

final-sanity.json: spatial_jacobian_shape = [6, 2], task_jacobian_rank = 2

Worked artifact: reduced planar POE diagnostic; source routines also cover revolute/prismatic SCARA-style chainsPDF pages 453-458
RobotLinks is best introduced as a record of modeling decisions. It stores enough information to turn joint axes into twist coordinates, twist coordinates into a product-of-exponentials pose map, and the pose map into a spatial Jacobian. The displayed artifact is a reduced planar diagnostic from the notebook, not the full source SCARA chain; use it to inspect exponentials and Jacobian shape while naming the broader revolute and prismatic routine family.
Differentiating POE

Spatial Jacobian: Differentiating The POE Chain

\[ J_s(\theta)= \begin{bmatrix} \xi_1 & \mathrm{Ad}_{e^{\hat{\xi}_1\theta_1}}\xi_2 & \cdots \end{bmatrix} \]

Each later column is transported by the motions generated by earlier joints.

Check payload: Jacobian shape and task rankSource grounding: Appendix B, pp. 435-440
This slide completes the computational chain. The spatial Jacobian is not a fresh object unrelated to forward kinematics; it is the derivative of the product-of-exponentials map, expressed in a declared frame. The first column is already in the space frame, while later columns must be transported through earlier joint motions. That transport is exactly why the adjoint belongs in the API.
