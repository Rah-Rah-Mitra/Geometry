Frame this slide as the translation layer. In conformal language a Euclidean point lifts to a null point and a line can be built algebraically. Rounds, Flats, And Rejection

Spheres And Planes Are Early Predicates

Sphere Predicate

\(\lVert o+t d-c\rVert^2=r^2\) gives an interval or proves a miss.

Plane Predicate

\(t=-\frac{n\cdot o+h}{n\cdot d}\) is useful only inside the active ray segment.

Acceleration Logic

Cheap tests are conservative: a miss is decisive; a hit merely licenses detailed triangle work.

Rounds and flats provide early, interpretable rejection tests. Chapter 23 / bounding spheres and plane queries.
Make conservative rejection concrete. A bounding sphere hit does not prove the mesh is hit; it only permits more expensive work. A sphere miss, or a plane interval outside the ray segment, is a certificate that later triangle tests are unnecessary. This is where conformal rounds and flats become acceleration-minded predicates.
Intersection To Interpolation

Triangle Hits Carry Barycentric Data

Hit Conditions

A valid hit has \(t>0\), nonnegative barycentric weights, and \(\beta_0+\beta_1+\beta_2=1\).

\(p=o+t d=\beta_0 v_0+\beta_1 v_1+\beta_2 v_2\)
Payload
  • Triangle index and nearest parameter
  • Barycentric weights for interpolation
  • Material and local surface normal
Barycentric weights make a hit usable by the shader. Chapter 23 / triangle intersection.
Slow down on barycentric weights because they are the bridge between intersection and shading. The ray parameter tells where the surface lies along the viewing query, while the three weights say whether the hit is inside the triangle and how normals, colors, or texture coordinates should be interpolated.
Meshes, Transformations, Normals

Normals Turn Hits Into Surface Attitude

Mesh Contract

Faces intersect as triangles; vertex normals shade as a smooth attitude field attached to the surface.

\(n(p)=\operatorname{normalize}(\beta_0 n_0+\beta_1 n_1+\beta_2 n_2)\)
Object-Level Motion

Conformal versors explain rotations and translations as object transformations; the lab stores already transformed world-space arrays for tracing.

Notebook Checks

Positive face areas, unit face normals, unit vertex normals, and conserved rigid-motion lengths prevent rendering bugs from impersonating geometry.

The hit point becomes useful when it carries local surface attitude. Chapter 23 / mesh representation and transformations.
Treat normals as local surface attitude, not as decorative arrows. The mesh is intersected as flat triangles, but the shader may use an interpolated unit normal to represent smoother lighting. That compromise is mathematically honest only when the notebook checks positive areas, unit normals, and transformed model invariants.
Operators At The Surface

Reflection And Refraction Spawn New Rays

Reflection

\(d_{\mathrm{out}}=d-2(d\cdot n)n\), matching the plane-reflection sandwich habit.

Refraction

Snell's law returns a transmitted unit direction when the discriminant is valid; otherwise total internal reflection wins.

Spawn Discipline

Secondary rays start just above the surface and carry finite intervals for shadow tests or recursive intervals for reflection.

Reflection preserves length; refraction may fail by total internal reflection. Chapter 23 / spawned ray operators.
Connect the vector formulas to the algebraic habit students already know. Reflection is a sandwich-style operation in geometric algebra and a short dot-product formula in the shader. Refraction adds Snell's law and the possibility of total internal reflection, so the ray-spawning rule must return either a unit direction or a principled failure.

Cost Trade

Extra box and node predicates are worthwhile only when they prevent enough expensive primitive tests.

Separate correctness from cost. The BSP traversal is allowed to change the order and number of primitive tests, but it is not allowed to change the closest hit or miss result. The notebook's ray bundle comparison is the right evidence: agreement of answers first, then fewer triangle tests as the payoff.
Surface Event To Color

Shading Is Local Geometry Plus Visibility

Local Data At A Hit

Point, normal, material, view direction, light direction, and finite shadow interval.

\(C=C_a+\sum_{\ell \in V} I_\ell(k_d \max(0,n\cdot \ell)c+k_s(r\cdot v)^s)\)
Secondary Queries

Shadow rays test visibility to lights; reflection rays sample mirror contribution; refraction rays would continue through transmissive surfaces.

Color emerges from visibility predicates and angle predicates at one surface event. Chapter 23 / shading, shadows, and secondary rays.
At the shading stage, the ray tracer stops asking which surface is first and starts asking which outgoing directions matter. The hit point, normal, view direction, finite shadow segment, light direction, and reflected ray are all local geometric objects. Color is the result of those objects passing visibility and angle tests.
