Homogeneous coordinates

A Point Is A Ray Of Representatives

Definition 11.1 - Representative

A finite 2-D point is stored as a nonzero vector \(X=[x,y,w]\) with \(w \ne 0\).

affine location \(= (x/w,\;y/w)\)

Projective Equivalence

\(X\) and \(\lambda X\) name the same point when \(\lambda \ne 0\).

Implementation Habit

Normalize only when a finite location is needed; keep raw representatives for incidence computations.

base space R^n embedded in representation space R^(n+1)ratios carry the finite location
Use the scale-ray picture to separate a homogeneous representative from the finite point it names. Any nonzero scalar multiple lies on the same representation ray, and the affine chart reads the point only after dividing by weight. This is why raw coordinate length is not geometric evidence.
Weight separates roles

Zero Weight Means Direction, Not Failure

Finite Points

\([x,y,w]\) with \(w \ne 0\) can be dehomogenized to a location.

\([3,6,3] \sim [1,2,1]\)

Points At Infinity

\([u,v,0]\) stores an attitude: the direction shared by a family of parallel lines.

\([u,v,0]\) has no finite affine chart

Reading Rule

Inspect the weight before naming the geometric object returned by a computation.

finite: w not zeroinfinite: w equals zero
Slow down at the zero-weight line. Students often hear point at infinity as a poetic phrase; here it is a precise direction carrier. Finite points have recoverable locations, while zero-weight representatives store attitudes. Parallelism becomes an incidence event instead of an exception branch.
Modeling contract

Add Representatives Only When The Weights Have Meaning

Meaningful Sum

When weights are barycentric or physical, add representatives and normalize the result.

\(\bar X = \frac{\alpha A+\beta B}{\alpha+\beta}\)

Direction From Difference

Equal finite weights cancel the extra coordinate and leave a zero-weight direction.

\(B-A=[\Delta x,\Delta y,0]\)

Caution

Projective scale is arbitrary unless the construction declares what the weight represents.

weighted addition supports affine combinationsunlabeled scale is not a measurement
Frame addition as a contract rather than a universal geometric operation. If the weights represent masses or barycentric coefficients, normalizing a sum has meaning. If the scales are arbitrary, the same array addition may be meaningless. The difference of equal-weight finite points is the clean direction example.
Blades from points

The Join Of Two Points Is The Line They Span

Direct Construction

\(L = P \wedge Q\)

Two point vectors span a 2-blade in homogeneous space.

Dual Coordinate Shadow

\(l=[a,b,c],\quad l\cdot X=0\)

Incidence Question

A test point belongs to the line when it satisfies the span condition or the dual equation.

join builds the smallest containing flatdual equations support fast point-line tests
Present the line as the span of two homogeneous point vectors. In coordinates the dual line equation is familiar, but the construction is more general: join creates a flat, and the incidence test asks whether another point belongs to that span or satisfies the corresponding equation.
Meet without special cases

Parallel Lines Meet At Their Shared Point At Infinity

Generic Meet

\(X = L_1 \vee L_2\)

In 2-D coordinates, this is the cross product of dual line vectors.

Finite Result

If \(w(X)\ne 0\), dehomogenize and draw an ordinary intersection point.

Infinite Result

If \(w(X)=0\), read the returned point as the common direction of the parallel lines.

parallelism becomes incidence at infinitybranch on interpretation, not on failed algebra
This slide is the first major payoff. Two nonparallel lines meet at a finite point, while parallel lines meet at a zero-weight point recording their shared direction. The operation does not fail; the interpretation changes according to the weight of the returned representative.
Grade shift

A k-Flat Becomes A k Plus One Blade

Point

0-flat in base space; vector in representation space.

Line

1-flat in base space; 2-blade such as \(P\wedge Q\).

Plane

2-flat in base space; 3-blade from three points.

Hyperplane

Often most convenient as a dual equation.

Direct Flat Pattern

\(F = P \wedge A_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A_k\)

One finite support point plus k directions carries offset and attitude.

direct blades encode spansthe grade is one higher than the base-space dimension
Move from lines to the general rule. A k-dimensional offset flat in the base space is represented directly by a k plus one blade in the homogeneous space. The extra spanning factor carries location information, while the other factors carry attitude. That grade shift is the organizing memory hook.
Two equivalent lenses

Direct Flats Span; Dual Flats Constrain

Direct Representation

Build the flat from incident points and directions.

\(F = P \wedge Q \wedge R\)

Dual Representation

Store an equation that vanishes on incident points.

\(f\cdot X=0\)

Transformation Rule

Direct factors move by \(M\); dual equations move by \(M^{-T}\).

same geometry, different storage and motion lawsinverse-adjoint protects incidence equations
Keep the two representations conceptually distinct. Direct blades are built from spanning points and directions; dual flats are equations that annihilate incident points. Both describe the same geometry, but they transform differently. This distinction prevents a common implementation bug when moving lines and planes.
Meet, join, residual

Incidence Becomes A Reusable Data Flow

Join

Smallest flat containing the inputs.

points \(\to\) line, line + point \(\to\) plane

Meet

Common subflat shared by the inputs.

line + plane \(\to\) point or direction class

Residual

A small scalar check confirms whether a point satisfies a dual equation.

line-plane incidence generalizes point-line testsclassification follows from the same algebraic factors
Now assemble the workflow. Joins build containing flats, meets recover common subflats, and residual tests confirm incidence. In three dimensions this includes line-plane meetings and Plucker-style line diagnostics. The main teaching point is generic data flow: fewer special cases, more algebraic classification.
Transformations

Affine Maps Preserve Infinity; Projective Maps Can Move It

Affine Matrix

\(\begin{bmatrix}A&t\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\)

Finite points stay finite and parallel directions remain at infinity.

Projective Matrix

\(\begin{bmatrix}*&*&*\\ *&*&*\\ h_1&h_2&1\end{bmatrix}\)

Weights may depend on position, so the affine chart can tilt.

Invariant

Incidence is preserved even when lengths, angles, and parallelism are not.

direct blades transform by outermorphismdual flats transform by inverse-adjoint
Contrast the last row of the matrix. Affine maps keep the line at infinity fixed, so parallelism is preserved. A projective map can move that line, so formerly parallel finite lines can acquire a finite vanishing point. Incidence remains trustworthy even when metric intuition changes.
Projective measurement

Cross Ratio Survives When Spacing Does Not

Four Collinear Points

Choose a projective basis on the line and compare two ratios of coordinates.

\((A,B;C,D)\)

Projective Invariant

The number is unchanged by any nonsingular homography that keeps the four images defined.

Why It Matters

Camera images distort distance, but collinearity and cross ratio can still carry usable information.

metric-looking, but projectiveuseful for reconstruction and rectification
Use cross ratio as the chapter's positive invariant story. The apparent spacing of four collinear points may change dramatically under a projective transformation, but the projective ratio survives. This is a useful measurement when a camera image has incidence but lacks an honest Euclidean ruler.
Beyond flats

Quadratic Incidence And Perspective Still Use Homogeneous Rules

Conic Form

A conic is not a flat blade, but homogeneous points satisfy a quadratic equation.

\(X^T C X = 0\)

Perspective Projection

For each scene point, join it with the camera center and meet the image plane.

Transformation

Homographies move conics by inverse pullback so transformed points still satisfy transformed equations.

flats use blades; conics use quadratic formsprojection is repeated join and meet
Explain conics as a boundary case for the blade language. Lines and planes are flats, but conics are quadratic incidence equations. They still fit homogeneous transformation rules, and perspective projection can be read as repeatedly joining a camera center to a point and meeting an image plane.
What the model does not give for free

The Extra Coordinate Is Not A Euclidean Metric

Common Mistake

Do not treat a dot product of raw homogeneous representatives as a Euclidean distance or angle.

Safe Metric Read

For finite points, dehomogenize first and then use the base-space metric.

\(\|p-q\| = \|(x_p/w_p)-(x_q/w_q)\|\)

Good Native Tasks

Incidence, affine maps, projective maps, vanishing points, cross ratios, and projection pipelines.

model limit: metric meaning is not automaticlater models make more Euclidean objects native
Make the warning explicit before students overuse the model. Homogeneous coordinates are excellent for incidence, affine maps, projective maps, and ratios, but a naive representation-space inner product is not Euclidean distance. Metric questions must be dehomogenized or handed to a richer model.
Chapter synthesis

Choose The Homogeneous Model For Incidence And Projection

Use It For

  • Finite points, directions, lines, planes, and k-flats.
  • Meet and join workflows with points at infinity.
  • Affine maps, projective maps, cross ratios, and camera projection.

Reach Elsewhere When

  • Distances, angles, circles, and spheres must be primitive.
  • Euclidean translations and rotations should share one metric operator.
  • Inner products need direct geometric interpretation.

Working Pipeline

embed \(\to\) join/meet \(\to\) transform \(\to\) dehomogenize if finite

Discussion Exercise

Given a graphics scene, label each object as finite point, point at infinity, direct flat, dual flat, projective transform, or metric query.
