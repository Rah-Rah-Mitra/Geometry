Model boundary

Directions Are Native; Locations Are Bookkeeping

What The Model Knows

  • Linear combinations of directions.
  • Metric overlap and orthogonality.
  • Oriented subspaces and rotors.

What It Does Not Know Structurally

  • Offset points, lines, and planes as primitives.
  • Translations as uniform sandwich operators.
  • Incidence among displaced objects.

Blades as directions

A \(k\)-Blade Is A Weighted Oriented \(k\)-Subspace

Blade Ladder

\(a\), \(a \wedge b\), \(a \wedge b \wedge c\)

Attitude, orientation, and weight travel together.

Dual Complement

\(B^\ast = B I_n^{-1}\)

A plane in \(R^3\) may be stored by its normal direction.

Important Constraint

Not every sum of \(k\)-vectors is a simple \(k\)-blade; decomposability is geometric data.

source theme: k-directions, duality, orthogonal complementsall objects pass through one origin
Treat blades as weighted oriented subspaces, not as decorative parallelograms. A vector is a one-direction blade, a bivector records an oriented plane element, and a trivector records volume orientation. The dual picture is important because a subspace can also be represented by its orthogonal complement.
Algebraic direction computations

The Geometric Product Keeps Overlap And Sweep Together

Split The Product

\(ab = a \cdot b + a \wedge b\)

Scalar Part

\(a \cdot b = |a||b|\cos\theta\)

Bivector Part

\(a \wedge b = |a||b|\sin\theta\,I\)

Computational Habit

A scalar angle alone forgets the plane \(I\). The product carries both the amount and the direction of rotation.

angular relationships are ratios of unit directional elementsnot just scalar trigonometry
This is the computational heart of the first half of the chapter. The scalar part of a product measures metric overlap, while the bivector part measures oriented sweep. Keeping both parts together prevents angle computations from losing the plane and orientation in which the angle lives.
Planar triangle laws

A Closed Direction Sum Becomes Oriented Area

Start With Edge Directions

\(a + b + c = 0\)

Wedge With Each Side

\(a \wedge b = b \wedge c = c \wedge a\)

One blade equation contains the sine law and the oriented area.

Area As A Plane Quantity

\(\Delta I = \frac{1}{2}a\wedge b\)
printed pp. 249-251: planar triangleswedge equations retain orientation
Present the triangle as a closed sum of three direction vectors, then show what happens when the same equality is wedged with each side. The classical sine law appears, but the blade equation is stronger because it still remembers the oriented plane and area element.
Contraction as metric geometry

Projection Splits A Direction Into In-Plane And Orthogonal Parts

Projection By A Unit Blade

\(v_{\parallel B} = (v \rfloor B)B^{-1}\)

Rejection Is Orthogonal

\(v = v_{\parallel B} + v_{\perp B}\), \quad \(v_{\perp B}\cdot v_{\parallel B}=0\)

Teaching Check

Projection is not a coordinate trick. It is a metric operation on directions and subspaces.

source summary: projection of k-directions by contractionorthogonality is encoded by the metric
The chapter summary stresses that orthogonal projection of k-directions is well defined by contraction. Use the picture to make that algebraic: a vector splits into an in-plane part and an orthogonal rejection. The bivector plane is a subspace with a metric, not merely a shaded patch.

Spherical trigonometry as rotor algebra

A Spherical Triangle Is Three Directions And Three Side Planes

Vertices As Unit Directions

\(\check a,\check b,\check c \in S^2\)

Side Bivectors

\(\check a/\check b=e^C,\quad \check b/\check c=e^A,\quad \check c/\check a=e^B\)

Cosine Laws From Grade Parts

\(e^C e^B e^A = -1\)
vertices are directions, sides are angular bivectorsunit-sphere area equals spherical excess
A spherical triangle is a triangle of directions on the unit sphere. Its vertices are unit vectors, its sides are bivector plane rotations, and its internal angles compare those side planes. The scalar cosine laws can be read from products, but the rotor and bivector data are the real geometry.
Computing with 3-D rotors

Known Plane Plus Known Angle Gives The Rotor Directly

Rotor Formula

\(R = e^{-I\phi/2}\)

The half-angle appears because vectors are rotated by sandwiching.

Action On A Direction

\(x \mapsto R x R^{-1}\)

Orientation Convention

The product \(I\phi\) is geometric; flipping \(I\) flips the signed angle convention.

printed p. 256: determining a rotor from plane and anglerotors preserve metric structure
A rotor is the even operator that rotates directions by sandwiching. When the rotation plane and angle are known, the formula is immediate, but the slide should stress the half-angle and orientation. The plane-angle product is the geometric quantity; changing orientation changes the sign convention consistently.
Exact and noisy construction

Direction Correspondences Can Determine Or Estimate A Rotor

One Unit Vector Pair

\(R = \frac{1+ba}{\sqrt{2(1+a\cdot b)}}\)

Frame Rotation

Images of enough basis directions determine the same orthogonal operator.

Noisy Data

Choose \(R\) minimizing \(\sum_i \|Ra_iR^{-1}-b_i\|^2\).

printed pp. 257-260: frame and noisy rotor estimationdirection pairs replace coordinate angles
The chapter gives several construction patterns. One unit direction and its image determine the smallest-turn rotor except at the antipodal case. A full frame determines a unique rotor, and many noisy direction pairs ask for a best-fit rotor. The slide links exact algebra to estimation practice.
Rotor logarithms

Interpolate Orientations By Scaling The Bivector Log

Relative Rotor

\(R_{\mathrm{rel}} = R_2 R_1^{-1}\)

Fractional Step

\(R(t)=e^{t\log(R_{\mathrm{rel}})}R_1\)

Why This Matters

The path remains in the rotor group, so every frame stays orthonormal.

printed pp. 258-259 and programming example 10.7.1roots of rotors use the logarithm
Interpolation should stay on the rotation group. Instead of averaging matrices or Euler angles, take the relative rotor, compute its logarithm, scale the bivector, exponentiate, and apply the partial rotation. The sampled frames show that every intermediate state remains orthogonal and length preserving.
Finite directional symmetry

Point Groups Are Sandwich Operators Around One Fixed Point

Reflection By A Unit Normal

\(x \mapsto -a x a\)

Even Products Rotate

\(R = ab,\quad x \mapsto R x R^{-1}\)

Cubic Example

Proper signed permutation symmetries produce 24 equivalent local directions.

printed pp. 254-256 and 267-268: crystallography implementationpoint group means fixed-point symmetry
Crystallography is a natural vector-space-model application because the symmetry is local around a fixed point. Reflections use unit normal vectors as versors, and even products generate rotations. The cube orbit gives a finite, inspectable example of equivalent directions produced by repeated sandwiching operations.
Estimation in the vector-space model

Camera Calibration Uses Direction Rays, Then Tracks Location Roles

After Internal Calibration

image point \(\leadsto\) unit camera ray

Alternating Unknowns

  • Depths along rays.
  • Camera translations and rotors.
  • World marker positions.

Warning

Locations enter by convention. The ray directions are native to the model; points and centers must be named carefully.

printed pp. 260-263 and 268-270: external camera calibrationrays are directions; centers are locations
External camera calibration is the chapter's cautionary application. Image measurements become ray directions after internal calibration, but camera centers and world markers are locations. The vector-space model can still support the computation if names, depths, translations, and ray directions are kept in separate algebraic roles.
Chapter synthesis

Use The Vector-Space Model When The Computation Is Directional

It Is The Right Model For

  • Angles, oriented areas, and subspace projections.
  • Rotors, logarithms, and orientation interpolation.
  • Fixed-point point groups and ray direction data.

It Starts To Strain For

  • Translated points, offset lines, and incidence.
  • Uniform representation of rotations and translations.
  • Circles, spheres, and contact conditions as primitives.

One Algebraic Checklist

direction \(\to\) blade \(\to\) contraction \(\to\) ratio \(\to\) rotor
