Join And Meet Are The Application Grammar

Join: \(A \wedge B\) builds the smallest projective object containing both inputs.
Meet: \(A \vee B\) recovers the shared part, often by dualizing a join.

Programming Translation

  • Two finite points join to a homogeneous line.
  • Two planes meet in a line when their normals are not parallel.
  • A camera image line back-projects to a ray plane.
Use this slide to fix the grammar. A join builds the smallest projective object containing the given ingredients, while a meet recovers their common part. Keep the language concrete: two points generate a line, two planes meet in a line, and every later camera construction is just this grammar with matrices attached.

A 3-D Line Stores Direction Plus Moment

Line Blade Split

For affine points \(p,q\): \(d=q-p\), \(m=p \times d\), and \(L=(d,m)\).

d = (2.5, 1.3, 1.3) m = (-0.65, 0.10, 1.15) d dot m = -2.22e-16

Implementation Checks

  • Moving \(p\) along the same line leaves \(m\) unchanged.
  • The direction is perpendicular to the moment.
  • The nearest point to the origin is perpendicular to \(d\).
Explain Plucker coordinates as the line blade seen through an implementation lens. The direction tells how the line points, and the moment tells how it misses the origin. The two checks on the slide are small but decisive: shifting along the line preserves the moment, and d dot m remains zero.
Line And Plane Incidence
Coordinate tests are shadows of blade incidence.

Incidence Becomes A Small Number Of Dot Products

Containment Test

For \(x(t)=p+t\,d\), the whole line lies in the plane when \(n\cdot d=0\) and \(n\cdot p-\delta=0\).

Meet Test

If \(n\cdot d\neq 0\), the parameter \(t=(\delta-n\cdot p)/(n\cdot d)\) gives the intersection point.

Why Homogeneous?

Blade products keep the same incidence story when points become image rays, planes become camera back-projections, and lines are represented by Plucker coordinates.

Incidence is where the algebra starts paying rent. Rather than branching over many special cases, the code asks whether a line direction is tangent to the plane and whether one point on the line satisfies the plane equation. The blade statement and the dot-product statement are two interfaces to the same test.

Frame the four line relationships as outputs of one representation rather than separate recipes. Intersecting and coincident lines are exact incidence cases, parallel lines expose a degenerate direction bivector, and skew lines produce a shortest connector. The connector being orthogonal to both directions is the computational certificate.
Projective Transforms
OpenGL-style matrix checks: view center maps to the origin.

4-By-4 Matrices Move Incidence, Not Just Coordinates

Point: \(X' \sim M X\)
Plane: \(\pi' \sim M^{-T}\pi\)
Line: \(L'= (M P)\wedge(M Q)\)

Invariant check: if \(P\) lies on \(\pi\), then \(M P\) lies on \(M^{-T}\pi\). A graphics matrix convention is correct only if the incidence survives.

Describe a homogeneous matrix as an adapter between algebra and graphics APIs. Points move by direct multiplication, planes move by inverse transpose, and lines may be rebuilt from transformed endpoints. The practical invariant is simple: incidence before the transform must still be incidence after the transform.
Vanishing Points
Parallel 3-D directions become projective image points.

A Vanishing Point Is The Image Of A Direction

Projective Rule

A direction is an ideal point \(D=(d_x,d_y,d_z,0)\). Under a camera, \(v \sim P D\).

Why It Matters

All 3-D lines sharing direction \(D\) project to image lines that pass through the same vanishing point \(v\), unless the direction lies parallel to the image plane.

Checkable Insight

Vanishing points are not artifacts of drawing perspective. They are ordinary projective images of homogeneous representatives with final coordinate zero.

Vanishing points are a good sanity check for projective thinking because nothing mystical happens at infinity. A family of parallel world lines shares an ideal direction; the camera maps that direction to a finite image point when it is not parallel to the image plane. The diagram asks students to see the point as an image of a direction.

Projection Is Linear Before The Final Division

Camera Map

World point \(X\) maps to image point \(x \sim P X\). Any \(\lambda x\) with \(\lambda \neq 0\) is the same ray.

Back-Projection

An image point and the camera center join to a 3-D ray. An image line back-projects to a plane of rays.

left image marker: (-0.247, -0.346, 1) camera center = null(P)
Emphasize that the image coordinate is only a representative of a ray. Scaling the homogeneous image vector does not change the observation, and dividing by the last coordinate is a display step. The projection matrix, its null-space camera center, and the back-projected ray are the same data organized three ways.

The Epipolar Constraint Is A Meet Statement

Incidence Chain

  1. Left image point chooses a left camera ray.
  2. Right camera center joins that ray into a plane.
  3. The right image sees the plane as a line.

Numeric Audit

\(\max_i |x_{R,i}^{T}F x_{L,i}| = 2.22\times 10^{-16}\)

Search Consequence

The matching right point is not searched across the whole image; it is constrained to the projective line delivered by the meet.

Use the epipolar slide to replace search intuition with incidence. A point in the left image chooses a ray, the right camera center joins that ray into a plane, and the right image sees a line. An Image Line Back-Projects To A Plane Of Rays

Dual Camera Move

Image line \(\ell\) gives world ray plane \(\Pi=P^T\ell\). Two views give \(L=\Pi_L\vee \Pi_R\).

Notebook Certificates

  • Absolute direction alignment with original: 1.0.
  • Endpoint plane residuals are at floating-point zero.
  • Recovered point on line: (-0.0879, 0.3741, 0.0569).
Line-based stereo is the dual version of point stereo. An observed image line back-projects to a whole plane of rays, so two views recover a spatial line by meeting planes. The alignment and endpoint residuals give students a numerical target: the recovered line is not just drawn, it is certified.
Interpolation
Blend representatives only after choosing a convention, then normalize and recheck.

Interpolation Must Respect Homogeneous Representatives

Point Representative Path

A projective point path can be sampled as \(X(t)\sim (1-t)X_0+tX_1\), followed by normalization when \(w(t)\neq 0\).

Transform Path

For camera or rigid-object motion, interpolate meaningful parameters or a group logarithm. Then test that transformed incidences and matrix conventions still hold.

Failure Mode

Averaging already divided image coordinates can hide a crossing through \(w=0\), where a finite point becomes an ideal direction.

Interpolation is included to keep the lecture honest about animation and calibration workflows. Homogeneous representatives can be blended only after a convention is chosen and must be normalized before interpretation. For rigid or camera transforms, interpolate meaningful parameters or a group logarithm, then recheck incidence instead of trusting the picture.
