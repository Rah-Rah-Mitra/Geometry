Geometric Algebra for Computer Science - Chapter 14

New Primitives For Euclidean Geometry

Conformal blades turn points, point pairs, circles, spheres, flats, rounds, tangents, dual forms, and incidence tests into one computational grammar.

direct rounds dual probes zero-size tangents linearized intersections
Open by framing the chapter as a change in the unit of computation. The conformal model does not merely add another coordinate trick; it lets points, point pairs, circles, spheres, flats, rounds, and tangents enter the same product language. Ask students to watch how incidence data and probe equations become two views of one primitive vocabulary.

Primitive Roster

points, flats, rounds, tangents
Separation Tests
flat: \(X \wedge \infty = 0\)
tangent: \(X^2 = 0\), but \(X \wedge \infty \ne 0\)
round: \(X^2 \ne 0\), finite size

Interpretation Is A Contract

  • A blade can be manipulated algebraically before it is drawn.
  • Direct form answers: what incidence data span the primitive?
  • Dual form answers: how does a point probe the primitive?
  • Orientation and weight travel with the blade.
Use this roster to settle terminology before formulas accumulate. A point is a null vector, a flat carries infinity, a round has finite squared size, and a tangent is the zero-size round with a direction attached. The visual point is that these are not separate data structures; they are blade families with different tests.

The Metric Engine

Point Lift And Probe

null points
Conformal Point
\(X = x + n_o + \tfrac12 |x|^2 n_\infty\)

Its squared norm is zero, but its inner product with another lifted point remembers Euclidean distance.

Distance Probe
\(-2(X \cdot Y)=|x-y|^2\)
Sphere Probe
\(S = C - \tfrac12 r^2 n_\infty\),   \(-2(X \cdot S)=|x-c|^2-r^2\)
Emphasize the dual role of the lifted point. It is a null vector encoding location, but it also makes distance into an inner product. Once students accept that a point can probe another point, the same act of probing a dual sphere naturally returns power, incidence, and signed inside-outside information.

The direct blade is best when observations define the object; parameter recovery is a later operation rather than the original representation.

Center-Radius Form

Dual Rounds As Probes

power values
Dual Sphere
\(S = C - \tfrac12 r^2 n_\infty\)

A point \(X\) lies on the sphere exactly when \(X \cdot S = 0\).

Power Function
\(\mathrm{pow}_S(x) = -2(X \cdot S)=|x-c|^2-r^2\)

Use Cases

  • fast incidence tests;
  • inside/outside classification;
  • sphere fitting by linear algebra;
  • power diagrams and radical axes.
Now reverse the direction of use. A center-radius description is most useful as a dual object because a point can test itself against it by one inner product. Highlight that the sign of the power value is meaningful: on-shell is zero, outside is positive, and inside is negative for the displayed convention.

Representation Choice

Direct And Dual Forms

build vs probe
Direct Round
\(R = p_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge p_k\)

Use when points or intersections span the primitive.

Dual Round
\(R^\ast \sim (C - \tfrac12 r^2 n_\infty)E^\perp\)

Use when the primitive is an equation, a test, or a fitted object.

Carrier Flat
\(R \wedge n_\infty\)

Stores the plane, line, or space in which the round lives.

The slide is a map for choosing a representation, not a demand to memorize signs. Direct form fits incidence construction, while dual form fits testing, fitting, and intersections expressed through dual meets. The carrier flat keeps orientation visible, and infinity tells the algebra whether a primitive is flat or round.

Limiting Intersections

Tangents Are Zero-Size Rounds

real - tangent - imaginary

Real Circle

The meet has positive squared radius.

Tangent Contact

The round has zero size but still has direction.

Imaginary Circle

The sign changes, but the algebraic object remains.

Use the three panels as a continuity argument. The same two-sphere meet passes from a real circle, to a zero-radius contact, to an imaginary circle. Tangents are therefore not infinitesimal exceptions; they are finite conformal elements whose squared size is zero while their weight and direction can remain nonzero.

Why Rounds Are Blades

Representative Paraboloid

2-D conformal picture
Lift
\((x,y) \mapsto (x,y,\tfrac12(x^2+y^2))\)

Inspection Targets

  • A lifted point lies on the paraboloid.
  • Its dual point behaves like a tangent plane.
  • A dual circle is a plane whose cut projects to a Euclidean circle.
  • Intersecting rounds becomes intersecting affine planes.
The paraboloid is only a visualization of part of the conformal model, but it is extremely useful. Lifted Euclidean points sit on the graph, a point determines a tangent plane, and a dual circle appears as an affine plane cutting the graph. The ordinary circle is the shadow of that plane section.

Meet Geometry

Intersections Become Linear

circle meet as point pair
Dual Meet
dual circles: \(\kappa_1, \kappa_2\)
direct intersection: \((\kappa_1 \wedge \kappa_2)^{-\ast}\)
Why This Helps

Two round equations in Euclidean space become two linear plane objects in the lifted picture. The meet is computed once, then classified as real, tangent, or imaginary.

Same Pattern In 3-D

  • sphere and plane meet in a circle;
  • two spheres meet in a circle or tangent;
  • circle and plane meet in a point pair;
  • line is a circle through infinity.
Make the key simplification explicit. In Euclidean coordinates, intersecting circles looks quadratic. In the conformal lift, the same circles are represented by planes, so their meet is a line. Where that line cuts the paraboloid we recover a point pair; if it misses, the algebra still reports an imaginary pair.

Power Geometry

Power, Voronoi, Delaunay

paraboloid lift
Power Cell
\(\operatorname{pow}_i(x)=|x-c_i|^2-r_i^2\)

Cell boundaries are radical axes because the common \(|x|^2\) term cancels.

Conformal Reading

  • Each site is a lifted point or dual circle.
  • Comparing powers is comparing inner products.
  • Delaunay edges are the dual network to Voronoi edges.
  • The convex hull of lifted sites is not a trick; it is the conformal picture exposed.
Connect the chapter to computational geometry. Ordinary Voronoi cells compare squared distances, while power diagrams compare squared distance minus radius squared. The paraboloid lift explains why the boundaries are linear after cancellation, and duality explains why the Delaunay triangulation appears as the convex hull of lifted sites.

Noisy Data

Fitting Spheres And Planes

dual primitive by SVD
Linearized Sphere Equation
\(x^2+y^2+z^2+a x+b y+c z+d=0\)
Conformal Fit

Collect point rows, apply the conformal metric, and read the smallest singular direction as a dual primitive.

Important Case

Coplanar data should return a plane, not an exception. In conformal geometry, a plane is a sphere whose carrier includes infinity.

The fitting example shows the practical value of dual rounds. Noisy points become rows in a linear algebra problem, and the smallest singular direction gives the dual primitive. A plane is not a failure here; it is the conformal answer when the best sphere has moved through infinity.

Lecture Recap

Carry Forward

chapter 14 checks
Modeling Rule

Choose a conformal primitive that preserves the question: direct for span and incidence data, dual for probing and fitting, tangents for zero-size contact, flats when infinity is structural.

Sanity Checks To Keep

  • conformal point: \(X^2 = 0\);
  • distance: \(-2X \cdot Y\);
  • round incidence: \(X \cdot R^\ast\);
  • classification: sign of squared size;
  • fitted primitive: residual and kind.
Close with the modeling message. Chapter 14 is less about collecting exotic objects than about reducing casework. Once circles, spheres, point pairs, flats, rounds, dual forms, and tangents are native blades, later construction algorithms can combine probing, fitting, transformation, and intersection without leaving the conformal algebra.
