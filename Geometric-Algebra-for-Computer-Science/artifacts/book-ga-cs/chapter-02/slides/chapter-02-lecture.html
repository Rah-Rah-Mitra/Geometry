Section 2.1

The Nonmetric Contract

withheld: length withheld: angle withheld: normals

Allowed Structure

  • Vector addition and scalar multiplication.
  • Linear dependence and independence.
  • Orientation from ordered spanning data.
  • Relative full-grade weights in one carrier.

Delayed Until Chapter 3

  • Lengths of unrelated vectors.
  • Angles between attitudes.
  • Perpendicular complements and metric duals.
Set the rule of the game before formulas appear. In this chapter, length, angle, perpendicular normal vectors, and orthonormal bases are deliberately withheld. That restriction is productive because it reveals which geometric facts come from spanning alone: dependence, orientation sign, incidence, and relative full-grade measurement inside a common carrier.
Line Elements

Attitude, Orientation, And Weight

same vocabulary, every grade

Attitude

The occupied homogeneous subspace: line, plane, or volume carrier.

Orientation

The sign-sensitive ordering on that carrier.

Weight

A relative amount within the same attitude, not a metric comparison across attitudes.

Use the line element as the smallest example of the vocabulary that will scale to higher grades. Attitude is the carrier line, orientation chooses one of the two senses along that carrier, and weight records relative amount. Stress that the same three words will describe bivectors and trivectors without changing language.
Section 2.3

The Outer Product Builds Oriented Area

\( \wedge : V \times V \to \bigwedge^2 V \)

Algebraic Rules

  • Antisymmetry: \(a \wedge b = -b \wedge a\).
  • Scaling: \(a \wedge (\beta b) = \beta(a \wedge b)\).
  • Distributivity over vector addition.
repeated direction -> zero new span
Introduce the outer product as the operation that turns spanning into an algebraic object. Antisymmetry makes repeated directions vanish, bilinearity makes area scale correctly with either vector, and associativity lets the same construction climb from line elements to oriented area and volume elements.
Sign Discipline

Orientation Lives In The Order Of Factors

\(a \wedge b = - b \wedge a\)

A sign change can preserve the same attitude while reversing the orientation carried by the blade.

This is the first sign discipline students must internalize. Swapping vector factors does not draw a different carrier plane, but it reverses the orientation of the same plane element. The algebraic minus sign is therefore not decoration; it is the encoded difference between two traversal orders.
Bivector Reshapeability

A Bivector Is Not Anchored To One Boundary

same attitude + same orientation + same weight

The Algebra Keeps

Carrier plane, orientation, and relative area weight.

The Drawing Adds

A corner, a boundary, and a particular shape chosen only for visualization.

Separate the bivector from any favorite parallelogram. A drawn boundary is only a representative used by the screen. Shearing the factors can preserve the same oriented area element in the same carrier, so the algebra remembers attitude, orientation, and weight while forgetting the accidental corner shape.
Section 2.5

Area Ratios Solve Coordinates In A Plane

\(x = \alpha a + \beta b\)
\(\alpha = {x \wedge b \over a \wedge b}\)
\(\beta = {a \wedge x \over a \wedge b}\)

The numerator and denominator are same-attitude bivectors, so their ratio is a scalar.

Connect the wedge product to a familiar linear algebra task without letting coordinates take over. If x is decomposed in the basis a and b, wedging with the opposite basis vector kills one coefficient. The remaining ratio compares two same-attitude bivectors, which is why the result is scalar before a metric appears.
Planar Application

Offset Line Intersections Still Use Area Ratios

anchors + directions -> one intersection

Set Up

Line one uses anchor \(p\) and direction \(u\). Line two uses anchor \(q\) and direction \(v\).

\(p + \lambda u = q + \mu v\)
solve \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) by comparing planar bivectors

The test is oriented and affine, but it still does not need distances to either line.

Show that the same area-ratio idea is not confined to origin-based examples. Offset planar lines are reconstructed from anchors and directions by comparing oriented parallelograms in one plane. The computation uses differences and bivector ratios, not normal vectors or Euclidean distance to the lines.
Blades As Incidence Objects

Direct Subspace Representation

\(x \in A \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad x \wedge A = 0\)

Line Blade

\(x \wedge a = 0\) means \(x\) and \(a\) fail to span area.

Plane Blade

\(x \wedge (a \wedge b) = 0\) means adding \(x\) fails to span volume.

\(A \wedge B = 0\) detects shared direction, not automatic containment of one whole blade in another.

Make the containment test feel like incidence geometry. A candidate vector lies in a blade-represented subspace exactly when adding it by the outer product fails to raise the span. The zero result means the direction was already present, but it must be interpreted in the grade context that produced it.

Use the grade ladder to organize the new objects. Grade zero is not outside the algebra; it is the scalar layer. Section 2.9

Every Blade Is A k-Vector; Not Every k-Vector Is A Blade

\(e_{12} + e_{34}\) is the warning example

Blade

A factored outer product \(a_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge a_k\) represents one flat k-dimensional subspace with orientation and weight.

General k-Vector

A homogeneous grade-k multivector can be algebraically valid without representing one direct subspace.

for \(B=e_{12}+e_{34}\), the containment test has no nonzero vector
Pause on the distinction because later models use four and five algebraic dimensions. Every blade is a homogeneous k-vector, but a sum of k-blades may fail to represent one flat k-dimensional subspace. The four-dimensional example e12 plus e34 is valid algebra but not a single plane.

Compare two full-grade elements that live in the same attitude.

Not Yet Allowed

Clarify what measurement the chapter does and does not allow. Two full-grade elements in the same carrier differ by a scalar, so ratios of areas or volumes are meaningful. Comparing different carriers requires a metric that tells how attitudes relate, which is exactly why Chapter 3 comes next.
Monadic Operations

Reversion And Grade Involution Are Sign Machines

Grade k Example Reverse sign Grade involution sign
0\(\alpha\)++
1\(a\)+-
2\(a \wedge b\)-+
3\(a \wedge b \wedge c\)--
4\(e_{1234}\)++

Reversion

Reverse the order of vector factors. The sign is \((-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\).

Grade Involution

Flip every odd grade. The sign is \((-1)^k\).

Present reversion and grade involution as simple sign bookkeeping that becomes important later. Reversion reverses vector-factor order, so its sign depends on the number of swaps needed. Grade involution flips odd grades. Both operations extend linearly, which makes them reliable on full multivectors rather than only on pure blades.
Programming Lab 1

Drawing Bivectors: Rotation Makes Sign Visible

\(B(\theta)=e_1 \wedge (\cos\theta\,e_1+\sin\theta\,e_2)\)

Inspection Target

Watch the signed \(e_{12}\) coefficient pass through zero as the moving vector crosses the fixed direction.

\(B(\theta)=\sin\theta\;e_{12}\)

The drawn patch changes color because the orientation has changed, not because a different metric length has been measured.

Tie the first lab back to orientation. With v1 fixed and v2 rotating, the bivector coefficient crosses zero at parallel alignment and changes sign after the crossing. The picture is a computational version of antisymmetry, not an illustration pasted onto the algebra after the fact.
Programming Lab 2

Backface Culling Is A 2-D Outer Product Test

\((b-a) \wedge (c-a)\) decides screen orientation
\(s = \operatorname{coord}_{e_{12}}\big((b-a)\wedge(c-a)\big)\)

If \(s > 0\)

The projected vertices follow the chosen front-facing orientation.

If \(s < 0\)

The polygon is back-facing and can be skipped before rasterization.

Use the graphics exercise as an immediate payoff. A projected triangle is front-facing or back-facing according to the sign of the 2-D outer product of two edge vectors from the same anchor. This test lives entirely in the projected plane, so no 3-D normal vector is needed.
Programming Lab 3

Trivectors Detect Wrapped Vector Fields

sum normalized surface triangles on the unit sphere

Procedure

Sample the field on a cube, normalize vectors, triangulate the direction samples, and add the resulting trivectors.

radial point score: \(0.9533\)
empty cube score: \(0.00078\)

A score near one signals a wrap around an isolated point singularity.

Frame the third lab as oriented volume doing topological work. Normalize field samples on a cube boundary, triangulate the resulting directions on the unit sphere, and sum small trivectors. A wrap around a point singularity produces a score near one, while an empty region cancels almost to zero.
Recap

What Carries Into Metric Products

Outer Product

Builds a span and records orientation by factor order.

Blades

Represent direct homogeneous subspaces when factored.

Grades

Separate scalars, vectors, bivectors, trivectors, and top elements.

Local Measurement

Allows ratios only inside the same full-grade carrier.

The habit to keep: ask whether a later computation is using spanning information, metric information, or both.

Close by distinguishing spanning from measuring. Chapter 2 gives the outer product, grades, orientation signs, blades, and same-carrier ratios. Chapter 3 adds metric products so different attitudes can be compared by lengths, angles, projections, and duals. That separation is the mental model students should carry forward.
