Metric Setup

A Null Basis Buys A Euclidean Metric

The conformal model adds two null directions to ordinary Euclidean space: origin-like \(n_o\) and infinity \(n_i\).

Representational Metric

\(e_1\)\(e_2\)\(e_3\)\(n_o\)\(n_i\)
\(e_1\)10000
\(e_2\)01000
\(e_3\)00100
\(n_o\)0000-1
\(n_i\)000-10
Euclidean part: positive Added pair: null Cross term: metric memory
Spend time on the metric table before using any formula. The Euclidean basis vectors behave as usual, but the two added directions are null and coupled to each other. That single negative cross term is the mechanism that lets a quadratic Euclidean distance reappear as a bilinear conformal inner product.
Point Representation

Embedding Points On The Null Cone

A finite point becomes a null vector with unit weight and a quadratic infinity coefficient.

Embedding Formula

\(X = x + n_o + \frac{1}{2}\lVert x\rVert^2 n_i\)

Three Immediate Tests

  • \(X^2 = 0\): finite points are null.
  • \(-X \cdot n_i = 1\): normalized point weight.
  • The Euclidean \(x\) is recovered from the finite part after normalization.

Why The Quadratic Term?

It stores \(\lVert x\rVert^2\) so that later products can recover squared Euclidean distances bilinearly.

Use the cone drawing to make the lift feel geometric rather than syntactic. A finite point does not become just a coordinate vector with two extras attached; it lands on a null cone with a fixed weight. The quadratic infinity coefficient is precisely what stores enough information for later distance recovery.
Metric Predicate

Distance Is An Inner Product After Normalization

The conformal metric turns squared distance into a scalar product between lifted point representatives.

Normalized Point Identity

\(-2 X \cdot Y = \lVert x-y\rVert^2\)

Weighted Representatives

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are scaled representatives, divide by their weights before interpreting the scalar as a Euclidean distance.

\(d^2(x,y)=\frac{-2 X\cdot Y}{(-X\cdot n_i)(-Y\cdot n_i)}\)

Operational Payoff

Any operator preserving the conformal metric and \(n_i\) preserves the recovered Euclidean distances automatically.

Emphasize that the raw representative is projective in spirit, so weights matter. Once points are normalized, the conformal inner product gives squared Euclidean distance without returning to a special distance subroutine. This is the first operational payoff: metric facts become product-level facts.
Planes And Spheres

Vectors Become Geometric Probes

A vector can represent a point, a dual plane, or a dual sphere; pairing it with \(X\) evaluates a geometric predicate.

Point Probe

\(P \cdot X = -\frac{1}{2}d^2(p,x)\)

Dual Plane

\(\pi = n + h n_i,\quad \pi \cdot X = n\cdot x - h\)

Dual Sphere

\(S = C - \frac{1}{2}r^2 n_i,\quad -2 S\cdot X = \lVert x-c\rVert^2-r^2\)

Implementation Habit

Keep each representative's weight explicit before treating a scalar residual as a Euclidean fact.

Present point, plane, and sphere probes as one family of zero tests. The object is not interrogated by a custom class method; it is represented by a vector and paired with a lifted point. The sign of the result then carries side, inside-outside, or distance information depending on the vector.
Incidence Grammar

Joins And Meets Share The Same Grammar

Outer products build objects from points; duality lets intersections be expressed by the same algebraic language.

Direct Joins

line: \(L = P(a)\wedge P(b)\wedge n_i\)

plane: \(\Pi = P(a)\wedge P(b)\wedge P(c)\wedge n_i\)

Round Joins

sphere: \(S^\ast = P(a)\wedge P(b)\wedge P(c)\wedge P(d)\)

Meet Pattern

Convert to dual representatives, combine with the outer product, then dualize back and inspect the resulting grade and residual.

Numerical Warning

Near-tangent or nearly parallel objects need tolerance checks; a tiny blade can mean geometry, degeneracy, or scale trouble.

Use this as the algebraic grammar slide. Joins build an object from spanning points and the infinity vector, while meets extract intersections through duality and outer products. Students do not need every blade grade memorized today; they need to see that span and intersection have a common syntax.
Distance And Angle Tests

Distance And Angle Predicates Are Auditable

Each Euclidean question becomes a scalar residual with a named representative, normalization, and expected sign.

Predicate Dashboard

point-point

\(-2X\cdot Y\) recovers squared distance.

point-plane

\(\pi\cdot X\) gives signed distance for unit \(n\).

point-sphere

\(-2S\cdot X\) gives sphere power.

plane-plane angle

\(\cos\theta = n_1\cdot n_2\) after normalizing normals.

contact

Zero residual plus tangent-grade check separates touch from crossing.

invariance

Transform first, then re-evaluate the same residual.

Audit Rule

Do not compare raw coordinate arrays when the represented object lives up to scale. Normalize the representative first.

Turn the predicate grid into a programming checklist. Every geometric question should name its representative, its normalization, and the scalar expected from an inner product or contraction. This prevents code from mixing unweighted representatives with Euclidean objects and makes angle or contact tests reproducible.
Versors And Motion

Euclidean Operators Preserve Infinity

Metric preservation is not enough for Euclidean behavior; the operator must also keep \(n_i\) fixed.

Conformal Action

\(X' = V X V^{-1}\)

In coordinates, the notebook checks the corresponding \(5\times5\) matrix shadow.

Euclidean Gate

\(M^T G M = G,\quad M n_i = n_i\)

Consequences

  • Embedded points map to embedded points.
  • Distances recovered from inner products are preserved.
  • Translations, rotations, and reflections sit in one operational family.
Make the preservation of the infinity vector the gatekeeper for Euclidean behavior. A conformal map may preserve angles while moving infinity, but then it is no longer an ordinary Euclidean motion. Rotations, translations, and reflections keep infinity fixed, so the distance identity remains valid after transformation.
Flats And Ideal Parts

Flats Separate Position From Direction

Including \(n_i\) in flat representations makes direction data independent of where the flat is placed.

Flat Join

\(L = P(a)\wedge P(b)\wedge n_i\)

Plane: \(P(a)\wedge P(b)\wedge P(c)\wedge n_i\)

Direction Test

Translations alter offset or moment data. Rotations alter the direction itself.

Homogeneous Continuity

The conformal model keeps the earlier ideal-point intuition but now attaches metric predicates and Euclidean motions to it.

Connect this slide back to the homogeneous and Plucker chapters. A line or plane has finite placement data and ideal direction data. Translations change the moment or offset, but not the ideal direction. That separation is why the infinity vector is not an ornament; it is part of the flat's Euclidean meaning.
Planar Reflection

Plane Reflection As A Uniform Operation

The dual plane vector is both an incidence predicate and the reflecting element.

Coordinate Shadow

\(x' = x - 2(n\cdot x-h)n\)

Conformal Reading

The same plane vector \(\pi=n+h n_i\) probes points and participates in the versor-style reflection action.

Checks

  • Signed plane distance changes sign.
  • Applying reflection twice returns the input.
  • The conformal metric and \(n_i\) are preserved.
Use reflection to show why the conformal model is operationally attractive. The dual plane vector is both a predicate and a reflecting element. Signed distances flip, applying the reflection twice returns the original object, and the same sandwich idea can act on richer objects than isolated points.
Rigid Body Motions

Motors Package Rigid Motion And Interpolation

Even conformal versors compose rotations and translations; logarithms turn motion into a generator for interpolation.

Motor Action

\(X' = M X \widetilde{M}\)

Composition

Rotations and translations multiply as one object, so a rigid pose is not split across unrelated APIs.

Interpolation

\(M(t)=\exp(t\log M)\)

Every \(t\) should produce another valid rigid motion.

Programming Check

Verify endpoints, orthogonality, determinant, and distance preservation instead of visually trusting a smooth path.

Treat the screw path as the proper-motion counterpart to reflection. Motors compose rotations and translations inside the conformal algebra, and logarithm-based interpolation follows a generator rather than blending matrices entry by entry. The crucial teaching point is that every intermediate pose should remain a valid rigid motion.
Differential Planar Reflection

Differentiating A Moving Mirror

When the mirror plane moves, its normal and offset both contribute to the reflected-point velocity.

Moving Plane

\(\pi(t)=n(t)+h(t)n_i\)

Derivative Structure

Differentiate the signed distance \(n(t)\cdot x-h(t)\) and the normal direction \(n(t)\).

Finite-Difference Audit

Compare the analytic velocity against reflected points at \(t-\epsilon\) and \(t+\epsilon\).

Common Pitfall

A mirror through the origin hides \(h'(t)\). Offset hinges make the missing term visible.

Use the moving mirror to reconnect Chapter 13 with geometric differentiation. When the plane rotates about an offset hinge, both the normal and the offset change. A finite-difference check is not a formality here; it protects against forgetting the offset derivative and misreading the velocity field.
Lecture Recap

Operational Euclidean Geometry

The conformal model is valuable when Euclidean questions can be answered by a small set of algebraic operations and residual checks.

Represent

Lift finite points as normalized null vectors.

Encode planes and spheres as dual probe vectors.

Build flats and rounds with joins that include the right ideal data.

Operate

Use inner products for distance, power, side, and incidence residuals.

Use meets and duals for intersections, with degeneracy tolerances.

Apply Euclidean versors, reflections, motors, and screw interpolation.

Audit

Check nullness, weights, and distance identity residuals.

Check metric preservation and fixed \(n_i\) for Euclidean operators.

Check reflection involution, motion endpoints, and differential residuals.

CGA pays two null dimensions so Euclidean geometry becomes a library of product-level tests.

Close by naming the practical contract. A conformal implementation should check nullness, weight, metric preservation, infinity preservation, incidence residuals, and motion endpoints. The chapter's theme is not more coordinates for their own sake; it is a representation where Euclidean questions become algebraic operations with inspectable residuals.
