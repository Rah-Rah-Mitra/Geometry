Null Coordinates

Conformal Point Bookkeeping

Metric idea:
\(e_o^2=e_\infty^2=0\), \(e_o\cdot e_\infty=-1\)
$$X(x)=x+e_o+\frac{1}{2}\lVert x\rVert^2 e_\infty$$
$$X(x)^2 = 0,\qquad -2 X(x)\cdot X(y)=\lVert x-y\rVert^2$$

Why This Matters

  • Finite points sit on the null cone.
  • Distance is stored as an inner product.
  • Linear conformal actions become ordinary geometric motions after normalization.

Translator

Translators Fix Infinity

Prototype:
\(T(a)=\exp(-a e_\infty / 2)\)
$$X' = T(a) X \widetilde{T(a)} = X(x+a)$$

Invariant Checks

  • \(X'^2=0\) for lifted finite points.
  • The conformal metric is preserved by the translator matrix.
  • The infinity vector is fixed, so straightness at infinity is unchanged.
finite displacement metric preserving fixes \(e_\infty\)
The translator is the baseline operator: visible motion, stable infinity.04 / 16
Treat translation as the least surprising conformal operator and use it to set the standard. The translator moves every finite point by the same vector while leaving the infinity direction fixed. The visual grid shift is deliberately simple so the algebraic invariant, not the drawing, carries the force.

Euclidean Motion

Rotors And Motors

Motor pattern:
\(M=T(a)R\)
$$R=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}B\right),\qquad M=T(a)R$$

What Survives

  • Point distances are preserved for motors without dilation.
  • Circles, lines, planes, and spheres are transported as whole objects.
  • The reverse \(\widetilde M\) supplies a cheap inverse when the versor is normalized.
$$M(A\wedge B) \widetilde M = (M A \widetilde M)\wedge(M B \widetilde M)$$
Motors convert point motion into object motion through covariance.05 / 16
Connect Euclidean rotors to motors by composing rotation and translation. The point samples move, but the circle remains a circle and the oriented line remains a line because the sandwich action is covariant. Emphasize that a motor is not merely a point transform; it transports whole geometric objects.

Scale Rotor

Dilations Are Null-Plane Boosts

Scale factor \(s>0\):
\(D_s=\exp(-\log(s)\,e_o\wedge e_\infty/2)\)
$$D_s X(x) \widetilde{D_s} \sim X(sx)$$

Finite Recovery

The embedded vector changes its null-coordinate weight. The finite point is recovered by normalizing the \(e_o\) coefficient back to one.

Present uniform scaling as a conformal operation rather than an affine afterthought. In the embedded point, the null coordinates trade weight, and finite coordinates appear scaled only after normalization. This is why the dilation has a clean exponential form in the plane spanned by the two null directions.

Noncommutativity

Order Locates The Center

Notebook check:
\(\Delta=(s-1)a\)
$$S\,T(a)\neq T(a)\,S$$
$$S(T_a(x))-T_a(S(x))=(s-1)a$$

Interpretation

  • The algebraic order records the center of dilation.
  • A missing commutator is often a geometry bug, not a rounding bug.
  • The notebook compares the two triangle images point by point.
Operator products carry geometry in their order.07 / 16
Use the noncommuting triangle paths as a concrete debugging test. Translating and then scaling is not the same as scaling and then translating because the translation vector is itself scaled in one order. The difference vector tells students where the dilation center has effectively moved.

Sphere Reflection

Inversion Is Sphere Reflection

Radial rule:
\(x'=c+r^2(x-c)/\lVert x-c\rVert^2\)
$$I_c^r(I_c^r(x))=x$$

Local Conformal Test

The Jacobian of inversion is a scalar times a Euclidean reflection, so tiny angles are preserved while orientation reverses.

Sphere reflection is the first non-Euclidean-looking conformal operator.08 / 16
Name inversion as reflection in a sphere and make the local conformal claim precise. Points move radially by reciprocal distance, the mirror circle is fixed, and applying the operation twice returns the original sample. The line-to-circle image prepares students for round objects changing visible type.

Object Transport

Sandwich Actions Preserve Round Families

Round objects:
points, point pairs, circles, lines, spheres, planes
$$A' = V A \widetilde V$$

The Type Can Change

  • A Euclidean line can become a circle through the inversion center.
  • A circle through the pole can become a Euclidean line.
  • The extended round family stays closed under conformal versors.
$$V(A\wedge B\wedge C)\widetilde V = A'\wedge B'\wedge C'$$
Covariance lets object-level code share the point-level operator.09 / 16
Slow down on object covariance. A line may become a circle after inversion, but it stays inside the extended family of round objects. The same sandwich action transports the object representation, so incidence and angle checks remain meaningful even when the Euclidean category label changes.

Inversion - Translation - Inversion

Transversions Bend The Grid

Product view:
\(K(a)=I\,T(a)\,I\)
$$x\mapsto I(T_a(I(x)))$$

Mobius Behavior

  • Infinity is no longer a fixed background object.
  • There is a finite pole where ordinary coordinates run outward.
  • The inverse is tested by changing the transversion parameter sign.
$$K(-a)\,K(a)\; \text{returns ordinary samples}$$
Transversions show how conformal maps bend global shape while preserving local angles.10 / 16
Define a transversion as inversion, translation, and inversion again. It looks nonlinear in the plane because infinity is no longer held fixed, yet it is still conformal away from the pole. Ask students to identify the inverse check: changing the parameter sign brings ordinary samples back.

Repeated Action

Similarity Orbits And Motors

Product family:
\(V=T R D\)
$$V=T(a)R(\theta)D_s$$

Orbit Reading

  • The shell trail is a sequence of round objects, not a sequence of unrelated curves.
  • Dilation creates radial drift; the rotor creates turn; the translator moves the center.
  • A circular motor orbit can sweep a torus in three dimensions.
$$\max_n \operatorname{roundness}(C_n) \approx 0$$
Iteration turns conformal operators into geometric dynamics.11 / 16
Move from one-shot actions to repeated actions. Combining a translator, rotor, and dilation gives a similarity motor whose orbit can generate shell-like trails of circles. The important invariant is not the artistic spiral; it is the repeated circle-fit residual that stays small at every iteration.
