Coordinate Kernel

A Nonlinear Product Starts With Sparse Basis-Blade Arithmetic

Dense Storage, Sparse Thinking

A multivector may be stored as a dense coordinate array, but almost every product loop should skip zeros and reason about blade masks.

for I where a[I] != 0 for J where b[J] != 0 c[K] += sign * metric * a[I] * b[J]

The same accumulator feeds geometric products, outer products, inner products, delta products, matrix inverses, and classification checks.

Storage format is not the algorithm.
Use this slide to separate representation from computation. Students may hear dense array and imagine dense algebra, but the product loops should still walk only occupied coordinates. Emphasize that nonlinear routines reuse this kernel repeatedly, so a small sign, grade, or metric mistake here becomes a false inverse, false blade, or wrong meet later.
Geometric Product

Sign Counting And Metric Contractions Decide Each Term

Kernel Formula

\(e_I e_J = \sigma(I,J)\prod_{i\in I\cap J} g_i\, e_{I\oplus J}\)

  • \(\sigma(I,J)\): parity from moving basis vectors into canonical order.
  • \(g_i\): metric square applied only to duplicate basis vectors.
  • \(I\oplus J\): mask of surviving basis vectors.

This is the mechanical source of grade changes, scalar collapses, and delta-product top grades.

Every nonlinear diagnostic eventually asks whether this term arithmetic was correct.
Work through the displayed example slowly. The useful habit is to separate three decisions: count swaps for sign, contract repeated basis vectors through the metric, and accumulate into the mask produced by exclusive-or. This is the engine underneath the versor scalar check, the generic inverse matrix, and the delta product grade selection.
Outer And Inner Products

Grade Filters Turn The Same Kernel Into Different Products

Outer Product

\(A_r \wedge B_s = \langle A_r B_s\rangle_{r+s}\)

If basis masks overlap, the candidate term has a repeated factor and the exterior term is zero.

Inner Product

\(A_r \rfloor B_s \approx \langle A_r B_s\rangle_{s-r}\)

A contraction is a selected grade after the geometric product has applied the metric to shared factors.

Delta Product

\(A\,\Delta\,B = \langle AB\rangle_{\max}\)

The highest surviving grade carries the factors not shared by both blades.

Products share a kernel; algorithms differ by the filters they enforce.
Make the grade filter the main idea. The outer product is not a separate mysterious multiplication; it is the geometric product with the grade that represents span growth. The inner product keeps contraction grades after the metric has acted. The delta product used later is another grade filter, but it chooses the highest nonzero grade.
Inversion

Use Structure Before Asking For A General Inverse

Fast Path: Versor

\(V^{-1} = \widetilde V / \langle V\widetilde V\rangle_0\)

  • Reverse cancels vector factors in the opposite order.
  • The denominator should be scalar.
  • A residual check decides whether the fast path is justified.
Fallback: Matrix Solve

\(L_A x = 1\)

  • Build left multiplication by \(A\).
  • Invert or solve the resulting linear system.
  • Conditioning and roundoff become part of the report.
The inverse is nonlinear, but the right fast path is a structural question.
Contrast the two inverses as engineering choices. A versor inverse is cheap because the object is already a product of invertible vectors, so the reverse recovers the inverse up to one scalar. The general multivector inverse is valuable as a fallback and comparison, but it should report conditioning and identity error rather than pretending to be equally stable.
Elementary Functions

Function Algorithms Split By Scalar-Square Cases

Implementation Split
  • Blades and many versor generators often have scalar squares.
  • Special cases use trigonometric or hyperbolic closed forms.
  • General bivectors may require a polynomial series with rescaling.
  • Logarithms are best treated as case libraries, not one universal routine.

This mirrors the chapter principle: exploit recognized structure, then fall back carefully.

Closed forms are algorithmic shortcuts justified by algebraic structure.
This slide prevents the lecture from making inverse, meet, and join feel like the only nonlinear operations. The same design rule applies to exponentials: if the square is scalar, choose the closed form; otherwise scale the input, evaluate a bounded series, and square back. Logarithms are presented as specialized families rather than a solved general problem.
Classification

Blade Or Versor Is A Behavioral Test, Not Just A Grade Label

Three Checks
  1. Does the reverse-based candidate behave like an inverse?
  2. Does sandwiching every basis vector preserve grade one?
  3. If it passes, is the multivector pure grade?

Pure grade is the last check, not the first conclusion.

Classification tests the action of the multivector.
Stress that classification is not a cosmetic label for display. The implementation must know whether fast inverse formulas and geometric transformations are legitimate. A uniform-grade test alone is tempting and wrong, so the algorithm first checks inverse behavior and the grade-one action on vectors before deciding whether the surviving object is a blade or a more general versor.
Blade Factorization

Recover Vector Factors From Additive Coordinates

What Factorization Returns
  • A list of vector factors for the represented subspace.
  • A separate scale, so unit factors do not hide magnitude.
  • A reconstruction residual, so the routine can refuse nonblades.

\(B \approx s\, f_1\wedge f_2\wedge \cdots \wedge f_k\)

The notebook uses an inspectable nullspace version; the lecture also names the projection loop from the source span.

A blade stored additively must be turned back into factors before many nonlinear routines are stable.
Explain why factorization is a recovery problem. The coordinate array has lost the explicit list of vectors, but meet, join, and geometric display often need those vectors back. The largest-coordinate basis blade gives stable candidates in the source algorithm, while the notebook nullspace view makes the same subspace condition inspectable through the equation x wedge B equals zero.

Delta Product

The Highest Grade Of \(AB\) Predicts Incidence

Grade Arithmetic

\(A\Delta B = \langle AB\rangle_{\max}\)

\(grade(A\cup B)=\frac{a+b+d}{2}\)

\(grade(A\cap B)=\frac{a+b-d}{2}\)

Here \(d\) is the grade of the delta product. Shared factors contract away; unshared factors remain visible at the top grade.

Delta product turns geometric product output into a meet/join grade prediction.
Connect this slide back to the grade-filter discussion. The delta product does not require first factoring both blades; the geometric product naturally contracts shared factors, and the highest remaining grade records what is unshared. Once that grade is known, the meet and join grades follow before we construct either object.
Meet And Join

Build Either The Meet Or The Join, Then Recover The Other

Algorithm Skeleton
S = dual(A Delta B) factor S into s_i M = 1 J = I_n for each s_i: project s_i onto A reject s_i from A grow M or shrink J stop when target grade is reached

The same factorization supplies both outputs, so the algorithm can terminate when either grade target is met.

Projection grows the meet; rejection shrinks the join.
Walk students through the loop as a paired search. A candidate factor either has a projection that belongs in the common part, or a rejection that helps remove directions outside the join. The practical point is that the routine is allowed to stop early when one target grade is reached, then compute the companion product from the meet or join relation.
Robustness

Thresholds Decide When Almost Common Means Common

Numerical Contract
  • Projection or rejection equal to zero becomes projection or rejection below tolerance.
  • Principal angles expose whether two factors are shared or merely close.
  • Tests must cover the transition zone, not only exact incidence.

gap \(= 1-\cos(\theta)\)

Changing tolerance can legitimately change the classified meet grade for nearly coincident inputs.

Robust nonlinear products include tolerances in their public behavior.
Use this as the implementation realism slide. Symbolic geometry says two subspaces either share a factor or they do not. Floating point geometry says the decision depends on scale, noise, and tolerance. The threshold plot should make clear why tests around nearly coincident planes are more informative than tests that only use exact coordinate axes.
Sparse Accumulation

Grade Filters Need Auditable Accumulators

What To Log In A Kernel Test
  • Input masks \(I,J\), output mask \(K\), and output grade.
  • Swap parity, metric contraction factor, and coefficient product.
  • Accumulator collision cases where several pairs land in the same coordinate.
  • Final grade projection used by outer, inner, and delta products.

This evidence makes nonlinear failures traceable instead of mysterious.

Sparse accumulation is where algebraic signs become executable evidence.
This slide is a bridge from mathematical formulas to debuggable code. When a nonlinear routine fails, the error often entered through a product bucket much earlier. Encourage logging or table-driven tests that show masks, signs, contractions, and grade filters for representative collisions, because those records make meet and inverse bugs far easier to localize.

Recap

Debug Nonlinear Products By Asking What Structure Was Recognized

Implementation Checklist
  • Can the product kernel explain each sign, contraction, output mask, and grade?
  • Does the object justify a specialized inverse or exponential path?
  • Can a blade be reconstructed from recovered factors and scale?
  • Does the delta product predict the same meet and join grades the construction returns?
  • Are tolerance choices visible in near-degenerate cases?
The practical win is a geometry kernel that reports why it trusts a nonlinear result.
End by giving students a debugging ritual rather than a memory dump. When a nonlinear product looks wrong, ask which structure the implementation claimed to recognize and what evidence supported that claim. The best routines expose product-kernel records, inverse residuals, factorization residuals, delta grade predictions, and threshold decisions, so failures become diagnosable instead of opaque.
