Construction verbs

Join Builds Objects; Meet Solves Shared Constraints

Join

Use samples to span the intended primitive: point pair, line, circle, plane, or sphere.

Meet

Use dual constraints to ask for the common object satisfying all incidences.

Plunge

When ordinary intersection is not real, keep the algebraic object and classify it.

meet/join operations drive the construction workflownotebook route: meet-and-plunge
Present join and meet as complementary construction verbs. A join records the simplest object supported by given samples, while a meet asks which object satisfies several constraints at once. The diagram deliberately shows both directions so students do not identify construction with only intersection or only fitting.
Intersection classification

Two Real Points

Return a point pair and check both points against the line and circle.

Tangent Point

The limiting case is classified after the same meet construction.

Imaginary Pair

The algebra still stores where the intersection would continue through tangency.

Walk through the three circle-line cases as one discriminant story. The same algebraic meet can produce two real points, one tangent point, or an imaginary pair. The implementation lesson is to classify the result after construction, because the limiting tangent case should not require a different geometric pipeline.

Ordinary Meet

When constraints intersect in real Euclidean points, the meet draws the expected object.

Plunge Outcome

When the real intersection disappears, the algebraic construction remains a point pair, circle, or sphere with a sign telling the story.

Implementation Test

\(\max_i |\,\mathrm{power}_{S_i}(x)\,|\)

The plunge is the chapter's way of keeping failed-looking intersections meaningful. When real objects do not intersect, the algebra can still return the simplest object contained in the constraints, often with imaginary radius or imaginary point-pair data. That retained information is useful for visualization and numerical continuation.
Euclidean nuggets

Tangency Is A Constraint, Not A Parameter Derivative

Tangent At A Point

\(T_p C:\quad p\in C,\quad (p-c)\cdot t=0\)

Carrier Check

\(t\cdot n_C = 0\)

Numerical Sanity

Finite differences should align with the algebraic tangent direction.

source span: tangents without differentiating, printed p. 445notebook artifact: tangent-without-differentiating
Stress that tangency is expressed by incidence plus a point contact, not by taking a derivative of a parameterization. At a point on a circle, the tangent line is perpendicular to the radius and remains inside the carrier plane. The finite-difference check is only a sanity test, not the definition.
Carriers and surrounds

A Round Factors Into A Flat Carrier And A Surround

Surround

The smallest round containing the object carries center and radius data.

Carrier

The flat part records the affine subspace in which the round lives.

Factorization Check

\(C = \mathrm{carrier}(C)\cap\mathrm{surround}(C)\)
source span: surrounds and factorization, printed pp. 446-447notebook artifact: round-factorization
Explain that a round object carries two pieces of geometry: its flat carrier and its surrounding sphere. Their meet recovers the original circle in the ordinary real case. This factorization is powerful because it makes carrier tests and radius tests separate, inspectable, and reusable in later constructions.

Input Object

Target Carrier

The projected object must satisfy the new carrier plane by residual test.

Algebraic Shape

project, classify, then draw

Projection is not merely dropping a shadow in a drawing. The point is to keep the projected object's defining equations visible after the visual operation.

Affine combinations

Linear Combinations Of Points Decode As Dual Spheres

Normalized Combination

\(V(\lambda)=\lambda P+(1-\lambda)Q\)

Decoded Center

\(c(\lambda)=\lambda p+(1-\lambda)q\)

Decoded Radius

\(r^2=-\lambda(1-\lambda)\|p-q\|^2\)
source span: affine combinations, printed pp. 447-448notebook artifact: affine-dual-spheres
Describe affine combinations as a warning that linear algebra on conformal points changes object type. Between two finite points the normalized combination decodes as a dual sphere whose center follows the Euclidean affine point and whose squared radius can become negative. This is a feature to inspect, not a paradox.

Robust construction cases

Construct First, Then Classify With Residuals

Policy

Build the algebraic object, decode the geometric class, and expose the tolerance used for the decision.

Common Failure Modes

  • Nearly tangent intersections flip sign under roundoff.
  • Concentric spheres do not define a stable radical circle.
  • Collinear samples cannot join to a unique circle.

Deliverable

A construction should return both geometry and diagnostics.

robust cases belong in classification, not in hidden drawing branchesresiduals make the branch auditable
This slide is the practical engineering checkpoint. Near tangency, near concentric spheres, and nearly collinear samples should be classified with tolerances and residuals, not hidden inside ad hoc branches. Students should leave with a policy: construct algebraically, classify geometrically, and report the residual that justified the branch.
