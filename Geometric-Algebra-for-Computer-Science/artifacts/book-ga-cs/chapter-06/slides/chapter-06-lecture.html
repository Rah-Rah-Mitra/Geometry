The Vector Product Carries Two Readouts

6.1

Definition

\(ab = a \cdot b + a \wedge b\)
  • Scalar part: metric alignment.
  • Bivector part: oriented plane area.
  • The full product is invertible when the divisor is non-null.

Inspection target: reversing order preserves the scalar part and flips the oriented area.

Use the vector diagram to separate two readings of the same product. The dot term measures alignment along a metric, while the wedge term records oriented area. The point is not that two old products have been packed together, but that the geometric product is the richer object being sampled.

Symmetric And Antisymmetric Parts

6.1.2

Symmetric Half

\(a \cdot b = \frac{1}{2}(ab + ba)\)

Order reversal cancels oriented area and leaves the metric readout.

Antisymmetric Half

\(a \wedge b = \frac{1}{2}(ab - ba)\)

Order reversal doubles the signed plane element instead of cancelling it.

The half-sum and half-difference formulas are the first diagnostic for noncommutativity. Ask students what changes when the order reverses. The scalar alignment survives the swap, while the oriented bivector changes sign, so symmetry and antisymmetry retrieve two geometrically different pieces.

Basis Rules Become The Product Engine

6.1.4

Euclidean \(E_3\) Basis Table

\(e_1\)\(e_2\)\(e_3\)
\(e_1\)\(1\)\(e_1 \wedge e_2\)\(e_1 \wedge e_3\)
\(e_2\)\(-e_1 \wedge e_2\)\(1\)\(e_2 \wedge e_3\)
\(e_3\)\(-e_1 \wedge e_3\)\(-e_2 \wedge e_3\)\(1\)

The table is the visual version of the notebook artifact `basis-product-table.json`.

Two Local Rules

\(e_i e_i = 1\)
\(e_i e_j = -e_j e_i\), for \(i \ne j\)
  • Repeated basis directions collapse through the metric.
  • Independent directions span a blade.
  • Canonical order records orientation by signs.
Treat the basis table as an implementation contract, not a memorization grid. Equal basis vectors collapse to the metric scalar, distinct basis vectors anticommute, and every larger product in a Euclidean orthonormal basis is built by distributing these local rules through the operands.

Dividing By A Vector

6.1.5

Local Inverse

\(a^{-1} = \frac{a}{a^2}\), when \(a^2 \ne 0\)

A non-null vector supplies its own inverse because its square is scalar.

\((xa)a^{-1} = x(aa^{-1}) = x\)
Vector division should feel surprisingly ordinary once a non-null vector squares to a scalar. Walk through encoding x by right multiplication and decoding by multiplying on the same side by the inverse. The side matters because geometric products are generally not commutative.

Ratios Of Vectors Act As Operators

6.1.6

Operator Reading

\(R = s^{-1}t,\quad x \mapsto xR\)
  • \(sR = t\) by construction.
  • Nearby probes inherit the same scale and plane turn.
  • No matrix is required; the algebra element stores the action.
A vector ratio is a compact operator: it carries one vector to another and lets nearby vectors ride along. The grid visualization shows scale and rotation together, previewing versors without introducing the full sandwich form. The algebra element stores the action directly.

Multivector Products Mix Grades

6.2

Distribute, Then Inspect

\(A = \langle A\rangle_0 + \langle A\rangle_1 + \langle A\rangle_2\)
\(AB = \sum_{r,s} \langle A\rangle_r \langle B\rangle_s\)

Products may lower grade, preserve grade, or raise grade depending on shared directions and new span.

Mixed grades are a feature rather than an error. When multivectors multiply, each grade in the left operand interacts with each grade on the right. Some directions cancel through the metric, others span new dimensions, and the resulting grade lanes tell us which geometric questions can be asked.

Subspace Products Retrieved By Grade

6.3

Vector Times Plane

\(vB = \langle vB\rangle_1 + \langle vB\rangle_3\)
Grade 1metric interaction with the plane, contraction-like
Grade 3new volume from the out-of-plane component

Notebook check: selected grades match the left contraction and outer product for the sample \(v(e_1 \wedge e_2)\).

For a vector times a plane, the grade-one part is the metric interaction with the plane and the grade-three part is the volume made by the out-of-plane component. This is the cleanest moment to argue that contractions and outer products can be retrieved as selected grades.

Blade Inverses Enable Geometric Division

6.4.1

Invertible Subspace Element

\(B^{-1} = \frac{\widetilde{B}}{B\widetilde{B}}\)

The reverse \(\widetilde{B}\) changes orientation order, while \(B\widetilde{B}\) becomes scalar for a nondegenerate blade.

  • Line blade: a non-null vector.
  • Plane blade: a nonzero bivector.
  • Division: compare objects to the subspace represented by \(B\).
Blade inverses make subspace division possible. Keep the language concrete: an invertible blade represents a nondegenerate oriented subspace, and division by that blade lets the algebra ask for components relative to the subspace without changing formulas for lines, planes, and higher-dimensional spans.

Projection, Rejection, Reflection

6.4.2 - 6.4.3

One Decomposition, Three Uses

\(x = x_{\parallel} + x_{\perp}\)
\(x' = x_{\parallel} - x_{\perp}\)
  • Projection lies in the represented subspace.
  • Rejection is orthogonal to that subspace.
  • Reflection flips only the rejected component.
Projection, rejection, and reflection are three readings of the same decomposition. The projected vector remains in the blade, the rejected vector is perpendicular to it, and reflection keeps one piece while changing the sign of the other. This visual makes the shared algebraic source visible.

  • Encode each blade by a basis-bit mask.
  • Use xor to find surviving basis directions.
  • Apply the metric when repeated directions collapse.
  • Count swaps needed for canonical order to get the sign.
