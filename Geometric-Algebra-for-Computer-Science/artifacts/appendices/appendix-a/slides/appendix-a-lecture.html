Bilinear Form

Metric As Measurement Contract

$$Q : V \times V \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad x \cdot y := Q(x,y)$$

Once the algebra defines \(Q\), every metric product must inherit the same measurement rule.

Emphasize that the bilinear form is the object that all metric products inherit. The coordinate formula is only a convenient diagonal basis view. In code, this means the metric should be stored with the algebra and used consistently by inner products, contractions, norms, and inverse checks.

Signature

Signatures Change The Geometry

Euclidean \( \mathbb{R}^{3,0} \)

All nonzero squares are positive. Length intuition is reliable.

Mixed \( \mathbb{R}^{1,1} \)

Sign is geometric information. Null vectors can be nonzero.

Degenerate \( \mathbb{R}^{2,0,1} \)

Zero square may signal missing measurement, not cancellation.

Use the three signature panels to separate Euclidean certainty from mixed and degenerate behavior. The same coordinate vector can be positive, negative, or null depending on the metric, so a bare array of numbers is not geometric data until the signature has been named.

Null Vectors

Null Lines In The Minkowski Plane

Reciprocal null pair
$$n_+ = \frac{e_1+e_2}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad n_- = \frac{e_2-e_1}{\sqrt{2}}$$
self squares

\(n_+ \cdot n_+ = 0\) and \(n_- \cdot n_- = 0\).

mutual pairing

\(n_+ \cdot n_- = 1\). Null does not mean orthogonal to all information.

The notebook check records both null self-squares and the nonzero mutual inner product.

Walk the class through the cancellation. The two diagonal contributions have opposite signs, so the lines where time and space coordinates balance are nonzero vectors with zero square. Stress that the reciprocal pair has mutual inner product one, which is the operational fact algorithms use.

Isotropic Cone

The Isotropic Cone

Three-dimensional mixed metric
$$x \cdot x = -t^2 + x^2 + y^2$$

The null set satisfies \(x^2+y^2=t^2\), so it forms a double cone through the origin.

Move from the two-dimensional null lines to the three-dimensional picture. The cone is the locus of zero square, not the zero vector. Inside and outside the cone the sign of the square changes, so normalization and rotor intuition must be checked against the metric before use.

Degeneracy

Degenerate Nulls Are Different

Mixed-signature null by cancellation

Zero square comes from opposite signs canceling inside a nondegenerate metric.

Degenerate null by rank loss

Zero square comes from missing measurement, so inverses and projections need care.

Make the comparison explicit. Mixed-signature null vectors are created by cancellation in a nondegenerate metric and can have reciprocal partners. Degenerate null directions are invisible because the metric lost rank. Those two cases can share square zero while supporting very different inverse and projection behavior.
Always report square and metric rank together7 / 10
Null Blades

Null Blades Need Inspection

Trap

A null factor is necessary for a null blade in this setting, but it is not a sufficient test.

Implementation habit

Compute the blade square or determinant-like Gram quantity directly. Do not classify a composite element only by scanning whether one factor is null.

$$B^2 \text{ depends on the pairings among all factors.}$$
Use this slide to prevent the common shortcut. A blade containing one null factor may square to zero, but a blade containing a reciprocal null pair need not be null. The square of the whole blade depends on the pairing structure among its factors, not just on each factor separately.

Conformal Preview

Conformal Null Basis Intuition

Why the appendix matters later

Conformal geometric algebra uses null directions as meaningful anchors. A Euclidean point is encoded as a null representative, not as a zero object.

$$P(x) = o + x + \frac{1}{2}\|x\|^2 n_\infty, \qquad P(x)^2 = 0$$
origin-like null

\(o^2=0\), chosen as one anchor.

infinity-like null

\(n_\infty^2=0\), paired with the anchor.

Connect the appendix to later conformal modeling without deriving the full model. The origin-like and infinity-like null directions act as anchors whose mutual pairing carries scale. Euclidean points become null representatives, so zero square is a feature of the encoding rather than a reason to discard data.
