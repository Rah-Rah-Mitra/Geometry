Why Divisors Are Not Enough

codimension matters
\(A^0\)whole \(X\)
\(A^1\)divisors
\(A^2\)surfaces cut again
\(A^n\)zero-cycles
Graded Product

\(A^r(X)\times A^s(X)\longrightarrow A^{r+s}(X)\). Intersections do not always become numbers; they first become higher-codimension classes.

Degree

For \(\dim X=n\), the numerical map is \(\deg:A^n(X)\to \mathbb Z\). Lower codimension data must be multiplied until it reaches the top.

Old model

On a surface, two divisors already land in \(A^2(X)\), so the product can be read as an integer. Threefolds and beyond need the whole ladder.

Appendix section 1: intersection theoryVisible grounding: \(A^rA^s\to A^{r+s}\), \(\deg:A^n\to \mathbb Z\)
Use this slide to slow down the first conceptual shift. Divisors are codimension-one objects, so they are only the first nontrivial layer of the Chow ring. On a surface, multiplying two divisor classes already reaches zero-cycles, which explains why the older surface theory looked purely numerical. In higher dimension, the product may be a curve class, a surface class, or another intermediate class before any degree can be taken.

Cycles And Rational Equivalence

legal movement
Cycles

A codimension \(r\) cycle is a finite formal sum \[ Z^r(X)=\bigoplus_{\operatorname{codim}Y=r}\mathbb Z[Y]. \]

Rational Equivalence

The Chow group is \(A^r(X)=Z^r(X)/{\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}}\). The quotient records which cycles can be moved through a rational one-parameter family.

Teaching rule

Replace equality of subvarieties by equality after allowed motion. Computation begins once a better-positioned representative is available.

Notebook anchor: rational equivalence feeds the Chow groups \(A^r(X)\)Source span: section 1 begins on printed page 425
Define cycles as deliberately formal objects: the coefficients remember multiplicity and sign, while the symbols remember irreducible subvarieties of fixed codimension. Rational equivalence is the permission slip for movement. It is the reason intersection theory can ask for a representative that meets another cycle properly without changing the class being computed. Stress that this quotient is not a technical nuisance; it is the mechanism that makes products robust.

Push, Pull, And The Diagonal

product machinery
Diagonal Product

For cycles in good position, the intersection product can be organized as \[ Y\cdot Z=\Delta^*(Y\times Z). \]

Functorial Control

Proper maps give \(f_*\), flat maps give \(f^*\), and the projection formula makes the two compatible with products.

Why this matters

The product is not a point-counting trick. It is a functorial operation that survives maps, equivalence, and local multiplicity corrections.

Appendix section 1: axioms for the Chow ringKey formula: \(Y\cdot Z=\Delta^*(Y\times Z)\)
This slide is the bridge from cycles as generators to the Chow ring as a computational object. The diagonal is the conceptual trick: a product inside \(X\) is pulled back from an external product on \(X\times X\). Pushforward, pullback, and the projection formula are the compatibility checks that stop the construction from depending on accidental choices. The graph should be read as a proof dependency map rather than a taxonomy.

Local Multiplicity Needs Tor

local algebra
Naive Length

The quotient length \(\operatorname{length}_A A/(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b)\) can fail when the local intersection carries hidden non-transverse or embedded algebra.

Tor-Corrected Multiplicity

\[ i(Y,Z;x)=\sum_i(-1)^i \operatorname{length}_A \operatorname{Tor}_i^A(A/\mathfrak a,A/\mathfrak b). \]

Instructor cue

Tor measures the failure of the naive tensor product to behave like a clean transverse intersection.

Source span: section 1 local multiplicitiesNo exercise text used; formula stated as the computational principle
Use Tor as the first moment where the appendix reveals how much algebra is underneath the geometry. Students may know length from curve intersections on surfaces, so explain why that model is too optimistic in general. The alternating Tor formula is a compact way to say that intersection multiplicity is not just the size of one quotient; it is the Euler characteristic of a local derived tensor product. That is exactly the kind of correction needed for the Chow product to be well defined.

When Singularities Break The Rules

hypotheses matter
Warning

Smoothness and proper-position hypotheses are not cosmetic. On singular spaces, cycles can meet at a point where the tangent and local-ring behavior no longer supports the same rules.

Quadric-cone lesson

Two rulings can be visually simple but algebraically delicate at the vertex. A product rule proven for nonsingular quasi-projective varieties should not be applied blindly.

How the appendix responds

It separates hypotheses, uses moving arguments when available, and later points to singular variants as a different branch of the theory.

Section 1 hypotheses: nonsingular quasi-projective varietiesComplements return to singular variants only after the smooth theory is built
This warning prevents a common overextension. The surface-looking picture of a cone is intentionally simple, because the point is not to solve a cone problem but to expose the role of hypotheses. Smoothness, proper intersection, and a usable moving lemma are doing real work in the Chow ring construction. When singularities enter, the local algebra and equivalence relations need different tools. That is why the appendix later treats singular Riemann-Roch as a separate generalization rather than a routine corollary.

Properties That Make Computation Possible

Chow toolkit

Picard

\(A^1(X)=\operatorname{Pic}X\) for nonsingular \(X\). Divisor classes sit inside the Chow ring.

Affine Invariance

\(A(X\times \mathbb A^m)\cong A(X)\). Adding affine coordinates should not change cycle classes.

Localization

\(A(Y)\to A(X)\to A(U)\to 0\) for \(U=X-Y\). Remove a closed part, track what remains.

Projective Bundle

\(A(\mathbb P(E))\) is free over \(A(X)\) with basis \(1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{r-1}\).

Appendix section 2: properties of the Chow ringSource coverage table: appendix-a-source-coverage.csv
Present these properties as the reason Chow rings become usable rather than merely definitional. Picard identifies the old divisor language inside \(A^1\). Affine invariance says that adding irrelevant affine directions does not alter the group. Localization lets one compute by removing and replacing closed pieces. The projective bundle formula is the key that unlocks Chern classes, because it gives a controlled basis in which the tautological class satisfies a relation.

Chern Classes From Projective Bundles

bundle data to Chow data
Projective Bundle Relation

\[ \sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^i\pi^*c_i(E)\xi^{r-i}=0. \]

Total Chern Polynomial

\[ c_t(E)=1+c_1(E)t+\cdots+c_r(E)t^r. \]

Meaning

A vector bundle is translated into a finite list of Chow classes, one in each codimension up to its rank.

Appendix section 3: Chern classesNotebook artifact: chern-class-splitting-dashboard.png
The projective bundle formula is the structural source of Chern classes in this appendix. The tautological class \(\xi\) gives a basis up to \(\xi^{r-1}\), so \(\xi^r\) must be rewritten in that basis. The coefficients are the Chern classes. This is a good moment to contrast line bundles, where \(c_1\) is enough, with higher-rank bundles, where the total polynomial records all codimension contributions.

Top Chern And Self-Intersection

zeros and normals
Zero Locus

If a section \(s\) of a rank \(r\) bundle \(E\) has zero scheme \(Z(s)\) of codimension \(r\), then \[ c_r(E)=[Z(s)]. \]

Self-Intersection

For \(i:Y\hookrightarrow X\) with normal bundle \(N\), \[ i^*i_*(1)=c_r(N). \]

Interpretation

The top Chern class is where a bundle's section is forced to vanish; the normal bundle records how \(Y\) meets itself inside \(X\).

Appendix section 3: C6 and C7 style propertiesBridge to the surface-in-\(\mathbb P^4\) formula
This slide is the geometric payoff of Chern classes before Riemann-Roch appears. A generic section of a rank \(r\) bundle should vanish in codimension \(r\), and the top Chern class records that zero locus. The self-intersection formula then says that pushing \(Y\) into \(X\) and pulling back again measures the top Chern class of the normal bundle. This is exactly the kind of mechanism used when the appendix computes constraints for surfaces in projective four-space.

Chern Character And Todd Class

rational coefficients
Formal roots
\(c_t(E)=\prod_i(1+a_i t)\)

The \(a_i\) are bookkeeping symbols; symmetric expressions descend to Chow classes.

Chern character
\(\operatorname{ch}(E)=\sum_i e^{a_i}\)

Exponential additivity converts exact-sequence behavior into a sum-friendly invariant.

Todd class
\(\operatorname{td}(E)=\prod_i\frac{a_i}{1-e^{-a_i}}\)

The Todd correction supplies the lower-degree terms that ordinary intersection numbers miss.

Why \(\mathbb Q\)?

The expansions contain denominators: \[ \frac{x}{1-e^{-x}}=1+\frac{x}{2}+\frac{x^2}{12}-\frac{x^4}{720}+\cdots, \] so HRR naturally lives in \(A(X)\otimes\mathbb Q\) before the top degree becomes an integer Euler characteristic.

Appendix section 4: characteristic classes for HRRChern roots are formal, but the final symmetric classes are real Chow data
Pause on the formal roots. The roots are not extra geometry living on \(X\); they are a computational fiction justified by the splitting principle. The Chern character and Todd class are symmetric power series in those roots, so they can be rewritten in Chern classes. The denominators explain why rational Chow groups appear. The important reassurance is that HRR will take the degree of the top component and return an integral Euler characteristic.

Complements And Exit Map

what the appendix opens
Positivity

Nakai-Moishezon tests ampleness by positivity of \(D^r\cdot Y\) on every integral \(Y\).

Hodge Sign

For primitive middle cycles on a complex projective variety of dimension \(2k\), the sign pattern is \((-1)^kY^2>0\).

GRR Branch

\[ \operatorname{ch}(f_!x)=f_*(\operatorname{ch}(x)\operatorname{td}(T_f)). \]

Appendix section 5: complements and generalizationsExit line: intersection theory is the bookkeeping language for future geometry
Close by widening the map without pretending to prove the complements. Nakai-Moishezon says intersection numbers detect positivity. The Hodge index theorem adds a sign rule for middle-dimensional intersections under a primitivity condition. Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch makes HRR functorial by measuring how Chern character fails to commute with pushforward. Singular variants keep the same bookkeeping ambition but require new hypotheses and constructions. The final takeaway is that Appendix A builds the language used by these later branches.
