Divisors As Signed Point Ledgers

Genus And Divisors

Definition

A divisor on a nonsingular projective curve is a finite formal sum \(D=\sum n_P P\), with degree \(\deg D=\sum n_P\).

Equivalence Move

Two ledgers are linearly equivalent when their difference is \((f)\), the zero-pole ledger of a rational function.

Notebook translation guide: divisor = signed ledger02 / 18
Use the signed point ledger before formulas become dense. A divisor records deposits and debts at points, degree is the total balance, and linear equivalence says two ledgers differ by the divisor of a rational function. Genus is the persistent background invariant controlling how many sections these ledgers can buy.

Riemann-Roch Balance

\(l(D)-l(K-D)=\deg D+1-g\)
Balance Reading

\(l(D)\) counts visible sections. \(l(K-D)\) counts the residual sections. Riemann-Roch fixes their difference.

  • Negative degree: effective divisors vanish.
  • High degree: residual term disappears.
  • The orange line in the notebook plot marks \(\deg K=2g-2\).
Riemann-Roch Theorem anchor: source coverage section 103 / 18
Point to the notebook figure as a numerical sanity check rather than a proof. The blue and red quantities move, but their difference is pinned by degree and genus. Emphasize the two inspection regions: negative degree, where effectivity disappears, and high degree, where the residual term vanishes and dimensions become linear.
Residual Ledger \(K-D\)

Special Versus Nonspecial

Special Divisor

\(D\) is special when \(l(K-D)>0\). The residual ledger has a section, so \(D\) is not explained by degree alone.

\(\deg K=2g-2\)
Nonspecial Region

When \(l(K-D)=0\), Riemann-Roch simplifies to \(l(D)=\deg D+1-g\). This is the linear tail in the notebook plot.

\(\dim |D|=l(D)-1\)

Genus 0

\(\deg K=-2\); positive divisors quickly become nonspecial.

Genus 1

\(\deg K=0\); degree-zero classes control the group story.

Genus \(\ge 2\)

The canonical divisor has positive degree and produces a map.

Specialness is the residual term in Riemann-Roch04 / 18
Translate specialness into the same ledger language. A divisor is special exactly when the residual ledger K-D still has sections. The canonical degree 2g-2 marks the size of the residual budget. Genus zero and one deserve separate memory hooks because their canonical degrees are negative and zero.

Hurwitz As Cover Accounting

\(2g(X)-2=n(2g(Y)-2)+\deg R\)
Cover Ledger

A finite separable map \(f:X\to Y\) multiplies the base canonical contribution by \(n\), then adds ramification.

  • Hyperelliptic covers: branch points determine genus.
  • Unramified covers over \(g(Y)>0\): no branch debt.
  • Nonintegral genus signals a bad ramification count.
Hurwitz's Theorem anchor: source coverage section 205 / 18
Introduce Hurwitz as the cover version of the same accounting habit. The target genus is multiplied by the cover degree, and every ramification point adds a defect term. The saved notebook rows all have residual zero, which is the useful classroom check: a wrong branch count breaks the genus equation immediately.
Ramification Warning

Tame, Wild, Inseparable

Tame

Use the clean ramification sum \(R=\sum(e_P-1)P\).

Wild

Hurwitz still has a different term, but not merely \(e_P-1\).

Inseparable

Do not apply the separable cover formula without checking the map.

Ramification accounting depends on separability06 / 18
Make the warning precise without turning it into a detour. The displayed Hurwitz formula is the clean separable story, and in tame ramification the correction is the visible e minus one contribution. Wild ramification adds more different, while inseparable maps such as Frobenius have vanishing differentials and require different handling.

Read very ampleness as three tests on a ledger of sections. Base-point freeness gives a morphism, separating points gives injectivity, and separating tangent directions gives an immersion. Projection After A Large Embedding

Every Curve Reaches \(\mathbb{P}^3\)

Projection Strategy

Embed \(X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^n\) by a sufficiently positive divisor, then project from a center avoiding the bad secant and tangent loci.

  • Secants detect whether two points collapse.
  • Tangent lines detect whether the differential collapses.
  • A general center gives a model \(X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^3\).
Linear systems control projective models08 / 18
Explain this as a projection theorem rather than magic compression. A sufficiently positive line bundle embeds the curve in a large projective space. A general projection down to P3 keeps the curve embedded when its center avoids the secant and tangent bad loci, so the curve becomes inspectable in three-dimensional projective space.
Singular Plane Models, Smooth Abstract Curves

Plane Models With Nodes

\(g=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-r\)
Node Accounting

For a degree \(d\) plane curve with \(r\) ordinary nodes and no worse singularities, each node lowers the geometric genus by one.

Model Versus Curve

The plane equation can be singular even when the abstract curve being modeled is nonsingular.

Projection can trade embedding dimension for singularities09 / 18
Use the node picture to separate arithmetic genus from geometric genus. A smooth plane curve of degree d has arithmetic genus (d-1)(d-2)/2. Each ordinary node identifies two branches and lowers the geometric genus by one, so the same abstract curve can be represented by a singular plane model.
Elliptic Curves From Four Branch Points

Elliptic Curves From Branch Points

Characteristic Not 2

A genus-one double cover of \(\mathbb{P}^1\) is determined, up to isomorphism, by four distinct branch points modulo projective change of coordinate.

\(\{0,1,\lambda,\infty\}\quad\leadsto\quad j(\lambda)=256\frac{(1-\lambda+\lambda^2)^3}{\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2}\)
Orbit Reminder

\(\lambda\) is identified with the six cross-ratio transforms: \(\lambda,1/\lambda,1-\lambda,1/(1-\lambda),\lambda/(\lambda-1),(\lambda-1)/\lambda\).

Elliptic curves anchor: source coverage section 410 / 18
Frame the elliptic classification through a four-point ledger on P1 in characteristic not two. Three branch points can be normalized to 0, 1, and infinity; the fourth is lambda, but lambda is only remembered up to the usual cross-ratio orbit. The j-formula packages that orbit into an isomorphism invariant.

A Cubic Becomes A Group

collinear \(P,Q,R\quad\Rightarrow\quad P+Q+R=0\)
Group Law

A smooth plane cubic with a chosen origin turns line intersection into addition. Tangency handles the case \(P=Q\).

Notebook Example

For \(y^2=x^3-x+1\), the line \(y=1\) meets at \(P=(0,1)\), \(Q=(1,1)\), and \(R=(-1,1)\); hence \(P+Q=(-1,-1)\).

Divisor classes become a concrete group law11 / 18
Connect the notebook's exact residual check to the group law. A line meets a smooth cubic in three points counted with multiplicity, and the chosen origin turns collinearity into P plus Q plus R equals zero. The plotted example verifies the relevant points on the cubic, so the drawing is tied to algebra.
From Points To Degree-Zero Classes

Pic0 And The Jacobian

Genus One Identification

With a base point \(P_0\), the map \(P\mapsto\mathcal{O}_X(P-P_0)\) identifies a genus-one curve with \(\mathrm{Pic}^0(X)\).

Higher Genus

\(\mathrm{Pic}^0(X)\) is the Jacobian: a \(g\)-dimensional group variety that records degree-zero divisor classes.

Picard functor packages divisor classes12 / 18
Move from the chord picture to the functorial language. On a genus-one curve with base point P0, the map P to O(P-P0) identifies the curve with its degree-zero Picard group. For higher genus, Pic0 becomes the Jacobian, a group variety that records divisor classes rather than individual points.

Positive Characteristic Detector

Frobenius And Hasse Invariant

Computation

For a smooth plane cubic in characteristic \(p\), the Hasse invariant is visible as one coefficient of \(f^{p-1}\).

Interpretation

Frobenius is not a complex-analytic operation; it is the positive-characteristic structure testing ordinary versus supersingular behavior.

Frobenius leaves a coefficient-level trace14 / 18
Treat the Hasse invariant as a computable detector in positive characteristic. For a smooth plane cubic, take f to the p minus one power and read the coefficient of (xyz) to the p minus one. Nonzero means ordinary behavior; zero means supersingular behavior, so Frobenius leaves a visible algebraic trace.
The Canonical Divisor As A Model Builder

Canonical Morphism

\(\deg K_X=2g-2,\qquad |K_X|:X\to\mathbb{P}^{g-1}\)
Main Split

For \(g\ge 2\), a nonhyperelliptic curve is embedded by its canonical system. A hyperelliptic curve is the exception.

Hyperelliptic Exception

The canonical map factors through the \(2:1\) map \(X\to\mathbb{P}^1\), so it does not separate the paired points.

Canonical embedding anchor: source coverage section 515 / 18
Return to the canonical divisor as a projective machine. Its degree is 2g-2, and for genus at least two the complete canonical system maps the curve to projective space of dimension g minus one. The main split is nonhyperelliptic curves embedding versus hyperelliptic curves factoring through a double cover of P1.
Special Divisors Cannot Be Too Efficient

Clifford's Bound

\(\dim |D| \le \frac{\deg D}{2}\)
Clifford

For a special divisor \(D\) on a smooth curve, the complete linear system cannot have dimension larger than half the degree.

Equality Cases

Equality is accounted for by the trivial and canonical ends, and by hyperelliptic multiples of the \(g^1_2\) in the classical exceptional family.

Clifford bridges specialness to P3 restrictions16 / 18
Use Clifford's theorem as the restraint on overly optimistic linear systems. For a special divisor, the dimension of the complete linear system cannot exceed half its degree, aside from the known equality patterns. This is the bridge from Riemann-Roch counting to the degree-genus restrictions that appear in P3.
