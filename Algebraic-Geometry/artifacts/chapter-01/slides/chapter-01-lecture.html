Affine varieties

Zero Sets And Ideals Form A Radical Loop

Theorem Pattern
Hilbert Nullstellensatz in the affine dictionary

\(I(Z(\mathfrak a))=\sqrt{\mathfrak a}\) for an algebraically closed field \(k\).

Assumption

The clean loop uses algebraic closure. Over smaller fields, rational points may not remember every geometric component.

Section 1: Affine Varietiesdictionary: equations to loci to radical ideals
Treat the Nullstellensatz as the algebraic closure of the first translation loop. Starting from equations gives a closed set; starting from a set gives all equations vanishing on it; doing both returns the radical ideal. Stress the algebraically closed field assumption because the loop is not this clean over arbitrary fields.
Topology from vanishing

The Zariski Line Is Coarse

Affine Line

Closed subsets of \(\mathbb A^1\) are finite sets or all of \(\mathbb A^1\). Nonempty opens are complements of finite closed sets.

Dense Open

If \(U=\mathbb A^1\setminus\{a_1,\dots,a_m\}\), then every nonzero polynomial vanishing on \(U\) must vanish everywhere.

Inspection Target

The open set looks large in Euclidean space because algebraic openness records where finitely many denominators are allowed to be nonzero.

Artifact: figures/zariski-dense-open-line.pnginvariant: finite closed complements are dense opens
Use the line picture to reset Euclidean intuition. On the affine line, proper closed sets are finite, so a nonempty open set is the complement of finitely many points and is already dense. That explains why regular functions are allowed to be quotients on open patches and why dense-open agreement later becomes strong.
Prime ideals as geometry

Irreducible Pieces Carry Dimension

Correspondence

Irreducible closed subsets \(Y\subset \mathbb A^n\) correspond to prime ideals \(I(Y)\subset k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\).

Dimension

\(\dim Y = \dim A(Y)\), the Krull dimension of the coordinate ring.

Irreducible closed set <-> prime idealdimension is read algebraically
This slide puts irreducibility, prime ideals, and dimension on one dependency diagram. The key point is that an irreducible closed set has a coordinate ring with no product splitting through zero, so its ideal is prime. Dimension is then read as Krull dimension of the coordinate ring, not as visual thickness.
One equation, one wall

Hypersurfaces Make Degree Visible

Hypersurface

If \(f\in k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) is irreducible, then \(Z(f)\) is an affine hypersurface.

Plane Curve

\(f(x,y)=y^2-x^3+x\) gives a curve in \(\mathbb A^2\). Its degree controls generic line intersections.

Bridge To Intersections

One equation gives codimension one; two equations intersect by solving both. Degree becomes the bookkeeping device.

Section 1 to Section 7 bridgedegree will reappear as intersection count
Use hypersurfaces as the first place where an equation has a geometric size. A single irreducible polynomial cuts one codimension-one affine variety, and the degree of that polynomial foreshadows intersection counts. The point is not to prove every dimension theorem here, but to connect one equation, one prime ideal, and one geometric wall.
Projective varieties

Homogeneous Coordinates Hide Scale

Projective Point

\([x_0:\cdots:x_n]=[\lambda x_0:\cdots:\lambda x_n]\) for \(\lambda\ne 0\).

Homogeneous Equation

If \(F\) has degree \(d\), then \(F(\lambda x)=\lambda^dF(x)\). The zero condition is scale-invariant.

Notebook Check

The recorded homogeneous scaling residual is \(0\), so the conic equation respects projective scaling exactly.

Artifact: figures/projective-conic-chart-atlas.pngcheck: homogeneous_scaling_residual = 0
Projective coordinates should be introduced as scale-free coordinates, not as mysterious extra points. A homogeneous equation respects scaling because every term has the same degree. The conic atlas from the notebook shows that one projective curve has several affine computational windows, each seeing a different part of the same object.
Local computation in projective space

Charts Make Projective Objects Computable

Dehomogenize

On \(D_+(x_i)\), set \(x_i=1\) and rewrite every coordinate as a ratio \(x_j/x_i\).

Computation Window

A chart is not the whole projective object. It is a faithful affine window where local algebra can be performed.

Teaching Cue

When a point disappears from one chart, it should reappear in another. That is the atlas doing its job.

Section 2: Projective Varietiesglobal equation, local ratios
Charts are the working interface to projective objects. On the chart where one coordinate is nonzero, divide by that coordinate and compute with ratios. This slide should make clear that dehomogenization is not a loss of rigor; it is the local calculation that lets global projective geometry be handled in coordinates.
Functions on varieties

Regular Functions Are Local Quotients

Regular At P

\(f\) is regular at \(P\) if near \(P\), \(f=g/h\) with \(h(P)\ne 0\).

Local Ring

\(\mathcal O_{P,Y}\) stores all such local quotients; \(K(Y)\) is the function field for irreducible \(Y\).

Common Mistake

A quotient can be valid on one open patch and invalid at a point where its denominator vanishes.

Section 3 setup: regular functionslocal denominator control
Regular functions are local because denominators can fail at some points and behave perfectly at others. Emphasize the condition h(P) nonzero and the local ring as the place where two quotients are compared near P. The function field is the larger ambient field where these local quotients live.
Contravariant algebra

Morphisms Pull Functions Back

Pullback

For a morphism \(\varphi:X\to Y\), the pullback sends \(f\in A(Y)\) to \(\varphi^\#(f)=f\circ\varphi\in A(X)\).

Direction Reversal

Geometry moves \(X\to Y\). Coordinate rings move \(A(Y)\to A(X)\).

Section 3: Morphismsregular maps are seen by their action on functions
This is the contravariant move that students must own early. A morphism from X to Y pulls every regular function on Y back to a regular function on X by composition. The geometry arrow points one way, while the coordinate-ring arrow points the other way, and the square encodes that reversal cleanly.
Dense-open geometry

Rational Maps Live On Dense Opens

Rational Map

A rational map \(X\dashrightarrow Y\) is represented by a morphism \(U\to Y\) on some nonempty open \(U\subset X\).

Equivalence

Two representatives define the same rational map when they agree on a nonempty open subset of the overlap.

Callback

On the Zariski line, dense opens are complements of finite sets, so agreement there is algebraically rigid.

Section 4: Rational Mapspartial functions with dense-domain memory
Rational maps are honest morphisms only on a dense open part of the source. The important equivalence is dense agreement: two formulas that agree where both are defined represent the same rational map. Tie this back to the Zariski line, where dense opens are large enough to remember polynomial information.
Birational local geometry

Blowup Separates Directions

Direction Coordinate

Set \(u=y/x\). The strict transform in this chart satisfies \(u^2=x+1\).

Exceptional Fiber

Over \(x=0\), the separated directions are \(u=\pm 1\).

Recorded Check

blowup_node_tangent_directions gives \([-1, 1]\) in the final sanity artifact.

Artifact: figures/node-blowup-direction-separator.pngbirational map, new coordinate records tangent direction
Use the node blowup as the chapter's concrete birational event. The coordinate u equals y divided by x, so it records a direction through the origin. The strict transform equation u squared equals x plus one meets the exceptional fiber at u equals plus or minus one, separating the two tangent directions.
Nonsingularity by first derivatives

Tangent Spaces Come From First Derivatives

Tangent Space

If \(Y=Z(f_1,\dots,f_r)\), then \(T_PY=\ker J(P)\), where \(J(P)\) is the Jacobian matrix at \(P\).

Singular Signal

A point is singular when the tangent space is larger than the expected local dimension.

Notebook Check

The cusp Jacobian at the origin is \([0,0]\), so first derivatives fail to cut the tangent space down.

Artifact: figures/jacobian-rank-singularity-dashboard.pngcheck: cusp_jacobian_at_origin = [0, 0]
The Jacobian criterion turns singularity into first-order linear algebra. At a point, plug the coordinates into the matrix of first derivatives; the tangent space is the kernel. Smooth points have the expected tangent dimension, while singular points have too many first-order directions or multiple branches hidden in the same location.
Local algebra detects shape

Local Rings Remember Singular Type

Local Test

\(Y\) is nonsingular at \(P\) exactly when the local ring \(\mathcal O_{P,Y}\) is regular.

Completion

The completed local ring packages the formal analytic type near \(P\), distinguishing smooth branches, nodes, and cusps.

Section 5: Nonsingular Varietieslocal algebra decides the analytic neighborhood
Local rings remember the germ of the variety around a point. Passing to the completion turns that local algebra into an analytic formal model, which can distinguish a smooth branch from a node or cusp. The nonsingular condition is local: the local ring must be regular, with the right number of parameters.
Nonsingular curves

Curves, Valuations, And Points

Curve Viewpoint

For an irreducible curve \(C\), the function field \(K(C)\) has transcendence degree one.

Nonsingular Point

At a nonsingular point \(P\), \(\mathcal O_{P,C}\) is a discrete valuation ring, and \(\operatorname{ord}_P(f)\) measures zeros and poles.

Preview

Divisors turn these local orders into global arithmetic on curves.

Section 6: Nonsingular Curvespoints become valuations on the function field
For curves, points can be read through valuations. At a nonsingular point, the local ring is a discrete valuation ring, and ord_P measures zero or pole order of a function. This gives a bridge from visible points to divisors and prepares the later curve chapter without needing the full machinery yet.
Degree as algebraic count

Intersections Count Algebraically

Dimension Bound

If \(Y,Z\subset \mathbb P^n\) have dimensions \(r,s\), then every component of \(Y\cap Z\) has dimension at least \(r+s-n\).

Plane Curves

Proper plane curves of degrees \(m\) and \(n\) meet with algebraic count \(mn\), including multiplicities and points at infinity.

Intersection theory corrects the visual count. A real drawing may miss complex points, tangencies, multiplicities, or points at infinity, so the algebraic count uses resultants and degree.
