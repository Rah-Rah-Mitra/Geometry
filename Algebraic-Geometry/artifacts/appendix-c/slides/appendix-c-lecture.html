The Weil Conjectures:
Counts Become Cohomology

Finite-field point counts are packed into a zeta function, re-read as Frobenius fixed points, and then explained by traces and determinants on \( \ell \)-adic cohomology.

Notebook: Algebraic-Geometry/appendix-c-weil-conjectures/appendix-c-weil-conjectures.ipynb
Open the lecture by naming the appendix as a bridge, not a bag of disconnected formulas. The raw arithmetic object is the sequence \(N_r=\#X(\mathbb F_{q^r})\). The zeta function stores that sequence, Frobenius turns the same numbers into fixed-point counts, and the Lefschetz philosophy replaces fixed points by traces. The rest of the deck explains how determinant factors, duality, and Deligne's weights recover the four advertised Weil-conjecture claims.

Generating Function

The Zeta Function Packages All Counts

Artifact: zeta-coefficient-dashboard.png
Definition
\(Z(X,t)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{r\ge 1} N_r\,\frac{t^r}{r}\right)\)
Reading The Formula
The logarithm is the ledger: the coefficient of \(t^r\) in \(\log Z(X,t)\) is \(N_r/r\).

Why This Package?

The exponential makes disjoint unions and closed-point products behave like Euler products, while still preserving every \(N_r\).

The zeta function is not an extra invariant placed on top of the point counts; it is a compact encoding of the same sequence. Emphasize the logarithm: if one knows \(Z(X,t)\) as a formal power series, one can recover all \(N_r\). The notebook's second dashboard makes this concrete for \(q=5\), comparing the projective-line count sequence with the elliptic model and displaying the \(N_r/r\) coefficients for \(\mathbb P^1\).
Control Case

Projective Line: The Whole Story In Two Factors

\(X=\mathbb P^1/\mathbb F_q\)
Counts
\(N_r=q^r+1\)
One affine coordinate over \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\), plus the point at infinity.
Zeta Function
\(Z(\mathbb P^1,t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-qt)}\)
Use \(\mathbb P^1\) as the calibration case. The point count is transparent over every extension, so the zeta function can be summed directly. Its two denominator factors are exactly what cohomology predicts: \(H^0\) contributes the eigenvalue \(1\), and \(H^2\) contributes the eigenvalue \(q\). There is no middle cohomology, so this example makes the later alternating determinant formula feel inevitable rather than mysterious.
Weil Conjectures

Four Predictions, One Package

Smooth projective \(X/\mathbb F_q\), \(\dim X=n\)
1. Rationality \(Z(X,t)\) is a rational function, even though it begins life as a formal exponential series.
2. Functional Equation The substitution \(t\mapsto 1/(q^n t)\) returns \(Z\) up to the expected power of \(q\), power of \(t\), and sign.
3. Polynomial Factors \(Z=\prod_i P_i(t)^{(-1)^{i+1}}\), with \(P_0=1-t\) and \(P_{2n}=1-q^n t\).
4. Weights And Betti Numbers If \(P_i(t)=\prod_j(1-\alpha_{ij}t)\), then \(|\alpha_{ij}|=q^{i/2}\), and \(\deg P_i\) matches the relevant Betti number.
Unifying View
The conjectures say that arithmetic counts behave as if a good cohomology theory over characteristic \(p\) were controlling traces, determinants, duality, and weights.
Present the four statements as one coordinated package. Rationality asks for determinant-like finite data behind the infinite sequence. The functional equation asks for a duality symmetry. The factorization asks for cohomological degrees \(0\) through \(2n\). The root-size statement asks for a precise weight attached to each degree. This framing is useful because each later tool answers one structural demand rather than proving an isolated formula.
Historical Pressure

Why The History Matters

Source sections 1-2
Teaching Point
The timeline separates the tools: curves suggested the pattern, \(p\)-adic analysis proved rationality in high dimension, cohomology supplied traces and duality, and Deligne supplied the decisive weight theorem.
This is not a decorative history slide; it explains why the appendix changes methods. Weil's formulation came from point-count patterns and the curve case. Dwork showed that rationality could be attacked by \(p\)-adic analysis. Grothendieck's program supplied the cohomological machine that explains rationality, duality, and Betti numbers. Deligne's theorem then proves the deepest root-size statement. The sequence prepares students to expect several different proof ideas rather than one single calculation.

Fixed-Point Mechanism

Frobenius Turns Counts Into Fixed Points

\(F(a)=a^q\)
Frobenius
\(F(a_0:\cdots:a_m)=(a_0^q:\cdots:a_m^q)\)
Count As Fixed Points
\(N_r=\#\operatorname{Fix}(F^r)\)

Why It Works

A point is fixed by \(F^r\) exactly when its coordinates are rational over \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\). The count has become a fixed-point problem.

This slide is the conceptual pivot from arithmetic to geometry. Frobenius raises coordinates to their \(q\)-th powers, and the equations defining \(X\) have coefficients in \(\mathbb F_q\), so Frobenius preserves \(X\). A point fixed by \(F^r\) has coordinates in \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\), and conversely. Therefore \(N_r\) is the number of fixed points of an iterate, which is exactly the kind of problem a Lefschetz trace formula is designed to handle.
Cohomological Translation

Lefschetz Replaces Fixed Points By Traces

Readable redraw of trace-formula graph
Trace Formula
\(N_r=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^i\operatorname{Tr}\!\left((F^r)^*\mid H^i(X,\mathbb Q_\ell)\right)\)

  • \(H^0\)-trace: \(1\)
  • \(H^1\)-trace: \(-3\) in the elliptic sample
  • \(H^2\)-trace: \(q=5\)
  • Alternating sum: \(1-(-3)+5=9=N_1\)
The slide-native trace map redraws the notebook's mechanism without the clipped labels from the supporting PNG. Once the count is a fixed-point count of Frobenius, the Lefschetz formula converts it into an alternating sum of traces on cohomology. The elliptic toy ledger is useful because the signs become visible: \(H^1\) appears with a minus sign, so a trace of \(-3\) contributes \(+3\) to the count. This is the bridge from the sequence \(N_r\) to eigenvalues of Frobenius.
Linear Algebra Engine

Trace-To-Determinant Lemma

Source section 4
Lemma
If \(\phi\) acts on finite-dimensional \(V\), then
\(\displaystyle \exp\!\left(\sum_{r\ge1}\operatorname{Tr}(\phi^r)\frac{t^r}{r}\right)=\det(1-\phi t)^{-1}.\)
Eigenvalue Proof Move
For eigenvalues \(\lambda_j\), traces give \(\sum_j\lambda_j^r\). Exponentiating the logarithmic series turns those sums into \(\prod_j(1-\lambda_j t)^{-1}\).
The determinant lemma is the algebraic conversion that makes rationality plausible. After the trace formula, \(\log Z\) contains traces of powers of Frobenius. For a linear operator, those traces are power sums of eigenvalues. The elementary logarithmic identity converts the exponential of the power sums into a product of determinant factors. The cohomology has finite dimension, so the determinant is a polynomial rather than an infinite object.
First Payoff

Rationality From Cohomology

Theorem 4.2 pattern
Cohomological Factors
\(P_i(t)=\det(1-F^*t\mid H^i(X,\mathbb Q_\ell))\)
Factorization
\(Z(X,t)=\prod_{i=0}^{2n}P_i(t)^{(-1)^{i+1}}\)

Endpoint Factors

For connected smooth projective \(X\), \(P_0(t)=1-t\) and \(P_{2n}(t)=1-q^n t\).

Now combine the previous two slides. The trace formula expresses \(N_r\) as alternating traces, and the determinant lemma converts the trace series for each degree into \(P_i(t)\). The alternating sign determines whether the factor lands upstairs or downstairs. This proves rationality over the coefficient field of cohomology, and then the rational-coefficient power series pins the zeta function down as a rational function over \(\mathbb Q\).
Duality Payoff

Functional Equation From Duality

Poincare duality
Functional Equation
\(Z\!\left(X,\frac{1}{q^n t}\right)=\pm q^{nE/2}t^E Z(X,t)\)
Proof Move
Duality pairs \(H^i\) with \(H^{2n-i}\). Compatibility with cup product makes the Frobenius eigenvalues pair to \(q^n\), so the determinant factors transform reciprocally.

Control Check

For \(\mathbb P^1\), \(n=1\) and \(E=2\), giving \(Z(1/(qt))=qt^2Z(t)\).

Here the functional equation is no longer a formal miracle. Poincare duality pairs degree \(i\) and degree \(2n-i\), and the top cohomology sees Frobenius by multiplication by \(q^n\). Therefore eigenvalues in paired degrees are reciprocal after scaling by \(q^n\). Multiplying the determinant identities across all degrees yields the displayed functional equation, with \(E\) the Euler-Poincare characteristic coming from the alternating sum of Betti numbers.
Deep Theorem

Deligne's Weight Statement

Artifact: frobenius-eigenvalue-circle.png
Weight \(i\)
\(P_i(t)=\prod_j(1-\alpha_{ij}t),\qquad |\alpha_{ij}|=q^{i/2}\)
Do Not Prove Here
The notebook checks the elliptic curve behavior numerically. The general proof uses deeper \(\ell\)-adic machinery and Deligne's theorem.

Geometric Meaning

Each cohomological degree has its own radius. Degree \(1\) gives \(\sqrt q\); degree \(2\) gives \(q\); degree \(i\) gives \(q^{i/2}\).

This slide states the difficult part without pretending to prove it. For the elliptic sample, the Frobenius eigenvalues, equivalently the reciprocal roots of \(1-at+qt^2\), lie on the circle of radius \(\sqrt q\), which is the \(i=1\) case. Deligne's theorem says that in every smooth projective dimension and every cohomological degree, the Frobenius eigenvalues have exactly the radius predicted by the degree. This completes the analogue of the Riemann hypothesis and identifies the cohomological factors with the Weil-conjecture factors.
