Section 1 · Geometry on a Surface

The Intersection Pairing As A Lattice

Definition

For \(D=aH-\sum b_iE_i\) and \(D'=a'H-\sum b'_iE_i\), the blowup model uses

\(D\cdot D' = aa' - \sum_i b_i b'_i.\)

Inspection Target

  • One positive class: \(H^2=1\).
  • Exceptional directions: \(E_i^2=-1\).
  • Orthogonality: \(H\cdot E_i=0\).

Intersection arithmetic

Self-Intersection Changes The Meaning Of Degree

Intersection Table
\(H^2=1,\qquad E_i^2=-1,\qquad H\cdot E_i=0.\)

Why The Sign Matters

On a surface, a curve can meet a moved copy of itself negatively after a blowup. Degree becomes a quadratic datum, not just a linear count.

\(D^2\) is geometry, not decorationBasis: \(H,E_i\)
Slow down on self-intersection because it changes the meaning of degree. H behaves like the old plane degree, but exceptional curves carry negative self-intersection, and orthogonality to H records that exceptional geometry is introduced over a point. This is why surfaces are not curves with one extra coordinate.
Global invariants from local pairings

Adjunction And Surface Riemann-Roch

Adjunction
\(2g(C)-2 = C\cdot(C+K_X).\)

The genus of a nonsingular curve \(C\subset X\) is read from \(C^2\) and the canonical class.

Surface Riemann-Roch
\( \chi(\mathcal O_X(D))=\chi(\mathcal O_X)+\frac{1}{2}D\cdot(D-K_X).\)
Canonical class enters the arithmeticSection 1
Connect adjunction and Riemann-Roch as two ways the same pairing enters global geometry. Adjunction reads genus from C squared and C dot K_X, while surface Riemann-Roch reads Euler characteristic from D dot D minus D dot K_X. The canonical class is now part of the arithmetic.

From signature to positivity

Nakai-Moishezon As A Positivity Test

Criterion

A divisor \(D\) on a nonsingular projective surface is ample exactly when the intersection arithmetic passes both tests:

\(D^2>0,\qquad D\cdot C>0\)

for every irreducible curve \(C\subset X\).

Positivity lives inside the latticeSection 1
Present Nakai-Moishezon as a positivity gate, not as a vague ampleness slogan. A divisor must have positive square and positive intersection with every irreducible curve. The test explains why the earlier lattice picture matters: ampleness is detected numerically across all curve directions.
Section 2 · Ruled Surfaces

Ruled Surfaces Have Two Coordinates

Section/Fiber Basis
\(f^2=0,\qquad C_0\cdot f=1,\qquad C_0^2=-e.\)

Coordinate Rule

Write divisor classes as \(D=aC_0+bf\). The integer \(a\) measures section direction; \(b\) measures fiber direction.

Two coordinates, one intersection tableRuled surfaces
Introduce ruled surfaces as the first family where two coordinates are enough for many computations. The section C0 and a fiber f have a tiny intersection table, but it already records the fibration, the base curve, and the twisting parameter e. This prepares the positivity grid.

Section 3 · Monoidal Transformations

A Blowup Adds One Negative Class

Picard Update
\( \operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Bl}_P X)=\pi^*\operatorname{Pic}(X)\oplus \mathbb Z[E].\)
\(E^2=-1,\qquad K_{\widetilde X}=\pi^*K_X+E.\)

Birational Meaning

The old surface data pulls back; the blowup records directions through \(P\) as a new exceptional curve.

One new generator, one new negative directionSection 3
Explain a blowup as adding exactly one new negative class. Pullbacks keep the old Picard lattice, the exceptional curve contributes E squared equals minus one, and the canonical divisor shifts by E. This is the local move that later factors birational morphisms.
Monoidal transformation arithmetic

Strict Transform Accounting

Formula
\(C'=\pi^*C-mE\)
\((C')^2=C^2-m^2.\)

Reading The Bars

Multiplicity is squared. A triple point can drop self-intersection by \(9\), which is why singularity resolution quickly changes the lattice.

Section 4 · The Cubic Surface in \(P^3\)

Cubic Surface From Six Blown-Up Points

Anticanonical Model
\(-K_X=3H-\sum_{i=1}^6 E_i\)
\(K_X^2=9-6=3.\)

Teaching Point

The cubic surface appears from plane data once six point conditions are encoded by exceptional classes.

Six exceptional classes control the modelSection 4
Move from abstract blowups to the cubic surface model. Six points in the plane create six exceptional classes, and the anticanonical class three H minus the six E_i embeds the blowup as a cubic surface. The quick check K squared equals three is the numerical fingerprint.

Section 5 · Birational Transformations

Birational Morphisms Factor Into Blowups

Common-Resolution Strategy

  • Replace an indeterminate rational map by a surface \(Z\).
  • Let \(Z\to X\) and \(Z\to Y\) be monoidal chains.
  • Compare divisors upstairs where morphisms are honest.
birational geometry \(=\) blowups \(+\) contractions \(+\) intersection bookkeeping
Monoidal transformations become a factorization languageSection 5
Frame birational morphisms as chains of monoidal moves. Instead of trying to draw a rational map directly, resolve its indeterminacy upstairs, then compare the two downward morphisms. This common-resolution picture explains why blowups and contractions are the grammar of surface birational geometry.
Contraction criterion

Castelnuovo Contraction And Minimality

Decision Rule

If \(C\cong P^1\) and \(C^2=-1\), then \(C\) is contractible to a nonsingular point.

Minimal Model

A nonsingular surface is minimal when no contractible exceptional curve remains.

Contraction reverses a blowup when the arithmetic allows itSection 5
Castelnuovo's criterion runs the blowup arithmetic backward. A rational curve with self-intersection minus one can be contracted to a nonsingular point, removing one negative exceptional direction. Minimality means no such contractible exceptional curve remains on the model being studied.
