Affine Equation

\(q(x)=x^T A x+2b^T x+c\)

The classification is procedural: reduce the expression, name the remaining invariant, then test the drawing against the equation.

What Affine Maps Preserve

  • incidence, parallelism, centers, and directions at infinity
  • rank and degeneracy of the quadratic part under congruence
  • whether a null direction carries a residual linear term
  • not lengths, angles, or numerical axis lengths
Use this slide as the organizing promise for the lecture. The key distinction is between a remembered catalog and a reproducible reduction. Students should leave with an algorithmic habit: symmetrize, translate when possible, diagonalize, inspect the nullspace, and only then attach the familiar names ellipsoid, hyperboloid, cone, paraboloid, or cylinder.

Affine algebra

The Translation Identity Locates Centers

Identity

\[ q(x_0+y)=y^T A y+2(Ax_0+b)^T y+q(x_0) \]

Center Criterion

A point \(x_0\) is a center exactly when \(Ax_0+b=0\). If \(A\) is invertible the center is unique; if \(A\) has a nullspace, there may be no center or a whole affine family of centers.

The translation formula is the main algebraic engine of the chapter. Work through it slowly enough that the center condition appears naturally rather than as a definition dropped from the sky. The surviving term is the diagnostic: if the coefficient of y vanishes, the quadric is centered; if it cannot vanish in a null direction, parabolic geometry remains.

Completing squares as a picture

Centers, Asymptotes, Parabolas, Cylinders

Nullspace Test

If a vector \(v\) lies in \(\ker A\), the quadratic term cannot control motion along \(v\). A remaining linear term in that direction opens the quadric like a parabola.

Four Readings

  • full rank plus center gives a centered oval or hyperbola
  • mixed signs introduce escape directions and asymptotes
  • rank one plus linear null term gives a parabola
  • rank one without that term leaves a cylinder direction
Let the figure do real work here. The point is that completing squares is not just a symbolic trick; it tells us whether the origin can be moved to a balanced point. The bottom row is especially important because students often diagonalize A and then stop. The null direction still needs to be interrogated for surviving affine information.

Real Versus Complex

Over \(\mathbb C\), signs can be rescaled away. Over \(\mathbb R\), signs decide empty, compact, connected, split, and singular cases.

This slide converts the classification table into a lab protocol. Emphasize that the names are conclusions, not inputs. The same symbolic equation can look unfamiliar until rank, signature, remaining linear terms, and level are recorded. The real field matters because signs cannot be erased there, while over the complex field the rank and affine residue carry more of the classification.

Polarity

Homogenization Turns The Same Equation Into A Polar Machine

Projective Matrix

\[ X^T C X=0,\qquad X=(x,1) \]

\[ \operatorname{polar}(P):\ (CP)^T X=0 \]

Affine Readings

  • point on the quadric gives the tangent hyperplane
  • external point gives the chord of contact in conics
  • centered directions \(u,v\) are conjugate when \(u^TAv=0\)
This is where the affine chapter reconnects to projective quadrics without losing the affine viewpoint. Homogenization adds the coordinate at infinity and packages the equation into one symmetric matrix C. The pay-off is computational: the same multiplication CP gives tangent lines for points on the conic and polar lines for points away from it.

One bilinear form, two geometries

Tangent Planes And Conjugate Diameters Share The Matrix

Tangent Hyperplane

If \(p\) lies on \(q(x)=0\), then the tangent hyperplane is

\[ (Ap+b)^T(x-p)=0. \]

Proof Roadmap

  1. differentiate \(q(p+t v)\) at \(t=0\)
  2. use \(q(p)=0\) to isolate first-order contact
  3. write all tangent directions \(v\) satisfying \((Ap+b)^T v=0\)
Conjugate Directions

For a centered quadric, the bilinear form \(B(u,v)=u^TAv\) controls paired diameter directions.

\[ u\ \hbox{conjugate to}\ v \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u^TAv=0. \]

Geometric Meaning

Parallel chords in one direction have midpoints lying in a diameter hyperplane. The conjugate direction is the direction whose chords that diameter bisects.

Keep the focus on reuse of matrix data. Students may think tangent planes, polar lines, and conjugate diameters are separate classical constructions. Here they are different readings of the same bilinear structure. The derivative gives tangent directions; homogenization gives polarity; centering turns the bilinear form into a rule for which chord families bisect each other.

Euclidean refinement

Euclidean Structure Adds Measured Axes

Centered Orthogonal Form

\[ \lambda_1 y_1^2+\lambda_2 y_2^2+\lambda_3 y_3^2=-q(x_0) \]

When all \(\lambda_i\) and \(-q(x_0)\) are positive, the semi-axis lengths are \(a_i=\sqrt{-q(x_0)/\lambda_i}\).

Affine Versus Euclidean

  • affine maps preserve type but can change lengths and angles
  • Euclidean rotations preserve lengths and reveal principal axes
  • axis lengths are numerical invariants only after a metric is fixed
This slide separates affine and Euclidean classification cleanly. In affine geometry, all nondegenerate ellipsoids are equivalent after a suitable invertible change of coordinates. In Euclidean geometry, that is too coarse because rotations and translations cannot change the semi-axis lengths. The spectral theorem is therefore not just algebra; it is the bridge from type to measurement.

Mental checks

Common Pitfalls In Affine Quadric Classification

Diagonal is not reduced. Linear terms can remain, especially along \(\ker A\).

A center need not lie on the quadric. It is a gradient-balancing point, not automatically a surface point.

Same \(A\), different level. A cone and nearby hyperboloids can share the quadratic part.

Real signs matter. Over \(\mathbb R\), signs decide empty, compact, split, and connected cases.

Fast Diagnostic

Before naming a quadric, ask three questions: does \(Ax+b=0\) have a solution, what is the signature of \(A\), and what remains on the nullspace?

Metric Caution

Affine equivalence can erase axis lengths. Euclidean equivalence cannot. Do not report semi-axis lengths as affine invariants.

Use the pitfalls as a short oral diagnostic. Each warning corresponds to a common premature conclusion: stopping after diagonalization, confusing the center with a point of the surface, forgetting the constant level, or moving between real and complex fields without noticing that signs have changed meaning. The metric caution prepares students for the Euclidean conic material that follows later.

In-class exercise

Exercise: Diagnose The Affine Type Before Plotting

Problem

Classify the real affine quadric in \(\mathbb R^3\):

\[ x^2+2y^2-4z+3=0. \]

Find whether it has a center, identify the null direction behavior, and name the expected topology.

Discussion Prompt

What information is visible from \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,0)\), and what information is carried only by the linear term in \(z\)?

Give students a minute to classify before showing any plot. The matrix has rank two and a null direction in z. The linear term in that null direction cannot be translated away, so the object has no center and should be an elliptic paraboloid. The exercise reinforces the main habit: diagnose algebraically first, then draw.

Takeaways

Affine Quadrics: The Five-Part Habit

Reduction

  • Start with \(q(x)=x^TAx+2b^Tx+c\) and symmetrize \(A\).
  • Use \(Ax_0+b=0\) to decide whether a center exists.
  • Inspect \(\ker A\) before declaring the equation reduced.
  • Use rank, signature, and level to predict topology.
  • Homogenize for polarity; diagonalize orthogonally for Euclidean axes.
Closing Principle

Reduce the equation, draw the normal form, and then check the drawing numerically. That loop keeps the classification table attached to geometry.

Affine

type, centers, parallelism, directions at infinity.

Projective

polars, tangents, contact chords after homogenization.

Euclidean

orthogonal axes and measured semi-axis lengths.

Computational

residual checks that certify the classification.

Close by returning to the lecture's promise. The chapter is not a bag of surface names; it is a workflow for extracting geometry from a quadratic equation. The reduction tells the affine story, homogenization recovers polarity, and Euclidean structure adds measured axes. The computational checks in the notebook are the discipline that keeps all three readings honest.
