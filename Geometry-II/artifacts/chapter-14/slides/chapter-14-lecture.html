Open by separating the object from its chart picture. A projective quadric is an equation class in homogeneous coordinates, while every drawn oval, pair of arcs, or ruled surface is a chart reading of that class. The lecture will keep three ledgers visible: matrix invariants, incidence data, and dual hyperplanes.
Definition and invariance

The Equation Must Survive Two Choices

Representatives change; the zero set in projective space does not.

Projective Quadric

A quadric in \(\mathbb P(E)\) is the projective zero set of a nonzero homogeneous quadratic form, encoded by a symmetric matrix class \([A]\).

\(Q_A=\{[x]\in\mathbb P(E):x^T A x=0\}\)

Two Invariance Tests

  • Point scaling: \(q_A(\lambda x)=\lambda^2 q_A(x)\).
  • Equation scaling: \(q_{cA}(x)=c q_A(x)\).
  • The zero condition is well-defined modulo both scalars.
Matrix class first; chart coordinates second. \([x]=[\lambda x]\), \([A]=[cA]\)
Use this slide to make scaling harmless. Multiplying the vector representative changes the quadratic value by a square, and multiplying the matrix representative changes the equation by a nonzero scalar. Therefore the zero condition survives both choices. Students should hear that projective geometry begins by asking which formulas are independent of representatives.
First classification language

Rank And Signature Explain The Chart Gallery

The same projective habit reads empty charts, degenerate conics, ovals, and arcs as matrix phenomena.

Notebook artifact: affine chart \(z=1\) for six representative symmetric matrices.

Rank

Rank detects whether the quadratic equation is proper or degenerate. Low rank may collapse the visible object to a point, a repeated line, or a pair of lines.

Signature

Over the real numbers, signs decide whether the form has isotropic directions. Equal signs give no real points; mixed signs produce real projective points.

Chart Warning

An oval and two affine arcs can be different chart readings of projective data. Points at infinity may decide what the eye misses.

Invariant: rank and signature of a quadratic form. Visible chart: helpful, not authoritative.
The gallery is deliberately chart-level evidence, not a complete classification theorem. Ask students to read each panel twice: first as an affine picture, then as a matrix diagnosis. Rank detects degeneracy, while signature records whether real isotropic directions exist and how the chosen chart cuts them.

Real topology and line families

A Neutral Quadric Carries Two Rulings

The affine equation \(z=xy\) is a chart of \(xy-zw=0\).

Ruled Model

In the chart \(w=1\), the projective quadric \(xy-zw=0\) becomes \(z=xy\). Fixing \(x\) or fixing \(y\) gives a straight line contained in the surface.

\(L_a=\{(a,t,at,1):t\in\mathbb R\}\), \(M_b=\{(s,b,sb,1):s\in\mathbb R\}\)

Inspection Target

The surface bends globally, but every blue and teal trace is a line. This is topology and incidence, not just curvature in a drawing.

Notebook check: ruled_quadric_residual = 0.0 Two line systems, one quadric.
This is the moment to make a curved drawing feel linear. On the quadric xy minus zw equals zero, fixing one parameter gives an entire projective line, and fixing the other gives a second family. The affine surface bends, but each ruling is a genuine straight line contained in the quadric.
The central duality mechanism

Polarity Turns Points Into Hyperplanes

For invertible \(A\), the bilinear form supplies a point-hyperplane correspondence.

The polar line of an external point joins the two tangent contact points.

\(\Pi_x:\quad (Ax)^T y=0\)

On The Quadric

If \(x^T A x=0\), then \(\Pi_x\) is the tangent hyperplane at \([x]\).

Outside A Real Conic

The same linear equation gives the chord of contact for the two real tangents from the external point.

Inside The Conic

The polar line still exists algebraically, even when the contact points are no longer real in the chosen chart.

Notebook checks: contact_on_conic_error = 0.0; contact_on_polar_error = 0.0 Incidence becomes conjugacy.
Move slowly from matrix multiplication to geometry. For a proper quadric, A sends a point representative to coefficients of a hyperplane. When the point lies on the quadric, the hyperplane is tangent there; when the point is external to a real conic, the polar line joins the two contact points from the point.
Dual projective space

The Tangents Themselves Form A Quadric

A hyperplane coefficient vector is a point of the dual projective space.

Left: tangent envelope in \(\mathbb P(E)\). Right: the same family as points in the dual chart.

Tangential Equation

For a proper quadric with matrix \(A\), a hyperplane \(\ell^T x=0\) is tangent exactly when its dual coordinates satisfy

\(\ell^T A^{-1}\ell=0\)

Unit Conic Sample

For \(x^2+y^2-z^2=0\), the tangent at angle \(\theta\) has coefficients \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta,-1)\).

Teaching Read

The blue lines and orange locus are not separate phenomena; they are one tangent family in two projective spaces.

Notebook check: tangential_dual_error = 2.22e-16 Proper quadrics have proper duals.
The dual conic should not feel like a new object invented after the fact. It is the same tangent family viewed in the dual projective plane. A hyperplane coefficient vector is tangent precisely when it satisfies the inverse-matrix equation. The visual pair lets students compare the envelope of lines with the locus of their coefficients.
Coordinate changes act contragrediently

Move Points Forward, Move Equations Backward

The transformed equation is chosen so truth of \(x^T A x=0\) is preserved.

Transformed sample points lie on the transformed conic after the coordinate change.

\(y=Hx,\qquad A' = H^{-T} A H^{-1}\)

Why The Inverse Transpose?

  • Substitute \(x=H^{-1}y\) into \(x^T A x=0\).
  • The new matrix must satisfy \(y^T A' y=0\).
  • Hyperplanes transform by the dual rule.

Group Of A Quadric

The projective symmetries of a proper quadric are the classes of \(H\) with \(H^T A H\) proportional to \(A\).

Notebook check: projective_action_error = 1.33e-15 Contravariance keeps equations honest.
Emphasize the direction reversal in the formula. Points move by H, while equations move by the inverse transpose so that the truth of the quadratic equation is preserved. The figure verifies this with sample points: after transforming the conic, the moved samples still satisfy the moved equation up to numerical tolerance.

Applied lab

Design The Quadric Before Choosing The Chart

Constraints become equations in the entries of \(A\).

Unknown Matrix

Start with \(A=A^T\). A point condition \([x]\in Q_A\) is the linear equation \(x^T A x=0\) in the entries of \(A\).

Tangency Or Polarity

Use \(Ax\) as the coefficient vector of the polar hyperplane. Tangency asks this hyperplane to match the required contact data.

If A Pencil Remains

Study \(\det(A_0+tA_1)\) before selecting a member. A beautiful chart picture may hide a degenerate limit.

Lab prompt: design from incidence, tangency, and determinant checks. Do not let one chart choose the object.
Use the workflow as an applied lab rather than a recipe to memorize. Incidence constraints are linear in the entries of A, tangency constraints come from A times x, and a remaining pencil should be checked by its determinant before drawing. The lesson is to design projectively first and choose a chart last.

Recap: Choose The Right Ledger

Given a new matrix class \([A]\), decide what survives before trusting the picture.

Exercise

A conic in \(P^2\) has matrix \(A=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)\). Classify its chart \(z=1\), say whether polarity is an isomorphism, and predict what a generic pencil through it should check first.

Expected Reading

Rank two and mixed signs give a degenerate pair of real lines. Because \(A\) is singular, polarity is not an isomorphism. A pencil containing it should inspect determinant roots.

1. EquationName the homogeneous matrix class and the projective zero set.
2. MatrixCompute rank, signature, determinant, and inverse if it exists.
3. IncidenceCheck points, tangents, base locus, and polar hyperplanes.
4. FamilyIf parameters remain, study the discriminant before choosing a member.
5. ChartChoose an affine window only after the projective object is fixed.
6. SymmetryTransform equations by \(H^{-T}AH^{-1}\), not by visual guesswork.
Takeaway: projective quadrics are matrix classes plus invariant incidence. Next bridge: affine quadrics and metric normal forms.
Close by making students choose the right ledger for a new quadric. They should identify the matrix class, decide whether rank and signature explain the visible chart, check whether polarity is available, and say which statement would remain true after a projective change of coordinates. That is the chapter habit in miniature.
