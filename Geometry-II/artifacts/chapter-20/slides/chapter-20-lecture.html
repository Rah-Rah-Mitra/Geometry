Definition Frame

One Homogeneous Equation Contains Spheres And Planes

Coordinate Vector
Represent a generalized sphere in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) by the coefficients of \[ S(x)=a\|x\|^2-2\langle b,x\rangle+d=0, \qquad S=(a,b,d). \] Multiplying \(S\) by a nonzero scalar does not change the sphere.
Normalization
If \(a\neq 0\), the center is \(c=b/a\) and \(r^2=(\langle b,b\rangle-ad)/a^2\). If \(a=0\), the equation is a plane.
The drawn object lives in \(\mathbb{R}^n\); the lecture studies its coefficient point.
Spend time on why the homogeneous equation is the right entrance. Scaling the coefficients does not change the zero set, so the sphere itself behaves like a projective point. This makes planes, point spheres, and ordinary spheres live in one bookkeeping system instead of three separate cases.
Fundamental Form

The Form Measures Radius And Pairwise Contact

Quadratic Data
\[ Q(S)=\langle b,b\rangle-ad \] is homogeneous of degree two. For \(a\neq 0\), \(Q(S)/a^2=r^2\).
Polar Form
For \(S=(a,b,d)\) and \(T=(\alpha,e,f)\), \[ B(S,T)=\langle b,e\rangle-\frac{af+\alpha d}{2}. \] Orthogonality is the linear equation \(B(S,T)=0\).
The key algebra is the quadratic form, not the drawn circle. Explain that Q records the squared radius after normalization, while its polar form records pairwise relations. Once students accept that orthogonality is a bilinear equation, the rest of the chapter starts looking linear and computable.
Conceptual Picture

A Sphere Moves As A Point In Parameter Space

Point Spheres
A point \(x\) is the degenerate sphere \[ P_x=(1,x,\langle x,x\rangle), \qquad Q(P_x)=0. \] A real sphere is recovered by asking which point spheres lie on it.
Inspection Target
In the parameter space, the location of \([S]\) stores the entire Euclidean sphere. Moving a single point there changes center, radius, or even type.
This is where the abstract space becomes useful. A point of the large coordinate space stores a whole sphere, while the null cone stores degenerate point spheres. The picture should make students separate the physical plane from the parameter space in which a sphere itself moves.

Incidence

Intersections Appear After One Linear Cancellation

Subtract Two Normalized Spheres
If \[ \|x-c\|^2-r^2=0,\qquad \|x-d\|^2-s^2=0, \] then common points also satisfy \[ 2\langle d-c,x\rangle+\|c\|^2-\|d\|^2-r^2+s^2=0. \]
  • The quadratic terms cancel, leaving the radical axis or radical hyperplane.
  • A pencil keeps this linear incidence data visible across a family.
The radical axis slide links intersection geometry to linear algebra. Subtracting two normalized sphere equations removes the quadratic term, so common points lie on an affine hyperplane. That one cancellation explains why pencils, coaxal families, and intersection checks can be treated with linear combinations.
Families

A Pencil Is A Projective Line Of Spheres

Linear Pencil
The pencil generated by \(S\) and \(T\) is \[ [\lambda S+\mu T], \qquad (\lambda:\mu)\in\mathbb{P}^1. \] Its visible members may be circles, planes, or degenerate limits.
Notebook Lift
The plotted curve uses center-radius coordinates to show a coaxal family as a structured path, not a pile of unrelated circles.
Artifact: a coaxal pencil lifted to \((\hbox{center }x,\hbox{center }y,\hbox{radius})\).
The pencil is the first place where the space-of-spheres viewpoint pays rent. A one-parameter family of circles becomes a line in coefficient space, and the notebook lift plots part of that line in center-radius coordinates. Ask students to watch which coordinate changes and which incidence relation stays organized.
Symmetry

The Circular Group Preserves The Sphere Language

Structural Rule
A circular transformation sends generalized spheres to generalized spheres and preserves angle data encoded by \(B\). In coefficient language, it acts by transformations preserving the quadratic form up to scale.
Examples To Keep Together
Euclidean motions, similarities, inversions, and Mobius-type maps are best read as parts of one sphere-preserving transformation story.
Frame the circular group as the symmetry group of the sphere language. Inversions, similarities, and Mobius-type transformations may move centers and change radii, but they preserve the family of generalized spheres and the orthogonality relation. That is why the quadratic form is a structural object rather than a decoration.
Worked Transformation

Inversion Swaps Two Coefficients But Preserves \(Q\)

Unit Inversion
Put \(x=y/\|y\|^2\) in \[ a\|x\|^2-2\langle b,x\rangle+d=0. \] After multiplying by \(\|y\|^2\), the image sphere is \[ d\|y\|^2-2\langle b,y\rangle+a=0. \]
Invariant
The coordinate change \(S=(a,b,d)\mapsto S'=(d,b,a)\) leaves \(Q(S)=\langle b,b\rangle-ad\) unchanged.
Inversion is a useful laboratory because the calculation fits on one slide. Substituting x equals y over the squared norm swaps the leading and constant coefficients of a sphere equation. The zero set changes visibly, but the quadratic value Q is unchanged, so orthogonality tests survive the transformation.
Coordinate Endpoint

Polyspheric Coordinates Read Points Through A Sphere Frame

Coordinate Rule
Choose a sphere frame \(S_0,\ldots,S_{n+1}\). A point \(x\) can be recorded by polar data \[ u_i=B(P_x,S_i). \] The \(u_i\) are constrained by the same quadratic form.
Reading The Coordinates
Powers, signed contacts, and angle relations become coordinate entries. The payoff is not more numbers; it is a coordinate system adapted to spheres.
Use polyspheric coordinates as the chapter endpoint: after spheres become coordinate vectors, a point can be described by its powers or polar products against a chosen sphere frame. The coordinates are not arbitrary measurements; they inherit one quadratic compatibility relation from the same form that controlled spheres.
Live Check

Orthogonality Residuals Make Claims Auditable

Notebook Example
Circle \(A\): \(c_A=(0,0)\), \(r_A=1\).
Circle \(B\): \(c_B=(1.25,0)\), \(r_B=0.75\). \[ \|c_A-c_B\|^2=1.5625,\qquad r_A^2+r_B^2=1.5625. \]
Residual
\[ \rho(S,T)=\|c-d\|^2-r^2-s^2. \] The check file records \(\rho(A,B)=0.0\), \(\rho(A,C)=-0.2794\), and 80 sampled pencil members.
Run the diagnostic as an instructor habit, not as a numerical afterthought. Compute the center distance, compare it to the radius squares, and read the JSON residuals. The point is to train students to attach every sphere-space claim to an invariant that can be checked independently.
