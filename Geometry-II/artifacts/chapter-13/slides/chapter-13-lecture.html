1. Quadratic Data

From Formula To Polar Form

Definition

A real quadratic form is represented in coordinates by a symmetric matrix \(A\):

\(q(x)=x^T A x,\qquad q(tx)=t^2q(x)\)

The matrix entries depend on the basis, but the geometric counts we extract from \(A\) do not.

Polarization

The hidden bilinear form is recovered from \(q\) alone:

\(2B(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)\)
Quadratic form and polar formq and B carry the same information in characteristic not 2
Spend time on the cross term. The coefficient of x1 x2 appears twice in x transpose A x, once from each off-diagonal position, so the symmetric matrix is not bookkeeping trivia. Then derive polarization aloud as the operation that turns level-set intuition into the orthogonality relation used for radicals and reflections.
2. Coordinate Change

The Matrix Moves By Congruence

Basis replacement

If \(x=P y\), the same form in \(y\)-coordinates has matrix

\(A' = P^T A P\)
  • Congruence preserves the quadratic geometry.
  • The numerical entries of \(A\) are coordinate evidence, not invariants.
  • Diagonal form is a coordinate achievement, not a new form.
Stable counts

The reduction target is the signature triple:

\((p,n,z)=(\#\lambda_i>0,\#\lambda_i<0,\#\lambda_i=0)\)
p

positive independent directions

n

negative independent directions

z

radical dimension

Sylvester viewpointSimilarity tracks linear operators; congruence tracks forms
Contrast congruence with the more familiar similarity transformation without turning the slide into a side lecture. The key is that a quadratic form eats one vector twice, so changing coordinates introduces P transpose on the left and P on the right. The classifying information is therefore the inertia, not the raw eigenvalue list under arbitrary congruence.
3. Two-Dimensional Reading Practice

Signature Is A Visible Invariant

Inspection target
  • Definite forms have the origin as the only zero direction.
  • Indefinite forms have two isotropic lines in the plane.
  • Degenerate forms stretch level sets along forgotten directions.
Misconception check

Seeing \(q(x)=0\) away from the origin does not automatically mean degeneracy. It may also mean cancellation between positive and negative directions.

6. Diagonalization

Sylvester's Law Of Inertia

Statement

After a real congruence reduction, a quadratic form becomes diagonal with \(p\) positive, \(n\) negative, and \(z\) zero entries. The triple \((p,n,z)\) is independent of the reducing basis.

What changes

Diagonal entries can be rescaled, and a dense Gram matrix can become sparse. The counts cannot change.

The right panel separates negative, zero, and positive directions for the same form.

7. Proof Roadmap

Orthogonalization Splits What The Form Can See

Reduction procedure
  1. Choose a vector with nonzero value if one exists.
  2. Split off its \(B\)-orthogonal complement.
  3. Repeat until only isotropic or radical behavior remains.
  4. Pair isotropic directions when positive and negative signs coexist.
  5. Record the remaining positive, negative, and zero dimensions.
Algebraic test
\(\operatorname{rad}(q)=\ker A,\qquad \dim\operatorname{rad}(q)=z\)

If \(A r=0\), then \(B(r,w)=r^T A w=0\) for every \(w\). The form cannot detect motion along \(r\).

Teaching warning

Orthogonal diagonalization by a symmetric matrix theorem is efficient, but the geometric classification is about congruence and the subspaces it exposes.

Radicals are split or quotiented before nondegenerate geometryKeep q and B in parallel
Give the class a proof map without burying them in computations. The induction idea is to find one direction the form sees, remove all directions paired with it, and repeat. If the process reaches invisible directions, those are radical. If it reaches isotropic directions that still pair with each other, the next slide names the hyperbolic plane.
8. Indefinite Normal Form

Witt Decomposition Pairs Isotropic Directions

Hyperbolic plane
\(H=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad q(x,y)=2xy\)

The coordinate axes are isotropic but not radical: each axis pairs nontrivially with the other.

Witt index over the reals
\(\operatorname{wi}(q)=\min(p,n)\)

Each paired positive-negative direction becomes one hyperbolic plane; unpaired signs form an anisotropic remainder.

Hyperbolic planes are structure, not pathologyReal Witt index equals min(p,n)
The hyperbolic plane is the moment where isotropic directions become constructive. Stress that e and f each have zero value, yet neither is invisible because B of e and f is nonzero. Changing to e plus f and e minus f recovers one positive and one negative square, explaining why Witt decomposition is just inertia organized into paired geometry.
9. Form-Preserving Motions

Reflections Are Built From The Polar Form

Reflection formula
\(s_v(x)=x-2\,{B(x,v)\over B(v,v)}\,v,\qquad q(v)\ne 0\)
  • \(s_v(v)=-v\).
  • The hyperplane \(B(x,v)=0\) is fixed.
  • The identity \(s_v^T A s_v=A\) proves preservation.
Composition

In the Minkowski plane, two reflections move points along a branch of \(q=1\), the indefinite analogue of a rotation generated by mirror moves.

Artifact: figures/reflection-factorization.pngPreservation test: T^T A T = A
Connect the formula to the earlier polar form. A reflection is not guessed from the picture; it is forced by the requirement to flip v while fixing every vector B-orthogonal to v. In indefinite signature the picture looks unfamiliar, but the same preservation calculation keeps every point on the same quadratic level.
10. Applied Lab

Classify A New Form By A Short Checklist

Robust workflow
  1. Symmetrize \(A\); the skew part contributes nothing to \(x^T A x\).
  2. Compute \((p,n,z)\), rank, and radical basis.
  3. If \(p,n>0\), inspect isotropic geometry; if \(z>0\), split the radical.
  4. For transformations, verify \(T^T A T=A\).
