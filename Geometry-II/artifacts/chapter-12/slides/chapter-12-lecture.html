Definitions

From convex hulls to polytopes

Formal contract
\(K\) is convex if \(tx+(1-t)y\in K\) for \(0\le t\le 1\).
A polytope is \(P=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,\ldots,v_m\}\), equivalently a bounded intersection of finitely many half-spaces.
  • Vertices certify extremal data.
  • Facets certify inequalities.
  • Faces record where support is attained.
Definition slideV form and H form
Pause on the equivalence between vertices and half-spaces, because it is the workhorse behind the rest of the lecture. A picture of a polygon is not yet a mathematical object. Once students can name the vertex list and the inequalities, they can ask containment, support, area, and face questions without relying on visual guesswork.
Compact convex sets

Support functions turn a body into directional data

Support function
\(h_K(u)=\max_{x\in K}\langle u,x\rangle\)

For compact \(K\), the maximum exists. The exposed face in direction \(u\) is where the supporting hyperplane touches.

Recovering a convex body from support data is a geometric version of sampling every shadow.
Compactness supplies maximaSupport data
Connect compactness to existence instead of treating it as a topological decoration. The support value is useful only because the maximum is attained, and the points of attainment form a face. For a polytope this face may be a vertex, edge, or facet; for a smooth body it may shrink to one point.

Area and volume

Measurement begins with decompositions that can be checked

Computable invariant
\(A(P)=\frac12\left|\sum_i \det(v_i,v_{i+1})\right|\)
  • The formula is invariant under cyclic relabeling.
  • Orientation changes the sign, not the absolute area.
  • Higher-dimensional volume uses the same principle: decompose into signed simplices.
Area as signed determinant bookkeepingMeasurement contract
Make area feel like a reproducible computation. The determinant formula is not merely a shortcut; it records orientation and lets the notebook compare polygons with a stable numerical convention. This prepares the regular polygon approximation, where the same signed-area machinery becomes evidence for convergence toward the disk rather than a one-off calculation.

Regularity

Schlafli data compresses symmetry into two integers

Three-dimensional test
\(\{p,q\}\quad\text{with}\quad (p-2)(q-2)<4\)

Here \(p\) is the number of edges per face and \(q\) is the number of faces meeting at each vertex.

\(\{3,3\}\)tetrahedron
\(\{4,3\}\)cube
\(\{3,4\}\)octahedron
\(\{5,3\}\)dodecahedron
\(\{3,5\}\)icosahedron
Regular convex polyhedraCombinatorics plus angle deficit
Use the Schlafli symbols to connect symmetry, incidence, and angle deficit. The inequality is a compact way to say that the faces can close around each vertex in positive curvature. It also explains why the list is finite in three dimensions, even though regular polygons in the plane form an infinite family.
Rigidity

Cauchy's theorem: same convex faces, same shape

Proof roadmap
  • Compare corresponding dihedral angles edge by edge.
  • Mark each edge by whether the angle increased or decreased.
  • Use convex vertex geometry to rule out the required sign alternation.
  • Conclude all dihedral angles match, so the convex polyhedra are congruent.
Cauchy rigidityGlobal shape from local face data
Present Cauchy rigidity as a warning against flexible intuition. A paper model may wiggle when creased, but a closed convex polyhedron with corresponding congruent faces and the same incidence pattern has no such freedom. The proof idea tracks changes in dihedral angles and uses convexity to prevent the sign pattern that flexibility would require.
Approximation

Compact convex sets can be squeezed by finite data

Finite squeeze

Sample directions \(u_1,\ldots,u_N\), keep their support inequalities, and obtain an outer polytope. Sample boundary points and take their convex hull for an inner polytope.

\(P_{\mathrm{in}}\subset K\subset P_{\mathrm{out}}\)
The approximation improves when the sampled directions or boundary points become dense.
Compact convex approximationInside and outside finite models
Stress that approximation is not a retreat from exact geometry. It is the bridge from smooth compact bodies to finite polytopes. Outer approximations remember support inequalities, while inner approximations remember sampled boundary points. The squeeze is powerful because many quantities, including area and support values, can be compared from both sides.
Isoperimetry

The round body is the extremal limit

Inequalities to remember
\(L^2\ge 4\pi A\)
\(S^3\ge 36\pi V^2\)

Equality singles out the circle in the plane and the sphere in space. Polytopes approximate the extremal shape but keep a measurable deficit.

Isoperimetric limitExtremal compact convex sets
Treat isoperimetry as the chapter's global endpoint. Earlier slides asked how finite data represents, measures, and approximates convex objects. Here the question is extremal: among bodies with a fixed boundary measure, which one encloses the most? The polygon experiments make the circle and sphere feel reachable, while the inequality states the exact limit.
