Tangent Geometry

Normals Turn Focal Laws Into Reflection Laws

From invariant
to optical behavior
Principle
Gradient Of A Distance Sum
On an ellipse, differentiating \(d(P,F_1)+d(P,F_2)\) shows that the normal direction is the sum of the two unit focal directions.
\(n(P)\parallel u_1+u_2\)

Ellipse

A ray from one focus reflects to the other focus.

Hyperbola

The tangent bisects the angle between one focal ray and the extension of the other.

Computation

Unit vectors are easier to test than an angle diagram.

Geometry

The tangent line is where the first-order change of the invariant vanishes.

Tangent equals first-order invariantreflection law
Here the invariant becomes a tangent statement. If the focal sum is constant, then any tiny motion along the ellipse has zero first-order change in that sum. The normal therefore points in the combined direction of the two unit focal vectors. That is the analytic reason the familiar optical reflection law belongs in a Euclidean conics chapter.
Projective Shadow

Cyclic Points Encode Euclidean Angle

Metric statements
with projective bookkeeping
Fact
Cyclic Points
In the complex projective completion of the Euclidean plane, the two points \(I=(1:i:0)\) and \(J=(1:-i:0)\) lie on every circle.
Why This Matters
Angles and orthogonality can be tracked projectively by remembering how a conic interacts with the line at infinity and these two complex points.
  • Metric data has a projective shadow.
  • Focal constructions often pass through complex tangency data.
  • The real diagram is only one chart of the full conic story.
Cyclic points are bookkeeping, not plotted real pointscomplex projective plane
This is the bridge back to the previous projective conics chapter. The cyclic points are complex points on the line at infinity, so students should not look for them as real dots in the drawing. Their role is structural: they let Euclidean angle and circularity be described inside projective language, which explains why focal facts have projective shadows.
Dual View

Tangential Pencils Package Contact

Conic geometry
seen by tangent lines
Dual Formula
Tangent At A Point
For a conic \(X^T C X=0\), the tangent at a nonsingular point \(X\) is the line of points \(Y\) satisfying
\(X^T C Y=0\)
Lecture Use
A tangential pencil lets the class reason about contact, envelopes, and limiting positions without sampling the whole curve as points.
  • Point model: draw the locus and test distances.
  • Tangent model: draw the envelope and test contact.
  • Both models are needed for foci and cyclic-point constructions.
Dual conic: points become tangent linestangential pencils
Move from points on the conic to lines touching the conic. This dual view is useful because several Euclidean constructions are easier to describe through tangent contact than through sampled points. The formula X transpose C Y equals zero is compact, but the diagram makes the meaning clear: the curve is the envelope organized by its tangent lines.

Confocal families are a good antidote to static definitions. When a parameter moves, students can see which features remain fixed and which ones respond. The foci and axes stay organized, while the visible curves change shape. This prepares the class for normal and tangent behavior, because the family acts like a coordinate web rather than isolated examples.
