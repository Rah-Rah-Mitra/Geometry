Open by treating the sphere as the main character, not as a picture attached to plane geometry. The lecture route is deliberately invariant-first: charts and projections give coordinates, while angular distance, surface measure, circle preservation, and symmetry tell us what survives when the picture changes.

Working Model: The Unit Sphere

Coordinates are contracts
Definition
The unit sphere is \(S^2=\{p=(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}\). A chart assigns local coordinates, but the object being measured remains the embedded locus.
Three reading levels
  • Synthetic: points, planes, circles, tangencies, and intersections.
  • Algebraic: unit residuals, dot products, determinants, and parametrized curves.
  • Metric or topological: angle, area, compactness, orientation, and connectedness.
Misconception to disarm
A map of the sphere is a measurement device. It is not a flat replacement for the sphere.
Objects: sphere charts, great circles, tangent planes Invariant checks: unit length and orthogonality
This slide fixes the computational language for the rest of the deck. Emphasize that a point on the sphere is not merely a pair of coordinates; it is a unit vector, and any local coordinate description must respect the residual x squared plus y squared plus z squared equals one.

Stereographic Projection: Visible Circles, Distorted Lengths

Chart versus metric
Projection from the north pole
For \(p=(x,y,z)\ne N\), set
\(\sigma(p)=\left({x\over 1-z},{y\over 1-z}\right)\).
The inverse chart is
\(\sigma^{-1}(u,v)=\frac{1}{1+r^2}(2u,2v,r^2-1)\), \(r^2=u^2+v^2\).
What survives the projection?
  • Angles survive: stereographic projection is conformal.
  • Circles survive as generalized circles: circles or lines.
  • Lengths and areas do not survive without a scale factor.
Inspection target
A latitude circle looks like an ordinary Euclidean circle in the chart, but its radius is not its spherical length.
Notebook theme: draw the chart, then name the invariant Projection point removed: \(S^2\setminus\{N\}\)
The important distinction is not that stereographic projection is complicated; the formula is quite explicit. The distinction is what the formula promises. It preserves angles and generalized circles, so it is excellent for circle geometry, but it needs metric factors before it can answer length or area questions.

Measure And Intrinsic Distance

The sphere measures itself
Metric contract
For unit vectors \(p,q\in S^2\),
\(\displaystyle d_{S^2}(p,q)=\arccos(p\cdot q)\).
The distance is angular: it is the length of the shorter great-circle arc when \(p\ne -q\).
Area in two useful coordinates
Latitude-longitude: \(\ dA=\cos\phi\,d\lambda\,d\phi\).
Stereographic chart: \(\displaystyle dA={4\over(1+u^2+v^2)^2}\,du\,dv\).

This is the slide where students should stop trusting the drawing as a ruler. The chart gives useful coordinates, but the sphere supplies the measuring rules. Angular distance comes from the dot product, and surface measure appears as a density that changes across the chart.

Great Circles Are The Geodesics

Plane sections through the origin
Definition
A great circle is \(S^2\cap P\), where \(P\subset\mathbb R^3\) is a two-dimensional linear plane through the origin.
Geodesic statement
If \(p\ne\pm q\), the unique shortest path from \(p\) to \(q\) is the shorter arc of the great circle in the plane \(\operatorname{span}\{p,q\}\). Its length is \(\arccos(p\cdot q)\).
Proof roadmap
  • Rotate coordinates so the two points lie in an equatorial plane.
  • Compare any competitor with its projected angular coordinate.
  • Use symmetry: rotations preserve both the sphere and arc length.
Great circle = linear plane section Antipodal endpoints give many shortest half-circles
Use this slide to connect a familiar synthetic object with the metric definition. A great circle is easy to draw as a plane section, but its importance is intrinsic: away from antipodal ambiguity, it is the path selected by the sphere's own distance function.

Spherical Triangles: Curvature Made Visible

Angle excess is area
Girard formula on the unit sphere
For a geodesic triangle with interior angles \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\),
\(\operatorname{Area}(\Delta)=\alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi.\)
Positive curvature is detected by angle sum greater than \(\pi\).

Topology: No Single Honest Flat Atlas Page

Two charts, one compact surface
Compactness obstruction
The sphere is compact. A single global chart homeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^2\) would make it noncompact, so any plane coordinate system must either remove a point or identify boundary behavior.
North and south stereographic charts
On the overlap, the transition map has the form
\((u,v)\longmapsto {1\over u^2+v^2}(u,v)\)
up to orientation convention. The transition is smooth away from the origin.
Topological data to keep visible
  • Removing one point makes a plane chart possible.
  • Orientation is tracked by compatible transition maps.
  • The sphere closes up at the point a chart sends to infinity.
Topology explains why infinity appears in a chart An atlas is a compatibility statement
The topological point is modest but powerful. A stereographic chart is almost global, which is why it feels so useful, but the missing point matters. The second chart is not bureaucratic overhead; it records how the sphere closes and how computations transfer across infinity.

Clifford Parallelism: Circles Filling \(S^3\)

A higher-dimensional sphere lesson
Hopf-style circle family
Inside \(S^3\subset\mathbb C^2\), fix a complex line \([a:b]\). The set
\(H_{[a:b]}=\{(e^{it}a,e^{it}b):t\in\mathbb R\}\)
is a great circle. These circles partition \(S^3\).
Clifford parallel intuition
  • Distinct fibers are disjoint but evenly spaced.
  • Every fiber has the same length and the same local role.
  • Any two fibers are linked once in the projected picture.
Vocabulary caution
Parallel here is spherical and global, not Euclidean coplanar parallelism.
Chapter theme extends from \(S^2\) to circle families on \(S^3\) Projection makes linking visible
Clifford parallelism is a good place to slow down the word parallel. In Euclidean space, parallel lines avoid meeting because they share a direction. In the Hopf picture, the circles avoid meeting while filling the sphere with a uniform global organization.

Villarceau Circles From Clifford Tori

Projection turns fibers into torus circles
Clifford torus in \(S^3\)
For \(0\(T_r=\{(z_1,z_2): |z_1|=r,\ |z_2|=\sqrt{1-r^2}\}\).
Hopf fibers lie on these tori as distinguished circular curves.
Projected picture
After stereographic projection from \(S^3\) to \(\mathbb R^3\), the torus becomes a Euclidean torus and some Hopf fibers appear as Villarceau circles: slanted circles drawn on the torus.
Geometric lesson
A circle can be intrinsic to a sphere-family construction even when its projected Euclidean position looks surprising.
Villarceau circles are a projection-friendly trace of the circle-family story Do not mistake a slanted drawing for a non-circular object
This slide is meant to make the Villarceau circle feel less magical. The circle is not discovered by staring at a torus alone; it is explained by a sphere and projection story. The visual surprise is exactly why preserving circle-ness is such a useful invariant.

The Mobius Group Acts On The Riemann Sphere

Symmetry through stereographic coordinates
Mobius transformation
On \(\widehat{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}\),
\(z\longmapsto {az+b\over cz+d},\qquad ad-bc\ne0.\)
Stereographic projection identifies \(\widehat{\mathbb C}\) with the sphere.
What the group preserves
  • Generalized circles map to generalized circles.
  • Angles are preserved: the action is conformal.
  • The cross-ratio of four points is preserved.
  • Spherical distance is preserved only by the rotation subgroup.

The Mobius group is the natural end point of the projection story. Once the plane is completed by infinity, fractional linear maps become sphere transformations. Stress the hierarchy: circle preservation and angle preservation are broad Mobius facts, while distance preservation belongs to a smaller subgroup.

Worked Example: Same Points, Two Readings

An invariant check for a chart drawing
Choose three coordinate points
Let \(A=(1,0,0)\), \(B=(0,1,0)\), and \(C=(0,0,1)\). Then
\(A\cdot B=B\cdot C=C\cdot A=0\)
and every spherical side length is \(\arccos(0)=\pi/2\).
Now project
In a stereographic chart the Euclidean distances among the plotted points are not all spherical lengths. The chart is useful, but the dot-product check is decisive.
Instructor prompt
Move \(C\) along the meridian and predict which check changes: unit residual, angular side length, chart radius, or angle excess.
Computation separates representation from interpretation The check is short enough to rerun by hand
This worked example is intentionally small because it should be runnable in the room. The three coordinate points make the dot products obvious, so the spherical distances are obvious. The projected drawing can then be used as a foil: it helps visualization but does not replace the invariant calculation.

Recap: How To Read The Sphere

One deck, five contracts
Charts
Use charts to compute locally. Expect distortion unless the invariant explicitly says otherwise.
Metric
Use \(d(p,q)=\arccos(p\cdot q)\). Great-circle arcs, not Euclidean chart segments, are geodesic candidates.
Measure
Area is intrinsic and appears in charts through a density. For triangles, excess records curvature.
Topology
The missing point in stereographic projection is not a flaw; it is the trace of compactness.
Symmetry
Mobius maps preserve generalized circles and angles. Rotations form the distance-preserving part.
Exit exercise
Give one example of a chart-visible feature and one invariant feature for the same spherical construction. Explain which one should survive a Mobius transformation.
Final invariant checklist
