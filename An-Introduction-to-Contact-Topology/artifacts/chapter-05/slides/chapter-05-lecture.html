Open with the boundary-interface question. Up to this point the class has treated contact manifolds mostly intrinsically; this chapter asks when the same manifold can be read as the boundary of a symplectic world. The picture should make orientation and transversality visible before the definitions split into weak, dominating, and strong forms.
Definition 5.1.1

Filling Taxonomy

weak / dominating / strong

Weak Filling

$$\omega^{\,n-1}|_{\xi}>0$$

Requires positivity on contact hyperplanes. It does not require an exact collar near the boundary.

Dominating

$$\omega|_{\xi}\in [\,d\alpha|_{\xi}\,]$$

Matches the conformal class determined by the contact form and its coorientation.

Strong Filling

$$\xi=\ker(i_Y\omega|_{TM})$$

A Liouville vector field \(Y\) is defined near \(\partial W\) and points outward along \(M\).

All three assume \(\partial W=M\) as oriented manifolds Different boundary control, different theorem behavior
Separate the three adjectives carefully because they encode different amounts of boundary control. Weak filling is only a positivity condition on the contact hyperplanes. Domination fixes the conformal symplectic class on those hyperplanes. Strong filling adds an outward Liouville field, which is the data that later makes collars and gluing behave cleanly.
Contact Type Boundary

Strong Collar Model

\(Y=\partial_t\)
Strong Filling Data

\((W,\omega)\) is strong when a Liouville field \(Y\) exists near \(M\), points outward, and induces the cooriented contact structure.

Collar Calculation

Along the Liouville flow, \(\alpha_t=e^t\alpha\), so the symplectic collar is governed by \(d(e^t\alpha)=e^t(dt\wedge\alpha+d\alpha)\).

Orientation Check

The same local form with the arrow turned inward describes a concave boundary, not a convex filling boundary.

Redrawn from the local strong-collar evidence, not embedded from the PNG Exactness near \(M\) is the extra strong datum
Use the collar as the operational meaning of strong filling. Near the boundary the Liouville flow supplies a coordinate t and the symplectic form is the exact model d of e to the t times alpha. The arrow points outward at t equals zero; reversing that arrow would change convexity into concavity.
Theorem 5.1.5

Dimension Split

implication graph
Implications not displayed can fail in general Strong always implies domination, and domination always implies weak
The theorem is a small implication graph with a dimension-dependent hinge. In dimension three, weak positivity and domination coincide because the contact plane is two dimensional and all positive area forms differ by a positive function. In higher dimensions, domination and strong filling coincide, while weak filling remains genuinely broader.
Proof Move

Domination To Strong

\(n>2\)
Boundary Algebra

Choose \(\alpha\) so that \(\omega|_{\xi}=d\alpha|_{\xi}\). On \(TM\), write the remaining error as

$$\omega|_{TM}=d\alpha+\alpha\wedge\beta.$$

Where \(n>2\) Enters

The relation \((d\alpha\wedge\beta)|_{\xi}=0\) can be tested on two \(d\alpha\)-orthogonal vectors in \(\xi\), forcing \(\beta|_{TM}=0\).

Then Apply The Lemma

With \(\omega|_{TM}=d\alpha\), Lemma 5.1.4(c) gives a Liouville vector field \(Y\) near \(M\), hence a strong filling.

Higher-dimensional contact planes leave room for the wedge test The conclusion is exact boundary contact type
Sketch the proof move, not every calculation. Domination lets us choose alpha so that omega and d alpha agree on xi. The higher-dimensional wedge argument forces the extra beta term to vanish on the boundary, yielding omega restricted to T M equals d alpha. Then the lemma supplies the Liouville vector field.

Example 5.1.6

Convex And Concave At Once

\(S^1\subset\mathbb{R}^2\)
Interpretation

Symplectic convexity is a contact-type boundary condition, not naive visual convexity of the embedded hypersurface.

One hypersurface, two transverse Liouville choices Contact direction beats geometric appearance
This slide prevents a common intuition trap. Symplectic convexity is not ordinary visual bulging: the same unit circle in the plane can be made convex or concave by choosing different Liouville fields near it. What matters is the contact-type behavior of the vector field relative to the oriented boundary.
Definition 5.2.1

Cobordism Direction

\(M_-\to M_+\)
Oriented Boundary

$$\partial W=M_+\sqcup(-M_-).$$

The sign records that \(M_-\) is included with reversed orientation.

Boundary Contact Forms

Near \(\partial W\), \(\alpha=i_Y\omega\) restricts to contact forms defining \(\xi_+\) and \(\xi_-\).

Filling As Cobordism

A strong filling is a symplectic cobordism from \(\varnothing\) to \((M,\xi)\).

Cobordisms prepare the contact surgery language of the next chapter Positive boundary is convex; negative boundary is concave
A symplectic cobordism is a directed transition between contact manifolds. The positive boundary is convex because the Liouville vector points outward there; the negative boundary is concave because it points inward there. A strong filling is the special case with no negative boundary, so the empty set feeds into M.
Proposition 5.2.5

Gluing Cobordisms

transitivity

Match Contact Structures

On the shared \(M\), both contact forms define the same cooriented \(\xi\).

Insert Bridge

Use \(W_0=\{(t,x):0\le t\le f(x)\}\) with the symplectization form.

Conclude

The glued topological cobordism carries a symplectic form from \(M_-\) to \(M_+\).

Strong collars glue; weak cobordisms generally do not The bridge absorbs the conformal mismatch
Explain gluing as collar bookkeeping. The two contact forms on the shared boundary define the same cooriented contact structure, so they differ by a positive conformal factor. The bridge region interpolates in the symplectization direction, and the form d of e to the t times alpha minus keeps the glued manifold symplectic.
Section 5.3

Real Convexity Baseline

Hessian test
Defining Function

\(G\subset\mathbb{R}^m\) has smooth boundary when \(G=\{x:\rho(x)<0\}\) and \(d\rho\neq0\) near \(\partial G\).

Second Derivative Test

Convexity is detected by the Hessian of \(\rho\) on tangent directions \(X\in T_p(\partial G)\).

Why It Appears Here

Levi pseudoconvexity is modeled on this test but must be invariant under holomorphic coordinate changes.

Real convexity supplies the template, not the final contact structure The complex test lives on complex tangencies
Before entering complex tangencies, recall the real template. A defining function rho marks the region by rho less than zero and identifies the tangent hyperplane by d rho equals zero. Convexity is detected by the Hessian along tangent directions. The Levi form keeps this second-derivative spirit but discards the non-holomorphic part.
Definitions 5.3.2 And 5.3.5

Complex Tangencies And Levi Form

\(J\)-invariant tangent space
Complex Tangent Space

$$T_p^C(\partial G)=T_p(\partial G)\cap J(T_p(\partial G)).$$

It is the largest \(J\)-invariant subspace of the real tangent hyperplane.

Levi Form

$$L_p\rho(Z)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^2\rho}{\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k}(p)Z_j\bar Z_k.$$

Strict Levi pseudoconvexity means \(L_p\rho(Z)>0\) for \(0\ne Z\in T_p^C(\partial G)\).

Levi positivity is checked on complex tangencies, not every real tangent vector Biholomorphic invariance is the point of the complex Hessian
Introduce the complex tangent space as the largest J invariant part of the real tangent hyperplane. The Levi form is the Hermitian second derivative restricted to those complex tangencies. Strict positivity of this form is the complex-analytic analogue that will produce a contact structure on the boundary.
Proposition 5.4.2

Levi Positivity To Contact

\(\alpha=-d\rho\circ J\)
Contact Structure From Levi

If \(G\subset\mathbb{C}^n\) is strictly Levi pseudoconvex, then \(\xi_p=T_p^C(\partial G)\) is a contact structure on \(\partial G\).

Mechanism

\(\alpha=-d\rho\circ J\) has kernel \(T_p^C(\partial G)\), and Levi positivity makes \(d\alpha|_{\xi}\) symplectic.

Contact Translation

Complex boundary convexity is converted into the familiar contact condition on a hyperplane field.

Levi form measures exactly the positivity needed by \(d\alpha|_{\xi}\) Complex tangencies become contact planes
Connect the analytic definition to contact topology. The one-form alpha equals minus d rho composed with J, so its kernel on the boundary is exactly the complex tangent distribution. The identity d alpha of X and J X equals four times the Levi form turns strict Levi positivity into a symplectic form on xi.

Definition 5.4.5 + Theorems 5.4.6, 5.4.9

Stein And Holomorphic Fillings

\(i_{\nabla\rho}\omega=\alpha\)
Stein Input

A Stein manifold admits an exhausting strictly plurisubharmonic function \(\rho\).

Regular Sublevel

For a regular value \(c\), \(W_c=\{p:\rho(p)\le c\}\) strongly fills \(M_c=\rho^{-1}(c)\); \(i_{\nabla\rho}\omega=\alpha\) supplies the Liouville collar.

Holomorphic Filling

A strictly Levi pseudoconvex boundary with \(\xi=T^C(\partial W)\), oriented as the complex boundary, is holomorphic; in dimension three it is strongly fillable.

Every Stein filling is strong Holomorphic fillings in dimension three route to strong fillability
Close the construction chain in two steps. Stein regular sublevels give strong fillings because the gradient of rho points outward and contraction with the Kahler form gives the boundary contact form. A holomorphic filling is a strictly Levi pseudoconvex boundary with xi equal to the complex tangent distribution and the correct boundary orientation; in dimension three the source theorem routes it to strong fillability through Stein filling and Gray stability.
