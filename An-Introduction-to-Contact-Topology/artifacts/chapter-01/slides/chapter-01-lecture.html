Hyperplane Fields Before Contact

Hyperplane Field

A coorientable codimension-one distribution is written

\[\xi=\ker\alpha\subset TM.\]

The one-form is not unique: multiplying \(\alpha\) by a nowhere-zero function leaves \(\xi\) unchanged.

Frobenius Contrast

Integrability means local leaves exist with \(T_qN=\xi_q\). For \(\xi=\ker\alpha\), the test is

\[\alpha\wedge d\alpha\equiv 0.\]

Contact geometry begins after Frobenius fails maximally\(\xi\) is geometric, \(\alpha\) is a measuring choice
Start from the ordinary language of distributions. A coorientable hyperplane field can be written as the kernel of a global one-form, so the geometry can be tested by differential forms. Contrast this with Frobenius integrability: if alpha wedge d alpha vanishes, the planes fit together into leaves; contact geometry asks for the opposite local behavior.
Definition 1.1.3
dimension \(2n+1\)

Maximal Non-Integrability

Contact Condition

A hyperplane field \(\xi=\ker\alpha\) is contact when

\[\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n\ne 0\]

as a top-degree form at every point.

Dimension 3

\[\alpha\wedge d\alpha\ne 0.\]

Standard Model

\[\alpha_1=dz+\sum_j x_j\,dy_j.\]

Rescaling \(\alpha\mapsto \lambda\alpha\) multiplies the volume form by \(\lambda^{n+1}\), so nonvanishing is a property of \(\xi\).

Contact is open: nearby nonzero volume coefficients remain contact\((d\alpha)^n|_\xi\) is symplectic on each contact plane
Make the definition feel like a volume test. The one-form alpha picks the hyperplane field, while d alpha measures how the planes rotate. Requiring alpha wedge d alpha to the n to be nowhere zero says that the rotation fills every remaining direction and cannot be removed by rescaling the defining form.

The Standard Model

Spanning Directions

\[\xi_1=\ker(dz+x\,dy)=\langle \partial_x,\ \partial_y-x\partial_z\rangle.\]

Shear To Inspect

The direction \(\partial_y-x\partial_z\) tilts upward for one sign of \(x\) and downward for the other. The bracket detects the vertical escape:

\[[\partial_x,\partial_y-x\partial_z]=-\partial_z\notin\xi_1.\]

Projection Habit

Do not look for an integral surface. The visual is a quiver of the distribution itself because the contact condition says such a surface should not exist.

Standard contact plane fieldVisual target: shear in the tilted \(y\)-direction
Use the local figure as a reading exercise. The blue direction is partial x, while the tilted direction is partial y minus x partial z. At x equals zero the tilt disappears, and on either side it leans in opposite vertical directions. The bracket records the same shear algebraically by producing a direction outside the plane field.

Reeb Direction And Lambda Check

Reeb Vector Field

For a contact form \(\alpha\), \(R_\alpha\) is uniquely defined by

\[d\alpha(R_\alpha,-)=0,\qquad \alpha(R_\alpha)=1.\]

Standard Form

\[R_{\alpha_1}=\partial_z.\]

Saved Checks

final-sanity.json: artifact existence evidence for the chapter output tree.

Grounding check: \(\mathrm{volume}-\lambda=0\)Zero is not weak contact; it is failure of the condition
Separate the contact structure from the chosen contact form. For a fixed form, the Reeb vector is the unique direction killed by d alpha and normalized by alpha. In the lambda family, the saved check says that the volume coefficient equals lambda: zero is integrable failure, nonzero values are contact, and the standard Reeb direction is partial z.
Space Of Contact Elements
first-order hyperplane data

Contact Elements

Canonical Contact Structure

On the projectivized cotangent bundle, the natural contact hyperplane field is the kernel induced by the Liouville form.

\[\xi=\ker(\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}),\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{can}}=\sum_j p_j\,dq_j\ \text{upstairs}.\]

In the chart \(p_1=1\): \(\xi=\ker(dq_1+\sum_{j\ge2}p_j\,dq_j)\).

Reading The Bundle

A point of \(PT^*B\) is a base point plus a tangent hyperplane. The covector is only remembered up to nonzero scale.

Why This Matters

Line elements, slopes, fronts, and first-order differential equations all live naturally on the same contact manifold.

\(PT^*B\) stores tangent hyperplanes, not full tangent vectorsThe Liouville one-form supplies the contact field
Now explain why the name contact geometry is historically natural. A contact element is first-order tangent hyperplane data at a base point. Projectivized cotangent space records those hyperplanes up to scale, and the kernel of the Liouville one-form descends to a canonical hyperplane field on this bundle without choosing coordinates on the base.
Lie's Line Elements
contact transformations preserve lifted solutions

ODEs And Contact Transformations

Line Element Contact Form

\[dz-p\,dx=0.\]

A solution graph \(z=z(x)\) lifts to \((x,z(x),z'(x))\), which is tangent to this contact structure.

Contact Transformation Criterion

\[f^*(dz_1-p_1\,dx_1)=\rho(dz-p\,dx),\qquad \rho\ne0.\]

Legendre Transform Example

\[f(x,z,p)=(p,\ px-z,\ x)\]

ODE solutions become Legendrian curves in line-element spaceContact transformations move the equation and its solutions together
Translate the bundle picture into the classical line-element picture. A graph z of x lifts to the curve of its position and slope, and the equation dz minus p dx equals zero is exactly the tangency condition. A contact transformation preserves this condition up to a nowhere-zero scale, so it carries solution lifts to solution lifts.
Projective Facet
conic incidence as contact data

Projective Polarity

Polarity

A nondegenerate conic determines inverse maps between projective points and projective lines.

\[\phi(q)=q^T A,\qquad \phi^*(p)=A^{-1}p^T.\]

Contact Element Map

\[\Phi(q,p)=(\phi^*(p),\phi(q)).\]

If \(p(q)=0\), then \(\phi(q)\bigl(\phi^*(p)\bigr)=0\). Incidence survives while base and dual coordinates trade roles.

Projective geometry supplies a non-analytic source of contact transformationsPolarity swaps point and line data
Use the conic picture to show that contact geometry is not only analytic. A conic defines a polarity, sending a point to its polar line and a line to its pole. The induced map on contact elements reverses the base and fibre roles while preserving incidence, which is the projective version of preserving contact data.

Symplectic Linear Algebra Bridge

Dimension Formula

For a subspace \(U\subset(V,\omega)\), define \(U^\perp=\{v:\omega(v,u)=0\ \forall u\in U\}\). Then

\[\dim U+\dim U^\perp=\dim V.\]

TypeSymplectic testGeometric role
isotropic\(U\subset U^\perp\)allowed tangent directions
coisotropic\(U\supset U^\perp\)constraint hypersurfaces
Lagrangian\(U=U^\perp\)maximal isotropic data

For contact \(\xi=\ker\alpha\), \(d\alpha|_\xi\) is symplectic on each fibre of \(\xi\).

Contact manifolds are odd-dimensional; contact planes are even-dimensional symplectic fibresIsotropic dimension bounds later control Legendrian objects
Pause on the linear algebra because it is the bridge between contact and symplectic geometry. The symplectic orthogonal has the dimension formula shown on the slide. On each contact plane, d alpha is nondegenerate, so contact geometry contains a symplectic vector space at every point, one dimension below the ambient tangent space.
Hamiltonian Facet
phase space cotangent bundle

Classical Mechanics

Liouville And Symplectic Forms

\[\lambda=\sum_j p_j\,dq_j=p\,dq,\qquad \omega=d\lambda=dp\wedge dq.\]

Hamiltonian Vector Field

\[\omega(X_H,-)=-dH.\]

Energy Preservation

Skew-symmetry gives \(dH(X_H)=-\omega(X_H,X_H)=0\), so the flow stays on each level of \(H\).

The contact element form is the projectivized shadow of \(\lambda\)Hamiltonian dynamics turn forms into vector fields
Present mechanics as the cotangent-bundle facet of the same story. The Liouville form is the canonical one-form on phase space, its exterior derivative is symplectic, and a Hamiltonian determines a vector field by contraction with omega. The skew-symmetry then forces dH of X_H to vanish, which is the coordinate-free statement of energy preservation.
From Symplectic To Contact
hypersurfaces of contact type

Contact Type And Hopf Reeb Flow

Contact-Type Principle

If a hypersurface is both a Hamiltonian level and of contact type, its Reeb flow is a reparametrized Hamiltonian flow.

Standard \(S^3\) Example

\[\alpha=\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^2(x_j\,dy_j-y_j\,dx_j).\]

\[R_\alpha=2(x_1\partial_{y_1}-y_1\partial_{x_1}+x_2\partial_{y_2}-y_2\partial_{x_2}).\]

Dynamical Picture

The flow rotates both complex coordinates at the same speed, so each orbit is a Hopf circle.

Reeb dynamics live on contact hypersurfacesHamiltonian dynamics live in the surrounding symplectic manifold
Connect the mechanics slide to a contact hypersurface. On a contact-type level set, the restricted Liouville form becomes a contact form, and the Reeb dynamics agree with Hamiltonian dynamics up to reparametrization. The displayed standard sphere form explains the factor of two in the Hopf Reeb field.
Geodesic Flow
metric lifts tangent to cotangent

Cogeodesic Reeb Flow

Unit Cotangent Theorem

The Liouville form restricts to a contact form on \(ST^*B\), and

\[T\Psi(G)=R_\lambda.\]

Metric Dictionary

\(\Psi_b(X)=g_b(X,-)\) carries the geodesic field \(G\) on \(STB\) to the cogeodesic flow on \(ST^*B\).

With \(H(u)=\tfrac12 g^*(u,u)\), the same vector field is also Hamiltonian along \(H^{-1}(1/2)\).

Cogeodesic flow is geodesic flow after the metric lifts tangent vectors to covectorsThis is the engine behind wavefront propagation
Use the theorem as a compression of several languages. The metric identifies tangent and cotangent bundles by Psi. Under that identification, the geodesic vector field on the unit tangent bundle becomes the Reeb vector field of the Liouville contact form on the unit cotangent bundle. This is why geodesic propagation is a contact phenomenon.
Infinitesimal Automorphisms
strict versus conformal preservation

Contact Vector Fields

Strict Contact Vector Field

\[\mathcal L_X\alpha=0.\]

The flow preserves the chosen contact form.

Contact Vector Field

\[\mathcal L_X\alpha=\mu\,\alpha.\]

The flow preserves \(\xi=\ker\alpha\) while allowing rescaling of \(\alpha\).

Lie derivative tests the infinitesimal behavior of the flowKernel preservation is invariant under rescaling of \(\alpha\)
Distinguish strict preservation of a form from preservation of its kernel. If the Lie derivative of alpha is zero, the flow preserves the chosen measuring form. If the Lie derivative is mu times alpha, the form may rescale but its kernel is unchanged. This distinction will matter whenever the geometry is the plane field rather than a selected alpha.
Geometric Optics
front equals projection; lift stores tangency

Legendrian Fronts And Huygens

Contact Interpretation

A wavefront in \(B\) lifts to cooriented contact elements in \(ST^*B\). Propagate the lift by \(R_\lambda\), then project back to \(B\).

Huygens Principle

The evolved front is an envelope of propagated tangent data, not merely a pointwise displacement of the visible curve.

The same formula \(T\Psi(G)=R_\lambda\) explains why geodesic normals govern the optical construction.

Fronts may develop singularities; the Legendrian lift remains the cleaner objectContact elements carry tangent hyperplanes through propagation
Read the wavefront diagram through contact elements. A front is the visible projection; the Legendrian lift stores the missing tangent hyperplane or coorientation. Huygens propagation evolves those contact elements by the cogeodesic Reeb flow, and the next front is reconstructed as the envelope of the propagated tangent data.
Geometric Characterization
local first-jet test

Order Of Contact

Local Plane Field

\[\xi=\ker(dz+a(x,y,z)\,dx+b(x,y,z)\,dy),\quad a(0)=b(0)=0.\]

Contact Test At The Origin

\[a_y(0)\ne b_x(0).\]

Why A First-Order Test?

If equality holds, one can build a surface \(z=f(x,y)\) whose tangent plane matches \(\xi\) to second order. A contact plane field forbids that extra order of contact.

In dimension three, contact means every surface has contact order at most oneThe exterior derivative is hiding in \(a_y-b_x\)
Return to the original non-integrability test, now without relying on the exterior derivative as the first language. Locally, a plane field can be written as the kernel of dz plus a dx plus b dy. The inequality a_y at zero not equal to b_x at zero is exactly the first-order obstruction to finding a surface with second-order contact.

Topology Applications

Cerf Theorem Sketch

Classification of contact structures on \(S^3\) supplies rigidity for diffeomorphisms preserving the standard contact structure, feeding an extension problem over \(D^4\).

Property P

A knot \(K\subset S^3\) has Property P when every non-trivial surgery on \(K\) gives a 3-manifold that is not simply connected.

Theorem Preview

Kronheimer and Mrowka proved that every non-trivial knot in \(S^3\) has Property P; symplectic fillings of contact manifolds enter the proof strategy.

These are previews: proofs need later chaptersThe chapter shows why contact methods reach topological questions
Keep this slide as a dependency map, not as a proof. The chapter points forward to two topology stories. For Cerf's theorem, classification and rigidity of contact structures on the three-sphere enter the extension problem. For Property P, contact and symplectic filling ideas are part of the route to the theorem about non-trivial knot surgeries.
