Proof Pattern

A Reusable Proof Pattern

local check -> global construction
1. Datum Bundle, page, link, cover, plumbing chart, or group action.
2. Candidate Write a one-form from a connection, primitive, pullback, or collar model.
3. Diagnostic Check volume, Reeb invariance, Liouville transversality, or momentum.
4. Survival Prove the check persists after gluing, smoothing, filling, or quotienting.
Contact Diagnostic

$$\alpha \wedge (d\alpha)^n \neq 0$$

Nonzero top-degree form gives a cooriented contact structure.

Common Auxiliary Checks

$$\alpha(R)=1,\quad \iota_R d\alpha=0,\quad d\langle\mu,X\rangle=-\iota_{X_M}d\alpha$$

Repeated proof obligation: build, test, preserve Use this checklist on every construction below
The chapter repeatedly uses the same four-step proof pattern. Start with a local model, bundle datum, page, or group action. Write down a candidate one-form. Check contact volume, Reeb dynamics, exactness, or a momentum identity. Finally show that the form survives gluing, covering, filling, quotienting, or smoothing. This slide gives students the checklist to reuse.
Algebraic Links

Brieskorn Setup

hypersurface link in a sphere
Brieskorn Manifold

$$V(a)=\{z\in\mathbb C^{n+1}:\sum_{j=0}^n z_j^{a_j}=0\}$$

$$\Sigma(a)=V(a)\cap S^{2n+1}$$

Ambient One-Form

$$\alpha=\frac{i}{4}\sum_j(z_j\,d\bar z_j-\bar z_j\,dz_j)$$

Restrict \(\alpha\) to the link and test the contact volume.

Inspection Target

The link is not drawn as a copied figure; it is a schematic for the algebraic constraint plus the sphere constraint.

Input: weighted polynomial and unit sphere Output candidate: restricted standard contact form
Introduce Brieskorn manifolds as algebraic links cut out by a weighted complex hypersurface and the unit sphere. The ambient one-form is the standard contact form on the sphere, then the question becomes whether its restriction remains contact on the link. The diagram is intentionally schematic: the sphere supplies compactness, the hypersurface supplies the link, and the one-form supplies the test.
Brieskorn Contact Check

Weighted Reeb Flow

coordinate rotations preserve the link
Weighted Reeb Field

$$R_{\alpha_1}=2i\sum_j\frac1{a_j}\left(z_j\partial_{z_j}-\bar z_j\partial_{\bar z_j}\right)$$

Why The Weights Work

The flow sends \(z_j\) to \(e^{2it/a_j}z_j\), so each term \(z_j^{a_j}\) is multiplied by the same factor \(e^{2it}\).

Diagnostic

The Reeb direction is tangent to \(\Sigma(a)\), and the restricted top-degree contact volume remains nonzero.

Check reference: Reeb tangency plus contact volume Construction mechanism: weighted algebraic symmetry
The weighted Reeb field is memorable because each coordinate rotates at speed inversely proportional to its exponent. That choice makes the Brieskorn polynomial rotate by a common phase, so the zero set is preserved. The contact check is not a generic sphere argument alone; it uses compatibility between the weighted algebraic equation and the Reeb dynamics.
Filling Mechanism

Stein Filling From Smoothing

singular link -> smooth fiber boundary
Stein Domain Model

$$W_\varepsilon=f^{-1}(\varepsilon)\cap B_\delta,\qquad \lambda=-d^c\rho$$

The boundary contact form is \(\alpha=\lambda|_{\partial W_\varepsilon}\).

  • Plurisubharmonic radius gives a Liouville vector field.
  • The boundary is of contact type.
  • Gray stability protects the contact type for small smoothings.
Check reference: Liouville transversality at the boundary Survival mechanism: smoothing plus Gray stability
Move from the link to its filling by replacing the singular fiber with a nearby smooth fiber inside the ball. The plurisubharmonic radius gives a Liouville form, and the boundary carries the induced contact structure. Gray stability explains why small smoothings do not change the contact type unexpectedly. The point is that the algebraic construction comes packaged with a Stein filling.
Circle Bundles

Boothby-Wang Forward

normalized integral class -> contact total space
Input

$$[\omega]/2\pi\in H^2(B;\mathbb Z)$$

Choose a principal \(S^1\)-bundle whose connection has curvature \(\pi^*\omega\).

Contact Form

$$d\alpha=\pi^*\omega,\qquad \alpha(R)=1$$

$$\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n=\alpha\wedge\pi^*(\omega^n)$$

Diagnostic

The contact volume is the fiber direction times the symplectic volume of the base.

Input: [omega over two pi] integral Output: regular contact form with circle-fiber Reeb flow
For the forward Boothby-Wang construction, the input is the normalized integral class omega over two pi on the base, with Euler class minus that class. A principal circle bundle with a connection one-form has curvature equal to the pullback of the symplectic form. The contact volume factorizes as the connection form times the symplectic volume downstairs, and the Reeb field is the circle generator.
Regular Reeb Fields

Boothby-Wang Converse

contact dynamics -> symplectic quotient
Converse

If the Reeb flow is regular, \(M\to B=M/S^1\) is a principal circle bundle.

Descended Form

$$d\alpha=\pi^*\omega,\qquad [\omega/2\pi]\in H^2(B;\mathbb Z)$$

Because \(d\alpha\) is horizontal and invariant, it descends to the base.

Check Reference

Regularity turns Reeb dynamics into bundle geometry; the contact volume upstairs becomes symplectic nondegeneracy downstairs.

Input: regular Reeb flow Output: symplectic quotient with normalized integral curvature class
The converse begins with a regular Reeb flow: all orbits are organized as the fibers of a circle bundle. The quotient inherits a symplectic form because d alpha is basic, and the connection form is alpha itself. This is a useful reversal of perspective. Regularity upgrades a dynamical condition on M into a symplectic base whose normalized omega over two pi is integral.
Open Books

Open Book Topology

binding + pages + monodromy
Topological Data

Page \(P\), binding \(B=\partial P\), and monodromy \(\phi:P\to P\) with \(\phi=\mathrm{id}\) near \(\partial P\).

Manifold Assembly

$$M=(P_\phi)\cup_{\partial P\times S^1}(\partial P\times D^2)$$

Boundary Condition

The identity collar near \(\partial P\) is what lets the binding neighborhood be glued with a clean polar model.

Input: page and exact monodromy near the boundary Next slide: add the contact form
An open book decomposes the manifold into a binding and pages swept around the binding. The page is a compact manifold with boundary, and the monodromy is the return map used in the mapping torus. The boundary condition that the monodromy is the identity near the boundary is what allows the binding neighborhood to be glued cleanly.
Supported Contact Structures

Contact Form For An Open Book

exact page + collar model
Exact Page Data

$$ (P,d\lambda),\qquad \phi^*\lambda-\lambda=dh,\qquad \phi=\mathrm{id}\ \text{near}\ \partial P$$

Page Check

\(d\alpha\) restricts to a symplectic form on every page.

Binding Check

\(\alpha\) is positive on the oriented binding.

Construction Habit

Exactness supplies a mapping-torus form; the collar model supplies the missing binding neighborhood.

Check reference: page symplecticity and binding positivity Survival mechanism: identity collar near the boundary
The contact form compatible with an open book is built from exact symplectic data on the page and a collar model near the binding. Exact monodromy controls the mapping torus part, while the boundary polar coordinates control the binding. Students should read the two checks together: d alpha is symplectic on each page and alpha is positive along the binding.
Higher-Dimensional Product

Bourgeois On \(M\times T^2\)

open book data adds two torus directions
Bourgeois Form

$$\beta=\alpha+x_1\,d\theta_1-x_2\,d\theta_2$$

The functions \(x_1,x_2\) are supplied by the open-book map near the binding.

Contact Volume Role

The two torus directions are coupled to open-book coordinates, supplying the missing factors in the top-degree test.

Construction Lesson

Giroux-Mohsen existence of compatible open books feeds directly into a product contact structure.

Input: contact M supported by an open book Output: contact structure on M x T2
The Bourgeois construction adds two torus directions to a contact manifold supported by an open book. The source convention writes the added terms as x one d theta one minus x two d theta two, with the open-book coordinates supplying x one and x two. The extra terms are not decorative; they supply the missing directions in the contact-volume calculation on M times T two.
Gluing Operation

Fiber Connected Sum

match contact neighborhoods before gluing
Neighborhood Form

$$\alpha_N+r^2\,d\theta$$

Use codimension-two contact embeddings and a fibre-orientation-reversing normal-bundle isomorphism \(\Phi:N_0\to N_1\).

Remove

Delete standard tubular neighborhoods of the chosen contact fibers.

Glue

Identify collars with reversed angular coordinate and matched normal forms.

Check Reference

The local normal form makes the contact condition visible before any global interpolation is attempted.

Input: contact submanifolds with matched normal data Survival mechanism: standard neighborhoods and collar gluing
The fiber connected sum is a controlled gluing operation along codimension-two contact embeddings with a fibre-orientation-reversing normal-bundle isomorphism. Standard neighborhoods look like N times a disk with alpha_N plus r squared d theta. Removing tubular neighborhoods and gluing the collars with reversed angular direction lets the contact forms patch after scaling and interpolation.
Covering Operation

Branched Cover Local Model

pullback plus repair term
Local Cover

$$p_k(z,t)=(z^k,t)$$

The branch image must be a contact submanifold: \(TM'_0\cap\xi_0\) is contact; pullback contactness is then repaired near \(z=0\).

Repair Form

$$\beta=C\,p^*\alpha_0+g(r)r^2\gamma$$

Choose \(C\) large and \(g\) adapted near the branch locus.

Diagnostic

The pullback dominates away from the branch set; the \(r^2\gamma\) term restores the missing angular contact direction near it.

Input: branched covering map and base contact form Survival mechanism: explicit local repair term
The branched covering model p_k sends the normal complex coordinate z to z to the k, and the branch image must itself be a contact submanifold. Pulling back the contact form is good away from the branch locus but degenerates in the normal direction near the branch. The repair term g of r times r squared times gamma restores the missing angular contribution, and taking C large keeps the inherited contact behavior dominant elsewhere.
Exact Symplectic Assembly

Plumbing Cotangent Disc Bundles

swap base and fiber coordinates
Exact Input

Cotangent disc bundles carry the canonical primitive \(\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}\).

$$\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}=\sum_i p_i\,dq_i$$

Plumbing Move

In overlapping Darboux charts, use the signed exchange \((q,p)\mapsto(-p,q)\); the sign is what makes the Liouville bookkeeping work.

Contact Boundary

The resulting Liouville domain has a boundary contact form after corners are rounded.

Input: exact cotangent domains Check reference: canonical primitive across the plumbing chart
Plumbing builds new exact symplectic domains from cotangent disc bundles using the signed local map from q,p to minus p,q. The canonical one-form is the bookkeeping device: the sign in the pullback is what makes the local symplectic model patch correctly. The contact manifold of interest is then the boundary of the plumbed Liouville domain.
Boundary Contact Type

Smoothing The Plumbed Boundary

corners rounded, Liouville field transverse
Contact-Type Boundary

If a Liouville vector field \(Y\) is transverse to the smoothed boundary, then \(\alpha=\iota_Y\omega|_{\partial W}\) is contact.

Before

Plumbing produces corners where local boundary faces meet.

After

Rounding keeps the Liouville field outward along the new boundary.

Same Pattern

Candidate primitive -> contact-type boundary check -> smoothing preserves the check.

Check reference: Liouville transversality after rounding Output: contact boundary of the plumbed domain
A plumbing creates corners, so the boundary has to be rounded before it is read as a contact manifold. The Liouville vector field gives the check: after smoothing, it remains transverse to the boundary, and the restricted primitive is a contact form. This is the same construction pattern again, now with the survival mechanism being contact type under smoothing.
Quotient Construction

Contact Reduction

momentum map controls descent
Contact Momentum Map

$$\langle\mu(p),X\rangle=\alpha_p(X_M(p))$$

Derivative Identity

$$d\langle\mu,X\rangle=-\iota_{X_M}d\alpha$$

It follows from \(L_{X_M}\alpha=0\) and Cartan's formula.

Reduction Check

At \(\mu=0\), group directions are in \(\ker\alpha\); after regularity and freeness, the quotient inherits a contact hyperplane field.

Input: contact action preserving alpha Survival mechanism: zero momentum level and quotient descent
For contact reduction, a Lie group acts preserving alpha and the momentum map records alpha applied to infinitesimal generators. The derivative identity follows from Cartan's formula and invariance of alpha. On the zero level, the group directions lie inside the contact hyperplane and the quotient inherits the correct hyperplane distribution when regularity and freeness hypotheses hold.
