Topological Move

The Surgery Move

Definition 6.1.1
Surgery Formula

Given an embedded framed tube \(S^k \times D^{n-k} \subset M\), form

\[ M^*=\bigl(M\setminus(S^k\times \operatorname{Int}D^{n-k})\bigr) \cup_{S^k\times S^{n-k-1}} (D^{k+1}\times S^{n-k-1}). \]

Input

Embedding plus trivialization of the normal bundle.

Output

The belt sphere \(S^{n-k-1}\) remembers the reverse surgery.

Topological surgery depends on the framed embedding. Reverse move: surgery along the belt sphere.
Begin with the purely smooth operation. The formula removes a framed tubular neighborhood of the attaching sphere and inserts the complementary product. The diagram should be read left to right: the attaching sphere determines the missing tube, and the replacement swaps which sphere is visible as the new belt sphere.
Cobordism Language

Cobordism As A Handle

Index \(k+1\)
Movie Of The Surgery

A cobordism \(W\) is obtained from \([-1,1]\times M\) by attaching a handle to the upper boundary.

\[ \partial W \cong (-M)\sqcup M^* . \]

Lower Boundary

Looks like \(S^k\times \operatorname{Int}D^{n-k}\), the attaching region.

Upper Boundary

Looks like \(D^{k+1}\times S^{n-k-1}\), the replacement region.

Contact surgery keeps this handle movie, then replaces the gradient flow by a Liouville flow.

Core = what is attached. Cocore = where the reverse surgery lives.
Translate the cut-and-paste into a movie. A handle cobordism has a lower boundary that remembers the old manifold and an upper boundary that is the surgered manifold. The core and cocore are bookkeeping devices: the core records what is attached, while the cocore records the belt sphere for the reverse move.
Contact Upgrade

Why Contact Surgery Needs More Data

Liouville collar
Smooth Gluing Is Not Enough

A contact boundary must come from a symplectic cobordism whose Liouville vector field induces the boundary contact forms.

Standard Collar

\[ \omega=d(e^t\alpha), \qquad Y=\partial_t,\qquad \alpha=i_Y\omega\big|_{TM}. \]

Target For The Handle

Replace the topological gradient lines by Liouville trajectories that identify neighborhoods symplectically.

Contact boundary = hypersurface transverse to a Liouville vector field. The handle must glue symplectically, not only smoothly.
Stress that a smooth handle is not yet a contact construction. Contact geometry needs a symplectic cobordism with a Liouville vector field pointing the right way near the boundary. The symplectization formula is the standard collar model, and contact surgery asks the handle to carry the same Liouville-flow logic.
Local Matching

Strict Neighborhood Input

Theorem 6.2.2, using 2.5.8
Symplectic Normal Bundle

\[ SNM(L)=(TL)^\perp/TL. \]

The quotient is measured inside the contact planes using \(d\alpha\).

Input Data

  • isotropic submanifolds \(L_0,L_1\),
  • a diffeomorphism \(L_0\to L_1\),
  • a symplectic-bundle isomorphism \(SNM(L_0)\to SNM(L_1)\).

Output

A strict contactomorphism of neighborhoods matching the prescribed normal data.

Strict means the chosen contact forms are matched, not merely their kernels. The model handle can be glued only after this local identification.
Use the neighborhood theorem as the local matching device. An isotropic submanifold is not determined only by its image; its symplectic normal bundle records the transverse contact-plane data. A strict contactomorphism of neighborhoods exists once the base diffeomorphism and the symplectic normal bundle isomorphism are aligned.
Model Handle

Weinstein Model Handle

Symplectic coordinates
Standard Symplectic Handle

\[ \omega_0=\sum_{j=1}^n dp_j\wedge dq_j \]

Handle Function And Liouville Form

\[ g(q,p)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(-\frac12 q_j^2+p_j^2\right) +\frac14\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}(q_j^2+p_j^2) \]

\[ \alpha_0=i_Y\omega_0. \]

The handle is the part of \(\mathbb R^{2n}\) with \(-1\le g(q,p)\le 1\) swept out by the \(Y\)-flow through a lower neighborhood of \(S_H^{k-1}\).

The model supplies both the smooth handle and its contact boundary forms. \(\alpha_0=i_Y\omega_0\) is the boundary contact form.
This is the analytic heart of the construction. The quadratic function g gives the handle its lower and upper levels, and the vector field Y is both Liouville for the standard symplectic form and gradient-like for g. The displayed one-form alpha zero is the contact form induced on the level hypersurfaces.
Main Construction

Contact Surgery Theorem

Theorem 6.2.5
Theorem 6.2.5

If \(S^{k-1}\subset (M,\xi=\ker\alpha)\) is isotropic and its conformal symplectic normal bundle is trivialized, then there is a symplectic cobordism from \((M,\xi)\) to the manifold \(M^*\) obtained by surgery along \(S^{k-1}\) with the natural framing.

Proof Skeleton
  1. Use the strict neighborhood theorem near the attaching sphere.
  2. Identify it with the lower neighborhood of the model handle.
  3. Glue along Liouville flow lines.
  4. Read the upper boundary as the new contact manifold.
Hypothesis: trivialized conformal symplectic normal bundle. Conclusion: contact structure on \(M^*\), unchanged away from the local operation.
State Theorem 6.2.5 as the first completed upgrade. If the isotropic attaching sphere has a trivialized conformal symplectic normal bundle, the model handle glues into a symplectic cobordism. The output contact structure is unchanged away from the surgery region, so the construction is local but its topology is global.

Natural Framing

What the handle uses
Normal Bundle Split

\[ NL \cong R \oplus J(TL) \oplus SNM(L). \]

Fixed Part

The handle framing uses the natural trivialization of \(-R\oplus J(TS^{k-1})\) together with the tangent framing.

Chosen Part

A symplectic trivialization of \(SNM(L)\) completes the normal framing used by the contact handle.

Dimension Three

For a Legendrian knot, the natural surgery framing is one left twist relative to the contact framing.

Framing is not extra decoration; it is the gluing datum for surgery. The contact model restricts which smooth framings can occur.
Explain why the word natural is doing work. A topological framing of an isotropic sphere splits into the Reeb line, the complex image of the tangent bundle, and the symplectic normal data. The first two are fixed by the stabilized tangent trivialization, so the chosen symplectic normal trivialization supplies the remaining freedom.
Homotopical Criterion

Framing Theorem 6.3.1

\(n>2\) caveat
Theorem 6.3.1

Let \((M,\xi)\) have dimension \(2n-1\), \(n>2\), and let a framed \((k-1)\)-sphere be described by \(f:S^{k-1}\times D^{2n-k}\to M\). If the almost complex structure on a convex filling \(W\) extends over the \(k\)-handle, then \(W\cup_f H\) carries a symplectic form and the new boundary is produced by contact surgery.

Read The Caveat

The tight Legendrian knot case \(n=k=2\) is not governed by the same free framing behavior.

Necessary and sufficient in the high-dimensional range described by the theorem. The three-dimensional Legendrian case has a separate story.
Present the theorem as a compatibility test rather than as another construction. For dimensions with n greater than two, a framed smooth attaching sphere can be realized by contact surgery exactly when the induced almost complex structure extends over the handle. The dimension three Legendrian case needs separate care, which the next slides isolate.
Flexibility Check

H-Principle And Rotation

Legendrian immersions
Formal To Genuine

The h-principle starts with a fibrewise injective complex bundle map \(TL\otimes \mathbb C \to \xi\) and homotopes toward an isotropic immersion.

Dimension Three Counter

\[ \operatorname{rot}(\gamma)=\frac{c_- - c_+}{2}. \]

For Legendrian immersions of \(S^1\) in \((\mathbb R^3,\xi_{\mathrm{st}})\), rotation controls regular homotopy.

The theorem supplies embeddings after general position in the high-dimensional cases; the three-dimensional knot case must track classical invariants.

Formal isotropic data can often be made geometric. For Legendrian knots, front cusps make rotation visible.
The h-principle separates formal isotropic data from genuine isotropic embeddings. In dimension three, Legendrian regular homotopy of immersed circles is governed by rotation. The front formula is included because it makes rotation computable: downward and upward cusp counts record the winding of the tangent direction in the contact planes.
Dimension Three Warning

Stabilization Changes The Framing

Bennequin obstruction
Negative Twists Are Easy

Adding a front zigzag changes the contact framing by a negative twist and changes rotation by the sign of the stabilization.

Tightness Restriction

In a tight contact 3-manifold, the Bennequin inequality prevents arbitrary positive twisting for null-homologous Legendrian knots.

Consequence

The high-dimensional framing theorem fails in the tight \(n=k=2\) case.

Zigzags are local operations; the obstruction is global tightness. This is why contact Dehn surgery needs its own coefficient language.
Use this slide as the warning for dimension three. Adding a zigzag always gives a negative twist relative to the contact framing and changes rotation by a sign. Positive twists are not freely available in tight contact manifolds because the Bennequin inequality restricts how large the classical invariants can become.
Three-Dimensional Language

Contact Dehn Surgery Grid

Section 6.4
Contact Coefficient

For a Legendrian knot \(K\), the contact framing gives the longitude \(\lambda_c\) on \(\partial \nu K\). Contact Dehn coefficients are measured relative to \(\lambda_c\).

Contact \((-1)\) Surgery

The contact surgery of Theorem 6.2.5 along a Legendrian knot is contact \((-1)\)-surgery relative to the contact framing.

Topological integer surgery is familiar, but the contact-friendly coefficients are \(1/k\), especially \(+1\) and \(-1\).

\(\mu\) = meridian, \(\lambda_c\) = contact longitude. Changing the reference framing changes the numerical coefficient.
Now switch to the three-dimensional language. The contact longitude supplies the reference slope on the boundary torus, so the coefficient is read relative to the contact framing rather than a Seifert surface unless one is specified. The special coefficient minus one is the Weinstein handle surgery from Theorem 6.2.5.
Coefficient Warning

The Plus-Minus One Warning

Contact \(r\)-surgery
Not Just Topology

For a general rational \(r\), the glued solid torus may admit more than one tight contact structure compatible with the boundary data.

Well-Behaved Family

For \(r=1/k\), the relevant tight solid torus is unique, so the contact operation is well defined.

Presentation Algebra

Contact \((+1)\) and \((-1)\) surgeries are the elementary moves used in the Ding-Geiges theorem.

The contact coefficient carries both a slope and a contact fill-in choice. The \((\pm1)\) moves are the reliable generators for presentations.
Contact r-surgery is more delicate than topological Dehn surgery because the tight contact structure on the glued solid torus may not be unique. The coefficients one over k are better behaved, and plus or minus one surgeries become the natural algebra for presentations. The slide keeps that warning adjacent to the cancellation picture.
Local Inverse Law

Cancellation Lemma

Proposition 6.4.5
Cancellation Principle

After contact \((-1)\)-surgery on a Legendrian knot \(K\), contact \((+1)\)-surgery on the corresponding belt sphere cancels the operation.

Why It Works

The Liouville flow carries a Legendrian push-off of the attaching sphere to the belt sphere.

This lemma turns \((+1)\)-surgery into an operation that can be undone by adding symplectic \((-1)\)-handles later.

Attaching sphere and belt sphere are the two sides of one handle. The cancellation is contact-geometric, not only topological.
Read the cancellation lemma as the local inverse law for Legendrian handle moves. A contact minus one surgery creates a belt sphere, and a contact plus one surgery on that belt sphere recovers the original contact manifold. The Liouville-flow picture is what identifies a Legendrian push-off with the belt sphere.
Presentation Theorem

Ding-Geiges Presentation

Theorem 6.4.4
Statement

Every closed contact 3-manifold can be obtained from \((S^3,\xi_{\mathrm{st}})\) by contact \((\pm1)\)-surgery along a Legendrian link.

Topological Ingredient

Lickorish-Wallace gives integer surgery presentations of closed oriented 3-manifolds.

Contact Ingredient

Legendrian realization, stabilization, Lutz-twist control, and cancellation convert the presentation to \((\pm1)\)-moves.

The theorem is stronger than mere existence of contact structures. It gives a surgery calculus based at \((S^3,\xi_{\mathrm{st}})\).
The presentation theorem is the payoff for the three-dimensional discussion. Lutz twists handle the passage to an overtwisted reference when needed, the Lickorish-Wallace theorem supplies an integer surgery description, and the contact conversion rewrites the result as plus or minus one Legendrian surgeries from the standard three-sphere.
Filling Consequences

Fillings And Tightness

Section 6.5
Embedding Theorem

Any weak symplectic filling of a contact 3-manifold embeds into a closed symplectic 4-manifold.

Eliashberg-Gromov Tightness

\[ \text{weakly symplectically fillable} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{tight}. \]

Contradiction Route

An overtwisted disc gives a Legendrian boundary with \(tb=2\); a \((-1)\)-handle produces a positive self-intersection sphere, forbidden in the closed symplectic setting.

Contact surgery turns filling questions into handle and cap questions. Weak fillability rules out overtwistedness.
Close by showing why the surgery presentation matters beyond existence. It lets one cap strong fillings, attach handles after a weak filling is made locally strong near Legendrian knots, reduce to homology spheres, and finally prove that weak symplectic fillability rules out overtwisted discs. The dependency graph should make the tightness theorem feel engineered rather than mysterious.
