Proof dependency map

The Chapter Thesis

Moser pattern
Moser Equation

\[ \dot\alpha_t+i_{X_t}d\alpha_t=\mu_t\alpha_t . \]

The unknown is a time-dependent vector field \(X_t\), up to a harmless conformal factor \(\mu_t\).

One Repeated Move

Choose \(X_t\) so the derivative of the pulled-back contact data lies inside the ideal generated by the current contact form.

Why It Works

The contact condition makes \(d\alpha_t\) nondegenerate on \(\xi_t\), so the horizontal part of \(X_t\) is solvable once the Reeb part is chosen.

Chapter mapMoser to Gray, Darboux, neighborhoods, and isotopy extension
Frame the whole lecture around a single proof dependency. Moser-type equations produce vector fields; vector fields produce flows; flows produce equivalences and normal forms. Darboux, Gray stability, neighborhood theorems, and isotopy extension should feel like variations of one method, not isolated theorems.
Examples of contact manifolds

First Examples: Same Contact Structure

contactomorphism test
Contactomorphism

A diffeomorphism \(f:(M_1,\xi_1)\to(M_2,\xi_2)\) is contact if \(Tf(\xi_1)=\xi_2\). For contact forms:

\[ f^*\alpha_2=\lambda\,\alpha_1,\qquad \lambda\neq 0. \]

Standard Model In Polar Coordinates

\[ \alpha_2=dz+\sum_j r_j^2\,d\phi_j =dz+\sum_j(x_j\,dy_j-y_j\,dx_j). \]

Pullback Check

For \(\alpha_1=dz+\sum_j x_j\,dy_j\), set \(F(x,y,z)=(x,y/2,z+\frac12\sum_jx_jy_j)\). Then \(F^*\alpha_2=\alpha_1\).

Section 2.1Contactomorphism is a plane-field statement, not literal equality of forms
Use this slide to separate the form from the contact structure. A contactomorphism only has to carry the kernel distribution to the other kernel, so the pullback may be multiplied by a nowhere-zero function. The explicit standard-to-polar calculation is a quick sanity check that formulas are not decorative.
Example contrast

Standard Versus Overtwisted \( \mathbb R^3 \)

original redraw
Standard Form

\[ \alpha_{\mathrm{st}}=dz+r^2\,d\phi \quad\text{or}\quad dz+x\,dy-y\,dx . \]

Overtwisted Model

\[ \alpha_{\mathrm{ot}}=\cos r\,dz+r\sin r\,d\phi . \]

Contactness is local; overtwistedness is detected by the global behavior of an embedded disk.

Teaching Point

This comparison prepares later flexibility results: the same local contact inequality can coexist with very different global disk behavior.

Section 2.1Contact examples include both rigid-looking and flexible-looking local models
The overtwisted example should be drawn as changing contact planes along a radial ray, not as a copied textbook figure. The point is that the local nonintegrability condition can hold while the plane field contains a disk-level witness for flexibility that standard contact space does not.
Gray stability

Gray Stability: Follow The Planes

closed \(M\)
Gray Target

\[ T\psi_t(\xi_0)=\xi_t . \]

Equivalently, after choosing forms \(\alpha_t\), \(\psi_t^*\alpha_t\) is a conformal multiple of \(\alpha_0\).

Mechanism

Differentiate the desired pullback relation, use Cartan's formula, and solve the resulting equation for \(X_t\in\xi_t\).

Hypothesis To Notice

Closedness prevents the flow from running into boundary or escape issues during the time interval.

Section 2.2Stability is about contact structures, not rigid preservation of a chosen form
Explain Gray stability as the global version of moving along the family rather than freezing one form. On a closed manifold, a smooth family of contact structures is tracked by an isotopy whose differential carries the initial plane field to the current one.
Why Gray is conformal

Strict Contact Forms Fail To Stay Fixed

Reeb dynamics on \(S^3\)
Strict Request

For a family \(\alpha_t\), asking for \(\psi_t^*\alpha_t=\alpha_0\) would preserve the full Reeb dynamics of the form, not just the plane field.

Orbit Closure Test

On invariant tori in \(S^3\), a Reeb flow with rational slope closes; with irrational slope it fills a torus densely.

Gray's Correct Target

Let the form scale: \(\psi_t^*\alpha_t=\lambda_t\alpha_0\). This preserves \(\ker\alpha\), while allowing Reeb vector fields to change.

Section 2.2The contact structure can be stable while the chosen contact form is not
This is the cautionary slide. Gray stability concerns contact structures, not strict equality of contact forms. On S three, changing a contact form can change Reeb orbit behavior, such as rational versus irrational closures on tori, and strict contactomorphisms would preserve that behavior.
Contact Hamiltonians

Contact Hamiltonians Encode Vector Fields

Theorem 2.3.1
Equations For \(X_H\)

\[ \alpha(X_H)=H,\qquad i_{X_H}d\alpha=dH(R_\alpha)\,\alpha-dH . \]

Reeb Component

The equation \(\alpha(X_H)=H\) chooses the component in the \(R_\alpha\) direction.

Horizontal Component

The contraction equation solves inside \(\xi=\ker\alpha\), where \(d\alpha\) is symplectic.

Compared With Symplectic Hamiltonians

The Reeb term \(dH(R_\alpha)\alpha\) is the extra contact bookkeeping.

Section 2.3A function determines a contact vector field after a contact form is chosen
Put the two equations in the center and dwell on their asymmetry. The first equation fixes the Reeb component of the vector field. The second equation then chooses the horizontal part so the contact condition is preserved up to conformal scaling.

Interlude

Symplectic Vector Bundles

almost contact data
Compatible Complex Structure

On each fiber \((E_b,\omega_b)\), choose \(J_b^2=-1\) such that \(\omega_b(v,J_bv)>0\).

Contractibility

The space \(J(\omega)\) of compatible complex structures is contractible, so the choice behaves like soft auxiliary data.

Almost Contact Structure

A contact distribution \(\xi\) carries the symplectic bundle \((\xi,d\alpha|_\xi)\). Choosing \(J\) gives \(TM\cong \mathbb R R_\alpha\oplus \xi\) with complex data on \(\xi\).

Section 2.4Neighborhood theorems remember symplectic vector-bundle data
This interlude supplies the language used by neighborhood theorems. Once each contact plane is a symplectic vector space, compatible complex structures and almost contact structures become flexible choices rather than extra rigid data. Contractibility is the reason the choices do not introduce new local invariants.
Darboux theorem

Darboux: No Local Invariants

local normal form
Darboux Normal Form

\[ \alpha|_U=dz+\sum_{j=1}^n x_j\,dy_j . \]

Near any point, every contact form is contactomorphic to this model up to rescaling.

Contact Scaling

\[ \delta_\varepsilon(x,y,z)=(\varepsilon x,\varepsilon y,\varepsilon^2 z), \qquad \delta_\varepsilon^*\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}=\varepsilon^2\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}. \]

Interpretation

The \(z\)-coordinate scales like a quadratic quantity relative to \(x,y\). This anisotropy is the local signature of contact geometry.

Section 2.5.1Darboux is the local normal-form consequence of the vector-field method
Present Darboux as the local endpoint of Moser thinking. Around a point, every contact form can be changed by coordinates into the standard model. The anisotropic scaling explains why contact geometry treats the z direction as quadratic relative to x and y.
Isotropic submanifolds

Isotropic Neighborhood Data

CSNM
Conformal Symplectic Normal

\[ \operatorname{CSNM}(L)=(TL)^\perp/TL . \]

The quotient uses the conformal class of \(d\alpha|_\xi\), so it depends only on \(\xi\).

Normal Splitting

\[ NL\simeq \mathbb R R_\alpha\oplus J(TL)\oplus \operatorname{CSNM}(L). \]

Neighborhood Theorem Input

Identify isotropic submanifolds and match their conformal symplectic normal bundles; the ambient contact neighborhoods then match.

Section 2.5.2Isotropic neighborhoods are controlled by conformal symplectic normal data
For isotropic submanifolds, the important invariant is not the whole ambient manifold but the conformal symplectic normal data. The splitting shows what gets remembered: the Reeb line, the J image of tangent directions, and the conformal symplectic normal quotient.
Legendrian case

Legendrian Neighborhood Model

\(J^1(L)\)
Universal Model

\[ J^1(L)=\mathbb R\times T^*L,\qquad \alpha=dz-\lambda_{\mathrm{can}} . \]

A Legendrian \(L\) has a standard neighborhood modeled on the zero section in \(J^1(L)\).

Plane-Field Equation In A Local Picture

\[ \cos\theta\,dx-\sin\theta\,dy=0 . \]

This reads the contact plane as a rotating line field in a two-dimensional slice.

Why Simpler Than Isotropic

For Legendrians, \(TL\) is Lagrangian inside \(\xi|_L\), so \((TL)^\perp=TL\) and \(\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{CSNM}(L)=0\).

Section 2.5.2The first jet space is the universal local Legendrian neighborhood
Legendrian neighborhoods are the cleanest case because TL is Lagrangian in xi along L, so the conformal symplectic normal part disappears. The first jet space model uses the source convention alpha equals dz minus the canonical one-form; it should sound familiar from front projections: base position, covector slope, and height form the universal local contact chart.
Contact submanifolds

Contact Submanifold Neighborhoods

CSNM input
Contact Normal Bundle

\[ \operatorname{CSNM}(M')=(\xi')^\perp\subset \xi|_{M'} . \]

The orthogonal complement is taken with respect to \(d\alpha|_\xi\).

Neighborhood Principle

The input is a contactomorphism on \(M'\) plus an isomorphism of conformal symplectic normal bundles; Reeb alignment is part of the chosen local model.

Model Normalization

After choosing contact forms, the proof normalizes the transverse direction so the contactomorphism extends from intrinsic and CSNM data.

Section 2.5.3Contact submanifolds carry both intrinsic contact data and symplectic normal data
A contact submanifold has its own contact planes, so the normal data is symplectic inside the ambient contact distribution. The theorem's invariant input is the contactomorphism on the submanifold together with a CSNM isomorphism; Reeb direction alignment belongs to the proof's local-model normalization after forms are chosen.
Hypersurfaces

Characteristic Foliations On Hypersurfaces

surface data
Characteristic Foliation

\[ S_\xi=(TS\cap \xi|_S)^\perp . \]

In dimension three this is the singular line field \(TS\cap\xi\) on the surface \(S\).

Vector Field Equation

\[ i_X\Omega=\beta_0\wedge(d\beta_0)^{n-1}. \]

This constructs a vector field whose integral curves represent the characteristic foliation.

Surface Equivalence

For closed surfaces in contact 3-manifolds, matching characteristic foliations is the data that drives the neighborhood and isotopy statements.

Section 2.5.4Hypersurfaces remember contact planes through a singular foliation
This slide shifts from submanifolds that inherit contact data to hypersurfaces that record a line field. In dimension three the characteristic foliation is especially visible: tangent directions are those that lie in both the surface tangent plane and the contact plane.
Isotopy extension

Isotopy Extension By Hamiltonians

extend then flow
Hamiltonian Along The Isotopy

If \(j_t:L\to M\) is the moving submanifold, set \[ H_t(j_t(q))=\alpha(\partial_t j_t(q)). \]

Extension Recipe

Extend \(H_t\) off \(j_t(L)\) using the normal model, multiply by a bump function, then integrate \(X_{H_t}\).

Local Support

The bump keeps the ambient contactomorphism equal to the identity away from the chosen neighborhood.

Section 2.6Submanifold isotopies become ambient contact isotopies by extending Hamiltonians
Now turn the direction of reasoning around. Given an isotopy of a special submanifold, define a Hamiltonian along it, extend that function with a bump, and integrate the corresponding contact Hamiltonian vector field. The bump is what keeps the construction local.
Contact disc theorem

Contact Disc Theorem And Alexander Trick

local contact embeddings
Alexander Trick Form

\[ \delta_t^{-1}\circ j\circ \delta_t \longrightarrow S_0j \qquad (t\to 0). \]

Shrink the disc, apply the embedding, rescale back, and read the contact linearization.

Linear Obstruction

The contact linearization keeps the linear \(x,y\) terms and surviving linear/quadratic \(z\) terms, with conformal symplectic \(x,y\)-part.

Why It Vanishes

\(CSp^+(2n)\) is connected, so the linear contact data can be joined by an isotopy.

Section 2.6.2The local embedding problem reduces to connected linear conformal symplectic data
Describe the contact disc theorem as a final local flexibility statement. Shrinking a contact embedding by the anisotropic contact dilations gives the contact linearization S0j, not merely the ordinary derivative. Its conformal symplectic linear part is controlled by the connectedness of the positive conformal symplectic group, which removes the remaining linear obstruction.
