Moving Maps
path of embeddings and velocity along the image

A Path \(j_t:N\to M\) Carries Its Own Velocity Field

Definition
Velocity Field Along A Moving Embedding

\[X_t\circ j_t=\frac{d j_t}{dt}\]

The derivative is taken pointwise in the source variable: \(q\mapsto j_t(q)\).

What The Equation Says

  • \(X_t\) is evaluated at the moving point \(j_t(q)\).
  • The right side is a tangent vector in \(T_{j_t(q)}M\).
  • A global extension of \(X_t\) is useful for proofs, but the first object lives along the image.

Inspection target: never differentiate \(j_t\) as though its output stayed in a fixed tangent space without naming the base point.

Formula: velocity along \(j_t(N)\)Next: differentiate a pulled-back form
Make the phrase vector field along the image precise. The point q in the source is fixed while its image j_t(q) moves through M. Differentiating that path gives a vector at j_t(q), so the equation X_t composed with j_t equals j-dot_t is a definition of velocity along the moving submanifold.
Lemma B.1
pullback derivative and Cartan terms

Differentiate The Pullback, Not Just The Coefficients

Lemma B.1
Pullback Derivative For A Moving Embedding

\[ \frac{d}{dt}(j_t^*\alpha) =d\bigl(j_t^*(\iota_X\alpha)\bigr)+j_t^*(\iota_X d\alpha). \]

Proof Roadmap

  1. Extend the velocity along \(j_t(N)\) to a vector field near the image.
  2. Turn the path of embeddings into a flow comparison.
  3. Apply Cartan: \(\mathcal L_X=d\iota_X+\iota_Xd\).
\(\alpha\) is fixed in this lemmaThe motion is carried by \(j_t\)
Present Lemma B.1 as the central computational identity. A pulled-back form changes because the map moves, not because alpha itself changes. The two Cartan terms separate the exact contribution from the contraction with d alpha. This is the appendix version of measuring motion through differential forms.
Proof Mechanism
ordinary vector field on a product

Why The Proof Works: Put Time Into The Manifold

Proof Device
Lift To \(M\times\mathbb{R}\)

\[ X(p,t)=X_t(p)+\partial_t. \]

Once time is a coordinate, the time-dependent field becomes an ordinary vector field upstairs.

Lifted Embedding

\(J(q,t)=(j_t(q),t)\)

The moving family becomes one map whose image is a track through the cylinder.

The cylinder trick is not a change of theorem. It is a way to use ordinary flow calculus without hiding the time coordinate.

Ordinary calculus upstairsTime-dependent identities downstairs
The proof is short because it refuses to treat time as mysterious. Lift the whole problem to the product cylinder M x R, where the time-dependent vector field becomes an ordinary vector field X plus partial_t. Then the usual formula for an ordinary flow can be restricted back to a time slice.
Flow Lift
from \(X_t\) to an ordinary flow on \(M\times\mathbb{R}\)

The Lifted Flow Advances Space And Time Together

Lifted Flow
Flow Of \(X+\partial_t\)

\[ \psi_h(p,t)=\bigl(\psi_{t+h}\circ\psi_t^{-1}(p),\,t+h\bigr). \]

Read The Formula

  • \(t\) labels the starting time slice.
  • \(h\) is the ordinary flow parameter upstairs.
  • The spatial component compares two time-dependent flow charts.

This is the bridge between a family of vector fields and one honest vector field on a larger manifold.

Lifted flow: \((p,t)\mapsto(\text{space},t+h)\)Next: pull back a fixed form along \(\psi_t\)
Use the displayed formula to distinguish two clocks. The variable h is ordinary flow time on M x R, while t names the slice where the point starts. The map first returns p to the time-zero flow chart, then advances it to t plus h, and finally records the new time coordinate.
Lie Derivative
instantaneous generator of a moving pullback

A Fixed Form Viewed By A Moving Observer

Identity
Derivative Of A Pulled-Back Fixed Form

\[ \psi_t^*\mathcal L_{X_t}\alpha =\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\psi_t^*\alpha\bigr). \]

Hypotheses To Hear

  • \(\alpha\) is not varying with \(t\).
  • \(\psi_t\) is generated by the time-dependent field \(X_t\).
  • The pullback puts all forms on the same initial copy of \(M\).

This identity is about motion of the observer. Variation of the form itself is a separate term.

Fixed \(\alpha\)Moving \(\psi_t\)
Now specialize to a flow psi_t generated by X_t and a fixed form alpha. Pulling alpha back by psi_t lets the observer sit on the initial manifold while the map moves. The derivative of that pulled-back family is exactly the pullback of the Lie derivative taken with the instantaneous vector field.
Cartan Formula
time dependence does not change the operator at a slice

Cartan Formula Survives Slice By Slice

Cartan Formula
For Each Fixed \(t\)

\[ \mathcal L_{X_t}=d\,\iota_{X_t}+\iota_{X_t}\,d. \]

Why Nothing New Appears

  • \(\mathcal L_{X_t}\) differentiates in the spatial direction \(X_t\).
  • The exterior derivative \(d\) is still the exterior derivative on \(M\).
  • The time parameter selects the vector field; it is not another input to \(d\).

Use this as the local rule inside the product-cylinder proof and inside Moser-type equations.

Freeze \(t\), apply ordinary CartanNo hidden \(\dot\alpha_t\) term
Stress the phrase freeze the time. For each fixed value of t, X_t is just an ordinary vector field on M, so the usual Cartan formula applies without amendment. Time dependence tells us which vector field to choose at each slice; it does not add a hidden time derivative to the operator.
Direct Product Proof
commuting pullback, contraction, and exterior derivative

Do Ordinary Calculus Upstairs, Then Restrict Downstairs

Proof Schematic
Three Commuting Moves
  1. Lift \(\alpha\) to \(M\times\mathbb R\).
  2. Apply \(\mathcal L_{X+\partial_t}\) and Cartan upstairs.
  3. Pull back by \(J(q,t)=(j_t(q),t)\), then set the chosen \(t\).

\[ J^*(d\iota_{X+\partial_t}\widetilde\alpha+\iota_{X+\partial_t}d\widetilde\alpha) \Longrightarrow d(j_t^*\iota_X\alpha)+j_t^*(\iota_Xd\alpha). \]

Exterior derivative commutes with pullbackContraction records the velocity field
Read the diagram from the cylinder downward. Exterior derivative and pullback commute, contraction is evaluated against the lifted vector field, and then restriction to the slice t returns the original time-dependent formula. The proof is a transportation argument: do ordinary calculus upstairs, then project the bookkeeping downstairs.
Convention Trap
\(\mathcal L_{X_t}\alpha_t\) is not the total time derivative

Do Not Hide \(\dot\alpha_t\) Inside The Lie Derivative

Convention Warning
Spatial Lie Derivative Only

\(\mathcal L_{X_t}\alpha_t\) means: freeze \(t\), then differentiate the form \(\alpha_t\) along the spatial vector field \(X_t\).

Total transported derivative

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\psi_t^*\alpha_t\bigr) =\psi_t^*\bigl(\dot\alpha_t+\mathcal L_{X_t}\alpha_t\bigr). \]

Missing \(\dot\alpha_t\) is the common error when a time-varying contact form is transported by a time-varying flow.

Freeze time for \(\mathcal L_{X_t}\)Add \(\dot\alpha_t\) for a varying form
This warning prevents a serious notation error. The symbol L_{X_t} alpha_t differentiates alpha_t in the spatial direction X_t at a fixed time. It does not include the explicit variation of alpha_t with t. A transported time-varying form therefore has two terms: alpha-dot_t and the Lie derivative.
Contact-Topology Use
Moser and Gray style bookkeeping, not a theorem proof

The Appendix Supplies The Isotopy Equation Language

Schematic Use
Control A Transported Contact Form

\[ \dot\alpha_t+\mathcal L_{X_t}\alpha_t=f_t\,\alpha_t. \]

The right side says the transported contact form is allowed to change by scale, preserving the kernel distribution.

What Must Be Solved

  • Choose \(X_t\) from the geometry of \(\alpha_t\).
  • Use Cartan to rewrite \(\mathcal L_{X_t}\alpha_t\).
  • Check the equation modulo the contact line generated by \(\alpha_t\).
Useful for Gray/Moser argumentsHypotheses and existence are theorem-specific
Connect the appendix to the familiar Gray and Moser style equation without proving the theorem here. The task is to choose X_t so the transported derivative of alpha_t is controlled, often only up to multiplication by alpha_t. This is why separating alpha-dot_t from L_{X_t} alpha_t matters.
