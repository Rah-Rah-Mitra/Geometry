An Introduction to Contact Topology

Appendix A: The Generalised Poincare Lemma

pp. 401-403 / PDF 419-421

The appendix proves a relative exactness statement by turning a homotopy into an explicit primitive.

Cylinder \(M\times \mathbb R\) records endpoint change.
Operator \(I\) Contract with \(\partial_t\), then integrate in \(t\).
Cochain Homotopy \(j_1^*-j_0^*=dI+Id\).
Local Primitive Radial contraction gives \(\eta=d(-H\eta)\).
Goal

If a closed form near a zero section has zero pullback to that section, the fibre contraction writes it as an exact form locally.

Appendix AGeneralised Poincare Lemma as a homotopy-operator proof
Open by naming the appendix as a proof tool rather than a side topic. The theorem says that a closed form which disappears along the zero section has a local primitive near that section. The route on the slide is the whole argument: build an interval homotopy operator, turn it into a cochain homotopy, apply it to radial contraction in a vector bundle, and then read exactness from the endpoint terms.
Step 1

The Cylinder That Measures Change

maps \(j_0,j_1,j_t\)
Cylinder Inclusions

\[ j_t:M\longrightarrow M\times\mathbb R,\qquad j_t(x)=(x,t). \]

Flow View

The translation flow \(\varphi_t(x,s)=(x,s+t)\) has vector field \(\partial_t\), and \(j_t=\varphi_t\circ j_0\).

The proof will convert endpoint difference into an integral of infinitesimal change along \(\partial_t\).

Appendix AEndpoint maps on \(M\times\mathbb R\)
Use the cylinder as the geometric source of every sign in the proof. The inclusions j zero and j one pick the two ends of M times R, while the vector field partial t pushes forms from one slice to the next. The formula j t equals phi t after j zero is the reason the derivative with respect to t can be rewritten as a Lie derivative.
Step 2

Integration Along Fibres

degree \(k\mapsto k-1\)
Homotopy Operator

\[ I:\Omega^k(M\times\mathbb R)\longrightarrow \Omega^{k-1}(M), \qquad I(\eta)=\int_0^1 j_t^*(i_{\partial_t}\eta)\,dt . \]

Read The Formula In Three Verbs

  1. Insert \(\partial_t\) into \(\eta\).
  2. Restrict the result to the \(t\)-slice.
  3. Integrate the slice family from \(0\) to \(1\).

The interval variable is not retained; it is used to assemble one form on \(M\).

Appendix ADefinition of \(I:\Omega^k(M\times\mathbb R)\to\Omega^{k-1}(M)\)
Make the degree shift explicit. The operator first inserts the interval direction into eta, so a k-form becomes a k minus one form on each slice. Then the pullback to M removes the cylinder coordinate and the integral averages those slice contributions from zero to one. This is not ordinary integration of a scalar function; it is integration after contraction with the homotopy direction.
Step 3

Lemma A.1: Boundary Equals Commutator

Cartan formula
Lemma A.1

\[ j_1^*\eta-j_0^*\eta=dI(\eta)+I(d\eta). \]

FTC\(j_1^*\eta-j_0^*\eta=\int_0^1 \frac{d}{dt}(j_t^*\eta)\,dt\)
flow\(\frac{d}{dt}(j_t^*\eta)=j_t^*(\mathcal L_{\partial_t}\eta)\)
Cartan\(\mathcal L_{\partial_t}=d\,i_{\partial_t}+i_{\partial_t}d\)
Appendix AThe interval boundary is \(dI+Id\)
Present Lemma A.1 as the de Rham version of the fundamental theorem of calculus. The left side is endpoint change. The right side is what Cartan's formula makes endpoint change become after we integrate the Lie derivative: one term where d falls outside the integral and one term where d first hits eta. This identity is the engine that will later manufacture primitives.
Step 4

Homotopic Maps Give Cochain Homotopies

Proposition A.2
Homotopy Data

For \(f_0,f_1:X\to Y\) joined by \(h:X\times\mathbb R\to Y\), define

\[ H=I\circ h^*:\Omega^k(Y)\to\Omega^{k-1}(X). \]

Cochain Homotopy

\[ f_1^*-f_0^*=dH+Hd. \]

The same operator \(I\) now compares pullbacks along any two homotopic maps.

Appendix AUse \(H=Ih^*\) to compare endpoint pullbacks
Move from the cylinder on M to any smooth homotopy. Pull a form on the target back to the cylinder, apply the same I operator, and obtain H equals I after h star. The square is meant to be read algebraically: H is not a map between spaces, but a degree minus one operator between de Rham complexes whose boundary is the difference of the endpoint pullbacks.
Relative hypothesis

Two Meanings Of Vanishing

zero section language
Weaker Test

\(\sigma_0^*\eta=0\) only evaluates \(\eta\) on tangent vectors lying in \(TB\).

Vanishes On \(B\)

The form is zero on all ambient tangent directions based along \(B\): \(\eta|_{TE|_B}=0\).

Second Order On \(B\)

Coefficients and their first partial derivatives vanish at points of \(B\).

The proposition needs pullback zero for exactness, and stronger ambient vanishing for a second-order primitive.

Appendix APullback zero, ambient zero, and second-order zero are distinct
Slow down on terminology because this is where many mistakes enter. Pullback zero only tests tangent vectors that lie in the submanifold. Vanishing on the submanifold is stronger: the form must vanish on all ambient tangent directions based at the submanifold. Second-order vanishing is stronger again for the primitive; it says the coefficients and their first derivatives disappear along the submanifold.
Step 5

Vector Bundle Setup

radial contraction
Bundle Data

\[ \pi:E\to M,\qquad \sigma_0:M\to E,\qquad \pi\circ\sigma_0=\operatorname{id}_M . \]

Fibre Scaling

The visual contraction is \(c_t(e)=t e\). For the proof identity below we orient it as \(h_t(e)=c_{1-t}(e)=(1-t)e\).

Endpoint Pullbacks

\[ h_0=\operatorname{id}_U,\qquad h_1=\sigma_0\circ\pi . \]

This sign choice matches the primitive \(-H(\eta)\).

Appendix AStar-shaped fibres let radial contraction stay inside \(U\)
Now place the abstract homotopy in the vector bundle. The zero section sits inside E, projection pi remembers the base point, and radial scaling moves each vector inside its own fibre. The slide shows the scaling c t of e equals t e for intuition, while the proof uses the reversed path h t equals c one minus t so that h zero is the identity and h one is sigma zero after pi.
Step 6

Generalised Poincare Lemma Proof

Proposition A.3
Homotopy Identity On \(U\)

\[ \eta=\pi^*\sigma_0^*\eta-dH(\eta)-H(d\eta). \]

Endpoint

\(\pi^*\sigma_0^*\eta\) records the zero-section value.

Closed

If \(d\eta=0\), the \(H(d\eta)\) term is gone.

Relative

If \(\sigma_0^*\eta=0\), the endpoint term is gone.

Primitive

\[ d\eta=0,\quad \sigma_0^*\eta=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \eta=d\zeta,\qquad \zeta=-H(\eta). \]

Appendix AThe primitive is produced, not guessed
This is the main proof slide. Apply the cochain homotopy formula to the fibre contraction. With the proof orientation, the endpoint at t one is pi star sigma zero star and the endpoint at t zero is the identity, giving eta equals pi star sigma zero star eta minus dH eta minus H d eta. If eta is closed and its pullback to the zero section is zero, the first and third terms vanish and eta equals d of minus H eta.
Strengthened conclusion

Why Second Order Appears

Taylor order near \(B\)
Local Order Count

In bundle coordinates \((x,y)\) with \(B=\{y=0\}\):

\[ V_t=T h(\partial_t)=O(|y|),\qquad \eta|_B=0\Rightarrow \eta=O(|y|). \]

Integrand For \(H\eta\)

\[ i_{V_t}\eta=O(|y|^2),\qquad H(\eta)=\int_0^1 h_t^*(i_{V_t}\eta)\,dt. \]

Consequence

If \(\eta\) vanishes on \(B\) in the ambient sense, the primitive \(\zeta=-H(\eta)\) can be chosen to vanish to second order on \(B\).

Appendix ASecond order comes from two first-order vanishings
Explain the strengthened conclusion without expanding every coordinate formula. Near the zero section, the contraction vector field has size proportional to the fibre coordinate. If eta itself vanishes on the submanifold in the stronger ambient sense, its coefficients also contribute a vanishing factor. The integrand defining H eta is therefore a product of two first-order vanishing quantities, which is second order along the zero section.
Exit map

Submanifold Corollary

Corollary A.4
Relative Poincare Lemma Near \(L\)

If \(L\subset M\), \(d\eta=0\), and \(j_L^*\eta=0\), then on some neighborhood \(U\) of \(L\)

\[ \eta=d\zeta,\qquad \zeta|_L=0. \]

Tubular Neighborhood Move

Replace a neighborhood of \(L\) in \(M\) by a neighborhood of the zero section in the normal bundle \(N_LM\).

Recap

The proof is a local exactness machine: endpoint pullback vanishes, closedness kills \(H(d\eta)\), and radial integration supplies the primitive.

Appendix ATubular neighborhoods reduce submanifolds to vector bundles
Close by converting the vector-bundle statement back into the geometric form used in contact topology. A tubular neighborhood identifies directions normal to L with a neighborhood of L in M. Once that identification is made, the vector-bundle lemma applies to the normal directions. The recap should leave students with one portable sentence: local exactness near a submanifold comes from integrating the radial contraction homotopy.
