Knots in Contact 3-Manifolds

Contact geometry adds incidence data to an ordinary knot: does the curve lie in the contact planes, or cut through them with a chosen sign?

Geometry AtlasChapter 3: Knots in Contact 3-Manifolds
Open by separating the topological knot from the extra contact-geometric datum. A smooth knot in a 3-manifold can be moved in many ways, but once a contact plane field is present, the angle between the tangent direction and the planes becomes structure. This lecture follows that angle through diagrams, push-offs, linking numbers, and finally the classical invariants.
Incidence With The Plane Field

Legendrian Means Tangent; Transverse Means Crossing

Definition

For a cooriented contact structure \(\xi=\ker\alpha\), a knot \(K\) is Legendrian when \(T K\subset \xi\).

Definition

A knot is positive transverse when \(\alpha(\gamma'(t))>0\) everywhere; negative transverse uses the opposite sign.

Local Test In \(\mathbb R^3\)

\(\alpha_{st}=dz+x\,dy,\qquad \alpha_{st}(\gamma')=z'+x\,y'.\)

  • Legendrian: \(z'+x\,y'=0\).
  • Transverse: \(z'+x\,y'\) never vanishes.
Incidence is the extra structure2 / 17
The contact form turns a geometric picture into an equation along the knot. Legendrian knots solve the equation alpha on the velocity equals zero, so the tangent line is always contained in the contact plane. Transverse knots deliberately avoid that equation and keep a fixed sign. Emphasize that changing alpha by a positive function does not change these incidence notions.
A Local Model Near The Knot

Push-Offs Compare Nearby Copies Of The Same Knot

Neighborhood Picture

A small tubular neighborhood has torus boundary. A nonzero normal vector field along \(K\) gives a parallel copy \(K'\) on that boundary.

Contact Push-Offs

For a Legendrian \(K\), choose a vector field inside \(\xi\) and transverse to \(K\). Its two oriented choices produce nearby transverse knots \(K_+\) and \(K_-\).

Why It Matters

The linking number of \(K\) with a contact-defined copy measures how the contact framing twists relative to a surface framing.

Push-offs turn plane-field data into linking data3 / 17
Push-offs are the bridge from local contact geometry to topological invariants. The same embedded circle can be copied in many normal directions, but the contact planes provide a preferred family. Once the copy sits on the boundary torus, its homology class and linking number record how much the chosen framing twists around the original knot.

Front Projection

The Missing Coordinate Is The Slope

Front In The Source Convention

\(\gamma_F=(y,z),\qquad x=-{z'\over y'}=-{dz\over dy}.\)

Thus a front is not merely a shadow: away from cusps it encodes the Legendrian lift.

Generic Front Rules
  • No vertical tangencies away from cusp points.
  • Semicubical cusps replace the missing vertical tangent.
  • Crossings require distinct slopes, hence distinct \(x\)-coordinates.

Fronts trade one coordinate for a derivative5 / 17
The contact equation makes fronts powerful. In ordinary projection one expects to lose information, but a Legendrian front recovers the missing coordinate by differentiating the visible curve. At a cusp, the projected curve has a singularity while the lifted Legendrian remains smooth. Mention the local JSON check because it ties the diagram to a sampled residual in the actual contact equation.
Lagrangian Projection

The \(z\)-Coordinate Is Recovered By Area

Projection To \((x,y)\)

\(\gamma_L=(x,y),\qquad z(s_1)=z(s_0)-\int_{s_0}^{s_1}x(s)y'(s)\,ds.\)

Closed Lift Criterion

\(\hbox{closed Legendrian lift}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \int_\gamma x\,dy=0.\)

Signed lobes in the Lagrangian projection must cancel for the lift to close in \(z\).

Lagrangian shadows remember \(z\) by integration6 / 17
The Lagrangian projection loses z instead of x, so the contact equation becomes a reconstruction formula. Integrating minus x dy along the projected path tells us the change in z. For a closed Legendrian knot, the total signed area has to vanish. This gives a different visual language from the front: crossings and area lobes become especially visible.
Transverse Fronts

A Transverse Knot Keeps A Strict Sign

Positive Transverse Test

\(z'(t)+x(t)y'(t)>0\quad\hbox{for every }t.\)

Reading A Front

Unlike a Legendrian front, a transverse front is checked together with a chosen \(x\)-lift. Crossings and tangencies are acceptable only when the lift keeps the same contact sign.

Common Misread

A projected smooth tangency is not automatically bad. The forbidden event is a tangent direction that actually lands in \(\xi\), making \(\alpha_{st}(\gamma')=0\).

Transverse means strict inequality, not slope reconstruction7 / 17
Contrast this with the Legendrian front. For Legendrian knots, the front itself determines the missing coordinate almost everywhere. For transverse knots, the key datum is the strict sign of alpha on the velocity, so one checks a proposed lift rather than solving equality. This is why transverse diagrams carry inequality rules and why a zero-contact event is the real forbidden feature.
Approximation

Small Zig-Zags Force Tangency To The Contact Planes

Legendrian Approximation Principle

After an arbitrarily small \(C^0\) perturbation, a smooth curve can be replaced by a Legendrian curve with the same coarse route through the manifold.

Front Construction

In a Darboux chart, subdivide the visible arc and replace each short segment by a front arc whose slope supplies the required \(x\)-coordinate.

  • Zig-zags absorb the error between the desired path and the contact equation.
  • The perturbation is small in position but not necessarily small in tangent direction.
Approximation is visual before it is invariant-theoretic8 / 17
The approximation theorem should feel plausible from the front picture. The contact equation asks us to prescribe a slope-dependent coordinate, and small front zig-zags give enough freedom to do that while staying close to the original curve in space. Stress the topology versus geometry distinction: the image moves little, but the tangent behavior changes dramatically.
Parametric Version

Subdivision Lets The Construction Vary In Families

Family-Friendly Recipe

Choose a common subdivision, solve the endpoint matching problem on each small block, and insert a standard two-cusp front where the tangent needs correction.

What Is Preserved
  • Endpoints and coarse position can be kept fixed where required.
  • The construction varies continuously when the input family varies continuously.
  • Extra cusps are the visible cost of enforcing \(\alpha(\gamma')=0\).
Instructor Cue

The local zig-zag is not a decorative wrinkle; it is the mechanism that changes the slope without leaving the small chart.

Parametric control comes from local standard pieces9 / 17
Use this slide to make the approximation theorem operational. A single curve is handled by inserting local zig-zags, while a family requires choices that vary coherently. A shared subdivision and a standard two-cusp block provide that coherence. This is also a good moment to foreshadow stabilization: adding zig-zags changes classical invariants.
Linking Interlude

Meridians Record How A Push-Off Winds Around \(K\)

Boundary Coordinates

On \(\partial\nu K\), a longitude follows \(K\), while a meridian \(\mu\) bounds a disk in the tubular neighborhood.

Push-Off Class In The Complement

\([K']-[\lambda]=n[\mu]\quad\Rightarrow\quad [K']=n[\mu]\ \hbox{in the complement quotient.}\)

The integer \(n\) becomes the linking number once orientations are fixed.

Framing data is boundary-torus data10 / 17
Before defining Thurston-Bennequin, remind students how a push-off is measured topologically. The boundary of a tubular neighborhood has a longitude direction and a meridian direction. A preferred push-off may follow the longitude but also wind meridionally; that meridional coefficient is what linking detects. This is the topological accounting system for framings.
Seifert Surface Interpretation

Linking Counts Intersections With A Spanning Surface

Linking Via A Surface

\(\operatorname{lk}(K,K')=K'\cdot \Sigma,\qquad \partial\Sigma=K.\)

Orientation Rule

Each intersection contributes \(+1\) or \(-1\) according to whether the ordered tangent data agrees with the ambient orientation.

Contact Interpretation

If \(K'\) is the contact push-off, this same integer measures how the contact framing twists relative to the surface framing.

Linking is an intersection number in disguise11 / 17
This slide grounds linking in a surface calculation. Choose a Seifert surface for the original knot, push the companion knot off so it intersects the surface transversely, and count signed intersections. The definition is independent of the chosen surface in the usual homological setting, which is why it can serve as a stable invariant for the contact push-off.

Contact Framing Versus Surface Framing

\(tb\) Is Relative Twisting Of A Legendrian Knot

Definition

For an oriented Legendrian knot \(K\) bounding \(\Sigma\), \(tb(K,\Sigma)\) is the linking number of \(K\) with a push-off determined by the contact framing.

Framing Difference

\(tb(K,\Sigma)=\operatorname{lk}(K,K_{\xi})=\operatorname{fr}_{\xi}-\operatorname{fr}_{\Sigma}.\)

Mental Model

Contact planes provide a ribbon along \(K\). The surface provides another ribbon. \(tb\) counts their relative twisting.

\(tb\) is a framing comparison13 / 17
Define Thurston-Bennequin as a linking number, not as a front formula first. The front formula is a computation method; the invariant itself compares the contact framing against a chosen Seifert surface framing. For null-homologous knots the surface is part of the notation because rotation and self-linking also depend on that homological choice.

\(rot(K)={1\over 2}(c_- - c_+).\)

The rotation number is different in nature; it measures the winding of the tangent direction in the contact planes, and the front converts that winding into signed cusp counts. Restrictions And First Classifications

Not Every Pair \((tb,rot)\) Can Occur

Bennequin Inequality

\(tb(K,\Sigma)+|rot(K,[\Sigma])|\le -\chi(\Sigma).\)

Parity Constraint

\(tb(K)+rot(K)\equiv 1\pmod 2.\)

Unknot Snapshot

For a disk-bounding unknot, \(-\chi(D^2)=-1\). The maximal point is \((-1,0)\), and stabilizations move diagonally down.

Inequalities turn diagram counts into existence tests15 / 17
Use the lattice to show that the formulas are constrained by geometry, not arbitrary combinatorics. The Bennequin inequality bounds how positive the contact framing and rotation can be relative to a Seifert surface. The parity condition cuts the lattice further. For the unknot, this produces the familiar staircase beginning at the maximal standard unknot.
Transverse Invariants

Self-Linking Comes From Transverse Push-Offs

Self-Linking

For a null-homologous transverse knot \(T\), \(sl(T,\Sigma)\) is the linking of \(T\) with a nonzero section of \(\xi|_\Sigma\) pushed along the boundary.

Front And Push-Off Formulas

\(sl(K)=\operatorname{writhe}(\hbox{front}).\)

\(sl(K_\pm,\Sigma)=tb(K)\mp rot(K,[\Sigma]).\)

Convention Note

The sign in \(tb\mp rot\) follows the chosen positive and negative transverse push-off convention.

Transverse classification begins with \(sl\)16 / 17
End the invariant story by relating transverse knots back to Legendrian knots. A Legendrian knot has two natural transverse push-offs, and their self-linking numbers combine the Thurston-Bennequin and rotation numbers with opposite signs. This formula explains why Legendrian data is finer: it contains two transverse self-linking values rather than just one.

Big Picture
  • Legendrian fronts encode a missing coordinate by slope.
  • Lagrangian projections encode closedness by signed area.
  • Linking-based push-offs produce \(tb\), \(rot\), and \(sl\).
