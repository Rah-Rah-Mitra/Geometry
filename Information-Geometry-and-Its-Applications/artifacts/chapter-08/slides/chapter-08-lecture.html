EM as Alternating ProjectionLift to complete data

Each Step Holds a Different Constraint Fixed

E-Step

Keep the observed marginal fixed and choose hidden conditionals from the current model.

M-Step

Keep the completed empirical distribution fixed and return to the model family.

Geometry

The path is not Euclidean; monotonicity comes from a divergence decomposition.

Artifact: em_projection_diagram.pngLifted complete-data simplex
The alternating-projection diagram is the conceptual heart. The E-step changes hidden completions while respecting the observed marginal, and the M-step returns to the parametric complete-data model. Each move solves a local problem, which is why the observed likelihood improves without guaranteeing a global optimum.

Data ProcessingHidden variables as observation maps

Observing Less Cannot Increase KL Separation

Data processing

\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Pp:Pq)\le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p:q)\)

Many-to-One Map

Several complete states are merged before the statistician observes them.

Loss

Distinguishability disappears unless the discarded conditional information is irrelevant.

Artifact: coarse_graining_kl.pngHidden variables are non-invertible observations
Coarse graining puts hidden variables into the broader data-processing story. A many-to-one observation map cannot increase KL divergence, so merging states or hiding labels erases distinguishability unless the discarded conditional information is irrelevant for the model being compared.
MisspecificationProjection target changes

The Closest Model Point Replaces the Literal Truth

Wrong Family

The model cannot contain the data-generating mixture.

KL Projection

The estimator targets the closest available point in the chosen family.

Visible Target

With fixed variance, the best single-Gaussian mean is the mixture average.

Artifact: single_gaussian_projection.pngWrong model, meaningful projection
Misspecification changes the target. If the model family cannot contain the true mixture, estimation aims at the closest available point, not the literal data-generating distribution. The fixed-variance single-Gaussian example makes this projection target visible as the mixture mean.
Applied LabOverlap changes geometry

Overlap Makes Responsibilities Less Decisive

More Overlap

Component labels become harder to infer from visible samples.

More Missing Information

Likelihood directions flatten and EM may require more iterations.

Experiment

Change overlap and inspect responsibility decisiveness and convergence.

Artifact: overlap_lab.pngGeometry changes with separability
Use the overlap lab as an active diagnostic. As components move closer or variances grow, responsibilities become less decisive, missing information increases, and the EM path often settles more slowly. The geometry changes because the lifted complete-data families meet at a worse angle.
TakeawaysEnd of Chapter 8

Ask What Each EM Step Holds Fixed

E-Step

Observed data stay fixed while hidden completions are rebuilt.

M-Step

The completed empirical distribution stays fixed while the model is refit.

Responsibilities

They are conditional distributions, not hard labels.

Information Loss

Hidden labels and coarse maps reduce distinguishability.

Misspecification

The target is a projection onto the model family.

Overlap

The geometry worsens as latent labels become harder to separate.

End of Chapter 8EM as disciplined alternating projection
Close by asking students to identify what each step holds fixed. EM holds the observed marginal fixed during completion and holds the completed empirical distribution fixed during model fitting. That two-step discipline is the durable takeaway for mixture models, coarse observations, and misspecified latent-variable learning.
