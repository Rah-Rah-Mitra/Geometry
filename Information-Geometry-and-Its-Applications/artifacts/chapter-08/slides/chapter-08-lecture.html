Complete Data and Its Shadow

Complete data

The pair $(x,z)$ lives in an enlarged space.

Observation map

Only $x$ is seen; $z$ is marginalized out.

Observed law

$p(x;\theta) = \sum_z p(x,z;\theta)$

Difficulty

A log of a sum makes the likelihood non-convex.

Printed pp. 179-189Complete Data and Its Shadow
A hidden-variable model carries complete data x and z, but observation exposes only x. The missing label z enlarges the complete-data space, and the observed law is a marginal. Inference must recover model parameters while the latent coordinate remains unseen, which is exactly what makes the likelihood awkward.

EM as Alternating Projection

Data manifold

Completions consistent with the observed marginal.

Model manifold

The parametric family lifted to complete data.

Path

Not a Euclidean shortest line, but it converges.

Printed pp. 179-189EM as Alternating Projection
Place complete-data distributions in a large ambient simplex. The data manifold collects every completion that marginalizes to what we saw; the model manifold is the parametric family lifted to complete data. EM alternates projections between these two sets, zig-zagging toward their meeting point rather than following a straight path.

The E-Step: m-Projection

Fix $\theta$

Complete the empirical law using the model posterior.

Responsibilities

$p(z\mid x;\theta)$ for each component.

m-Projection

$q^{\star}(z) = p(z\mid x;\theta_t)$

Lands on

The data manifold, marginal held fixed.

Printed pp. 179-189The E-Step: m-Projection
The E-step holds the current parameter fixed and chooses hidden conditionals from the model, completing the empirical distribution. Geometrically this is an m-projection that lands on the data manifold. In a Gaussian mixture it produces responsibilities: the conditional probability that each observed point came from each component.

The M-Step: e-Projection

Fix $q$

Maximize the expected complete-data log likelihood.

Closed form

Weighted means and variances for a mixture.

e-Projection

$\theta_{t+1} = \arg\max_\theta\; \mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log p(x,z;\theta)\big]$

Lands on

The model manifold, completion held fixed.

Printed pp. 179-189The M-Step: e-Projection
The M-step holds the completed empirical distribution fixed and finds the model point closest to it, maximizing the expected complete-data log likelihood. Geometrically this is an e-projection back onto the model manifold. For a mixture it reduces to responsibility-weighted means and variances, the same updates a known label would give.

Free Energy and KL Decomposition

Variational $q$

Any distribution over the hidden variables.

Two terms

A free-energy bound plus a non-negative gap.

Roles

E-step zeroes the gap; M-step raises the bound.

Decomposition

$\log p(x;\theta) = \mathcal{F}(q,\theta) + \mathrm{KL}\!\big(q \,\Vert\, p(z\mid x;\theta)\big)$

Printed pp. 179-189Free Energy and KL Decomposition
Introduce a variational distribution q over hidden variables. The observed log likelihood splits into a free-energy bound plus a non-negative KL gap between q and the true posterior. The E-step zeroes that gap; the M-step raises the bound. This decomposition is the engine behind every monotonicity claim.

Monotone Likelihood Ascent

Guarantee

Each cycle cannot decrease $\log p(x;\theta)$.

Caveat

Local optima and label switching persist.

Ascent

$\log p(x;\theta_{t+1}) \;\ge\; \log p(x;\theta_t)$

Printed pp. 179-189Monotone Likelihood Ascent
Because each step solves a local projection that never decreases the bound, the observed log likelihood rises until improvement becomes negligible. The notebook trace climbs from roughly minus six hundred and saturates. EM is a local method: each step is principled, but multimodality and label switching mean the starting point still matters.

Responsibilities and Missing Information

Boundary

Responsibilities near $0$ or $1$: labels almost known.

Overlap

A point fits both components: information is missing.

Missing information

$I_{\text{miss}} = I_{\text{complete}} - I_{\text{observed}} \;\succeq\; 0$

Curvature

Observed information is flatter where labels are uncertain.

Printed pp. 179-189Responsibilities and Missing Information
On the mixture boundary responsibilities are nearly zero or one, so labels are almost known. In the overlap band the same point fits both components, and that fuzziness is missing information made visible. Observed information is smaller than complete information because curvature is spent explaining label uncertainty.

Data Processing and KL Contraction

Coarse-graining

A many-to-one map merges states before we see them.

Sufficiency

No loss exactly when the statistic is sufficient.

Data-processing inequality

$\mathrm{KL}(P\Vert Q) \;\ge\; \mathrm{KL}(TP \Vert TQ)$

Notebook

Six states merged into three bins: KL shrinks.

Printed pp. 179-189Data Processing and KL Contraction
Hidden variables are a non-invertible observation map; coarse-graining that merges states is another. KL divergence cannot increase under such many-to-one maps. The notebook collapses six states into three bins and watches the divergence shrink. A statistic is sufficient exactly when this loss vanishes for the family in question.

Misspecification as KL Projection

Wrong family

One fixed-variance Gaussian for mixture data.

Shadow point

The optimum mean is the mixture average.

Projection target

$\theta^{\star} = \arg\min_{\theta\in M}\; \mathrm{KL}\!\big(p_{\text{true}} \,\Vert\, p_\theta\big)$

Lesson

Model checking decides what the estimator means.

Printed pp. 179-189Misspecification as KL Projection
A hidden-variable model can still be wrong. Fit one fixed-variance Gaussian to two-component mixture data and the best mean is not a component mean; it is the KL projection of the truth onto the restricted family, namely the mixture average. Misspecification redefines the estimation target as the closest available model point.
