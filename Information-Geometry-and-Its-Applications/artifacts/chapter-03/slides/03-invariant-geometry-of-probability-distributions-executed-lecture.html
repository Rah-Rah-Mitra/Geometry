The Invariance Criterion

Push-forward

Each law on $x$ pushes forward along $y=k(x)$ to a law on $y$.

The demand

Losing observational detail may never make two laws more distinguishable.

Consequence

Divergence is preserved exactly when the report is a sufficient statistic.

Monotonicity

$$D(Pp : Pq) \le D(p : q)$$

Reading

$P$ is the coarse-graining map. A lossy observation cannot sharpen contrast.

Printed pp. 51-70The Invariance Criterion
Replacing an outcome x by a reported value y equals k of x pushes every law forward to the coarse alphabet. A disciplined geometry must never let this lossy report make two distributions look more distinguishable. That single demand, stated as monotonicity of divergence, organizes the entire chapter and selects its central objects.

Coarse Graining as a Markov Map

Partition

A coarse cell sums the probabilities of the fine outcomes it contains.

Row-stochastic map

$(Pp)_j = \sum_{i \in A_j} p_i$

Structure

One $1$ per fine outcome; columns select which cell it lands in.

Printed pp. 51-70Coarse Graining as a Markov Map
On a finite alphabet a coarse graining is a partition: each coarse cell sums the fine probabilities inside it, giving a row-stochastic matrix with one entry equal to one per fine outcome. Monotonicity then reads as divergence of the mapped pair never exceeding divergence of the original pair.

f-Divergences: The Decomposable Invariant Family

Definition

$$D_f(p:q) = \sum_i p_i\, f\!\left(\frac{q_i}{p_i}\right)$$

Generator

$f$ is convex and normalized with $f(1)=0$.

Gauge freedom

Adding a linear term to $f$ leaves $D_f$ unchanged on the simplex.

Scale convention

$f''(1)=1$ fixes the second-order normalization.

Why "decomposable"

One local contribution per outcome, summed across the alphabet.

Printed pp. 51-70f-Divergences
Decomposable invariant divergences add one local contribution per outcome and take the form sum of p f of q over p, with f convex and f of one equal to zero. Adding a linear term leaves the divergence unchanged on the simplex, and the convention f double prime of one equals one fixes the scale.

Monotonicity is Jensen in Disguise

Merge two cells

The merged ratio $q/p$ is a $p$-weighted average of the old ratios.

Jensen step

$$f\!\Big(\textstyle\sum w_k u_k\Big) \le \sum w_k\, f(u_k)$$

Therefore

Contribution after averaging $\le$ contribution before. Loss $\ge 0$.

Printed pp. 51-70Monotonicity is Jensen
Why is every f-divergence monotone? When two fine cells merge, the new likelihood ratio is a probability-weighted average of the old ratios. Convexity of f, applied through Jensen's inequality, forces the merged contribution to fall below the weighted sum of the originals, so distinguishability can only shrink.

Equality and Sufficient Statistics

Equality case

Holds when each block's conditional $p(x\mid y)$ is parameter-free.

Sufficiency

$p(x\mid y)=q(x\mid y)\;\Rightarrow\;D(Pp:Pq)=D(p:q)$

Diagnostic

Perturb a hidden within-block conditional and the fine divergence detects it.

Printed pp. 51-70Equality and Sufficiency
Equality in the monotonicity inequality is the diagnostic for sufficiency. If the within-block conditional distribution is identical for both parameter values, the coarse report discards nothing about the parameter. Our experiment confirms a vanishing residual in the sufficient case and a strictly positive gap once a hidden conditional is perturbed.

Fisher Metric Invariance

Fisher form

$$g_p(v,v)=\sum_i \frac{v_i^2}{p_i},\qquad \sum_i v_i=0$$

Sufficient embedding

Split mass $q_j$ into $r_{ij}q_j$ with fixed conditional weights $r_{ij}$.

Length preserved

$\sum_{i\in A_j}\frac{(r_{ij}v_j)^2}{r_{ij}q_j}=\frac{v_j^2}{q_j}\sum_i r_{ij}=\frac{v_j^2}{q_j}$

Printed pp. 51-70Fisher Metric Invariance
A sufficient embedding splits coarse mass into fixed conditional weights and splits the tangent vector in the same proportions. Substituting into the Fisher quadratic form, the conditional weights sum to one and cancel, leaving the original length. This algebraic cancellation is the computational heart of Chentsov's uniqueness theorem on the finite simplex.

Positive Measures and the Square-Root Picture

On the positive cone

$$ds^2=\sum_i \frac{dm_i^2}{m_i}$$

Change of coordinates

Let $\xi_i=2\sqrt{m_i}$; then $ds^2=\sum_i d\xi_i^2$ becomes Euclidean.

The simplex

$\sum_i m_i=1$ maps to a sphere of radius $2$ in $\xi$-coordinates.

Printed pp. 51-70Square-Root Coordinates
On the positive cone the invariant line element is sum of d m squared over m. Setting xi equal to twice the square root of m turns it into the ordinary Euclidean metric, so the probability simplex becomes a sphere of radius two. This is the Hellinger picture: the Euclidean shadow of the invariant geometry.

Takeaways

  • Coarse graining tests whether a divergence respects information loss.
  • Decomposable invariant divergences are exactly the $f$-divergences.
  • Equality under coarsening is the signature of sufficiency.
  • Every standard $f$-divergence shares the Fisher second-order metric.
  • Chentsov: up to scale, Fisher information is the unique invariant metric.
