Printed pp. 51-70Invariance and Sufficiency
A meaningful geometry should not change when data are summarized by a sufficient statistic or relabeled by an invertible map. This requirement of invariance under Markov morphisms is the guiding principle that restricts which metrics and connections deserve to be called canonical.

Chentsov's Theorem

Uniqueness

One invariant metric, up to scale.

It is Fisher

The Fisher information matrix.

Fisher metric

$g_{ij} = E\big[\partial_i \log p\, \partial_j \log p\big]$

Meaning

Why Fisher is central.

Printed pp. 51-70Chentsov's Theorem
Chentsov's theorem states that, up to a constant scale, the Fisher information metric is the only Riemannian metric invariant under sufficient statistics. This remarkable uniqueness result explains why the Fisher metric occupies the central place it does throughout statistics and information geometry.

The Alpha-Connections

A family

One connection for each alpha.

Endpoints

$\alpha=\pm1$ give e and m.

Interpolation

$\nabla^{(\alpha)} = \tfrac{1+\alpha}{2}\nabla^{(e)} + \tfrac{1-\alpha}{2}\nabla^{(m)}$

Middle

$\alpha=0$ is Levi-Civita.

Printed pp. 51-70The Alpha-Connections
Beyond the metric, invariance permits a one-parameter family of affine connections indexed by a real number alpha. The values plus one and minus one give the exponential and mixture connections, while alpha equal to zero recovers the self-dual Levi-Civita connection of the Fisher metric.

f-Divergences

Convex f

Applied to the density ratio.

Invariant

Under sufficient statistics.

Definition

$D_f[p:q] = \int q\, f\!\big(\tfrac{p}{q}\big)\,dx$

Members

KL, Hellinger, chi-squared.

Printed pp. 51-70f-Divergences
An f-divergence is built from a convex function applied to the ratio of two densities, and every such divergence is invariant under sufficient statistics. The Kullback-Leibler, Hellinger, and chi-squared divergences are members, each inducing the same invariant Fisher metric to second order.

The alpha-divergences form the invariant family that interpolates between the exponential and mixture sides. Their geometry reproduces the Fisher metric and the alpha-connections, closing the circle: invariant divergences, invariant metric, and invariant connections all arise from the same principle.
