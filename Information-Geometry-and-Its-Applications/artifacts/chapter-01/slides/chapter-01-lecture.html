Printed pp. 3-30Manifold of Distributions
A parametric family of probability distributions is viewed as a smooth manifold whose points are distributions and whose coordinates are the parameters. Changing parameters moves smoothly along the manifold, so the tools of differential geometry apply directly to statistical models and their structure.

Divergence Functions

Nonnegative

Zero only when the points coincide.

Asymmetric

Direction matters; not a true distance.

Kullback-Leibler

$D[p:q] = \int p \log \tfrac{p}{q}\,dx$

Canonical case

The basic measure between distributions.

Printed pp. 3-30Divergence Functions
A divergence assigns a nonnegative number to an ordered pair of points, vanishing only when they coincide, yet it need not be symmetric or satisfy the triangle inequality. The Kullback-Leibler divergence is the canonical example, measuring how one distribution differs from another.

The Riemannian Metric

Second order

Expand the divergence near a point.

Fisher information

The metric for statistical models.

Metric

$g_{ij} = \partial_i \partial_j' D[p:q]\big|_{q=p}$

Result

Intrinsic distance and angle.

Printed pp. 3-30Metric from Divergence
Expanding a divergence to second order about a point yields a positive-definite quadratic form, which serves as a Riemannian metric on the manifold. For statistical models this construction recovers the Fisher information matrix, giving the manifold an intrinsic notion of distance and angle.

Dually Flat Structure

Two connections

A primal and dual pair, both flat.

Legendre

Convex potentials link the coordinates.

Duality

$\eta_i = \partial_i \psi(\theta), \quad \theta^i = \partial^i \varphi(\eta)$

Coordinates

Affine in two complementary ways.

Printed pp. 3-30Dually Flat Structure
A divergence also induces a pair of affine connections that are dual with respect to the metric. When both connections are flat the manifold is dually flat, admitting two affine coordinate systems linked by the Legendre transform of a convex potential function.

Generalized Pythagorean Theorem

Orthogonal meeting

Primal geodesic crosses dual geodesic.

Divergences add

Like squared lengths of a right triangle.

Identity

$D[p:r] = D[p:q] + D[q:r]$

Consequence

Projection theorems for estimation.

Printed pp. 3-30Generalized Pythagorean Theorem
In a dually flat manifold a generalized Pythagorean relation holds: when a primal geodesic meets a dual geodesic orthogonally, the divergences add like squared lengths. This identity underlies projection theorems and the geometric view of estimation developed throughout the book.
