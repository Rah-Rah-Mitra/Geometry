The Exponential Family

Log-linear

Log density is linear in parameters.

Cumulant

The normalizer is a convex potential.

Canonical form

$p(x;\theta) = \exp\!\big(\theta^i x_i - \psi(\theta)\big)$

Examples

Gaussian, Poisson, Bernoulli.

Printed pp. 31-50The Exponential Family
An exponential family writes the log density as a linear combination of fixed functions plus a normalizing term. The coefficients are the natural parameters, and the cumulant generating function plays the role of a convex potential whose derivatives produce the expectation parameters.

Natural and Expectation Parameters

Two charts

Natural $\theta$ and expectation $\eta$.

Legendre

Potentials $\psi$ and $\varphi$ are dual.

Duality

$\eta_i = \partial_i \psi(\theta), \quad \varphi(\eta) = -H$

Metric

Printed pp. 31-50Natural and Expectation Parameters
The natural parameters and the expectation parameters are linked by the Legendre transform of the cumulant function. They form the two dual affine coordinate systems of the manifold, with the Fisher metric relating their differentials and the negative entropy serving as the dual potential.

The Mixture Family

Convex mix

Combinations of fixed distributions.

Weights

The mixing coefficients are coordinates.

Mixture form

$p(x) = \sum_i \eta_i\, p_i(x)$

Flatness

Affine in the mixture connection.

Printed pp. 31-50The Mixture Family
A mixture family consists of convex combinations of fixed distributions, with the mixing weights as coordinates. These weights are affine in the mixture sense, making the mixture family flat under the connection dual to the one that flattens the exponential family.

e-Flat and m-Flat

e-flat

Linear in natural parameters.

m-flat

Linear in expectation parameters.

Dual connections

$\nabla^{(e)} \;\perp_g\; \nabla^{(m)}$

Duality

One notion mirrors the other.

Printed pp. 31-50e-Flat and m-Flat
The exponential connection makes exponential families flat, while the mixture connection makes mixture families flat. A submanifold is e-flat or m-flat when it is a linear subspace in the respective coordinates, and these notions are dual to each other throughout the theory.

e- and m-Geodesics

e-geodesic

Straight in natural parameters.

m-geodesic

Straight in expectation parameters.

Pythagoras

$D[p:r] = D[p:q] + D[q:r]$

Use

Projections that solve inference.

Printed pp. 31-50e- and m-Geodesics
Geodesics of the exponential connection are straight lines in natural parameters, while geodesics of the mixture connection are straight lines in expectation parameters. The two geodesic families and the generalized Pythagorean theorem together drive the projection methods used in inference.
