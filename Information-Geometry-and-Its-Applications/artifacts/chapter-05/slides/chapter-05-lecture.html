Local linear arena

Three faces

Printed pp. 109-130Manifolds and Tangent Spaces
A tangent space is the local linear arena at a point, the first-order model of a curved surface there. A tangent vector appears as an arrow, a directional derivative operator, or, on a statistical model, a score random variable. These are three faces of one geometric object.

The Riemannian Metric

Inner product

A positive definite $g_{ij}$ on each tangent space.

Fisher case

The metric is the inner product of scores.

Fisher metric

$g_{ij}(\xi) = E\!\big[\partial_i \ell\,\partial_j \ell\big]$

Length

$\,ds^2 = g_{ij}\,d\xi^i\,d\xi^j\,$ measures displacement.

Printed pp. 109-130The Riemannian Metric
A Riemannian metric supplies an inner product on each tangent space, written in coordinates as a positive definite matrix. Lengths and angles of tangent vectors become measurable. On probability models the Fisher information matrix is the natural metric, since it is the inner product of score functions.

Metric as a Local Ruler

Sphere line element

$ds^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\!\theta\, d\phi^2$

Reading it

A unit step in $\phi$ shrinks in length near the poles.

Printed pp. 109-130Metric as a Local Ruler
On the unit sphere the line element is one in the latitude direction but scales with sine squared of the colatitude in longitude. The same unit displacement therefore leaves a different coordinate footprint at different latitudes, which the metric ellipse field makes visible as stretched measuring devices.

Affine Connections

Compares across points

A connection links neighboring tangent spaces.

Christoffel symbols

$\Gamma^k_{ij}$ records basis motion.

Covariant derivative

$\nabla_i v^k = \partial_i v^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\, v^j$

Correction term

$\Gamma$ subtracts the apparent change of the basis.

Printed pp. 109-130Affine Connections
A metric compares vectors inside one tangent space, but an affine connection compares vectors across neighboring tangent spaces. Its Christoffel coefficients record how the coordinate basis itself rotates and stretches from point to point, and the covariant derivative corrects ordinary component derivatives by that basis motion.

Christoffel Symbols on the Sphere

Levi-Civita

Metric-compatible, torsion-free connection.

Computed symbolically

Verified against the sphere metric.

Nonzero symbols

$\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta,\quad \Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi} = \cot\theta$

From the formula

$\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac12 g^{kl}(\partial_i g_{lj} + \partial_j g_{li} - \partial_l g_{ij})$

Printed pp. 109-130Christoffel Symbols on the Sphere
For the round sphere the symbolic computation gives the longitude-longitude Christoffel symbol of the colatitude as minus sine times cosine, and the mixed symbol as cotangent of the colatitude. These coefficients are exactly what the covariant derivative subtracts to keep differentiation coordinate independent.

Geodesics and Parallel Transport

Geodesic equation

$\ddot{\xi}^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\,\dot{\xi}^i\dot{\xi}^j = 0$

Parallel transport

Carry $v$ with $\nabla_{\dot\xi} v = 0$ along the curve.

Printed pp. 109-130Geodesics and Parallel Transport
A geodesic keeps its own tangent direction parallel to itself, the connection's notion of a straightest path. Parallel transport carries a vector along a curve with vanishing covariant derivative. The geodesic generally departs from the naive straight chart line, which is only a coordinate artifact.

Curvature and Holonomy

Riemann curvature

$R^\theta_{\;\phi\theta\phi} = \sin^2\!\theta$

Two readings

Loop defect of transport; failure of $\nabla$ to commute.

Printed pp. 109-130Curvature and Holonomy
Riemann-Christoffel curvature is the infinitesimal failure of parallel transport around a closed loop to return a vector unchanged. Equivalently it is the obstruction to commuting two covariant derivatives. On the sphere the transported vector returns rotated, and that holonomy angle measures the enclosed curvature.

Fisher Geometry of Normals

Coordinates

Mean $\mu$ and log-scale $\alpha = \log\sigma$.

Cost asymmetry

Mean motion is costly when $\sigma$ is small.

Fisher matrix

$g = \mathrm{diag}\big(e^{-2\alpha},\, 2\big)$

Numerical check

Monte Carlo scores match the analytic $g$ within tolerance.

Printed pp. 109-130Fisher Geometry of Normals
For the normal family in mean and log-scale coordinates the Fisher metric is diagonal with entries exp of minus twice the log-scale and two. Mean motion costs more when the distribution is narrow, while log-scale motion has constant Fisher cost. A Monte Carlo score estimate confirms the analytic matrix.

Submanifolds and Embedding Curvature

Induced structure

Differentiate the embedding for tangents and metric.

Normal component

The off-tangent part is embedding curvature.

Intrinsic vs extrinsic

$\nabla^{M} = \Pi_{T}\,\nabla^{\text{amb}}, \quad \mathrm{II} = \Pi_{\perp}\,\nabla^{\text{amb}}$

Cylinder

Bends in space, yet $R = 0$ intrinsically.

Printed pp. 109-130Submanifolds and Embedding Curvature
A submanifold inherits tangent vectors and a metric by differentiating its embedding map. The induced connection is the ambient covariant derivative projected back, and the normal component is embedding curvature. A cylinder bends in ambient space yet carries zero intrinsic Riemann-Christoffel curvature, separating the two notions.

Takeaways

Build order

  • Tangent spaces are local linear models.
  • Metrics measure them; Fisher is the statistical metric.
  • Connections compare neighboring tangent spaces.

From connection to curvature

  • Geodesics and parallel transport follow from $\nabla$.
  • Curvature is path-dependent transport around loops.
  • Intrinsic curvature differs from embedding curvature.
Printed pp. 109-130Takeaways
Tangent spaces are local linear models, metrics measure tangent vectors with Fisher information as the statistical case, connections compare neighboring tangent spaces and define covariant derivatives, geodesics and parallel transport follow from the connection, and curvature records path-dependent transport distinct from extrinsic embedding curvature.
