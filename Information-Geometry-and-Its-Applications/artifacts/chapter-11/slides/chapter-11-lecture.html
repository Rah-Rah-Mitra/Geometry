Bregman Clustering

Bregman divergence

Convex potential $\phi$ measures distance to centers.

Mean stays optimal

The right-sided average minimizes total divergence.

Special case

Squared $\phi$ recovers ordinary k-means.

Divergence

$D_\phi[x:c]=\phi(x)-\phi(c)-\nabla\phi(c)^{\!\top}(x-c)$

Alternation

Assign to nearest center, then recompute centers.

Printed pp. 231-278Bregman Clustering
Bregman divergences generalize squared distance while keeping the mean as the optimal center. Hard clustering alternates assigning each point to its nearest divergence center and recomputing centers as right-sided Bregman averages, which is an m-projection. The squared-Euclidean special case recovers ordinary k-means and Voronoi cells.

Restricted Boltzmann Machines

Factorized conditionals

Exponential family

Energy

$E(v,h)=-b^{\!\top}v-c^{\!\top}h-v^{\!\top}Wh$

Goal

Printed pp. 231-278Restricted Boltzmann Machines
A restricted Boltzmann machine is a bipartite energy model whose visible and hidden layers carry no within-layer edges, so each conditional factorizes. The model defines an exponential family over binary states, and learning moves its parameters to reduce the divergence between the data distribution and the model marginals.
