The Alpha Representation

Power coordinate

Each component is raised to a fixed power set by $\alpha$.

Logarithmic limit

At $\alpha = 1$ the representation becomes $\log m$.

Dual chart

The $-\alpha$ representation supplies the dual flat coordinate.

Alpha representation

$$h_\alpha(m) = m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}, \qquad h_1(m) = \log m$$

Inverse

$$m = h_\alpha^{-1}(y) = y^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}$$

Printed pp. 71-106The Alpha Representation
For a positive measure, the alpha representation raises each coordinate to the power one minus alpha over two, with a logarithmic limit at alpha equal to one. Its dual is the minus-alpha representation. On the positive cone these power coordinates straighten a Bregman geometry, giving a whole alpha family of dually flat structures rather than a single one.

Why Simplex Geodesics Bend

Straight upstairs

In $h_\alpha$ coordinates the geodesic is a line segment.

Curved downstairs

Renormalizing onto the simplex bends the curve.

Geodesic

$$h_\alpha(\gamma_t) = (1-t)\,h_\alpha(p) + t\,h_\alpha(r)$$

Printed pp. 71-106Why Simplex Geodesics Bend
On the positive cone an alpha geodesic is a straight segment in alpha coordinates. On the simplex we draw the same segment but must renormalize, so the curve bends. The visible bend changes with alpha because a flat connection upstairs meets the curved normalization constraint downstairs, leaving only limiting mixture and exponential cases affine.

Alpha Divergence and Pythagoras

Bregman in $h_\alpha$

The alpha divergence is a Bregman divergence in alpha coordinates.

Square-root case

At $\alpha = 0$ it is a constant times squared Euclidean distance.

Numerical check

The notebook records identity error exactly $0$.

Alpha divergence

$$D_\alpha[p:q] = \tfrac{4}{1-\alpha^2}\!\left(\tfrac{1-\alpha}{2}\textstyle\sum p + \tfrac{1+\alpha}{2}\textstyle\sum q - \textstyle\sum p^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}} q^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\right)$$

Pythagorean identity

$$D[m:k] = D[m:n] + D[n:k]$$

Printed pp. 71-106Alpha Divergence and Pythagoras
The alpha divergence reads as a Bregman divergence in alpha coordinates. At alpha equal to zero the square-root coordinate makes it a constant times squared Euclidean distance, so orthogonality gives a literal Pythagorean identity. The notebook verifies this on a small example, with the identity error coming out exactly zero.

Alpha Means Interpolate Averages

Average in $h_\alpha$

Weight values in alpha coordinates, then invert.

Arithmetic end

$\alpha = -1$ gives the arithmetic mean.

Geometric end

$\alpha = 1$ gives the geometric mean.

Weighted alpha mean

$$M_\alpha = \left(\textstyle\sum_i w_i\, x_i^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}$$

Printed pp. 71-106Alpha Means Interpolate Averages
The weighted alpha mean averages values in the alpha representation and inverts back. As alpha sweeps the interval, the mean slides continuously between the arithmetic average at one endpoint and the geometric average at the logarithmic limit. This single tunable knob recovers the familiar Pythagorean family of means as special cases.

Tsallis $q$-Entropy

Deformed entropy

A $q$-logarithm replaces $\log$ in Shannon entropy.

Shannon limit

As $q \to 1$ it returns to Shannon entropy.

Dictionary

Linked to alpha geometry by $\alpha = 2q - 1$.

Tsallis entropy

$$S_q(p) = \frac{1 - \sum_i p_i^{\,q}}{q - 1}$$

Parameter relation

$$\alpha = 2q - 1$$

Printed pp. 71-106Tsallis q-Entropy
Tsallis q-entropy deforms Shannon entropy with a q-logarithm and approaches it as q tends to one. The chapter ties the deformation to alpha geometry through the dictionary alpha equals two q minus one. The deformed power coordinate flattens differently near the simplex boundary, which is why q-entropy optimization can emphasize rare or dominant events.

Escort Distributions

Power reweighting

Raise each $p_i$ to the power $q$ and renormalize.

Tilts mass

$q>1$ amplifies large coordinates; $q<1$ favors small ones.

q-escort

$$P_i = \frac{p_i^{\,q}}{\sum_j p_j^{\,q}}$$

Printed pp. 71-106Escort Distributions
The q-escort distribution raises each probability to the power q and renormalizes. For q above one large coordinates are amplified; for q below one small coordinates gain relative mass. Escorts are not ad hoc smoothing weights but coordinates of a second dual-flat construction, with a metric conformally related to the invariant geometry in the special case.

Pool in $h_\alpha$

Average expert distributions linearly in alpha coordinates, then normalize.

Mixture vs product

$\alpha<0$ leans mixture; $\alpha>0$ leans multiplicative.

Risk selects $\alpha$

Best risk falls near $\alpha = -1$ against the target.

Alpha integration

$$\hat p = \mathrm{norm}\!\left(h_\alpha^{-1}\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_k w_k\, h_\alpha(p_k)\Big)\right)$$

Selection

$$\alpha^\star = \arg\min_\alpha D_\alpha[\,t : \hat p_\alpha\,]$$

Printed pp. 71-106Lab: Alpha Integration of Experts
Alpha integration pools expert categorical predictions linearly in alpha coordinates, then normalizes. Negative alpha behaves like mixture pooling, positive alpha like multiplicative exponential pooling, and intermediate values tune the compromise. Scoring the pooled prediction against a held-out target by alpha divergence selects the best representation, here near alpha equal to minus one.

SPD Matrices and Log-Det Divergence

Spectra as measures

An SPD matrix generalizes a positive measure to eigenvalues and orientation.

Stein / log-det

Nonnegative, zero only when $P = Q$.

Congruence-invariant

Unchanged under $P \mapsto APA^\top$, $Q \mapsto AQA^\top$.

Log-det divergence

$$D(P:Q) = \operatorname{tr}(PQ^{-1}) - \log\det(PQ^{-1}) - n$$

Invariance

$$D(APA^\top : AQA^\top) = D(P:Q)$$

Printed pp. 71-106SPD Matrices and Log-Det Divergence
A positive-definite matrix extends a positive measure from diagonal entries to full spectra and orientations. The Stein or log-det divergence uses trace of P Q-inverse minus log determinant minus n. It is nonnegative, zero only when the matrices coincide, and invariant under congruence transformations by any invertible A acting on both arguments.

Steep wall

Printed pp. 71-106Geometry of the SPD Cone
Positive-definite matrices form an open convex cone whose boundary is the loss of positive definiteness. The log-det divergence rises steeply toward that wall, as the contour landscape around the identity shows. Congruence invariance is the matrix analogue of changing coordinates, mirroring how alpha representations rescale positive measures while preserving geometric content.

Synthesis and Pitfalls

Three coordinate choices

  • Cone: alpha representations flatten it.
  • Simplex: the constraint bends most of them.
  • Escorts and SPD: generalize the same idea.

Unifying thread

Choose a representation, work linearly there, return to probabilities or matrices.

Pitfalls

  • Treating $q$ and $\alpha$ as unrelated: $\alpha = 2q-1$.
  • Forgetting renormalization inside the simplex.
  • Assuming every invariant divergence is flat.
  • Reading escorts as ad hoc weights.

Takeaway

One deformation, many faces: means, entropy, escorts, matrices.

Printed pp. 71-106Synthesis and Pitfalls
Three coordinate choices organize the chapter: alpha representations flatten the cone, the simplex bends most of them, and escorts plus SPD matrices generalize the idea. Common pitfalls are treating q and alpha as unrelated, forgetting renormalization inside the simplex, and assuming every invariant divergence is flat when the log-det family includes both.
