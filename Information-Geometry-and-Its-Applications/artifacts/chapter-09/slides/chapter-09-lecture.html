Nuisance Fibers Over a Target

Fiber bundle picture

The model splits as $(\psi,\lambda)$; fixing $\psi$ leaves a fiber.

Coordinate split

$\theta = (\psi,\lambda),\quad \psi \in \mathbb{R}^k,\ \lambda \in \mathcal{N}$

Classical assumption

Standard asymptotics fix $\dim\lambda$ as $n\to\infty$.

Printed pp. 191-213Nuisance Fibers
Write the model as a target coordinate psi and a nuisance coordinate lambda. Fixing psi leaves a fiber of nuisance values, and the manifold becomes a bundle of such fibers. Classical asymptotics assume the fiber dimension is fixed while data grow; the incidental-parameter regime violates that assumption directly.

The Neyman-Scott Variance Experiment

Model

$Y_{ij}=\theta_i+\varepsilon_{ij},\ \varepsilon_{ij}\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$

Target

Common variance $\sigma^2$.

Nuisance

One mean $\theta_i$ per group, estimated from only $m$ points.

Profile estimator

$\hat\sigma^2_{\text{prof}}=\dfrac{1}{nm}\sum_{i,j}(Y_{ij}-\bar Y_i)^2$

Simulation

For $m=2$ the profile mean sits at $0.5$ across all $n$; truth is $1$.

Printed pp. 191-213Neyman-Scott Experiment
With two observations per group and one mean per group, the profile likelihood variance estimator converges to half the truth no matter how many groups we collect. The simulation confirms the persistent bias: concentrating around the wrong value is worse than being noisy, because more data buys false confidence.

Incidental-Parameter Inconsistency

Persistent bias

$E[\hat\sigma^2_{\text{prof}}]=\frac{m-1}{m}\sigma^2$, independent of $n$.

The fix

Divide residuals by $n(m-1)$ to restore consistency.

Degrees of freedom

$\hat\sigma^2_{\text{eff}}=\dfrac{1}{n(m-1)}\sum_{i,j}(Y_{ij}-\bar Y_i)^2$

Printed pp. 191-213Incidental-Parameter Inconsistency
The bias is structural, not a small-sample wrinkle. Each group mean consumes one degree of freedom, so dividing the residual sum by group size m instead of m minus one shrinks the estimate by the factor m minus one over m. Dividing by n times m minus one restores consistency.

Estimating Functions

Unbiasedness

$E_{\psi,\lambda}\!\big[\,G(Y,\psi)\,\big]=0\ \ \forall\,\lambda$

Estimator

Solve $G(Y,\hat\psi)=0$ without a full likelihood.

What $G$ encodes

Orthogonality to nuisance motion and correct degrees of freedom.

Printed pp. 191-213Estimating Functions
An estimating function G is unbiased when its expectation vanishes at the true target value for every nuisance value. Solving G equals zero defines an estimator without committing to a full likelihood. The right G encodes orthogonality to nuisance motion and the correct degrees-of-freedom accounting, which is exactly what the profile likelihood missed.

Nuisance Tangent Space and the Efficient Score

Decompose the score

Split the target score into nuisance projection plus residual.

Efficient score

$\tilde\ell_\psi = \ell_\psi - \Pi_{\mathcal{T}_\lambda}\,\ell_\psi$

Pythagoras

$\|\ell_\psi\|^2 = \|\Pi\ell_\psi\|^2 + \|\tilde\ell_\psi\|^2$

Printed pp. 191-213Efficient Score Projection
The naive target score leans partly into nuisance directions. Project it onto the nuisance tangent space and subtract: the residual is the efficient score, orthogonal to every nuisance direction. The Pythagorean split of squared norms verifies the decomposition, and orthogonality is the geometric definition of efficiency.

Semiparametric Projection

Nuisance as a function

Baseline density, censoring law, or firing-rate background.

$L^2$ projection

$\tilde\ell = \ell - \mathrm{proj}_{\,\overline{\mathcal{T}_\lambda}}\,\ell$

Check

Residual is orthogonal to every nuisance basis direction.

Printed pp. 191-213Semiparametric Projection
In a semiparametric model the nuisance object can be a whole function: a baseline density, a censoring law, a firing-rate background. The efficient score is still a projection, now in an L-squared inner product. We approximate the infinite nuisance space by a finite basis, project the target score, and keep the residual.

Spike Intervals as a Local Diagnostic

Point-process view

Rate background is nuisance; stimulus modulation is the target.

Burst warning

Inter-spike intervals shorten where the background rate rises.

Rule, not admiration

Separate bursts with an estimating equation or projection.

Printed pp. 191-213Spike-Interval Diagnostic
The nuisance theme reappears in point processes. A rate background is nuisance; a stimulus modulation is the target. Inter-spike intervals shorten where the background rate rises, so local bursts can mimic target signal. The diagnostic warns that bursts must be separated by an estimating rule, not admired by the raw likelihood.

What the Geometry Teaches

Inspect the geometry

Likelihood is fine until nuisance dimension grows with $n$.

Estimating functions

Encode orthogonality and degrees-of-freedom accounting.

Semiparametric efficiency

Projection away from nuisance tangent directions.

One question

$\tilde\ell_\psi = \ell_\psi - \Pi_{\mathcal{T}_\lambda}\ell_\psi$

Practical repairs

Within-group centering plus replication $m$ control incidental bias.

Recap

Nuisance directions are geometry, not just extra symbols.

Printed pp. 191-213What the Geometry Teaches
The lesson is not that likelihood is bad but that its geometry must be inspected when nuisance dimension changes with sample size. Estimating functions and semiparametric projection answer one question: which variation is genuinely informative about the target after nuisance motion has been removed? Replication and within-group centering are the practical repairs.
