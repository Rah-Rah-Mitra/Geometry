The Jordan Curve Theorem

Statement

Two components: a bounded inside and unbounded outside.

Boundary

The curve is the common boundary of both.

Components

$\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus C = \text{Int} \sqcup \text{Ext}$

Proof

Fundamental group and covering spaces.

Printed pp. 372-402The Jordan Curve Theorem
The Jordan curve theorem states that a simple closed curve in the plane divides it into exactly two connected components, a bounded inside and an unbounded outside, and is their common boundary. Intuitively obvious yet famously hard to prove, it is established here using the fundamental group and covering space machinery.

The Jordan Separation Theorem

Arcs

An arc does not separate the plane.

Curves

A simple closed curve separates it.

Technique

Mayer-Vietoris style fundamental group arguments.

Goal

Build toward the full Jordan theorem.

Printed pp. 372-402The Jordan Separation Theorem
A preliminary separation theorem shows that any arc in the plane does not separate it, while a simple closed curve separates it into at least two pieces. These local-to-global statements are assembled with Mayer-Vietoris style arguments on fundamental groups to control how subsets disconnect the plane. They build directly toward the full Jordan theorem.

Invariance of Domain

Statement

An injective continuous map of an open set is open.

Consequence

Such a map is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Openness

$U$ open, $f$ injective continuous $\Rightarrow f(U)$ open

Dimension

The plane does not embed in a line.

Printed pp. 372-402Invariance of Domain
Invariance of domain asserts that a continuous injective map from an open subset of the plane into the plane is open, so its image is open and the map is a homeomorphism onto it. This guarantees dimension is a topological invariant in low dimensions and forbids the plane from embedding into a line.

The Winding Number

Definition

Net signed revolutions of a loop about a point.

Source

From the fundamental group of the punctured plane.

Winding number

$n(\gamma, p) \in \mathbb{Z}$

Use

Distinguishes inside from outside.

Printed pp. 372-402The Winding Number
The winding number of a loop about a point counts net signed revolutions and is computed from the fundamental group of the punctured plane. It is invariant under homotopies avoiding the point and provides the quantitative tool that distinguishes inside from outside in the Jordan curve theorem and detects when curves enclose a region.
