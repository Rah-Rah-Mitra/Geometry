Printed pp. 443-467Coverings and Lifting
Recall that a covering map has evenly covered neighborhoods and that paths lift uniquely. A covering induces an injection of fundamental groups, and the image subgroup records exactly which loops downstairs lift to loops upstairs. This subgroup is the invariant that will index the entire classification of coverings.

The Lifting Criterion

Statement

A map lifts when its group image fits inside the covering's.

Hypotheses

Path connected and locally path connected domain.

Criterion

$f_*\,\pi_1(Y) \subseteq p_*\,\pi_1(\widetilde{X})$

Role

Printed pp. 443-467The Lifting Criterion
The general lifting lemma says a continuous map from a path connected, locally path connected space lifts through a covering precisely when the image of its fundamental group lies inside the image subgroup of the covering. This single criterion governs when maps lift and underlies the equivalence and existence theorems that follow.

The Classification Theorem

Bijection

Coverings match conjugacy classes of subgroups.

Order

Larger coverings, smaller subgroups.

Correspondence

$\{\text{coverings}\} \leftrightarrow \{\text{subgroups of } \pi_1\}$

Parallel

Mirrors Galois theory of fields.

Printed pp. 443-467The Classification Theorem
For a suitably nice base space there is a bijection between connected coverings up to equivalence and conjugacy classes of subgroups of the fundamental group. Larger coverings correspond to smaller subgroups, exactly mirroring the Galois correspondence between field extensions and subgroups, a striking structural parallel across mathematics.

The Universal Cover

Definition

The simply connected covering dominating all others.

Existence

Needs semilocal simple connectedness.

Trivial subgroup

$p_*\,\pi_1(\widetilde{X}) = \{1\}$

Prototype

The real line over the circle.

Printed pp. 443-467The Universal Cover
The simply connected covering, when it exists, is the universal cover; it dominates every other covering and corresponds to the trivial subgroup. It exists whenever the base is path connected, locally path connected, and semilocally simply connected. The universal cover of the circle is the real line, the prototype example.

Deck Transformations

Printed pp. 443-467Deck Transformations
The deck transformations of a covering are its self-homeomorphisms over the base; they form a group isomorphic to a quotient related to the corresponding subgroup. For the universal cover this group is the whole fundamental group acting freely. With this the course closes, having built topology from sets to symmetry. Thank you.
