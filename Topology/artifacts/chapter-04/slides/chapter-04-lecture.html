Printed pp. 187-227Countability Axioms
A space is first countable when each point has a countable neighborhood basis and second countable when the whole topology has a countable basis. Second countability implies separability and the Lindelof property. These conditions limit how large a space can be and make sequences adequate for detecting closure and continuity.

Printed pp. 187-227Separation Axioms
The separation axioms order spaces by how cleanly disjoint points and closed sets can be enclosed in disjoint open sets. Hausdorff separates points, regular separates a point from a closed set, and normal separates two disjoint closed sets. Compact Hausdorff spaces and metric spaces are automatically normal, the key examples.

The Urysohn Lemma

Statement

In a normal space, disjoint closed sets are separated by a function.

Idea

A rational-indexed scale of nested open sets.

Urysohn function

$f:X\to[0,1],\ f|_A=0,\ f|_B=1$

Power

Continuous functions exist where only open sets did.

Printed pp. 187-227The Urysohn Lemma
The Urysohn lemma states that in a normal space any two disjoint closed sets can be separated by a continuous real-valued function taking the value zero on one and one on the other. Its proof builds the function from a dense scale of nested open sets indexed by the rationals, a striking constructive argument.

Urysohn Metrization Theorem

Statement

Regular plus countable basis implies metrizable.

Method

Embed into the Hilbert cube.

Embedding

$X \hookrightarrow [0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$

Coordinates

A countable family of Urysohn functions.

Printed pp. 187-227Urysohn Metrization
The Urysohn metrization theorem says every regular space with a countable basis is metrizable. The proof embeds the space into a countable product of intervals, the Hilbert cube, using a countable family of Urysohn functions as coordinates. This gives a clean sufficient condition for a topology to arise from a metric.

The Tietze Extension Theorem

Statement

Extend a continuous function from a closed set to all of X.

Proof

Uniform approximation via Urysohn functions.

Significance

Shows the strength of normality.

Printed pp. 187-227The Tietze Extension Theorem
The Tietze extension theorem extends a continuous real-valued function defined on a closed subset of a normal space to the whole space. It is proved by uniformly approximating with functions supplied by the Urysohn lemma and summing the corrections. Together these results show how powerful normality is. Next: the Tychonoff theorem.
