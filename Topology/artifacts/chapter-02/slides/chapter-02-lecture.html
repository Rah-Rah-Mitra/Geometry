Topologies and Bases

Axioms

Closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.

Basis

Generates a topology by taking unions.

Topology

$\varnothing,X\in\tau,\quad \textstyle\bigcup U_i\in\tau,\quad U\cap V\in\tau$

Examples

Standard, order, and lower-limit topologies.

Printed pp. 73-144Topologies and Bases
Define a topology on a set as a collection of open sets closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections, containing the whole space and the empty set. A basis generates a topology by taking unions. The standard, order, and lower-limit topologies on the real line illustrate how different bases yield genuinely different spaces.

Closed Sets and Closure

Closed

Complements of open sets.

Closure

The smallest closed set containing a given set.

Interior

The largest open set inside a given set.

Neighborhood test

A point is in the closure iff every neighborhood meets the set.

Printed pp. 73-144Closed Sets and Closure
Closed sets are complements of open sets. The closure of a set is the smallest closed set containing it, and the interior the largest open set inside it. A point lies in the closure exactly when every neighborhood meets the set, giving the limit-point description that connects topology to convergence.

Subspace and Product Topologies

Subspace

Open sets are intersections with the subset.

Product

Generated by products of open sets.

Product basis

$\prod U_\alpha,\ \ U_\alpha=X_\alpha\ \text{for all but finitely many }\alpha$

Subtlety

Only finitely many constraints in infinite products.

Printed pp. 73-144Subspace and Product Topologies
Two fundamental constructions: the subspace topology, where open sets are intersections with a subset, and the product topology, generated by products of open sets. The product topology on infinite products uses only finitely many constraints, a subtle choice that makes Tychonoff's theorem and continuity of projections work correctly.

Continuous Functions

Definition

Preimages of open sets are open.

Homeomorphism

A continuous bijection with continuous inverse.

Continuity

$U\ \text{open}\ \Rightarrow\ f^{-1}(U)\ \text{open}$

Tools

Pasting lemma and maps into products.

Printed pp. 73-144Continuous Functions
A function is continuous when the preimage of every open set is open, generalizing the epsilon-delta definition. Homeomorphisms are continuous bijections with continuous inverse, the isomorphisms of topology. Pasting continuous pieces and forming continuous maps into products give the practical tools for building and recognizing continuous functions.
