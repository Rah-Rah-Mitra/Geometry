Surfaces and Connected Sums

Surface

Locally homeomorphic to the plane.

Connected sum

Remove a disk from each and glue boundaries.

Operation

$S_1 \# S_2$

Atoms

Sphere, torus, projective plane.

Printed pp. 468-498Surfaces and Connected Sums
A surface is a space locally homeomorphic to the plane. The connected sum operation removes a disk from each of two surfaces and glues them along the resulting boundary circles. This operation builds all compact surfaces from a few basic pieces, the sphere, the torus, and the projective plane, the atoms of the classification.

Fundamental Polygons

Edge words

Identify polygon edges in pairs.

Examples

Torus and projective plane from squares.

Torus word

$a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b^{-1}$

Benefit

Printed pp. 468-498Fundamental Polygons
Every compact surface is obtained from a polygon by identifying edges in pairs according to a word. The torus comes from the word that commutes a pair of generators, the projective plane from identifying antipodal boundary points. These planar models make the surface combinatorial and ready for algebraic computation.

The Classification Theorem

Statement

Each compact surface is exactly one standard form.

Forms

Sphere, g tori, or k projective planes.

Normal forms

$S^2,\quad \#_g T^2,\quad \#_k \mathbb{RP}^2$

Proof

Cut-and-paste reduction of edge words.

Printed pp. 468-498The Classification Theorem
The classification theorem states that every compact surface is homeomorphic to exactly one of the sphere, the connected sum of g tori, or the connected sum of k projective planes. The proof reduces an arbitrary edge word to one of these standard forms by a sequence of cut-and-paste moves on the polygon.

Euler Characteristic

Definition

Vertices minus edges plus faces of a triangulation.

Invariance

Independent of the triangulation chosen.

Euler characteristic

$\chi = V - E + F$

Completeness

With orientability, distinguishes all surfaces.

Printed pp. 468-498Euler Characteristic
The Euler characteristic, vertices minus edges plus faces of any triangulation, is a topological invariant. Together with orientability it completely distinguishes the surfaces: the sphere has characteristic two, each handle lowers it by two, and each cross-cap by one. These two invariants are a complete set for compact surfaces.
