Build the natural numbers and the principle of induction, then the well-ordering property that every nonempty set of positive integers has a least element. Recursive definitions are justified here. These tools let us define finite sets precisely and prove their basic combinatorial properties before passing to infinite sets.

Countable and Uncountable Sets

Countable

Injects into the integers; the rationals qualify.

Stability

Countable unions of countable sets stay countable.

Cantor

$|\mathbb{R}| > |\mathbb{N}|$

Diagonal

The reals are uncountable.

Printed pp. 1-72Countable and Uncountable Sets
Define a set as countable when it injects into the integers. Finite unions and countable unions of countable sets stay countable, and the rationals are countable. Cantor's diagonal argument shows the reals are uncountable, establishing a genuine hierarchy of infinite sizes that matters for many topological constructions.

State the axiom of choice and its equivalents: Zorn's lemma and the well-ordering theorem. These principles let us select elements from infinitely many sets simultaneously and order any set.
