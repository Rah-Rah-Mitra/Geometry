The Baire Category Theorem

Baire space

Countable unions of nowhere dense sets miss the interior.

Which spaces

Complete metric and locally compact Hausdorff.

Equivalent form

$\bigcap_n U_n$ dense when each $U_n$ dense open

Consequences

Existence without construction.

Printed pp. 292-316The Baire Category Theorem
A Baire space is one in which a countable union of nowhere dense closed sets has empty interior, equivalently a countable intersection of dense open sets is dense. The Baire category theorem guarantees that complete metric spaces and locally compact Hausdorff spaces are Baire, a result with surprising existence consequences.

Printed pp. 292-316Applications of Baire
Baire category yields striking existence proofs without explicit construction. The set of continuous nowhere differentiable functions is dense, real numbers are uncountable, and the uniform boundedness principle of functional analysis follows. The method shows that a generic point or function has a property even when no single example is easy to write down.

Topological Dimension

Covering dimension

Defined through orders of open refinements.

Intuition

Curves are one, surfaces are two.

Order

$\dim X \le n$ if covers refine to order $\le n+1$

Invariance

A topological invariant of the space.

Printed pp. 292-316Topological Dimension
The covering dimension assigns to a space the smallest integer such that every open cover has a refinement whose order, the maximum number of overlapping sets, is at most one more than that integer. For nice spaces it matches the intuitive dimension, giving curves dimension one and surfaces dimension two.

The Embedding Theorem

Statement

Dimension n embeds in Euclidean space of dimension 2n+1.

Method

General position plus Baire category.

Embedding

$\dim X \le n \Rightarrow X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{2n+1}$

Meaning

Bounds the room a space requires.

Printed pp. 292-316The Embedding Theorem
A compact metrizable space of covering dimension at most a given integer embeds in Euclidean space of dimension twice that integer plus one. The proof uses general position arguments and the Baire category theorem to perturb maps into embeddings. This bounds how much room a finite-dimensional space needs to sit in Euclidean space.
