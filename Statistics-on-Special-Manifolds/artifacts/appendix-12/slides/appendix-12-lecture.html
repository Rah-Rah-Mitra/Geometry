Orthogonal Action and Invariants

Congruence action

For $H\in O(m)$, the map $S\mapsto H S H^{\top}$ preserves symmetry and eigenvalues.

Invariant polynomial

A polynomial $P$ with $P(HSH^{\top})=P(S)$ depends on $S$ only through its spectrum.

Fundamental theorem

$P(S)=Q\big(p_1,\dots,p_m\big),\quad p_j=\operatorname{tr}(S^{j})$

Consequence

Invariants are symmetric functions of eigenvalues, so degree-$k$ invariants are spanned by partitions of $k$.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
Everything begins with the congruence action of the orthogonal group on symmetric matrices. A polynomial that survives this action depends only on eigenvalues, hence only on the elementary symmetric functions or power sums. This is the algebraic fact that forces our polynomials to live on partitions rather than raw matrix entries.

Partitions Index the Theory

Partition of $k$

$\kappa=(k_1\ge k_2\ge\cdots\ge k_p>0)$ with $\sum k_i=k$, written $\kappa\vdash k$.

Young diagram

Rows of boxes of lengths $k_1,k_2,\dots$ picture the partition and its weight.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
Partitions of an integer k are the index set for the whole construction. Each partition is a weakly decreasing sequence summing to k, drawn as a Young diagram. The number of partitions controls how many independent invariant polynomials of a given degree exist, so partitions are the discrete skeleton beneath the matrix analysis.

Zonal Polynomials $C_\kappa(S)$

What they are

Homogeneous symmetric polynomials of degree $|\kappa|$ in the eigenvalues of $S$, indexed by $\kappa$.

Eigenfunction property

They diagonalize the orthogonally invariant Laplace–Beltrami operator on symmetric matrices.

Low-degree examples

$C_{(1)}=\operatorname{tr}S$

$C_{(2)}=\tfrac{1}{3}\big((\operatorname{tr}S)^2+2\operatorname{tr}S^2\big)$

$C_{(1,1)}=\tfrac{2}{3}\big((\operatorname{tr}S)^2-\operatorname{tr}S^2\big)$

Normalization

Coefficients fixed so the splitting identity on the next slide holds exactly.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
Zonal polynomials are the symmetric homogeneous polynomials indexed by partitions that diagonalize the orthogonally invariant Laplace operator. They expand the power of a trace and are normalized so their sum over partitions of k reproduces that power. They are the matrix-argument analogue of single-variable monomials.

The Splitting Identity

Defining normalization

The $k$-th power of the trace is the sum of all zonal polynomials of weight $k$.

Check at $k=2$

$C_{(2)}+C_{(1,1)}=(\operatorname{tr}S)^2$, exactly.

Splitting

$$(\operatorname{tr}S)^{k}=\sum_{\kappa\vdash k} C_\kappa(S)$$

Why it matters

It converts ordinary scalar powers into the partition-indexed basis, term by term, inside every matrix series.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
The defining normalization says that the k-th power of the trace splits as a sum of zonal polynomials over all partitions of k. This identity is what lets every term-by-term computation of a matrix hypergeometric series proceed, because it converts ordinary scalar powers into the partition-indexed basis.

Hypergeometric Functions of Matrix Argument

Series definition

Numerator and denominator parameters $a_i,b_j$ multiply zonal polynomials over all partitions.

Special cases

${}_0F_0=\operatorname{etr}(S)$; ${}_1F_1$ is confluent; ${}_2F_1$ is the matrix Gauss function.

Definition

$${}_pF_q(a;b;S)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\kappa\vdash k}\frac{(a_1)_\kappa\cdots(a_p)_\kappa}{(b_1)_\kappa\cdots(b_q)_\kappa}\frac{C_\kappa(S)}{k!}$$

Role

These functions are the densities and moment generators of matrix-variate distributions.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
Hypergeometric functions of a symmetric matrix argument are defined by series whose general term is a zonal polynomial weighted by ratios of generalized Pochhammer symbols. Choosing the numerator and denominator parameter counts recovers matrix analogues of the exponential, the binomial, and confluent functions used throughout multivariate analysis.

The Generalized Pochhammer Symbol

Per-part shift

Each part $k_i$ of $\kappa$ contributes a rising factorial shifted by $\tfrac{1}{2}(i-1)$.

Encodes parameters

It carries all the $a_i,b_j$ dependence of a hypergeometric coefficient.

Definition

$$(a)_\kappa=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\Big(a-\tfrac{1}{2}(i-1)\Big)_{k_i}$$

where $(x)_n=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)$.

Why partitions

The shift depends on the row index $i$, so a bare degree cannot encode the weight — the full partition is needed.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
The generalized Pochhammer symbol attached to a partition is a product of ordinary rising factorials shifted along each part of the partition. It encodes the parameter dependence of every matrix hypergeometric coefficient, and its appearance is exactly why partitions, not just degrees, are needed to bookkeep the series.

Integral over the Orthogonal Group

Group average

Averaging $\operatorname{etr}(SHTH^{\top})$ over $H\in O(m)$ with Haar measure removes orientation.

James’s formula

$$\int_{O(m)}\!\operatorname{etr}(SHTH^{\top})\,dH=\sum_{k,\kappa}\frac{C_\kappa(S)\,C_\kappa(T)}{k!\,C_\kappa(I_m)}$$

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
The central analytic tool averages an exponential of a trace over the orthogonal group with Haar measure. The result is a zonal-polynomial series in the eigenvalues of the two matrix arguments. This integral is the bridge from group symmetry to explicit special functions on the manifold of symmetric matrices.

Invariant Polynomials of Several Matrices

Two arguments

$C_\phi^{\kappa,\lambda}(S,T)$ is invariant under the simultaneous action $S\mapsto HSH^{\top},\ T\mapsto HTH^{\top}$.

Triple index

Outer partitions $\kappa\vdash k$, $\lambda\vdash l$ and a coupling $\phi\vdash k+l$.

Reduction

$C_\phi^{\kappa,\lambda}(S,I)=\theta\,C_\phi^{\kappa}(S)$

collapses to a single-argument zonal polynomial when one matrix is the identity.

Use

Moments of products $S^{a}T^{b}$ and noncentral matrix distributions expand in these polynomials.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
To handle products and several matrices at once we need invariant polynomials of two or more symmetric arguments. These are indexed by several partitions together with a coupling, and they reduce to zonal polynomials when only one argument is active. They are the tool for moments of products and for noncentral distributions.

Integrals over the Stiefel Manifold

Setup

$X\in V_{k,m}$ ranges over the Stiefel manifold; integrate an invariant of the projection $XX^{\top}$.

Pattern

$$\int_{V_{k,m}}\! C_\kappa(X^{\top}SX)\,(dX)=\frac{C_\kappa(S)}{C_\kappa(I_m)}\,\big[k/2\big]_\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}$$

Output

The result depends on $S$ only through $C_\kappa(S)$ — a ratio in the eigenvalues.

Application

Normalizing constants for the matrix Langevin and Bingham densities on $V_{k,m}$.

Takeaway

Group symmetry plus zonal expansion turns a manifold integral into a closed partition series.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsAppendix 12
Integrating an invariant polynomial of the projection over the Stiefel manifold yields a ratio that depends only on the eigenvalues of the argument. This is the mechanism behind densities of the matrix Langevin and Bingham families, where invariant polynomials supply the normalizing constants and the moment structure.

Worked Example: a Degree-Two Coefficient

Goal

Read off the $k=2$ term of ${}_1F_1(a;b;S)$ from the machinery.

Term

$\dfrac{(a)_{(2)}}{(b)_{(2)}}\dfrac{C_{(2)}(S)}{2!}+\dfrac{(a)_{(1,1)}}{(b)_{(1,1)}}\dfrac{C_{(1,1)}(S)}{2!}$

Pochhammer values

$(a)_{(2)}=a(a+1)$

$(a)_{(1,1)}=a\big(a-\tfrac12\big)$

Assemble

Substitute the two zonal polynomials from slide 4; the result is an explicit polynomial in $\operatorname{tr}S$ and $\operatorname{tr}S^2$.

Recap: Why Invariant Polynomials Matter

Symmetry → invariance

Orthogonal congruence forces dependence on eigenvalues alone.

Partitions → basis

$\kappa\vdash k$ indexes a finite basis of degree-$k$ invariants.

Zonal → series

$C_\kappa$ builds matrix hypergeometric functions term by term.

Integrals → closed forms

Group and Stiefel integrals collapse to partition ratios.

The throughline

$$\text{action}\ \Rightarrow\ \kappa\vdash k\ \Rightarrow\ C_\kappa\ \Rightarrow\ \textstyle\int\ \Rightarrow\ {}_pF_q$$
