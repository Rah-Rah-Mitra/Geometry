Sampling on the Stiefel Manifold

The data

Each $X_a$ is a $p\times r$ matrix with orthonormal columns, a frame on $V_{r,p}$.

The question

From $X_1,\dots,X_n$ recover the frame they scatter around.

Stiefel manifold

$V_{r,p}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}: X^{\top}X=I_r\}$

Special cases

$r=1$ is the sphere $S^{p-1}$; $r=p$ is the orthogonal group.

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
A point of the Stiefel manifold is a matrix with orthonormal columns, an orientation frame rather than a free vector. Each observation is one such frame, so the sample is a cloud of frames scattered around an unknown modal frame whose recovery is the central estimation task of this chapter.

The Matrix Langevin Model

Linear in the data

The log density is a trace functional of $X$.

Parameter split

$F=M\,\mathrm{diag}(\kappa)\,V^{\top}$ separates mode from strength.

Density

$p(X;F)=\dfrac{\exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr} F^{\top}X\big)}{{}_0F_1\!\big(\tfrac{p}{2};\tfrac14 F^{\top}F\big)}$

Reading $F$

$M,V$ orthogonal give orientation; $\kappa\ge 0$ gives concentration.

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
The matrix Langevin density is proportional to the exponential of the trace of F transpose X. The parameter matrix F factors into a modal orientation and a concentration, so its singular value decomposition cleanly separates where the mode points from how tightly the sample concentrates around it.

The Sample Mean Matrix

Resultant

Average the frames entrywise to get $\bar X$.

Sufficiency

The likelihood sees the data only through $\bar X$.

Statistic

$\bar X=\tfrac1n\sum_{a=1}^{n}X_a$

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
Averaging the observed frames entrywise gives the sample mean matrix, the resultant of the data. Because the density depends on the data only through the trace against F, this average is a sufficient statistic. All the information the sample carries about the parameter is compressed into this one matrix.

The Likelihood Equations

Log likelihood

Sum the trace terms and subtract $n$ copies of the log normalizer.

Score

Differentiate in $F$ and set to zero.

Log likelihood

$\ell(F)=n\operatorname{tr} F^{\top}\bar X-n\log c(F)$

Score equation

$\bar X=\nabla_F\log c(F)=\mathbb{E}_F[X]$

Writing the log likelihood for the Langevin model and differentiating in the parameter matrix produces the score equations. Setting the score to zero shows the maximum likelihood estimate must match the observed mean matrix to the expected value predicted by the normalizing constant, a balance condition we solve next.

Maximum Likelihood: A Balance

Two forces

Data resultant pulls toward concentration; the normalizer pulls toward spread.

Mean matching

The MLE equates observed and expected sufficient statistics.

Stationarity

$\hat F:\ \mathbb{E}_{\hat F}[X]=\bar X$

The estimator balances two forces. The observed resultant pulls the parameter toward the data, while the derivative of the log normalizing constant pulls back toward dispersion. The maximum likelihood estimate sits where these tensions cancel, mirroring how exponential family means equate observed and expected sufficient statistics.

Estimating the Modal Orientation

Decompose

Take the singular value decomposition of $\bar X$.

Read the mode

The orthogonal factors estimate $M$ and $V$.

SVD of the resultant

$\bar X=\hat M\,D\,\hat V^{\top}$

Polar reading

The orientation is the polar part of $\bar X$; the diagonal $D$ feeds concentration.

Decompose the sample mean matrix by singular value decomposition. The left and right orthogonal factors estimate the modal orientation directly, while the singular values feed the concentration estimate. The modal frame is therefore read off the polar part of the resultant, a clean separation of direction from strength.

Estimating the Concentration

One per axis

Each singular value $d_j$ fixes one concentration $\hat\kappa_j$.

Monotone

Tighter clusters give larger $d_j$, hence larger $\hat\kappa_j$.

Per-axis equation

$\dfrac{\partial_{\kappa_j}\, c}{c}\Big|_{\hat\kappa}=d_j$

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
With the orientation fixed, the concentration parameters solve a one dimensional equation per singular value. The ratio of a derivative of the normalizing constant to itself must equal the observed singular value. Larger singular values force larger concentrations, so a tightly clustered sample yields a sharply peaked fitted model.

The Matrix Bingham Model

Quadratic form

The exponent uses $\operatorname{tr} X^{\top}A X$ with symmetric $A$.

Axes, not arrows

Antipodal symmetry: $X$ and $-X$ are equally likely.

Density

$p(X;A)\propto \exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr} X^{\top}A X\big),\quad A=A^{\top}$

Inference target

Eigenvectors of $A$ give modal axes; eigenvalues give concentrations.

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
The Bingham density replaces the linear trace with a quadratic form, the trace of X transpose A X. It is antipodally symmetric, so it models axes rather than directions. Inference targets the symmetric parameter matrix A, whose eigenvectors give modal axes and whose eigenvalues give concentrations.

Testing Uniformity

Null model

Uniformity is the Langevin density with $F=0$: no preferred frame.

Evidence

A large resultant $\|\bar X\|$ contradicts pure randomness.

Hypotheses

$H_0:F=0\quad\text{vs}\quad H_1:F\neq 0$

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
The uniform distribution is the null model of no preferred orientation, the Langevin density with parameter matrix zero. Testing uniformity asks whether the observed resultant is large enough to reject pure randomness. The mean matrix near zero supports uniformity, while a strong resultant is evidence against it.

Testing a Specified Orientation

The claim

Test $H_0:M=M_0$ for a hypothesized modal frame $M_0$.

Likelihood ratio

Compare the free maximum against the constrained one.

LR statistic

$\Lambda=-2\big(\ell(M_0)-\ell(\hat M)\big)\ \xrightarrow{d}\ \chi^2_{q}$

Decision

Reject when $\Lambda$ exceeds the chi-square quantile.

Chapter 05 · Inference on ParametersStatistics on Special Manifolds
To test a claimed modal orientation we compare the fitted estimate against the hypothesized frame. The likelihood ratio contrasts the maximized likelihood with the likelihood constrained to the specified orientation. Under the null its statistic has a known limiting chi squared law, giving calibrated rejection regions.

Recap: The Inference Workflow

Estimate

Form $\bar X$, take its SVD, read $\hat M,\hat V$, solve for $\hat\kappa$.

Test

Uniformity via $F=0$; orientation via the likelihood ratio.

Why it works

Sufficiency of $\bar X$ loses nothing; the score balance gives interpretation.

One line

$\bar X=\hat M D\hat V^{\top}\ \Rightarrow\ (\hat M,\hat V,\hat\kappa)$

The inference pipeline is compact. Form the sample mean matrix, take its singular value decomposition, read off the modal orientation, solve for the concentration, then test uniformity or a specified orientation. Sufficiency guarantees nothing is lost, and the score balance gives every estimate a clear interpretation.
