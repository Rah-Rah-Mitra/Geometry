Data

Parameters

Modal orientation $\Theta$ and concentration $\kappa$.

Likelihood

$\hat\theta_n = \arg\max_{\theta}\; \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p(X_i;\theta)$

Goal

Track $\hat\theta_n$ as $n$ grows large.

Here we name the objects: a population distribution on a Stiefel or Grassmann manifold, a true modal orientation, a concentration parameter, and a maximum likelihood estimator built from independent draws. Fixing this notation early lets every later asymptotic statement attach cleanly to a concrete parameter and a concrete estimator.

Consistency of the MLE

Statement

$\hat\theta_n \to \theta_0$ in probability as $n\to\infty$.

Engine

Uniform convergence plus a well separated maximum.

Limit objective

$\ell_n(\theta)\xrightarrow{p}\; \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0}\!\big[\log p(X;\theta)\big]$

Conclusion

The sample maximizer follows the population maximizer.

Asymptotics on special manifoldsConsistency of the MLE
Consistency is the first guarantee: as the sample grows the estimator settles onto the true parameter. We sketch the argument through uniform convergence of the normalized log-likelihood and a well separated maximum, so the maximizer of the sample objective converges to the maximizer of its expectation.

Tangent Space at the Mode

Why linearize

Curved manifolds have no global flat coordinates.

Chart

The exponential map sends $T_{\Theta}\mathcal{M}$ back to $\mathcal{M}$.

Asymptotics on special manifoldsTangent Space at the Mode
Because the manifold is curved, fluctuations cannot live in a flat parameter vector. We linearize at the modal point and pass to its tangent space, the natural flat arena where small deviations add like vectors. The exponential map carries these tangent vectors back onto the manifold near the mode.

Asymptotic Normality

Rescale

Multiply the tangent deviation by $\sqrt{n}$.

Limit

A centered multivariate Gaussian appears.

Central limit

$\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{Log}_{\Theta}(\hat\theta_n)\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\,I(\Theta)^{-1}\big)$

Curvature

Enters only through the chart, not the law.

Asymptotics on special manifoldsAsymptotic Normality
This is the central limit statement for manifold estimators. Rescaled by the square root of the sample size, the tangent coordinate of the estimator converges to a centered Gaussian. The curvature enters only through the choice of chart, while the limiting law itself is an ordinary multivariate normal on the tangent space.

Fisher Information

Score

Gradient of the log-likelihood along tangent directions.

Curvature

Hessian of the expected log-likelihood at $\Theta$.

Information matrix

$I(\Theta)=\mathbb{E}\big[\, s(X;\Theta)\, s(X;\Theta)^{\!\top}\big]$

Role

It is the precision of the limiting Gaussian.

Asymptotics on special manifoldsFisher Information
The Fisher information matrix quantifies how sharply the likelihood distinguishes nearby parameters along tangent directions. Computed as the expected outer product of the score, it sets the curvature of the expected log-likelihood at the mode and becomes the precision matrix of the limiting Gaussian distribution we derived.

Asymptotic Covariance

Formula

Efficiency

More information means a tighter limit.

Limiting covariance

$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\theta_n)\;\approx\;\tfrac{1}{n}\,I(\Theta)^{-1}$

The shape of the confidence ellipse.

Asymptotics on special manifoldsAsymptotic Covariance
Asymptotic efficiency closes the loop: the limiting covariance of the estimator is the inverse Fisher information scaled by one over the sample size. Larger information yields a tighter limit, and the inverse directly predicts the size and shape of the confidence ellipse drawn in the tangent space.

Estimating Concentration

Parameter

Concentration $\kappa$ controls dispersion about $\Theta$.

Coupling

Strong $\kappa$ sharpens orientation information.

Joint limit

$\sqrt{n}\begin{pmatrix}\hat\Theta_n-\Theta\\ \hat\kappa_n-\kappa\end{pmatrix}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,I^{-1})$

Block form

Asymptotics on special manifoldsEstimating Concentration
Beyond orientation we estimate concentration. Its score and information decouple partially from the orientation block, and its maximum likelihood estimator inherits the same root-n normal behavior. We note how strong concentration sharpens orientation information, coupling the two limiting variances through the population law.

CLT for the Mean Matrix

Statistic

The sample mean matrix $\bar X_n=\tfrac1n\sum_i X_i$.

Limit

A Gaussian array on matrix space.

Matrix CLT

$\sqrt{n}\,\big(\bar X_n-\mathbb{E}X\big)\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$

Projection

Mapped to $T_{\Theta}\mathcal{M}$ it gives normality.

Asymptotics on special manifoldsCLT for the Mean Matrix
The sample mean matrix is the workhorse statistic. A matrix central limit theorem shows its entries, recentered and rescaled, converge jointly to a Gaussian array whose covariance reflects the population second moments. Projecting this limit onto the tangent space recovers the estimator's asymptotic normality.
