Why Matrix-Argument Polynomials

The obstacle

Noncentral latent-root densities appear as slow zonal-polynomial series in the noncentrality matrix.

The remedy

Re-expand in polynomials orthogonal to the natural weight so coefficients decouple by degree.

Series viewpoint

$f(L) = w(L)\sum_{\kappa} c_\kappa\, P_\kappa(L)$

Payoff

Each $c_\kappa$ isolates one piece of the noncentrality; truncation gives controlled approximations.

This slide frames the practical payoff that motivates the whole construction.

Orthogonality is a Weighted Inner Product

Definition

$\langle P_j,P_k\rangle_w=\!\int P_j P_k\, w = h_k\,\delta_{jk}$

Geometric reading

Distinct degrees are perpendicular under the weight; the Gram matrix is diagonal.

The Matrix-Variate Normal Weight

Argument

A symmetric $m\times m$ matrix $X$; integration runs over the space of symmetric matrices.

Trace form

The exponent is a trace quadratic form, the matrix analogue of $-x^2/2$.

Weight

$w(X)\propto \exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\,\mathrm{tr}\,X^2\big)$

Reduction

At $m=1$ this is the scalar Gaussian, recovering ordinary Hermite.

Generalized Hermite Polynomials

Partition index

$H_\kappa(X),\quad \kappa=(k_1\ge\dots\ge k_m)$

Leading term

The top piece is the zonal polynomial $C_\kappa(X)$; lower partitions correct it.

Matrix Rodrigues

$H_\kappa(X)\,w(X)=(-\partial_X)^{\kappa}\,w(X)$

Key shift

The single degree $k$ becomes a partition $\kappa$ of the matrix size.

Generating Functions

Hermite kernel

$\mathrm{etr}\!\big(TX-\tfrac12T^2\big)=\sum_\kappa \dfrac{C_\kappa(T)}{k!}\,H_\kappa(X)$

Laguerre kernel

${}_0F_1(\alpha; TS)\,\mathrm{etr}(-T)=\sum_\kappa c_\kappa\,L_\kappa^{(\alpha)}(S)$

How to read it

Expand the kernel in zonal polynomials, then match partition coefficients.

Why useful

The fastest route to explicit low-order polynomials and their norms $h_\kappa$.

Both families admit generating functions written as hypergeometric functions of matrix argument with two matrix slots. Expanding the kernel in zonal polynomials and matching partition coefficients recovers the individual polynomials. This is the most efficient route to explicit low-order polynomials and to their orthogonality constants.

The tail of the series controls the approximation, giving a monitored bound.
