Stiefel and Grassmann Manifolds

Stiefel

Orthonormal $k$-frames in $\mathbb{R}^p$.

Grassmann

The $k$-planes those frames span.

Stiefel manifold

$V_{k,p} = \{\, X \in \mathbb{R}^{p\times k} : X'X = I_k \,\}$

Descent

Right-invariant laws on $V_{k,p}$ pass to $G_{k,p}$.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsFrames and Subspaces
The Stiefel manifold collects orthonormal frames of k columns in p dimensions, while the Grassmann manifold collects the k-planes those frames span. Distributions defined invariantly on the frame descend to the subspace whenever they ignore the orthogonal rotation of the frame, a recurring theme throughout the chapter.

The Uniform Distribution

Invariant

Unchanged by left and right orthogonal maps.

Null model

The hypothesis of no preferred direction.

Invariance

$d\mu(QXR) = d\mu(X), \quad Q\in O(p),\; R\in O(k)$

Limit

Statistics on Special ManifoldsThe Uniform Baseline
The uniform law is the unique probability measure invariant under both left and right orthogonal transformations. It is the natural null hypothesis of no preferred direction, the reference against which concentration is measured, and the limiting case every concentrated family approaches as its concentration parameter shrinks to zero.

Matrix Langevin Density

Linear exponent

Density grows with $\operatorname{tr}(F'X)$.

von Mises-Fisher

The Stiefel generalization of the sphere law.

Density

$p(X) = c(F)^{-1}\,\exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr}(F'X)\big)$

Parameter

$F \in \mathbb{R}^{p\times k}$ sets mode and strength.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsMatrix Langevin
The matrix Langevin density is proportional to the exponential of the trace of F-transpose times X. This linear-in-X exponent generalizes the von Mises-Fisher law from the sphere to the Stiefel manifold, with the matrix F encoding both where the distribution peaks and how tightly it concentrates there.

Modal Orientation and Concentration

SVD of F

$F = \Gamma\,\Lambda\,\Theta'$ splits direction from strength.

Mode

The density peaks at $X = \Gamma\,\Theta'$.

Decomposition

$\operatorname{tr}(F'X) = \operatorname{tr}\!\big(\Lambda\,\Theta'X'\Gamma\big)$

Concentration

Singular values $\lambda_i$ tighten mass at the mode.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsMode and Strength
Writing F through its singular value decomposition separates direction from strength. The orthogonal factors fix the modal orientation that maximizes the density, while the singular values act as concentration parameters. Larger singular values pull mass tightly around the mode; vanishing singular values return the uniform distribution.

Matrix Bingham Density

Quadratic exponent

Density grows with $\operatorname{tr}(X'AX)$.

Antipodal

Unchanged when any column flips sign.

Density

$p(X) = d(A)^{-1}\,\exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr}(X'AX)\big)$

Use

Axes and undirected orientations, not signed rays.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsMatrix Bingham
The matrix Bingham density is proportional to the exponential of the trace of X-transpose A X, quadratic in X. Because the exponent is unchanged when any column flips sign, the law is antipodally symmetric, making it the correct model for axes and undirected orientations rather than signed directions.

Axial Symmetry and Eigenstructure

Eigenvalues of $A$

Set where mass gathers and where it thins.

Identity shift

$A \mapsto A + cI$ leaves the law unchanged.

Only gaps matter

$\operatorname{tr}(X'(A+cI)X) = \operatorname{tr}(X'AX) + ck$

Orientation

Top eigenvectors of $A$ fix the axis up to sign.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsEigenstructure
The symmetric matrix A controls Bingham shape through its eigenvalues. Mass concentrates along eigenvectors with the largest eigenvalues and is depleted along the smallest. Adding a multiple of the identity leaves the law unchanged, so only eigenvalue differences matter, fixing the orientation up to sign.

Bipolar and Girdle Patterns

Statistics on Special ManifoldsBipolar vs Girdle
Two eigenvalue regimes give two signature shapes. A single dominant eigenvalue produces a bipolar pattern with mass at both poles of an axis; one small eigenvalue produces a girdle of mass spread around a great circle. These patterns classify real axial data such as crystal and fault orientations.

Normalizing Constants

Langevin

A $_0F_1$ in the squared singular values.

Bingham

A $_1F_1$ in the eigenvalues of $A$.

Hypergeometric of matrix argument

$c(F) \propto {}_0F_1\!\big(\tfrac{p}{2}; \tfrac14\Lambda^2\big)$

Series

Statistics on Special ManifoldsNormalizing Constants
Both concentrated families have normalizing constants that are hypergeometric functions of matrix argument, written with zonal polynomials. The Langevin constant is a zero-F-one in the singular values squared; the Bingham constant is a one-F-one in the eigenvalues of A. These series make moments and inference computable.
