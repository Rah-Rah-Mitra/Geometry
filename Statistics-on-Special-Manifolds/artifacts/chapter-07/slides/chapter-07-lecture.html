The Matrix Langevin Density

Trace exponent

Density grows with the alignment between $X$ and the parameter $F$.

Normalizer

A hypergeometric function of matrix argument closes the density.

Density on $V_{p,n}$

$p(X) = \dfrac{1}{{}_0F_1(\tfrac{n}{2};\tfrac14 F^\top F)}\,\exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr}F^\top X\big)$

Parameter $F$

Its polar factor names the mode; its singular values set concentration.

Chikuse, Chapter 07The Matrix Langevin Density
We recall the matrix Langevin density on the Stiefel manifold, written through the trace of F transpose X. The matrix parameter F encodes both the modal orientation and the strength of concentration via its singular values, and the normalizing constant is a hypergeometric function of matrix argument.

Stiefel Manifold and Modal Orientation

Orthonormal frames

$V_{p,n}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}: X^\top X = I_p\}$.

The mode

The orientation aligned with $F$ via its polar part $\Gamma$.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Stiefel Manifold and Modal Orientation
The Stiefel manifold collects orthonormal p-frames in n-dimensional space. The mode of the matrix Langevin density is the orientation aligned with the parameter, obtained from its polar part. Inference asks how sharply observed frames cluster around this modal orientation, which sets the concentration scale.

Low versus High Concentration

Spread regime

Small singular values: frames scatter near-uniformly over $V_{p,n}$.

Cap regime

Large singular values: a tight cap forms around the mode.

Why it matters

Only the cap regime makes local Gaussian approximation accurate.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Low vs High Concentration
At low concentration the sampled frames spread broadly, nearly uniformly, over the manifold. As the singular values of the parameter grow, the mass collapses into a tight cap around the mode. The high-concentration regime is exactly where local Gaussian approximation becomes accurate and useful.

The Concentration Parameter

Polar split

Write $F=\Gamma\,\kappa\,\Delta^\top$: orientation times a scale.

Large $\kappa$

The exponent decays steeply away from the mode.

High-concentration scaling

$\operatorname{tr}F^\top X \;=\; \kappa\,\operatorname{tr}\Delta\,\Gamma^\top X,\qquad \kappa\to\infty$

Consequence

Steep decay is what licenses every asymptotic expansion ahead.

Chikuse, Chapter 07The Concentration Parameter
We separate the parameter into a modal orientation and a scalar concentration multiplying a fixed direction structure. Large concentration means large singular values, which means the exponent in the density decays steeply as a frame departs from the mode. This steep decay drives every asymptotic argument that follows.

Tangent Space at the Mode

Tangent constraint

$T\in T_\Gamma V_{p,n}$ satisfies $\Gamma^\top T + T^\top\Gamma = 0$.

Local chart

Coordinates on this plane carry the linearized model.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Tangent Space at the Mode
At the modal orientation we attach the tangent space to the Stiefel manifold. Tangent vectors satisfy a skew-symmetry constraint with the mode, giving the space its dimension. Local coordinates on this tangent plane will carry the linearized model and convert manifold inference into Euclidean inference.

Linearizing the Density

Second order

Expand $\operatorname{tr}F^\top X$ about $\Gamma$ in a tangent coordinate $T$.

Stationary mode

The linear term vanishes; only a quadratic survives.

Quadratic exponent

$\operatorname{tr}F^\top X \approx \operatorname{tr}F^\top\Gamma - \tfrac12\,\kappa\,\langle T, T\rangle_{H}$

Negative definite

The Hessian form $H$ makes the leading shape a Gaussian.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Linearizing the Density
We expand the trace exponent of the matrix Langevin density to second order in a tangent coordinate about the mode. The linear term vanishes because the mode is a stationary point, leaving a negative-definite quadratic form. Exponentiating that quadratic produces a Gaussian shape on the tangent space.

The Gaussian Approximation

Matrix-normal limit

$T$ is approximately matrix-normal on the tangent space.

Covariance

Inverse of the concentration-scaled Hessian, $\propto \kappa^{-1}$.

Chikuse, Chapter 07The Gaussian Approximation
The leading local approximation is a matrix-normal law on the tangent coordinate, with covariance set by the inverse of the concentration-scaled Hessian. As concentration grows the approximation error shrinks, so the curved Stiefel problem is replaced by a flat matrix-normal problem near the mode.

Normalizing Constant Expansion

Hypergeometric

${}_0F_1(\tfrac n2;\tfrac14 F^\top F)$ resists direct evaluation.

Large-$\kappa$ form

Leading exponential times a Gaussian volume factor.

Asymptotic expansion

$\log {}_0F_1 \;\sim\; \textstyle\sum_i \kappa_i \;-\; \tfrac12\log\det H \;+\; O(\kappa^{-1})$

Payoff

Likelihoods and moments become computable in closed form.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Normalizing Constant Expansion
The hypergeometric normalizing constant admits a high-concentration asymptotic expansion. The leading factor is the exponential of the summed singular values times a Gaussian-integral volume term, with polynomial corrections in inverse concentration. This expansion makes likelihoods and moments computable without evaluating the full hypergeometric series.

Matrix-Normal Inference

Reduced problem

Estimating mode and concentration becomes a Gaussian fit.

Estimators

Mode from the sample mean frame; $\kappa$ from residual spread.

Approximate MLE

$\hat\Gamma = \text{polar}(\bar X),\qquad \hat\kappa^{-1} \propto \tfrac1m\textstyle\sum_k \|T_k\|^2$

Closed form

No hypergeometric inversion needed in the cap regime.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Matrix-Normal Inference
Because the linearized model is matrix-normal, estimation of the modal orientation and concentration reduces to familiar Gaussian computations. The maximum likelihood estimate aligns with the sample mean frame, and the concentration is read from the spread of tangent residuals, giving closed-form approximate inference.

Diagnostic: Resultant Length

Mean resultant

$R = \|\bar X\|$ measures how tightly frames cluster.

Reading it

$R\to 0$ near uniform; $R\to 1$ under strong concentration.

Chikuse, Chapter 07Diagnostic: Resultant Length
A finite diagnostic tracks the norm of the mean resultant of sampled frames. Near uniformity it stays small; under strong concentration it approaches one. Watching this scalar as concentration varies confirms that the tight-cap regime, where the Gaussian approximation is trusted, has genuinely been entered.
