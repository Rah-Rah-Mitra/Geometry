The Sample Sum Matrix

Build it

Add up $n$ frames $X_1,\dots,X_n$ on the Stiefel manifold $V_{k,m}$.

Decompose it

The singular value decomposition splits orientation from strength.

Sample sum and its SVD

$$S=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i = H\,D\,G^{\top}$$

Reading it

$H,G$ are orthogonal orientations; $D=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_k)$ holds the latent roots.

Source: structure and terminology onlyChapter 04: Distributional Problems
Each observation is a frame on the Stiefel manifold; summing the sample produces an unconstrained matrix whose singular value decomposition splits cleanly. The orthogonal factors record orientation while the diagonal of singular values records strength. This factorization is the spine of every distributional result in Chapter 04.

The Matrix Langevin Model

Linear weight

A frame is weighted by $\operatorname{tr}(F^{\top}X)$ against a parameter $F$.

Concentration dial

Singular values of $F$ tune how sharply orientation is pinned.

Langevin density

$$p(X)\propto \exp\!\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\,F^{\top}X\bigr)$$

Limits

$F\to 0$ gives the uniform law; large $F$ pins the modal frame.

Source: structure and terminology onlyChapter 04: Distributional Problems
The matrix Langevin density weights a frame by the trace of the parameter matrix against it, so the singular values of that parameter set the concentration. Large singular values pin orientation near a modal frame; small ones flatten the density toward the uniform law on the manifold, giving us a tunable dial.

The Bingham Model

Quadratic weight

A frame is weighted through a symmetric form $A$.

Antipodal symmetry

$X$ and $-X$ share the same density: axial data.

Bingham density

$$p(X)\propto \exp\!\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\,X^{\top}A\,X\bigr)$$

Home

Lives naturally on the Grassmann manifold of subspaces.

Source: structure and terminology onlyChapter 04: Distributional Problems
The Bingham family weights a frame quadratically, through the trace of a symmetric form sandwiched by the frame and its transpose. Because the weight is even, the model is antipodally symmetric and lives naturally on the Grassmann manifold, describing axial data where a direction and its negative are indistinguishable.

Integrating Out the Orientation

Average over O(m)

Marginalize the orthogonal factors to isolate concentration.

Not elementary

The group integral yields a hypergeometric function of matrix argument.

To isolate concentration we average the joint density over the orthogonal group, integrating away the orientation factors. The integral over O(m) is not elementary; it produces a hypergeometric function of matrix argument whose two matrix parameters are the concentration matrix and the latent roots themselves.

Zonal and Invariant Polynomials

Indexed by partitions

$C_\kappa(B)$ for a partition $\kappa$ of an integer $r$.

They diagonalize

Integration over O(m) becomes bookkeeping over partitions.

Average of a zonal polynomial

$$\int_{O(m)} C_\kappa(B H A H^{\top})\,dH = \frac{C_\kappa(A)\,C_\kappa(B)}{C_\kappa(I_m)}$$

Role

The matrix analogue of symmetric power sums.

Source: structure and terminology onlyChapter 04: Distributional Problems
Zonal polynomials are symmetric functions indexed by partitions that diagonalize integration over the orthogonal group. They are the matrix analogue of power symmetric functions, and every group integral in this chapter expands as a series in zonal polynomials, turning intractable integrals into bookkeeping over partitions.

Hypergeometric Functions of Matrix Argument

Zonal series

Sum zonal polynomials with Pochhammer coefficients over all partitions.

What it captures

Langevin and Bingham normalizers, and marginal root densities.

Series definition

$${}_pF_q(a;b;B)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\kappa\vdash r}\frac{(a_1)_\kappa\cdots(a_p)_\kappa}{(b_1)_\kappa\cdots(b_q)_\kappa}\frac{C_\kappa(B)}{r!}$$

Payoff

Closed-form language for otherwise unevaluable integrals.

Source: structure and terminology onlyChapter 04: Distributional Problems
Collecting zonal polynomial terms with Pochhammer coefficients defines hypergeometric functions of matrix argument. The normalizing constants of the Langevin and Bingham models, and the marginal latent-root densities, are all special cases. These series are the closed-form language Chapter 04 uses for otherwise unevaluable manifold integrals.

Marginal and Conditional Laws

Marginal

Integrate orientation away to leave the latent-root law.

Conditional

Fix the roots to leave an orientation law on the Stiefel manifold.

Two routes

$$p(D)=\!\int_{O}\! p(H,D)\,dH,\qquad p(H\mid D)=\frac{p(H,D)}{p(D)}$$

Complementary

One answers concentration, the other answers direction.

Source: structure and terminology onlyChapter 04: Distributional Problems
Marginal laws come from integrating one factor away; conditional laws fix it and study the rest. Conditioning on the latent roots leaves an orientation distribution on the Stiefel manifold, while marginalizing over orientation leaves the latent-root law. The two routes answer complementary inferential questions about the same sample.
