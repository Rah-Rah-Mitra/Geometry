What grows

Ambient dimension $m$; frame size $p$ fixed or scaling with $m$.

What to track

Setting

$V_{p,m}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}: X^\top X = I_p\}, \quad m\to\infty$

Reading limits

Hold $p$ fixed, send $m\to\infty$: the cleanest asymptotics.

Here we fix the bookkeeping for the whole deck. The ambient dimension m grows, the frame size p may stay fixed or grow with m, and every limiting statement is read against that ratio. Students should leave this slide knowing which quantity is held constant and which is sent to infinity throughout.

Concentration of Measure on Spheres

Equatorial mass

Almost all area of $S^{m-1}$ lies near any fixed equator.

Band width

The band shrinks like $1/\sqrt{m}$ as dimension grows.

Tail bound

$\sigma\big(\{x: |x_1|>t\}\big) \le 2\,e^{-mt^2/2}$

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsConcentration of Measure
The first phenomenon is concentration of measure. On a high-dimensional sphere almost all surface area sits in a thin band near any equator. We give the exponential tail bound and stress that the width of that band shrinks like one over the square root of the dimension, which drives everything later in the chapter.

Levy's Lemma and Lipschitz Statistics

From area to functions

Any $L$-Lipschitz $f$ on $S^{m-1}$ clusters at its median.

Why it matters

Smooth functionals of a random frame inherit the same tails.

Levy's lemma

$\mathbb{P}\big(|f - \mathrm{med}\,f| > t\big) \le 2\,e^{-mt^2/(2L^2)}$

Consequence

Estimators on $V_{p,m}$ are sharply concentrated.

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsLevy's Lemma
Levy's lemma upgrades concentration of area into concentration of functions. Any Lipschitz statistic on the sphere clusters tightly around its mean with Gaussian tails. We connect this directly to estimators on the Stiefel manifold, since smooth functionals of a random frame inherit the same sharp concentration behavior in growing dimension.

Near-Orthogonality of Random Frames

The slogan

Two random unit vectors are almost orthogonal in high $m$.

The rate

Their inner product scatters with width $1/\sqrt{m}$.

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsNear-Orthogonality
This slide makes precise the slogan that random high-dimensional vectors are nearly orthogonal. The inner product of two independent uniform unit vectors concentrates at zero with standard deviation one over the square root of the dimension, so a sampled frame behaves like an exactly orthonormal one as m grows large.

Inner Products Concentrate at Zero

Scaled limit

$\sqrt{m}\,\langle u,v\rangle$ tends to a standard normal.

Geometry preserved

This underpins Johnson-Lindenstrauss embeddings.

Limiting law

$\sqrt{m}\,\langle u,v\rangle \;\xrightarrow{d}\; \mathcal{N}(0,1)$

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsInner Products at Zero
We quantify the previous slogan with a distribution. The scaled inner product of two uniform unit vectors is asymptotically standard normal, and its histogram collapses onto a spike at the origin as dimension climbs. This is the engine behind Johnson-Lindenstrauss style preservation of geometry under random projection.

Limiting Law of the Largest Coordinate

Gaussian coordinates

$\sqrt{m}\,x_i$ are asymptotically i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.

Slow maxima

The largest entry grows only like $\sqrt{2\log m / m}$.

Extreme value

$\sqrt{m}\,\max_i x_i \approx \sqrt{2\log m}$

Caution

No single coordinate dominates; mass spreads evenly.

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsLargest Coordinate
A uniform point on a high-dimensional sphere has coordinates that are jointly close to independent Gaussians after scaling. We use this to describe extreme-value behavior of the largest coordinate and to caution that maxima grow only like the square root of a logarithm of the dimension, which is remarkably slow.

Stiefel and Grassmann as $m$ Grows

Stiefel columns

Columns become asymptotically independent Gaussian directions.

Grassmann spans

A uniform subspace is the span of such directions.

Gaussian surrogate

$\sqrt{m}\,X \;\xrightarrow{d}\; G, \quad G_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$

Where exact

Fixed $p$, growing $m$: the surrogate is sharp in the limit.

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsStiefel and Grassmann
Now we lift single-vector facts to frames and subspaces. Columns of a uniform Stiefel matrix become asymptotically independent Gaussian directions, and a uniform Grassmann subspace looks like the span of such directions. The fixed-p, growing-m regime is where these clean Gaussian surrogates become exact in the limit.

Two independent uniform subspaces meet at principal angles whose cosines concentrate near zero as dimension grows, so random subspaces are almost orthogonal complements. We give the leading-order law of these cosines and tie it back to near-orthogonality of the underlying frames from the earlier slides.

Gaussian Surrogate and the CLT

Center and scale

Subtract the mean, multiply by $\sqrt{m}$.

Tangent covariance

The limit variance comes from the tangent space.

Manifold CLT

$\sqrt{m}\big(T(X)-\mu\big) \;\xrightarrow{d}\; \mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$

Use

Builds tests and confidence regions on $V_{p,m}$.

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsGaussian Surrogate and CLT
This slide states the central-limit backbone. Smooth statistics of a uniform frame, once centered and scaled by the square root of the dimension, converge to Gaussian limits whose covariance we can compute from the tangent space. This is the asymptotic distribution result that powers tests and confidence regions on the manifold.

Worked Estimate: Frame Coherence

Coherence

Largest off-diagonal inner-product magnitude of a random frame.

Tools

Union bound over $\binom{n}{2}$ pairs plus Gaussian tail.

High-probability bound

$\mu(F) \le C\sqrt{\tfrac{\log n}{m}} \quad \text{w.h.p.}$

Reading

Many vectors can stay nearly orthogonal at once.

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsFrame Coherence
We run a concrete calculation. The mutual coherence of a random frame, the largest off-diagonal inner product magnitude, is bounded with high probability by a square-root-log-over-dimension rate. Students see the union bound and the Gaussian tail combine into a usable finite-sample guarantee for sampled high-dimensional frames.

Pitfalls and Recap

Pitfalls

  • Three-dimensional intuition misleads.
  • Fixed thresholds lose meaning.
  • Always track the ratio $p/m$.

Recap

  • Concentration: mass near the equator.
  • Near-orthogonality: $\langle u,v\rangle \approx 0$.
  • Gaussian limit: scaled statistics are normal.
One toolkit

$\text{concentration} + \text{near-orthogonality} \Rightarrow \text{Gaussian inference}$

High-Dimensional AsymptoticsPitfalls and Recap
We close by naming traps: intuition from three dimensions misleads, fixed thresholds become meaningless, and the ratio of frame size to ambient dimension must be tracked. The recap reassembles concentration, near-orthogonality, and the Gaussian limit into one coherent toolkit for high-dimensional manifold statistics and asymptotic inference.
