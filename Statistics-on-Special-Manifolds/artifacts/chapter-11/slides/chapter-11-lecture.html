The Cross-Product Matrix

Definition

Each pair collapses to $C = X^{\top}Y$, a compact summary of alignment.

Read-out

Singular values of $C$ measure how directions agree.

Summary matrix

$C = X^{\top}Y, \qquad \|C\|^2 = \operatorname{tr}(C^{\top}C)$

Statistics on Special ManifoldsThe Cross-Product Matrix
The transpose product of two paired frames is the chapter workhorse. It collapses each pair into a compact matrix whose singular values summarise alignment. We interpret large singular values as strong directional agreement and a vanishing average as the signature of independence between frames.

Independent versus Associated

Statistics on Special ManifoldsIndependent versus Associated
Two contrasting pictures anchor intuition. Under independence the second frame is uniformly random given the first, so relative orientations scatter uniformly. Under strong association the second frame clusters near a fixed rotation of the first, and the resultant cross-product matrix grows away from zero.

Joint Matrix-Langevin Model

Bilinear coupling

Frames interact through a single coupling matrix $A$.

Independence limit

$A=0$ factorises the density into free marginals.

Joint density

$f(X,Y) \propto \exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr}(A^{\top} X^{\top} Y)\big)$

Coupling matrix

$A$ acts as a directional regression coefficient between frames.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsJoint Matrix-Langevin Model
We build a joint density coupling the two frames through a bilinear exponent. The coupling matrix plays the role of a directional regression coefficient: setting it to zero factorises the density into independent marginals, while a large coupling concentrates the conditional orientation of one frame around the other.

Conditional Orientation

Condition on $X$

The law of $Y$ given $X$ is again matrix-Langevin.

Centre

It concentrates around the rotated frame $XA$.

Conditional law

$f(Y\mid X) \propto \exp\!\big(\operatorname{tr}((XA)^{\top} Y)\big)$

Interpretation

$A$ predicts the orientation of $Y$ from $X$: directional regression.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsConditional Orientation
Conditioning one frame on the other yields again a matrix-Langevin law, now centred at a rotated copy of the conditioning frame. This conditional view turns the coupling matrix into a predictor of orientation and explains why the model behaves like directional regression between frames.

Measures of Association

Mean cross-product

Population summary $M = E[X^{\top}Y]$.

Coefficients

Normalised singular values $\rho_i\in[0,1]$.

Association scale

$\rho_i = \sigma_i(M), \quad 0=\text{none},\; 1=\text{deterministic}$

Zero test

$M=0$ exactly characterises absence of association.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsMeasures of Association
We define scalar summaries of dependence built from the mean cross-product matrix. Its singular values, suitably normalised, give canonical association coefficients lying between zero and one, with zero meaning no association and one meaning a deterministic orthogonal relationship between the two frames.

Canonical Correlation of Frames

SVD of $M$

$M = U\,\Sigma\,V^{\top}$ pairs matched directions.

Canonical pairs

Columns of $U,V$ are maximally associated direction pairs.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsCanonical Correlation of Frames
Singular vectors of the mean cross-product matrix pick out paired directions that are maximally associated, exactly mirroring classical canonical correlation analysis. Each singular value is a canonical correlation between a direction in one frame and a matched direction in the other frame.

Estimating the Coupling

Sample mean

Average the per-pair cross-products.

Decompose

Take the SVD of $\bar{M}$ to read off the fit.

Moment estimator

$\bar{M} = \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{a=1}^{n} X_a^{\top} Y_a = \hat{U}\hat{\Sigma}\hat{V}^{\top}$

Read-out

$\hat{U},\hat{V}$ estimate alignment rotations; $\hat{\Sigma}$ estimates the $\rho_i$.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsEstimating the Coupling
Given a sample of paired frames we average the cross-product matrices and take its singular value decomposition. The orthogonal factors estimate the alignment rotations and the singular values estimate the canonical association coefficients, giving a complete moment-style fit of the coupling structure.

Testing Independence

Hypothesis

$H_0: A=0$, equivalently $M=0$, no association.

Statistic

Built from the resultant length of $\bar{M}$.

Test statistic

$T = n\,\|\bar{M}\|^2 \;\xrightarrow{\;H_0\;}\; \chi^2_{\,df}$

Decision

Reject independence for large $T$ against the chi-squared quantile.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsTesting Independence
Independence corresponds to a zero coupling matrix, so its sample analogue tests the hypothesis directly. We form a statistic from the summed squared singular values of the mean cross-product matrix, whose null distribution is approximately chi-squared by a standard large-sample expansion.
