The Stiefel Manifold $V_{k,m}$

Orthonormal frames

$k$ mutually perpendicular unit vectors arranged as the columns of $X$.

Special cases

$V_{1,m}$ is the unit sphere $S^{m-1}$; $V_{m,m} = O(m)$.

Definition

$V_{k,m} = \{\, X \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times k} : X^{\top}X = I_k \,\}$

Reading the constraint

Each column has unit length; distinct columns are orthogonal.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsThe Stiefel Manifold
The Stiefel manifold collects every orthonormal k-frame inside m-dimensional space, encoded as a tall matrix whose columns are mutually perpendicular unit vectors. The defining equation X transpose X equals the identity is a clean system of constraints that we will count carefully in a moment.
Statistics on Special ManifoldsChapter 01

Counting the Dimension of $V_{k,m}$

Ambient entries

A matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}$ has $mk$ free coordinates.

Constraints

$X^{\top}X = I_k$ is symmetric, giving $\tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1)$ equations.

Dimension

$\dim V_{k,m} = mk - \tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1)$

Check

$V_{1,m}$ gives $m-1$, the sphere $S^{m-1}$.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsCounting Stiefel Dimensions
Dimension counting subtracts constraints from the ambient coordinate count. A tall matrix has m times k entries, and orthonormality imposes k unit-length conditions plus the symmetric off-diagonal pairings. The bookkeeping yields the formula every later integral on the Stiefel manifold quietly assumes.

The Orthogonal Group Acts Transitively

Left action

$Q \in O(m)$ sends $X \mapsto QX$, another orthonormal frame.

Homogeneous space

Any frame reaches any other; $V_{k,m}$ is a single orbit.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsThe Orthogonal Group Acts
Left multiplication by an m by m orthogonal matrix rotates a frame into another frame while preserving every inner product. This action is transitive, so the Stiefel manifold is a single orbit, a homogeneous space, and the symmetry is the engine behind invariance arguments throughout the chapter.

The Grassmann Manifold $G_{k,m}$

Subspaces, not frames

A point is a $k$-dimensional plane through the origin of $\mathbb{R}^m$.

Smallest case

$G_{1,m}$ is the set of lines through the origin: projective space.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsThe Grassmann Manifold
The Grassmann manifold forgets which frame spans a subspace and remembers only the subspace itself. Each point is a k-dimensional plane through the origin, so a line through the origin in the plane is the smallest nontrivial example and fixes intuition for the higher cases.

From Frames to Subspaces

Same span

$X$ and $XR$ span one subspace whenever $R \in O(k)$.

Right action

$O(k)$ rotates the frame within its own span.

Equivalence

$X \sim X' \iff X' = XR,\; R \in O(k)$

Reading it

The subspace is the invariant; the frame is a choice of basis.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsFrames to Subspaces
Two frames span the same subspace exactly when one is obtained from the other by a k by k orthogonal change of basis. Quotienting the Stiefel manifold by this right action of the smaller orthogonal group produces the Grassmann manifold as the space of orbits.

The Quotient $V_{k,m}/O(k) = G_{k,m}$

Quotient relation

$G_{k,m} \;=\; V_{k,m}\,/\,O(k)$

Dimensions subtract

$\dim G_{k,m} = k(m-k)$

Statistics on Special ManifoldsThe Quotient Picture
The quotient relation packages frames, subspaces, and rotations into one diagram. The Stiefel manifold sits above, the Grassmann manifold below, and the fibre over each subspace is a copy of the orthogonal group recording the frames that span it. Dimensions subtract cleanly across this map.

The Invariant (Haar) Measure

Invariance

A measure unchanged by $X \mapsto QX$ for every $Q \in O(m)$.

Uniqueness

Transitivity forces a unique normalized probability measure.

Defining property

$\mu(Q\!\cdot\! A) = \mu(A),\quad \mu(V_{k,m}) = 1$

Why it matters

It is the meaning of "uniformly at random" on the manifold.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsInvariant Measure
Because the orthogonal group acts transitively, there is a unique probability measure invariant under that action, the normalized Haar measure. It is the only sensible notion of choosing a frame or a subspace uniformly at random, and it underwrites every distribution defined later.

Sampling Uniformly via Gaussians

Recipe

Draw $Z$ with i.i.d. $N(0,1)$ entries, then orthonormalize its columns.

Why it works

The Gaussian law is rotation invariant, so the frame is uniform.

Construction

$Z = X\,T,\quad X = Z\,(Z^{\top}Z)^{-1/2} \sim \mu$

Bonus

Its column span is uniform on $G_{k,m}$.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsUniform Sampling
A concrete recipe makes the invariant measure tangible. Fill a tall matrix with independent standard Gaussian entries and orthonormalize the columns; rotational symmetry of the Gaussian guarantees the resulting frame is uniform. The same draw projects down to a uniform random subspace on the Grassmannian.

Tangent Spaces and the Splitting

Differentiate the constraint

$X^{\top}\dot{X} + \dot{X}^{\top}X = 0$ at every tangent vector.

Horizontal vs vertical

Horizontal moves the subspace; vertical only spins the frame.

Tangent space

$T_X V_{k,m} = \{\,\dot{X} : X^{\top}\dot{X}\text{ skew}\,\}$

Grassmann tangents

Only the horizontal part survives the quotient.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsTangent Spaces
Differentiating the constraint X transpose X equals identity along a curve shows the velocity must satisfy a skew-symmetry condition after projection. The tangent space splits into a horizontal part that moves the subspace and a vertical part that only spins the frame in place.

Exponential and Polar Coordinates

Matrix exponential

$A$ skew-symmetric gives $\exp(A) \in O(m)$, sweeping curves of frames.

Polar retraction

$Y \mapsto Y(Y^{\top}Y)^{-1/2}$ pulls full-rank matrices onto $V_{k,m}$.

Two charts

$X(t) = \exp(tA)\,X_0,\qquad \pi(Y) = Y(Y^{\top}Y)^{-1/2}$

Use

Coordinates for analysis, geodesics, and simulation.

Statistics on Special ManifoldsExponential and Polar Coordinates
Local coordinates make the manifolds computable. The matrix exponential of a skew-symmetric generator sweeps out geodesic-like curves, while the polar decomposition retracts any nearby full-rank matrix back onto the Stiefel manifold, giving two practical charts for analysis and simulation.
