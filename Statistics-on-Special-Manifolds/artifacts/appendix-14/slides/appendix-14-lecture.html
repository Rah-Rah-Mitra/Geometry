Appendix 14Edgeworth and Saddle-Point Expansions
This appendix builds two refinements of the normal approximation for statistics defined on special manifolds. The Edgeworth series corrects the Gaussian by skewness and kurtosis terms, while the saddle-point method tilts the density to follow its dominant exponential behaviour, giving far smaller relative error in the tails.

Cumulants and the Cumulant Generating Function

Definition

The CGF is the log of the moment generating function.

Coefficients

Its Taylor coefficients are the cumulants $\kappa_r$.

Generating function

$K(t)=\log \mathbb{E}\,e^{tX}=\sum_{r\ge 1}\kappa_r\,\dfrac{t^r}{r!}$

Shape data

$\kappa_3$ skewness, $\kappa_4$ kurtosis; both shrink with $n$.

Appendix 14Cumulants and the CGF
Every approximation here starts from the cumulant generating function, the logarithm of the moment generating function. Its Taylor coefficients are the cumulants, with the third and fourth controlling skewness and kurtosis. For a normalised sum of independent pieces these cumulants shrink like inverse powers of the sample size.

From Characteristic Function to Series

Factor out the Gaussian

The characteristic function splits into normal times correction.

Invert term by term

Fourier inversion of each power gives a density correction.

Exponent expansion

$\hat f(t)=e^{-t^2/2}\exp\!\Big(\sum_{r\ge 3}\dfrac{\kappa_r(it)^r}{r!}\Big)$

Scaling

Powers of $n^{-1/2}$ order the correction terms.

Appendix 14From Characteristic Function to Series
The characteristic function of a standardised statistic factors into the Gaussian piece times a correction whose exponent collects the higher cumulants. Expanding that exponent in powers of the scaling parameter and inverting the Fourier transform term by term produces the Edgeworth series in physical space.

The Edgeworth Series

Leading term

Order $n^{-1/2}$ skewness correction via $H_3$.

Next term

Order $n^{-1}$ kurtosis and squared skewness.

Density expansion

$f(x)=\phi(x)\Big[1+\dfrac{\kappa_3}{6}H_3(x)+\cdots\Big]$

Caution

Asymptotic, not convergent; truncate carefully.

Appendix 14The Edgeworth Series
The Edgeworth expansion writes the density as the standard normal multiplied by a polynomial built from Hermite polynomials. The leading correction is the skewness term of order one over root n; the next gathers kurtosis and squared skewness at order one over n. Truncation gives an asymptotic, not convergent, approximation.

The Saddle-Point Idea

Inversion

Recover the density by inverse Laplace transform.

Stationary point

Deform through where the exponent is flat.

Saddle equation

$K'(\hat t)=x,\qquad f(x)\approx \dfrac{e^{K(\hat t)-\hat t x}}{\sqrt{2\pi K''(\hat t)}}$

Concentration

Integrand decays steeply away from the saddle.

Appendix 14The Saddle-Point Idea
The saddle-point method recovers the density by inverse Laplace transform and deforms the integration contour through the point where the exponent is stationary. That saddle lies on the real axis at the value solving the tilt equation, and the integrand decays steeply away from it, concentrating all the mass there.

Exponential Tilt and the Legendre Transform

Re-centring

Tilt so the target point becomes the mean.

Conjugate

The exponent is the Legendre transform of $K$.

Legendre rate

$K^{*}(x)=\sup_t\,\big(tx-K(t)\big)=\hat t x-K(\hat t)$

Link

Same conjugate as large deviations and dual flatness.

Appendix 14Exponential Tilt and Legendre
Evaluating at the saddle is an exponential tilt: the distribution is re-centred so the point of interest becomes its mean. The exponent of the approximation is the Legendre transform of the cumulant function, the same convex conjugate that governs large-deviation rates and dually flat geometry on these manifolds.

Relative Error in the Tails

Saddle-point

Relative error $O(n^{-1})$, uniform into the tails.

Edgeworth

Absolute error only; can go negative far out.

Error orders

$\dfrac{f(x)}{\hat f_{\mathrm{sp}}(x)}=1+O(n^{-1})$

Why it matters

Manifold tests reach extreme statistic values.

Appendix 14Relative Error in the Tails
The saddle-point density carries a relative error of order one over n that stays uniform into the tails, whereas the Edgeworth series controls only absolute error and can turn negative far out. This tail accuracy is exactly what manifold inference needs when test statistics reach extreme values.

Application to Statistics on Manifolds

Cumulant source

Matrix moments on Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds.

Test statistics

Invariant functions of the sample.

Pipeline

$\text{moments}\;\to\;\kappa_r\;\to\;K(t)\;\to\;\hat f(x)$

Payoff

Appendix 14Statistics on Special Manifolds
On Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds the cumulants of invariant statistics come from matrix moments of the underlying distribution. Feeding those cumulants into either expansion yields tractable densities for test statistics, so the abstract machinery becomes a concrete recipe for p-values and confidence regions on the manifold.

Summary and Numerical Checks

One engine

Cumulants drive both expansions.

Two roles

Edgeworth for the body, saddle-point for tails.

One bridge

The Legendre transform joins them.

Sanity check

$\displaystyle\int \hat f(x)\,dx \approx 1,\quad \mathrm{TV}(\hat f,f)<\mathrm{TV}(\phi,f)$

Habit

Confirm the correction beats the plain normal.

Appendix 14Summary and Checks
To summarise: cumulants drive both methods; Edgeworth adds Hermite corrections for the body, saddle-point tilts the density for the tails, and the Legendre transform links them. A good numerical check confirms the corrected density integrates near one and beats the plain normal in total variation.
