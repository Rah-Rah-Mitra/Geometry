The Stiefel Manifold

Orthonormal frames

Matrices $X$ whose $k$ columns are orthonormal vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Group action

Left and right rotations move frames without altering geometry.

Definition

$V_{k,n} = \{\, X \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times k} : X'X = I_k \,\}$

Invariant measure

A unique normalized measure $(X'dX)$ we aim to factor.

The Stiefel manifold collects matrices with orthonormal columns, the rectangular cousins of orthogonal matrices. Its invariant measure is the object we want to factor. Before differentiating anything, we fix notation: an n by k frame, its column space, and the rotations that act on the left and right without changing geometry.

Singular Value Decomposition

Three factors

An orthonormal $H$, a diagonal $D$ of singular values, a rotation $G'$.

Latent roots

The diagonal entries are the radial coordinates of a density.

Factorization

$X = H\,D\,G', \quad D = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k)$

Ordering

Fixing $\sigma_1 > \cdots > \sigma_k > 0$ removes ambiguity.

Decompositions and JacobiansStatistics on Special Manifolds
Every real matrix factors as a left orthogonal frame, a diagonal of singular values, and a right rotation. Geometrically this is rotate, stretch along axes, rotate again. The singular values are the latent roots that will carry the radial part of any matrix-variate density, while the two orthogonal factors carry orientation.

Polar Decomposition

Orientation × stretch

Split a matrix into an orthonormal frame and a symmetric factor.

From the SVD

Set $P = G\,D\,G'$ and absorb the rest into the frame.

Decomposition

$X = U\,P, \quad U'U = I_k, \quad P = P' \succeq 0$

Projection

$U$ is the nearest orthonormal frame to $X$.

Decompositions and JacobiansStatistics on Special Manifolds
The polar decomposition writes a matrix as an orthogonal frame times a positive semidefinite symmetric factor, gluing two halves of the singular value decomposition. It separates a pure orientation from a pure stretch. On the Stiefel manifold this projection onto orthonormal columns is exactly how we map data back to the frame it most resembles.

QR and Gram-Schmidt

Sequential frame

Orthonormalize columns one by one to build $Q$.

Triangular record

$R$ stores the projections removed; its positive diagonal fixes uniqueness.

Factorization

$X = Q\,R, \quad Q'Q = I_k, \; R \text{ upper triangular}$

Jacobian-friendly

Each column adds one clean layer to differentiate.

Gram-Schmidt orthonormalizes columns one at a time, producing an orthonormal frame Q and an upper triangular R that records the projections discarded along the way. The triangular factor's positive diagonal makes the decomposition unique. This sequential view gives the cleanest path to a Jacobian, since each column adds one controllable layer.
