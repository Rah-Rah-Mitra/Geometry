Determinants

Read the determinant before computing it

\( \det[a\ b] = a_x b_y - a_y b_x \)
\( \det[u\ v\ w] = (u \times v)\cdot w \)
Graphics reading
The sign says whether orientation is preserved or flipped. The magnitude says how much area or volume a frame spans. Zero means collapse.
Determinant as geometryorientation / area / volume / collapse
Pause before writing formulas. A determinant is a measurement of the frame built by the columns. In two dimensions it tests triangle winding and area scale; in three dimensions it tests handedness and volume. This geometric reading explains why a zero determinant is a collapse, not just a bad denominator.
Determinant rules

Scaling changes size; shearing keeps it

Rules to keep in working memory
  • \(\det(k a, b) = k\,\det(a,b)\)
  • \(\det(a + k b, b) = \det(a,b)\)
  • \(\det(a, b+c) = \det(a,b)+\det(a,c)\)
  • \(\det(A^{T}) = \det(A)\)
  • \(\det(A B) = \det(A)\det(B)\)
These rules are also a compact way to inspect transform pipelines: scale accumulates multiplicatively, while pure shear can change shape without changing area.
Determinant invariantssigned size behaves like a multilinear measurement
The point of these rules is not memorization for its own sake. Scaling one vector scales the measured size, while sliding one side parallel to itself preserves the size. Those two pictures make determinant algebra feel inevitable and prepare students for Cramer ratios and determinant tests later.
Matrices

A matrix is a map described by its columns

Column combination view
\( A x = x_1 A e_1 + x_2 A e_2 + x_3 A e_3 \)
  • The first column is where the first basis vector lands.
  • The input coordinates weight those transformed basis columns.
  • A square matrix changes coordinates, shapes, and local frames.
Matrix-vector multiplicationcolumns are images of basis directions
Stress the column view because it is the most useful graphics mental model. When a vertex or direction vector is multiplied by a transform, its coordinates choose a linear combination of transformed basis directions. This also clarifies why scale, shear, and rotation are visible in the columns.
Matrix multiplication

The product rule is engineered for composition

Entry view
\( C_{ij}=\sum_k A_{ik}B_{kj} \)

Every entry is a row-column dot product. This is the loop-level view used in implementations.

Column view
\( C_{\*,j}=A\,B_{\*,j} \)

The product transforms all columns of \(B\) by \(A\), then stacks the results.

Composition view
\( A(Bx)=(AB)x \)

With column vectors, the rightmost transform acts first. This is the source of many graphics bugs.

Matrix product interpretationsrow-dot / column-transform / composition
Connect the abstract product to rendering practice. The row-column rule looks strange until it is tied to composition, because one composite matrix can transform many vertices. Make students say the order aloud: AB applied to x means B acts first, then A under the column-vector convention.
Operations on matrices

Identity, transpose, inverse, determinant

Operation
Algebra
Graphics meaning
Identity
\(I x = x\)
Leaves every point or direction unchanged.
Transpose
\((AB)^T=B^T A^T\)
Swaps row and column readings; order reverses.
Inverse
\(A A^{-1}=I\)
Undo a transform or move rays into object space.
Determinant
\(\det(A^{-1})=1/\det(A)\)
Tracks area or volume scale and singularity.
Product determinant
\(\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)\)
Scale factors multiply through a pipeline.
Orthogonal inverse
\(R^{-1}=R^T\)
Rigid frame inversion is cheap and stable.
Implementation caution
The inverse of a product reverses order: \((AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}\). Applying inverse camera and model transforms in the wrong order produces a plausible but incorrect scene.
Matrix operations as pipeline contractsspecial cases are speed and correctness tools
This slide is a compact reference, but it should be taught as a collection of contracts. Identity is the do-nothing transform, transpose changes the viewpoint on the array, inverse is an undo operation, and determinant tells whether that undo operation can exist without degeneracy.
Special matrix families

Structure makes computation cheaper and meaning clearer

Diagonal
\(D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,s_2,s_3)\)

Independent axis scales. Easy eigenvalues, easy determinant.

Symmetric
\(A=A^T\)

Real orthogonal eigenbasis. Excellent for quadratic forms and ellipses.

Orthogonal
\(Q^TQ=I\)

Length and angle preserving. Determinant is plus or minus one.

Singular
\( \det(A)=0 \)

Collapses a dimension. Inverse and unique solve fail.

Special matricesuse structure before reaching for a general algorithm
Move from left to right and ask what each structure buys. Diagonal matrices isolate axes, symmetric matrices provide clean orthogonal eigendirections, orthogonal matrices make inverse equal transpose, and singular matrices explain collapsed dimensions. These categories become shortcuts in graphics code and diagnostics.
Computing determinants

Cofactors organize the sign bookkeeping

Expansion pattern
\( \det(A)=\sum_i a_{ij} C_{ij} \)

A cofactor is the determinant of a smaller matrix, multiplied by the alternating checkerboard sign for its position.

\( C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\det(M_{ij}) \)
For graphics-sized 2D and 3D derivations, cofactors are inspectable. For production solvers, factorization-based numerical methods are usually preferred.
Cofactor expansiongood for derivation, not a high-performance determinant routine
Explain that cofactors are a disciplined way to avoid losing signs. The checkerboard sign and the smaller determinant are conceptually simple, but the work grows quickly. That is why the lecture uses cofactors to understand inverses and Cramer rule, while still recommending robust numerical routines in real code.
Inverses and singularity

An inverse exists only when the map does not collapse space

\( A^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(A)/\det(A) \)
Practical interpretation
  • The adjoint formula explains the determinant denominator.
  • Small determinant means the undo operation amplifies error.
  • A singular transform destroys at least one degree of freedom.
Inverse via adjoint and determinantcollapse is geometric before it is algebraic
Treat the adjoint formula as explanatory rather than as the recommended implementation. The determinant in the denominator says exactly what can go wrong: if the frame area or volume has vanished, no algebraic undo can recover the lost direction. Near-zero determinants should trigger suspicion even when a library returns numbers.
Linear systems

Solving \(A x=b\) means finding coordinates in a frame

Cramer-style coordinate ratios
\( x_i=\det(A_i \leftarrow b)/\det(A) \)
  • The denominator measures the original frame.
  • The numerator measures the frame with one column replaced.
  • If \(\det(A)=0\), the coordinate request is not unique.
Use this as geometric intuition. Production code should favor stable solve routines over forming explicit inverses.
Linear systemscoordinate recovery in a basis frame
Present Cramer rule as a lens on coordinates. The unknown coordinate is an area or volume ratio, so the same determinant that tests invertibility appears in the denominator. This also explains why a collapsed or nearly collapsed frame creates ambiguous or unstable solutions.
Eigenvectors

Some directions survive the transform

Definition
\( A v = \lambda v,\quad v\ne 0 \)

An eigenvector may stretch, shrink, or reverse, but it stays on the same line. The scale factor is the eigenvalue.

\( \det(A-\lambda I)=0 \)
Eigenvalue equationinvariant directions are transform fingerprints
Make the invariant-line idea vivid. Most vectors turn under a transform, but an eigenvector stays on its line. The determinant equation appears because a nonzero vector can solve the shifted homogeneous system only when that shifted matrix is singular.
Symmetric matrices

Diagonalization chooses the right coordinate frame

Spectral factorization
\( A = Q D Q^T \)
  • \(Q^T\) expresses vectors in the eigenbasis.
  • \(D\) scales each eigendirection independently.
  • \(Q\) returns the result to world coordinates.
  • Real symmetric matrices have orthogonal eigenvectors.
Symmetric diagonalizationrotate to eigen-coordinates, scale, rotate back
The right-to-left reading is crucial. First rotate into the eigenbasis, then apply independent diagonal stretches, then rotate back. This slide should make diagonalization feel like a coordinate choice rather than a mysterious formula. The symmetry assumption is what gives the clean orthogonal basis.
Singular value decomposition

SVD is the geometry of a general real matrix

Factorization
\( A = U\Sigma V^T \)
  • \(V\): orthonormal input directions.
  • \(\Sigma\): nonnegative stretches.
  • \(U\): orthonormal output directions.
  • \(\sigma_i^2\) are eigenvalues of \(A^T A\) or \(A A^T\).
SVD geometryinput directions / singular scales / output directions
Use SVD as the generalization that remains geometrically honest when eigenvectors become inconvenient. A nonsymmetric matrix may shear the circle into an ellipse without a clean orthogonal eigenbasis. Singular vectors still give orthonormal input directions, nonnegative scale factors, and orthonormal output directions.
Area, conditioning, diagnostics

Singular values tell how fragile a transform is

Area or volume scale
\( |\det(A)|=\prod_i \sigma_i \)

The determinant magnitude is the product of singular stretches.

Conditioning
\( \kappa(A)=\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min} \)

Large ratios mean small input errors can become large output errors.

Collapse test
\( \sigma_{\min}\approx 0 \)

A near-zero singular value reveals almost-lost dimension even if entries look ordinary.

SVD diagnosticssingular values describe stretch and numerical risk
The SVD is not just a decomposition; it is a diagnostic report. The product of singular values gives area or volume scale, and the ratio between largest and smallest singular values predicts sensitivity. This is why SVD language appears in robust graphics tools and geometry processing.
Applied lab

Normals are perpendicularity constraints, not ordinary arrows

Correct normal transform
\( n'=(A^{-1})^T n \)
  • Tangents transform by \(A\).
  • Normals must remain perpendicular to transformed tangents.
  • Naively applying \(A n\) fails under shear or nonuniform scale.
Normal-vector labpreserve perpendicularity after transforming the surface
This is the payoff slide for graphics. Positions and tangents are carried forward by the linear part of the transform, but normals encode the constraint of being perpendicular to the tangent plane. The inverse transpose is the transform that preserves that dot-product constraint after shear or nonuniform scale.

Recommended graphics debugging habit
  • Assert determinant sign and magnitude when a transform changes orientation or scale.
  • Assert \(A A^{-1}\) and residual norms before trusting inverse-based workflows.
  • Assert \(Q D Q^T\) or \(U\Sigma V^T\) reconstruction when explaining decompositions.
  • Assert transformed normal perpendicularity after any nonuniform scale or shear.

Chapter 06 summary

Linear algebra becomes a graphics inspection toolkit

Takeaways
  • Determinants measure signed size and reveal orientation or collapse.
  • Matrix multiplication is designed so composite transforms work.
  • Inverses and linear systems depend on nonzero, well-conditioned frames.
  • Symmetric eigen decomposition turns maps into rotate-scale-rotate stages.
  • SVD extends that story to general real matrices.
  • Normals use inverse transpose because they preserve perpendicularity.
