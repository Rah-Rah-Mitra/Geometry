Section 14.1

Photons Give Spectral Energy A Handle

Photon Packet

Graphics uses a photon as an energy packet with position, direction, wavelength, and medium-dependent speed.

$$q = h f = {h c \over \lambda}$$
Invariant

For visible wavelengths, shorter wavelength means larger packet energy. Binning changes totals, not the density being estimated.

Sensor Translation

RGB images are channel summaries of spectra, so color errors often begin as wavelength bookkeeping errors.

Treat photons as computational packets, not as a demand to simulate quantum physics. The useful facts are wavelength, direction, speed in a medium, and energy. The decreasing curve explains why blue packets carry more energy, while the RGB bands show how spectra become channel counters.
Section 14.6

Radiance Is The Quantity A Renderer Carries

Flux And Power

Total energy per unit time. Useful for lamps, but too coarse for a ray.

Irradiance

Incident power per surface area. It answers how much light arrives at a patch.

Radiance

Power density per projected area and solid angle. In empty space it stays constant along a ray.

$$H = \int_{\Omega^+} L_f(k_i)\cos\theta_i\,d\sigma_i,\qquad H=\pi L \text{ for constant } L$$
Slow down on the distinction between totals and densities. Flux counts total power, irradiance measures power per surface area, and radiance adds direction and projected area. The invariance of radiance along a ray is the reason most renderers transport radiance rather than raw photon counts.
Sections 14.2 and 14.3.1

Fresnel Splits Energy At Smooth Interfaces

$$R(\theta)=R_0+(1-R_0)(1-\cos\theta)^5$$

Dielectrics

Normal reflectance comes from the refractive indices on the two sides of the interface.

Metals

Normal reflectance is often specified per wavelength or color channel, with absorption inside the surface.

Energy Split

Transmission receives what reflection does not, before any medium attenuation is applied.

Present Fresnel reflection as the first local energy split at an interface. Normal incidence is controlled by material indices or metal reflectance, while grazing angles climb toward full reflection. Schlick's formula is valuable because it preserves the main shape with a compact implementation.
Section 14.3.2

Refraction Bends Direction; Total Internal Reflection Removes It

Snell Constraint

The tangential component of wave direction is constrained by the two refractive indices.

$$n_i \sin\theta_i = n_t \sin\theta_t$$

Total Internal Reflection

When a dense-to-thin transition asks for \(\sin\theta_t > 1\), no transmitted ray exists.

Renderer Rule

Sample reflection or transmission according to Fresnel, then trace the selected outgoing direction.

Connect Snell's law to the geometry of a ray crossing a smooth interface. The normal component changes to keep the sine relationship correct, and the transmitted ray disappears when the requested sine would exceed one. That disappearance is total internal reflection, not a numerical special effect.
Absorption In Dielectrics

Beer Attenuation Is Path Length Made Visible

$$T(s)=a^s=\exp(-Cs),\qquad I(s)=I(0)T(s)$$

Medium Property

Absorption belongs to the distance traveled inside the material, not just to the entry or exit surface.

Debugging Check

For channel transmittance below one, transmitted intensity should decrease monotonically with distance.

Color Consequence

Different per-channel attenuation constants let transparent materials tint thick regions more strongly.

Beer attenuation is the medium rule that lives between interface events. Longer paths multiply transmittance again and again, which becomes an exponential decay per color channel. Emphasize that colored glass is not just surface color; the same material can look different at different thicknesses.
Section 14.7.1

A BRDF Measures Directional Scattering

Definition Role

A bidirectional reflectance distribution function maps incident direction and outgoing direction to reflected radiance per incident irradiance.

$$\rho(k_i,k_o)={dL_s(k_o)\over dH_i(k_i)}$$

Energy Bound

Directional hemispherical reflectance must stay at or below one for every incoming direction.

A BRDF is a measurement protocol for directional scattering at a surface point. Incident irradiance from one direction is compared with outgoing radiance in another. The key implementation check is that the hemispherical integral cannot reflect more energy than the surface receives.
Ideal Diffuse And Glossy Scattering

Lobe Shape Is Not The Same As Energy

$$R(k_i)=\int_{\Omega^+}\rho(k_i,k_o)\cos\theta_o\,d\sigma_o\leq 1$$

Lambertian Anchor

A perfectly diffuse reflector has constant \(\rho=1/\pi\) for unit reflectance.

Use the lobe comparison to separate shape from total energy. A glossy lobe can be tall and narrow without violating conservation if its integral is normalized. Lambertian reflection is the anchor case: constant BRDF value, view-independent radiance, and the required one over pi factor.
Section 14.8

The Rendering Equation Is A Hemisphere Sum

$$L_s(k_o)=\int_{\Omega^+}\rho(k_i,k_o)L_f(k_i)\cos\theta_i\,d\sigma_i$$

Term Audit

  • \(\rho\) decides how the material scatters each direction.
  • \(L_f\) is incoming radiance from the scene.
  • \(\cos\theta_i\) projects incoming direction onto the surface.

Failure Mode

Forgetting the cosine makes grazing light contribute as strongly as normal light.

This is the central synthesis slide. Surface radiance leaving in one direction is the sum of incident radiance from every visible hemisphere direction, weighted by BRDF and the incident cosine. Point to each term and name the implementation mistake it prevents.
Geometry Form Of Transport

Area Sampling Makes Visibility And Distance Explicit

$$G(x,y)=V(x,y){\cos\theta_x\cos\theta_y\over \|x-y\|^2}$$

Why Area Form Helps

Sampling a point on an emitter requires distance, two surface orientations, and a line-of-sight test.

Zero Contributions

A visible patch can still contribute zero if either cosine is negative or if geometry blocks the segment.

Implementation Habit

Keep the geometry factor and visibility as named values so direct-light bugs are inspectable.

The area form turns direction sampling into a relationship between two surface patches. Distance, the two cosines, and visibility become explicit, so many bugs become visible: back-facing lights, missing blockers, or forgetting the inverse-square factor when sampling an emitter by area.
Section 14.10

Monte Carlo Estimates The Integral By Sampling Directions

$$\widehat I_N={1\over N}\sum_{j=1}^N {f(X_j)\over p(X_j)}$$

Unbiased Accounting

The contribution is divided by the probability density that produced the sample.

Variance Control

Sampling directions shaped like the integrand makes the same expected value less noisy.

Common Error

Changing the sampler without changing the PDF changes the image, not just the noise.

Monte Carlo enters because the rendering equation is an integral too large to evaluate exactly. The estimator is simple but unforgiving: samples must be divided by the probability density that produced them. Better sampling does not change the answer; it reduces variance.
Recursive Ray Transport

Path Tracing Recursively Reuses The Same Estimator

rgb radiance(ray): p = first surface hit if no hit: return environment(ray) q = sample direction from material weight = rho(q, -ray) * cos(theta) / pdf(q) return emitted(p) + weight * radiance(p, q)

Throughput

The path carries a multiplicative weight that records BRDF, cosine, attenuation, and PDF factors.

Noise Story

One path is a rough estimate. Many independent paths reveal the expected radiance.

Path tracing is recursive Monte Carlo applied to radiance. A camera ray finds a surface, adds emitted or direct light, samples a new direction from the scattering rule, and continues with throughput updates. Noise decreases with more paths, while bias enters when the transport rule changes.
Section 14.9

Practical Materials Are Layered Scattering Decisions

Diffuse Body

Subsurface and rough surface effects often appear as a diffuse term with texture-controlled albedo.

Glossy Microstructure

Roughness and microfacet terms shape view-dependent highlights.

Specular Or Transmission

Mirror, glass, and clearcoat behavior are often selected or weighted by Fresnel.

shader = weighted sum of energy-aware terms
Materials in practiceLocal terms remain accountable to energy and sampling rules
Materials in practice combine several idealized scattering terms rather than using a single textbook BRDF. Fresnel often blends diffuse, glossy, mirror, and transmission behavior. The important habit is to keep weights, normal conventions, and energy budgets explicit as the shader becomes artistic.

Takeaways

Physics-Based Rendering Is A Chain Of Accountable Estimates

Carry Radiance

Radiance is stable along empty-space rays and becomes irradiance only after integration over projected directions.

Conserve Or Absorb

Fresnel, refraction, Beer attenuation, and BRDFs must split, redirect, or absorb energy deliberately.

Sample Honestly

Path tracing estimates the rendering equation by random paths weighted by the PDF that generated them.

Final Diagnostic

A renderer can look plausible while losing a cosine, misusing a PDF, or violating energy bounds. The chapter's discipline is to make those errors measurable.
