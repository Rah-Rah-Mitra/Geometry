Rendering Loop

Object-Order Rendering Changes The Loop Nest

Image-Order Question

For each pixel, which object and light paths determine its color?

Object-Order Question

For each primitive, which pixel samples can it influence?

Object-order rendering makes rasterization central.2 / 17
Contrast the two mental loops carefully. Image-order rendering starts at a pixel and asks which scene objects matter there. Object-order rendering starts at a primitive and asks which pixels it can influence. That reversal explains why streaming geometry once can be fast, but also why visibility, aliasing, and edge ownership become pipeline responsibilities.

Before Rasterization

Vertex Processing Prepares Screen-Space Work

Output Must Include

  • screen-space positions for primitive assembly
  • interpolatable attributes for later fragments
  • homogeneous terms when perspective correction is needed

Coordinate Warning

Shading angles require an orthonormal coordinate system; nonuniform scale and perspective projection do not preserve them.

Vertex work decides what the rasterizer can know.4 / 17
Emphasize that vertex processing is not merely multiplication by a matrix. It also decides which quantities are computed per vertex, which coordinate system is orthonormal enough for shading, and which homogeneous quantities must be preserved so the rasterizer can interpolate them correctly after projection.
Rasterization

Rasterization Emits Fragments At Pixel Centers

Rasterizer Jobs

  1. Enumerate sample locations covered by the primitive.
  2. Attach attributes to each fragment at that location.

Fragment

A candidate contribution to one framebuffer sample, carrying position, depth, coverage, and interpolated data.

Coverage is a sampling convention over a geometric primitive.5 / 17
Frame rasterization as two jobs performed together: enumerate covered sample locations and attach interpolated attributes to each resulting fragment. Pixel centers are a sampling convention, not a fact about geometry, so the class should see immediately why edge cases and antialiasing require additional policy.
Triangle Algebra

Barycentric Coordinates Are The Triangle Workhorse

Inside Test

alpha, beta, gamma ≥ 0 and alpha + beta + gamma = 1

Interpolation

The same weights blend color, depth, normals, texture coordinates, or any stage-approved attribute.

Convexity Check

If vertex colors are valid and weights are nonnegative, the interpolated color stays in range.

One algebraic object answers both coverage and attribute questions.6 / 17
Walk through the weights as a practical test and a practical interpolator. Nonnegative weights with sum one mean the sample is inside the triangle, and the same weights blend color, depth, texture coordinates after correction, or normals. This dual use is the reason triangles dominate pipeline design.
Exact Boundaries

Shared Edges Need Deterministic Ownership

Without A Rule

  • both triangles draw the same sample
  • neither triangle draws the boundary sample
  • color depends on draw order

With A Rule

A top-left style convention or equivalent tie policy makes a tessellated surface continuous in the sampled image.

Invariant

For every sample on a shared edge: exactly one owner.

Rasterizers are also policy engines for degeneracies.7 / 17
This slide addresses a subtle bug students often miss. Adjacent triangles should tile a surface without gaps or double writes along their common boundary. A deterministic ownership convention assigns exact edge samples to one side, so the image does not depend on accidental draw order or floating point tie behavior.
Attributes Under Projection

Perspective-Correct Interpolation Keeps Attributes Honest

The Trap

After perspective division, equal steps in screen space are not equal steps along the original 3D primitive.

The Repair

interpolate u/w and 1/w, then recover u = (u/w) / (1/w)

Pipeline Meaning

The vertex stage must pass compatible homogeneous attributes before the rasterizer can do the right interpolation.

Screen-linear is not automatically world-linear after division by w.8 / 17
Slow down on the algebraic idea without drowning the class in formulas. After perspective division, screen-linear interpolation is no longer world-linear. The repair is to interpolate homogeneous ratios such as u over w and one over w, then divide back at the fragment.
View Volume Safety

Clipping Keeps Invalid Geometry Out Of Rasterization

Why Clip?

Projection and rasterization can fail or create nonsense when a primitive crosses the eye plane or leaves the valid view region.

Invariant

Every primitive emitted to rasterization lies inside the accepted view domain, possibly after replacing it with a trimmed polygon.

Engineering Choice

Simple clipping exposes the idea; production clippers optimize the same geometric contract.

Clipping is both correctness and bounded work.9 / 17
Explain that clipping is a safety gate, not just an optimization. Primitives outside the view volume can be discarded, while primitives crossing a boundary must be trimmed so later division and rasterization operate on meaningful coordinates. The invariant is simple: every emitted vertex satisfies the view bounds.
After Rasterization

Fragment Processing And Blending Decide Pixel Winners

Fragment Processing

Compute or refine color, depth, coverage, masks, and material effects from interpolated data.

Blending / Depth Test

Compare the candidate fragment with current framebuffer state and decide whether color and depth are updated.

Local Pixel State

A pixel may receive many fragments; the framebuffer stores the resolved sample state.

Fragments are candidates; framebuffer samples are resolved state.10 / 17
Connect this slide to the data contracts from the beginning. The rasterizer can create several fragments for the same pixel, but the framebuffer stores one resolved result per sample. Fragment processing supplies color and depth, while blending or depth testing determines whether the candidate becomes visible state.
Hidden Surfaces

The Z-Buffer Localizes Hidden-Surface Decisions

Algorithm

if fragment.z < zbuffer[pixel]: color[pixel] = fragment.color zbuffer[pixel] = fragment.z else: discard fragment

Why It Works

The ordering problem is solved only where samples exist, avoiding a global sort of all primitives.

Ties Still Need Policy

Equal depths can reveal draw-order decisions, precision limits, or deliberate blending behavior.

The z-buffer is a per-pixel memory for visibility.11 / 17
Present the z-buffer as a deliberately local alternative to sorting geometry. It only solves the visibility question at pixels that are actually sampled, which is why it is so practical. The important teaching point is that the final visible color can be independent of primitive order when depths are well defined.
Precision

Depth Precision Is A Near-Far Design Choice

Integer Depth Bins

A finite z-buffer maps continuous depth into discrete values, so near and far plane choices allocate precision.

Perspective Effect

larger world depth means coarser world-space separation per bin

Rule Of Thumb

Place the near plane as far out and the far plane as close in as the scene allows.

Depth precision is part of camera design, not a fixed constant.12 / 17
Use the curve to make the near plane feel consequential. Perspective depth spends much more resolution near the eye than far away, so moving the near plane too close can make distant bins very coarse. Students should leave with the engineering rule: maximize near, minimize far, within scene requirements.
Shading Frequency

Per-Vertex And Per-Fragment Shading Trade Cost For Detail

Per-Vertex Shading

Cheap, but small lighting features can disappear inside large primitives.

Per-Fragment Shading

More work, but lighting is sampled where the final image samples lie.

Choose shading frequency to match the scale of visible detail.13 / 17
Keep the comparison tied to sampling frequency. Per-vertex shading is cheap when illumination changes slowly or geometry is dense enough, but it can miss a small highlight inside a large triangle. Per-fragment shading evaluates the lighting model where fragments actually land, at higher cost.
Texture Coordinates

Texture Mapping Adds An Image-Valued Attribute Source

Attribute Contract

Each vertex carries texture coordinates that are interpolated across the primitive, usually with perspective correction.

Lookup Contract

The fragment stage uses those coordinates to query an image or procedural domain for material detail.

Preview

Once a lookup is image-valued, filtering becomes essential when one pixel covers many texels or one texel covers many pixels.

Texture coordinates are just attributes until a fragment shader uses them.14 / 17
Describe texture coordinates as another attribute contract. The primitive carries coordinates that identify where it lives in a texture domain, and the fragment shader performs a lookup to obtain material detail. This also previews why filtering and mipmaps matter in the later texture chapter.
Antialiasing

Antialiasing Separates Coverage Samples From Shading Samples

Aliased Rasterization

Each pixel is inside or outside according to one sample, so diagonal edges stair-step.

Supersampling

Estimate partial coverage by averaging many samples in the pixel area.

Multisampling

Store several coverage and depth samples while often reusing one shaded color.

Visibility can be sampled more often than shading.15 / 17
Use the split pixel diagram to distinguish visibility from color. Standard rasterization makes a binary decision at one sample location, which creates jagged edges. Supersampling estimates coverage by many samples, while multisampling reuses a color calculation but stores several coverage and depth decisions.
Efficiency

Culling Saves Work With Conservative Tests

View-Volume Culling

Backface Culling

Culling is correct only when the rejection test is conservative enough.16 / 17
Make the performance logic explicit. Culling is valuable only when a cheap test discards work that cannot affect the framebuffer. View-volume culling can reject a whole bounded object conservatively, while backface culling relies on closed, consistently oriented surfaces whose rear-facing triangles are guaranteed to be hidden.
Debugging Checklist

Pipeline Debugging Means Checking Invariants

Geometry

Coordinates are transformed into the expected space before rasterization.

Coverage

Pixel centers, edge ties, and clipping rules produce stable fragments.

Attributes

Barycentric interpolation uses consistent quantities, especially under perspective.

Visibility

Depth tests choose the nearer fragment with enough precision for the scene.

Sampling

Shading and coverage rates match the scale of image detail.

Efficiency

Culling discards only work that cannot change the framebuffer.

Stable rendering comes from preserving the right invariant at each stage.17 / 17
Close by turning the chapter into a debugging checklist. Students should be able to name the invariant at each stage: valid coordinates, single edge ownership, corrected interpolation, clipped primitives, depth winners, adequate sampling, and conservative culling. Those invariants are what make a renderer predictable.
