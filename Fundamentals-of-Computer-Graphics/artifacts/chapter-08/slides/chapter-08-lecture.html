Composition order
Rightmost map acts first

A Matrix Product Is A Contract

Orthographic viewing

p_screen = M_vp M_orth M_cam p_world

Perspective viewing

p_screen = M_vp M_orth P M_cam p_world

  • M_cam expects world coordinates and emits camera coordinates.
  • P expects camera coordinates and changes homogeneous w.
  • M_orth expects a box and emits the canonical cube.
  • M_vp expects canonical x,y and emits pixel-center x,y.
Emphasize that matrix order is not notation decoration. The rightmost matrix consumes the original world point, and each later matrix assumes the previous coordinate system has already been produced. Ask students to name the coordinate space before and after every multiplication in the product.
Viewport transform
Canonical square to pixel centers

Viewport As A Pixel Window Map

Window-map formula

x_p = (n_x / 2) x_c + (n_x - 1) / 2

y_p = (n_y / 2) y_c + (n_y - 1) / 2

  • Canonical coordinates remain continuous until sampling decisions happen.
  • Depth can be carried unchanged for hidden-surface work.
  • The endpoints describe image boundaries, not only stored samples.
Explain the pixel-center convention before writing the matrix. The canonical square is continuous, but a raster image stores samples at pixel centers. Mapping -1 to -0.5 and 1 to nx minus 0.5 makes the visible image boundary align with the outer edges of the first and last pixels.
Orthographic projection
A 3D window map

Orthographic Box To Canonical Cube

  • Input view box: [l,r] x [b,t] x [f,n].
  • Output canonical cube: [-1,1] x [-1,1] x [-1,1].
  • Right-handed camera convention looks along negative z.

Corner checks

(l,b,f) maps to (-1,-1,-1)

(r,t,n) maps to (1,1,1)

Frame orthographic projection as the same windowing idea in three coordinates. Nothing about perspective is needed yet; the matrix merely recenters and rescales an axis-aligned view box. Stress the sign convention: in the right-handed camera setup, farther points usually have more negative z values.
Camera frame
eye, gaze, and up become u,v,w

Camera Basis From Eye, Gaze, And Up

Basis construction

w = -g / ||g||

u = normalize(t x w)

v = w x u

Degenerate input

If the view-up vector is parallel to gaze, the cross product cannot supply a sideways axis.

This is the most common source of viewing bugs. The inputs eye, gaze, and up do not automatically form an orthonormal frame. The construction turns gaze into the backward camera axis, removes the up-vector skew through a cross product, and then rebuilds a consistent right-handed basis.
Camera transformation
Rigid basis change

The Camera Matrix Is Translate Then Rotate

p_cam = [u^T; v^T; w^T] (p_world - e)

1Translate the world so the eye position becomes the origin.
2Measure coordinates against u, v, and w through dot products.
3Verify the eye maps to zero and the basis determinant stays positive.
Separate the two jobs of the camera matrix. Translation moves the eye to the origin, while the basis rows measure coordinates along the camera axes. The eye must map to zero, and the basis rows must remain orthonormal; those two checks catch many sign and ordering errors.
Projective coordinates
Let w carry a denominator

Homogeneous Coordinates Add A Denominator

Scale equivalence

[x, y, z, w] ~ [lambda x, lambda y, lambda z, lambda w]

Read Cartesian coordinates after the product

(x / w, y / w, z / w)

Projective coordinates are the conceptual turn of the chapter. Affine transforms keep w equal to one, but viewing benefits from letting w change with depth. Only after the matrix product is finished do we divide by w to recover Cartesian coordinates for clipping and rasterization.
Projective behavior
Straightness survives

Projective Maps Keep Lines Straight

  • A line maps to a line when w does not cross zero on the segment.
  • Parallel edges may meet after projection.
  • Midpoints and equal spacing are not generally preserved.

Do not interpolate too late

Perspective-correct interpolation exists because screen-space linear fractions are not the original 3D fractions.

Use the square-to-quadrilateral visual to separate what survives from what does not. Incidence and straightness survive under a projective map, while parallelism and midpoint ratios can change. This distinction prepares students for perspective images, where railroad tracks may meet but edges still draw as lines.
Perspective volume
Frustum to box

Perspective Starts With A Frustum

Perspective step P

Reshape the pyramid slice into an orthographic view box before applying the usual cube normalization.

  • Near-plane x and y are left unchanged after homogeneous division.
  • Far-plane x and y are compressed to fit the near window.
  • The eye plane is dangerous because w can vanish there.
The frustum diagram should be read from the eye outward. Points on the near plane keep their x and y positions after the perspective step, while the larger far rectangle is squeezed to match the orthographic box. That makes the later orthographic machinery reusable instead of special-casing perspective everywhere.
Perspective product
Reuse orthographic infrastructure

Insert P Before The Orthographic Map

M_per = M_orth P

p_screen = M_vp M_per M_cam p_world

Implementation benefit

Orthographic and perspective views can share clipping, canonical-space diagnostics, and the viewport stage after the projection matrix is chosen.

Keep the matrix ordering visible here. The perspective matrix P operates in camera coordinates, before the orthographic normalization and before the viewport. Once P has reshaped the frustum into a box, the same canonical-cube map and screen-window map can serve both projection styles.
Depth mapping
Nonlinear, but monotone after clipping

Depth Becomes Nonlinear But Ordered

Depth transform shape

z_new = n + f - (f n) / z

  • Depth order is preserved for points inside the frustum.
  • Spacing is compressed unevenly, which matters for depth buffers.
  • Segments crossing the eye plane must be clipped before division.
Students often expect depth to be linear because x and y are easy to picture. The useful invariant is weaker and more important: inside a clipped view volume, nearer and farther points keep their order after the transform. The depth curve explains why z precision becomes concentrated near the eye.
Perspective property
Line preservation without affine parameters

Lines Survive, Parameters Do Not

Invariant

Projected samples remain collinear when the whole segment stays in one valid projective chart.

Non-invariant

Equal steps in 3D do not become equal steps after homogeneous division.

The line preservation claim is exact, but the sampling parameter is not preserved. A point halfway along a 3D segment can project away from the midpoint between projected endpoints. This is the reason interpolation in later pipeline stages must respect homogeneous coordinates rather than naive screen-space fractions.
Field of view
Angle and aspect choose l,r,b,t

Field Of View Chooses The Near Window

Centered vertical FOV

t = |n| tan(theta / 2)

r = (n_x / n_y) t

  • Large angles widen the frustum quickly through the tangent.
  • Aspect ratio sets width after the vertical choice is made.
  • Distortion at wide FOV is a geometric consequence, not a matrix bug.
Field of view is not an extra projection method. It is a compact way to compute the near-plane window before constructing the general perspective map. Increasing the vertical angle increases the window height through a tangent, and the aspect ratio expands width without changing the chosen vertical angle.
Debugging signatures
Matrix bugs leave geometry clues

Viewing Bugs Have Signatures

Mirrored scene

Check the cross-product order and the determinant of the camera basis.

Shifted pixels

Check pixel-center offsets and whether the viewport uses nx or nx minus one.

Objects vanish

Check near and far signs, then clip before any divide by w.

Use this slide as a diagnostic lab. A mirrored image points to a handedness or cross-product issue, a shifted image points to viewport or window bounds, clipped near objects suggest frustum signs, and w crossing zero warns that geometry needs clipping before the homogeneous divide.
Operational recipe
Wireframe viewing before rasterization

A Rendering Recipe For Wireframe Viewing

1Build camera axes from eye, gaze, and up; reject degenerate input.
2Choose orthographic bounds or compute a perspective near window from FOV.
3Compose the matrix product once for the whole batch of line endpoints.
4Transform endpoints, divide by w when using projective coordinates, and clip invalid pieces.
5Send pixel-center coordinates to the line drawing stage.

Implementation principle

Compute the composite map once, but keep the factorization visible in tests and debug tools.

Walk through the recipe as executable thinking rather than API ceremony. Build camera axes, compose the projection and viewport matrices once, transform every endpoint, divide when required, reject or clip invalid segments, and finally draw in pixel coordinates. The order is what makes the implementation testable.

Invariant Ledger

1Viewport endpoints match the pixel-center convention.
2Orthographic corners map to canonical corners.
3Camera rows are orthonormal and map the eye to zero.
4Homogeneous scale changes do not change represented points.
5Perspective preserves depth order and straight lines inside the valid chart.
6FOV and aspect formulas produce the intended near-plane window.
