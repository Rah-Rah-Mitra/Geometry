Fundamentals of Computer Graphics

Chapter 16: Computer Animation

Controlling change over time with keys, timing, rotations, deformation, character rigs, simulation, procedures, and group behavior.

Source span: printed pages 429-460; PDF pages 446-477.

Lecture Question

When an animation looks wrong, what should be measured first: path, timing, tangent, orientation, constraint residual, energy, or control priority?

Notebook Lens

Each method becomes a small diagnostic: interpolation error, speed continuity, quaternion norm, skinning weights, energy drift, or acceleration budget.

Open by defining animation as controlled change over time. The animator gives sparse intent through keys, constraints, procedures, forces, or captured samples, while the system fills in frames. Keep the lecture focused on diagnostics: when motion looks wrong, ask which invariant was violated.
Chapter Map

Four Ways To Supply Motion Intent

Keyframing

Sparse times and values are fitted by curves. Control is direct, but tangents and timing can create hidden artifacts.

Procedures

A compact generator uses parameters, rules, or noise to create rich motion without keying every value.

Physics

Forces and equations fill in motion. The price is stability, collision handling, and less direct artistic control.

Capture

Real movement is sampled and retargeted. Cleanup, labeling, and constraints turn data into usable animation.

Use the four-lane map to orient the chapter. Keyframing gives direct artistic control, procedural methods expose compact parameter sets, physics produces motion from equations, and capture imports real movement. None is automatically best; the useful question is what the animator can still inspect and adjust.
Section 16.1

Principles Are Readability Tests

Timing

Speed communicates weight, intent, emotional state, and whether the viewer has enough time to notice the action.

Action Layout

Staging, anticipation, and follow-through tell the eye what matters before, during, and after the main action.

Squash And Stretch

Deform along motion or impact while preserving apparent volume, unless size change is itself the story.

Connect traditional animation principles to measurable computer graphics choices. Timing sets weight and intent, staging makes the important action readable, anticipation and follow-through help viewers parse cause and effect, and squash/stretch must preserve apparent volume unless growth is the intended signal.
Section 16.2

Keys Are Samples; Curves Are Commitments

Interpolation Contract

For every key \((t_k, f_k)\), the curve must satisfy \(f(t_k)=f_k\). That does not guarantee readable motion.

Derivative Contract

The perceived parameter speed is \(\frac{df}{dt}\). Kinks, overshoot, or sudden sign changes often explain visual artifacts.

diagnose keys by checking value error, tangent continuity, and speed range
Treat keyframes as sparse data samples, not finished images. Once values are set at selected times, the interpolation curve commits to tangents, speeds, overshoot, and continuity. The graph should make students look at derivative behavior, because a path can pass through every key and still move poorly.
Catmull-Rom And TCB Control

Local Tangents Are An Animator Interface

Tension \(T\)

Scales tangents down or up, trading smooth passage through keys against tighter, less energetic segments.

Continuity \(C\)

Allows incoming and outgoing tangents to differ, which can make a corner intentional rather than accidental.

Bias \(B\)

Pulls a segment toward the previous or next neighbor, shifting how the curve approaches a key.

TCB = edit \(m_k^-\) and \(m_k^+\), then evaluate cubic Hermite segments
Frame TCB splines as an editor vocabulary for local tangent control. Tension shortens motion and reduces overshoot, continuity lets incoming and outgoing behavior differ, and bias favors one neighboring segment. The important teaching point is that curve editing is motion editing, not just shape editing.
Motion Controls

Path Shape Is Not Timing

Path

\(P(u)\) gives positions along a geometric curve. Equal parameter steps usually do not mean equal distances.

Arc Length

An arc-length table converts distance targets into curve parameters, making constant-speed sampling possible.

Timing

Slow-in and slow-out come from composing the path with \(s(t)\), not from redrawing the path.

Separate the geometric path from the clock. A curve P(u) describes where an object can travel, while a timing law s(t) decides how far along the path each frame should be. Arc-length reparameterization and easing are practical tools for decoupling shape from perceived speed.
Interpolating Orientation

Stay On The Rotation Group

Matrix Lerp Fails

Elementwise interpolation can leave the set of valid rotations: columns are not orthonormal and determinant can drift away from one.

Euler Angles Are Fragile

They are convenient keys, but axis order matters and gimbal lock can collapse one degree of freedom.

Unit Quaternions

Represent orientation on a sphere in four dimensions; normalize or slerp so each sampled frame is still a valid rotation.

check \(R^T R \approx I\), \(\det R \approx 1\), and \(\lVert q\rVert = 1\)
Rotation interpolation is the first place where ordinary linear interpolation becomes actively misleading. Rotation matrices must remain orthonormal with determinant one, and Euler angles can lose a degree of freedom. Unit quaternions keep orientation on a sphere, so interpolation can be projected back to valid rotations.
Section 16.3

Deformation Animates Parameters, Not Only Vertices

Global Deformations

Bend, twist, and taper are compact controls, but the whole object usually participates.

Local Influence

Move a seed vertex, weight nearby surface points, or use a lattice to concentrate change where the animator needs it.

Animation Handle

Keyframe \(\gamma(t)\), the deformation parameter vector, instead of storing a new mesh at every frame.

Deformation introduces a different animation target: parameters of a shape-changing function. Bend, twist, taper, and FFD lattices can be keyframed without keying every vertex. The tradeoff is control locality: global functions are compact, while lattices and surface weights give more localized influence.
Section 16.4

Characters Split Visible Shape From Control

Skin

The rendered surface carries detail, expression, silhouette, and material response.

Skeleton

A joint tree stores a lower-dimensional kinematic abstraction that is easier to pose and constrain.

FK vs IK

Forward kinematics sets joint angles; inverse kinematics sets an end target and solves backward for internal joints.

Introduce character animation as a two-layer model. The visible skin is detailed, but the animator usually controls a simpler joint hierarchy underneath. Forward kinematics composes joint transforms from root to leaves; inverse kinematics asks for end-effector motion and solves for internal joint changes.
Kinematic Diagnostics

IK And Skinning Are Numeric Contracts

IK Residual

The end effector should approach the target without forcing impossible limb stretch or implausible flipped poses.

Jacobian Check

Finite differences should agree with the analytic or geometric derivative before trusting an iterative solver.

Skinning Check

Blend weights should sum to one and vary smoothly near joints to avoid cracks, collapsing volume, or rigid kinks.

Make the numeric contract explicit. IK uses a Jacobian to relate small joint changes to small end-effector changes, often with more unknowns than constraints. Skinning then blends nearby bone transforms, so the checks are residual error, plausible pose selection, and weights that sum correctly.
Faces And Capture

Recorded Motion Still Needs A Rig

Facial Controls

Blend shapes, muscle-like controls, or parameterized expressions convert sparse sliders into many surface displacements.

Capture Data

Markers or image features provide time-varying positions, but they may be noisy, occluded, mislabeled, or attached to a different body.

Retargeting

Map sampled motion onto the character skeleton, then enforce feet, hands, contacts, and joint limits with constraints.

This slide keeps facial animation and motion capture from sounding automatic. A captured marker cloud must be labeled, filtered, retargeted, and constrained before it drives a character. Facial rigs similarly translate sparse controls into expression geometry, so cleanup and semantic mapping are part of animation.
Section 16.5

Physics Gives Motion, But Also Stability Tests

Equation

For particles, cloth, rigid bodies, or elastic shapes, forces define acceleration and the integrator advances state.

Cost

Collision detection and force evaluation can dominate the frame budget, especially for cloth or dense contact.

Diagnostic

Watch energy growth, constraint violation, penetration depth, and whether the time step changes the story.

Physics-based animation is attractive because equations produce secondary motion that would be tedious to key. It is also dangerous because time stepping, collisions, and force evaluation can become unstable or expensive. Emphasize that visual realism depends on numerical behavior, not only on using physical vocabulary.
Section 16.6

Procedural Motion Is A Parameterized Generator

Waves And Noise

Animate functions like \(A\cos(\omega t-kx+\phi)\) or move through a noise lattice over time.

Rules

Cellular automata and L-systems evolve by repeatedly applying local or grammar-like update rules.

Control Problem

The output can be rich and cheap, but good parameters often come from tuning, experience, and visible diagnostics.

Procedural animation should feel like a compact generator with exposed controls. Waves, animated noise, cellular automata, and L-systems can create rich motion cheaply, but their parameters are often empirical. The animator gains scale and repeatability while accepting a tuning problem.
Section 16.7

Group Motion Negotiates Urges, Not Positions

Highest Priority

Collision avoidance and basic safety receive acceleration before aesthetic or long-term goals.

Local Rules

Neighbors inside a limited field of view drive velocity matching and flock centering.

Final Pose

The negotiated velocity still has to become wing beats, leg poses, steering angle, or body orientation.

For group motion, show that each agent does not need a global plan. A boid negotiates local urges such as collision avoidance, velocity matching, flock centering, and goal seeking. Priorities matter because simple averaging can cancel intentions and leave the agent motionless.
Particle Systems

Many Short-Lived Objects Can Read As One Phenomenon

Birth And Death

Emit particles with randomized lifetime, position, velocity, size, and color, then remove them when their role is finished.

Fields

Wind, attraction, collision, and gravity are easier controls than hand-keying each particle path.

Use Case

Best for fuzzy phenomena such as spray, sparks, smoke, dust, debris, and other effects without sharp boundaries.

Particles are a deliberately simpler group model. The system creates and destroys many short-lived independent objects, then moves them through force fields, collisions, and random variation. This makes fire, spray, smoke, and debris controllable, but it is usually an appearance model rather than full fluid physics.
