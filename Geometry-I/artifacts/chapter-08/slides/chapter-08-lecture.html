Definition Layer Printed 151-199

Euclidean Structure Is A Positive Pairing

Definition 8.1 - Euclidean Vector Space

A finite-dimensional real vector space \(E\) becomes Euclidean when it carries a symmetric bilinear form satisfying \( \langle v,v\rangle > 0 \) for every nonzero \(v\).

$$\|v\|^2=\langle v,v\rangle,\qquad d(u,v)=\|u-v\|.$$

Coordinate Shadow

In a basis, the inner product appears as a symmetric positive matrix \(G\):

$$\langle u,v\rangle = u^{T}Gv.$$

Changing basis changes \(G\), not the geometric pairing itself.

Inspection Target

Lengths, angles, and volumes should be read as quantities produced by the pairing, not by the drawing alone.

Gram Volume

The squared \(k\)-volume of \(v_1,\ldots,v_k\) is \( \det(\langle v_i,v_j\rangle) \), so metric data controls higher-dimensional size.

Inner product -> norm -> distance -> volume 3 / 13
Emphasize that the inner product is not an extra formula pasted onto a vector space; it is the device that lets the space answer metric questions. The Gram matrix is the coordinate shadow of that device. If the basis changes, the matrix changes, but the bilinear pairing and its positivity do not.

The Orthogonal Group Preserves The Pairing

Notebook artifact: orthogonal rotations preserve length.
Orthogonality Test

A linear map \(A:E\to E\) is orthogonal when

$$\langle Au,Av\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle.$$

With Gram matrix \(G\), this becomes \(A^{T}GA=G\).

Immediate Invariants
  • \(\|Av\|=\|v\|\)
  • orthogonality of vectors is preserved
  • Gram determinants, hence Euclidean volumes, are preserved up to orientation sign
\(O(E)=\{A:A^{T}GA=G\}\) 4 / 13
This slide converts the definition into a test for maps. In coordinates with Gram matrix G, orthogonality is the matrix equation A transpose G A equals G. Ask learners to inspect the visual rotation artifact first, then read the equation as the reason lengths and inner products survive the motion.
Angles And Orientation Euclidean Vector Spaces

Angles Are Normalized Inner Products

Unsigned Angle

For nonzero vectors \(u,v\), the Euclidean angle is determined by

$$\cos \theta=\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|u\|\,\|v\|}.$$

Cauchy-Schwarz is the existence theorem behind this formula.

Oriented Plane Angle

In an oriented Euclidean plane, the pair \( (\langle u,v\rangle,\det(u,v)) \) records both cosine and sine data. That pair distinguishes clockwise from counterclockwise rotation.

Angle = normalized metric data + orientation when needed 5 / 13
The angle formula is where metric and linear structure visibly meet. Cauchy-Schwarz makes the quotient lie between minus one and one, so arccos is legal. In an oriented plane, the determinant supplies the sign information that arccos alone forgets, which is why orientation has to be named separately.

A Rotation Changes Coordinates But Not Length

Concrete Vector

Take \(v=(1,\tfrac14)\), so \( \|v\|^2=1+\tfrac1{16}=\tfrac{17}{16}\).

$$R_{\theta}= \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then \(R_\theta^{T}R_\theta=I\), hence \( \|R_\theta v\|^2=\|v\|^2\).

Similarity Geometry Scale + Orthogonality

Similarities Preserve Angle, Not Size

Definition 8.2 - Linear Similarity

A linear map \(S\) is a similarity with ratio \(\lambda>0\) when

$$\langle Su,Sv\rangle=\lambda^2\langle u,v\rangle.$$

Equivalently, \(S=\lambda Q\) with \(Q\in O(E)\).

Orientation Signal

\(\det Q=1\) gives a direct similarity; \(\det Q=-1\) gives an inverse similarity.

Similarity = uniform scale times orthogonal motion 7 / 13
A similarity is allowed to change scale, but only uniformly. That is why it preserves angles while multiplying every length by the same positive factor. The determinant distinguishes direct similarities from inverse ones, giving students a clean way to connect orientation with the algebraic sign of a transformation.
Rotations In Three Dimensions Quaternions

Unit Quaternions Package Rotations

Rotation Formula

For a unit imaginary axis \(a\) and angle \(\phi\), set

$$q=\cos(\phi/2)+a\sin(\phi/2).$$

Then imaginary vectors rotate by \(x\mapsto qxq^{-1}\).

Why It Fits Euclidean Geometry

Conjugation by a unit quaternion preserves the Euclidean norm and composes rotations by multiplication.

Multiplication model for rotations 8 / 13
Treat quaternions as a structural encoding of rotations rather than as a computational trick. The unit quaternion carries an axis and a half-angle, and conjugation rotates imaginary vectors without leaving the three-dimensional subspace. The point is not to compute many products, but to see why SO three has a compact multiplicative model.
Orthogonal Group Anatomy Components And Generators

Determinant Separates The Two Kinds Of Orthogonal Motion

Special Orthogonal Group

\(SO(E)=\{Q\in O(E):\det Q=1\}\) preserves both metric data and orientation.

Plane rotations and quaternion rotations live here.

Other Component

Orthogonal maps with determinant \(-1\) preserve lengths and angles but reverse orientation.

Reflections are the simplest examples.

Lecture Check

An orthogonal transformation can preserve every length and angle while changing handedness.

That is why the metric group \(O(E)\) and the oriented metric group \(SO(E)\) are not the same object.

Metric preservation does not automatically preserve orientation 9 / 13
This is the group-theoretic pause. Orthogonal maps split according to determinant, with SO E containing orientation-preserving motions and the other component containing orientation-reversing ones. Reflections are useful generators because they make the preserved hyperplane and flipped normal visible, turning abstract matrices into geometric operations.

Zero Length Is A Diagnostic For The Form

Notebook artifact: isotropic cone quadratic condition.
Euclidean Baseline

For a positive-definite inner product, \( \langle v,v\rangle=0 \) implies \(v=0\).

Indefinite Contrast

A quadratic form such as \(Q(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2-z^2\) has many nonzero vectors with \(Q=0\), forming a cone.

Why It Belongs Here

Common Pitfalls Check The Hypotheses

Most Errors Come From Preserving The Wrong Thing

Coordinate Change

A new basis is not automatically an orthogonal basis. Check \(P^{T}GP\), not just invertibility of \(P\).

Zero Vectors

The angle formula divides by norms, so it only applies to nonzero vectors.

Orientation Loss

The cosine of an angle does not remember clockwise versus counterclockwise direction.

Repair Strategy

Before using a theorem, identify the exact preserved object: inner product, norm, angle, orientation, scale ratio, or quadratic form.

Instructor Prompt

When a transformation is presented as geometric, ask which equation certifies it.

This turns visual intuition into a proof obligation.

Invariant first, computation second 11 / 13
Use this slide to prevent three common errors before they harden. A coordinate change is not automatically an isometry, an angle formula requires nonzero vectors, and orientation data can disappear if one looks only at absolute cosines. Each warning is really a reminder to check the hypothesis behind the invariant.
Board Exercise Proof + Computation + Classification

Three Fast Checks For Euclidean Structure

A. Orthogonal Test

Let \(G=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\). Find a nontrivial matrix \(A\) satisfying \(A^{T}GA=G\).

B. Oriented Angle

For \(u=(1,0)\) and \(v=(1,1)\), compute both \( \langle u,v\rangle \) and \( \det(u,v) \). What orientation does the pair record?

C. Similarity

Classify \(S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-3\\3&0\end{pmatrix}\): orthogonal, similarity, direct, or inverse?

Expected Methods
  1. Write the preservation equation.
  2. Use the inner product and determinant together.
  3. Separate scale from orthogonal motion.

Chapter 8 Recap Euclidean Vector Spaces

What The Inner Product Adds

Core Chain

$$\langle u,v\rangle \Rightarrow \|v\|,\ \theta,\ \mathrm{vol} \Rightarrow O(E),\ SO(E),\ \mathrm{Sim}(E).$$

  • Metric structure makes length, angle, and orthogonality meaningful.
  • Orthogonal groups are the linear symmetries of that metric.
  • Similarities preserve angle after allowing one uniform scale factor.
  • Quaternions and cones show how metric ideas reorganize higher-dimensional geometry.
