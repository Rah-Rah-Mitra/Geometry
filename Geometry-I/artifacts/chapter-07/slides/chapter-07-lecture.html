Complexification Adds Scalars Without Rebuilding the Geometry

A real vector, affine, or projective object gains complex coordinates; the lecture tracks which structures extend canonically and which points are genuinely new.

Scalars

Replace real coefficients by complex ones: $$V_{\mathbb C}=V\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C.$$

Structure

Linear maps, subspaces, and polynomial equations extend by the same formulas.

Inspection

Zeros, directions, and projective points can appear outside the original real locus.

Open by framing complexification as a disciplined change of scalars, not as a license to forget the real problem. The chapter asks what a real object gains when complex coefficients are allowed. Emphasize that the same formulas continue to operate, but the set of allowable points, roots, and projective directions becomes larger and often easier to organize.

Vector Space Complexification

Definition
$$V_{\mathbb C}=V\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C\cong V\oplus iV$$
$$v+iw \quad (v,w\in V),\qquad i(v+iw)=-w+iv$$
Real locus

The original space sits inside as \(V\cong V\otimes 1\). Complexification adds independent imaginary directions instead of moving the old vectors.

Source orientation: complexification of a real vector space.

Define the construction in the tensor-product form first, then immediately translate it into the concrete pair notation V plus iV. The point of the pair model is that students can see what i does: it swaps real and imaginary components with a sign. Stress that the real space is still present as the real slice, while the complexified space has twice the real dimension.

Functoriality Extends Maps Once

Maps
$$T_{\mathbb C}(v+iw)=T(v)+iT(w)$$
$$\operatorname{id}_{V,\mathbb C}=\operatorname{id}_{V_{\mathbb C}},\qquad (S\circ T)_{\mathbb C}=S_{\mathbb C}\circ T_{\mathbb C}$$
Why this matters

Once a real construction is written as a linear map, its complex companion is forced. There is no extra choice to make.

Source orientation: functoriality and complexified morphisms.

Present functoriality as the safety rail for the whole chapter. If students complexify objects but forget how maps behave, the construction becomes ad hoc. The displayed rule says that the complexified map is determined componentwise. The identities for composition and identity maps mean that diagrams and algebraic dependencies from the real setting survive after changing scalars.

Subspaces and Linear Equations Complexify Together

Subspaces
$$U_{\mathbb C}=U\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C=\{u_1+iu_2:u_1,u_2\in U\}$$
Equation test

If \(U=\{x:Ax=0\}\) over \(\mathbb R\), then \(U_{\mathbb C}=\{z:Az=0\}\) over \(\mathbb C\).

Common mistake

Do not complexify only the coordinates and forget the constraints. The equations must also be read over the new scalar field.

Source orientation: subspaces and complexifications.

This slide is a good place to slow down and connect the abstract tensor product to computations. A vector lies in the complexified subspace exactly when both its real and imaginary parts satisfy the original subspace condition. If the subspace was defined by a matrix equation, the same matrix equation applies to complex vectors because matrix multiplication is polynomial in the entries.

Polynomials Gain Complex Zeros

Equations
Complexified polynomial

A real-coefficient polynomial is read with complex inputs and the same coefficients. The equation can acquire solutions not visible over the real line.

$$p(x)\in\mathbb R[x]\quad\leadsto\quad p(z)\in\mathbb C[z]$$

Real Directions Acquire Imaginary Companions

Visual model
What to inspect

The black axis remains the real locus. The colored curves encode different imaginary scales, making visible that complexification adds directions transverse to the original real line.

Interpretation

A point \(x+iy\) should be read as a real part together with an independent imaginary component, not as a point that abandoned its real origin.

Projection caution

A two-dimensional drawing can display only a slice of the complexified object. The algebra tells which parts of the picture are structural.

Affine Complexification Keeps Difference Vectors

Affine
Affine version

An affine space modeled on \(V\) complexifies to an affine space modeled on \(V_{\mathbb C}\). Differences of points are complexified; points still require an origin or chart to be written as coordinates.

$$f(p+v)=q+T(v)\quad\leadsto\quad f_{\mathbb C}(p+\zeta)=q+T_{\mathbb C}(\zeta)$$
Affine weights

Barycentric formulas continue to make sense when coefficients are complex, provided the weights still sum to 1.

Source orientation: affine complexification and coordinate discipline.

Move from vector spaces to affine spaces by emphasizing differences. Affine geometry does not have a distinguished zero vector, so one should not pretend that points are automatically vectors. Once a base point or chart is chosen, difference vectors live in the model vector space and can be complexified. Affine maps then extend because their linear parts extend functorially.

Projective Complexification Adds Lines Through the Complex Origin

Projective
$$\mathbb P(V_{\mathbb C})=\{ \text{complex lines in } V_{\mathbb C}\}$$
Real locus

A real projective point is a real line; after complexification it sits among all complex lines. Incidence relations are still expressed by linear dependence and equations.

Equivalence changes

Projective coordinates are identified by nonzero complex scaling, not only by nonzero real scaling.

Source orientation: affine and projective complexification.

Projective complexification is often where the change of scalars becomes geometrically vivid. The real projective line is not discarded; it becomes a real locus inside the complex projective object. The equivalence relation is broader because complex nonzero scalars are allowed. This is why a projective statement should be phrased using homogeneous equations, incidence, and ratios rather than a favorite coordinate representative.

Coordinates Are Auxiliary, Equations Carry the Object

Pitfall
Pitfall

Complex coordinates do not automatically identify the geometric object. A coordinate tuple is a representative, while the point or subspace is defined by its transformation rule and equations.

Good test

After changing charts or representatives, check formulas that are invariant: linear dependence, vanishing of homogeneous polynomials, incidence, and rank.

SettingRepresentativeInvariant reading
Vector\(v+iw\)real and imaginary parts transform by the same real-linear rule
Subspace\(Az=0\)solution space of the complexified equations
Affine\(p+\zeta\)differences live in \(V_{\mathbb C}\)
Projective\([z_0:\cdots:z_n]\)unchanged under nonzero complex scaling

Notebook emphasis: coordinates are not points, drawings are not proofs, and checks must name the invariant.

Use this slide as a conceptual guardrail before the final certificate. The chapter repeatedly warns against confusing a coordinate display with the object itself. In vector and affine settings, representatives depend on decomposition choices. In projective settings, many coordinate tuples represent the same point. The durable information is carried by equations, rank, incidence, and functorial behavior under maps.

Recap: What Complexification Preserves and Reveals

Synthesis
Preserves

Linear maps, composition, subspace equations, affine difference rules, and homogeneous projective incidence.

Reveals

New roots, new imaginary directions, and new projective points that can simplify the geometry of real questions.

Requires care

Coordinates and drawings must be checked against the invariant that defines the object.

Board Questions
