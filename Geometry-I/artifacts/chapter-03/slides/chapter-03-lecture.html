Motivation

Vector Addition Needs an Origin; Barycenters Do Not

Affine Combination

A formal expression \(\sum_i \lambda_i P_i\) is affine when the coefficients satisfy

\(\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_n=1.\)

The sum condition cancels the auxiliary origin used to compute coordinates.

Inspection target

Move the origin in the diagram. The coordinate vectors change, but the point determined by normalized weights does not.

Use this slide to separate two operations students often conflate. Vector addition belongs to a vector space with a chosen zero, while an affine combination is a statement about points whose coefficients sum to one. The diagram makes the origin look temporary, which is the correct mental model.
Definition

The Barycenter Is the Point That Balances the Weights

Barycenter Of Weighted Points

For points \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\) in an affine space and weights \(\lambda_i\) with \(\sum_i \lambda_i=1\), the barycenter \(G\) is written

\(G=\sum_i \lambda_i P_i.\)

Equivalently, for any origin used only to compute vectors,

\(\sum_i \lambda_i\,\overrightarrow{GP_i}=0.\)

Positive weights

Point lies in the convex hull of the support.

Zero weight

Point ignores that vertex but remains affine.

Negative weight

Point can leave the hull without leaving affine geometry.

Present the vector equation as the practical definition, because it avoids privileging coordinates. The weights may be masses, probabilities, or signed coefficients, but their normalized sum is what turns the formal expression into a point. Flag negative weights now so they are not treated as numerical failures later.
Barycentric Coordinates

A Triangle Turns Weights Into A Coordinate System

Affine Frame

If \(A,B,C\) are not collinear, then each point \(P\) in their affine plane has coordinates

\(P=\lambda_A A+\lambda_B B+\lambda_C C,\quad \lambda_A+\lambda_B+\lambda_C=1.\)

The coordinates are unique because the three vertices form an affinely independent frame.

How to read the colors

The lattice is not a decorative sampling. It marks the sign and magnitude pattern of barycentric weights. Interior points have three nonnegative entries; crossing an edge forces one coordinate through zero.

Use the notebook lattice as a visual coordinate chart. Each dot is a point, but the coordinates attached to it are weights on the three vertices rather than distances from an origin. Ask the room to predict which coordinate vanishes on each edge before discussing algebraic uniqueness.

Convex Versus Affine

Leaving the Triangle Is Allowed When Weights Are Signed

Convex hull

\(\lambda_i\ge 0\) and \(\sum\lambda_i=1\). The point stays inside the filled simplex.

Affine hull

Only \(\sum\lambda_i=1\) is required. Negative coefficients describe points beyond faces.

Two-point line: \((1-t)A+tB\), with \(t\in\mathbb{R}\).
This is the main misconception slide. Convex combinations are important, but they are only the nonnegative part of the affine story. On a line, the formula with parameter t already shows every case: between the points, at an endpoint, and beyond the segment when one coefficient is negative.
Universal Construction

The Free Affine Space Stores All Formal Barycenters

Universal Affine Space On \(S\)

Start with formal symbols \([s]\) for \(s\in S\). Define

\(U(S)=\left\{\sum_{s\in F}\lambda_s[s]\;:\;F\subset S\text{ finite},\ \sum_{s\in F}\lambda_s=1\right\}.\)

The original set enters by \(j(s)=[s]\).

Why universal?

It contains no accidental geometry beyond affine combination. Any affine information assigned to the generators must factor through this space.

Introduce the universal space as the affine analogue of a free construction. The elements are not mysterious new points; they are formal finite barycenters of the original set. This lets the chapter talk about the affine space generated by arbitrary points before coordinates or a particular model are chosen.
Morphism Test

Every Assignment Of Generators Extends Uniquely

Universal Property

Given any function \(f:S\to A\) into an affine space \(A\), there is a unique affine morphism \(F:U(S)\to A\) with \(F\circ j=f\).

\(F\left(\sum_s\lambda_s[s]\right)=\sum_s\lambda_s f(s).\)

The formula is well-defined precisely because the coefficients sum to one.

The universal property should be stated as a promise of no choices. Once the images of the generating points are specified, affine behavior determines the image of every formal barycenter. The dashed arrow is not extra data; it is forced by the requirement that barycenters go to barycenters.
Theorem And Proof Roadmap

Affine Morphisms Are Exactly The Maps That Preserve Barycenters

Statement

A map \(f:A\to B\) between affine spaces is affine if and only if, for every finite barycentric combination,

\(f\left(\sum_i\lambda_iP_i\right)=\sum_i\lambda_i f(P_i),\quad \sum_i\lambda_i=1.\)

Proof Skeleton

  1. Choose one point \(O\) only to translate the statement into vectors.
  2. Use affine linearity on the associated vector map.
  3. Translate back and observe the origin has disappeared.
This theorem is the operational reason barycenters matter. Instead of checking affine maps through coordinates every time, one can check whether the map commutes with normalized weighted sums. Keep the proof roadmap brief: coordinates are a temporary translation device, not part of the final statement.
Functions On Affine Space

Polynomials Are Coordinate Expressions With Affine Changes Allowed

Definition By A Chart

Choose an affine frame and write \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\). A function \(p:A\to k\) is polynomial when it can be written as a polynomial in these affine coordinates.

\(p(x)=\sum_{\alpha}c_\alpha x^\alpha.\)

The class does not depend on the frame because affine coordinate changes are polynomial of degree one.

Affine Pullback Test

If \(F:A\to B\) is affine and \(q\) is polynomial on \(B\), then \(q\circ F\) is polynomial on \(A\). Barycentric coordinates give one natural coordinate supply for this test.

Keep polynomial functions tied to the affine theme instead of launching a separate algebra lecture. A polynomial expression is written after choosing a frame, but changing to another affine frame only substitutes linear expressions plus constants. That is why the notion belongs naturally to affine geometry.
Subspaces And Coordinates

Affine Span Is The Closure Under Barycenters

Affine Span

The affine span of points \(P_i\) is the set of all affine combinations

\(\operatorname{Aff}(P_i)=\left\{\sum_i\lambda_iP_i:\sum_i\lambda_i=1\right\}.\)

It is the smallest affine subspace containing the points.

Independence Test

The points form an affine frame exactly when the barycentric coordinates of each point in the span are unique.

\(\sum_i\lambda_iP_i=\sum_i\mu_iP_i\Rightarrow \lambda_i=\mu_i.\)
