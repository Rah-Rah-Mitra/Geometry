Definition and working language

Convexity Begins With Every Segment

Convex Set

A subset \(K\) of a real affine space is convex when

\(x,y\in K,\ 0\le t\le 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t x+(1-t)y\in K.\)

The quantifier is the point: one escaping chord disproves convexity.

Finite Combination Certificate

Convex hulls use weights

\(\lambda_i\ge 0,\quad \sum_i \lambda_i=1,\quad x=\sum_i \lambda_i p_i.\)

This is the algebraic form of repeatedly taking segments.

Emphasize that the definition is global: every pair of points and every parameter must work. The finite affine-combination version is the working language for hulls, computation, and proofs because it replaces infinitely many segments by weights that can be checked algebraically.
Dimension, interior, and closure

Topology Refines the Segment Test

Dimension Is Affine

A segment in the plane has empty ambient interior, but nonempty relative interior in its own affine span.

Closure Preserves Convexity

Limits of points on chords remain limits of points in the set, so the segment condition survives closure.

Radial Visibility Is Weaker

A star-shaped set may see every point from one center, while a chord between two other points leaves the set.

Use the artifact as a diagnostic map. The disk, segment, open disk, and star-shaped region separate four ideas that students often merge: ambient dimension, relative interior, closure, and radial visibility. Ask learners to identify which panel carries a positive theorem and which carries a counterexample.
Finite hulls and scalar support values

A Convex Hull Is a Positive-Weight Promise

Convex Hull

For a finite set \(P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\}\),

\(\operatorname{conv}(P)=\left\{\sum_i \lambda_i p_i:\lambda_i\ge0,\ \sum_i\lambda_i=1\right\}.\)

Support Function

For direction \(u\), the support value records the tightest halfspace normal to \(u\):

\(h_K(u)=\sup_{x\in K} u\cdot x.\)

One direction gives one certificate; many directions outline the body.
Treat the hull as the smallest convex promise forced by a finite point cloud. The support function then turns geometry into a scalar maximization problem: one direction gives one tight inequality, and all directions together recover the body in the closed convex case.
Separating an exterior point

Outside Means Violating a Linear Inequality

Separation Certificate

For a closed convex set \(K\) and \(p\notin K\), find \(u\) such that

\(u\cdot x \le h_K(u) \quad \text{for all } x\in K,\)

\(u\cdot p > h_K(u).\)

Proof Roadmap
  • Choose a closest boundary point \(q\) when compactness or local existence applies.
  • Use the normal \(u=p-q\).
  • Convexity forces the whole body to one side of the perpendicular support line.
Frame separation as the moment when convexity becomes a measuring device. An exterior point is not just outside; it violates a linear inequality that every point of the closed convex body obeys. The nearest boundary point supplies the normal direction in the computational model.
Frontier and extreme points

Support Lines Expose Faces, Not Always Points

Extreme Point

A point \(v\in K\) is extreme when

\(v=t x+(1-t)y,\ 0

Finite Model

In a polygon, vertices are extreme points. A support line may touch one vertex or an entire edge, giving an exposed face.

Do Not Confuse Boundary With Extreme

Every extreme point lies on the frontier for a full-dimensional compact body, but most boundary points on a smooth arc are not polygon vertices.

Connect frontier, support, and extreme points without overloading the terminology. A support line can touch a whole face, but an extreme point is one that cannot be decomposed as a nontrivial midpoint of two other points in the set. Polygon vertices are the finite model to keep in view.
Convex bodies as feasible regions

Intersections of Halfspaces Stay Convex

Halfspace Intersection

If \(H_i=\{x:a_i\cdot x\le b_i\}\), then

\(P=\bigcap_i H_i\)

is convex because every inequality is preserved under convex combinations.

Certificate Check
  • Take \(x,y\in P\).
  • For each row \(i\), \(a_i\cdot x\le b_i\) and \(a_i\cdot y\le b_i\).
  • Linearity gives \(a_i\cdot(tx+(1-t)y)\le b_i\).
Explain halfspace intersections as the algebraic partner of support inequalities. Each inequality cuts space, and the convex body is the common feasible region. The tetrahedron drawing is a static stand-in for the notebook artifact, where every vertex satisfies every halfspace inequality.

Finite intersection tests

Helly Compresses Many Intersections Into Small Ones

Helly Theorem

For a finite family of convex sets in \(\mathbb{R}^d\), if every \(d+1\) members intersect, then the whole family intersects.

Plane Case

In \(\mathbb{R}^2\), triple intersections are the right local tests. Pairwise intersections do not control the whole family.

Dimension Matters

The number \(d+1\) is not decorative: it is the affine size of a simplex that can witness a missing common point.

State Helly as a dimension-dependent compression principle. In the plane, checking all triples is enough for a finite convex family, while pairwise checks alone can fail. The visual contrast should make the number three feel geometric rather than arbitrary.

Convex functions are convex sets upstairs

Epigraphs Convert Inequalities Into Geometry

Epigraph

For \(f:C\to\mathbb{R}\),

\(\operatorname{epi}(f)=\{(x,t):x\in C,\ t\ge f(x)\}.\)

Equivalence

A function is convex exactly when its epigraph is a convex subset of one higher-dimensional affine space.

Chord Interpretation

In one variable, every chord between two graph points must stay above the graph.

Move from sets to functions by lifting the graph into one higher dimension. A function is convex exactly when its epigraph is a convex set, so the chord condition becomes ordinary segment containment above the graph. This unifies Jensen inequalities with the geometry already developed.
Function certificates and failure modes

One Bad Midpoint Is Enough To Disprove Convexity

Jensen Inequality

For \(0\le t\le 1\), convexity of \(f\) gives

\(f(tx+(1-t)y)\le t f(x)+(1-t)f(y).\)

Smooth Certificate

For twice differentiable one-variable examples, \(f''(x)\ge0\) on the interval is a symbolic certificate of convexity.

Sampling Is Evidence, Not Proof

Many successful Jensen samples are informative, but a proof must control all pairs and all weights.

Distinguish three evidence types for convex functions: a chord picture, sampled Jensen residuals, and a symbolic Hessian or second derivative certificate. The nonconvex curve is useful because one failing midpoint already disproves convexity, while many successful samples still do not prove it.

Five Certificates To Carry Forward

Question Certificate
Is the set convex? Every segment stays inside
Is the point in a hull? Nonnegative weights summing to one
Is a point outside? A violated support inequality
Does a family intersect? Dimension-sized subfamily tests
Is a function convex? A convex epigraph or Jensen inequality
