Affine-Projective Relationship: Applications

The chapter adds a boundary of directions to an affine space and then uses that boundary as a geometric workbench.

Completion

\(X\) becomes \(\widehat X = X \cup \infty_X\), with ideal points recording directions.

Incidence

Parallel affine objects meet on the hyperplane at infinity after completion.

Applications

Choose infinity strategically to turn projective theorems into simpler affine pictures.

Three Equivalent Ways To Complete \(X\)

Coordinate chart model
\[ x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \longmapsto [x_1:\cdots:x_n:1]\in P^n(K) \]

The affine space is the chart \(w\ne 0\); the new boundary is \(w=0\).

Vectorized model

Embed \(X\) as the section \(X_a\times \{1\}\) inside \(X_a\times K\). Projectivizing creates a visible copy of \(P(X_a)\) at \(w=0\).

Intrinsic model

The universal vector space \(\widetilde X\) gives \(\widehat X=P(\widetilde X)\). This version is coordinate-free and makes functorial statements cleaner.

Practical choice

Use coordinates for calculation, vectorization for diagrams, and the intrinsic model when proving uniqueness or compatibility.

Projective Completion Separates Finite And Ideal Data

Definition

For an affine space \(X\) with direction vector space \(V\), set

\[ \widehat X = X \cup \infty_X,\qquad \infty_X=P(V). \]

A point of \(X\) is finite. A point of \(\infty_X\) is a direction, not a far-away ordinary point.

Emphasize the category mistake the slide is designed to prevent. An ideal point is not a remote finite point reached after traveling a long time. It is the direction class of all finite lines with a common direction. Every Hyperplane Complement Is An Affine Chart

Projective to affine

Let \(P(E)\) be a projective space and let \(P(H)\subset P(E)\) be a hyperplane. The complement

\[ P(E)\setminus P(H) \]

has a natural affine structure. Choosing \(H\) is the same as choosing which directions are called infinite.

Chart formula

If \(H=\{w=0\}\), then \([x_1:\cdots:x_n:w]\mapsto (x_1/w,\ldots,x_n/w)\) identifies \(w\ne 0\) with \(K^n\).

Key habit: name the hyperplane at infinity before interpreting a drawing as affine.
This is the converse direction of the relationship. A projective space does not come with a preferred affine chart until a hyperplane is singled out as infinity. Once that choice is made, ratios with the last coordinate recover affine coordinates. The phrase move the boundary should mean changing the affine interpretation while the underlying projective incidence structure remains unchanged.

Affine Maps Are Projective Maps Preserving Infinity

Extension rule

An affine map \(f(x)=Ax+b\) is represented projectively by

\[ \begin{bmatrix}A&b\\0&1\end{bmatrix}. \]

Finite points stay finite; direction points are acted on by the linear part \(A\).

Boundary test
\[ [x:1]\mapsto [Ax+b:1],\qquad [v:0]\mapsto [Av:0]. \]
  • The hyperplane \(w=0\) is invariant.
  • Translations disappear at infinity.
  • A projective map preserving \(w=0\) restricts to an affine map on \(w\ne 0\).
The boundary stores linear direction data, not affine displacement.
Use the block matrix as the algebraic hinge of the lecture. The lower row says that the chosen hyperplane at infinity is preserved. The translation vector changes finite positions, but it has no effect on ideal points because ideal points only remember direction. Conversely, when a projective transformation respects the chosen boundary, its finite chart action must be affine.

Parallel Affine Lines Meet At One Ideal Point

Direction class

A line \(p+K v\subset X\) completes to

\[ \overline{p+Kv}=(p+Kv)\cup\{[v]\}. \]

If two affine lines have the same direction vector up to scalar, their projective closures intersect at the same point of \(\infty_X\).

Notebook artifact: the finite chart shows no crossing, while the projective completion records one shared ideal endpoint.

This is the chapter's most teachable slogan, but it should be stated precisely. Parallel affine lines do not secretly cross inside the affine chart. Their completed projective lines intersect on the boundary, at the ideal point determined by their common direction. Affine Subspaces Close By Adding Their Directions

Closure of a subspace
\[ \overline S = S\cup \infty_S,\qquad \infty_S=P(\operatorname{Dir}S). \]

The ideal part of \(S\) depends only on its direction vector space.

Incidence translation

  • Equal direction spaces give the same ideal subspace.
  • Direction containment becomes containment at infinity.
  • The projective closure is the smallest projective subspace containing \(S\).
The diagram is an original schematic of the section 5.3 correspondence.
Move from lines to arbitrary affine subspaces. The closure formula is the main idea: add the projectivized direction space. If two subspaces are parallel, their finite locations differ but their direction spaces agree, so their ideal parts agree. If one direction space sits inside another, that inclusion is visible as incidence inside the boundary hyperplane.

A Boundary Dictionary For Affine Questions

Affine object Projective completion What to inspect at infinity
Point \(p\) Same finite point \([p:1]\) No ideal part; position data is finite.
Line \(p+Kv\) Projective line through \([p:1]\) and \([v:0]\) One direction point \([v:0]\).
Plane \(p+W\) Projective plane \(\overline S\) Ideal line \(P(W)\subset \infty_X\).
Parallelism Incidence of closures on \(\infty_X\) Equal or contained direction subspaces.

Common Pitfall

Do not decide parallelism from a distorted drawing. Decide it from the ideal part of the completed subspace, or equivalently from the direction vector space before completion.

Affine tests become projective incidence tests after completion.
This slide slows the lecture down into a usable dictionary. The point of the chapter is not merely that projective geometry is larger. It is that certain affine questions become cleaner because the missing cases are moved into a boundary where ordinary incidence language applies. The warning is important because projective drawings can make nonparallel lines appear visually suggestive.

Sending Objects To Infinity Is A Proof Technique

Recipe
  1. Identify a line, hyperplane, or point whose finite position complicates the statement.
  2. Choose a projective transformation that places it on the new boundary.
  3. Use preserved incidence in the new affine chart.
  4. Translate the result back to the original chart.

Why this works

Projective transformations preserve collinearity and incidence. The affine language changes only because the chosen hyperplane at infinity changes.

This is the strategic move behind the applications in section 5.4.
Introduce sending to infinity as a disciplined change of chart, not as a mystical operation. A projective transformation preserves the incidence facts being proved. What changes is the affine interpretation after we choose a new boundary. This is why the same theorem can have a projective version and several affine shadows depending on which object has been placed at infinity.

Pappus: Projective Statement, Affine Shadows

Projective version

Two triples of points on two lines determine three cross-intersection points. Pappus says those three points are collinear.

Affine use

If the Pappus line is chosen as the line at infinity, collinearity at infinity becomes a statement that the corresponding affine lines are parallel.

Teaching target

The same theorem generates several affine special cases without separate diagram-by-diagram proofs.

Original schematic; not a source-page figure.
The source span uses Pappus as an application of sending objects to infinity. Keep the statement qualitative here: the projective theorem gives collinearity of three constructed intersection points. If that collinearity line is chosen as the new boundary, the affine interpretation says the corresponding finite lines have become parallel. The power is that one projective proof controls many affine-looking cases.

Desargues: Concurrency And Collinearity Trade Affine Forms

Projective form

If corresponding vertices of two triangles are joined by concurrent lines, then the three intersections of corresponding sides are collinear. The converse is also projective.

Two choices of infinity

  • Send the side-intersection line to infinity: corresponding sides become parallel.
  • Send the center of perspectivity to infinity: corresponding vertex joins become parallel.
Desargues is a second model for the same chart-changing technique.
Desargues is useful because it has two visible features that can be sent to infinity: the center of perspectivity and the axis of side intersections. The projective theorem treats them uniformly as incidence data. The affine reader sees different-looking theorems depending on which feature is placed on the boundary. This reinforces that affine special cases are shadows of one projective configuration.

Fundamental Theorem: Reduce Through An Affine Chart

Projective collineation route

A bijection \(F:P(E)\to P(E')\) that sends collinear triples to collinear triples can be studied by choosing a hyperplane \(H\subset E\).

\[ P(E)\setminus P(H)\quad \xrightarrow{\quad F\quad}\quad P(E')\setminus P(H') \]

On affine charts, the affine fundamental theorem gives a semilinear map. Projectivizing it recovers \(F\).

The section uses completion as a bridge between affine and projective structure theorems.
Do not try to prove the full fundamental theorem on the slide. Instead, show the reduction strategy. A hyperplane choice creates an affine chart. On that chart, collinearity preservation becomes an affine theorem about semilinear maps. Once the semilinear map is found, projectivizing it gives the projective transformation. This is another instance of choosing a boundary to make the problem accessible.

Recap: The Boundary Is A Computation Device

Short exercise

In the affine plane with coordinates \((x,y)\), the line \(y=mx+b\) has direction vector \((1,m)\).

\[ y=mx+b\quad \leadsto\quad [1:m:0]\in \infty_{\mathbb A^2}. \]

Explain why changing \(b\) changes the finite line but not its ideal point. What is the ideal point of a vertical line?

Takeaways

  • Projective completion is \(X\) plus projectivized directions.
  • Choosing a hyperplane at infinity is choosing an affine chart.
  • Parallelism becomes incidence on the boundary.
  • Projective changes of chart can turn hard finite cases into parallel affine cases.

Next habit

When a configuration has exceptional parallel or missing-intersection cases, complete it projectively before splitting into affine subcases.
