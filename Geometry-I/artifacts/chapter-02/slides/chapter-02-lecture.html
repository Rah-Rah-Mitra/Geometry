Definition 2.1
Affine space as a vector torsor
Affine Space

An affine space \(A\) modeled on a vector space \(V\) has a free and transitive action \(A \times V \to A\), written \(P+v\).

For every \(P,Q \in A\), there is a unique \(v \in V\) with \(Q=P+v\).

Point-Vector Separation

Expressions like \(Q-P\) are vectors. Expressions like \(P+Q\) require extra choices and are not affine operations.

Spend time on the difference between a point and a free vector. A vector can move any point, but it is not itself a point of the affine space. The simply transitive action gives exactly one displacement from P to Q, which is the operational meaning of subtracting points.
Coordinates
Frames name points, but do not create them

Affine Frames Are Choices

Frame Formula

A frame \((O; e_1,\ldots,e_n)\) assigns coordinates by

\(P = O + x_1 e_1 + \cdots + x_n e_n.\)

  • Changing \(O\) translates the coordinate tuple.
  • Changing the basis applies an invertible linear change.
  • The point \(P\), the line through points, and incidence relations stay put.
Introduce coordinates as a temporary language created by choosing an origin and a basis of directions. The same geometric point receives different coordinate tuples after the origin or frame changes. The teaching target is that affine statements must survive this relabeling.
One-Dimensional Affine Geometry
Parameters, collinearity, and ratios

Ratios Without Lengths

Affine Invariant

If \(A,B,C\) lie on one affine line, the scalar \(t\) in \(C=A+t(B-A)\) is independent of the chosen frame.

Affine maps preserve collinearity and ratios along a single line; they need not preserve distances, angles, or perpendicularity.

Use this slide to show why affine geometry can discuss lines and ratios without discussing angles. Once a point P and a direction v are chosen, the parameter t labels the whole line. Affine maps preserve that parameter up to an affine change, so ratios along a line survive.
Morphisms
Linear part plus the image of one point

The Coordinate-Free Rule

Affine Map

A map \(f:A\to B\) is affine if there is a linear map \(L:V_A\to V_B\) such that

\(f(P+v)=f(P)+L(v)\).

after a frame is chosen: \(x \mapsto A x + b\)
Make the formula for an affine map feel inevitable. Choose one base point, record where it goes, and then use a linear map on all displacement vectors. The coordinate expression x maps to A x plus b is useful, but the coordinate-free rule explains why collinearity and parallelism are preserved.
Affine Subspaces
Translated linear structure
Subspace Form

An affine subspace is \(P+W=\{P+w:w\in W\}\), where \(W\le V\) is a vector subspace.

  • Direction space: \(\operatorname{dir}(P+W)=W\).
  • Parallel copies share the same \(W\) but have different base points.
  • Intersections are empty or affine subspaces with direction \(W_1\cap W_2\).
Explain an affine subspace as a point plus a linear direction subspace. The same direction subspace can appear in many translated copies, which is the affine meaning of parallelism. Intersections are therefore governed by linear directions plus the separate question of whether the translated sets meet.

Classical Incidence I
Parallel projection preserves affine parameters

Thales, Affine Version

Ratio Statement

If parallel lines cut two transversals from the same vertex, corresponding points have the same affine parameter.

\(\dfrac{OA_i}{OA_j} = \dfrac{OB_i}{OB_j}\)

The formula is read as a statement about one-dimensional affine coordinates on each transversal.

Present Thales as a ratio test for affine thinking. Parallel cuts across two rays create corresponding segments with the same parameter value. The theorem is not about Euclidean measurement of angles; it is about how parallel projection transports the one-dimensional affine coordinate.
Classical Incidence II
A collinearity theorem as a frame-invariant test

Pappus As A Robust Incidence Test

Theorem Shape

Put three points on one line and three on another. Intersect the three pairs of cross-joins. The three resulting points are collinear.

Affine coordinates can verify an instance, but the statement is about incidence, so frame changes cannot alter its truth.

Pappus is an incidence stress test. Six points constrained to two lines produce three intersection points, and the theorem asserts that those three are collinear. In lecture, emphasize that affine coordinates can verify one instance, while the statement itself is geometric and survives frame changes.
Classical Incidence III
Perspective triangles and a collinearity conclusion

Desargues In The Affine Plane

Incidence Principle

If the joins \(AA'\), \(BB'\), and \(CC'\) are concurrent, then the intersections of corresponding sides are collinear whenever they are finite.

The theorem is often easiest to remember as a projection shadow of a three-dimensional configuration.

Desargues links affine and projective habits. If corresponding vertices of two triangles are joined by concurrent lines, then the intersections of corresponding sides fall on one line. The slide should be used to show why three-dimensional projection intuition can organize a planar incidence theorem.

Chapter 2 Board Summary

Objects

Points form a torsor over a vector space. Vectors compare points, but no point is the default zero.

Coordinates

A frame is a useful language. Changing it changes tuples, not the affine relation being described.

Morphisms

Affine maps have a linear part plus one point image, so they preserve lines, parallelism, and line ratios.

Tests

Thales, Pappus, and Desargues become stress tests for incidence and frame-invariant reasoning.

Affine geometry asks: what can be proved before a metric or origin is chosen?
Close by returning to the chapter question. Affine geometry has enough structure to move points, compare displacements, choose frames, map subspaces, and test incidence theorems, but it refuses to choose a canonical origin or metric.
