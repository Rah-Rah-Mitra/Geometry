Working habit

Ask what is fixed

When a diagram changes coordinates, raw positions are expendable. The invariant relationship among the four labeled points is the durable object.

Use this slide to set the rhythm for the hour. The projective line is not a decorated real line; it is a space of one-dimensional directions in a two-dimensional vector space. Once that object is fixed, coordinates become tools, homographies become allowed changes of projective frame, and the cross-ratio becomes the quantity worth tracking.
Definition

The projective line is directions in \(k^2\)

Definition 6.1

\( \mathbb{P}^1(k) = (k^2 \setminus \{0\})/k^\times \)

  • \([X:Y]\) records a line through the vector origin.
  • The chart \(Y \ne 0\) uses \(x=X/Y\).
  • The missing direction is \([1:0]\), the point at infinity for this chart.
Spend time separating the vector picture from the projective object. A nonzero vector is only a representative, and multiplying it by a nonzero scalar should not create a new point. The affine coordinate x appears only after choosing the chart Y not equal to zero. The point at infinity is not mysterious; it is simply the direction where this chart stops seeing finite x.
Definition

The ordered-four-point scalar

Definition 6.2

For four distinct finite chart coordinates \(a,b,c,d\), define

\[ (A,B;C,D)=\frac{(c-a)(d-b)}{(c-b)(d-a)}. \]

Cases involving \(\infty\) are obtained by cancellation or by taking the limiting coordinate.

Define the cross-ratio as a chart formula but immediately warn that the chart is not the point of the definition. The four labels are ordered, so swapping labels changes the scalar in controlled ways. The formula is built from differences, but its meaning is projective because the dependence on the chosen affine coordinate will cancel under homographies.
Allowed motion

Homographies are projective frame changes

Homography

\(M=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\ \gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\), with \(\det M \ne 0\)

\[ [X:Y] \mapsto [\alpha X+\beta Y:\gamma X+\delta Y]. \]

  • Scalar multiples of \(M\) give the same projective map.
  • On \(Y \ne 0\), the map is fractional linear.
  • Poles and infinity are part of the same projective line.
Explain homographies as the projective version of changing coordinates by an invertible linear map upstairs in k squared. The fractional linear expression is only the affine-chart face of the same operation. Students should notice that the denominator vanishing is not a failure of the map; it is the moment when a finite chart coordinate is sent to infinity.
The key theorem

Cross-ratio survives every homography

Theorem 6.3

If \(H\) is a homography of \(\mathbb{P}^1(k)\), then for every ordered quadruple of distinct points,

\[ (H A,H B;H C,H D)=(A,B;C,D). \]

Proof in homogeneous coordinates

\[ [u,v;w,z]= \frac{\det(u,w)\det(v,z)}{\det(v,w)\det(u,z)}. \]

  • Replace every vector by \(Mu, Mv, Mw, Mz\).
  • Each determinant gains the same factor \(\det M\).
  • Two factors upstairs and two downstairs cancel exactly.
Inspection target

Do not watch distance

Under a homography, gaps, slopes, and finite coordinates may change. The determinant ratio is the object that has no reason to move.

This is the conceptual center of the lecture. Give the affine formula first if needed, but let the homogeneous determinant proof carry the main explanation because it shows why the cancellation is structural. A homography multiplies every two-by-two determinant by the same determinant of M, so the four factors in the cross-ratio lose all dependence on the chosen projective frame.
Sharp triple transitivity

A homography is determined by three points

Normalization

For distinct \(A,B,C\), there is a unique homography sending

\[ A \mapsto 0,\quad B \mapsto 1,\quad C \mapsto \infty . \]

The cross-ratio of \((A,B;C,D)\) is the coordinate assigned to the fourth point by this normalized frame.

Use the three point statement to demystify why the cross-ratio needs exactly four points. Three distinct points can always be renamed as zero, one, and infinity by one and only one homography. After that normalization, the fourth point has nowhere to hide; its coordinate is the invariant scalar attached to the original ordered quadruple.
Ordering matters

Permuting four labels gives six possible values

Permutation orbit

If \(\lambda=(A,B;C,D)\), all 24 reorderings reduce to these six expressions:

ValueTypical reordering
\(\lambda\)identity or double swaps
\(1/\lambda\)swap \(A,B\) and compare
\(1-\lambda\)move \(B\) into the third slot
\(1/(1-\lambda)\)compose the previous two moves
\(\lambda/(\lambda-1)\)interchange a source and target label
\((\lambda-1)/\lambda\)the remaining companion value
This slide prevents a common mistake. Students often say the cross-ratio of four points as though the unordered set had one scalar. The ordered quadruple has a scalar, while the unlabeled four point set carries a six-value orbit. This is also a useful place to connect the notebook's suggestion to permute labels after checking homography invariance.
Special value

Harmonic division is the value \(-1\)

Harmonic quadruple

A pair \((C,D)\) is harmonic with respect to \((A,B)\) when

\[ (A,B;C,D)=-1. \]

  • The condition is projective, not metric.
  • It behaves like a midpoint relation after a suitable normalization.
  • It is the natural fixed value for many involution arguments.
Introduce harmonic division as the first named special cross-ratio, not as a decorative classical term. The value minus one means the two pairs have a symmetric projective relation that survives every homography. Avoid describing it as ordinary Euclidean midpoint data; in a favorable chart it may look midpoint-like, but the definition is the invariant cross-ratio condition.
Dual viewpoint

Four lines through one point also have a cross-ratio

Dual application

Intersect four lines of a pencil with any line not through the center. The resulting four points on the transversal define the cross-ratio of the pencil.

Changing the transversal applies a homography to the intersection line, so the scalar is independent of that auxiliary choice.

This is the cleanest way to bring duality into the lecture without leaving the projective line. A pencil of lines through a point is itself a projective line. By cutting the pencil with a transversal, students can compute using ordinary points, and invariance under changing the transversal follows because the two transversals are related by a perspectivity, hence a homography.
Order two homographies

Involutions pair the projective line

Involution

A nonidentity homography \(I\) is an involution when \(I^2\) is the identity projective map.

\[ x \mapsto \frac{\alpha}{x} \]

This normalized model swaps \(0\) with \(\infty\) and pairs each \(x\) with \(\alpha/x\).

Keep the involution slide concrete. After choosing coordinates, an order two homography can be modeled by a reciprocal form, which makes the pairing behavior visible. The important point for this chapter is not classification for its own sake; it is that cross-ratio and harmonic configurations give a language for recognizing when paired points belong to the same projective symmetry.

Instructor-led exercise

Compute, transform, permute

Step 1

Compute

Use \(a=-1.5\), \(b=-0.2\), \(c=0.7\), \(d=1.8\) and evaluate \((A,B;C,D)\).

Step 2

Transform

Apply \(x \mapsto (x+0.3)/(0.08x+1)\). Recompute the cross-ratio from the four image points.

Step 3

Permute

Swap \(B\) and \(C\). Decide which of the six companion values appears, rather than expecting the original scalar.

Discussion prompt

What exactly did not change?

Students should answer in projective language: the labeled quadruple under a homography kept its cross-ratio, while a relabeling changed the invariant being asked for.

This exercise is short enough to do live or assign as a three minute pair check. It reinforces two separate ideas that can otherwise blur together: homographies preserve the cross-ratio of the same ordered labels, while permutations ask a different ordered question. The notebook values make the arithmetic concrete, and the six-value slide gives students a place to classify the changed answer.
Recap

What the projective line teaches

Takeaways
  • \(\mathbb{P}^1(k)\) is a line of directions, not merely an affine line with a symbol attached.
  • Homographies are invertible projective frame changes and appear as fractional linear maps in a chart.
  • The cross-ratio is invariant for ordered quadruples and becomes a coordinate after normalizing three points.
  • Permutations, harmonic division, pencils, and involutions are all organized by the same scalar.
Close by tying every object back to the central habit from the title slide: decide which relations survive projective motion. The projective line supplies the simplest possible setting, yet it already contains the full pattern of frame change, invariant scalar, special values, dual pencils, and involutions. This prepares the next chapters to use projective thinking without treating infinity or coordinate changes as exceptional events.
